**D'Alembert's principle** (after *Jean-Baptiste le Rond d’Alembert*) states that a body is in dynamic equilibrium when the sum of the external forces acting on it is equal to zero, if you include a "fictitious" inertial force ($F=ma$).

The principle, which is a restatement of Newton's second law, converts a dynamics problem into a statics problem by introducing an inertial force ($F_{I}=-ma$) that opposes the body's acceleration ($F = ma$). This makes it easier to solve problems, especially those with constraints, by using methods from statics.
