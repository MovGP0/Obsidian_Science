> [!TLDR]
> Start with foundations and follow the numbered topic folders, then choose a study route for your goals.

## Prerequisite routes

- Start with [[Number sets and the real line]], [[Mathematical notation and reasoning - Overview|notation and proof]], [[Algebra - Overview|algebra]], and [[Functions - Overview|functions]]. Follow with [[Geometry and trigonometry - Overview|geometry and trigonometry]] and [[Vectors - Overview|vectors]].
- For calculus, read [[Differential calculus - Overview|limits and derivatives]], [[Integral calculus - Overview|integrals]], and [[Sequences and series - Overview|series]]. Then continue to [[Multivariable calculus - Overview|multivariable calculus]] and [[Vector calculus - Overview|vector calculus]].
- For differential equations, start with [[Ordinary differential equations - Overview|ordinary differential equations]]. Use [[Fourier analysis - Overview|Fourier series]] and [[Boundary eigenvalue problems|boundary eigenfunctions]] before [[Partial differential equations - Overview|separation of variables for PDEs]].
- For geometry and mathematical physics, read [[Linear algebra - Overview|linear algebra]], [[27.01 Topological language|elementary topology]], [[Manifolds and coordinate charts - Overview|manifolds]], and [[Groups and continuous symmetries - Overview|Lie groups and symmetries]] before [[Gauge connections - Overview|gauge connections]].
- For probability and statistics, start with [[Events and conditional probability]], then use [[Random variables and joint distributions]] before [[Sampling distributions]] and [[Confidence intervals and hypothesis tests]].

## Worked applications

- [[From row reduction to least squares]] connects elimination, factorization, and data fitting.
- [[Likelihood and validation]] develops a parameter estimate and explains how to test prediction on held-out data.
- [[Markov chains and the logistic map]] compares stationary distributions with the stability of a nonlinear update.
- [[Separation of variables on a circular membrane]] derives the radial eigenfunctions for a fixed circular rim.
- [[Numerical derivatives and extrapolation]] uses Taylor error terms to combine evaluations at two step sizes.
