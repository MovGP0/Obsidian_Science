---
title: "3.1 Mass on an inclined plane"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Resolve forces along and perpendicular to an inclined plane to calculate the motion of a sliding mass.

Fig. 3.1 shows a delivery worker pushing a heavy box up a ramp. To calculate the motion, add the forces acting on the box and use Newton's second law to find its acceleration.

Suppose the worker loses footing and releases the box. The box slides down the ramp under gravity. Assume the contact between the box and the ramp has negligible friction. Find the speed of the box at the bottom and determine which quantities affect it.

Start with a *free-body diagram* showing the forces on the box. Add the forces to find the acceleration $\mechanical{\vec a}=\sum\mechanical{\vec F}/\mechanical{m}$.[^1] Then use the acceleration to find the velocity. Fig. 3.2 shows the free-body diagram for the frictionless case.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-1.png|**Fig. 3.1** A worker pushes a box up a ramp toward a delivery truck.]]

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-2.png|**Fig. 3.2** Free-body diagram of a box on a frictionless ramp.]]

Once the worker releases the box, two forces act on it. The gravitational force $\mechanical{\vec F}_g$ points vertically downward.[^2] The normal force $\mechanical{\vec F}_n$ is perpendicular to the ramp and acts on the box at the contact surface. Without the normal force, gravity would accelerate the box vertically downward.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-3.png|**Fig. 3.3** Free-body diagram with the x-axis down the ramp and the y-axis normal to it.]]

Gravity acts on each part of the box and the ramp exerts a force across the contact surface. For the translational motion, draw their resultants at the center of mass and use $\mechanical{\vec a}_{\mathrm{CM}}=\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{net}}/\mechanical{m}$. The points of application matter when calculating torque and angular acceleration.

Choose the positive $x$-axis down the ramp and the positive $y$-axis perpendicular to the ramp, as in Fig. 3.3. The normal force then lies entirely along positive $y$, and the box slides along positive $x$. Gravity points vertically downward and generally has components along both axes.[^3]

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-4.png|**Fig. 3.4** Geometry of the angle between gravity and the negative y-axis.]]

The angle $\theta$ between the ramp and the horizontal is also the angle between $\mechanical{\vec F}_g$ and the negative $y$-axis. The two right triangles in Fig. 3.4(b) show the relation. You can also check the limiting cases $\theta=0^\circ$ and $\theta=90^\circ$.

As shown in Fig. 3.5, resolving gravity into its components gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\vec F}_{g,x} &= |\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\sin\theta\,\hat i,\\
\mechanical{\vec F}_{g,y} &= -|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\cos\theta\,\hat j.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.1}
$$

The minus sign shows that the $y$-component points into the ramp.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-5.png|**Fig. 3.5** Components of the gravitational force along and perpendicular to the ramp.]]

It is customary to write vector components as signed scalars, as in [[1.3 Vector components]]. The indices identify the coordinate directions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{F}_{g,x}&=|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\sin\theta,\\
\mechanical{F}_{g,y}&=-|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\cos\theta.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.2}
$$

When adding forces, add the signed coefficients along each axis separately. The normal force contributes only along $y$, so the net force components are

$$
\sum\mechanical{F}_x=|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\sin\theta, \tag{3.3}
$$

and

$$
\sum\mechanical{F}_y=|\mechanical{\vec F}_n|-|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\cos\theta. \tag{3.4}
$$

Together they give

$$
\sum\mechanical{\vec F}
=|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\sin\theta\,\hat i+
\left(|\mechanical{\vec F}_n|-|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\cos\theta\right)\hat j. \tag{3.5}
$$

### Acceleration and normal force

Newton's second law gives

$$
\mechanical{a}_x=\frac{\sum\mechanical{F}_x}{\mechanical{m}}=
\frac{|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\sin\theta}{\mechanical{m}}, \tag{3.6}
$$

and

$$
\mechanical{a}_y=\frac{\sum\mechanical{F}_y}{\mechanical{m}}=
\frac{|\mechanical{\vec F}_n|-|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\cos\theta}{\mechanical{m}}. \tag{3.7}
$$

In vector form,

$$
\mechanical{\vec a}=\frac{\sum\mechanical{\vec F}}{\mechanical{m}}
=\frac{|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\sin\theta}{\mechanical{m}}\hat i
+\frac{|\mechanical{\vec F}_n|-|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\cos\theta}{\mechanical{m}}\hat j. \tag{3.8}
$$

The magnitude of the gravitational force is $|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}$, where $\mechanical{g}\approx\quantity{9.8}{m\,s^{-2}}$ near Earth's surface.[^4] The box stays in contact with the straight ramp, so its perpendicular acceleration is zero. Thus

$$
\mechanical{a}_x=\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\sin\theta}{\mechanical{m}}
=\mechanical{g}\sin\theta, \tag{3.9}
$$

and

$$
\mechanical{a}_y=\frac{|\mechanical{\vec F}_n|-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\theta}{\mechanical{m}}
=0. \tag{3.10}
$$

For a frictionless ramp, the downhill acceleration depends only on $\mechanical{g}$ and $\theta$. The mass cancels.[^5] The magnitude is at most $\mechanical{g}$. The limit $\theta=90^\circ$ corresponds to free fall next to a vertical surface.

Solving Equation (3.10) for the normal force gives

$$
|\mechanical{\vec F}_n|=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\theta. \tag{3.11}
$$

The normal force depends on both the weight and the incline angle.

The normal force equals the full weight when $\theta=0^\circ$ and falls to zero when $\theta=90^\circ$. At intermediate angles it balances the component of the weight perpendicular to the ramp. Its magnitude is also needed for the friction calculation.

### Speed at the bottom of the ramp

The acceleration along the ramp is constant. For an initial velocity $\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{initial}}$, the final velocity after a time $t$ is

$$
\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{final}}
=\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{initial}}+\mechanical{a}_x t. \tag{3.12}
$$

If the box moves a distance $\mechanical{d}$ down the ramp, use

$$
\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{final}}^2
=\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{initial}}^2+2\mechanical{a}_x\mechanical{d}. \tag{3.13}
$$

For release from rest, substitute Equation (3.9).

$$
\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{final}}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}\sin\theta}. \tag{3.14}
$$

On a $\quantity{2}{m}$ ramp inclined at $30^\circ$ on Earth, the speed at the bottom is

$$
\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{final}}
=\sqrt{2(\quantity{9.8}{m\,s^{-2}})(\quantity{2}{m})\sin30^\circ}
\approx\quantity{4.4}{m\,s^{-1}}. \tag{3.15}
$$

Equation (3.12) gives a travel time of approximately $\quantity{0.9}{s}$.

### Friction

Static friction adjusts to the applied force up to a maximum and can keep the box at rest. After the box starts moving, kinetic friction opposes its motion. Its magnitude depends on the normal force and the coefficient of kinetic friction.[^6]

Add the kinetic friction force $\mechanical{\vec F}_f$ to the free-body diagram, as in Fig. 3.6. Because the box slides downhill, kinetic friction points up the ramp, in the negative $x$-direction.

The signed net force along the ramp becomes

$$
\sum\mechanical{F}_x=|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\sin\theta-|\mechanical{\vec F}_f|. \tag{3.16}
$$

The acceleration is

$$
\mechanical{a}_x
=\frac{\sum\mechanical{F}_x}{\mechanical{m}}
=\frac{|\mechanical{\vec F}_g|\sin\theta-|\mechanical{\vec F}_f|}{\mechanical{m}}. \tag{3.17}
$$

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-6.png|**Fig. 3.6** The kinetic friction force points up the ramp as the box slides down it.]]

The magnitude of kinetic friction is the product of the normal force magnitude and the coefficient of kinetic friction $\mechanical{\mu}_k$.

$$
|\mechanical{\vec F}_f|=\mechanical{\mu}_k|\mechanical{\vec F}_n|. \tag{3.18}
$$

Use Equation (3.11) for the normal force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{a}_x &=
\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\sin\theta
-\mechanical{\mu}_k\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\theta}{\mechanical{m}}\\
&=\mechanical{g}\sin\theta-\mechanical{\mu}_k\mechanical{g}\cos\theta.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.19}
$$

Friction reduces the downhill acceleration by $\mechanical{\mu}_k\mechanical{g}\cos\theta$. For the $\quantity{2}{m}$ ramp at $30^\circ$, use $\mechanical{\mu}_k=0.4$ to calculate the reduced speed.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{final}}
&=\sqrt{2(\quantity{2}{m})(\quantity{9.8}{m\,s^{-2}})
\left(\sin30^\circ-0.4\cos30^\circ\right)}\\
&\approx\quantity{2.5}{m\,s^{-1}}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.20}
$$

Equation (3.19) assumes that the box is sliding downhill. A negative $\mechanical{a}_x$ in that case means that it slows down. If the box starts at rest, compare the downhill component of its weight with the maximum static friction to determine whether it starts moving.[^7]

[^1]: In $\sum\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical m\mechanical{\vec a}$, it is the sum of all forces that determines the acceleration. The same force produces greater acceleration on a smaller mass.
[^2]: The model neglects local variations in gravity.
[^3]: With horizontal and vertical axes, gravity is vertical and the normal force has two components. Axes aligned with the ramp simplify other forces such as friction.
[^4]: Mass measures inertia. Weight is a force of magnitude $\mechanical m\mechanical g$ directed downward. Weight changes with the local gravitational field.
[^5]: Gravitational mass and inertial mass have the same value, so the mass cancels in the free-fall acceleration.
[^6]: Introductory physics texts by Serway and Jewett or Halliday, Resnick, and Walker discuss friction.
[^7]: The maximum static friction is the coefficient of static friction multiplied by the normal force magnitude. Compare it with the total force along the ramp excluding friction.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the box. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_g$, $\mechanical{F}_{g,x}$, $\mechanical{F}_{g,y}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Gravitational force | The weight of the box and its components. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_n$ | $\unit{N}$ | Normal force | The contact force perpendicular to the ramp. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_f$ | $\unit{N}$ | Kinetic friction force | The force opposing the sliding motion. |
| $\sum\mechanical{\vec F}$, $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{net}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Net force | The sum of the forces on the box. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}$, $\mechanical{\vec a}_{\mathrm{CM}}$, $\mechanical{a}_x$, $\mechanical{a}_y$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The acceleration of the box and its components. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The magnitude of the local free-fall acceleration. |
| $\theta$ | $\unit{{}^\circ}$ | Ramp angle | The angle between the ramp and the horizontal. |
| $\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{initial}}$, $\mechanical{v}_{x,\mathrm{final}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity along the ramp | The signed velocities at the beginning and end of the motion. |
| $\mechanical{d}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Travel distance | The distance down the ramp. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Travel time | The elapsed time of the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}_k$ | 1 | Coefficient of kinetic friction | The ratio of kinetic friction to normal force. |
