---
title: "3.4 The magnetic field"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A steady current produces a magnetic field, and that field exerts a perpendicular force on a moving charge.

Steady currents produce magnetostatic fields. For a long straight wire, the magnetic field lines form circles centered on the wire. Point the thumb of your right hand along the conventional current. Your curled fingers indicate the magnetic field direction. Reversing the current reverses the field direction.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-20.png|**Fig. 3.20** The magnetic field of a long straight wire carrying a steady current.]]

The magnetic field decreases with distance from the wire. For a thin, infinitely long wire carrying current $\electric{I}$ along the positive axis,

$$
\magnetic{\vec B}=\frac{\magnetic{\mu}_0\electric{I}}{2\pi\mechanical{r}}\hat\phi, \tag{3.35}
$$

where $\magnetic{\mu}_0$ is the permeability of free space, $\mechanical{r}$ is the perpendicular distance from the wire, and $\hat\phi$ points in the cylindrical azimuthal direction. The SI unit for $\magnetic{\vec B}$ is the tesla, $\unit{T}$.

### Magnetic divergence and curl

The magnetic field lines around the wire circulate, with no sources or sinks of the field. Gauss's law for magnetism expresses the vanishing divergence.

$$
\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\vec B}=0. \tag{3.36}
$$

For the field in Equation (3.35), the only cylindrical component is $\magnetic{B}_\phi$. It has no azimuthal dependence, so for $\mechanical{r}>0$,

$$
\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\vec B}
=\frac1{\mechanical{r}}\frac{\partial\magnetic{B}_\phi}{\partial\phi}=0.
$$

Use the cylindrical [[2.10 Curl|curl]] formula for the same field.

$$
\nabla\times\magnetic{\vec B}
=-\frac{\partial\magnetic{B}_\phi}{\partial\mechanical{z}}\hat r
+\frac1{\mechanical{r}}
\frac{\partial(\mechanical{r}\magnetic{B}_\phi)}{\partial\mechanical{r}}\hat z
=\vec 0,\qquad\mechanical{r}>0.
$$

The first derivative vanishes because the field is independent of $\mechanical{z}$. The second vanishes because $\mechanical{r}\magnetic{B}_\phi=\magnetic{\mu}_0\electric{I}/(2\pi)$ is constant. The wire axis is singular in the thin-wire model and carries the current that gives the nonzero distributional curl.

### Magnetic force on a moving charge

For a particle of charge $\electric{q}$ moving at velocity $\mechanical{\vec v}$ in a magnetic field,

$$
\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm B}=\electric{q}\mechanical{\vec v}\times\magnetic{\vec B}. \tag{3.37}
$$

Let $\theta$ be the angle between the velocity and the magnetic field. The magnitude of the force is

$$
|\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm B}|
=|\electric{q}|\,|\mechanical{\vec v}|\,|\magnetic{\vec B}|\sin\theta. \tag{3.38}
$$

Both electric and magnetic forces scale with the magnitude of the charge and of the corresponding field. Reversing the charge reverses the force. The magnetic force also depends on the speed and the angle between velocity and field, and it is perpendicular to both vectors.

The magnetic force vanishes when the particle is stationary or moves parallel or antiparallel to the field. At a fixed speed and field magnitude, the force is greatest when $\theta=90^\circ$.

Because the force is perpendicular to the velocity, it can change the direction of motion. Its tangential component vanishes, so it leaves the speed unchanged. See [[3.2 Curvilinear motion]] for the distinction between tangential and normal acceleration.

In Fig. 3.21 the particle moves to the right. Crosses in circles show a field pointing into the page.[^11]

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-21.png|**Fig. 3.21** A charged particle moves to the right in a magnetic field into the page.]]

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-22.png|**Fig. 3.22** The magnetic force points upward for positive charge and downward for negative charge in this configuration.]]

Use the right-hand rule to find $\mechanical{\vec v}\times\magnetic{\vec B}$. For a particle moving right in a field into the page, this product points up. Multiplication by a negative charge reverses that direction.

### Circular motion in a uniform field

In Fig. 3.23, a positive charge starts at the left of its path with velocity pointing up the page. The field points out of the page, so the initial magnetic force points right. At the top of the path, the velocity points right and the force points down. At the right, the velocity points down and the force points left. At the bottom, the velocity points left and the force points up. The inward force keeps the particle moving clockwise in a circle at constant speed.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-3-23.png|**Fig. 3.23** A positive charge follows a clockwise circle with the magnetic field pointing out of the page.]]

In the same field, a negative charge moves counterclockwise. Reversing the field reverses the sense of rotation for either charge.

If the velocity also has a component $\mechanical{v}_\parallel$ along the field, the particle moves along the field as it circles around it. The resulting path is a helix. The magnetic force has no component along the field, so $\mechanical{v}_\parallel$ stays constant when no other forces act.

[^11]: A circled cross ⊗ represents an arrow pointing away from the observer, and a circled dot ⊙ represents an arrow pointing toward the observer.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\vec B}$, $\magnetic{B}_\phi$ | $\unit{T}$ | Magnetic flux density | The magnetic field and its azimuthal component. |
| $\electric{I}$ | $\unit{A}$ | Electric current | The steady current in the wire. |
| $\magnetic{\mu}_0$ | $\unit{N\,A^{-2}}$ | Vacuum permeability | The constant in the magnetic field of a wire in vacuum. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Cylindrical radius | The perpendicular distance to the wire. |
| $\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Axial coordinate | The position along the wire. |
| $\electric{q}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Electric charge | The charge of the moving particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Magnetic force | The force on the moving particle due to the magnetic field. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$, $\mechanical{v}_\parallel$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The particle velocity and its component along the field. |
| $\theta$ | $\unit{{}^\circ}$ | Angle between velocity and field | The angle that determines the magnitude of the cross product. |
