---
title: "4.5 Dual basis vectors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A reciprocal basis reconstructs a vector from the covariant components obtained with the original basis.

Fig. 4.13 shows non-orthogonal basis vectors $\vec e_1$ and $\vec e_2$. The contravariant components $A^x$ and $A^y$ are the coefficients of the expansion in that basis.[^5]

$$
\vec A=A^x\vec e_1+A^y\vec e_2. \tag{4.20}
$$

Fig. 4.12(b) shows why multiplying the perpendicular-projection components $A_x$ and $A_y$ by $\vec e_1$ and $\vec e_2$ generally gives a different vector. To reconstruct $\vec A$ from these components, use the *reciprocal* or *dual* basis vectors.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-4-13.png|**Fig. 4.13** Parallel-projection components in the original basis.]]

The reciprocal vectors $\vec e^{\,1}$ and $\vec e^{\,2}$ each satisfy two conditions. The vector $\vec e^{\,1}$ is perpendicular to $\vec e_2$, and $\vec e^{\,2}$ is perpendicular to $\vec e_1$. Each also has dot product one with the original basis vector of the same index. Fig. 4.14 shows their directions.

Let $\theta_1$ be the angle between $\vec e^{\,1}$ and $\vec e_1$ and let $\theta_2$ be the angle between $\vec e^{\,2}$ and $\vec e_2$. The dot-product condition determines their lengths.[^6] In particular,

$$
|\vec e^{\,1}|=\frac1{|\vec e_1|\cos\theta_1}. \tag{4.21}
$$

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-4-14.png|**Fig. 4.14** Perpendicular-projection components and their dual basis vectors.]]

For the other reciprocal vector,

$$
|\vec e^{\,2}|=\frac1{|\vec e_2|\cos\theta_2}. \tag{4.22}
$$

To reconstruct $\vec A$, use the covariant coefficients $A_x$ and $A_y$ with the reciprocal basis vectors.

$$
\vec A=A_x\vec e^{\,1}+A_y\vec e^{\,2}. \tag{4.23}
$$

The upper indices on the reciprocal basis indicate that it transforms inversely to the original basis. In an orthonormal basis, the reciprocal vector of each index has the same direction as the corresponding original vector and length $1/(1\cos0^\circ)=1$. The two bases coincide.

In three dimensions, the reciprocal basis of $\vec e_1$, $\vec e_2$, and $\vec e_3$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec e^{\,1}&=\frac{\vec e_2\times\vec e_3}{\vec e_1\cdot(\vec e_2\times\vec e_3)},\\
\vec e^{\,2}&=\frac{\vec e_3\times\vec e_1}{\vec e_1\cdot(\vec e_2\times\vec e_3)},\\
\vec e^{\,3}&=\frac{\vec e_1\times\vec e_2}{\vec e_1\cdot(\vec e_2\times\vec e_3)}.
\end{aligned} \tag{4.24}
$$

Each numerator is perpendicular to the two basis vectors with different indices. The common denominator is the [[2.3 Triple scalar product|scalar triple product]], which gives the signed volume of the parallelepiped formed by the original basis vectors. Dividing by it gives dot product one with the basis vector of the same index.

Equations (4.20) and (4.23) give two representations of the same vector.

$$
\vec A=A^x\vec e_1+A^y\vec e_2=A_x\vec e^{\,1}+A_y\vec e^{\,2}. \tag{4.25}
$$

A component with an upper index pairs with a basis vector with a lower index. A component with a lower index pairs with a reciprocal basis vector with an upper index. In each expansion, the basis and component transformations cancel.

[^5]: Upper indices label contravariant components and lower indices label covariant components.
[^6]: See [[2.1 Scalar product]] for $\vec A\cdot\vec B=|\vec A|\,|\vec B|\cos\theta$.
