---
title: "4.9 Quantities that transform covariantly"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A scalar differential has covariant components that transform with the inverse Jacobian.

A scalar field can describe a temperature or density that varies with position. Its rates of change are given per unit coordinate, for example per meter for a Cartesian spatial coordinate. The components of the differential $\mathrm df$ are the standard example of quantities that transform covariantly. With the Euclidean metric, these are also the covariant components of the gradient vector.

For $f=f(x,y,z)$, use the chain rule to find the partial derivatives in the primed system.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x'}&=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial x'}
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial x'}
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x'},\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial y'}&=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial y'}
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial y'}
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial y'},\\
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z'}&=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial z'}
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial z'}
+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial z'}.
\end{aligned}
$$

With indices, the three factors in the row for $\partial f/\partial x^{\prime i}$ are $\partial x^1/\partial x^{\prime i}$, $\partial x^2/\partial x^{\prime i}$, and $\partial x^3/\partial x^{\prime i}$.

Write the three equations as a matrix product.

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\prime 1}}\\\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\prime 2}}\\\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\prime 3}}\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{\partial x^1}{\partial x^{\prime 1}}&\dfrac{\partial x^2}{\partial x^{\prime 1}}&\dfrac{\partial x^3}{\partial x^{\prime 1}}\\
\dfrac{\partial x^1}{\partial x^{\prime 2}}&\dfrac{\partial x^2}{\partial x^{\prime 2}}&\dfrac{\partial x^3}{\partial x^{\prime 2}}\\
\dfrac{\partial x^1}{\partial x^{\prime 3}}&\dfrac{\partial x^2}{\partial x^{\prime 3}}&\dfrac{\partial x^3}{\partial x^{\prime 3}}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x^{1}}\\\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x^{2}}\\\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x^{3}}\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4.59}
$$

Each row is a sum over $j$. With $i$ as the free index,

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\prime i}}
=\sum_{j=1}^3\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime i}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^j}. \tag{4.60}
$$

In Einstein notation,

$$
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{\prime i}}
=\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime i}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^j}. \tag{4.61}
$$

Equation (4.53) uses the Jacobian $\partial x^{\prime i}/\partial x^j$, while Equation (4.61) uses the inverse Jacobian entries $\partial x^j/\partial x^{\prime i}$. With the components stored as columns, the covariant transformation matrix is the transpose of the inverse Jacobian. The reciprocal basis is normal to coordinate surfaces and provides the corresponding expansion of the gradient. In orthonormal frames with the same Euclidean metric, the original and reciprocal bases coincide.

The general rule for covariant components is

$$
A'_i=\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime i}}A_j. \tag{4.62}
$$

The basis and components compensate for each other under a change of coordinates. Pair contravariant components with the coordinate basis, or covariant components with the reciprocal basis, to recover the same vector.

A vector is a tensor of rank one and its components use one index. A scalar is a tensor of rank zero and has no component index. See [[5 Higher-rank tensors]] for components with two or more indices.
