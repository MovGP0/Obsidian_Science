---
title: "4.3 Basis-vector vs. component transformations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For a fixed vector, the transformation of its components compensates for the transformation of the basis.

Compare the matrix in [[4.1 Coordinate-system transformations|Equation (4.7)]] with the matrix in [[4.2 Basis-vector transformations|Equation (4.15)]]. After rotating the coordinate system through $\theta$, the passive matrix gives the components of the same vector in the new system.

$$
M_{\mathrm{passive}}(\theta)
=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The active rotation matrix produces new basis vectors expressed in the original system.

$$
R(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The product of the two matrices is the identity.

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Thus $M_{\mathrm{passive}}(\theta)=R(\theta)^{-1}=R(\theta)^{\mathsf T}$. The equality of the inverse and transpose makes $R$ an orthogonal matrix. The component change compensates for the rotation of the basis, so the reconstructed vector stays fixed.

For more general basis changes, the transformation depends on the type of component used to represent the vector. [[4.4 Non-orthogonal coordinate systems]] shows how the two types arise.
