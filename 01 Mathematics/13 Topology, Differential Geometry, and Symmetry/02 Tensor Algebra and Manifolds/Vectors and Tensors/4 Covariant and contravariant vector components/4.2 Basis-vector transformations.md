---
title: "4.2 Basis-vector transformations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An active rotation gives new basis vectors and can be expressed as a matrix acting in the original coordinates.

[[4.1 Coordinate-system transformations]] expresses the same vector in a rotated basis. To rotate the basis vectors themselves, express the original and rotated vectors in the same coordinate system.

Covariant components transform with the basis vectors, while contravariant components transform with the inverse of that change.[^2] To apply these terms, distinguish between the rotation of a vector to produce a new vector and the re-expression of the same vector in new coordinates. Schutz described why this terminology can cause confusion.[^3]

In Figs. 4.7 and 4.8, rotating $\vec A$ counterclockwise through $\alpha$ produces $\vec A'$. In the fixed Cartesian system,

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_x&=|\vec A|\cos\theta,& A'_x&=|\vec A'|\cos\theta',\\
A_y&=|\vec A|\sin\theta,& A'_y&=|\vec A'|\sin\theta'.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\theta'=\theta+\alpha$, the angle addition formulas give

$$
\begin{aligned}
A'_x&=|\vec A'|(\cos\alpha\cos\theta-\sin\alpha\sin\theta),\\
A'_y&=|\vec A'|(\sin\alpha\cos\theta+\cos\alpha\sin\theta).
\end{aligned}
$$

A rigid rotation preserves length, so $|\vec A'|=|\vec A|$.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-4-7.png|**Fig. 4.7** Active rotation of a vector in fixed Cartesian coordinates.]]

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-4-8.png|**Fig. 4.8** Original angle θ, rotation angle α, and final angle θ′.]]

Use $|\vec A'|=|\vec A|$ to express the rotated components in terms of the original magnitude.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A'_x&=(|\vec A|\cos\theta)\cos\alpha-(|\vec A|\sin\theta)\sin\alpha,\\
A'_y&=(|\vec A|\cos\theta)\sin\alpha+(|\vec A|\sin\theta)\cos\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$

The parenthesized factors are the original components $A_x$ and $A_y$.

The component equations are

$$
\begin{aligned}
A'_x&=A_x\cos\alpha-A_y\sin\alpha,\\
A'_y&=A_x\sin\alpha+A_y\cos\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$

In matrix form,

$$
\begin{pmatrix}A'_x\\A'_y\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\alpha&-\sin\alpha\\\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A_x\\A_y\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4.15}
$$

For $\vec A=5\hat i+3\hat j$ and $\alpha=150^\circ$,

$$
\begin{pmatrix}A'_x\\A'_y\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\cos150^\circ&-\sin150^\circ\\\sin150^\circ&\cos150^\circ\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}5\\3\end{pmatrix}
\approx\begin{pmatrix}-5.83\\-0.10\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4.16}
$$

The new vector $\vec A'\approx-5.83\hat i-0.10\hat j$ points almost directly along the negative $x$-axis of the original system.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-4-9.png|**Fig. 4.9** The rotated Cartesian unit vectors expressed in the original coordinate system.]]

The same method rotates the original basis vectors. Let $R(150^\circ)$ be the matrix in Equation (4.15) evaluated at $150^\circ$. For $\hat i'$,

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\hat i'_x\\\hat i'_y\end{pmatrix}
=R(150^\circ)\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}\cos150^\circ\\\sin150^\circ\end{pmatrix}
\approx\begin{pmatrix}-0.866\\0.5\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4.17}
$$

The two entries are the coordinates of the rotated vector $\hat i'$ in the original basis, as shown in Fig. 4.9(a).

For $\hat j'$, Fig. 4.9(b) gives the corresponding result.

$$
\begin{pmatrix}\hat j'_x\\\hat j'_y\end{pmatrix}
=R(150^\circ)\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin150^\circ\\\cos150^\circ\end{pmatrix}
\approx\begin{pmatrix}-0.5\\-0.866\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4.18}
$$

The new basis vectors $\hat i'$ and $\hat j'$ have the components above in the original system. In the rotated system, they have components $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, respectively.

The source calls a transformation that produces new basis vectors *direct* or *active*. For an active matrix $R$ and a basis written as the ordered columns of $E$,

$$
E'=RE. \tag{4.19}
$$

Each column of $E'$ is a new basis vector expressed in the fixed coordinates. Compare this with the passive transformation in [[4.1 Coordinate-system transformations|Equation (4.14)]].
