> [!TLDR]
> Practice active and passive rotations, dual bases, and component transformations.

### 4.1 Rotation matrices

Write the passive matrix for rotating two-dimensional Cartesian axes through $70^\circ$ and the active matrix for rotating a vector through $70^\circ$. Show that their product is the identity matrix.

### 4.2 Components in the rotated frame

Use the passive matrix to find the components of $\vec A=2\hat i+5.5\hat j$ in the rotated coordinate system.

### 4.3 Rotated basis vectors

Use the active matrix to rotate $\hat i$ and $\hat j$ through $70^\circ$.

### 4.4 Compare an active and a passive rotation

Rotate the vector in Problem 4.2 through $-70^\circ$. Compare its Cartesian components in the original basis with the components of the original vector in the rotated basis.

### 4.5 Components from dot products

Use $\vec A\cdot\hat i'$ and $\vec A\cdot\hat j'$ to find the components of $\vec A$ in the rotated coordinate system.

### 4.6 Contravariant components

For $\vec A=-5\hat i+6\hat j$, $\vec e_1=\hat i+2\hat j$, and $\vec e_2=-2\hat i-\hat j$, find $A^1$ and $A^2$.

### 4.7 Dual basis

Find $\vec e^{\,1}$ and $\vec e^{\,2}$ for the basis in Problem 4.6.

### 4.8 Covariant components

Find $A_1$ and $A_2$ for $\vec A$ in Problem 4.6.

### 4.9 Solve for components

Use substitution and elimination to solve the simultaneous equations from [[4.6 Finding covariant and contravariant components|Equation (4.26)]].

### 4.10 Cartesian-to-polar Jacobian

Show that the columns of the Cartesian-to-polar transformation matrix are the polar components of the basis vectors tangent to the original Cartesian axes.
