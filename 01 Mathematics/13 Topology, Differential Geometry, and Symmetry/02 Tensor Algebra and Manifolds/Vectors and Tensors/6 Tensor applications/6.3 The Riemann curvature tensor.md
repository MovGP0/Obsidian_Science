---
title: "6.3 The Riemann curvature tensor"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Riemann curvature tensor measures the intrinsic curvature of space or spacetime from the metric and its derivatives.

After publishing special relativity in 1905, Einstein investigated why inertial and gravitational mass are equal. Inertial mass determines how strongly a body resists acceleration. Gravitational mass determines how it responds to a gravitational field. Einstein sought an explanation by extending relativity to frames whose relative motion could be accelerated.[^7]

Imagine rocks of different masses far from any substantial gravitational field. They are at rest in the inertial frame $K$. In a frame $K'$ accelerating uniformly relative to $K$, the rocks appear to accelerate together in the opposite direction at the same rate. An observer in $K'$ could interpret their common acceleration as due to a gravitational field. This local equivalence between uniform acceleration and gravity is the **principle of equivalence**.

Next align the $z'$-axis with the $z$-axis and let $K'$ rotate at uniform angular speed. Points further from the axis have greater speeds in $K$. The length contraction and time dilation of objects at rest in $K'$ therefore depend on position. Einstein used the equivalence principle to infer that gravity may likewise affect lengths and clock rates. The gravitational field thus influences the geometry of spacetime.

General relativity expresses spacetime geometry with tensors, using the [[5.5 Metric tensor|metric tensor]], Christoffel symbols, and covariant derivatives. The **Riemann curvature tensor**, also called the Riemann–Christoffel tensor, is named for Bernhard Riemann and Elwin Bruno Christoffel. Nonzero components indicate intrinsic curvature. Vanishing curvature throughout a neighborhood characterizes local flatness there.

There are two common ways to derive the tensor, using parallel transport or the commutator of covariant derivatives. In flat Cartesian space, parallel transport keeps a vector’s components constant as it moves. This preserves its length and direction.

For a curved example, model Earth as a sphere. Start at the equator near Quito with a tangent vector pointing north. Carry the vector along the meridian over the North Pole and on to the opposite point on the equator, keeping it tangent to the meridian. At the destination it points south. Now transport the same starting vector to the same destination along the equator, keeping it tangent to the surface and pointing north. It still points north on arrival. The transported vector depends on the path.

Vectors based at different points belong to different tangent spaces. To compare them directly, first transport one to the other’s location. In curved space, **parallel transport** along a curve means that the covariant derivative along the curve is zero. As in [[5.7 Tensor derivatives and Christoffel symbols|Section 5.7]], the Christoffel term accounts for changes in the basis. Parallel transport around a small loop leads to the curvature tensor.[^8]

The second derivation uses the **commutator** $[A,B]=AB-BA$, the difference between applying two operators in opposite orders. Covariant derivatives commute in flat space. Their failure to commute on a covector measures curvature. Begin by differentiating $V_\alpha$ covariantly with respect to $x^\beta$.

$$
V_{\alpha;\beta}=\frac{\partial V_\alpha}{\partial x^\beta}-\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\beta}V_\sigma.
\tag{6.21}
$$

Treat the result as a covariant rank-2 tensor and differentiate again with respect to $x^\gamma$.

Let $V_{\alpha\beta}=V_{\alpha;\beta}$ for this calculation.

$$
V_{\alpha\beta;\gamma}
=\frac{\partial V_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^\gamma}
-\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\gamma}V_{\tau\beta}
-\Gamma^\eta_{\beta\gamma}V_{\alpha\eta}.
\tag{6.22}
$$

Substitute Equation (6.21) and apply the product rule.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{\alpha\beta;\gamma}
&=\frac{\partial^2 V_\alpha}{\partial x^\gamma\,\partial x^\beta}
-\frac{\partial\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^\gamma}V_\sigma
-\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial V_\sigma}{\partial x^\gamma}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\gamma}
\left(\frac{\partial V_\tau}{\partial x^\beta}-\Gamma^\sigma_{\tau\beta}V_\sigma\right)\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\eta_{\beta\gamma}
\left(\frac{\partial V_\alpha}{\partial x^\eta}-\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\eta}V_\sigma\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.23}
$$

This computes the change along $x^\gamma$ of the covariant change along $x^\beta$. Repeat from the same starting point with the directions exchanged. First compute

$$
V_{\alpha;\gamma}=\frac{\partial V_\alpha}{\partial x^\gamma}-\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}V_\sigma.
\tag{6.24}
$$

Write $V_{\alpha\gamma}=V_{\alpha;\gamma}$, then differentiate with respect to $x^\beta$.

$$
V_{\alpha\gamma;\beta}
=\frac{\partial V_{\alpha\gamma}}{\partial x^\beta}
-\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\beta}V_{\tau\gamma}
-\Gamma^\eta_{\gamma\beta}V_{\alpha\eta}.
\tag{6.25}
$$

Substitute Equation (6.24) and expand.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{\alpha\gamma;\beta}
&=\frac{\partial^2 V_\alpha}{\partial x^\beta\,\partial x^\gamma}
-\frac{\partial\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}}{\partial x^\beta}V_\sigma
-\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial V_\sigma}{\partial x^\beta}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\beta}
\left(\frac{\partial V_\tau}{\partial x^\gamma}-\Gamma^\sigma_{\tau\gamma}V_\sigma\right)\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\eta_{\gamma\beta}
\left(\frac{\partial V_\alpha}{\partial x^\eta}-\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\eta}V_\sigma\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.26}
$$

Subtract Equation (6.26) from Equation (6.23). For a smooth covector the ordinary mixed partials are equal.

$$
\frac{\partial^2 V_\alpha}{\partial x^\gamma\,\partial x^\beta}
=\frac{\partial^2 V_\alpha}{\partial x^\beta\,\partial x^\gamma}.
$$

The terms involving derivatives of Christoffel symbols may differ. Two pairs of terms involving first derivatives of the covector cancel after renaming the summed index.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial V_\sigma}{\partial x^\gamma}
&=-\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial V_\tau}{\partial x^\gamma},\\
-\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial V_\tau}{\partial x^\beta}
&=-\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}\frac{\partial V_\sigma}{\partial x^\beta}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The products $\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\gamma}\Gamma^\sigma_{\tau\beta}V_\sigma$ and $\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\beta}\Gamma^\sigma_{\tau\gamma}V_\sigma$ may differ. The other pair of first-derivative terms cancels by symmetry of the lower indices in the Christoffel symbols.

$$
-\Gamma^\eta_{\beta\gamma}\frac{\partial V_\alpha}{\partial x^\eta}
=-\Gamma^\eta_{\gamma\beta}\frac{\partial V_\alpha}{\partial x^\eta}.
$$

The same lower-index symmetry cancels the last pair of products.

$$
\Gamma^\eta_{\beta\gamma}\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\eta}V_\sigma
=\Gamma^\eta_{\gamma\beta}\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\eta}V_\sigma.
$$

Factor $V_\sigma$ from the four surviving terms.

$$
V_{\alpha\beta;\gamma}-V_{\alpha\gamma;\beta}
=\left(
\frac{\partial\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}}{\partial x^\beta}
-\frac{\partial\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^\gamma}
+\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\gamma}\Gamma^\sigma_{\tau\beta}
-\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\beta}\Gamma^\sigma_{\tau\gamma}
\right)V_\sigma.
\tag{6.27}
$$

The factor in parentheses defines the Riemann curvature tensor in this sign convention.

$$
R^\sigma{}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
=\frac{\partial\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\gamma}}{\partial x^\beta}
-\frac{\partial\Gamma^\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^\gamma}
+\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\gamma}\Gamma^\sigma_{\tau\beta}
-\Gamma^\tau_{\alpha\beta}\Gamma^\sigma_{\tau\gamma}.
\tag{6.28}
$$

At a chosen point, you can choose normal coordinates in which every Christoffel symbol vanishes there. Their derivatives can still contribute to the curvature at that point. A region is locally flat where all curvature components vanish throughout the region.

$$
R^\sigma{}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=0.
\tag{6.29}
$$

Contract the first and third indices to obtain the **Ricci tensor**. With four coordinates numbered from 1 to 4, this is

$$
R_{\alpha\gamma}=R^\sigma{}_{\alpha\sigma\gamma}
=R^1{}_{\alpha1\gamma}+R^2{}_{\alpha2\gamma}
+R^3{}_{\alpha3\gamma}+R^4{}_{\alpha4\gamma}.
\tag{6.30}
$$

Raise one index of the Ricci tensor and take the trace to obtain the **Ricci scalar**.

$$
R=g^{\alpha\gamma}R_{\alpha\gamma}=R^\gamma{}_\gamma
=R^1{}_1+R^2{}_2+R^3{}_3+R^4{}_4.
\tag{6.31}
$$

The **Einstein tensor** combines the Ricci tensor, Ricci scalar, and metric.

$$
G_{\alpha\gamma}=R_{\alpha\gamma}-\tfrac12R\,g_{\alpha\gamma}.
\tag{6.32}
$$

Einstein’s field equation is commonly written

$$
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
=\frac{8\pi\mechanical{G}}{c^4}\energy{T}_{\mu\nu}.
\tag{6.33}
$$

Here $\energy{T}_{\mu\nu}$ is the stress–energy tensor, $\mechanical{G}$ is Newton’s gravitational constant, and $\Lambda$ is the cosmological constant, which Einstein introduced in a model of a static universe. The field equation relates the distribution of energy and momentum to the curvature of spacetime. In turn, spacetime curvature governs free motion.

For an explicit curvature calculation, consider the two-dimensional surface of a sphere of radius $\mechanical{a}$. The line element is

$$
ds^2=\mechanical{a}^2d\theta^2+\mechanical{a}^2\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2.
$$

Read off the metric components.

$$
g_{\theta\theta}=\mechanical{a}^2,\qquad
g_{\theta\phi}=g_{\phi\theta}=0,\qquad
g_{\phi\phi}=\mechanical{a}^2\sin^2\theta.
\tag{6.34}
$$

Use these in the formula for the Christoffel symbols.

$$
\Gamma^\ell_{ij}=\frac12g^{k\ell}
\left(\frac{\partial g_{ik}}{\partial x^j}
+\frac{\partial g_{jk}}{\partial x^i}
-\frac{\partial g_{ij}}{\partial x^k}\right).
$$

For each $\ell\in\{\theta,\phi\}$, expand the sum over $k$ in the four lower-index combinations.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\Gamma^\ell_{\theta\theta}
&=g^{\theta\ell}\partial_\theta g_{\theta\theta}
+g^{\phi\ell}(2\partial_\theta g_{\theta\phi}-\partial_\phi g_{\theta\theta}),\\
2\Gamma^\ell_{\theta\phi}
&=g^{\theta\ell}\partial_\phi g_{\theta\theta}
+g^{\phi\ell}\partial_\theta g_{\phi\phi},\\
2\Gamma^\ell_{\phi\theta}
&=g^{\theta\ell}\partial_\phi g_{\theta\theta}
+g^{\phi\ell}\partial_\theta g_{\phi\phi},\\
2\Gamma^\ell_{\phi\phi}
&=g^{\theta\ell}(2\partial_\phi g_{\theta\phi}-\partial_\theta g_{\phi\phi})
+g^{\phi\ell}\partial_\phi g_{\phi\phi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

By Equation (6.34), the off-diagonal components of the metric and its inverse vanish. The only nonzero derivative of a metric component is $\partial_\theta g_{\phi\phi}$. This leaves three potentially nonzero Christoffel symbols.

Away from the coordinate singularities at the poles, the three terms are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}
&=\tfrac12g^{\phi\phi}\partial_\theta g_{\phi\phi}
=\frac{2\mechanical{a}^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{2\mechanical{a}^2\sin^2\theta}
=\cot\theta,\\
\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}&=\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}=\cot\theta,\\
\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}
&=-\tfrac12g^{\theta\theta}\partial_\theta g_{\phi\phi}
=-\sin\theta\cos\theta.
\end{aligned}
$$

To evaluate Equation (6.28), let each of the four free indices and the summed index $\tau$ range over $\theta,\phi$. The two products in Equation (6.28) become four terms after expanding the sum. For example, the first two components with upper index $\theta$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\theta}
&=\partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}
-\partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}
+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}
-\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}
-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta},\\
R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\phi}
&=\partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}
-\partial_\phi\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}
+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}
-\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}
-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both components are zero.

The remaining six expansions with upper index $\theta$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^\theta{}_{\theta\phi\theta}&=\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}-\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta},\\[4pt]
R^\theta{}_{\phi\theta\theta}&=\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}-\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta},\\[4pt]
R^\theta{}_{\theta\phi\phi}&=\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}-\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi},\\[4pt]
R^\theta{}_{\phi\theta\phi}&=\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}-\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi},\\[4pt]
R^\theta{}_{\phi\phi\theta}&=\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}-\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta},\\[4pt]
R^\theta{}_{\phi\phi\phi}&=\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}-\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $\sigma=\theta$, the components $R^\theta{}_{\phi\theta\theta}$, $R^\theta{}_{\theta\phi\phi}$, and $R^\theta{}_{\phi\phi\phi}$ vanish because their last two indices are equal. Likewise $R^\theta{}_{\theta\phi\theta}=-R^\theta{}_{\theta\theta\phi}=0$ by antisymmetry. Substitution of the Christoffel symbols leaves

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^\theta{}_{\phi\theta\phi}
&=\partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}
-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi},\\
R^\theta{}_{\phi\phi\theta}
&=-\partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}
+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The derivative and product in these components are

$$
\partial_\theta\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}
=\sin^2\theta-\cos^2\theta,
\qquad
\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}
=-\cos^2\theta.
$$

Substitution gives

$$
R^\theta{}_{\phi\theta\phi}=\sin^2\theta,
\qquad
R^\theta{}_{\phi\phi\theta}=-\sin^2\theta.
$$

For $\sigma=\phi$, the eight component expressions before substituting the Christoffel symbols are

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^\phi{}_{\theta\theta\theta}&=\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}-\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta},\\[4pt]
R^\phi{}_{\theta\theta\phi}&=\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}-\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi},\\[4pt]
R^\phi{}_{\theta\phi\theta}&=\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}-\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta},\\[4pt]
R^\phi{}_{\phi\theta\theta}&=\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}-\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta},\\[4pt]
R^\phi{}_{\theta\phi\phi}&=\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}-\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi},\\[4pt]
R^\phi{}_{\phi\theta\phi}&=\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}-\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi},\\[4pt]
R^\phi{}_{\phi\phi\theta}&=\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}-\partial_{\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\theta}-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta},\\[4pt]
R^\phi{}_{\phi\phi\phi}&=\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}-\partial_{\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}
+\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}+\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\\
&\quad-\Gamma^\theta_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}-\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\phi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For upper index $\phi$, the only nonzero components are

$$
\begin{aligned}
R^\phi{}_{\theta\theta\phi}
&=\partial_\theta\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}
+\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta},\\
R^\phi{}_{\theta\phi\theta}
&=-\partial_\theta\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}
-\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\partial_\theta\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}=-(1+\cot^2\theta)$ and $\Gamma^\phi_{\theta\phi}\Gamma^\phi_{\phi\theta}=\cot^2\theta$, the results are

$$
R^\phi{}_{\theta\theta\phi}=-1,\qquad R^\phi{}_{\theta\phi\theta}=1.
$$

These components are nonzero, so the sphere has intrinsic curvature. Use the results to compute the Ricci tensor and scalar in [[6.4 Chapter 6 problems]].
