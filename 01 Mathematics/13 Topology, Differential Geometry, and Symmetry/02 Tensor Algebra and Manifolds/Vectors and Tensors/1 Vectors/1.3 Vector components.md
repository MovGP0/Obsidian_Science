---
title: "1.3 Vector components"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Use unit vectors and their components to reconstruct a vector.

Vector components give the parts of a vector along the directions specified by its [[1.2 Cartesian unit vectors|unit vectors]].

Fig. 1.5(a) shows a bound vector $\vec A$ from the origin to $(0,3,3)$. The vector lies in the $yz$-plane. To reach its endpoint along directions parallel to the axes, start with three unit steps in the positive $y$-direction. Then turn $90^\circ$ and take three unit steps in the positive $z$-direction, as in Fig. 1.5(b).

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-1-5.png|**Fig. 1.5** Vector A and its Cartesian components.]]

The $y$-component $A_y$ is $3$ and the $z$-component $A_z$ is also $3$. There is no step along $x$, so $A_x=0$.

$$
\vec A=A_x\hat i+A_y\hat j+A_z\hat k. \tag{1.1}
$$

The signed coefficients $A_x$, $A_y$, and $A_z$ give the steps along $\hat i$, $\hat j$, and $\hat k$ from the tail to the head of the vector.[^6]

In Cartesian coordinates and other orthogonal systems, the projections onto the coordinate axes give the corresponding components. The method of projection matters when the axes are [[4.4 Non-orthogonal coordinate systems|non-orthogonal]].

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-1-6.png|**Fig. 1.6** Vector components as projections onto the x- and y-axes.]]

Fig. 1.6(a) shows light traveling in the negative $y$-direction. The shadow of $\vec A$ on the $x$-axis is its projection perpendicular to that axis. In Fig. 1.6(b), light travels in the negative $x$-direction and projects $\vec A$ perpendicularly onto the $y$-axis.

In a non-orthogonal coordinate system, a projection parallel to one axis can differ from a projection perpendicular to another axis. This distinction affects the components measured in different reference frames. See [[4 Covariant and contravariant vector components]], [[5 Higher-rank tensors]], and [[6 Tensor applications]].

In Fig. 1.6(a), the angle from the positive $x$-axis to $\vec A$ is $\theta$. The vector magnitude $|\vec A|$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle. Its sides along the $x$- and $y$-axes give the components.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_x &= |\vec A|\cos\theta,\\
A_y &= |\vec A|\sin\theta.
\end{aligned} \tag{1.2}
$$

The bars in $|\vec A|$ indicate the magnitude of the vector. Measure $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis toward the positive $y$-axis. Equation (1.2) then gives the correct signs in all four quadrants.

For a displacement of $\quantity{7}{m}$ at $210^\circ$ counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{A}_x &= \mechanical{|\vec A|}\cos\theta
= \quantity{7}{m}\cos(210^\circ)\approx\quantity{-6.1}{m},\\
\mechanical{A}_y &= \mechanical{|\vec A|}\sin\theta
= \quantity{7}{m}\sin(210^\circ)=\quantity{-3.5}{m}.
\end{aligned} \tag{1.3}
$$

Both components are negative because the vector points down and to the left.

Given the Cartesian components of a two-dimensional vector, apply the Pythagorean theorem to find its magnitude.

$$
|\vec A|=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2}. \tag{1.4}
$$

The component ratio gives the reference for the direction angle.

$$
\theta=\arctan\left(\frac{A_y}{A_x}\right). \tag{1.5}
$$

Measure the direction counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis in the right-handed coordinate system. If $A_x<0$, add $180^\circ$ to the principal value of the arctangent. For the displacement in Equation (1.3), this changes $30^\circ$ to $210^\circ$. A four-quadrant arctangent, $\operatorname{atan2}(A_y,A_x)$, also gives the direction when $A_x=0$ and the vector is nonzero. If necessary, add $360^\circ$ to express the result in $[0^\circ,360^\circ)$.

See [[1.4 Vector addition and multiplication by a scalar]] for the operations that combine components into a vector.

[^6]: Some authors use *components* for the scalars $A_x$, $A_y$, and $A_z$. Others use it for the vectors $A_x\hat i$, $A_y\hat j$, and $A_z\hat k$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec A}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement | The displacement in the numerical example. |
| $\mechanical{\lvert\vec A\rvert}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement magnitude | The length of the displacement vector. |
| $\mechanical{A}_x$ | $\unit{m}$ | x-component of displacement | The signed displacement along the x-axis. |
| $\mechanical{A}_y$ | $\unit{m}$ | y-component of displacement | The signed displacement along the y-axis. |
| $\theta$ | $\unit{{}^\circ}$ | Direction angle | The angle counterclockwise from the positive x-axis. |
