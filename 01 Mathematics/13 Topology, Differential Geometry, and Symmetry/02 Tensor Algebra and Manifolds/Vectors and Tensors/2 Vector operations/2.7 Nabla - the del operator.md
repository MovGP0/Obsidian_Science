---
title: "2.7 Nabla – the del operator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The del operator combines coordinate derivatives to describe how scalar and vector fields vary in space.

The symbol $\nabla$ denotes the vector differential operator *nabla*, also called *del*. The way it is combined with a field determines the operation. Write $\nabla\psi$ for the [[2.8 Gradient|gradient]] of a scalar field, $\nabla\cdot\vec A$ for [[2.9 Divergence|divergence]], $\nabla\times\vec A$ for [[2.10 Curl|curl]], and $\nabla^2$ for the [[2.11 Laplacian|Laplacian]].

In Cartesian coordinates,

$$
\nabla=\hat i\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\hat j\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\hat k\frac{\partial}{\partial z}, \tag{2.29}
$$

where $\hat i$, $\hat j$, and $\hat k$ are unit vectors in the positive $x$-, $y$-, and $z$-directions. Each partial derivative acts on the field that follows it.

A scalar field assigns a scalar value to each location. Examples are air temperature inside a room and terrain height above sea level. A vector field assigns a vector to each location. Electric, magnetic, and gravitational fields are examples.
