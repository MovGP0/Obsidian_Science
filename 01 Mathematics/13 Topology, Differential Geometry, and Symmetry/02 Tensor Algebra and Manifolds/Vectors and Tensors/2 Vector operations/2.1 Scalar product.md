---
title: "2.1 Scalar product"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The scalar product gives a signed projection times the magnitude of the other vector.

The *scalar product*, also called the *dot product*, is useful for finding the projection of one vector onto another. For example, the work done by a constant force is the component of the force along the displacement multiplied by the length of the displacement. When the force and displacement point in the same direction, this reduces to force times distance.

For vectors $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ with known Cartesian components,

$$
\vec A\cdot\vec B=A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z. \tag{2.1}
$$

If you know the angle $\theta$ between the vectors, use their magnitudes $|\vec A|$ and $|\vec B|$.[^1]

$$
\vec A\cdot\vec B=|\vec A|\,|\vec B|\cos\theta. \tag{2.2}
$$

The result is a scalar. As shown in Fig. 2.1, the signed projection of $\vec A$ along $\vec B$ is $|\vec A|\cos\theta$. Multiplying by $|\vec B|$ gives the dot product. Projecting $\vec B$ along $\vec A$ and multiplying by $|\vec A|$ gives the same result, so $\vec A\cdot\vec B=\vec B\cdot\vec A$.

The dot product of a vector with a unit vector gives its signed component in the direction of that unit vector. For example, $\vec A\cdot\hat k$ gives the $z$-component of $\vec A$. If $\hat n$ is a unit normal to a surface, $\vec A\cdot\hat n$ gives the component perpendicular to the surface.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-2-1.png|**Fig. 2.1** Two vectors (a) and their scalar product as a projection times a magnitude (b).]]

To find the angle between two nonzero vectors, equate the two expressions for their scalar product.

$$
\vec A\cdot\vec B=|\vec A|\,|\vec B|\cos\theta=A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z. \tag{2.3}
$$

Divide by the product of the magnitudes.

$$
\cos\theta=\frac{A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z}{|\vec A|\,|\vec B|}.
$$

Then take the inverse cosine.

$$
\theta=\arccos\left(\frac{A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z}{|\vec A|\,|\vec B|}\right). \tag{2.4}
$$

For example, apply Equation (2.4) to $\vec A=5\hat i-2\hat j+4\hat k$ and $\vec B=3\hat i+\hat j+7\hat k$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta &=
\arccos\left(
\frac{5(3)+(-2)(1)+4(7)}
{\sqrt{5^2+(-2)^2+4^2}\sqrt{3^2+1^2+7^2}}
\right)\\
&=\arccos\left(\frac{41}{\sqrt{45}\sqrt{59}}\right)
\approx37.3^\circ.
\end{aligned}
$$

The dot product of a unit vector with itself is one. For example, $\hat i\cdot\hat i=(1)(1)\cos 0^\circ=1$. The dot product of two perpendicular unit vectors is zero. For example, $\hat i\cdot\hat j=(1)(1)\cos 90^\circ=0$.

[^1]: Problem 2.3 asks you to show that the component and geometric forms of the dot product are equivalent.
