---
title: "2.3 Triple scalar product"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The scalar triple product gives the signed volume spanned by three vectors.

The *triple scalar product*, also called the *scalar triple product*, combines a [[2.1 Scalar product|dot product]] with a [[2.2 Cross product|cross product]]. It has the form $\vec A\cdot(\vec B\times\vec C)$.[^4] First find the cross product.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec B\times\vec C
&=(B_yC_z-B_zC_y)\hat i\\
&\quad+(B_zC_x-B_xC_z)\hat j\\
&\quad+(B_xC_y-B_yC_x)\hat k.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.10}
$$

Use Equation (2.1) to take the dot product of $\vec A$ with the result.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\cdot(\vec B\times\vec C)
&=A_x(B_yC_z-B_zC_y)\\
&\quad+A_y(B_zC_x-B_xC_z)\\
&\quad+A_z(B_xC_y-B_yC_x).
\end{aligned} \tag{2.11}
$$

This is the determinant with the components of the three vectors as rows.

$$
\vec A\cdot(\vec B\times\vec C)=
\begin{vmatrix}
A_x & A_y & A_z\\
B_x & B_y & B_z\\
C_x & C_y & C_z
\end{vmatrix}. \tag{2.12}
$$

In Fig. 2.4, the parallelepiped with edges $\vec A$, $\vec B$, and $\vec C$ has base area $|\vec B\times\vec C|$. If $\phi$ is the angle between $\vec A$ and $\vec B\times\vec C$, the signed height is $|\vec A|\cos\phi$. Multiply the base area by the signed height to get $\vec A\cdot(\vec B\times\vec C)$. The magnitude of this scalar is the volume.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-2-4.png|**Fig. 2.4** The scalar triple product gives the signed volume of a parallelepiped.]]

The triple scalar product is positive when the ordered vectors $\vec A$, $\vec B$, and $\vec C$ form a right-handed set, and negative for a left-handed set.

If the three vectors are coplanar, the parallelepiped has zero height and the triple product is zero. Conversely, if the triple product is zero, the three vectors are coplanar.

$$
\vec A\cdot(\vec B\times\vec C)=0. \tag{2.13}
$$

A cyclic permutation preserves the signed volume.

$$
\vec A\cdot(\vec B\times\vec C)
=\vec B\cdot(\vec C\times\vec A)
=\vec C\cdot(\vec A\times\vec B)
=(\vec A\times\vec B)\cdot\vec C.
$$

One application is the calculation of reciprocal vectors in [[4.5 Dual basis vectors]].

[^4]: The grouping $(\vec A\cdot\vec B)\times\vec C$ is undefined because the dot product in parentheses gives a scalar.
