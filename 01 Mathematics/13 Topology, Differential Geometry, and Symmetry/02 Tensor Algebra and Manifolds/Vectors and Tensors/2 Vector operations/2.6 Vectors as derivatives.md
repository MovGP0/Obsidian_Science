---
title: "2.6 Vectors as derivatives"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The tangent to a path gives a directional derivative and can be expressed in a basis of partial derivative operators.

A vector can act as a directional derivative. In this representation, partial derivative operators such as $\partial/\partial x$ and $\partial/\partial y$ are basis vectors along the coordinate directions.

For example, consider motion along a path in the $xy$-plane with velocity $\mechanical{\vec v}$, as in Fig. 2.10. Assign a time $t$ to each point as the object moves. This *parameterizes* the curve by time.[^6] Equal time steps can cover unequal distances if the speed changes.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-2-10.png|**Fig. 2.10** A parameterized curve and tangent vectors at successive points.]]

Suppose the air temperature varies across the region. As you move along the path, you experience this spatial variation as a change in time. The rate depends on the direction of motion and on your speed.

If $\thermal{f}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})$ gives the temperature at each location, the [[2.5 Partial derivatives|chain rule]] gives the rate of change along the path.

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\thermal{f}}{\mathrm{d}t}
=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mechanical{x}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\partial\thermal{f}}{\partial\mechanical{x}}
+\frac{\mathrm{d}\mechanical{y}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\partial\thermal{f}}{\partial\mechanical{y}}. \tag{2.27}
$$

The coefficients $\mathrm{d}\mechanical{x}/\mathrm{d}t=\mechanical{v}_x$ and $\mathrm{d}\mechanical{y}/\mathrm{d}t=\mechanical{v}_y$ are the components of the velocity tangent to the path. The derivative of the temperature is the scalar rate measured along that tangent.

The relation applies to any differentiable function on the plane. Write it as an operator equation.

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}
=\frac{\mathrm{d}\mechanical{x}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{x}}
+\frac{\mathrm{d}\mechanical{y}}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{y}}. \tag{2.28}
$$

Each term is a velocity component multiplied by the corresponding coordinate basis operator.

In Equation (2.28), the operator $\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t$ represents the tangent vector to the curve. Its components are the rates of change of the coordinates, and its basis vectors are the partial derivative operators along those coordinates.

The function can represent any differentiable scalar field, such as the height of the road. You can also parameterize the curve by another parameter, such as $s$ or $\lambda$. The operator $\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}s$ or $\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}\lambda$ gives the tangent vector for that parameter, with the corresponding coordinate derivatives as components.

The connection between coordinate basis vectors and partial derivatives is useful in tensor calculations. See [[4.2 Basis-vector transformations]].

[^6]: Some authors reserve *curve* for a path whose points have parameter values.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The velocity along the path. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The parameter of the path. |
| $\mechanical{v}_x$, $\mechanical{v}_y$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity components | The rates of change of the two position coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{x}$, $\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinates | The coordinates of the moving point. |
| $\thermal{f}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The air temperature at a position. |
