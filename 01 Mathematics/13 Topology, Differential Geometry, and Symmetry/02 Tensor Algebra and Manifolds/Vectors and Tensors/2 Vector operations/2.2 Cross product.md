---
title: "2.2 Cross product"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The cross product gives a vector perpendicular to its two inputs, with direction set by the right-hand rule.

The cross product of two vectors is a vector. Use it to calculate effects such as the torque from a force on a lever or the force on a moving charge in a magnetic field.

For two vectors in a right-handed Cartesian basis,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\times\vec B
&=(A_y B_z-A_z B_y)\hat i\\
&\quad+(A_zB_x-A_xB_z)\hat j\\
&\quad+(A_xB_y-A_yB_x)\hat k.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.5}
$$

You can write the same calculation as a determinant expanded along the first row.

$$
\vec A\times\vec B=
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat i & \hat j & \hat k\\
A_x & A_y & A_z\\
B_x & B_y & B_z
\end{vmatrix}. \tag{2.6}
$$

For nonparallel vectors, the result is perpendicular to the plane of $\vec A$ and $\vec B$, as in Fig. 2.2. The right-hand rule selects its direction. Open your right hand, with your thumb perpendicular to your fingers in the plane of your palm.

Use your palm and fingers to turn $\vec A$ toward $\vec B$ through the smaller angle between them. Your thumb points in the direction of $\vec A\times\vec B$.[^2]

Reversing the order turns the thumb in the opposite direction and preserves the magnitude.

$$
\vec A\times\vec B=-\vec B\times\vec A. \tag{2.7}
$$

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-2-2.png|**Fig. 2.2** Direction of the cross product from the right-hand rule.]]

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-2-3.png|**Fig. 2.3** The magnitude of the cross product is the area of the parallelogram spanned by the two vectors.]]

The magnitude of the cross product is

$$
|\vec A\times\vec B|=|\vec A|\,|\vec B|\sin\theta, \tag{2.8}
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the vectors, taken in $[0,\pi]$.[^3] In Fig. 2.3, the parallelogram spanned by $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ has base $|\vec A|$ and height $|\vec B|\sin\theta$. Its area equals the magnitude of the cross product.

For parallel or antiparallel vectors, $\theta=0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$, and the cross product is zero. Its magnitude reaches $|\vec A|\,|\vec B|$ when the vectors are perpendicular.

The Cartesian unit vectors satisfy

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat i\times\hat i &= \vec 0, & \hat i\times\hat j &= \hat k, & \hat j\times\hat i &= -\hat k,\\
\hat j\times\hat j &= \vec 0, & \hat j\times\hat k &= \hat i, & \hat k\times\hat j &= -\hat i,\\
\hat k\times\hat k &= \vec 0, & \hat k\times\hat i &= \hat j, & \hat i\times\hat k &= -\hat j.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.9}
$$

Apply these identities to each pair of terms in the Cartesian expansions of $\vec A$ and $\vec B$ to obtain Equations (2.5) and (2.6).

Applications include torque $\mechanical{\vec\tau}=\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec F}$ and magnetic force $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm B}=\electric{q}\mechanical{\vec v}\times\magnetic{\vec B}$. See the [[2.12 Chapter 2 problems|Chapter 2 problems]].

[^2]: Another version of the rule uses the right index finger for the first vector and the middle finger for the second. The thumb gives the direction of the product.
[^3]: Problem 2.3 asks you to verify that Equation (2.8) gives the magnitude of Equation (2.5).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec\tau}$ | $\unit{N\,m}$ | Torque | The moment of the applied force about the reference point. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The displacement from the reference point to the point of application. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | The applied force. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Magnetic force | The force on a moving charge from a magnetic field. |
| $\electric{q}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Electric charge | The charge of the moving particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The particle's velocity. |
| $\magnetic{\vec B}$ | $\unit{T}$ | Magnetic flux density | The magnetic field in the force expression. |
