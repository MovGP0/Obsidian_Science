---
title: "2.8 Gradient"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The gradient points in the direction of fastest increase of a scalar field and gives the rate of increase.

Apply $\nabla$ to a scalar field to get its *gradient*. The gradient is a vector. Its direction points where the field increases fastest with distance, and its magnitude gives that rate of increase. For terrain height, the vector points uphill along the steepest slope.

For a scalar field $\psi$ in Cartesian coordinates,

$$
\operatorname{grad}\psi
=\nabla\psi
=\hat i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}
+\hat j\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}
+\hat k\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z}. \tag{2.30}
$$

Each Cartesian component of $\nabla\psi$ gives the slope along its corresponding coordinate axis. The magnitude $\sqrt{(\partial_x\psi)^2+(\partial_y\psi)^2+(\partial_z\psi)^2}$ gives the steepest slope at that point.

For the plane $\psi(x,y)=5x+2y$, apply Equation (2.30) in two dimensions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla\psi
&=\hat i\frac{\partial(5x+2y)}{\partial x}
  +\hat j\frac{\partial(5x+2y)}{\partial y}\\
&=5\hat i+2\hat j.
\end{aligned}
$$

The slope along $x$ is more than twice the slope along $y$. Both slopes are constant throughout the plane.

**Fig. 2.11(b)** Top view of the contours and gradient of $\psi=5x+2y$.

The gradient has magnitude $\sqrt{5^2+2^2}\approx5.39$ and points at $\arctan(2/5)\approx21.8^\circ$ from the positive $x$-axis.

In cylindrical coordinates, the gradient is

$$
\nabla\psi
=\hat r\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}
+\hat\phi\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\phi}
+\hat z\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial z}. \tag{2.31}
$$

In spherical coordinates,

$$
\nabla\psi
=\hat r\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}
+\hat\theta\frac1r\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\theta}
+\hat\phi\frac1{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\phi}. \tag{2.32}
$$

The [[2.11 Laplacian|Laplacian]] of a scalar field is the divergence of its gradient.
