title: "2.10 Curl"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Curl measures local circulation and points along the axis of that circulation by the right-hand rule.

The operation $\nabla\times\vec A$ is the *curl* of the vector field $\vec A$. Its magnitude describes the local circulation, and its direction is normal to the plane of maximum circulation.

In the fields in Fig. 2.14, compare vectors on opposite sides of a point. A change in magnitude or direction can make an imaginary small paddlewheel rotate. When it rotates, the curl at its center is nonzero and points along its axis. Curl the fingers of your right hand in the direction of rotation. Your thumb points in the direction of the curl.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-2-14.png|**Fig. 2.14** Vector fields with circulation (a), shear (b), and radial flow (c).]]

The paddlewheel would rotate strongly at points 1, 2, 3, and 5. It would also rotate at point 4. Uniform flow at point 6 and radial flow at point 7 have little or no local circulation.

In Cartesian coordinates,

$$
\nabla\times\vec A
=\left(\hat i\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
+\hat j\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
+\hat k\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)
\times(A_x\hat i+A_y\hat j+A_z\hat k). \tag{2.38}
$$

Write the cross product as a determinant.

$$
\nabla\times\vec A=
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat i & \hat j & \hat k\\
\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\\
A_x & A_y & A_z
\end{vmatrix}. \tag{2.39}
$$

Expanding Equation (2.39) gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla\times\vec A
&=\left(\frac{\partial A_z}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial A_y}{\partial z}\right)\hat i\\
&\quad+\left(\frac{\partial A_x}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial A_z}{\partial x}\right)\hat j\\
&\quad+\left(\frac{\partial A_y}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial A_x}{\partial y}\right)\hat k.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.40}
$$

Each component measures circulation in the corresponding perpendicular plane. For example, the $x$-component measures circulation in the $yz$-plane.

In Fig. 2.15(a), $A_z$ increases as $y$ increases, so $\partial A_z/\partial y>0$. Meanwhile $A_y$ decreases as $z$ increases, so $\partial A_y/\partial z<0$. Subtracting the negative derivative makes the $x$-component of the curl larger.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-2-15.png|**Fig. 2.15** Contributions of the two partial derivatives to the x-component of curl.]]

In Fig. 2.15(b), both derivatives are positive and their difference is small. A vector field with zero curl everywhere in its domain is *irrotational*.

### Cylindrical and spherical coordinates

In cylindrical coordinates,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla\times\vec A
&=\left(\frac1r\frac{\partial A_z}{\partial\phi}
-\frac{\partial A_\phi}{\partial z}\right)\hat r\\
&\quad+\left(\frac{\partial A_r}{\partial z}
-\frac{\partial A_z}{\partial r}\right)\hat\phi\\
&\quad+\frac1r\left(\frac{\partial(rA_\phi)}{\partial r}
-\frac{\partial A_r}{\partial\phi}\right)\hat z.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.41}
$$

In spherical coordinates,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla\times\vec A
&=\frac1{r\sin\theta}\left(
\frac{\partial(A_\phi\sin\theta)}{\partial\theta}
-\frac{\partial A_\theta}{\partial\phi}\right)\hat r\\
&\quad+\frac1r\left(
\frac1{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial A_r}{\partial\phi}
-\frac{\partial(rA_\phi)}{\partial r}\right)\hat\theta\\
&\quad+\frac1r\left(
\frac{\partial(rA_\theta)}{\partial r}
-\frac{\partial A_r}{\partial\theta}\right)\hat\phi.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.42}
$$

### An irrotational field with curved field lines

Consider $\vec A=(k/r)\hat\phi$, where $r$ is the cylindrical radius and $k$ is constant. In Equation (2.41), $A_r=A_z=0$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla\times\vec A
&=-\frac{\partial A_\phi}{\partial z}\hat r
+\frac1r\frac{\partial(rA_\phi)}{\partial r}\hat z\\
&=-\frac{\partial(k/r)}{\partial z}\hat r
+\frac1r\frac{\partial k}{\partial r}\hat z\\
&=\vec 0,\qquad r>0.
\end{aligned}
$$

In Fig. 2.16(a), the curvature of the flow tends to turn the paddlewheel clockwise. In Fig. 2.16(b), the flow weakens with distance from the center, so the bottom paddle receives a stronger push than the top paddle. This difference tends to turn the wheel counterclockwise. For the $1/r$ field, the two effects cancel everywhere away from the axis. The field is singular on the axis, where a distributional description uses a delta function.
