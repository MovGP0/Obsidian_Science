---
title: "2.4 Triple vector product"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The triple vector product is a linear combination of the vectors in its inner cross product.

The *triple vector product* $\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)$ applies to problems involving angular momentum and centripetal acceleration. It uses two cross products and gives a vector. The grouping matters because the cross product is generally nonassociative.

$$
\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)
=\vec B(\vec A\cdot\vec C)-\vec C(\vec A\cdot\vec B). \tag{2.14}
$$

The two dot products on the right are scalars. They multiply $\vec B$ and $\vec C$, so the result is a linear combination of those two vectors.

In Fig. 2.5, $\vec B\times\vec C$ is perpendicular to the plane of $\vec B$ and $\vec C$. Apply the right-hand rule to $\vec A$ and $\vec B\times\vec C$. Their cross product is perpendicular to $\vec B\times\vec C$, so it lies in the plane of $\vec B$ and $\vec C$.

The result is also perpendicular to $\vec A$. To remember Equation (2.14), use “BAC minus CAB” with the parentheses around the last two vectors of the original product.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-2-5.png|**Fig. 2.5** The triple vector product lies in the plane of B and C.]]

To derive Equation (2.14), use the Cartesian determinant for the outer cross product.

$$
\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)=
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat i & \hat j & \hat k\\
A_x & A_y & A_z\\
(\vec B\times\vec C)_x & (\vec B\times\vec C)_y & (\vec B\times\vec C)_z
\end{vmatrix}. \tag{2.15}
$$

The inner cross product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec B\times\vec C
&=(B_yC_z-B_zC_y)\hat i\\
&\quad+(B_zC_x-B_xC_z)\hat j\\
&\quad+(B_xC_y-B_yC_x)\hat k.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.16}
$$

Substitute Equation (2.16) into Equation (2.15).

$$
\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)=
\begin{vmatrix}
\hat i & \hat j & \hat k\\
A_x & A_y & A_z\\
B_yC_z-B_zC_y & B_zC_x-B_xC_z & B_xC_y-B_yC_x
\end{vmatrix}. \tag{2.17}
$$

Expand along the first row.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)
&=[A_y(B_xC_y-B_yC_x)-A_z(B_zC_x-B_xC_z)]\hat i\\
&\quad+[A_z(B_yC_z-B_zC_y)-A_x(B_xC_y-B_yC_x)]\hat j\\
&\quad+[A_x(B_zC_x-B_xC_z)-A_y(B_yC_z-B_zC_y)]\hat k.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.18}
$$

Collect the terms containing the corresponding components of $\vec B$ and $\vec C$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)
&=[(A_yC_y+A_zC_z)B_x-(A_yB_y+A_zB_z)C_x]\hat i\\
&\quad+[(A_zC_z+A_xC_x)B_y-(A_zB_z+A_xB_x)C_y]\hat j\\
&\quad+[(A_xC_x+A_yC_y)B_z-(A_xB_x+A_yB_y)C_z]\hat k.
\end{aligned} \tag{2.19}
$$

For each Cartesian component in Equation (2.19), add and subtract the product of the three coefficients for that same direction. These additions are

$$
\begin{aligned}
(A_xB_xC_x-A_xB_xC_x)\hat i,\\
(A_yB_yC_y-A_yB_yC_y)\hat j,\\
(A_zB_zC_z-A_zB_zC_z)\hat k.
\end{aligned}
$$

After regrouping, all terms that multiply components of $\vec B$ have the same scalar coefficient. The same is true for the terms that multiply components of $\vec C$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)
&=(A_xC_x+A_yC_y+A_zC_z)(B_x\hat i+B_y\hat j+B_z\hat k)\\
&\quad-(A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z)(C_x\hat i+C_y\hat j+C_z\hat k).
\end{aligned}
$$

The first factor on each line is a dot product. The second factor is $\vec B$ or $\vec C$, respectively. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec A\times(\vec B\times\vec C)
&=(\vec A\cdot\vec C)\vec B-(\vec A\cdot\vec B)\vec C\\
&=\vec B(\vec A\cdot\vec C)-\vec C(\vec A\cdot\vec B).
\end{aligned}
$$
