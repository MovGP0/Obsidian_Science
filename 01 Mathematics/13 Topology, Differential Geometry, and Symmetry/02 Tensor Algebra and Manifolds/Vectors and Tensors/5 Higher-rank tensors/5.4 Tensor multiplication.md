---
title: "5.4 Tensor multiplication"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The tensor product combines all the indices of its factors. Contraction sums an upper index with a lower one.

### Outer product

The *outer product* pairs every component of one tensor with every component of the other tensor. In Cartesian components, write one vector as a column and the other as a row.

$$
\vec A\otimes\vec B
\ \longleftrightarrow\
\begin{pmatrix}A_1\\A_2\\A_3\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}B_1&B_2&B_3\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}
A_1B_1&A_1B_2&A_1B_3\\
A_2B_1&A_2B_2&A_2B_3\\
A_3B_1&A_3B_2&A_3B_3
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The result for two vectors is a rank-two tensor. Another notation is $C^{ij}=A^iB^j$, which omits the outer-product symbol.

For higher-rank tensors, preserve the upper and lower indices of both inputs. For example,

$$
C^{ik}{}_{jlm}=A^i{}_jB^k{}_{lm}.
$$

This product has rank five, with two contravariant and three covariant indices. The numbers of upper indices add, and the numbers of lower indices add.

Apply the individual transformation laws to the two factors.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{\prime n}{}_{o}
&=\frac{\partial x^{\prime n}}{\partial x^i}
  \frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime o}}A^i{}_j,\\
B^{\prime p}{}_{qr}
&=\frac{\partial x^{\prime p}}{\partial x^k}
  \frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x^{\prime q}}
  \frac{\partial x^m}{\partial x^{\prime r}}B^k{}_{lm}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiply the equations and collect the transformation factors. For $C^{ik}{}_{jlm}=A^i{}_jB^k{}_{lm}$,

$$
C^{\prime np}{}_{oqr}
=\frac{\partial x^{\prime n}}{\partial x^i}
 \frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime o}}
 \frac{\partial x^{\prime p}}{\partial x^k}
 \frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x^{\prime q}}
 \frac{\partial x^m}{\partial x^{\prime r}}C^{ik}{}_{jlm}. \tag{5.11}
$$

This is the transformation law of a tensor with two upper and three lower indices.

### Contraction and inner product

A tensor inner product generalizes the [[2.1 Scalar product|dot product]]. It uses *contraction*: give an upper index and a lower index the same label and sum over that label. This removes two slots. For example, in three dimensions, contract the second and third slots of $C^{ij}{}_{kl}$.

$$
C^{ij}{}_{jl}=C^{i1}{}_{1l}+C^{i2}{}_{2l}+C^{i3}{}_{3l}=D^i{}_l.
$$

A direct contraction pairs indices in opposite positions. To see why, contract the first upper index and the middle lower index in Equation (5.11) by setting $q=n$ and summing.

The chain rule combines the Jacobian factor for $n$ with its inverse.

$$
\frac{\partial x^{\prime n}}{\partial x^i}
\frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x^{\prime n}}
=\frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x^i}
=\delta^l{}_i.
$$

The *Kronecker delta* is

$$
\delta^i{}_j=
\begin{cases}
1,& i=j,\\
0,& i\ne j.
\end{cases}
$$

Therefore the contracted tensor transforms with three factors.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C^{\prime np}{}_{onr}
&=\delta^l{}_i
\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime o}}
\frac{\partial x^{\prime p}}{\partial x^k}
\frac{\partial x^m}{\partial x^{\prime r}} C^{ik}{}_{jlm}\\
&=\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime o}}
\frac{\partial x^{\prime p}}{\partial x^k}
\frac{\partial x^m}{\partial x^{\prime r}} C^{ik}{}_{jim}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The rank drops from five to three because the upper and lower transformation factors cancel. An inner product takes an outer product and then contracts. The outer product of a vector and a covector contracts to a scalar. In Euclidean space, the metric converts one of two vectors to a covector for this pairing, which yields the dot product.
