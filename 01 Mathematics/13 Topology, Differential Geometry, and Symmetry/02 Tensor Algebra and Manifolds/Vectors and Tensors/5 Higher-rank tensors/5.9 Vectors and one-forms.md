---
title: "5.9 Vectors and one-forms"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A one-form acts linearly on a vector to give a scalar. The metric pairs vectors with one-forms.

In many texts on tensors and general relativity, *covector* and *one-form* describe the objects whose components transform covariantly.

In modern terminology, vectors and one-forms are distinct kinds of objects. A vector has contravariant components in the coordinate basis. A one-form, or covector, has covariant components in the dual basis. Velocity is an example of a tangent vector. The differential of a scalar field is a one-form. In Cartesian coordinates, velocity components contain a length in the numerator, while the components of a scalar differential are expressed per unit length.

![[vectors-and-tensors-fig-5-3.png|**Fig. 5.3** Vectors are represented by arrows and one-forms by a family of surfaces.]]

An arrow's length represents vector magnitude. In the surface picture for a one-form, closer spacing represents greater magnitude. With a chosen orientation and unit spacing in the one-form's value, the signed number of surfaces crossed illustrates the pairing of a vector with a one-form.

For $\vec A=A^i\vec e_i$ and a one-form $\omega=\omega_i\,\mathrm dx^i$, their pairing is $\omega(\vec A)=\omega_iA^i$. It is a scalar and requires only the dual pairing. Every vector acts linearly on one-forms by the same pairing. More generally, a tensor takes vectors and one-forms as arguments and returns a scalar, linearly in each argument.

The metric acts bilinearly on two vectors to give their inner product. Its components are its values on pairs of basis vectors, $g_{ij}=g(\vec e_i,\vec e_j)$. The inverse metric acts on two one-forms, and its components are its values on pairs of dual basis one-forms.
