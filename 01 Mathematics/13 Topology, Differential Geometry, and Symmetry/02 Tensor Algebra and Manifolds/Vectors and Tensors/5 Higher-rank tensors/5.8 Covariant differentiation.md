---
title: "5.8 Covariant differentiation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The covariant derivative combines component derivatives with terms from the connection so that the result transforms as a tensor.

The covariant derivative accounts for changes in a tensor's components and in the basis. It gives another tensor and applies in Euclidean space and in curved spaces, including the spacetime of general relativity.

In Euclidean space, parallel transport preserves a vector's magnitude and direction between points. In Cartesian coordinates it keeps every component fixed. In curvilinear coordinates, the basis may differ between those points, so the components of a parallel-transported vector generally change.

For a sphere embedded in three-dimensional Euclidean space, a tangent vector belongs to the tangent plane at its point. Holding its direction fixed in three dimensions during a move can give a direction outside the new tangent plane. The intrinsic covariant derivative on the sphere is the tangential projection of the ambient derivative. See [[6.3 The Riemann curvature tensor]] for curvature and transport.

As an example, expand a vector as $\vec A=A^1\vec e_1+A^2\vec e_2+A^3\vec e_3$.

Apply the product rule and substitute [[5.7 Tensor derivatives and Christoffel symbols|Equation (5.22)]] for the basis derivative.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_j\vec A
&=\partial_j(A^i\vec e_i)\\
&=(\partial_j A^i)\vec e_i+A^i\partial_j\vec e_i\\
&=(\partial_j A^i)\vec e_i+A^i\Gamma^k{}_{ij}\vec e_k.
\end{aligned}
$$

Rename the dummy indices $i$ and $k$ in the second term to collect the coefficient of $\vec e_i$. The covariant derivative of the contravariant components is

$$
A^i{}_{;j}=\frac{\partial A^i}{\partial x^j}+A^k\Gamma^i{}_{kj}. \tag{5.24}
$$

The semicolon introduces the index for the direction of differentiation. For covariant components, subtract the connection term.

$$
A_{i;j}=\frac{\partial A_i}{\partial x^j}-A_k\Gamma^k{}_{ij}. \tag{5.25}
$$

As an example, set $j=2$ to differentiate with respect to $\phi$ in cylindrical coordinates $(r,\phi,z)$.

The nonzero Christoffel symbols for cylindrical coordinates give

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^r{}_{;\phi}&=\partial_\phi A^r+A^\phi\Gamma^r{}_{\phi\phi}
=\partial_\phi A^r-rA^\phi,\\
A^\phi{}_{;\phi}&=\partial_\phi A^\phi+A^r\Gamma^\phi{}_{r\phi}
=\partial_\phi A^\phi+\frac{A^r}{r},\\
A^z{}_{;\phi}&=\partial_\phi A^z.
\end{aligned}
$$

The connection terms arise because the basis rotates as $\phi$ changes. In particular,

$$
\nabla_{\partial_\phi}\vec A
=(\partial_\phi A^r-rA^\phi)\vec e_r
+\left(\partial_\phi A^\phi+\frac{A^r}{r}\right)\vec e_\phi
+(\partial_\phi A^z)\vec e_z.
$$

For a higher-rank tensor, add a connection term for each upper index and subtract one for each lower index.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{ij}{}_{;k}
&=\partial_k A^{ij}+A^{lj}\Gamma^i{}_{lk}+A^{il}\Gamma^j{}_{lk},\\
B_{ij;k}
&=\partial_k B_{ij}-B_{lj}\Gamma^l{}_{ik}-B_{il}\Gamma^l{}_{jk},\\
C^i{}_{j;k}
&=\partial_k C^i{}_j+C^l{}_j\Gamma^i{}_{lk}-C^i{}_l\Gamma^l{}_{jk}.
\end{aligned}
$$
