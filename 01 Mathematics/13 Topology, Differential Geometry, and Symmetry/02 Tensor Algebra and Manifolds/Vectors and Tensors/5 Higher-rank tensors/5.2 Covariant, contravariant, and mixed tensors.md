---
title: "5.2 Covariant, contravariant, and mixed tensors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Each upper index of a tensor transforms with the Jacobian, and each lower index transforms with the inverse Jacobian.

For a contravariant vector, a coordinate change acts on the single component index with one Jacobian factor.

$$
A^{\prime i}=\frac{\partial x^{\prime i}}{\partial x^j}A^j. \tag{5.1}
$$

The Jacobian entries are the components of the original basis vectors in the primed coordinates. A rank-two contravariant tensor uses a factor for each of its two indices.

$$
A^{\prime ij}
=\frac{\partial x^{\prime i}}{\partial x^k}
 \frac{\partial x^{\prime j}}{\partial x^l}A^{kl}. \tag{5.2}
$$

The indices $i,j$ label the new components and $k,l$ are summed over the ranges of the original indices.

For covariant components, use the inverse Jacobian.

$$
A'_i=\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime i}}A_j. \tag{5.3}
$$

For a fully covariant tensor of rank two, apply it to both indices.

$$
A'_{ij}
=\frac{\partial x^k}{\partial x^{\prime i}}
 \frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x^{\prime j}}A_{kl}. \tag{5.4}
$$

The inverse Jacobian is related to the reciprocal basis, whose directions in Euclidean space are normal to coordinate surfaces.

A mixed tensor has an upper and a lower index. Apply the Jacobian for the upper index and the inverse Jacobian for the lower one.

$$
A^{\prime i}{}_{j}
=\frac{\partial x^{\prime i}}{\partial x^k}
 \frac{\partial x^l}{\partial x^{\prime j}}A^k{}_l. \tag{5.5}
$$
