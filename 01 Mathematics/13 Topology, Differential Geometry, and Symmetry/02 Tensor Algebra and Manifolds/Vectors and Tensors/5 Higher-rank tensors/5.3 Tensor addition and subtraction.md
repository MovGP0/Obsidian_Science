---
title: "5.3 Tensor addition and subtraction"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Add or subtract tensors of the same type by combining corresponding components in a common basis.

[[1.4 Vector addition and multiplication by a scalar|Vector addition]] starts with

$$
\vec C=\vec A+\vec B. \tag{5.6}
$$

This is one equation for each component in the common basis. A vector in three dimensions gives three such equations.

$$
C_x=A_x+B_x,\qquad C_y=A_y+B_y,\qquad C_z=A_z+B_z. \tag{5.7}
$$

For higher-rank tensors, add components with matching indices and index positions. The inputs and the result have the same tensor type.

$$
C_{ij}=A_{ij}+B_{ij},\qquad
C^{ij}=A^{ij}+B^{ij},\qquad
C^i{}_j=A^i{}_j+B^i{}_j. \tag{5.8}
$$

Each expression gives an equation for every possible set of free indices. To verify that the sum is a tensor, apply the transformation rule for a mixed tensor to both terms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{\prime k}{}_{l}
&=\frac{\partial x^{\prime k}}{\partial x^i}\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime l}}A^i{}_j,\\
B^{\prime k}{}_{l}
&=\frac{\partial x^{\prime k}}{\partial x^i}\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime l}}B^i{}_j.
\end{aligned} \tag{5.9}
$$

The Jacobian factors are the same in both terms.

$$
A^{\prime k}{}_{l}+B^{\prime k}{}_{l}
=\frac{\partial x^{\prime k}}{\partial x^i}\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime l}}
 (A^i{}_j+B^i{}_j).
$$

This is the transformation law for $C^i{}_j=A^i{}_j+B^i{}_j$:

$$
C^{\prime k}{}_{l}
=\frac{\partial x^{\prime k}}{\partial x^i}\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime l}}C^i{}_j.
$$

The same argument applies to a difference.

$$
C_{ij}=A_{ij}-B_{ij},\qquad
C^{ij}=A^{ij}-B^{ij},\qquad
C^i{}_j=A^i{}_j-B^i{}_j. \tag{5.10}
$$

See the [[5.10 Chapter 5 problems|Chapter 5 problems]] for the subtraction proof.
