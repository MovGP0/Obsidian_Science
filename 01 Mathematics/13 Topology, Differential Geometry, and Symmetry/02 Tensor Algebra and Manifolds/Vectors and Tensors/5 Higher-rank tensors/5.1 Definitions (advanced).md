---
title: "5.1 Definitions (advanced)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A tensor is characterized by how its components transform when the coordinate system changes.

[[1.1 Definitions (basic)|Chapter 1]] gives a geometric introduction to scalars, vectors, and tensors.[^2] Their transformation properties give a more general description.

> [!definition]
> A scalar represents a single value that is invariant under a change of coordinates.

The numerical value can depend on the unit. For example, $\quantity{1}{in}$ and $\quantity{2.54}{cm}$ represent the same length.

> [!definition]
> A vector has components that combine with a basis to give the same geometric object in any coordinate system.

In three dimensions there are three components. When a metric identifies the dual basis with reciprocal vectors, you can use either expansion.

$$
\vec A=A^i\vec e_i=A_i\vec e^{\,i}.
$$

Here $\vec e_i$ is the original basis vector and $\vec e^{\,i}$ is the reciprocal basis vector.

Contravariant and covariant vector components obey the respective rules

$$
A^{\prime i}=\frac{\partial x^{\prime i}}{\partial x^j}A^j,
\qquad
A'_i=\frac{\partial x^j}{\partial x^{\prime i}}A_j.
$$

The first set of partial derivatives gives the original coordinate basis vectors in the new basis. The inverse partial derivatives give the transformation factors for reciprocal quantities.

> [!definition]
> A rank-$n$ tensor has components with $n$ indices. They transform so that their combination with the appropriate basis factors represents the same tensor in every coordinate system.

There are $3^n$ component entries in three dimensions. A rank-two tensor has nine entries, a rank-one tensor has one component index, and a scalar has none.

Authors use various symbols to denote tensors, including $\overset{\rightrightarrows}{T}$, $\tilde T$, $\overleftrightarrow T$, and $\underset{\leftrightarrow}{T}$. Placeholder indices such as $T^{ij}$ and $T^a{}_b$ also show the numbers of contravariant and covariant slots.

[^2]: Specifying one direction in three dimensions takes two angles.
