$$(i \hbar \gamma^\mu \partial_\mu - m c)\psi = 0$$
Where the wave function $\psi$ is a bi-spinor field:

$$\psi = \begin{bmatrix}
\psi_{1} \\
\psi_{2} \\
\psi_{3} \\
\psi_{4}
\end{bmatrix}$$
In the Dirac representation, the **upper (large) components** are
$$\psi_{e} = \begin{bmatrix}
\psi_{1} \\
\psi_{2}
\end{bmatrix} \in ℂ^2$$
and the **lower (small) components** are
$$\psi_{p} = \begin{bmatrix}
\psi_{3} \\
\psi_{4}
\end{bmatrix} \in ℂ^2$$
Only in the nonrelativistic limit do these two parts separate into the particle (electron) and antiparticle (positron) parts. In the Weyl representation, the two parts are the left-handed and right-handed components.

## Parts of the Dirac Equation

The **mass shell** is given by the **Lorentz Invariance**:
$$p^μ p_{μ} = (mc)^2$$
**Quantum energy-momentum operators**:
$$\hat{p}_μ = i\hbar \partial_μ$$
With the signature $(+,-,-,-)$, this operator changes $(\gamma^μ \hat{p}_μ - mc)\psi = 0$ into $(i\hbar\gamma^μ\partial_μ - mc)\psi = 0$.
First order in Space-Time

> [!Note]
> The Klein-Gordon equation follows from the mass shell without spinors, but it is second order in time and describes spin-0 particles. A first-order equation needs matrices with $\{\gamma^μ,\gamma^ν\} = 2\eta^{μν}$, which act on four-component spinors. Thus the Dirac equation describes spin-1/2 particles (fermions).
