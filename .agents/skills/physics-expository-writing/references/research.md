# Research basis for physics exposition

The sources below have been checked against publisher, journal, or established research records. They motivate individual features of this skill. **They do not establish a uniquely optimal whole-book or article structure.** Many studies evaluate active learning or classroom interventions rather than standalone written articles; do not silently generalize their effect sizes to textbook writing.

## Physics education research

1. **Etkina, E. (2015).** "Millikan award lecture: Students of physics—Listeners, observers, or collaborative participants in physics scientific practices?" *American Journal of Physics*, **83**(8), 669–679. DOI: [10.1119/1.4923432](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4923432). Discusses an investigative approach in which students observe phenomena, construct and test explanations, and engage in the scientific practices of physics. **Writing implication:** structure exposition around physical questions and cycles of modeling, prediction, and testing. **Limit:** a methodology for instruction does not, by itself, prove a universal optimum for written chapter order.

2. **Etkina, E., Van Heuvelen, A., White-Brahmia, S., Brookes, D. T., Gentile, M., Murthy, S., Rosengrant, D., & Warren, A. (2006).** "Scientific abilities and their assessment." *Physical Review Special Topics—Physics Education Research*, **2**, 020103. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.2.020103](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.2.020103). Presents scientific-reasoning tasks and rubrics, including experimental design, prediction, and critical evaluation. **Writing implication:** ask the reader to construct or critique a test of a model, not only solve for a numerical answer.

3. **Docktor, J. L., Strand, N. E., Mestre, J. P., & Ross, B. H. (2015).** "Conceptual problem solving in high school physics." *Physical Review Special Topics—Physics Education Research*, **11**, 020106. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.11.020106](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.11.020106). Examines explicit qualitative analysis in physics problem solving instead of emphasizing mathematical operations alone. **Writing implication:** teach the selection and justification of physical principles before manipulating equations. **Limit:** the study setting is high school physics, not all physics readers.

4. **Kohl, P. B., & Finkelstein, N. D. (2006).** "Effects of representation on students solving physics problems: A fine-grained characterization." *Physical Review Special Topics—Physics Education Research*, **2**, 010106. DOI: [10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.2.010106](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.2.010106). Finds that problem-solving performance can depend on verbal, graphical, mathematical, or pictorial representation, in ways influenced by knowledge and context. **Writing implication:** coordinate multiple representations, teaching how they map to one another. **Limit:** more representations are not automatically better without integration.

5. **Samani, J., & Pan, S. C. (2021).** "Interleaved practice enhances memory and problem-solving ability in undergraduate physics." *npj Science of Learning*, **6**, article 32. DOI: [10.1038/s41539-021-00110-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41539-021-00110-x). An eight-week course experiment found improvements on later physics problem solving from interleaved homework. **Writing implication:** mix prior topics into practice rather than restricting problems to the newest formula. **Limit:** tested a practice schedule, not the prose architecture of physics books.

6. **Deslauriers, L., McCarty, L. S., Miller, K., Callaghan, K., & Kestin, G. (2019).** "Measuring actual learning versus feeling of learning in response to being actively engaged in the classroom." *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, **116**(39), 19251–19257. DOI: [10.1073/pnas.1821936116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116). Active instruction improved learning despite students reporting a lower feeling of learning than in passive instruction. **Writing implication:** do not equate smooth, effortless reading with durable understanding; include meaningful cognitive work. **Limit:** classroom intervention, not a direct comparison of textbook designs.

## Broader cognitive research

7. **Mayer, R. E., & Moreno, R. (2003).** "Nine ways to reduce cognitive load in multimedia learning." *Educational Psychologist*, **38**(1), 43–52. DOI: [10.1207/S15326985EP3801_6](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_6). **Writing implication:** align diagrams and explanatory text, remove extraneous detail, and avoid splitting attention across disconnected material.

8. **Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003).** "The expertise reversal effect." *Educational Psychologist*, **38**(1), 23–31. DOI: [10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4). **Writing implication:** scaffold novices while giving experienced readers a more compact path.

## Evidence-strength notes

- **Directly supported in physics education settings:** representation matters; principle selection and scientific reasoning can be taught; interleaved practice helps later problem solving in the studied context.
- **Well-motivated transfer to writing:** place a physical question and modeling assumptions before extended derivations, connect each equation to a physical representation, and give readers opportunities to predict and test.
- **Editorial proposal, not a universally proved optimum:** the exact ten-stage template, the length of each stage, the ratio of intuition to equations, and checklist scores.
- **Always distinguish physical evidence from pedagogical evidence.** A study about teaching does not validate a physics equation, and an elegant equation does not validate a teaching method.
