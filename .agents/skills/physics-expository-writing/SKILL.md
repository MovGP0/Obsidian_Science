# Physics expository writing

## Goal

Teach readers to **observe or pose a physical problem, select or build a model, derive and interpret predictions, test them against evidence, and understand the model's domain of validity**. Mathematical completeness alone is insufficient; physical reasoning must precede and follow the equations.

## When to activate

Use for authoring, rewriting, outlining, or evaluating expository physics articles, scientific tutorials, and textbook sections. For novel experimental or theoretical research papers, follow the research-reporting conventions of the publication; apply this skill to pedagogical exposition when appropriate.

## Working procedure

1. **Determine audience and physical scope.** Identify topic, background, level (conceptual, undergraduate, graduate), desired rigor, mathematics allowed, length, and whether an actual experiment or dataset is available. If unspecified, select explicit defaults and proceed.
2. **Find the physical question.** Start with an observation, discrepancy, prediction, thought experiment, or conceptual puzzle. For advanced theory, a conflict between existing models may be a stronger opening than a laboratory experiment.
3. **Create an assumption ledger.** Define the system boundary; distinguish observed facts, idealizations, postulates, empirical laws, constitutive relations, coordinate choices, and derived consequences. State validity range and neglected effects.
4. **Build representations.** Give an intelligible verbal picture and, when helpful, a free-body diagram, graph, field diagram, energy diagram, or dimensional picture. Connect each to the variables in the equations; do not rely on illustrations without explanation.
5. **Choose principles before algebra.** Say why conservation, force/torque balance, symmetry, statistical assumptions, field equations, or another law applies. Then derive equations in reproducible steps. Define reference frames, signs, dimensions, and initial/boundary conditions.
6. **Translate the mathematics back.** Interpret terms, signs, parameters, characteristic scales, limiting cases, and predictions. Test dimensions, conservation laws, and a solvable special case. Separate exact statements from approximations.
7. **Close the scientific loop.** Specify what measurement/observation would distinguish predictions, how uncertainty or experimental constraints matter, and where the model fails. Do not invent observations, error bars, or evidence. If no data is supplied, propose a falsifiable test explicitly as a proposal.
8. **Teach transfer and assess learning.** Use a worked example, an alternative scenario, a prediction-before-calculation question, and an interleaved or boundary-case problem when suitable. Reconnect the model to other phenomena without implying equivalence beyond its domain.
9. **Audit and deliver.** Check mathematical and physical validity, educational accessibility, attribution, and whether the output matches the requested form. Produce the actual article unless only an outline/critique was requested.

## Default article pattern

Follow the adaptable pattern in [structure-and-patterns](references/structure-and-patterns.md):

**Phenomenon/question → qualitative prediction → system & assumptions → physical principles → mathematical model & derivation → physical interpretation → test/uncertainty → limitations & generalization → practice/summary.**

Repeat the **model → prediction → evidence → revision** cycle when dealing with historically developed or multiple competing models. Not every article requires a real experiment, a solved differential equation, or every heading. For advanced theory, begin with the explanatory gap or axiom system, then identify observable consequences.

## Required quality rules

- **Keep epistemic categories distinct:** observation, assumption, mathematical theorem, empirical relation, numerical approximation, and speculative interpretation are not synonyms.
- **No unexplained formulas:** derive or motivate governing equations and say when a law is accepted as a starting postulate.
- **Model validity is part of the result:** state idealizations, regimes, uncertainty, and what changes when an approximation is relaxed.
- **Physics checks are mandatory:** units, sign conventions, frames, limits, conserved quantities, and at least one sanity check when applicable.
- **Do not fake validation:** invented readings, graphs, datasets, precision, and historical claims are unacceptable. Label hypothetical simulations and proposed experiments.
- **Make choices learnable:** explain why this model/principle applies and why common alternatives do not.
- **Use representations purposefully:** place related explanations and diagrams together; give meaningful captions, readable axes, units, and symbol definitions.
- **Balance guidance with effort:** embed prediction or self-explanation prompts, and fade worked steps as the reader's expertise increases.
- **Use the user's required style, language, notation, and format.**

## Completion standard

Before finishing, be able to answer: **What phenomenon are we explaining? Why is this model justified? What does it predict? How could we test it? When does it fail?** If a required answer is missing, revise the artifact or explicitly label the evidence gap.
