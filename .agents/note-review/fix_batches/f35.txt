=== 02 Physics/05 Relativity and Gravitation/05 Cosmic Relativity/01 The paradigm of Cosmic Relativity and its evidence/01.05 The Cosmic Gravitational Metric and the Potentials.md
- [Correctness] Eqs. (1.4)–(1.6): substituting $dx'=dx-v\,dt$ into a form written in primed coordinates yields the line element in unprimed coordinates. The Galilean transform of the rest metric ($H\to0$) is $ds^2=-(c^2-v^2)dt'^2+2v\,dx'dt'+dx'^2$, with cross coefficient $+v$ for $x'=x-vt$, and $\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}$ describes a clock at $dx'=0$. In the printed (1.6), a comoving clock ($dx=v\,dt$) shows no dilation at all; add this to the source callout.
- [Correctness] After Eq. (1.8): with $A_g=-v/c$, the curl is $\nabla\times A_g=-2\Omega/c$, not $-2\Omega$. Only $-\nabla\times\mathbf v$ equals $-2\Omega$; list this among the dimensional inconsistencies.

=== 02 Physics/05 Relativity and Gravitation/05 Cosmic Relativity/01 The paradigm of Cosmic Relativity and its evidence/01.07 Summary of the Primary Results of Cosmic Relativity.md
- [Correctness] Eq. (1.17): for $\mathbf v=\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r$, $-m\,\mathbf v\times\boldsymbol\Omega=m\,\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf r)=-m\Omega^2\mathbf r$ points inward, so the final outward $\hat{\mathbf r}\,mv^2/r$ has the opposite sign. - [Correctness] Coriolis paragraph: $\mathbf F=m_g\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_g$ with $\mathbf B_g=-2\boldsymbol\Omega$ gives $+2m\,\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf u$, the opposite sign of the standard Coriolis force $-2m\,\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf u$; flag this in a source callout.
- [Correctness] "The farthest 1/3 of the Universe has 4 times the amount of matter than the nearer 2/3" is wrong: the volume ratio is $(1-8/27)/(8/27)=19/8\approx2.4$. Only the potential share of the outer third, $1-(2/3)^2=5/9$, matches "more than half".
- [Correctness] Eq. (1.19) closes the spinless orbit phase at $2\pi n$, but the quoted levels $(i+\tfrac12)\hbar\omega$ imply the semiclassical condition $\Phi/\Phi_0=n+\tfrac12$ (Maslov phase $\pi$). - [Correctness] "Since any two points in space-time can be connected by light, it follows that their interval remains invariant" is wrong: spacelike-separated events cannot be joined by a light signal, and invariance of the null cone alone gives only $ds'^2=\lambda(v)\,ds^2$. === 02 Physics/05 Relativity and Gravitation/05 Cosmic Relativity/03 Electrodynamics, light and relativity/03.04 The Speed of Light in Moving Media - Fresnel Drag.md
- [Correctness] Fig. 3.4 caption and the sentence after it: for complete drag (speed $\mechanical{c}/\signal{n}\pm\mechanical{v}$) the fringe shift is $\Delta N\approx4\mechanical{L}\signal{n}^2\mechanical{v}/(\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{c})$, not proportional to $\mechanical{L}(\signal{n}-1)\mechanical{v}/(\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{c})$. With Fresnel drag it is $4\mechanical{L}(\signal{n}^2-1)\mechanical{v}/(\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{c})$.
- [Correctness] Caption: "the observed effect was still smaller, second order in the refractive index n" is wrong. The observed shift is the fraction $1-1/\signal{n}^2\approx0.44$ of the complete-drag value, which is first order in $\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$. The body text ("about half the effect") is correct.
- [Correctness] Caption: "At about 5 m/s the expected shift is only about 1/100 of a fringe". With Fizeau's tubes ($\mechanical{L}\approx1.5\,\unit{m}$ each, $\signal{\lambda}\approx0.53\,\unit{\mu m}$), complete drag at 5 m/s gives $\Delta N\approx0.3$ fringe. Fizeau observed about 0.23 fringe on reversing a 7 m/s flow. Replace the estimate.

=== 02 Physics/05 Relativity and Gravitation/05 Cosmic Relativity/03 Electrodynamics, light and relativity/03.05 The Doppler Effect.md
- [Correctness] "the situation where the observer is in transverse motion ... is not yet adequately probed in experiments" is outdated. Mössbauer rotor experiments with a moving absorber, for example Hay et al. (1960) and Kündig (1963), measured the transverse shift of a moving receiver and agreed with special relativity.
- [Correctness] Eqs. (3.13)–(3.14) mix $\mechanical{u}_s$ and $\mechanical{v}$ for the same speed (the callout mentions only $\mechanical{u}_s$ in 3.14). Use one symbol, $\mechanical{u}_s$ for the source case and $\mechanical{u}_o$ for the observer case, in both the exact and the approximate forms.
- [Correctness] Ives–Stilwell (1938) did not observe at 90°. They compared the forward- and backward-shifted lines, and the second-order shift of their mean gives $\sqrt{1-\mechanical{u}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$. Say this, so that "what was verified was Eq. (3.13)" is not read as a transverse measurement.
