## How to use this collection

These 25 chapters develop the elementary five-dimensional circle model from the metric through gauge transformations, action reduction, and matter modes. The explanations, equation expansions, worked examples, and exercise answers are in the notes. Reading the video or a cited book is not required to follow the calculation.

Work through the chapters in order. Reproduce each algebraic step, check units and signs, then solve the self-check before reading its answer. The SVG diagrams explain geometric relations with editable labels. Hidden comments retain source timestamps and provenance for verification; they are not part of the study argument.

The collection uses two explicitly identified coordinate conventions to explain both the angular geometry and the length-coordinate field calculation. Each technical chapter states the conversion locally. The section on notation below records the same relation in one place.

[[Electromagnetism as a Fifth Dimension - Kaluza-Klein]] is a short overview. Use the chapters for the full derivations. The bibliography is optional further reading.

## Notation and units

The collection uses a mostly-plus metric, with one negative time direction and four positive spatial directions. The compact direction is spacelike. The circular coordinate is length-valued unless a note explicitly introduces an angle. Its coordinate period is one circumference. The reference radius is separate from the radion, which multiplies the physical circle size.

Natural units set the speed of light and reduced Planck constant to one. Energy, momentum, and mass can then all be measured in GeV; length and time use inverse GeV. Explicit SI conversions restore the constants. Charge uses Heaviside–Lorentz normalization, so the fine-structure constant equals elementary charge squared divided by four pi.

The potential is normalized so that the Maxwell Lagrangian has its conventional coefficient of minus one quarter. A plus sign in the shifted circular differential and a plus sign in the circular-coordinate redefinition lead to a minus gradient in the potential transformation. The matter phase and covariant derivative use the matching signs. A potential sign, phase sign, and derivative sign must be changed together when changing convention.

Greek indices refer to ordinary spacetime. Capital Latin indices refer to five-dimensional coordinates. Lowercase Latin indices in the curvature chapter refer to orthonormal frames. Repeated indices are summed. Indices, operators, temporary matrix blocks, gauge parameters, and other mathematical helpers remain uncolored. Physical quantities use the semantic macros from the vault’s preamble.

The fixed-radius action calculation is a restricted calculation. The scalar equation of the full vacuum theory is restored in chapter 23. A cylinder-independent background and a Fourier expansion of a test matter field are also distinct assumptions. The latter does not include unrestricted backreaction.

## Angular and length-coordinate notation

An angular coordinate is dimensionless and has period two pi. A length coordinate has period equal to the reference circumference. The angular calculation uses a mostly-minus metric; the length-coordinate calculation uses a mostly-plus metric. Reversing the full metric sign also requires the indicated potential convention so that their completed-square expressions describe the same geometry.

For a fixed reference radius, the conversion is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{y}&=\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\theta},\\
g^{\mathrm{len}}_{\mu\nu}&=-g^{\mathrm{ang}}_{\mu\nu},\\
\electric{A}^{\mathrm{len}}_\mu&=-\electric{A}^{\mathrm{ang}}_\mu,\\
\mechanical{\kappa}&=\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\xi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The angular and length-coordinate squares are related by

$$
\begin{gathered}
-\mechanical{r}^{\,2}
\left(\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}-\mechanical{\xi}\electric{A}^{\mathrm{ang}}_\mu\,\mathrm d\mechanical{x}^\mu\right)^2
\\\longleftrightarrow\\
\left(\mathrm d\mechanical{y}+\mechanical{\kappa}\electric{A}^{\mathrm{len}}_\mu\,\mathrm d\mechanical{x}^\mu\right)^2.
\end{gathered}
$$

The overall sign changes with the full metric convention. A positive circular-coordinate shift gives a positive potential gradient in the angular convention and a negative potential gradient in the length convention. The matter-mode phase can keep the same negative sign in both conventions, with the matching covariant derivatives

$$
D^{\mathrm{ang}}_\mu=\partial_\mu+i\electric{q}\electric{A}^{\mathrm{ang}}_\mu,
\qquad
D^{\mathrm{len}}_\mu=\partial_\mu-i\electric{q}\electric{A}^{\mathrm{len}}_\mu.
$$

The angular scalar is the physical radius squared. The length-coordinate radion is a dimensionless ratio of physical radius to reference radius:

$$
\mechanical{\Phi}_{\mathrm{ang}}
=\mechanical{r}^{\,2}\mechanical{\Phi}_{\mathrm{len}}^{\,2}.
$$

For a frozen radius equal to the reference radius, the length-coordinate ratio is one. The two Phi symbols must not be substituted for each other directly. The Schwarzschild example in chapter 3 uses a geometric mass parameter equal to Newton’s constant times black-hole mass, so that parameter has units of length.

## Scope of the study text

The notes develop the elementary Abelian circle construction. They do not derive general non-Abelian compactifications, a complete chiral fermion model, a radion-stabilization potential, or a general vacuum decay rate. They define broader models with additional assumptions. The elementary construction, its consistency constraint, and its mass–charge failure are derived here. A mathematical relationship between gauge theory and higher-dimensional geometry is not by itself evidence that the simplest five-dimensional model describes nature.
