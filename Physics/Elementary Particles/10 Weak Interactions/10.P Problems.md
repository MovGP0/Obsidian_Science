## Problem 10.1

**(a)** Calculate $\langle|\mathcal M|^2\rangle$ for $\nu_\mu+e^-\to\mu^-+\nu_e$ using the more general coupling $\gamma^\mu(1+\epsilon\gamma^5)$. Check that your answer reduces to equation (10.11) in the case $\epsilon=-1$.

[*Answer:*

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{\text{spins}}|\mathcal M|^2
&=\frac12\left(\frac{g_w}{\mechanical{M_W}\mechanical{c}}\right)^4
[(1-\epsilon^2)^2(\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_4})(\mechanical{p_2}\cdot\mechanical{p_3})\\
&\qquad+(1+6\epsilon^2+\epsilon^4)
(\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_2})(\mechanical{p_3}\cdot\mechanical{p_4})]
\end{aligned}
$$

]

**(b)** Let $\mechanical{m_e}=\mechanical{m_\mu}=0$, and calculate the *CM* differential scattering cross section. Also, find the *total* cross section.

**(c)** If you had accurate experimental data on this reaction, how could you determine $\epsilon$?

$$

## Problem 10.11

Show that if $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2\ll\energy{E}$

$$
\gamma^5\signal{u}\cong
\begin{pmatrix}
\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}&0\\
0&\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}
\end{pmatrix}\signal{u}
$$

where $\signal{u}$ is a particle spinor satisfying the Dirac equation:

$$
\signal{u}=\begin{pmatrix}
\signal{u_A}\\
\dfrac{\mechanical{c}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)}
{\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2}\signal{u_A}
\end{pmatrix}
$$

with $\energy{E}>0$ [eq. (7.36)]. Show therefore that the *projection matrix*

$$
P_\pm\equiv\tfrac12(1\pm\gamma^5)
$$

picks out the helicity $\pm1$ component of $\signal{u}$:

$$
\boldsymbol\Sigma\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}(P_\pm\signal{u})
=\pm(P_\pm\signal{u})
$$

## Problem 10.14

**(a)** Show that as long as the KM matrix is *unitary* $(U^{-1}=U^\dagger)$, the GIM mechanism for eliminating $K^0\to\mu^+\mu^-$ works for three (or any number of) generations.

**(b)** How many independent real parameters are there in the general $3\times3$ unitary matrix? How about $n\times n$?

We are free to change the *phase* of each quark wave function (normalization of $\signal{u}$ really only determines $|N|^2$; see Problem 7.3), so $2n$ of these parameters are arbitrary—or rather, $(2n-1)$, since changing the phase of *all* quark wave functions by the same amount has no effect on $U$. *Question:* Can we thus reduce the KM matrix to a *real* matrix (if it is real and unitary, then it is *orthogonal*: $U^{-1}=\tilde U$).

**(c)** How many independent real parameters are there in the general $3\times3$ (real) orthogonal matrix? How about $n\times n$?

**(d)** So, what is the answer? *Can* you reduce the KM matrix to real form? How about for only two generations $(n=2)$?

## Problem 10.20

**(a)** Calculate the decay rate for $Z^0\to f+\bar f$, where $f$ is any quark or any lepton. Assume $f$ is so light (compared to the $Z$) that its mass can be neglected.

$$
\left[\textit{Answer: }\mechanical{\Gamma}(Z^0\to f+\bar f)
=\frac{g_z^2\mechanical{M_Z}\mechanical{c}^2}{48\pi\energy{\hbar}}
(|c_V^f|^2+|c_A^f|^2).\right]
$$

**(b)** Assuming these are the dominant decay modes, find the branching ratio for each species of quark and lepton (remember that the quarks come in three colors). Assume that $2\mechanical{m_t}<\mechanical{M_Z}$, and that the approximation in (a) is valid even for $t$.

[*Answer:* 3% each for $e,\mu,\tau$; 6% each for $\nu_e,\nu_\mu,\nu_\tau$; 10% each for $u,c,t$; 14% each for $d,s,b$.]

**(c)** Calculate the lifetime of the $Z^0$. How would it change if there exists a fourth generation? (Notice that an accurate measurement of the $Z^0$ lifetime will tell us how many quarks and leptons there can be with masses less than $45\,\unit{GeV}/\mechanical{c}^2$.)
