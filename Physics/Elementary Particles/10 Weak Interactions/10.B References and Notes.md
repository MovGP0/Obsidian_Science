> [!TLDR]
> These references document weak decays, quark mixing, neutral currents, and electroweak unification.

<!-- PDF page 346; printed page 338 -->

1. N. Cabibbo, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **10**, 531 (1963).
2. For more detailed calculations in weak interaction theory consult the classic treatise by R. E. Marshak, Riazuddin, and C. P. Ryan: *Theory of Weak Interactions in Particle Physics* (New York: Wiley, 1969), or the briefer account by E. D. Commins: *Weak Interactions* (New York: McGraw-Hill, 1973). For an up-to-date review of weak interactions in the quark model, see J. F. Donoghue, E. Golowich, and B. Holstein, *Phys. Rep.* **131**, 319 (1986) or E. D. Commins and P. H. Bucksbaum: *Weak Interactions of Leptons and Quarks* (Cambridge: Cambridge Univ. Press, 1983).
3. S. L. Glashow, J. Iliopoulos, and L. Maiani, *Phys. Rev.* **D2**, 1585 (1970). This and the other fundamental papers in modern weak interaction theory are reprinted in *Gauge Theory of Weak and Electromagnetic Interactions*, C. H. Lai, ed. (Singapore: World Scientific, 1981).
4. M. Kobayashi and K. Maskawa, *Prog. Theor. Phys.* **49**, 652 (1973).
5. Different authors use different conventions in writing the KM matrix; I follow the notation of L.-L. Chau, *Phys. Rep.* **95**, 2 (1983).
6. F. J. Gilman, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **56**, S297 (1984). For a useful discussion of the KM matrix, see T.-P. Cheng and L.-F. Li, *Gauge Theory of Elementary Particle Physics* (New York: Oxford, 1984), Sect. 12.2.
7. S. A. Bludman, *Nuovo Cimento* **9**, 443 (1958). In a sense the process shown in Figure (10.4) is already a neutral weak interaction, since $d$ and $s$ have the same charge. However, I shall reserve the word “neutral” for weak interactions mediated by the $Z^0$.
8. S. L. Glashow, *Nucl. Phys.* **22**, 579 (1961). Reprinted in Lai, ref. 3.
9. S. Weinberg, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **19**, 1264 (1967); A. Salam, in *Elementary Particle Theory*, N. Svartholm, ed. (Stockholm: Almquist and Wiksell, 1968). Reprinted in Lai, ref. 3.
10. G. 't Hooft, *Nucl. Phys.* **B33**, 173 (1971) and **B35**, 167 (1971). Reprinted in Lai, ref. 3.
11. F. J. Hasert et al., *Phys. Lett.* **46B**, 138 (1973); *Nucl. Phys.* **B73**, 1 (1974).
12. See, for example, D. H. Perkins, *Introduction to High-Energy Physics*, 2d Ed. (Reading, MA: Addison-Wesley, 1982), Sect. 8.8.2; I. J. R. Aitchison and A. J. G. Hey, *Gauge Theories in Particle Physics* (Bristol: Adam Hilger, 1982) Sect. 6.2; F. Halzen and A. D. Martin, *Quarks and Leptons* (New York: Wiley, 1984), Sects. 12.7 and 12.10; F. E. Close, *An Introduction to Quarks and Partons* (London: Academic, 1979), Sect. 11.3.
13. G. Arnison, et al., *Phys. Lett.* **122B**, 103 (1983) and **126B**, 398 (1983). For a review of these discoveries, see E. Radermacher, *Prog. Part. Nucl. Phys.* **14**, 231 (1985).
14. Data on $\nu_\mu+e^-\to\nu_e+\mu^-$ is from F. Bergsma et al., *Phys. Lett.* **122B**, 465 (1983); data on $\nu_\mu+e^-\to\nu_\mu+e^-$ is from R. H. Heisterberg et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **44**, 635 (1980) (earlier data by P. Alibran et al., *Phys. Lett.* **74B**, 422 (1978), which was inconsistent with the GWS model, turned out to be wrong, although it caused some consternation at the time; it was corrected by N. Armenise et al., *Phys. Lett.* **86B**, 225 (1979).
15. E. N. Fortson and L. Wilets, *Advances in Atomic and Molecular Physics*, **16**, 319 (1980); C. Y. Prescott et al., *Phys. Lett.* **77B**, 347 (1978) and **84B**, 524 (1979); S. L. Wu, *Phys. Rep.* **107**, 229 (1984).
16. See, for example, H. Frauenfelder and E. M. Henley, *Subatomic Physics*, (Englewood Cliffs, NJ: Prentice-Hall, 1974), Sect. 5.7.
