> [!TLDR]
> Neutral weak fields mix into the photon and Z fields, which relates the electromagnetic and weak coupling constants.

Now, the GWS model asserts that the three weak isospin currents couple, with strength $g_w$, to a weak isotriplet of intermediate vector bosons, $\mathbf W$, whereas the weak hypercharge current couples with strength $g'/2$ to an isosinglet intermediate vector boson, $B$:

$$
-i\left[g_w\mathbf j_\mu\cdot\signal{\mathbf W^\mu}
+\frac{g'}2j_\mu^Y\signal{B^\mu}\right]
\tag{10.149}
$$

Within this fundamental structure is contained all of electrodynamics and all of the weak interactions. The arrow denotes a three-vector in weak isospin space; the dot product can be written out explicitly:

$$
\mathbf j_\mu\cdot\signal{\mathbf W^\mu}
=j_\mu^1\signal{W^{\mu1}}+j_\mu^2\signal{W^{\mu2}}+j_\mu^3\signal{W^{\mu3}}
\tag{10.150}
$$

or, in terms of the charged currents, $j_\mu^\pm=j_\mu^1\pm ij_\mu^2$:

$$
\mathbf j_\mu\cdot\signal{\mathbf W^\mu}
=(1/\sqrt2)j_\mu^+\signal{W^{\mu+}}
+(1/\sqrt2)j_\mu^-\signal{W^{\mu-}}+j_\mu^3\signal{W^{\mu3}}
\tag{10.151}
$$

where

$$
\signal{W_\mu^\pm}\equiv(1/\sqrt2)(\signal{W_\mu^1}\mp i\signal{W_\mu^2})
\tag{10.152}
$$

are the wave functions representing the $W^\pm$ particles.

The couplings to $W^\pm$ can now be read off, from the coefficients of $\signal{W_\mu^\pm}$ in expression (10.149). For example, in the process $e^-\to\nu_e+W^-$ we have $j_\mu^-=\signal{\bar\nu_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{e_L}=\signal{\bar\nu}\gamma_\mu[(1-\gamma^5)/2]\signal{e}$ [see eq. (10.129)], giving a term

$$
-ig_w(1/\sqrt2)j_\mu^-\signal{W^{\mu-}}
=-\frac{ig_w}{2\sqrt2}
[\signal{\bar\nu}\gamma_\mu(1-\gamma^5)\signal{e}]\signal{W^{\mu-}}
\tag{10.153}
$$

The vertex factor is

$$
\frac{-ig_w}{2\sqrt2}\gamma_\mu(1-\gamma^5)
\tag{10.154}
$$

which is exactly what we started with [eq. (10.5)].

But the underlying $SU(2)_L\otimes U(1)$ symmetry is “broken,” in GWS theory: The two neutral states, $W^3$ and $B$, “mix,” producing one massless linear combination (the photon), and an orthogonal massive combination (the $Z^0$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{A_\mu}&=\signal{B_\mu}\cos\mechanical{\theta_w}
+\signal{W_\mu^3}\sin\mechanical{\theta_w}\\
\signal{Z_\mu}&=-\signal{B_\mu}\sin\mechanical{\theta_w}
+\signal{W_\mu^3}\cos\mechanical{\theta_w}
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.155}
$$

(You see now why $\mechanical{\theta_w}$ is called the “weak mixing angle.”) In terms of the *physical* states $(\electric{A^\mu}$ and $\signal{Z^\mu})$, then, the neutral portion of the electro-weak interaction (10.149) reads:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-i\left[g_wj_\mu^3\signal{W^{\mu3}}+\frac{g'}2j_\mu^Y\signal{B^\mu}\right]
&=-i\left\{
\left[g_w\sin\mechanical{\theta_w}\,j_\mu^3
+\frac{g'}2\cos\mechanical{\theta_w}\,j_\mu^Y\right]\electric{A^\mu}\right.\\
&\qquad\left.+\left[g_w\cos\mechanical{\theta_w}\,j_\mu^3
-\frac{g'}2\sin\mechanical{\theta_w}\,j_\mu^Y\right]\signal{Z^\mu}
\right\}
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.156}
$$

Of course, we *know* the electromagnetic coupling; in the present language it is

$$
-i\electric{g_e}\electric{j_\mu^{em}}\electric{A^\mu}
\tag{10.157}
$$

Meanwhile, from equation (10.147), $\electric{j_\mu^{em}}=j_\mu^3+\frac12j_\mu^Y$. Evidently consistency of the unified electro-weak theory with ordinary QED requires

$$
g_w\sin\mechanical{\theta_w}=g'\cos\mechanical{\theta_w}=\electric{g_e}
\tag{10.158}
$$

The weak and electromagnetic coupling constants are *not independent*.

There remains the weak coupling to the $Z^0$. Using equations (10.147), (10.156), and (10.158), we obtain

$$
-ig_z(j_\mu^3-\sin^2\mechanical{\theta_w}\,\electric{j_\mu^{em}})\signal{Z^\mu}
\tag{10.159}
$$

where

$$
g_z=\frac{\electric{g_e}}{\sin\mechanical{\theta_w}\cos\mechanical{\theta_w}}
\tag{10.160}
$$

From expression (10.159) we can pick out the neutral weak couplings. For example, the process $\nu_e\to\nu_e+Z^0$ comes exclusively from the $j_\mu^3$ term; referring back to equation (10.142), we have

$$
-i\frac{g_z}2(\signal{\bar\nu_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{\nu_L})\signal{Z^\mu}
=-\frac{ig_z}2
\left[\signal{\bar\nu}\gamma_\mu\left(\frac{1-\gamma^5}2\right)\signal{\nu}\right]\signal{Z^\mu}
$$

and hence the vector and axial vector couplings [eq. (10.93)] are $c_V^\nu=c_A^\nu=\frac12$. I'll leave it for you to work out the other entries in Table 10.1[^ep-10-7-3-independent] (Problem 10.26).

Finally, there is the obvious question: *why* is the underlying $SU(2)_L\otimes U(1)$ symmetry of the electroweak interactions “broken”—why do the $B$ and $W^3$ states “mix” [eq. (10.155)]—to form the $Z^0$ and the photon? If weak and electromagnetic interactions are, deep down, both manifestations of a single electroweak force, how come the weak mediators $(W^\pm$ and $Z^0)$ are so heavy, while the electromagnetic mediator $(\gamma)$ is massless? We shall address these matters in the next chapter.

[^ep-10-7-3-independent]: Since the weak mixing angle is undetermined, in the GWS model, there remain in effect *two* independent coupling constants $(\electric{g_e}$ and $g_w$, say, or $\electric{g_e}$ and $g_z)$; in this sense it is not a completely *unified* theory, but rather an *integrated* theory of weak and electromagnetic interactions.
