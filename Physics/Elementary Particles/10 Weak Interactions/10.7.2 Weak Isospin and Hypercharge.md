title: "Weak Isospin and Hypercharge"

> [!TLDR]
> Weak-isospin and hypercharge currents combine chiral fermion doublets under the electroweak symmetry group.

<!-- PDF page 342; printed page 334 -->

In addition to the negatively charged weak current

$$
j_\mu^-=\signal{\bar\nu_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{e_L}
$$

![[elementary-particles-weak-chapter10-negative-weak-current.svg|The negative weak current converts an electron to a neutrino and emits a W-minus boson.]]

describing the process $e^-\to\nu_e+W^-$, there is also, of course, a positively charged current

$$
j_\mu^+=\signal{\bar e_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{\nu_L}
$$

![[elementary-particles-weak-chapter10-positive-weak-current.svg|The positive weak current converts a neutrino to an electron and emits a W-plus boson.]]

representing the process $\nu_e\to e^-+W^+$. We can express them both in a more compact notation by introducing the left-handed doublet

$$
\signal{\chi_L}=\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\nu_e}\\\signal{e}\end{pmatrix}_L
\tag{10.138}
$$

and the $2\times2$ matrices

$$
\tau^+=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\tau^-=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\tag{10.139}
$$

so that

$$
j_\mu^\pm=\signal{\bar\chi_L}\gamma_\mu\tau^\pm\signal{\chi_L}
\tag{10.140}
$$

The matrices $\tau^\pm$ are linear combinations of the first two Pauli spin matrices [eq. (4.26)]:

$$
\tau^\pm=\tfrac12(\tau^1\pm i\tau^2)
\tag{10.141}
$$

<!-- PDF page 343; printed page 335 -->

(I use the letter $\tau$ here, instead of $\sigma$, to avoid possible confusion with ordinary spin.) This is all very reminiscent of *isospin*; in Chapter 4, Section 4.5, we put the proton and neutron into a doublet similar to (10.138). Indeed, we could contemplate a full “weak isospin” symmetry, if only there were a *third* weak current, corresponding to $\frac12\tau^3=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$:

$$
j_\mu^3=\signal{\bar\chi_L}\gamma_\mu\tfrac12\tau^3\signal{\chi_L}
=\tfrac12\signal{\bar\nu_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{\nu_L}
-\tfrac12\signal{\bar e_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{e_L}
\tag{10.142}
$$

“Perfect!” (I hear you exclaim.) “There's the *neutral* weak current!” Not so fast. *This* current only couples *left*-handed particles; in the older language it is pure $V$–$A$, whereas the neutral weak interaction involves right-handed components as well. But hang on—we're *almost* there.

Building on the parallel with isospin, we are led to consider a weak analog of *hypercharge* $(Y)$,[^ep-10-7-2-hypercharge] which is related to electric charge $(\electric{Q}$, in units of $\electric{e})$ and the third component of isospin $(I^3)$, by the Gell-Mann–Nishijima formula [eq. (4.37)]:

$$
\electric{Q}=I^3+\tfrac12Y
\tag{10.143}
$$

We introduce, then, the “weak hypercharge” current

$$
j_\mu^Y=2\electric{j_\mu^{em}}-2j_\mu^3
=-2\signal{\bar e_R}\gamma_\mu\signal{e_R}
-\signal{\bar e_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{e_L}
-\signal{\bar\nu_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{\nu_L}
\tag{10.144}
$$

This is an *invariant* construct, as far as weak isospin is concerned, for the latter does not touch *right*-handed components at all, and the combination

$$
\signal{\bar e_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{e_L}
+\signal{\bar\nu_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{\nu_L}
=\signal{\bar\chi_L}\gamma_\mu\signal{\chi_L}
$$

is itself invariant.[^ep-10-7-2-singlet] The underlying symmetry group is called $SU(2)_L\otimes U(1)$; $SU(2)_L$ refers to the weak isospin (with a subscript to remind us that it involves left-handed states only), and $U(1)$ refers to weak hypercharge (involving both chiralities).

I have developed all this in terms of the electron and its neutrino, but it is a trivial matter to extend it to the other leptons and quarks. From the left-handed doublets (Cabibbo-rotated, in the case of the quarks)

$$
\signal{\chi_L}\to
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\nu_e}\\\signal{e}\end{pmatrix}_L,\quad
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\nu_\mu}\\\signal{\mu}\end{pmatrix}_L,\quad
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\nu_\tau}\\\signal{\tau}\end{pmatrix}_L,\quad
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{u}\\\signal{d'}\end{pmatrix}_L,\quad
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{c}\\\signal{s'}\end{pmatrix}_L,\quad
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{t}\\\signal{b'}\end{pmatrix}_L
\tag{10.145}
$$

we construct three weak isospin currents

$$
\mathbf j_\mu=\tfrac12\signal{\bar\chi_L}\gamma_\mu\boldsymbol\tau\signal{\chi_L}
\tag{10.146}
$$

and a weak hypercharge current

$$
j_\mu^Y=2\electric{j_\mu^{em}}-2j_\mu^3
\tag{10.147}
$$

[^ep-10-7-2-hypercharge]: You have probably forgotten this word, but *hypercharge* is essentially the same as strangeness, only shifted, in the case of baryons, so that the center row of Eightfold Way diagrams will always carry $Y=0$. Specifically, $Y=S+A$, where $A$ is the baryon number.

[^ep-10-7-2-singlet]: If you care to think of it this way, what we have done is to combine two weak-isospin doublets to make an isotriplet, $\signal{\bar\nu_L}\signal{e_L}$, $(\signal{\bar\nu_L}\signal{\nu_L}-\signal{\bar e_L}\signal{e_L})$, $\signal{\bar e_L}\signal{\nu_L}$ [analogous to (5.89)], and an isosinglet $(\signal{\bar\nu_L}\signal{\nu_L}+\signal{\bar e_L}\signal{e_L})$ [analogous to (5.90)]. The first three go to make the weak isospin currents $j^\pm$ and $j^3$; the last, together with a right-handed piece, makes the weak hypercharge current, $j^Y$.

<!-- PDF page 344; printed page 336 -->

where $\electric{j_\mu^{em}}$ is the electric current:

$$
\electric{j_\mu^{em}}
=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\electric{Q_i}
(\signal{\bar u_{iL}}\gamma_\mu\signal{u_{iL}}
+\signal{\bar u_{iR}}\gamma_\mu\signal{u_{iR}})
\tag{10.148}
$$

(summed over the particles in the doublet, with $\electric{Q_i}$ the electric charge).[^ep-10-7-2-strong]

[^ep-10-7-2-strong]: You might ask what the *difference* is between weak isospin (and hypercharge) and their ordinary (“strong”) counterparts. The question is particularly pertinent when you come to the light quarks: The weak isospin doublet is $\begin{pmatrix}u\\d'\end{pmatrix}_L$, whereas the strong isospin doublet is $\begin{pmatrix}u\\d\end{pmatrix}$. Pretty similar . . . is there anything to this? Nope. After all, (i) weak isospin applies to leptons as well as quarks (and to all three quark generations); (ii) weak isospin involves only the *left*-handed chiralities, all right-handed states are *singlets*—i.e., invariant—as far as weak isospin is concerned; (iii) weak isodoublets are Cabibbo-rotated. To put it plainly, strong isospin and weak isospin have nothing to do with one another, save for a common mathematical structure (which, for that matter, they share with many other systems, such as ordinary spin $\frac12$) and the (perhaps unfortunate) similarity in their names.
