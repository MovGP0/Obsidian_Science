23. The “MIT Bag Model” offers a possible approach to relativistic light-quark systems, but at the cost of vastly oversimplified dynamics. The quarks are treated as free particles confined within a spherical “bag”, which is stabilized by an *ad hoc* external pressure. Many interesting calculations have been carried out using the bag model, but no one would pretend that it is a realistic picture of hadron structure. See F. E. Close, *An Introduction to Quarks and Partons* (London: Academic, 1979), Chapter 18.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{cm}}$ | Radius | The radial coordinate in the expectation values. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf J}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{erg\,s}}$ | Total angular momentum | The conserved angular momentum in the spin-orbit argument. |
| $\mechanical{L}^{2},\mechanical{S}^{2}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{erg^{2}\,s^{2}}}$ | Squared angular momenta | The orbital and spin magnitudes squared. |
| $\energy{\Delta H_{\mathrm{so}}}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{erg}}$ | Spin-orbit Hamiltonian | The interaction operator in the commutation statement. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{erg\,s}}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the oscillator ground-state energy. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{s^{-1}}}$ | Angular frequency | The frequency of a field oscillator mode. |
| $\mechanical{F_0}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{dyn}}$ | Confining force | The force parameter estimated from quarkonium data. |
