---
title: "The Relativistic Correction"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The first relativistic kinetic-energy correction produces a small hydrogen energy shift determined by Coulomb expectation values.

<!-- PDF page 160; printed page 152 -->

In developing the Schrödinger equation, I used the classical formula for kinetic energy: $\energy{T}=\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{2}/2\mechanical{m}$. The relativistic formula (3.45), expressed in terms of $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{T_{\mathrm{rel}}}
&=\sqrt{\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}+\mechanical{m}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{4}}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}
=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}\left[\sqrt{1+(\mechanical{\mathbf p}/\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2}-1\right]\\
&=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}\left[1+\tfrac{1}{2}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}/\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2-\tfrac{1}{8}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}/\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^4+\cdots-1\right]\\
&=\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{2}}{2\mechanical{m}}-\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{4}}{8\mechanical{m}^{3}\mechanical{c}^{2}}+\cdots
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.36}
$$

The lowest-order relativistic correction to the Hamiltonian is therefore

$$
\energy{\Delta H_{\mathrm{rel}}}
=-\left(\frac{1}{8\mechanical{m}^{3}\mechanical{c}^{2}}\right)\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{4}
\tag{5.37}
$$

Now, to apply equation (5.35), we need the expectation value of $\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{4}$ (using the quantum replacement (5.2) for $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$) in the state $\signal{\psi_{n,l,m_l}}\ldots$, and that does not look easy. Fortunately, we can get around it by using the fact that in such a state

$$
\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{2}}{2\mechanical{m}}=\energy{E_n}-\energy{V}
\tag{5.38}
$$

Thus

$$
\energy{\Delta E_{\mathrm{rel}}}
=-\frac{1}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}}
(\energy{E_n}^{2}-2\energy{E_n}\langle\energy{V}\rangle+\langle\energy{V}^{2}\rangle)
\tag{5.39}
$$

Since $\energy{V}=-\electric{e}^{2}/\mechanical{r}$, we now require only the expectation values of $1/\mechanical{r}$ and $1/\mechanical{r}^{2}$. These are worked out in the more advanced quantum texts:<sup>6</sup>

$$
\left\langle\frac{1}{\mechanical{r}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\mechanical{n}^{2}\mechanical{a}}
\tag{5.40}
$$

$$
\left\langle\frac{1}{\mechanical{r}^{2}}\right\rangle
=\frac{1}{(\mechanical{l}+\tfrac{1}{2})\mechanical{n}^{3}\mechanical{a}^{2}}
\tag{5.41}
$$

where $\mechanical{a}$, again, is the Bohr radius (5.29). Using these expressions in equation (5.39), we find that

$$
\energy{\Delta E_{\mathrm{rel}}}
=-\electric{\alpha}^{4}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}
\frac{1}{4\mechanical{n}^{2}}
\left[\frac{2\mechanical{n}}{(\mechanical{l}+\tfrac{1}{2})}-\frac{3}{2}\right]
\tag{5.42}
$$

The Bohr energy levels go like $\electric{\alpha}^{2}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}$ [eq. (5.26)]; the relativistic correction carries two more powers of $\electric{\alpha}$, and hence is smaller by a factor of about $10^{-4}$. So we're talking about a very small perturbation indeed. The *fine structure constant*, $\electric{\alpha}$, owes its name to the fact that it (or rather, $\electric{\alpha}^{2}$) sets the relative scale of the fine structure in hydrogen.[^ep-5-3-1-scale]

[^ep-5-3-1-scale]: However, one might say equally well that $\electric{\alpha}^{2}$ sets the scale of the Bohr levels themselves, in the sense that $\electric{\alpha}^{2}=-2\energy{E_1}/\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}$.
