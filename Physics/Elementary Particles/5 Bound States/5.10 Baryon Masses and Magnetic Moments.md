> Quark magnetic moments and pairwise spin interactions predict baryon magnetic moments and octet and decuplet masses.

As an application of the baryon spin/flavor wave functions, we now calculate the magnetic dipole moments of the particles in the octet.[^ep-5-10-measured] In the absence of orbital motion, the net magnetic moment of a baryon is simply the vector sum of the moments of the three constituent quarks:

$$
\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1}+\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}_2}+\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}_3}.
\tag{5.114}
$$

It depends on the quark flavors (because the three flavors carry different magnetic moments) and on the spin configuration (because that determines the relative orientations of the three dipoles). As I mentioned earlier (eq. (5.45)), the magnetic dipole moment of a spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ point particle of charge $\electric{q}$ and mass $\mechanical{m}$ is[^ep-5-10-radiative]

$$
\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\frac{\electric{q}}{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}\mechanical{\mathbf S}.
\tag{5.45}
$$

<!-- PDF page 189; printed page 181 -->

Its magnitude, then, is

$$
\magnetic{\mu}=\frac{\electric{q}\energy{\hbar}}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}.
\tag{5.115}
$$

More precisely, this is the value of $\magnetic{\mu_z}$ in the spin-up state, for which $\mechanical{S_z}=\energy{\hbar}/2$; if the charge is negative, so too is $\magnetic{\mu}$—this reminds us that the magnetic moment points opposite to the spin. It is customary to refer to $\magnetic{\mu}$, rather than $\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}}$ itself, as “the magnetic moment” of the particle. For the quarks, we have

$$
\magnetic{\mu_u}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}}{2\mechanical{m_u}\mechanical{c}},\qquad
\magnetic{\mu_d}=-\frac{1}{3}\frac{\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}}{2\mechanical{m_d}\mechanical{c}},\qquad
\magnetic{\mu_s}=-\frac{1}{3}\frac{\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}}{2\mechanical{m_s}\mechanical{c}}.
\tag{5.116}
$$

The magnetic moment of baryon $B$, then, is

$$
\magnetic{\mu_B}=\langle B\uparrow\lvert
(\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1}+\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}_2}+\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}_3})_z
\rvert B\uparrow\rangle
=\frac{2}{\energy{\hbar}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}
\langle B\uparrow\lvert(\magnetic{\mu_i}\mechanical{S_{iz}})\rvert B\uparrow\rangle.
\tag{5.117}
$$

### Example 5.3

Calculate the magnetic moment of the proton.

*Solution.* The wave function was found in Example 5.2. The first term is

$$
\frac{2}{3\sqrt{2}}[u(\uparrow)u(\uparrow)d(\downarrow)].
$$

Now

$$
\begin{aligned}
&(\magnetic{\mu_1}\mechanical{S_{1z}}+\magnetic{\mu_2}\mechanical{S_{2z}}+\magnetic{\mu_3}\mechanical{S_{3z}})
\lvert u(\uparrow)u(\uparrow)d(\downarrow)\rangle\\
&\qquad=\left[\magnetic{\mu_u}\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{2}
+\magnetic{\mu_u}\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{2}
+\magnetic{\mu_d}\left(-\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{2}\right)\right]
\lvert u(\uparrow)u(\uparrow)d(\downarrow)\rangle
\end{aligned}
$$

so this term contributes an amount

$$
\left(\frac{2}{3\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{2}
\sum_{i=1}^{3}
\langle u(\uparrow)u(\uparrow)d(\downarrow)\lvert
(\magnetic{\mu_i}\mechanical{S_{iz}})
\rvert u(\uparrow)u(\uparrow)d(\downarrow)\rangle
=\frac{2}{9}(2\magnetic{\mu_u}-\magnetic{\mu_d}).
$$

Similarly, the second term ($u(\uparrow)u(\downarrow)d(\uparrow)$) gives $\tfrac{1}{18}\magnetic{\mu_d}$, as does the third.[^ep-5-10-normalized] We could continue in this way to evaluate all nine terms, but the rest are simply permutations, in which $d$ occupies position 2 or position 1. The result, then, is

$$
\magnetic{\mu_p}
=3[\tfrac{2}{9}(2\magnetic{\mu_u}-\magnetic{\mu_d})
+\tfrac{1}{18}\magnetic{\mu_d}+\tfrac{1}{18}\magnetic{\mu_d}]
=\tfrac{1}{3}(4\magnetic{\mu_u}-\magnetic{\mu_d}).
$$

In this way we can calculate all the octet magnetic moments in terms of $\magnetic{\mu_u}$, $\magnetic{\mu_d}$, and $\magnetic{\mu_s}$ (Problem 5.29). The results are listed in the second column of Table 5.5. To get numbers, we need to know the quark magnetic moments (5.116). Using the baryon constituent quark masses in Table 4.4, we obtain the

figures in the third column of Table 5.5. The comparison with experiment is reasonably good, considering the uncertainties in the quark masses and anomalous magnetic moments. Somewhat better predictions are obtained if we take *ratios*. In particular, to the extent that $\mechanical{m_u}=\mechanical{m_d}$, we have

$$
\frac{\magnetic{\mu_n}}{\magnetic{\mu_p}}=-\frac{2}{3}
\tag{5.118}
$$

which compares well with the experimental value, $0.68497945\pm0.00000058$.

Finally, we come to the problem of baryon masses. The situation is the same as for the mesons: If flavor $SU(3)$ were a perfect symmetry, all the octet baryons would weigh the same. But they don't. We attribute this in the first instance to the fact that the $s$ quark is more massive than $u$ and $d$. But that can't be the whole story, or the $\Lambda$ would carry the same mass as the $\Sigma$'s and the $\Delta$'s would match the proton. Evidently there is a significant spin-spin (“hyperfine”) contribution, which, as before, we take to be proportional to the dot product of the spins and inversely proportional to the product of the masses. The only difference is that this time there are *three* pairs of spins to contend with:

$$
\mechanical{M}(\text{baryon})=\mechanical{m_1}+\mechanical{m_2}+\mechanical{m_3}
+A'\left[
\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}}{\mechanical{m_1}\mechanical{m_2}}
+\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}}{\mechanical{m_1}\mechanical{m_3}}
+\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}}{\mechanical{m_2}\mechanical{m_3}}
\right].
\tag{5.119}
$$

Here $A'$ [like $A$ in equation (5.99)] is a constant, which we adjust to obtain the optimal fit to the data.

The spin products are easiest when the three quark masses are equal, for

$$
\mechanical{J}^{2}=(\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}+\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}+\mechanical{\mathbf S_3})^{2}
=\mechanical{S_1}^{2}+\mechanical{S_2}^{2}+\mechanical{S_3}^{2}
+2(\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}
+\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}
+\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}),
\tag{5.120}
$$

and hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}
+\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}
+\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}
&=\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{2}[\mechanical{j}(\mechanical{j}+1)-\tfrac{9}{4}]\\
&=\begin{cases}
\tfrac{3}{4}\energy{\hbar}^{2},&\text{for }\mechanical{j}=\tfrac{3}{2}\text{ (decuplet)}\\
-\tfrac{3}{4}\energy{\hbar}^{2},&\text{for }\mechanical{j}=\tfrac{1}{2}\text{ (octet)}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.121}
$$

<!-- PDF page 191; printed page 183 -->

Thus the nucleon (neutron or proton) mass is

$$
\mechanical{M_N}=3\mechanical{m_u}-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{\mechanical{m_u}^{2}}A'
\tag{5.122}
$$

the $\Delta$ is

$$
\mechanical{M_\Delta}=3\mechanical{m_u}+\frac{3}{4}\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{\mechanical{m_u}^{2}}A'
\tag{5.123}
$$

and the $\Omega^{-}$ is

$$
\mechanical{M_\Omega}=3\mechanical{m_s}+\frac{3}{4}\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{\mechanical{m_s}^{2}}A'.
\tag{5.124}
$$

Indeed, in the case of the decuplet the spins are all “parallel” (every pair combines to make spin 1) so

$$
(\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}+\mechanical{\mathbf S_2})^{2}
=\mechanical{S_1}^{2}+\mechanical{S_2}^{2}
+2\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}=2\energy{\hbar}^{2}
\tag{5.125}
$$

(and the same for 1 and 3, or 2 and 3). Hence for the decuplet

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}
=\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}
=\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}
=\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{4}
\tag{5.126}
$$

[which is consistent, notice, with eq. (5.121)], and therefore

$$
\mechanical{M_{\Sigma^{*}}}=2\mechanical{m_u}+\mechanical{m_s}
+\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{4}A'
\left(\frac{1}{\mechanical{m_u}^{2}}+\frac{2}{\mechanical{m_u}\mechanical{m_s}}\right)
\tag{5.127}
$$

while

$$
\mechanical{M_{\Xi^{*}}}=\mechanical{m_u}+2\mechanical{m_s}
+\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{4}A'
\left(\frac{2}{\mechanical{m_u}\mechanical{m_s}}+\frac{1}{\mechanical{m_s}^{2}}\right).
\tag{5.128}
$$

The $\Sigma$ and $\Lambda$ can be done by noting that the up and down quarks combine to make isospin 1 and 0, respectively, and in order for the spin/flavor wave function to be symmetric, under the interchange of $u$ and $d$, the spins must therefore combine to a total of 1 and 0, respectively. For the $\Sigma$'s, then

$$
(\mechanical{\mathbf S_u}+\mechanical{\mathbf S_d})^{2}
=\mechanical{S_u}^{2}+\mechanical{S_d}^{2}
+2\mechanical{\mathbf S_u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_d}=2\energy{\hbar}^{2},
\quad\text{so that }\mechanical{\mathbf S_u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_d}=\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{4}
\tag{5.129}
$$

whereas for the $\Lambda$

$$
(\mechanical{\mathbf S_u}+\mechanical{\mathbf S_d})^{2}=0,
\quad\text{so that }\mechanical{\mathbf S_u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_d}=-\tfrac{3}{4}\energy{\hbar}^{2}.
\tag{5.130}
$$

Using these results together with equation (5.121), we find

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{M_\Sigma}
&=2\mechanical{m_u}+\mechanical{m_s}
+A'\left[
\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf S_u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_d}}{\mechanical{m_u}\mechanical{m_d}}
+\frac{(\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}
+\mechanical{\mathbf S_1}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}
+\mechanical{\mathbf S_2}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_3}
-\mechanical{\mathbf S_u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S_d})}{\mechanical{m_u}\mechanical{m_s}}\right]\\
&=2\mechanical{m_u}+\mechanical{m_s}
+\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{4}A'
\left(\frac{1}{\mechanical{m_u}^{2}}-\frac{4}{\mechanical{m_u}\mechanical{m_s}}\right)
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.131}
$$

I'll let you figure out the mass of the $\Xi$'s (Problem 5.32):

$$
\mechanical{M_\Xi}=2\mechanical{m_s}+\mechanical{m_u}
+\frac{\energy{\hbar}^{2}}{4}A'
\left(\frac{1}{\mechanical{m_s}^{2}}-\frac{4}{\mechanical{m_u}\mechanical{m_s}}\right).
\tag{5.133}
$$

Using the constituent quark masses in Table 4.4, and picking $A'=(2\mechanical{m_u}/\energy{\hbar})^{2}\cdot50\,\unit{\mathrm{MeV}}/\mechanical{c}^{2}$, we obtain an excellent fit to the experimental data (Table 5.6).

[^ep-5-10-measured]: No decuplet moments have been measured, so I won't bother with them. As for the mesons, the pseudoscalars have no spin, and hence have zero magnetic moment. The vector mesons do have magnetic moments, but as far as I know they have not been measured.
[^ep-5-10-radiative]: This ignores radiative corrections, which should be larger for quarks than for electrons, but still small relative to the total magnetic moment.
[^ep-5-10-normalized]: Note that everything is normalized, so that for instance $\langle u(\uparrow)\lvert u(\uparrow)\rangle=1$, and the states are orthogonal $\langle u(\uparrow)\lvert u(\downarrow)\rangle=0$.
