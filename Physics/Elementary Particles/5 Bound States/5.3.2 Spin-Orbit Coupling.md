---
title: "Spin-Orbit Coupling"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Spin-orbit coupling links the electron's magnetic moment to its orbital field; combined with the relativistic correction, it splits hydrogen levels by total angular momentum.

<!-- PDF page 161; printed page 153 -->

From the electron's point of view it is the *proton* that circles around, and this orbital motion creates a magnetic field at the center given by[^ep-5-3-2-orbit-field]

$$
\magnetic{B}=\frac{\electric{e}\mechanical{v}}{\mechanical{c}\mechanical{r}^{2}}
\tag{5.43}
$$

or, in terms of the electron's orbital angular momentum, $\mechanical{L}=\mechanical{r}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}$:

$$
\magnetic{\mathbf B}=\frac{\electric{e}}{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}\mechanical{r}^{3}}\mechanical{\mathbf L}
\tag{5.44}
$$

The spinning electron constitutes a tiny magnetic dipole, with dipole moment[^ep-5-3-2-gyromagnetic]

$$
\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}=-\frac{\electric{e}}{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}\mechanical{\mathbf S}
\tag{5.45}
$$

From classical electrodynamics we know that the energy of a magnetic dipole $\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}$ in the presence of a magnetic field $\magnetic{\mathbf B}$ is<sup>7</sup>

$$
\energy{W}=-\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}
\tag{5.46}
$$

But the electron is not in an inertial frame;[^ep-5-3-2-torque] to make all this rigorous we should really speak of the “instantaneously comoving frame” of the electron, the inertial system whose velocity coincides with that of the electron at a given moment. Following the motion of the electron, then, involves a continuous succession of infinitesimal Lorentz transformations, as we step from one comoving frame to the next. This procedure leads to the so-called *Thomas precession*,<sup>8</sup> which in the present context simply introduces a factor of $\tfrac{1}{2}$. The Hamiltonian for spin-orbit coupling is thus

$$
\energy{\Delta H_{\mathrm{so}}}
=\frac{\electric{e}^{2}}{2\mechanical{m}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}\mechanical{r}^{3}}
(\mechanical{\mathbf L}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S})
\tag{5.47}
$$

[^ep-5-3-2-orbit-field]: You can get this most simply by looking up the formula for the magnetic field at the center of a circular ring, $\magnetic{B}=2\pi\electric{I}/\mechanical{r}\mechanical{c}$, and using $\electric{I}=\electric{e}/\mechanical{t}$ for the “proton current,” where $\mechanical{t}=2\pi\mechanical{r}/\mechanical{v}$ is the time it takes to complete one revolution. Alternatively, exploit the fact that if $\magnetic{\mathbf B}=0$ in one inertial system (the proton's), then $\magnetic{\mathbf B}=-\mechanical{\mathbf v}/\mechanical{c}\times\electric{\mathbf E}$ in a system moving with velocity $\mechanical{\mathbf v}$; in this case $\electric{\mathbf E}$ is the field of the proton, $\electric{e}/\mechanical{r}^{2}$.

[^ep-5-3-2-gyromagnetic]: The proportionality factor between $\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}$ and $\mechanical{\mathbf S}$ is known as the *gyromagnetic ratio*. Classically, it should have the value $-\electric{e}/2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}$, and this is correct for *orbital* angular momentum. But for relativistic reasons spin is “twice as effective as it ought to be” in producing a magnetic dipole. One of the major successes of Dirac's original theory of the electron was its explanation of this extra 2. As it happens, however, even *this* is not quite right; there are minute corrections introduced by quantum electrodynamics that were first calculated by Schwinger in the late forties. By now both experimental and theoretical determinations of the *anomalous magnetic moment* of the electron have been carried out to fantastic precision, and stunning agreement. The Particle Data Booklet lists 13 significant digits! For our present purpose, though, the magnetic moment of the electron is $\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}/2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}$, the *Bohr magneton*.

[^ep-5-3-2-torque]: One can, of course, analyze the whole problem in the rest frame of the proton. From this perspective there is *no* magnetic field, and spin-orbit coupling is attributable to the torque $\mechanical{\boldsymbol\tau}$ on a magnetic dipole that moves with velocity $\mechanical{\mathbf v}$ through an electric field $\electric{\mathbf E}$: $\mechanical{\boldsymbol\tau}=-(1/\mechanical{c})\magnetic{\mathbf m}\times(\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\electric{\mathbf E})$.

<!-- PDF page 162; printed page 154 -->

In the presence of spin-orbit coupling neither $\mechanical{\mathbf L}$ nor $\mechanical{\mathbf S}$ is separately conserved; the conserved quantity is the *total* angular momentum, $\mechanical{\mathbf J}=\mechanical{\mathbf L}+\mechanical{\mathbf S}$.<sup>9</sup> Squaring, we have

$$
\mechanical{J}^{2}=\mechanical{L}^{2}+\mechanical{S}^{2}+2\mechanical{\mathbf L}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S}
\tag{5.48}
$$

and hence, using expressions (4.2) and (4.6), we have

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf L}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S}
=\tfrac{1}{2}\energy{\hbar}^{2}
[\mechanical{j}(\mechanical{j}+1)-\mechanical{l}(\mechanical{l}+1)-\mechanical{s}(\mechanical{s}+1)]
\tag{5.49}
$$

where $\mechanical{j},\mechanical{l}$, and $\mechanical{s}$ are the quantum numbers describing total, orbital, and spin angular momenta, respectively (for the electron, $\mechanical{s}=\tfrac{1}{2}$, of course). This time we need the expectation value of $\mechanical{r}^{-3}$:[^ep-5-3-2-zero-l]

$$
\left\langle\frac{1}{\mechanical{r}^{3}}\right\rangle
=\frac{1}{\mechanical{l}(\mechanical{l}+\tfrac{1}{2})(\mechanical{l}+1)\mechanical{n}^{3}\mechanical{a}^{3}}
\tag{5.50}
$$

Putting this into equation (5.35), we find

$$
\energy{\Delta E_{\mathrm{so}}}
=\electric{\alpha}^{4}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}
\frac{[\mechanical{j}(\mechanical{j}+1)-\mechanical{l}(\mechanical{l}+1)-\tfrac{3}{4}]}
{4\mechanical{n}^{3}\mechanical{l}(\mechanical{l}+\tfrac{1}{2})(\mechanical{l}+1)}
\tag{5.51}
$$

Notice that this is of the same order as the relativistic correction, $\electric{\alpha}^{4}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}$, even though the physical agencies involved are (in this treatment) entirely different. The combination of the two effects, equations (5.42) and (5.51), yields the total fine structure for hydrogen. Using the fact that $\mechanical{j}$ can only be $\mechanical{l}+\tfrac{1}{2}$ or $\mechanical{l}-\tfrac{1}{2}$ [eq. (4.12)], we obtain (Problem 5.9)

$$
\energy{\Delta E_{\mathrm{fs}}}
=-\electric{\alpha}^{4}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}
\frac{1}{4\mechanical{n}^{2}}
\left(\frac{2\mechanical{n}}{(\mechanical{j}+\tfrac{1}{2})}-\frac{3}{2}\right)
\tag{5.52}
$$

Curiously, this formula is identical to that for the relativistic correction alone, equation (5.42), except that $\mechanical{l}$ is replaced by $\mechanical{j}$. The energies are all depressed ($\energy{\Delta E_{\mathrm{fs}}}$ is negative). Since $\mechanical{l}$ can take on any integer value from 0 to $\mechanical{n}-1$, $\mechanical{j}$ can be any half-integer from $\tfrac{1}{2}$ to $\mechanical{n}-\tfrac{1}{2}$, so the $\mechanical{n}$th energy level, $\energy{E_n}$, splits into $\mechanical{n}$ sublevels (see Fig. 5.2).

[^ep-5-3-2-zero-l]: See reference 6. When $\mechanical{l}=0$, $\langle\mechanical{r}^{-3}\rangle$ blows up. Really, this case should be handled separately. Fortunately, the $\mechanical{l}$ cancels out, and our final result, equation (5.52), is correct even when $\mechanical{l}=0$. The spin-orbit coupling by itself is zero, of course, when the orbital angular momentum vanishes, but there is a compensating correction to the relativistic part of the fine structure, having to do with the non-Hermiticity of $\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{4}$ when $\mechanical{l}=0$.

**Fig. 5.2** Fine structure in hydrogen. The nth Bohr level (fine line) splits into n sublevels (dashed lines), characterized by j = ½, ³⁄₂, …, (n − ½). Except for the last of these, two different values of l contribute to each level: l = j − ½ and l = j + ½. Spectroscopists' nomenclature—S for l = 0, P for l = 1, D for l = 2, F for l = 3—is indicated. All levels are shifted downward, as shown (the diagram is not to scale, however).
