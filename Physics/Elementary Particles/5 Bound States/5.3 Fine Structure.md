> [!TLDR]
> Fine structure combines relativistic and spin-orbit effects; perturbation theory estimates each energy shift from an expectation value.

<!-- PDF page 159; printed page 151 -->

As the precision of experimental spectroscopy improved, small departures from the Rydberg formula were detected. Spectral lines were resolved into doublets, triplets, and even larger families of closely spaced peaks. This *fine structure* is actually attributable to two distinct mechanisms: one a small relativistic correction, and the other a magnetic coupling between the electron's spin and its orbital motion. We shall analyze the two effects separately, and then combine them.

But first a word about perturbation theory. Suppose we have solved the Schrödinger equation for some Hamiltonian $\energy{H}$, in the sense that we know the allowed energies $\energy{E_j}$ and the corresponding wave functions $\signal{\psi_j}$ ($j$ being whatever collection of indices is used to label the states; in the case of hydrogen, it stands for $\mechanical{n},\mechanical{l}$, and $\mechanical{m_l}$). Now suppose we change the Hamiltonian slightly, adding a small *perturbation* $\energy{\Delta H}$, so that the new Hamiltonian is $\energy{H}+\energy{\Delta H}$. This will presumably displace the energy levels a bit, and we may ask the question: By how much, $\energy{\Delta E_j}$, is the $j$th energy level shifted? The answer, to good approximation, is that $\energy{\Delta E_j}$ is the *average* value of $\energy{\Delta H}$, as computed in the state $\signal{\psi_j}$ (see Problem 5.8):[^ep-5-3-perturbation]

$$
\energy{\Delta E_j}=\langle\energy{\Delta H}\rangle_j
=\int\signal{\psi_j}^*(\energy{\Delta H})\signal{\psi_j}\,d^3\mechanical{x}
\tag{5.35}
$$

[^ep-5-3-perturbation]: I think this is a reasonably plausible result, but if you want to see it derived, look in any introductory quantum text (such as those in ref. 1) under “perturbation theory.”

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{H},\energy{\Delta H}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{J}}$ | Hamiltonian and perturbation | The original energy operator and the small added energy term. |
| $\energy{E_j},\energy{\Delta E_j}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{J}}$ | Energy and level shift | The original allowed energy and its first-order change. |
| $\signal{\psi_j}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m^{-3/2}}}$ | State wave function | The normalized spatial amplitude used for the expectation value. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Spatial coordinate | The position coordinate in the volume integral. |
