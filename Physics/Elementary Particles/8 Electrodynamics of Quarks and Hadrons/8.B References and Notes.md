> [!TLDR]
> These references give the scattering sources and define alternative proton form-factor conventions.

<!-- PDF page 285; printed page 277 -->

1. M. N. Rosenbluth, *Phys. Rev.* **79**, 615 (1950).

2. See, for example, H. Frauenfelder and E. M. Henley, *Subatomic Physics*, (Englewood Cliffs, N.J.: Prentice-Hall, 1974), Chapter 6. I should warn you that this subject is a notational nightmare in the literature. There are only two variables in the problem—the incident electron energy ($\energy{E}$) and the scattering angle ($\mechanical{\theta}$)—but it is common to encounter a more or less random mixture of $\energy{E}$, $\energy{E'}$, $\mechanical{\theta}$, $\mechanical{q}^2$, $\mechanical{Q}^2\equiv-\mechanical{q}^2$, $\tau\equiv-\mechanical{q}^2/(4\mechanical{M}^2\mechanical{c}^2)$, $\mechanical{\nu}\equiv\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q}/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{c})$, $\omega\equiv-2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q}/\mechanical{q}^2$, $\mechanical{W}=\sqrt{(\mechanical{q}+\mechanical{p})^2}$, $x\equiv-\mechanical{q}^2/(2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q})$, and $y\equiv\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q}/(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{p_1})$. Moreover, although there are only two independent form factors involved, there are many different ways to express them. Some authors favor $F_1$ and $F_2$, with $K_1=-\mechanical{q}^2(F_1+\magnetic{K}F_2)^2$ and $K_2=(2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{c})^2F_1^2-\magnetic{K}^2\mechanical{q}^2F_2^2$ ($\magnetic{K}=1.7928$ is the “anomalous” contribution to the proton's magnetic moment); others prefer $\electric{G_E}=F_1-\magnetic{K}\tau F_2$, $\magnetic{G_M}=F_1+\magnetic{K}F_2$. (The latter are related to the Fourier transforms of the charge and magnetic moment distributions, respectively; see F. Halzen and A. D. Martin, *Quarks and Leptons* (New York: Wiley, 1984), Sect. 8.2.) Anyone can play this game; $K_1$, and $K_2$ are my own contributions.

3. J. D. Bjorken, *Phys. Rev.* **179**, 1547 (1969); *Phys. Rev.* **163**, 1767 (1967). (Bjorken's functions $F_1$ and $F_2$ have no relation to the elastic form factors $F_1$ and $F_2$ of ref. 2. Sorry about that.)

4. C. G. Callan and D. Gross, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **22**, 156 (1969).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{E},\energy{E'}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{erg}}$ | Electron energies | The incident and scattered electron energies. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{rad}}$ | Scattering angle | The angle between the electron directions. |
| $\mechanical{q},\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p_1},\mechanical{W},\mechanical{\nu}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g\,cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Momentum variables | The momenta and momentum-scale invariant variables as printed. |
| $\mechanical{Q}^2$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g^2\,cm^2\,s^{-2}}}$ | Squared momentum transfer | The positive quantity equal to minus the transferred four-momentum squared. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g}}$ | Proton mass | The mass in the alternative kinematic variables. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Speed of light | The speed in the invariant normalization factors. |
| $\tau,\omega,x,y$ | $1$ | Kinematic ratios | The alternative dimensionless scattering variables. |
| $K_1,K_2$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g^2\,cm^2\,s^{-2}}}$ | Elastic response coefficients | The author's form factors for the proton response tensor. |
| $F_1,F_2,\electric{G_E},\magnetic{G_M}$ | $1$ | Elastic form factors | The alternative electric and magnetic response functions in reference 2. |
| $F_1,F_2$ | $1$ | Bjorken scaling functions | The distinct inelastic functions named in reference 3. |
| $\magnetic{K}$ | $1$ | Anomalous magnetic moment factor | The proton magnetic normalization contribution of 1.7928. |
