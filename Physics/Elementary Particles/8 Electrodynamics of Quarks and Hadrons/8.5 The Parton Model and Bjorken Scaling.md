> [!TLDR]
> The parton model gives Bjorken scaling and the Callan–Gross relation for spin-one-half quark constituents.

<!-- PDF page 277; printed page 269 -->

In the late sixties, Bjorken predicted that at very high energy the dependence of the inelastic structure functions on $\mechanical{q}^2$ fades away, and they become functions of $x$ alone. More precisely, he suggested that<sup>3</sup>

Here the quantity $-(\mechanical{q}^2/2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{c}^2x)W_2(\mechanical{q}^2,x)$ is plotted against $-\mechanical{q}^2$ (in $(\unit{\mathrm{GeV}}/\mechanical{c})^2$), for $x=0.25$. (Source: J. I. Friedman and H. W. Kendall; reproduced, with permission, from the Annual Review of Nuclear and Particle Science, Volume 22, © 1972 by Annual Reviews Inc.: page 227.)]]

$$
\mechanical{M}W_1(\mechanical{q}^2,x)\to F_1(x)
\tag{8.37}
$$

$$
\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{c}^2x}
W_2(\mechanical{q}^2,x)\to F_2(x)
\tag{8.38}
$$

in the so-called “deep inelastic scattering” regime, where $-\mechanical{q}^2[=(4\energy{E}\energy{E'}/\mechanical{c}^2)\times\sin^2(\mechanical{\theta}/2)]$ and $\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{p}[=\mechanical{M}(\energy{E}-\energy{E'})]$ are both large, but their *ratio* ($2x=-\mechanical{q}^2/\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{p}$) is *not*.[^ep-8-5-scaling] This behavior is known as “scaling,” and it was dramatically confirmed by experiments at SLAC in the early seventies (Fig. 8.6). As we shall see in a moment, scaling is a consequence of the fact that the proton is made of pointlike constituents (“partons”—hideous term—they were called in those days, when it was unfashionable to take quarks and gluons too seriously). In 1969, Callan and Gross<sup>4</sup> suggested that Bjorken's scaling functions are related:

$$
2xF_1(x)=F_2(x)
\tag{8.39}
$$

This, too, has been confirmed experimentally (Fig. 8.7). The Callan–Gross relation reflects the fact that the charged constituents of the proton carry spin $\frac12$ (for spin 0 one predicts $2xF_1/F_2=0$, instead of 1, and this is clearly inconsistent with the data). The experimental verification of Bjorken scaling and the Callan–Gross relation in deep inelastic scattering provided the first compelling evidence for the existence of quarks.

There are several ways to derive equations (8.37), (8.38), and (8.39), but the crucial point is that *at high energies the virtual photon interacts with a single essentially free quark*.[^ep-8-5-wavelength] We can treat this scattering, therefore, using the old

electron-muon results. According to equations (8.35) and (8.36), the structure functions for scattering off a quark of flavor $i$ are

$$
W_1^i=\frac{\electric{Q_i}^2}{2\mechanical{m_i}}\delta(x_i-1),
\qquad
W_2^i=-\frac{2\mechanical{m_i}\mechanical{c}\electric{Q_i}^2}
{\mechanical{q}^2}\delta(x_i-1)
\tag{8.40}
$$

Here $\mechanical{m_i}$ is the mass of the quark and

$$
x_i=-\frac{\mechanical{q}^2}{2\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{p_i}}
\tag{8.41}
$$

<!-- PDF page 280; printed page 272 -->

where $\mechanical{p_i}$ is the quark's momentum. As before, $\electric{Q_i}$ is the quark charge, in units of $\electric{e}$ ($\frac23$ for the $u$, $-\frac13$ for $d$ and $s$); we include a factor of $\electric{Q_i}^2$ in $W^i$ so that we don't have to touch the rest of the cross-section formula (8.33). The trouble is, although the proton momentum is $\mechanical{p}$, we do not know the momenta of the individual quarks. Let us suppose that $z_i$ is the *fraction* of the total momentum carried by quark $i$, so that

$$
\mechanical{p_i}=z_i\mechanical{p}
\tag{8.42}
$$

[This may sound reasonable, but it is actually a pretty slippery proposition, for it assumes that each component $\mechanical{p_i^\mu}$ gets the same fraction of $\mechanical{p^\mu}$. There is no room here for the quarks to move around within the proton; if the proton is at rest, so too are all the quarks. In particular, it follows (since $\mechanical{p_i}^2=\mechanical{m_i}^2\mechanical{c}^2$ and $\mechanical{p}^2=\mechanical{M}^2\mechanical{c}^2$) that

$$
\mechanical{m_i}=z_i\mechanical{M}
\tag{8.43}
$$

which implies that if $z_i$ is variable, so too is the quark mass![^ep-8-5-momentum-frame]] Equation (8.42) implies that

$$
x_i=\frac{x}{z_i}
\tag{8.44}
$$

and hence

$$
W_1^i=\frac{\electric{Q_i}^2}{2\mechanical{M}}\delta(x-z_i),
\qquad
W_2^i=-\frac{2x^2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{c}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}
\electric{Q_i}^2\delta(x-z_i).
\tag{8.45}
$$

Finally, let $f_i(z_i)$ be the *probability* that the $i$th quark carries momentum fraction $z_i$. Integrating over $z_i$, and summing over all the quarks in the proton, we conclude that

$$
W_1=\sum_i\int_0^1\frac{\electric{Q_i}^2}{2\mechanical{M}}
\delta(x-z_i)f_i(z_i)\,dz_i
=\frac{1}{2\mechanical{M}}\sum_i\electric{Q_i}^2f_i(x)
\tag{8.46}
$$

$$
W_2=\sum_i\int_0^1
\left(-\frac{2x^2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{c}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right)
\electric{Q_i}^2\delta(x-z_i)f_i(z_i)\,dz_i
=-\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{c}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}
x^2\sum_i\electric{Q_i}^2f_i(x)
\tag{8.47}
$$

Thus

$$
\mechanical{M}W_1=\frac12\sum_i\electric{Q_i}^2f_i(x)=F_1(x)
\tag{8.48}
$$

$$
-\frac{\mechanical{q}^2}{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{c}^2x}
W_2=x\sum_i\electric{Q_i}^2f_i(x)=F_2(x)
\tag{8.49}
$$

confirming the Bjorken scaling law. Comparing the two expressions, we obtain

$$
F_2(x)=2xF_1(x)
\tag{8.50}
$$

which is the Callan–Gross relation.

The importance of Bjorken scaling and the Callan–Gross formula lies in the fact that they provide for a clear experimental test of the quark-parton model,

<!-- PDF page 281; printed page 273 -->

a test that was passed with flying colors in SLAC's deep inelastic scattering experiments. Protons *really do* have charged constituents, and those constituents *really do* behave as pointlike Dirac particles, carrying spin $\frac12$. One modest dividend is a radical simplification in the analysis of high-energy electron-proton scattering, for in place of two unknown functions of two variables [$W_1(\mechanical{q}^2,x)$ and $W_2(\mechanical{q}^2,x)$] we now have only one unknown function of one variable to contend with [$F_1(x)$]. If we put equations (8.37), (8.38), and (8.39) back into (8.33), we find

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\energy{E'}\,d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{F_1(x)}{2\mechanical{M}}
\left(\frac{\electric{\alpha}\energy{\hbar}}
{\energy{E}\sin(\mechanical{\theta}/2)}\right)^2
\left[1+\frac{2\energy{E}\energy{E'}}{(\energy{E}-\energy{E'})^2}
\cos^2\frac{\mechanical{\theta}}2\right]
\tag{8.51}
$$

Moreover, equation (8.48) shows that to finish the job we need to know the probability functions $f_i(x)$, for then

$$
F_1(x)=\frac12\sum_i\electric{Q_i}^2f_i(x)
\tag{8.52}
$$

We consider this problem in the following section.

[^ep-8-5-scaling]: Experimentally, Bjorken scaling sets in for $-\mechanical{q}^2\gtrsim1\,(\unit{\mathrm{GeV}}/\mechanical{c})^2$ and $(\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{p})\gtrsim3.5\,(\unit{\mathrm{GeV}}/\mechanical{c})^2$. Notice that $x$ is restricted to the range $0\le x\le1$ (see Problem 8.13).

[^ep-8-5-wavelength]: At low energies (long wavelengths) the photon “sees” the whole proton as a simple point charge—that gives Mott scattering. At high energies (short wavelengths) the photon “sees” a single quark—this gives Bjorken scaling and the Callan–Gross relation. At intermediate energies the photon “sees” the proton in all its complex structure—this will clearly be the hardest case to analyse.

[^ep-8-5-momentum-frame]: More rigorous derivations of the scaling equations avoid this problem by working in the “infinite momentum frame,” in which the proton energy is so much greater than its mass that the proton and the quarks can be treated as massless particles, and (8.43) is trivially satisfied.
