> [!TLDR]
> Local color SU(3) invariance couples three quark color states to eight massless gluon gauge fields.

<!-- PDF page 363; printed page 355 -->

According to the colored quark model, each flavor of quark comes in three colors—red, blue, and green. Although the various *flavors* carry different masses (Table 4.4), the three *colors* of a given flavor are all supposed to weigh the same. Thus the free Lagrangian for a particular flavor reads

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal L}
&=[i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\signal{\bar\psi_r}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi_r}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2\signal{\bar\psi_r}\signal{\psi_r}]
+[i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\signal{\bar\psi_b}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi_b}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2\signal{\bar\psi_b}\signal{\psi_b}]\\
&\quad+[i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\signal{\bar\psi_g}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi_g}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2\signal{\bar\psi_g}\signal{\psi_g}]
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.70}
$$

As before, we can simplify the notation by introducing

$$
\signal{\psi}\equiv
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\psi_r}\\\signal{\psi_b}\\\signal{\psi_g}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\signal{\bar\psi}=(\signal{\bar\psi_r}\,\signal{\bar\psi_b}\,\signal{\bar\psi_g})
\tag{11.71}
$$

so that

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2\signal{\bar\psi}\signal{\psi}
\tag{11.72}
$$

This looks just like the original Dirac Lagrangian, only now $\signal{\psi}$ stands for a three-component column vector (each element of which is itself a four-component Dirac spinor). Just as the one-particle Dirac Lagrangian (11.14) has (global) $U(1)$ phase invariance, and the (equal mass) two-particle Lagrangian (11.41) admits $U(2)$ invariance, so this (equal mass) three-particle Lagrangian exhibits $U(3)$ symmetry. That is to say, it is invariant under transformations of the form

$$
\signal{\psi}\to U\signal{\psi}
\qquad(\signal{\bar\psi}\to\signal{\bar\psi}U^\dagger)
\tag{11.73}
$$

where $U$ is any unitary $3\times3$ matrix:

$$
U^\dagger U=1
\tag{11.74}
$$

But remember [eq. (11.50)], any unitary matrix can be written as an exponentiated Hermitian matrix:

$$
U=e^{iH},\qquad\text{with }H^\dagger=H
\tag{11.75}
$$

Moreover, any $3\times3$ Hermitian matrix can be expressed in terms of nine real numbers, $a_1$, $a_2$, . . . , $a_8$, and $\mechanical{\theta}$ (Problem 11.16):

$$
H=\mechanical{\theta}1+\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\mathbf a
\tag{11.76}
$$

where 1 is the $3\times3$ unit matrix, $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\ldots,\lambda_8$ are the Gell-Mann matrices [eq. (9.9)], and the dot product now denotes a sum from 1 to 8:

$$
\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\mathbf a
\equiv\lambda_1a_1+\lambda_2a_2+\cdots+\lambda_8a_8
\tag{11.77}
$$

<!-- PDF page 364; printed page 356 -->

Thus

$$
U=e^{i\mechanical{\theta}}e^{i\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\mathbf a}
\tag{11.78}
$$

We have already explored *phase* transformations $(e^{i\mechanical{\theta}})$; what is *new* is the second term. The matrix $e^{i\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\mathbf a}$ has determinant 1 (see Problem 11.17); it belongs to the group $SU(3)$.[^ep-11-5-u3] So what we are interested in is the invariance of the Lagrangian (11.72) under $SU(3)$ gauge transformations, a global symmetry that we now propose to make local.

That is: we modify $\energy{\mathcal L}$ in such a way as to render it invariant under *local* $SU(3)$ gauge transformations:

$$
\signal{\psi}\to S\signal{\psi},\qquad
\text{where }S\equiv e^{-iq\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\boldsymbol\phi(\mechanical{x})/\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}
\tag{11.79}
$$

(again, I let $\boldsymbol\phi\equiv-(\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}/q)\mathbf a$, with the coupling constant $q$ playing a role analogous to electric charge in QED). As always, the trick is to replace the ordinary derivative, $\partial_\mu$, by the “covariant derivative” $\mathcal D_\mu$:

$$
\mathcal D_\mu\equiv\partial_\mu+i\frac{q}{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}
\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}
\tag{11.80}
$$

and assign to the gauge fields $\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}$ (there are *eight* of them, notice) a transformation rule such that

$$
\mathcal D_\mu\signal{\psi}\to S(\mathcal D_\mu\signal{\psi})
\tag{11.81}
$$

Again [see eq. (11.58)], this entails

$$
\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\signal{\mathbf A'_\mu}
=S(\boldsymbol\lambda\cdot\signal{\mathbf A_\mu})S^{-1}
+i\left(\frac{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}q\right)(\partial_\mu S)S^{-1}
\tag{11.82}
$$

which, for the infinitesimal case, yields a formula identical to expression (11.61)

$$
\signal{\mathbf A'_\mu}\cong\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}+\partial_\mu\boldsymbol\phi
+\frac{2q}{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}(\boldsymbol\phi\times\signal{\mathbf A_\mu})
\tag{11.83}
$$

However, this time the cross-product notation is shorthand for

$$
(\mathbf B\times\mathbf C)_i=\sum_{j,k=1}^{8}f_{ijk}B_jC_k
\tag{11.84}
$$

where $f_{ijk}$ are the structure constants of $SU(3)$ [eq. (9.10)], analogous to $\epsilon_{ijk}$ for $SU(2)$. (See Problem 11.18.)

The modified Lagrangian

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal L}
&=i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\mathcal D_\mu\signal{\psi}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2\signal{\bar\psi}\signal{\psi}\\
&=[i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2\signal{\bar\psi}\signal{\psi}]
-(q\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\boldsymbol\lambda\signal{\psi})\cdot\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.85}
$$

is invariant under local $SU(3)$ gauge transformations [(11.79) and (11.82)] but as usual the cost is the introduction of gauge fields $\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}$ (eight of them, this time). In particle language, these correspond to the eight gluons, just as the $U(1)$ gauge field in Weyl's theory represents the photon.[^ep-11-5-ninth] To finish the job we must adjoin the free gluon Lagrangian

[^ep-11-5-u3]: In the language of group theory, we have shown that $U(3)=U(1)\otimes SU(3)$.

[^ep-11-5-ninth]: Remember that a “ninth gluon,” coupling universally to all quarks, is apparently excluded by experiment (see Problem 9.1).

$$
\energy{\mathcal L_{\text{gluons}}}
=-\frac1{16\pi}\signal{\mathbf F^{\mu\nu}}\cdot\signal{\mathbf F_{\mu\nu}}
\tag{11.86}
$$

where, as in the Yang–Mills case

$$
\signal{\mathbf F^{\mu\nu}}
\equiv\partial^\mu\signal{\mathbf A^\nu}-\partial^\nu\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}
-\frac{2q}{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}(\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}\times\signal{\mathbf A^\nu})
\tag{11.87}
$$

[with the $SU(3)$ “cross-product” defined by equation (11.84)].

*Conclusion:* The complete Lagrangian for chromodynamics is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=[i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2\signal{\bar\psi}\signal{\psi}]
-\frac1{16\pi}\signal{\mathbf F^{\mu\nu}}\cdot\signal{\mathbf F_{\mu\nu}}
-(q\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\boldsymbol\lambda\signal{\psi})\cdot\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}
\tag{11.88}
$$

Of course, we need six replicas of equation (11.88), each with the appropriate mass, to handle the six quark flavors. $\energy{\mathcal L}$ is invariant under local $SU(3)$ gauge transformations, and describes three equal mass Dirac fields (the three colors of a given quark flavor) in interaction with eight massless vector fields (the gluons). It derives from the requirement that the *global* $SU(3)$ symmetry of the original Lagrangian (11.70) should hold *locally*. The Dirac fields constitute eight color *currents*

$$
\mathbf J^\mu\equiv\mechanical{c}q
(\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\boldsymbol\lambda\signal{\psi})
\tag{11.89}
$$

which act as *sources* for the color fields $(\signal{\mathbf A_\mu})$, in the same way that *electric* currents act as sources for the *electromagnetic* field. The theory described here is very close in structure to that of Yang and Mills. In this case, however, we believe it to be the correct description of a phenomenon realized in nature: the strong interaction.
