> [!TLDR]
> Free field equations determine propagators, and interaction terms determine the fields and factors at each vertex.

<!-- PDF page 365; printed page 357 -->

Up to this point the Lagrangians we have considered might just as well describe *classical* fields as *quantum* ones; indeed, the Maxwell Lagrangian will be found in any textbook on classical electrodynamics. The passage from a classical field theory to the corresponding quantum field theory does not involve modification of the Lagrangian or the field equations, but rather a *reinterpretation* of the field variables; the fields are “quantized,” and particles emerge as quanta of the associated fields. Thus the photon is the quantum of the electrodynamic field, $\electric{A^\mu}$; leptons and quarks are quanta of Dirac fields; gluons are quanta of the eight $SU(3)$ gauge fields; and $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ are quanta of the appropriate Proca fields. The quantization procedure itself is recondite, and this is not the place to go into it;<sup>8</sup> for our purposes the essential point is that *each Lagrangian determines a particular set of Feynman rules*. What we need, then, is a prescription for working out the Feynman rules dictated by a given Lagrangian.

To begin with, notice that $\energy{\mathcal L}$ consists of two kinds of terms: the free Lagrangian for each participating field, plus various interaction terms $(\energy{\mathcal L_{\text{int}}})$. The former—Klein–Gordon, for spin 0; Dirac, for spin $\frac12$; Proca, for spin 1; or something more exotic, for a theory with higher spin—determines the *propagator*;

<!-- PDF page 366; printed page 358 -->

the latter—obtained by invoking local gauge invariance, or by some other means—determine the *vertex factors*:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Free Lagrangian}&\implies\text{propagator}\\
\text{Interaction terms}&\implies\text{vertex factors}
\end{aligned}
$$

Let us consider the propagators first.

Application of the Euler–Lagrange equation to the free Lagrangian yields the free field equations:

$$
\left[\partial^\mu\partial_\mu+
\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}{\energy{\hbar}}\right)^2\right]\signal{\phi}=0
\qquad\text{(Klein–Gordon, for spin 0)}
\tag{11.13}
$$

$$
\left[i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-
\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}{\energy{\hbar}}\right)\right]\signal{\psi}=0
\qquad\text{(Dirac, for spin }\tfrac12\text{)}
\tag{11.15}
$$

$$
\left[\partial_\mu(\partial^\mu\electric{A^\nu}-\partial^\nu\electric{A^\mu})
+\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}{\energy{\hbar}}\right)^2\electric{A^\nu}\right]=0
\qquad\text{(Proca, for spin 1)}
\tag{11.22}
$$

The corresponding “momentum-space” equations are obtained by the standard prescription [eq. (7.5)] $\mechanical{p_\mu}\leftrightarrow i\energy{\hbar}\partial_\mu$:

$$
[\mechanical{p}^2-(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2]\signal{\phi}=0
\tag{11.90}
$$

$$
[\not{\mechanical{p}}-(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})]\signal{\psi}=0
\tag{11.91}
$$

$$
[(-\mechanical{p}^2+(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2)g_{\mu\nu}
+\mechanical{p_\mu}\mechanical{p_\nu}]\electric{A^\nu}=0
\tag{11.92}
$$

The propagator is simply ($i$ times) the *inverse* of the factor in square brackets:

$$
\text{Spin-0 propagator: }\frac{i}{\mechanical{p}^2-(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2}
\tag{11.93}
$$

$$
\text{Spin-}\tfrac12\text{ propagator: }
\frac{i}{\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}
=i\frac{(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})}
{\mechanical{p}^2-(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2}
\tag{11.94}
$$

$$
\text{Spin-1 propagator: }
\frac{-i}{\mechanical{p}^2-(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2}
\left[g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\mechanical{p_\mu}\mechanical{p_\nu}}{(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2}\right]
\tag{11.95}
$$

> [!note]
> Note that in the second case this factor is a $4\times4$ matrix, and we want the *matrix* inverse; in the third case the factor is a second-rank tensor $(T_{\mu\nu})$, and we want the *tensor* inverse $(T^{-1})_{\mu\nu}$, such that $T_{\mu\lambda}(T^{-1})^{\lambda\nu}=\delta_\mu^\nu$. (See Problem 11.19.)

These are precisely the propagators we used in Chapters 6, 7, and 10.[^ep-11-6-normalization] Since we obviously cannot set $\mechanical{m}\to0$ in the Proca propagator (11.95), we must go back to the free field equation (11.22) to work out the photon propagator:

$$
\partial_\mu(\partial^\mu\electric{A^\nu}-\partial^\nu\electric{A^\mu})=0
\qquad\text{(Maxwell, for massless spin 1)}
\tag{11.96}
$$

[^ep-11-6-normalization]: Actually, this procedure only determines the propagator up to a multiplicative constant, since the field equations, (11.90), (11.91), and (11.92), can always be multiplied by such a factor. In the “canonical” form of these equations the coefficient of $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}$ or $(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2$ is taken to be $\pm1$, with the sign matching that of the mass term in $\energy{\mathcal L}$. Other conventions lead to a slightly different set of Feynman rules, but do not, of course, change the calculated reaction amplitudes.

<!-- PDF page 367; printed page 359 -->

As I have remarked before, this equation does not uniquely determine $\electric{A^\mu}$; if we impose the Lorentz condition

$$
\partial_\mu\electric{A^\mu}=0
\tag{7.82}
$$

then (11.96) reduces to

$$
\partial^2\electric{A^\nu}=0
\tag{11.97}
$$

which, in momentum space, can be written

$$
(-\mechanical{p}^2g_{\mu\nu})\electric{A^\nu}=0
\tag{11.98}
$$

So the photon propagator is

$$
\text{Massless spin-1 propagator: }-i\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{p}^2}
\tag{11.99}
$$

To get the vertex factors, first write down $i\energy{\mathcal L_{\text{int}}}$ in momentum space $(i\energy{\hbar}\partial_\mu\to\mechanical{p_\mu})$, and examine the *fields* involved; these determine the qualitative structure of the interaction. For example, in the case of the QED Lagrangian (11.35)

$$
i\energy{\mathcal L_{\text{int}}}
=-i(\electric{q}\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi})\electric{A_\mu}
\tag{11.100}
$$

there are *three* fields involved $(\signal{\bar\psi}$, $\signal{\psi}$, and $\electric{A_\mu})$, and this defines a vertex in which three lines are joined—an incoming fermion, an outgoing fermion, and a photon. To obtain the vertex factor itself, *simply rub out the field variables*:

$$
-i\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}}\electric{q}\gamma^\mu
=i\electric{g_e}\gamma^\mu
\qquad\text{(QED vertex factor for negatively charged particle)}
\tag{11.101}
$$

(In the case of the photon, what we actually rub out is $\sqrt{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}/4\pi}\electric{A^\mu}$; the extra factor is due to our use of cgs units which are, for this purpose, a little cumbersome.) The same goes for chromodynamics (11.88): The quark-gluon coupling

$$
\energy{\mathcal L_{\text{int}}}
=-(q\signal{\bar\psi}\gamma^\mu\boldsymbol\lambda\signal{\psi})\cdot\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}
\tag{11.102}
$$

yields a vertex of the form

![[elementary-particles-chapter11-quark-gluon-vertex.svg|The quark–gluon vertex obtained from the chromodynamic interaction term.]]

with the vertex factor

$$
-i\frac{g_s}2\gamma^\mu\boldsymbol\lambda
\tag{11.103}
$$

(The strong coupling constant is traditionally defined with a factor of 2: $g_s\equiv2\sqrt{4\pi/\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}\,q$, where $q$ is the “strong charge” appearing in the Lagrangian.) However, there are also direct gluon-gluon couplings, coming from the $\signal{\mathbf F^{\mu\nu}}\cdot\signal{\mathbf F_{\mu\nu}}$ term in $\energy{\mathcal L}$,

<!-- PDF page 368; printed page 360 -->

since $\signal{\mathbf F^{\mu\nu}}$ contains not only the “free” part, $\partial^\mu\signal{\mathbf A^\nu}-\partial^\nu\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}$, but also an interaction term $-2q/\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}(\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}\times\signal{\mathbf A^\nu})$ [eq. (11.87)]. Squaring it out, we find:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal L_{\text{int}}}
&=\left(\frac{q}{8\pi\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}\right)
[(\partial^\mu\signal{\mathbf A^\nu}-\partial^\nu\signal{\mathbf A^\mu})
\cdot(\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}\times\signal{\mathbf A_\nu})\\
&\qquad+(\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}\times\signal{\mathbf A^\nu})
\cdot(\partial_\mu\signal{\mathbf A_\nu}-\partial_\nu\signal{\mathbf A_\mu})]\\
&\quad-\frac{q^2}{4\pi(\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c})^2}
(\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}\times\signal{\mathbf A^\nu})
\cdot(\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}\times\signal{\mathbf A_\nu})
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.104}
$$

The first term carries three factors of $\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}$, and leads to the three-gluon vertex (9.18); the second term carries *four* factors of $\signal{\mathbf A^\mu}$, and gives the four-gluon vertex (9.19). (For some practice in extracting Feynman rules from Lagrangians, see Problems 11.20 and 11.21.)

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L},\energy{\mathcal L_{\text{int}}}$ | $\unit{erg/cm^3}$ | Lagrangian densities | Free-field and interaction functions that determine the Feynman rules. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{g^{1/2}\,cm^{1/2}/s}$ | Scalar field | The spin-0 field in the Klein–Gordon equation. |
| $\signal{\psi},\signal{\bar\psi}$ | $\unit{cm^{-3/2}}$ | Dirac fields | Fermion fields and their adjoints. |
| $\electric{A^\mu},\electric{A_\mu}$ | $\unit{G\,cm}$ | Vector field and photon potential | The spin-1 Proca field and massless Maxwell field. |
| $\signal{\mathbf A^\mu},\signal{\mathbf A_\mu}$ | $\unit{g^{1/2}\,cm^{1/2}/s}$ | Gluon gauge fields | Color fields in the quark and gluon interaction terms. |
| $\signal{\mathbf F^{\mu\nu}}$ | $\unit{g^{1/2}\,cm^{-1/2}/s}$ | Gluon field-strength tensor | The source of the three-gluon and four-gluon terms. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{g}$ | Particle mass | The mass in the free-field propagators. |
| $\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p_\mu}$ | $\unit{g\,cm/s}$ | Four-momentum | The momentum-space variable in the propagators. |
| $\electric{q}$ | $\unit{statC}$ | Electric charge | The QED particle charge. |
| $q$ | $\unit{statC}$ | Strong charge | The dimensional color coupling in the QCD Lagrangian. |
| $\electric{g_e},g_s$ | $1$ | Coupling constants | Dimensionless electromagnetic and strong vertex strengths. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{erg\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Converts field derivatives to momenta. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{cm/s}$ | Speed of light | Relates energy, momentum, and mass. |
