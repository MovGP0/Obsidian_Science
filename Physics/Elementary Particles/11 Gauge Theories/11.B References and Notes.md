> [!TLDR]
> These references cover Lagrangian mechanics, gauge theories, quantization, Goldstone's theorem, and the Higgs mechanism.

<!-- PDF page 376; printed page 368 -->

1. See, for example, R. Resnick and D. Halliday, *Physics*, 3d Ed., (New York: Wiley, 1977), Part I, Chap. 8.
2. The Euler–Lagrange equation can be obtained, in turn, from the Principle of Least Action. For further discussion of the Lagrangian method in classical particle mechanics, see J. B. Marion, *Classical Dynamics of Particles and Systems*, 2d Ed. (New York: Academic Press, 1970) or H. Goldstein, *Classical Mechanics*, 2d Ed. (Reading, MA: Addison-Wesley, 1980).
3. H. Weyl, *Ann. Phys.* **59**, 101 (1919). See also K. Moriyasu, *An Elementary Primer for Gauge Theory* (Singapore: World Scientific, 1983).
4. C. N. Yang and R. L. Mills, *Phys. Rev.* **96**, 191 (1954).
5. See, for example, C. Chevalley, *Theory of Lie Groups* (Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press, 1946).
6. This, and many other aspects of local gauge theories, are treated with extraordinary clarity and beauty in the unpublished research notes “Classical Chromodynamics” and “Bare Bones of the Classical Theory of Gauge Fields,” by N. A. Wheeler (Portland, OR: Reed College, 1981).
7. A. Actor, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **51**, 461 (1979).
8. The details will be found in any treatise on quantum field theory, such as those cited in the Introduction.
9. G. 't Hooft, *Nucl. Phys.* **B33**, 173 (1971), **B35**, 167 (1971). Reprinted in C. H. Lai, ed. (Singapore: World Scientific, 1981).
10. J. Goldstone, *Nuovo Cimento* **19**, 154 (1961); J. Goldstone, A. Salam, and S. Weinberg, *Phys. Rev.* **127**, 965 (1962).
11. For detailed application of the Higgs mechanism in the electroweak theory of Glashow, Weinberg, and Salam, see, for instance, Chapter 15 of F. Halzen and A. D. Martin, *Quarks and Leptons* (New York: Wiley, 1984).
