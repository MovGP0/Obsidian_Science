## Problem 7.7

Construct the normalized spinors $\signal{u^{(+)}}$ and $\signal{u^{(-)}}$ representing an electron of momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ with helicity $\pm1$. [That is, find the $\signal{u}$'s that satisfy equation (7.34), with positive $\energy{E}$, and are eigenspinors of the helicity operator ($\mechanical{\hat p}\cdot\boldsymbol\Sigma$) with eigenvalues $\pm1$.]

[*Solution:*

$$
\signal{u^{(\pm)}}=N
\begin{pmatrix}
\signal{u_A}\\
\dfrac{\pm\mechanical{c}|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|}
{(\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2)}\signal{u_A}
\end{pmatrix},
$$

where

$$
\signal{u_A}=\begin{pmatrix}
\mechanical{p_z}\pm|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|\\
\mechanical{p_x}+i\mechanical{p_y}
\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad
N^2=\frac{(\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2)}
{2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|\mechanical{c}(|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|\pm\mechanical{p_z})}
$$

]

## Problem 7.8

[The purpose of this problem is to demonstrate that particles described by the Dirac equation carry “intrinsic” angular momentum ($\mechanical{\mathbf S}$) in addition to their orbital angular momentum ($\mechanical{\mathbf L}$), neither of which is separately conserved, although their *sum* is. It should be attempted only if you are reasonably familiar with quantum mechanics.]

(a) Construct the Hamiltonian, $\energy{H}$, for the Dirac equation. [*Hint:* Solve equation (7.19) for $\mechanical{p^0}\mechanical{c}$. *Solution:* $\energy{H}=\mechanical{c}\gamma^0(\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})$, where $\mechanical{\mathbf p}\equiv(\energy{\hbar}/i)\nabla$ is the momentum operator.]

(b) Find the commutator of $\energy{H}$ with the orbital angular momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf L}=\mechanical{\mathbf r}\times\mechanical{\mathbf p}$.

[*Solution:* $[\energy{H},\mechanical{\mathbf L}]=-i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\gamma^0(\boldsymbol\gamma\times\mechanical{\mathbf p})$]

Since $[\energy{H},\mechanical{\mathbf L}]$ is not zero, $\mechanical{\mathbf L}$ by itself is not conserved. Evidently there is some *other* form of angular momentum lurking here. Introduce the “spin angular momentum,” $\mechanical{\mathbf S}$, defined by equation (7.48).

(c) Find the commutator of $\energy{H}$ with the spin angular momentum, $\mechanical{\mathbf S}=(\energy{\hbar}/2)\boldsymbol\Sigma$.

[*Solution:* $[\energy{H},\mechanical{\mathbf S}]=i\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\gamma^0(\boldsymbol\gamma\times\mechanical{\mathbf p})$]

It follows that the *total* angular momentum, $\mechanical{\mathbf J}=\mechanical{\mathbf L}+\mechanical{\mathbf S}$, is conserved.

(d) Show that every bispinor is an eigenstate of $\mechanical{\mathbf S}^2$, with eigenvalue $\energy{\hbar}^2\mechanical{s}(\mechanical{s}+1)$, and find $\mechanical{s}$. What, then, is the spin of a particle described by the Dirac equation?

## Problem 7.21

(a) Show that this $\lambda$ satisfies equation (7.84).

(b) Show that this gauge transformation has the effect of modifying $\signal{\epsilon^\mu}$: $\signal{\epsilon^\mu}\to\signal{\epsilon^\mu}+\kappa\mechanical{p^\mu}$. ## Problem 7.34

Starting with equation (7.105), determine the spin-averaged amplitude, [analogous to equation (7.126)] for electron-electron scattering. [*Hint:* You can read $\langle|\mathcal M_1|^2\rangle$ and $\langle|\mathcal M_2|^2\rangle$ from equation (7.126). $$
\langle\mathcal M_1\mathcal M_2^*\rangle
=\frac{-\electric{g_e}^4}
{4(\mechanical{p_1}-\mechanical{p_3})^2(\mechanical{p_2}-\mechanical{p_4})^2}
\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p_1}}\gamma^\nu
\not{\mechanical{p_4}}\gamma_\mu\not{\mechanical{p_2}}\gamma_\nu\not{\mechanical{p_3}})
$$

Notice that for *massless* particles the conservation of momentum ($\mechanical{p_1}+\mechanical{p_2}=\mechanical{p_3}+\mechanical{p_4}$) implies that $\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_2}=\mechanical{p_3}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}$, $\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_3}=\mechanical{p_2}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}$, and $\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}=\mechanical{p_2}\cdot\mechanical{p_3}$.]

[*Answer:*

$$
\langle|\mathcal M|^2\rangle=
\frac{2\electric{g_e}^4}
{(\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_3})^2(\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_4})^2}
[(\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_2})^4
+(\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_3})^4
+(\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_4})^4]
$$
