> QED Feynman rules assign spinors, polarization vectors, vertex factors, and propagators to each diagram.

In Section 7.2 we found that free electrons and positrons of momentum $\mechanical{p}=(\energy{E}/\mechanical{c},\mechanical{\mathbf p})$, with $\energy{E}=\sqrt{\mechanical{m}^2\mechanical{c}^4+\mechanical{\mathbf p}^2\mechanical{c}^2}$, are represented by the wave functions[^ep-7-5-summary]

$$
\begin{array}{cc}
\textbf{Electrons}&\textbf{Positrons}\\[4pt]
\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})=a e^{-(i/\energy{\hbar})\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\signal{u^{(s)}}(\mechanical{p})
&
\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})=a e^{(i/\energy{\hbar})\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\signal{v^{(s)}}(\mechanical{p})
\end{array}
\tag{7.93}
$$

where $s=1,2$ for the two spin states. The spinors $\signal{u^{(s)}}$ and $\signal{v^{(s)}}$ satisfy the *momentum space Dirac equations*:

$$
(\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p_\mu}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})\signal{u}=0
\qquad
(\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p_\mu}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})\signal{v}=0
\tag{7.94}
$$

and their *adjoints*, $\signal{\bar u}=\signal{u}^{\dagger}\gamma^0$, $\signal{\bar v}=\signal{v}^{\dagger}\gamma^0$, satisfy

$$
\signal{\bar u}(\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p_\mu}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})=0
\qquad
\signal{\bar v}(\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p_\mu}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})=0
$$

They are *orthogonal*,

$$
\signal{\bar u^{(1)}}\signal{u^{(2)}}=0
\qquad
\signal{\bar v^{(1)}}\signal{v^{(2)}}=0
\tag{7.95}
$$

<!-- PDF page 237; printed page 229 -->

*normalized*,

$$
\signal{\bar u}\signal{u}=2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}
\qquad
\signal{\bar v}\signal{v}=-2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}
\tag{7.96}
$$

and *complete*, in the sense that

$$
\sum_{s=1,2}\signal{u^{(s)}}\signal{\bar u^{(s)}}=
(\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p_\mu}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})
\qquad
\sum_{s=1,2}\signal{v^{(s)}}\signal{\bar v^{(s)}}=
(\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p_\mu}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})
\tag{7.97}
$$

(see Problem 7.22). A convenient explicit set ($\signal{u^{(1)}}$, $\signal{u^{(2)}}$, $\signal{v^{(1)}}$, $\signal{v^{(2)}}$) is given in equations (7.46) and (7.50). Ordinarily, we shall be averaging over electron and positron spins, and in that case it doesn't matter that these are not pure spin up and spin down; all we really need is completeness. For the occasional problem in which the spins are specified, we must, of course, use the spinors appropriate to the case at hand.

Meanwhile, a free *photon* of momentum $\mechanical{p}=(\energy{E}/\mechanical{c},\mechanical{\mathbf p})$, with $\energy{E}=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|\mechanical{c}$, is represented by the wave function

$$
\begin{gathered}
\textbf{Photons}\\[4pt]
\magnetic{A^\mu}(\mechanical{x})=
a e^{-(i/\energy{\hbar})\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\signal{\epsilon^\mu_{(s)}}
\end{gathered}
\tag{7.98}
$$

where $s=1,2$ for the two spin states (or “polarizations”) of the photon. The polarization vectors $\signal{\epsilon^\mu_{(s)}}$ satisfy the *momentum space Lorentz condition*:

$$
\signal{\epsilon^\mu}\mechanical{p_\mu}=0
\tag{7.99}
$$

They are *orthogonal*, in the sense that

$$
\signal{\epsilon^{\mu *}_{(1)}}\signal{\epsilon_{\mu(2)}}=0
\tag{7.100}
$$

and *normalized*

$$
\signal{\epsilon^{\mu *}}\signal{\epsilon_\mu}=1
\tag{7.101}
$$

In the Coulomb gauge

$$
\signal{\epsilon^0}=0,\qquad
\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}=0
\tag{7.102}
$$

and the polarization three-vectors obey the completeness relation (Problem 7.23)

$$
\sum_{s=1,2}(\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_{(s)}})_i
(\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon^*_{(s)}})_j
=\delta_{ij}-\mechanical{\hat p_i}\mechanical{\hat p_j}.
\tag{7.103}
$$

A convenient explicit pair ($\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_{(1)}}$, $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_{(2)}}$) is given in equation (7.92).

To calculate the amplitude, $\mathcal M$, associated with a particular Feynman diagram, proceed as follows:

1. *Notation.* Label the incoming and outgoing four-momenta $\mechanical{p_1}$, $\mechanical{p_2}$, …, $\mechanical{p_n}$, and the corresponding spins $\mechanical{s_1}$, $\mechanical{s_2}$, …, $\mechanical{s_n}$; label the internal four-momenta $\mechanical{q_1}$, $\mechanical{q_2}$, …. Assign arrows to the lines as follows: the arrows on *external* fermion lines indicate whether it is an electron or a positron; arrows on *internal* fermion lines are assigned so that the “direction of the flow” through the diagram is preserved (i.e., every vertex must have one arrow entering and one arrow leaving). The arrows on external

   photon lines point “forward”; for internal photon lines the choice is arbitrary. (See Fig. 7.1.)

2. *External Lines.* External lines contribute factors as follows:

   | Particle | Line | Diagram | Factor |

   | Electrons | Outgoing | ![[elementary-particles-qed-rule-electron-outgoing.svg\|Outgoing electron external line]] | $\signal{\bar u}$ |

   | Photons | Outgoing | ![[elementary-particles-qed-rule-photon-outgoing.svg\|Outgoing photon external line]] | $\signal{\epsilon^{\mu *}}$ |

3. *Vertex Factors.* Each vertex contributes a factor

   $$
   i\electric{g_e}\gamma^\mu
   $$

   The dimensionless coupling constant $\electric{g_e}$ is related to the charge of the positron: $\electric{g_e}=\electric{e}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}}=\sqrt{4\pi\electric{\alpha}}$.[^ep-7-5-units]

4. *Propagators.* Each internal line contributes a factor as follows:

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \textit{Electrons and positrons:}\quad&
   \frac{i(\gamma^\mu\mechanical{q_\mu}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})}
   {\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{m}^2\mechanical{c}^2}\\[6pt]
   \textit{Photons:}\quad&\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{q}^2}
   \end{aligned}
   $$

5. *Conservation of Energy and Momentum.* For each vertex, write a delta function of the form

   $$
   (2\pi)^4\delta^4(\mechanical{k_1}+\mechanical{k_2}+\mechanical{k_3})
   $$

<!-- PDF page 239; printed page 231 -->

   where the $\mechanical{k}$'s are the three four-momenta coming *into* the vertex (if an arrow leads *outward*, then $\mechanical{k}$ is *minus* the four-momentum of that line, except for external positrons[^ep-7-5-arrows]). This factor enforces conservation of energy and momentum at the vertex.

6. *Integrate Over Internal Momenta.* For each internal momentum $\mechanical{q}$, write a factor

   $$
   \frac{d^4\mechanical{q}}{(2\pi)^4}
   $$

   and integrate.

7. *Cancel the Delta Function.* The result will include a factor

   $$
   (2\pi)^4\delta^4(\mechanical{p_1}+\mechanical{p_2}+\cdots-\mechanical{p_n})
   $$

   corresponding to overall energy-momentum conservation. Cancel this factor, and what remains is $-i\mathcal M$.

As before, the procedure is to write down all diagrams contributing to the process in question (up to the desired order), calculate the amplitude ($\mathcal M$) for each one, and add them up to get the total amplitude, which is then inserted into the appropriate formula for the cross section or the lifetime, as the case may be. There's just one new twist, here: the antisymmetrization of fermion wave functions requires that we insert a minus sign in combining amplitudes that differ only in the interchange of two identical external fermions. It doesn't matter *which* diagram you associate the minus sign with, since the total will be squared eventually anyway; but there must be a *relative* minus sign between them.

8. *Antisymmetrization.* Include a minus sign between diagrams that differ only in the interchange of two incoming (or outgoing) electrons (or positrons), or of an incoming electron with an outgoing positron (or vice versa).

The handling of fermion loops will be discussed in the final section of this chapter.

[^ep-7-5-summary]: To make this section as self-contained as possible for easier reference, and also to emphasize the similarities and differences in the theories of electrons, positrons, and photons, I begin with a summary of the essential results from earlier sections. For the sake of argument I speak of “electrons” and “positrons,” but they could as well be $\mu^-$ and $\mu^+$, or $\tau^-$ and $\tau^+$, or (with the appropriate electric charges) quarks and antiquarks—in short, any point charges of spin $\frac12$.

[^ep-7-5-units]: In Heaviside–Lorentz units, with $\energy{\hbar}$ and $\mechanical{c}$ set equal to 1, $\electric{g_e}$ *is* the charge of the positron, and hence is written “$\electric{e}$” in most texts. In this book I use Gaussian units, and keep all factors of $\energy{\hbar}$ and $\mechanical{c}$. The easiest way to avoid trouble over units is to express all results in terms of the universal dimensionless quantity $\electric{\alpha}$. In writing the Feynman rules for QED I assume we are dealing with electrons and positrons. In *general*, the QED coupling constant is $-\electric{q}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}}$, where $\electric{q}$ is the charge of the *particle* (as opposed to the *antiparticle*). For electrons, $\electric{q}=-\electric{e}$, but for “up” quarks, say, $\electric{q}=\frac23\electric{e}$.

[^ep-7-5-arrows]: The problem here is that the arrows are being asked to do double duty: they establish the convention for the sign of momentum, and in the case of external fermion lines, they tell you whether it is a particle or an antiparticle (for internal lines we need not distinguish). The latter role takes precedence, so for external positrons the “positive” direction for momentum is *opposite* to the direction of the arrow.
