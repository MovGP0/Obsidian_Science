1. There are many good discussions of the history of elementary particle physics. My own favorite is a delightful little book by C. N. Yang, *Elementary Particles* (Princeton, N.J.: Princeton University Press, 1961). More up-to-date accounts are J. S. Trefil’s *From Atoms to Quarks* (New York: Scribners, 1980) and F. E. Close’s *The Cosmic Onion* (London: Heinemann Educational Books, 1983). The early days are treated well in A. Keller’s *The Infancy of Atomic Physics* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1983) and S. Weinberg’s *Subatomic Particles* (New York: Scientific American Library, 1983). For a fascinating and comprehensive account see A. Pais, *Inward Bound* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1986).

42. The formula for the $W$ and $Z$ masses was first obtained by S. Weinberg, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **19**, 1264 (1967). It involved a parameter $\mechanical{\theta_W}$ whose value was unknown at that time, and all Weinberg could say for sure was that $\mechanical{M_W}\geq37\,\unit{\mathrm{GeV}}/\mechanical{c}^2$ and $\mechanical{M_Z}\geq75\,\unit{\mathrm{GeV}}/\mechanical{c}^2$. In the next 15 years $\mechanical{\theta_W}$ was measured in a variety of experiments, and by 1982 the predictions had been refined, as indicated in equation (1.30).
