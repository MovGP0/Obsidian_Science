> [!TLDR]
> Direct and crossed exchange diagrams combine to give the lowest-order AA to BB scattering cross section.

<!-- PDF page 212; printed page 204 -->

The lowest-order contribution to the process $A+A\to B+B$ is shown in Figure 6.7. In this case there are two vertices (hence two factors of $-ig$), one internal line, with the propagator

<!-- PDF page 213; printed page 205 -->

![[elementary-particles-fig-6-7-aa-bb-direct.svg|**Fig. 6.7** Lowest-order contribution to A + A → B + B.]]

$$
\frac{i}{\mechanical{q}^{2}-\mechanical{m_C}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}}
$$

two delta functions:

$$
(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(\mechanical{p_1}-\mechanical{p_3}-\mechanical{q})
\quad\text{and}\quad
(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(\mechanical{p_2}+\mechanical{q}-\mechanical{p_4})
$$

and one integration:

$$
\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,d^{4}\mechanical{q}.
$$

Rules 1 through 5, then, yield

$$
-i(2\pi)^{4}g^{2}\int
\frac{1}{\mechanical{q}^{2}-\mechanical{m_C}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}}
\delta^{4}(\mechanical{p_1}-\mechanical{p_3}-\mechanical{q})
\delta^{4}(\mechanical{p_2}+\mechanical{q}-\mechanical{p_4})\,d^{4}\mechanical{q}.
$$

The second delta function serves to pick out the value of everything else at the point $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p_4}-\mechanical{p_2}$, so we have

$$
-ig^{2}\frac{1}{(\mechanical{p_4}-\mechanical{p_2})^{2}-\mechanical{m_C}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}}
(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(\mechanical{p_1}+\mechanical{p_2}-\mechanical{p_3}-\mechanical{p_4}).
$$

As promised, there is one remaining delta function, reflecting overall conservation of energy and momentum. Erasing it (rule 6), we are left with

$$
\signal{\mathcal M}=\frac{g^{2}}{(\mechanical{p_4}-\mechanical{p_2})-\mechanical{m_C}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}}.
\tag{6.49}
$$

But that's not the whole story, for there is another diagram of order $g^{2}$, obtained by “twisting” the $B$ lines (Fig. 6.8). (You don't get yet another new diagram by twisting the $A$ lines; the only choice here is whether $\mechanical{p_3}$ connects to $\mechanical{p_1}$ or to $\mechanical{p_2}$). Since this differs from Figure 6.7 only by the interchange $\mechanical{p_3}\leftrightarrow\mechanical{p_4}$, there is no need to compute it from scratch; quoting equation (6.49), we

![[elementary-particles-fig-6-8-aa-bb-crossed.svg|**Fig. 6.8** Second diagram contributing in lowest order to A + A → B + B.]]

<!-- PDF page 214; printed page 206 -->

![[elementary-particles-fig-6-9-aa-bb-cm-frame.svg|**Fig. 6.9** A + A → B + B in the CM frame.]]

can write down immediately the total amplitude (to order $g^{2}$) for the process $A+A\to B+B$:

$$
\signal{\mathcal M}
=\frac{g^{2}}{(\mechanical{p_4}-\mechanical{p_2})^{2}-\mechanical{m_C}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}}
+\frac{g^{2}}{(\mechanical{p_3}-\mechanical{p_2})^{2}-\mechanical{m_C}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}}.
\tag{6.50}
$$

Notice, incidentally, that $\signal{\mathcal M}$ is a Lorentz-invariant quantity. This is *always* the case; it is built into the Feynman rules.

Suppose we are interested in the differential cross section ($d\mechanical{\sigma}/d\mechanical{\Omega}$) for this process, in the CM system (Fig. 6.9). Say, for simplicity, that $\mechanical{m_A}=\mechanical{m_B}=\mechanical{m}$ and $\mechanical{m_C}=0$. Then

$$
(\mechanical{p_4}-\mechanical{p_2})^{2}-\mechanical{m_C}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}
=\mechanical{p_4}^{2}+\mechanical{p_2}^{2}-2\mechanical{p_2}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}
=-2\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})
\tag{6.51}
$$

$$
(\mechanical{p_3}-\mechanical{p_2})^{2}-\mechanical{m_C}^{2}\mechanical{c}^{2}
=\mechanical{p_3}^{2}+\mechanical{p_2}^{2}-2\mechanical{p_3}\cdot\mechanical{p_2}
=-2\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})
\tag{6.52}
$$

(where $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ is the incident momentum of particle 1), and hence

$$
\signal{\mathcal M}=-\frac{g^{2}}{\mechanical{p}^{2}\sin^{2}\mechanical{\theta}}.
\tag{6.53}
$$

According to equation (6.42), then,

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}g^{2}}
{16\pi\energy{E}\mechanical{p}^{2}\sin^{2}\mechanical{\theta}}\right)^{2}.
\tag{6.54}
$$

In this case, as for Rutherford scattering (Example 6.4), the *total* cross section is infinite.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $g$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g\,cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Toy coupling constant | The coupling factor at each ABC vertex. |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}$ | $1$ | Scattering amplitude | The combined lowest-order amplitude for the four-particle process. |
| $\mechanical{q}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g\,cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Internal four-momentum | The virtual C momentum in the propagator. |
| $\mechanical{p_1},\mechanical{p_2},\mechanical{p_3},\mechanical{p_4}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g\,cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | External four-momenta | The incoming A and outgoing B momenta. |
| $\mechanical{m_A},\mechanical{m_B},\mechanical{m_C},\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g}}$ | Particle masses | The toy particle masses and the equal A and B mass. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Speed of light | The speed in the propagator and cross section. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf p},\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g\,cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Incident three-momentum | The incident momentum vector and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $1$ | Scattering angle | The outgoing B angle in the center-of-momentum frame. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{erg}}$ | Incident energy | The particle energy in the cross-section formula. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{erg\,s}}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the scattering cross section. |
| $\mechanical{\sigma},d\mechanical{\sigma}/d\mechanical{\Omega}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{cm^{2}}}$ | Scattering cross sections | The total and differential effective scattering areas. |
| $d\mechanical{\Omega}$ | $1$ | Solid angle element | The outgoing angular region. |
