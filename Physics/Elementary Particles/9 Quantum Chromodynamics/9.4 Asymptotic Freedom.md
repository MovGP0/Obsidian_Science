> [!TLDR]
> Gluon antiscreening makes the strong coupling decrease at high momentum transfer, which permits short-distance QCD calculations.

<!-- PDF page 300; printed page 292 -->

In the last section of Chapter 7 we found that the loop diagram

![[elementary-particles-running-coupling-electron-loop.svg|Electron loop in the exchanged photon line.]]

in quantum electrodynamics makes the effective charge of the electron a function of the momentum transfer $\mechanical{q}$:[^ep-9-4-renormalization]

$$
\electric{\alpha}(|\mechanical{q}^2|)
=\electric{\alpha}(0)\left\{1+\frac{\electric{\alpha}(0)}{3\pi}
\ln\left(\frac{|\mechanical{q}^2|}{(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2}\right)\right\}
\qquad
(|\mechanical{q}^2|=-\mechanical{q}^2\gg(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2)
\tag{9.67}
$$

<!-- PDF page 301; printed page 293 -->

The coupling strength *increases* as the charges get closer together (larger $|\mechanical{q}^2|$), a fact that we interpret physically as a consequence of “vacuum polarization”: the vacuum functions as a kind of dielectric medium, partially screening the charge. The closer we approach, the less complete is the screening, and the greater is the effective charge. Of course, equation (9.67) is valid only to order $\electric{\alpha}(0)^2$. There are higher-order corrections, of which the dominant ones come from *chains* of bubbles:

![[elementary-particles-running-coupling-electron-loop-chains.svg|Chains of two and three electron loops.]]

As it happens, these can be summed explicitly, and the result is[^ep-9-4-geometric]

$$
\electric{\alpha}(|\mechanical{q}^2|)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}(0)}
{1-(\electric{\alpha}(0)/3\pi)
\ln(|\mechanical{q}^2|/(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2)}
\qquad
(|\mechanical{q}^2|\gg(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2)
\tag{9.68}
$$

Ostensibly, the coupling *blows up* at $\ln(|\mechanical{q}^2|/(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2)=3\pi/\electric{\alpha}(0)$. However, this is not to be taken too seriously, since it occurs at an energy of about $10^{280}\unit{\mathrm{MeV}}$, which (to put it mildly) is not an accessible region (see Problem 9.15).

Much the same thing happens in QCD: quark-antiquark bubbles

![[elementary-particles-running-coupling-quark-loop.svg|Quark–antiquark loop in the exchanged gluon line.]]

lead to a screening of the quark color which (modulo appropriate color factors) is the same as equation (9.67). However, there is a new twist to the story, for in QCD we also have virtual *gluon* bubbles

<!-- PDF page 302; printed page 294 -->

![[elementary-particles-running-coupling-gluon-loop-corrections.svg|Gluon-loop and gluon tadpole corrections.]]

as well as diagrams of the form

![[elementary-particles-running-coupling-gluon-vertex-correction.svg|Three-gluon correction at the quark vertex.]]

It turns out<sup>4</sup> that the gluon contribution works in the other direction, producing “antiscreening,” or “camouflage.” I do not know of a persuasive qualitative explanation of this effect<sup>5</sup>—suffice it to say that the formula for the running coupling constant in QCD [analogous to eq. (9.68)] is

$$
\alpha_s(|\mechanical{q}^2|)
=\frac{\alpha_s(\mechanical{\mu}^2)}
{1+(\alpha_s(\mechanical{\mu}^2)/12\pi)(11n-2f)
\ln(|\mechanical{q}^2|/\mechanical{\mu}^2)}
\qquad
(|\mechanical{q}^2|\gg\mechanical{\mu}^2)
\tag{9.69}
$$

where $n$ is the number of colors (3, in the Standard Model), and $f$ is the number of flavors (6, in the Standard Model). In any theory for which $11n>2f$, antiscreening will dominate, and the coupling constant will *decrease* with increasing $|\mechanical{q}^2|$; at short distances the “strong” force becomes relatively weak. This, of course, is the basis of *asymptotic freedom*, on which so much of what we can say quantitatively about the hadrons is predicated. Asymptotic freedom is what allows us to treat partons as essentially free particles, leading to Bjorken scaling; it is what licenses the use of the Feynman calculus in QCD to calculate interquark potentials; it is a basic ingredient in the theory of quarkonium; and it is presumably responsible for the OZI rule. Chromodynamics would have gone out of business if it had not been for the timely discovery of asymptotic freedom.<sup>6</sup>

You may have noticed the appearance of a new parameter, $\mechanical{\mu}$, in equation (9.69). In electrodynamics it is natural to define “the charge” of a particle as the long-range (fully screened) value—that's what Coulomb and Millikan measured, and it's what an engineer or a chemist or even an atomic physicist (unless he's measuring the Lamb shift) is concerned with. Thus $\electric{\alpha}(0)$ is the “good old” fine structure constant, $\frac1{137}$, and it is the sensible parameter in terms of which to do perturbation expansions. But we don't *have* to do it this way; we *could* work from any other value of $\mechanical{q}^2$ [provided only that we stay well below the singularity in (9.68), where $\electric{\alpha}(|\mechanical{q}^2|)$ runs larger than 1, and perturbation theory breaks down]. In QCD, however, we *cannot* work from $\mechanical{q}^2=0$, because that's where $\alpha_s$ is *large*. We must use as a reference some place where $\alpha_s$ is small enough to justify a perturbation expansion. That's why equation (9.69) is expressed in terms of $\alpha_s(\mechanical{\mu}^2)$, instead of $\alpha_s(0)$. Provided that it's large enough so that $\alpha_s(\mechanical{\mu}^2)<1$, it

<!-- PDF page 303; printed page 295 -->

doesn't matter *what* value of $\mechanical{\mu}$ we use (see Problem 9.16). Indeed, if we introduce a new variable $\mechanical{\Lambda}$, defined by

$$
\ln\mechanical{\Lambda}^2=\ln\mechanical{\mu}^2
-\frac{12\pi}{(11n-2f)\alpha_s(\mechanical{\mu}^2)}
\tag{9.70}
$$

the running coupling constant can be expressed in terms of a *single* parameter:

$$
\alpha_s(|\mechanical{q}^2|)
=\frac{12\pi}{(11n-2f)\ln(|\mechanical{q}^2|/\mechanical{\Lambda}^2)}
\qquad
(|\mechanical{q}^2|\gg\mechanical{\Lambda}^2)
\tag{9.71}
$$

(see Problem 9.17). This compact result tells us explicitly the value of the strong coupling at any $|\mechanical{q}^2|$, in terms of the constant $\mechanical{\Lambda}$. Unfortunately, it is hard to determine $\mechanical{\Lambda}$ precisely from experimental data, but $\mechanical{\Lambda}$ appears to lie somewhere in the range

$$
100\unit{\mathrm{MeV}}<\mechanical{\Lambda}\mechanical{c}<500\unit{\mathrm{MeV}}.
\tag{9.72}
$$

Notice that whereas the QED coupling varies only minutely over the accessible energy range (Problem 9.15), variation in the QCD coupling is substantial (Problem 9.18).

[^ep-9-4-renormalization]: It *also* introduces a *divergent* term, which we soak up in the “renormalized” charge [eq. (7.185)]. But that's an entirely different problem, one which (however troublesome you may find it) has no observable consequences, and once the appropriate words have been said is of no further importance. The perfectly *finite* dependence of $\electric{\alpha}$ on $\mechanical{q}^2$ is the *significant* matter, for it carries direct and measurable implications.

[^ep-9-4-geometric]: This is perhaps not so surprising. What we have, in effect, is the geometric series

    $$
    1+x+x^2+x^3+\cdots=\frac1{1-x}
    $$

    where $x$ is for one bubble, $x^2$ is for two, and so on. Although equation (9.68) is correct to all orders in $\electric{\alpha}(0)$, it is not *exact*, since we are ignoring diagrams such as

    ![[elementary-particles-running-coupling-electron-loop-nonchain.svg|Two electron loops connected by additional virtual photons.]]

    These can be shown to make a much smaller contribution in the limit $|\mechanical{q}^2|\gg(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2$. Equation (9.68) is called the “leading log” approximation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The running electromagnetic coupling measure. |
| $\alpha_s$ | $1$ | Strong coupling | The running QCD coupling measure. |
| $\mechanical{q}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g\,cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Momentum transfer | The momentum that sets the interaction scale. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g}}$ | Electron mass | The mass in the QED reference scale. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Speed of light | The speed that converts momentum scales to energy. |
| $\mechanical{\mu},\mechanical{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{g\,cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | QCD momentum scales | The chosen reference scale and invariant coupling scale. |
| $n,f$ | $1$ | Color and flavor counts | The numbers of quark colors and flavors in the coupling formula. |
| — | $\unit{\mathrm{MeV}}$ | Formal pole energy | The quoted energy scale of the QED coupling singularity. |
