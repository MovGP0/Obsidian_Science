> [!TLDR]
> Three QCD diagrams give the two-gluon annihilation amplitude and the spin-zero quarkonium decay rate.

<!-- PDF page 297; printed page 289 -->

In this section we consider the process quark plus antiquark $\to$ two gluons—the QCD analog of pair annihilation. The calculation is quite similar to Example 7.8; however, in QCD there are *three* contributing diagrams, in lowest order:

![[elementary-particles-qcd-pair-annihilation-three-diagrams.svg|Three lowest-order diagrams for quark–antiquark annihilation into two gluons.]]

The amplitude for diagram 1 is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
-i\mathcal M_1={}&\signal{\bar v}(2)\signal{c_2}^\dagger
\left[-i\frac{g_s}{2}\lambda^\beta\gamma^\nu\right]
[\signal{\epsilon_{4\nu}^*}\signal{a_4^{\beta *}}]
\left[\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{q}}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})}
{\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{m}^2\mechanical{c}^2}\right]\\
&\times\left[-i\frac{g_s}{2}\lambda^\alpha\gamma^\mu\right]
[\signal{\epsilon_{3\mu}^*}\signal{a_3^{\alpha *}}]
\signal{u}(1)\signal{c_1}
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.40}
$$

(To simplify the already overburdened notation I'll leave the $*$ off the gluon polarization vectors and color states until the end.) Here $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p_1}-\mechanical{p_3}$, so

$$
\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{m}^2\mechanical{c}^2
=\mechanical{p_1}^2-2\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_3}
+\mechanical{p_3}^2-\mechanical{m}^2\mechanical{c}^2
=-2\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_3}
\tag{9.41}
$$

<!-- PDF page 298; printed page 290 -->

and hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_1={}&\frac{-g_s^2}{8}\frac1{\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_3}}
\signal{\bar v}(2)
[\not{\signal{\epsilon_4}}
(\not{\mechanical{p_1}}-\not{\mechanical{p_3}}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})
\not{\signal{\epsilon_3}}]\signal{u}(1)\\
&\times\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
(\signal{c_2}^\dagger\lambda^\beta\lambda^\alpha\signal{c_1})
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.42}
$$

Similarly, for diagram 2

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_2={}&\frac{-g_s^2}{8}\frac1{\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}}
\signal{\bar v}(2)
[\not{\signal{\epsilon_3}}
(\not{\mechanical{p_1}}-\not{\mechanical{p_4}}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})
\not{\signal{\epsilon_4}}]\signal{u}(1)
\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
(\signal{c_2}^\dagger\lambda^\alpha\lambda^\beta\signal{c_1})
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.43}
$$

Notice that the $\lambda$'s appear this time in the opposite order. Finally, for diagram 3:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-i\mathcal M_3={}&
\signal{\bar v}(2)\signal{c_2}^\dagger
\left[-i\frac{g_s}{2}\lambda^\delta\gamma_\sigma\right]
\signal{u}(1)\signal{c_1}
\left[-i\frac{g^{\sigma\lambda}\delta^{\delta\gamma}}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right]\\
&\times\{-g_sf^{\alpha\beta\gamma}
[g_{\mu\nu}(-\mechanical{p_3}+\mechanical{p_4})_\lambda
+g_{\nu\lambda}(-\mechanical{p_4}-\mechanical{q})_\mu
+g_{\lambda\mu}(\mechanical{q}+\mechanical{p_3})_\nu]\}\\
&\times[\signal{\epsilon_3^\mu}\signal{a_3^\alpha}]
[\signal{\epsilon_4^\nu}\signal{a_4^\beta}]
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.44}
$$

In this case $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p_3}+\mechanical{p_4}$, so $\mechanical{q}^2=2\mechanical{p_3}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}$; simplifying (and using $\signal{\epsilon_3}\cdot\mechanical{p_3}=\signal{\epsilon_4}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}=0$), we find (Problem 9.10):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_3={}&i\frac{g_s^2}{4}\frac1{\mechanical{p_3}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}}
\signal{\bar v}(2)
[(\signal{\epsilon_3}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_4})
(\not{\mechanical{p_4}}-\not{\mechanical{p_3}})
+2(\mechanical{p_3}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_4})\not{\signal{\epsilon_3}}
-2(\mechanical{p_4}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_3})\not{\signal{\epsilon_4}}]\signal{u}(1)\\
&\times f^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
(\signal{c_2}^\dagger\lambda^\gamma\signal{c_1})
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.45}
$$

So far, this is all completely general (and rather messy). To make things more manageable, let's assume (as we did in our study of $e^+e^-$ annihilation) that the initial particles are at rest:

$$
\mechanical{p_1}=\mechanical{p_2}=(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c},\mathbf0),
\qquad\mechanical{p_3}=(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c},\mechanical{\mathbf p}),
\qquad\mechanical{p_4}=(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c},-\mechanical{\mathbf p})
\tag{9.46}
$$

Then

$$
\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_3}
=\mechanical{p_1}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}
=(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mechanical{p_3}\cdot\mechanical{p_4}=2(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2
\tag{9.47}
$$

Meanwhile, in the Coulomb gauge, equation (9.7)

$$
\mechanical{p_3}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_4}
=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_4}
=-\mechanical{p_4}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_4}=0
\tag{9.48}
$$

(likewise $\mechanical{p_4}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_3}=0$), so two terms in $\mathcal M_3$ drop out. Using equations (7.137) and (7.138) to simplify $\mathcal M_1$ and $\mathcal M_2$, we find that the total amplitude ($\mathcal M=\mathcal M_1+\mathcal M_2+\mathcal M_3$) can be written

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M={}&-\frac{g_s^2}{8(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})^2}
\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
\signal{\bar v}(2)\signal{c_2}^\dagger
[\not{\signal{\epsilon_3}}\not{\signal{\epsilon_4}}
\not{\mechanical{p_4}}\lambda^\alpha\lambda^\beta
+\not{\signal{\epsilon_4}}\not{\signal{\epsilon_3}}
\not{\mechanical{p_3}}\lambda^\beta\lambda^\alpha\\
&-i(\signal{\epsilon_3}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_4})
(\not{\mechanical{p_4}}-\not{\mechanical{p_3}})
f^{\alpha\beta\gamma}\lambda^\gamma]\signal{c_1}\signal{u}(1)
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.49}
$$

We may as well orient our coordinates so that the $\mechanical{z}$ axis lies along $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$; then

$$
\not{\mechanical{p_3}}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}(\gamma^0-\gamma^3),
\quad\not{\mechanical{p_4}}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}(\gamma^0+\gamma^3),
\quad\not{\mechanical{p_4}}-\not{\mechanical{p_3}}=2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}\gamma^3
\tag{9.50}
$$

From equations (7.142) and (7.143) we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\not{\signal{\epsilon_3}}\not{\signal{\epsilon_4}}
&=-(\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_3}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_4})
-i(\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_3}\times\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_4})\cdot\boldsymbol\Sigma,\\
\not{\signal{\epsilon_4}}\not{\signal{\epsilon_3}}
&=-(\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_3}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_4})
+i(\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_3}\times\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_4})\cdot\boldsymbol\Sigma
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.51}
$$

Putting this into equation (9.49), and exploiting the commutation relation (9.10) for the $\lambda$'s, we obtain

<!-- PDF page 299; printed page 291 -->

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M={}&\frac{g_s^2}{8\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}
\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
\signal{\bar v}(2)\signal{c_2}^\dagger
((\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_3}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_4})
\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}\gamma^0\\
&+i(\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_3}\times\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_4})
\cdot\boldsymbol\Sigma
([\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta]\gamma^0
+\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}\gamma^3))
\signal{c_1}\signal{u}(1)
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.52}
$$

where curly brackets denote the anticommutator: $\{A,B\}\equiv AB+BA$. (You might compare this result with the corresponding expression in QED (7.146), to which it reduces if you set all the $\lambda$'s equal to 1, drop the color states $\signal{a}$ and $\signal{c}$, and let $g_s/2\to\electric{g_e}$.)

Suppose now we put the quarks into a *spin-0* (singlet) state (the triplet state cannot go to two gluons anyway; it needs at least three):

$$
\mathcal M=(\mathcal M_{\uparrow\downarrow}-\mathcal M_{\downarrow\uparrow})/\sqrt2
\tag{9.53}
$$

For $\mathcal M_{\uparrow\downarrow}$ we have [see eqs. (7.150) and (7.151)]

$$
\signal{\bar v}(2)\gamma^0\signal{u}(1)
=\signal{\bar v}(2)\boldsymbol\Sigma\gamma^0\signal{u}(1)=0,
\qquad
\signal{\bar v}(2)\boldsymbol\Sigma\gamma^3\signal{u}(1)
=-2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\hat z}
\tag{9.54}
$$

As before, $\mathcal M_{\downarrow\uparrow}=-\mathcal M_{\uparrow\downarrow}$, and we are left with[^ep-9-3-three-gluon]

$$
\mathcal M=-i\sqrt2\,\frac{g_s^2}{4}
(\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_3}\times\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon_4})_z
\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
(\signal{c_2}^\dagger\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}\signal{c_1})
\qquad\text{(spin singlet)}
\tag{9.55}
$$

Once again, we have obtained a result that is identical to the one in QED (eq. 7.155), except that $\electric{g_e}\to g_s$, and there is a color factor

$$
f=\tfrac18\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
(\signal{c_2}^\dagger\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}\signal{c_1})
\tag{9.56}
$$

In particular, if the quarks occupy the color singlet state, $(1/\sqrt3)(r\bar r+b\bar b+g\bar g)$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
f={}&\frac18\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
\frac1{\sqrt3}\biggl\{
(1\;0\;0)\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}\\
&+(0\;1\;0)\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}
+(0\;0\;1)\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}
\biggr\}\\
={}&\frac1{8\sqrt3}\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\beta}
\operatorname{Tr}\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.57}
$$

But

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\{\lambda^\alpha,\lambda^\beta\}
=2\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda^\alpha\lambda^\beta)=4\delta^{\alpha\beta}
\tag{9.58}
$$

(Problem 9.3), so

$$
f=\frac1{2\sqrt3}\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\alpha}
\qquad\text{(color singlet)}
\tag{9.59}
$$

Now, the singlet state for two gluons (see Problem 9.12) is

$$
|\mathrm{singlet}\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt8}
\sum_{n=1}^{8}|n\rangle_1|n\rangle_2
\tag{9.60}
$$

<!-- PDF page 300; printed page 292 -->

Evidently

$$
\signal{a_3^\alpha}\signal{a_4^\alpha}=\frac1{\sqrt8}(8)=2\sqrt2
\tag{9.61}
$$

and hence

$$
f=\sqrt{2/3}
\tag{9.62}
$$

*Conclusion:* for $q+\bar q\to g+g$ in the spin singlet, color singlet configuration, with the quarks at rest, the amplitude is

$$
\mathcal M=-4\sqrt{2/3}\,g_s^2
\tag{9.63}
$$

[see eq. (7.160)], and the cross section is

$$
\mechanical{\sigma}=\frac23\frac{4\pi}{\mechanical{c}\mechanical{v}}
\left(\frac{\energy{\hbar}\alpha_s}{\mechanical{m}}\right)^2
\tag{9.64}
$$

[see eq. (7.165)]. Just as the cross section for $e^++e^-\to\gamma+\gamma$ indicated, the positronium decay rate

$$
\mechanical{\Gamma}=\mechanical{\sigma}\mechanical{v}|\signal{\psi}(0)|^2
\tag{9.65}
$$

[eq. (7.168)], so we can now give a formula for the decay of a spin-0 quarkonium state (such as $\eta_c$—note that $\psi$ and $\Upsilon$ themselves carry spin 1, and go to *three* gluons):

$$
\mechanical{\Gamma}(\eta_c\to2g)
=\frac{8\pi}{3\mechanical{c}}
\left(\frac{\energy{\hbar}\alpha_s}{\mechanical{m}}\right)^2
|\signal{\psi}(0)|^2
\tag{9.66}
$$

As it stands, this is not terribly useful, since we don't know $\signal{\psi}(0)$. However, the electromagnetic decay $\eta_c\to2\gamma$ involves the same factor, and we *can* derive a clean expression for the branching ratio (see Problem 9.13).

[^ep-9-3-three-gluon]: At this stage all terms in $\signal{\epsilon_3}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_4}$ drop out. The fact that $\mathcal M_3$ is proportional to $\signal{\epsilon_3}\cdot\signal{\epsilon_4}$ [eq. (9.49)] means that the diagram containing a three-gluon vertex makes no contribution, when the quarks are at rest in the spin singlet configuration. Most books simply ignore it from the start, but in principle it should be included (see also Problem 9.11).
