9. A qualitatively plausible mechanism is suggested by the “MIT Bag Model”. Free quarks of mass $\mechanical{m}$, confined within a spherical shell of radius $\mechanical{R}$, are found to have an effective mass $\mechanical{m_{\mathrm{eff}}}=\sqrt{\mechanical{m}^{2}+(\energy{\hbar}x/\mechanical{R}\mechanical{c})^2}$, where $x$ is a dimensionless number around 2.5. Using the radius of the proton (say, $1.5\times10^{-13}\,\unit{\mathrm{cm}}$) for $\mechanical{R}$, we obtain $\mechanical{m_{\mathrm{eff}}}=330\,\unit{\mathrm{MeV}}/\mechanical{c}^{2}$ for the up and down quarks. See F. E. Close, *An Introduction to Quarks and Partons* (London: Academic, 1979), Section 18.1.

11. C. S. Wu et al., *Phys. Rev.* **105**, 1413 (1957). In the interest of clarity I am ignoring the formidable technical difficulties involved in this experiment. To keep the cobalt nuclei aligned, the sample had to be maintained at a temperature of less than $1^\circ\,\unit{\mathrm{K}}$ for $10\,\unit{\mathrm{minutes}}$. Small wonder that no earlier experiments had stumbled on evidence of parity violation.

26. The $TCP$ Theorem was discovered by J. Schwinger and G. Lüders, and perfected by W. Pauli, *Niels Bohr and the Development of Physics*, W. Pauli, ed., (New York: McGraw-Hill, 1955). At first no one paid much attention to the $TCP$ theorem, because at that time everyone thought $T$, $C$, and $P$ were all perfect symmetries individually. It was only with the fall of parity, and especially with the failure of $CP$, that the importance of this theorem was fully appreciated.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{m_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{MeV}}/\mechanical{c}^{2}$ | Quark masses | The bare and effective masses in the MIT Bag Model estimate. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{cm}}$ | Confinement radius | The spherical shell radius used for the quark estimate. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}}$ | Speed of light | The speed in the effective-mass relation. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{J\,s}}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the confinement contribution to effective mass. |
| $Y_l^m$ | $1$ | Angular wave function | The spherical harmonic that describes the angular part of the state. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi}$ | $1$ | Angular coordinates | The polar and azimuthal angles in the spherical harmonic. |
| — | $\unit{\mathrm{K}}$ | Sample temperature | The source temperature limit for the aligned cobalt sample. |
| — | $\unit{\mathrm{minutes}}$ | Alignment interval | The time for which the low sample temperature was maintained. |
