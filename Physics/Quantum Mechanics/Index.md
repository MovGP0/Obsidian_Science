## Choose the question you want to answer

These collections serve different purposes. Mathematical foundations explain the structure of states and operators. Statistical mechanics uses microscopic models and probability to predict macroscopic behavior. Quantum field theory develops a relativistic framework for fields, particles, and interactions.

- **How do states, observables, and probabilities fit together?** Start with Mathematical Foundations. It develops the mathematical tools and their quantum interpretation; it does not replace a course on specific atomic or material systems.
- **How do many microscopic states produce thermodynamic properties?** Use Statistical Mechanics. Begin with thermodynamics and ensemble choice before quantum gases and phase transitions. Check the system boundary and equilibrium assumptions for each application.
- **How can a quantum model describe particle creation and relativistic interactions?** Use Quantum Field Theory after quantum mechanics, special relativity, and Lagrangian mechanics. Follow the route from free fields to amplitudes and observable rates.

The collection numbers organize this index; they are not a claim that every note in one collection is a prerequisite for the next. Use the reader guidance in each introduction to fill the required gaps. Mathematical consistency checks and experimental tests have different roles throughout: a correct derivation predicts consequences of a model, while measurements test that model under stated conditions.
