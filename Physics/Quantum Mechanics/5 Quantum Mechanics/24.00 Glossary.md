---
title: "Glossary"
---

> [!TLDR]
> This glossary defines the states, operators, and physical effects used throughout the quantum mechanics notes.

**Probability amplitude.** The complex number $\langle\phi\mid\psi\rangle$. Its squared magnitude is the probability of finding the system in $\lvert\phi\rangle$ when it starts in $\lvert\psi\rangle$.

**Spherical harmonics.** An orthonormal basis of $L^2(S^2)$. These functions are simultaneous eigenstates of $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^2$ and $\mechanical{\hat L_z}$, with integer $\ell\ge0$ and $\lvert m\rvert\le\ell$.

**Eigenstate.** A vector $\lvert v\rangle$ that satisfies $\hat A\lvert v\rangle=a\lvert v\rangle$ for some $a\in\mathbb C$. For a Hermitian operator, $a\in\mathbb R$.

**Computational basis.** The basis $\{\lvert0\rangle,\lvert1\rangle\}$ of $\mathbb C^2$. These eigenstates of $\sigma_z$ form the conventional basis in quantum information.

**Boson.** A particle with integer spin. A state of identical bosons is symmetric under particle exchange.

**Bra.** A linear functional $\langle\psi\rvert$ on the Hilbert space, dual to the ket $\lvert\psi\rangle$.

**CSCO (complete set of commuting observables).** A set of compatible observables whose simultaneous eigenvalues uniquely specify their common eigenstates.

**Collapse.** The reduction of the state after a measurement to the eigenspace of the observed result, as described by Postulate IV.

**Commutator.** The operator $[\hat A,\hat B]=\hat A\hat B-\hat B\hat A$. For compatible observables, the commutator vanishes.

**Blackbody.** An ideal absorber. In thermal equilibrium it emits radiation with a Planck spectrum. This spectrum motivated the introduction of quantization in 1900.

**Probability density.** In position space, $\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^2$ gives the probability per unit length of finding the particle near $x$. In an infinitesimal interval $[x,x+dx]$, the probability is $\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^2\,dx$.

**Slater determinant.** The antisymmetrized state for $N$ fermions, $\det[\phi_i(j)]/\sqrt{N!}$.

**Dirac notation.** Bra-ket notation uses $\lvert\psi\rangle$ for a ket, $\langle\psi\rvert$ for a bra, and $\langle\phi\mid\psi\rangle$ for an inner product.

**Tunneling.** A particle can cross a potential barrier with $\energy E<\energy V_0$. The transmission probability is exponentially small for a sufficiently thick barrier.

**Ehrenfest theorem.** The equations for $\langle\mechanical{\hat x}\rangle$ and $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle$ have the form of Hamilton's equations. They recover classical motion for wave packets localized on the scale over which the force varies.

**Entanglement.** A pure state of a composite system that cannot be factored into states of its subsystems. It is a resource in quantum information.

**Fermion.** A particle with half-integer spin. A state of identical fermions is antisymmetric under particle exchange, which gives the Pauli exclusion principle.

**Fermi's golden rule.** The transition rate under a harmonic perturbation is $\Gamma=(2\pi/\mechanical{\hbar})\lvert\energy V_{fi}\rvert^2\rho(\energy E_f)$.

**Wavefunction.** The state in the position basis, $\psi(x)=\langle x\mid\psi\rangle$.

**Hamiltonian.** The operator $\energy{\hat H}$ that represents the total energy and generates time evolution.

**Heisenberg picture.** Operators carry the time dependence while state vectors are fixed.

**Hermitian (self-adjoint).** An operator with $\hat A=\hat A^\dagger$ has a real spectrum. For a discrete spectrum, its eigenstates can be chosen to form an orthonormal basis.

**Hilbert space.** A complete complex vector space with an inner product. Quantum state vectors belong to this space.

**Uncertainty relation.** The standard deviations of two observables satisfy $\Delta A\,\Delta B\ge\lvert\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\rangle\rvert/2$.

**Ket.** A Hilbert-space vector, written $\lvert\psi\rangle$.

**Angular momentum.** A vector of operators. $\mechanical{\mathbf L}=\mechanical{\mathbf r}\times\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ is orbital angular momentum, $\mechanical{\mathbf S}$ is intrinsic spin, and $\mechanical{\mathbf J}$ is total angular momentum.

**Observable.** A Hermitian operator that represents a measurable physical quantity.

**Wave packet.** A superposition of plane waves. A localized particle can be described by a wave packet with finite widths in position $x$ and momentum $\mechanical p$.

**Parity.** The operator $\hat\Pi$ inverts position, $\mechanical{\mathbf r}\mapsto-\mechanical{\mathbf r}$. It satisfies $\hat\Pi^2=\hat I$ and has eigenvalues $\pm1$.

**Pauli matrices.** The matrices $\sigma_x$, $\sigma_y$, and $\sigma_z$. With a factor of $\mechanical{\hbar}/2$, they give the spin-$1/2$ angular momentum operators.

**Pauli exclusion principle.** Two identical fermions cannot occupy the same one-particle state.

**Perturbation theory.** An approximation for a small perturbation $\energy{\hat V}$. Expand energies and eigenstates as series in a parameter $\lambda$.

**Postulates.** The five basic statements cover the state, observables, measurement, collapse, and time evolution.

**Born probability.** The rule $P(a_n)=\lvert\langle a_n\mid\psi\rangle\rvert^2$ gives the probability of the outcome associated with $\lvert a_n\rangle$.

**Projector.** The operator $\hat P=\lvert\phi\rangle\langle\phi\rvert$ projects onto a normalized state. The sum $\sum_i\lvert\phi_i\rangle\langle\phi_i\rvert$ projects onto the subspace spanned by the orthonormal states $\lvert\phi_i\rangle$. A projector is Hermitian and idempotent.

**Quantization.** The assignment of operators to classical quantities. The associated spectra can contain discrete or continuous values.

**Rabi oscillation.** A coherent oscillation of the populations of two levels under a resonant drive.

**Representation.** A choice of basis for describing states and operators. Common choices are the position, momentum, and energy bases.

**Schrödinger equation.** The time-dependent form is $i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_t\lvert\psi\rangle=\energy{\hat H}\lvert\psi\rangle$. The stationary form is $\energy{\hat H}\lvert\psi\rangle=\energy E\lvert\psi\rangle$.

**Spin.** Intrinsic angular momentum. The spin quantum number is integer for bosons and half-integer for fermions.

**Bloch sphere.** The unit sphere that parametrizes pure states of a qubit, whose state space is $\mathbb C^2$.

**Coherent state.** An eigenstate of the annihilation operator $\hat a$. It follows classical harmonic motion and has the minimum uncertainty product $\Delta x\,\Delta\mechanical p=\mechanical{\hbar}/2$.

**Stationary state.** An eigenstate of $\energy{\hat H}$. Its time evolution only changes its global phase.

**Stern–Gerlach experiment.** The 1922 experiment showed that a component of the atomic magnetic moment is quantized. The splitting in the original experiment is now attributed to electron spin.

**Tensor product.** The state space $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$ of a composite system with subsystems $A$ and $B$.

**Unitary operator.** An operator that satisfies $\hat U\hat U^\dagger=\hat U^\dagger\hat U=\hat I$. Unitary operators describe time evolution and continuous symmetries.

**Expectation value.** For a normalized state $\lvert\psi\rangle$, $\langle\hat A\rangle_\psi=\langle\psi\lvert\hat A\rvert\psi\rangle$.

**Variational method.** For a normalized trial state, $\langle\phi\lvert\energy{\hat H}\rvert\phi\rangle\ge\energy E_0$. The expectation value gives a rigorous upper bound on the ground-state energy.

**WKB.** The Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin semiclassical approximation in $\mechanical{\hbar}$. It gives a form of Bohr–Sommerfeld quantization.

**Zeno effect.** Frequent measurements can inhibit the evolution of a quantum system.
