---
title: "Ground State and Typical Scales"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The hydrogen ground state is spherically symmetric. Its most probable radius is the Bohr radius, and its mean radius is $3a_0/2$.

The ground-state wave function is

$$
\signal{\psi}_{100}(\mathbf r)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a_0^3}}e^{-r/a_0}.
$$

The radial and angular factors obey $\int_0^\infty R_{10}^2r^2\,dr=1$ and $\int_{S^2}\lvert Y_{00}\rvert^2\,d\Omega=1$. The mean radius is

$$
\langle r\rangle_{10}=\int_0^\infty r\,R_{10}(r)^2r^2\,dr=\frac32a_0.
$$

## Mean inverse radius and energies

$$
\left\langle\frac1r\right\rangle_{10}
=\int_0^\infty R_{10}(r)^2r\,dr=\frac1{a_0}.
$$

The mean potential energy is $\langle\energy V\rangle_{10}=-\electric e^{\,2}/(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0a_0)\simeq-27.2\,\unit{eV}$. Since $\energy E_1\simeq-13.6\,\unit{eV}$, the mean kinetic energy is $\langle\energy T\rangle_{10}\simeq13.6\,\unit{eV}=-\energy E_1$. This agrees with the virial theorem $2\langle\energy T\rangle=-\langle\energy V\rangle$ for an inverse-distance potential.

## Radial probability

The probability in a thin shell is $P(r)\,dr=R_{10}(r)^2r^2\,dr$. The radial density peaks at $r=a_0$, the mode of the radius distribution. The mean radius is $3a_0/2$. These radii describe the distribution of measurements.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}_{100},R_{10}$ | $\unit{m^{-3/2}}$ | Ground-state amplitudes | The full position wave function and its radial factor. |
| $Y_{00}$ | $1$ | Angular factor | The constant normalized spherical harmonic. |
| $r,a_0,\langle r\rangle_{10}$ | $\unit m$ | Radii | The relative separation, Bohr radius, and mean ground-state radius. |
| $P(r)$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Radial probability density | The probability per unit radius after angular integration. |
| $\lvert\signal{\psi}_{100}\rvert^2$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Volume probability density | The probability per unit volume at a position. |
| $\energy E_1,\energy T,\energy V$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energies | The ground-state total energy and the kinetic and potential terms. |
| $\electric e,\electric{\varepsilon}_0$ | $\unit C,\unit{F\,m^{-1}}$ | Charge and permittivity | The constants in the Coulomb potential. |
