---
title: "Wave Functions and Degeneracy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Hydrogen wave functions are radial factors times spherical harmonics. The radial node count is $n-\ell-1$, and the Coulomb energy is independent of $\ell,m$ at fixed $n$.

The eigenfunctions are $\signal{\psi}_{n\ell m}(\mathbf r)=R_{n\ell}(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)$. At fixed $\ell$, the real radial factors are orthonormal.

$$
\int_0^\infty R_{n'\ell}(r)R_{n\ell}(r)\,r^2\,dr=\delta_{n'n}.
$$

The first examples are

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{10}(r)&=2a_0^{-3/2}e^{-r/a_0},\\
R_{20}(r)&=(2a_0)^{-3/2}(2-r/a_0)e^{-r/(2a_0)},\\
R_{21}(r)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}(2a_0)^{-3/2}(r/a_0)e^{-r/(2a_0)},\\
R_{30}(r)&\propto(27-18r/a_0+2r^2/a_0^2)e^{-r/(3a_0)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The radial factor has $n_r=n-\ell-1$ nodes for $r>0$. Figure 12.1 compares the radial probability densities $r^2\lvert R_{n\ell}(r)\rvert^2$.

## Degeneracy

At level $n$, $\ell$ has the $n$ values $0,\ldots,n-1$. Each $\ell$ gives $2\ell+1$ values of $m$. Thus the spatial level has $\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell+1)=n^2$ states. Including spin doubles this to $2n^2$ before spin-dependent corrections.

The $n^2$ degeneracy is sometimes called **accidental degeneracy**. Ordinary rotation symmetry accounts for $2\ell+1$ states at a given $\ell$. The exact Coulomb potential has an additional conserved operator, the **Runge–Lenz vector**,

$$
\hat{\mathbf M}=\frac{1}{2\mechanical m_e}
(\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}\times\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}
-\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}\times\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}})
-\frac{\electric e^{\,2}}{4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0}\frac{\mathbf r}{r}.
$$

For the bound spectrum, this gives the larger dynamical symmetry $\mathrm{SO}(4)$.

## Physical meaning

The exact inverse-distance potential gives the extra symmetry. Changes such as screening or polarizability in multielectron atoms can break it. The energies can then depend on $\ell$ as well as $n$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}_{n\ell m},R_{n\ell}$ | $\unit{m^{-3/2}}$ | Position and radial wave functions | The full amplitude and its radial factor. |
| $r^2\lvert R_{n\ell}\rvert^2$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Radial probability density | The probability per unit radius after angular integration. |
| $Y_{\ell m}$ | $1$ | Spherical harmonic | The normalized angular factor. |
| $r,a_0$ | $\unit m$ | Radius and Bohr radius | The radial coordinate and hydrogen length scale. |
| $n,\ell,m,n_r$ | $1$ | Quantum numbers | The principal, orbital, magnetic, and radial node labels. |
| $\hat{\mathbf M}$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Runge–Lenz vector | The additional conserved operator for the Coulomb potential. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}},\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}},\unit{J\,s}$ | Linear and angular momentum | The operators that appear in the Runge–Lenz vector. |
| $\mechanical m_e,\electric e,\electric{\varepsilon}_0$ | $\unit{kg},\unit C,\unit{F\,m^{-1}}$ | Electron mass, charge magnitude, and permittivity | The constants in the Coulomb model. |
