> [!TLDR]
> The Schrödinger equation gives the evolution of a quantum state. This chapter develops its position representation and the conservation laws that follow.

Postulate V says that quantum time evolution is unitary and that the Hamiltonian generates it. For a particle in a potential, the position representation gives a partial differential equation, the **Schrödinger equation**.

This chapter derives its time-dependent and time-independent forms. It introduces stationary states and wave packets, then develops probability conservation, the Ehrenfest theorem, and the relation between the Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures.

## Chapter summary

- The time-dependent Schrödinger equation is $i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_t\signal{\psi}=\energy{\hat H}\signal{\psi}$.
- For a time-independent Hamiltonian, $\hat U=e^{-i\energy{\hat H}t/\mechanical{\hbar}}$. The dynamics reduce to the spectral problem $\energy{\hat H}\lvert E\rangle=\energy E\lvert E\rangle$.
- Stationary states $\lvert\signal{\psi}(t)\rangle=e^{-i\energy E t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert E\rangle$ have time-independent probabilities for time-independent observables.
- The continuity equation is $\partial_t\signal{\rho}+\nabla\cdot\signal{\mathbf J}=0$, where $\signal{\mathbf J}=(\mechanical{\hbar}/\mechanical m)\operatorname{Im}(\signal{\psi}^{*}\nabla\signal{\psi})$.
- A free Gaussian wave packet moves with group velocity $\mechanical v_g=\mechanical p_0/\mechanical m$. Its width at large times is $\sigma(t)\sim\mechanical{\hbar}t/(2\mechanical m\sigma)$.
- The Ehrenfest equations are $d\langle\hat x\rangle/dt=\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle/\mechanical m$ and $d\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle/dt=-\langle\energy{V'}(\hat x)\rangle$. They approach the classical equations for well-localized packets in smooth potentials.
- In the Heisenberg picture, $\hat A_H(t)=\hat U^\dagger\hat A\hat U$ and $i\mechanical{\hbar}\,d\hat A_H/dt=[\hat A_H,\energy{\hat H}_H]$ if $\hat A$ has no explicit time dependence.
- A time-independent observable is conserved when $[\hat A,\energy{\hat H}]=0$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}$ | $\unit{m^{-3/2}}$ in 3D | Position wave function | The probability amplitude over position. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy E$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energy | The generator of evolution and a stationary energy eigenvalue. |
| $\signal{\rho},\signal{\mathbf J}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}},\unit{m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}$ in 3D | Probability density and current | The local probability and its flux density. |
| $\mechanical v_g,\mechanical p_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}},\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Group velocity and mean momentum | The velocity of a free packet and its mean momentum. |
| $\sigma,\sigma(t)$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position width | The initial and evolved standard deviations. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant that relates energy to phase evolution. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The elapsed time. |
