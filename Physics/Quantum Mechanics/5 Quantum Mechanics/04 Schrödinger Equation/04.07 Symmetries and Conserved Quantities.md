---
title: "Symmetries and Conserved Quantities"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A time-independent observable is conserved for all states if and only if it commutes with the Hamiltonian, under the usual domain assumptions.

For a time-independent operator $\hat A$, the [[04.06 Ehrenfest Theorem|expectation-value equation]] gives $d\langle\hat A\rangle/dt=\langle[\hat A,\energy{\hat H}]\rangle/(i\mechanical{\hbar})$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 4.2. Conserved quantities
> A time-independent $\hat A$ is a **conserved quantity** if and only if
>
> $$
> [\hat A,\energy{\hat H}]=0.
> $$
>
> Its expectation value and its measurement distribution are then constant in time.

## Examples

- **Free particle.** For $\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hat p}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical m)$, $\mechanical{\hat p}$ commutes with $\energy{\hat H}$. Momentum is conserved.
- **Central potential $\energy V(r)$.** Both $\mechanical{\hat L}_z$ and $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}$ commute with $\energy{\hat H}$. The angular momentum is conserved.
- **Parity-invariant Hamiltonian.** If $\energy V(-x)=\energy V(x)$, the parity operator $\hat\Pi$ commutes with $\energy{\hat H}$ and parity is conserved.

The quantum version of Noether's theorem gives the general relation between [[17.00 Symmetries and Conservation Laws|symmetries and conserved quantities]].

## Physical meaning

The Schrödinger equation gives the evolution of a **probability amplitude**. In place of a classical trajectory, it gives a complex field $\signal{\psi}(\mathbf r,t)$ that propagates, interferes, and diffracts like an electromagnetic field. A position measurement yields a position outcome and changes the state according to the [[02.04 Wave Function Collapse|state reduction postulate]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat A$ | Depends on observable | Observable | A time-independent operator whose conservation is tested. |
| $\energy{\hat H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The operator that generates time evolution. |
| $\mechanical{\hat p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The conserved momentum of a free particle. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\energy V$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The central or parity-invariant potential in the examples. |
| $\mechanical{\hat L}_z$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Angular momentum component | The angular momentum along the $z$ axis. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}$ | $\unit{J^2\,s^2}$ | Squared angular momentum | The square of the magnitude of the angular momentum. |
| $\hat\Pi$ | $1$ | Parity operator | The operator for spatial reflection. |
| $\signal{\psi}(\mathbf r,t)$ | $\unit{m^{-3/2}}$ | Position wave function | The normalized probability amplitude over three-dimensional position. |
