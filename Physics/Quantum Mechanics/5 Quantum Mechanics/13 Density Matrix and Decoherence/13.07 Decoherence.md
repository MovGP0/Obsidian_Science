> [!TLDR]
> Coupling to the environment can turn a pure subsystem state into a mixed reduced state by entangling the system with its surroundings.

Hamiltonian evolution preserves the purity of an isolated whole system. A real subsystem also interacts with its surroundings, such as a heat bath, an electromagnetic field, or lattice vibrations. The joint evolution can correlate the subsystem with degrees of freedom omitted from its description. The total state can become entangled, and the off-diagonal elements of the reduced state can decay after tracing out the environment.

## A system correlated with its environment

Start with $\lvert\signal{\psi}_S\rangle\otimes\lvert E_0\rangle$, where $\lvert\signal{\psi}_S\rangle=c_0\lvert0\rangle+c_1\lvert1\rangle$. An idealized controlled-NOT interaction correlates the system basis states with environmental states.

$$
(c_0\lvert0\rangle+c_1\lvert1\rangle)\otimes\lvert E_0\rangle
\xrightarrow{\hat U_{SE}}
c_0\lvert0\rangle\otimes\lvert E_0\rangle+c_1\lvert1\rangle\otimes\lvert E_1\rangle.
$$

Taking the partial trace over the environment gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat\rho_S
&=\lvert c_0\rvert^2\lvert0\rangle\langle0\rvert+\lvert c_1\rvert^2\lvert1\rangle\langle1\rvert\\
&\quad+c_0c_1^*\langle E_1\mid E_0\rangle\lvert0\rangle\langle1\rvert
+c_0^*c_1\langle E_0\mid E_1\rangle\lvert1\rangle\langle0\rvert.
\end{aligned}
$$

If the two environmental states are orthogonal, the off-diagonal terms vanish. The reduced state then gives the statistics of a classical mixture in the basis $\lvert0\rangle,\lvert1\rangle$.

For a macroscopic environment the overlap $\langle E_0(t)\mid E_1(t)\rangle$ can decay exponentially on a short **decoherence time** $\tau_d$. Figure 13.2 shows the resulting changes to the density matrix.

## Decoherence time

For a spin-$1/2$ system coupled to an oscillator bath, the source gives a model with

$$
\rho_{01}(t)=\rho_{01}(0)e^{-t/\tau_d},\qquad
\tau_d^{-1}\propto\sum_k g_k^2\coth\left(\frac{\thermal{\beta}\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}_k}{2}\right),
$$

where $g_k$ is the coupling to bath mode $k$ and $\thermal{\beta}=1/(\thermal{k_B}\thermal T)$. The time dependence and rate depend on the bath and the approximations used. In many dephasing-dominated systems, $\tau_d\ll\tau_R$, where $\tau_R$ is the population relaxation time. Coherence then decays well before the populations relax.

## Physical meaning. The measurement problem

The projection postulate assigns the postmeasurement state to the eigenspace of the recorded outcome. In the unitary description of the system, apparatus, and environment together, the measurement interaction produces an entangled joint state. The interaction selects a **pointer basis** in which the reduced system loses observable coherence. With many environmental degrees of freedom this process is effectively irreversible. The joint state still contains the coherence, but a local measurement cannot detect it.

This explains the suppression of interference between macroscopic alternatives and the practical success of using a diagonal mixture for unconditioned local measurements. Whether the unitary account also explains a single observed outcome is a question of interpretation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat\rho_S,\rho_{01}$ | $1$ | Reduced state and coherence | The local density operator and one off-diagonal element. |
| $c_0,c_1,\langle E_0\mid E_1\rangle$ | $1$ | Amplitudes and environment overlap | The two system-state coefficients and the overlap of their environmental records. |
| $\hat U_{SE}$ | $1$ | Joint evolution operator | The interaction that correlates the system and environment. |
| $t,\tau_d,\tau_R$ | $\unit s$ | Time scales | Elapsed time, decoherence time, and population relaxation time. |
| $g_k$ | Depends on bath convention | Mode coupling | The strength of coupling to mode $k$. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}_k$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Bath angular frequency | The frequency of an oscillator mode. |
| $\thermal{\beta},\thermal{k_B},\thermal T$ | $\unit{J^{-1}},\unit{J\,K^{-1}},\unit K$ | Thermal parameters | Inverse thermal energy, Boltzmann constant, and bath temperature. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The factor converting mode angular frequency to energy. |
