---
title: "Lindblad Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Lindblad equation describes continuous Markovian evolution of an open quantum system. Its dissipative terms account for changes that unitary evolution alone cannot produce.

Tracing out an environment after joint unitary evolution generally gives nonunitary reduced dynamics and can change purity. Under the time-homogeneous Markov assumption, the Lindblad form generates a completely positive, trace-preserving quantum dynamical semigroup.

The **Lindblad equation** is

$$
\frac{d\hat\rho}{dt}
=-\frac{i}{\mechanical{\hbar}}[\energy{\hat H},\hat\rho]
+\sum_k\left(\hat L_k\hat\rho\hat L_k^\dagger
-\frac12\{\hat L_k^\dagger\hat L_k,\hat\rho\}\right).
$$

The commutator gives coherent Hamiltonian evolution. The **jump operators**, also called Lindblad operators, give the dissipative channels. Braces denote the anticommutator.

## Qubit relaxation and dephasing

- **Energy relaxation.** Use $\hat L=\sigma_-/\sqrt{T_1}$, with $\sigma_-=\lvert0\rangle\langle1\rvert$ for the energy labeling in this example. The excited population decays to the ground state with time constant $T_1$.
- **Pure dephasing.** Use $\hat L=\sigma_z/\sqrt{2T_\phi}$. The coherence satisfies $\rho_{01}(t)=\rho_{01}(0)e^{-t/T_\phi}$ in the absence of Hamiltonian phase evolution. The populations are unchanged.

When both mechanisms are present, $1/T_2=1/(2T_1)+1/T_\phi$. Thus $T_2\le2T_1$ in this model. Energy relaxation already contributes $1/(2T_1)$ to coherence decay, in addition to pure dephasing.

## Preservation of the state conditions

The trace of the commutator vanishes. By cyclicity, $\operatorname{Tr}(\hat L_k\hat\rho\hat L_k^\dagger)=\operatorname{Tr}(\hat L_k^\dagger\hat L_k\hat\rho)$, so the dissipative contribution to the trace also vanishes. Hermiticity follows by taking the adjoint. The Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad theorem (1976) characterizes the generators of completely positive, trace-preserving semigroups under the applicable assumptions.

> [!note] Environmental memory
> The Markov approximation neglects environmental memory. Systems such as superconducting qubits at low temperatures and atoms in high-finesse cavities can show non-Markovian effects, including partial revivals of coherence. The time-homogeneous Lindblad semigroup does not capture this memory. More general descriptions can use equations with memory kernels.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat\rho,\rho_{01}$ | $1$ | Density operator and coherence | The reduced state and its off-diagonal element. |
| $\energy{\hat H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The generator of coherent evolution. |
| $\hat L_k$ | $\unit{s^{-1/2}}$ | Jump operator | The dissipative operator with the rate absorbed into its normalization. |
| $\sigma_-,\sigma_z$ | $1$ | Qubit operators | The energy-lowering and diagonal operators in the examples. |
| $T_1,T_\phi,T_2,t$ | $\unit s$ | Time scales | The energy relaxation, pure dephasing, and total coherence times, and elapsed time. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The factor converting the Hamiltonian into a time-evolution generator. |
