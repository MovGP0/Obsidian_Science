---
title: "Liouville–von Neumann Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A closed system evolves by unitary conjugation of its density operator. This preserves the eigenvalues, purity, and entropy.

Each $\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle$ obeys the Schrödinger equation $i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_t\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle=\energy{\hat H}\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle$. Differentiate the ensemble with fixed probabilities $p_i$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_t\hat\rho
&=i\mechanical{\hbar}\sum_i p_i
\left[(\partial_t\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle)\langle\signal{\psi}_i\rvert
+\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle(\partial_t\langle\signal{\psi}_i\rvert)\right]\\
&=\sum_i p_i\left[\energy{\hat H}\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle\langle\signal{\psi}_i\rvert
-\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle\langle\signal{\psi}_i\rvert\energy{\hat H}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The **Liouville–von Neumann equation** is

$$
i\mechanical{\hbar}\frac{d\hat\rho}{dt}=[\energy{\hat H},\hat\rho].
$$

It is analogous to the classical Liouville equation $\partial_t f=\{\energy H,f\}$ for a phase-space density. The quantum equation uses the commutator divided by $i\mechanical{\hbar}$ in place of the Poisson bracket.

For a time-independent Hamiltonian, $\hat U(t)=e^{-i\energy{\hat H}t/\mechanical{\hbar}}$ and $\hat\rho(t)=\hat U(t)\hat\rho(0)\hat U(t)^\dagger$. The conjugation preserves the eigenvalues. A pure state stays pure and a mixed state retains its purity. The von Neumann entropy also stays constant, as expected for reversible Hamiltonian dynamics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat\rho$ | $1$ | Density operator | The state of the closed system. |
| $p_i$ | $1$ | Preparation probability | A constant weight in the ensemble decomposition. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy H$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonians | The quantum generator and its classical counterpart. |
| $\hat U(t)$ | $1$ | Evolution operator | The unitary operator that conjugates the density operator. |
| $f$ | Inverse phase-space volume | Classical density | The probability density in classical phase space. |
| $t,\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit s,\unit{J\,s}$ | Time and reduced Planck constant | The time variable and the scale of the quantum generator. |
