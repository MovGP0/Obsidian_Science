---
title: "Photoelectric Effect"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The photoelectric effect shows that each photon gives an electron energy proportional to the light frequency.

When monochromatic light of frequency $\signal{\nu}$ strikes a metal, it can release electrons from the surface. The experiment gives three results that the classical wave theory does not explain.

1. Below a threshold frequency $\signal{\nu}_0$, no electrons are emitted in the ordinary single-photon regime, regardless of intensity.
2. Above the threshold, the maximum electron kinetic energy is independent of intensity and increases linearly with $\signal{\nu}$.
3. Above the threshold, emission occurs without the delay that classical wave theory would predict at low intensities.

In 1905, Einstein proposed that light consists of quanta, now called **photons**, each with energy $\energy{E}=h\signal{\nu}$. The minimum energy required to release an electron from the metal is the **work function** $\energy{W}$. A photon with $h\signal{\nu}<\energy{W}$ cannot eject an electron. For $h\signal{\nu}>\energy{W}$, the maximum kinetic energy is

$$
\energy{K}_{\max}=h\signal{\nu}-\energy{W}.
$$

The threshold frequency is $\signal{\nu}_0=\energy{W}/h$. At a fixed frequency, greater intensity gives a higher photon arrival rate per unit area. This increases the emission rate while leaving the energy of each photon unchanged.

Millikan's precision experiment in 1916 confirmed the linear relation between $\energy{K}_{\max}$ and $\signal{\nu}$. Its measured slope agreed with the value of $h$ obtained from blackbody radiation. Two different experiments gave the same fundamental constant.

## Physical significance

Blackbody radiation and the photoelectric effect show that matter and radiation exchange energy in discrete packets $h\signal{\nu}$. Electromagnetic radiation has particle properties as well as the wave properties described by Maxwell's theory. The relation $\energy{E}=h\signal{\nu}$ connects the wave frequency to the particle energy. The relation $\mechanical{p}=h/\signal{\lambda}$ similarly connects momentum to wavelength.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\nu},\signal{\nu}_0$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Frequency | The incident light frequency and the emission threshold. |
| $\energy{E},\energy{W},\energy{K}_{\max}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energies | The photon energy, work function, and maximum electron kinetic energy. |
| $h$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | The slope of the maximum electron kinetic energy versus frequency. |
