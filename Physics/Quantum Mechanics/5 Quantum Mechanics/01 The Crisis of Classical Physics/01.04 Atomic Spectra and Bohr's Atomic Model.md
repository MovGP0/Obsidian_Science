---
title: "Atomic Spectra and Bohr's Atomic Model"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Bohr model uses quantized orbital angular momentum to explain the discrete spectral lines of hydrogen.

Atoms emit and absorb light at discrete frequencies. The resulting **line spectrum** is characteristic of each element. The wavelengths of hydrogen lines obey the empirical Rydberg formula.

$$
\frac{1}{\signal{\lambda}_{mn}}=
R_H\left(\frac{1}{n^2}-\frac{1}{m^2}\right),
\qquad m>n,\quad m,n\in\mathbb N^+.
\tag{1.3}
$$

The Rydberg constant is $R_H\simeq\quantity{1.097\times10^7}{m^{-1}}$ at the precision used here. The Lyman series has $n=1$, the Balmer series $n=2$, and the Paschen series $n=3$.

In 1913, Niels Bohr derived Eq. (1.3) by combining Rutherford's nuclear model with the quantum hypothesis. The model has three assumptions.

1. The electron can move in stable circular orbits around the nucleus.
2. The orbital angular momentum has the allowed magnitudes $\mechanical{L}=n\hbar$, where $n=1,2,3,\ldots$ and $\hbar=h/(2\pi)$.
3. A transition between orbital energies $\energy{E}_m$ and $\energy{E}_n$, with $\energy{E}_m>\energy{E}_n$, emits or absorbs a photon of frequency $\signal{\nu}_{mn}=(\energy{E}_m-\energy{E}_n)/h$. The downward transition emits the photon, and the upward transition absorbs it.

Equate the centripetal force $\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{r}$ to the Coulomb force $\electric{e}^2/(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\mechanical{r}^2)$ and apply $\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{v}\mechanical{r}=n\hbar$. In the fixed-nucleus approximation, the allowed radii are

$$
\mechanical{r}_n=n^2\mechanical{a}_0,\qquad
\mechanical{a}_0=\frac{4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\hbar^2}{\mechanical{m}_e\electric{e}^2}
\simeq\quantity{5.29\times10^{-11}}{m}.
$$

The length $\mechanical{a}_0$ is the **Bohr radius**. The total energy of the electron is

$$
\energy{E}_n=-\frac{1}{n^2}
\frac{\mechanical{m}_e\electric{e}^4}{2(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0)^2\hbar^2}
\simeq-\frac{\quantity{13.6}{eV}}{n^2}.
$$

The ground level $n=1$ has energy about $\quantity{-13.6}{eV}$ relative to the separated electron and proton. The magnitude is the independently measured hydrogen ionization energy at this precision. The transition energies give the Rydberg formula with $R_H\simeq\lvert\energy{E}_1\rvert/(hc)$ at the accuracy of the model used here.

The Bohr model predicts the main spectral lines and characteristic scales of hydrogen and hydrogen-like ions. Its circular orbits are assumptions of the model. In quantum mechanics, the hydrogen ground state has zero orbital angular momentum. The model fails for more complex atoms.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\lambda}_{mn}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Transition wavelength | The wavelength for a transition between levels $m$ and $n$. |
| $R_H$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Rydberg constant for hydrogen | The proportionality constant in the hydrogen spectrum. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Orbital angular momentum | The magnitude quantized in the Bohr model. |
| $h,\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constants | The Planck constant and its reduced form. |
| $\signal{\nu}_{mn}$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Transition frequency | The photon frequency for the transition. |
| $\energy{E}_n$ | $\unit{eV}$ | Level energy | The electron energy relative to the ionization threshold. |
| $\mechanical{m}_e$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Electron mass | The mass used in the fixed-nucleus approximation. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Orbital speed | The electron speed in the circular orbit. |
| $\mechanical{r},\mechanical{r}_n,\mechanical{a}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radii | The orbital radius, allowed radius, and Bohr radius. |
| $\electric{e}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Elementary charge | The magnitude of the electron charge. |
| $\electric{\varepsilon}_0$ | $\unit{F\,m^{-1}}$ | Vacuum permittivity | The electric constant in the Coulomb force. |
| $c$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | The speed of light in vacuum. |
