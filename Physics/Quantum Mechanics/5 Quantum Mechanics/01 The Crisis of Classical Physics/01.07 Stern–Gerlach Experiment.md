---
title: "Stern–Gerlach Experiment"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An inhomogeneous magnetic field splits a silver atom beam into two components, which reveals two possible spin projections.

In 1922, Otto Stern and Walther Gerlach sent a beam of silver atoms through a magnet with a nonuniform field. A classical magnetic moment $\magnetic{\vec\mu}$ could point in any direction relative to the field, which would give a continuous distribution at the detector. Instead, the beam split into two distinct, symmetrically deflected components.

![[quantum-mechanics-fig-1-4-stern-gerlach.svg|**Fig. 1.4** An atom beam travels between a wedge-shaped north pole and a flat south pole. The pole geometry produces an inhomogeneous magnetic field. The two outgoing components correspond to the two electron spin projections, +ℏ/2 and −ℏ/2.]]

The result shows that the measured component $\magnetic{\mu}_z$ has only two possible values. Silver has one unpaired electron. The splitting was later understood in terms of electron spin, with projection quantum numbers $m_s=+1/2$ and $m_s=-1/2$.

The experiment established that a measurable physical quantity can be discrete. The measurement postulate formalizes this result. A measurement outcome belongs to the spectrum of the observable. Here the spectrum has two values.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\vec\mu},\magnetic{\mu}_z$ | $\unit{J\,T^{-1}}$ | Magnetic moment | The atomic magnetic moment and its projection along the field-gradient axis. |
| $m_s$ | $1$ | Spin projection quantum number | The two dimensionless labels for the electron spin projection. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale of the spin angular momentum. |
