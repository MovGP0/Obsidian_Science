---
title: "Spin ½ and the Pauli Matrices"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A spin-$1/2$ state has two components. Its spin observables are the Pauli matrices multiplied by the reduced Planck constant divided by two.

The spin Hilbert space is $\mathbb C^2$. The basis states are $\lvert+\rangle=\lvert\uparrow\rangle$ for spin up along $z$ and $\lvert-\rangle=\lvert\downarrow\rangle$ for spin down. Write $\mechanical{\hat S}_i=(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)\sigma_i$, where the **Pauli matrices** are

$$
\sigma_x=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\sigma_y=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\sigma_z=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

## Properties

- $\sigma_i^2=\hat I$, $\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_i=0$, and $\det\sigma_i=-1$.
- $\sigma_i\sigma_j=\delta_{ij}\hat I+i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k$.
- $[\sigma_i,\sigma_j]=2i\epsilon_{ijk}\sigma_k$ and $\{\sigma_i,\sigma_j\}=2\delta_{ij}\hat I$.
- The eigenvalues of each $\sigma_i$ are $\pm1$. For $\sigma_z$, the eigenvectors are $\lvert+\rangle=(1,0)^T$ and $\lvert-\rangle=(0,1)^T$.

The eigenvectors of $\sigma_x$ are

$$
\lvert+x\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\lvert-x\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The eigenvectors of $\sigma_y$ are

$$
\lvert+y\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\i\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\lvert-y\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\-i\end{pmatrix}.
$$

A normalized state in $\mathbb C^2$ is a **Pauli spinor**.

$$
\lvert\signal{\chi}\rangle=\alpha\lvert+\rangle+\beta\lvert-\rangle,
\qquad
\lvert\alpha\rvert^2+\lvert\beta\rvert^2=1.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat S}_i$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Spin component | The spin observable along the Cartesian axis $i$. |
| $\sigma_i$ | $1$ | Pauli matrix | The spin operator divided by $\mechanical{\hbar}/2$. |
| $\lvert\signal{\chi}\rangle,\alpha,\beta$ | $1$ | Spinor and amplitudes | The two-component state and its coefficients in the $z$ basis. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant that sets the spin eigenvalues. |
