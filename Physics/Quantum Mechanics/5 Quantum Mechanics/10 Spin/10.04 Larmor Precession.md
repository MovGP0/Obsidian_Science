title: "Larmor Precession"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In a uniform magnetic field, the spin components transverse to the field precess at the Larmor frequency.

Put the spin in a field $\magnetic{\mathbf B}=\magnetic B\hat{\mathbf z}$. The interaction Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{\hat H}
=-\magnetic{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}
=-\magnetic{\gamma}\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}
=-\magnetic{\gamma}\magnetic B\mechanical{\hat S}_z
=-\frac{\magnetic{\gamma}\magnetic B\mechanical{\hbar}}2\sigma_z.
$$

The **gyromagnetic ratio** for the electron is $\magnetic{\gamma}=-g\electric e/(2\mechanical m_e)$, with spin $g$ factor $g\simeq2$ and positive elementary charge $\electric e$. The minus sign comes from the negative electron charge. The eigenvalues are $\energy E_\pm=\mp\magnetic{\gamma}\magnetic B\mechanical{\hbar}/2$.

Define the signed Larmor angular frequency $\mechanical{\omega}_L=\magnetic{\gamma}\magnetic B$. For an electron with $\magnetic B>0$, $\mechanical{\omega}_L<0$ and the physical angular frequency is $\lvert\mechanical{\omega}_L\rvert$. The Hamiltonian becomes

$$
\energy{\hat H}=-\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}_L}{2}\sigma_z.
$$

For $\lvert\signal{\psi}(0)\rangle=\lvert+x\rangle=(\lvert+\rangle+\lvert-\rangle)/\sqrt2$,

$$
\lvert\signal{\psi}(t)\rangle
=\frac{e^{i\mechanical{\omega}_L t/2}\lvert+\rangle+e^{-i\mechanical{\omega}_L t/2}\lvert-\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Remove the global phase $e^{i\mechanical{\omega}_L t/2}$ to obtain the equivalent spinor

$$
\frac{\lvert+\rangle+e^{-i\mechanical{\omega}_L t}\lvert-\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

On the [[10.03 Bloch Sphere|Bloch sphere]], this state has $\theta=\pi/2$ and $\phi=-\mechanical{\omega}_L t$. The mean spin precesses in the $xy$ plane at angular frequency $\lvert\mechanical{\omega}_L\rvert$. The longitudinal component stays constant. ## Example. Magnetic resonance

In nuclear magnetic resonance, apply a static field $\magnetic{\mathbf B}_0=\magnetic B_0\hat{\mathbf z}$ and a weak oscillating field $\magnetic{\mathbf B}_1(t)=2\magnetic B_1\cos(\mechanical{\omega}t)\hat{\mathbf x}$. The static field gives energy splitting $\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}_0$, where $\mechanical{\omega}_0=\lvert\magnetic{\gamma}\magnetic B_0\rvert$. At $\mechanical{\omega}=\mechanical{\omega}_0$, transitions between the two spin levels are resonant. The transition amplitude initially grows linearly with time. This is used in nuclear magnetic resonance and magnetic resonance imaging. [[16.00 Perturbation Theory]] gives an explicit calculation.
