---
title: "Feynman Path Integrals Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Calculate propagators and apply time slicing, stationary phase, and geometric phases.

1. Calculate the free propagator for mass $\mechanical m=1.00\times10^{-26}\,\unit{kg}$, between $x_i=0$ and $x_f=1.00\times10^{-9}\,\unit m$ in $T=1.00\times10^{-11}\,\unit s$. Give its magnitude and phase.  
   **Result.** $\lvert\signal K\rvert=\sqrt{\mechanical m/(2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}T)}\simeq1.23\times10^9\,\unit{m^{-1}}$. The phase is $\signal{\varphi}=\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}T)-\pi/4\simeq(4.74-0.785)\,\unit{rad}$.
2. Verify by direct substitution that Eq. (5.4) solves $i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_T\signal K=-\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}\partial_{x_f}^2\signal K/(2\mechanical m)$.  
   **Result.** The two sides agree after differentiation of the Gaussian.
3. For an electron with $T=1.00\times10^{-12}\,\unit s$, estimate the displacement scale $\ell$ from zero displacement at which the phase of Eq. (5.4) changes by one radian.  
   **Result.** $\ell=\sqrt{2\mechanical{\hbar}T/\mechanical m}\simeq1.52\times10^{-8}\,\unit m$.
4. Calculate the phase $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ that a free classical particle with $\mechanical m=9.11\times10^{-31}\,\unit{kg}$ and $\mechanical v=1.00\times10^6\,\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ accumulates over $\ell=1.00\times10^{-7}\,\unit m$.  
   **Result.** $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}/\mechanical{\hbar}=\mechanical m\mechanical v^{\,2}t/(2\mechanical{\hbar})=\mechanical m\mechanical v\ell/(2\mechanical{\hbar})\simeq4.32\times10^2\,\unit{rad}$.
5. Verify that Eq. (5.6) reduces to Eq. (5.4) for $\mechanical{\omega}T\ll1$ at fixed $T$, with corrections of order $(\mechanical{\omega}T)^2$.  
   **Result.** Use $\sin(\mechanical{\omega}T)\simeq\mechanical{\omega}T$ and $\cos(\mechanical{\omega}T)\simeq1$. The phase reduces to the free-particle phase.
6. Model a double slit as the sum of two free amplitudes for paths of lengths $L_1$ and $L_2$. Find the total amplitude and the intensity as functions of $\Delta L=L_2-L_1$.  
   **Result.** For equal magnitudes, $\lvert\signal K_1+\signal K_2\rvert^2=2\lvert\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}\rvert^2(1+\cos\Delta\signal{\varphi})$, with $\Delta\signal{\varphi}=\mechanical m\mechanical v\Delta L/\mechanical{\hbar}$ in the fixed-speed interference limit.
7. Verify the composition law by evaluating the Gaussian integral.
   $$
   \int dx\,\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}(x_f,T_2;x,0)\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}(x,T_1;x_i,0)
   =\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}(x_f,T_1+T_2;x_i,0).
   $$
   **Result.** The Gaussian integral gives the prefactor $\sqrt{\mechanical m/(2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}(T_1+T_2))}$ and the action phase $\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}(T_1+T_2))$.
8. Calculate the classical action in Eq. (5.5) for $x_i=0$, $x_f=1.00\times10^{-9}\,\unit m$, $\mechanical{\omega}T=\pi/4$, $\mechanical m=1.00\times10^{-26}\,\unit{kg}$, and $\mechanical{\omega}=1.00\times10^{14}\,\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$.  
   **Result.** $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}=\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}x_f^2\cos(\mechanical{\omega}T)/(2\sin(\mechanical{\omega}T))\simeq5.00\times10^{-31}\,\unit{J\,s}$, so $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}/\mechanical{\hbar}\simeq4.7\times10^3\,\unit{rad}$.
9. Use Eq. (5.6) to find the partition function. After the Wick rotation $T\mapsto-i\thermal{\beta}\mechanical{\hbar}$, set $x_f=x_i=x$ and integrate over $x$ to take the trace.  
   **Result.** $\thermal Z(\beta)=1/[2\sinh(\thermal{\beta}\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}/2)]$.
10. Two electron beams pass through regions with $\magnetic{\mathbf B}=0$. Their paths enclose a solenoid with magnetic flux $\magnetic{\Phi}$. Derive the Aharonov–Bohm phase from the line integral of the vector potential, then calculate the relative phase for $\magnetic{\Phi}=\mechanical h/(2\electric e)=2.07\times10^{-15}\,\unit{Wb}$. See [[18.00 Geometric Phases]] for the full treatment.  
    **Result.** The phase difference is $\pi$ modulo $2\pi$, because $\electric e\magnetic{\Phi}/\mechanical{\hbar}=\pi$. For otherwise in-phase beams this gives destructive interference.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal K,\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Propagator | The general and free position-space kernels. |
| $x_i,x_f,\ell,L_1,L_2,\Delta L$ | $\unit{m}$ | Positions and lengths | The endpoints, displacement scale, and interference path lengths and difference. |
| $t,T,T_1,T_2$ | $\unit{s}$ | Times | The elapsed and propagation times. |
| $\mechanical m,\mechanical v$ | $\unit{kg},\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Mass and speed | The particle properties used in the propagation examples. |
| $\signal{\varphi},\Delta\signal{\varphi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Phase and phase difference | The argument of the kernel and the relative phase between paths. |
| $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Classical action | The action evaluated on the classical trajectory. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The natural frequency of the oscillator. |
| $\thermal Z,\thermal{\beta}$ | $1,\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Partition function and inverse thermal energy | The trace in imaginary time and its thermal parameter. |
| $\magnetic{\mathbf B},\magnetic{\Phi}$ | $\unit{T},\unit{Wb}$ | Magnetic flux density and flux | The field along the paths and the enclosed flux. |
| $\electric e$ | $\unit{C}$ | Elementary charge | The magnitude of the electron charge. |
| $\mechanical h,\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck and reduced Planck constants | The action constants that set the phase scale. |
