---
title: "Equivalence to the Schrödinger Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Expand a single short-time propagation step to first order in time. Its Gaussian moments reproduce the kinetic and potential terms of the Schrödinger equation.

Consider a single step of duration $\epsilon$ in Eq. (5.2) of [[05.02 Time Discretization and the Feynman Formula]]. To the order needed here, the wave function is

$$
\signal{\psi}(x,t+\epsilon)=
\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon}}
\int dy\,\exp\left[\frac{i\mechanical m(x-y)^2}{2\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon}
-\frac{i\epsilon}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\energy V(x)\right]\signal{\psi}(y,t).
$$

Here the source places the potential at the final position $x$. Using the initial position $y$ as in Eq. (5.2) gives the same first-order evolution for smooth functions.

Set $y=x+\xi$. For small $\epsilon$, the integrand oscillates rapidly and the characteristic contributing scale is $\lvert\xi\rvert\lesssim\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon/\mechanical m}$. Expand $\signal{\psi}(x+\xi,t)$ to second order in $\xi$ and the potential phase to first order in $\epsilon$. The odd Gaussian moment vanishes. The remaining moments, defined by convergence from the damped Gaussian, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int d\xi\, e^{i\mechanical m\xi^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon)}
&=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon}{\mechanical m}},\\
\int d\xi\,\xi^2 e^{i\mechanical m\xi^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon)}
&=\frac{i\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon}{\mechanical m}
\sqrt{\frac{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon}{\mechanical m}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Substitution gives

$$
\signal{\psi}(x,t+\epsilon)=\signal{\psi}(x,t)
+\frac{i\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon}{2\mechanical m}\frac{\partial^2\signal{\psi}}{\partial x^2}
-\frac{i\epsilon}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\energy V(x)\signal{\psi}(x,t)+O(\epsilon^2).
$$

Subtract $\signal{\psi}(x,t)$, divide by $\epsilon$, and let $\epsilon\to0$. Multiply by $i\mechanical{\hbar}$ to obtain

$$
i\mechanical{\hbar}\frac{\partial\signal{\psi}}{\partial t}
=-\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical m}\frac{\partial^2\signal{\psi}}{\partial x^2}
+\energy V(x)\signal{\psi}.
\tag{5.8}
$$

This is the [[04.00 Schrödinger Equation|Schrödinger equation]]. The path integral in Eq. (5.3) is its kernel for initial data $\delta(x-x_i)$. Equation (5.1) then evolves general initial data.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Position wave function | The probability amplitude in one dimension. |
| $x,y,\xi$ | $\unit{m}$ | Positions and displacement | The final and initial positions in one step and their difference. |
| $t,\epsilon$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time and time step | The time of the initial state and the duration of the step. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\energy V$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The multiplicative term in the Hamiltonian. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the kinetic and time-evolution terms. |
