---
title: "Free Particle"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For a free particle, Gaussian integrations give an exact propagator. Its phase is the classical action of the constant-velocity path.

When $\energy V=0$, the discretized action is quadratic in the intermediate positions. The integrals in Eq. (5.2) of [[05.02 Time Discretization and the Feynman Formula]] can be performed one at a time. By induction, after $n$ integrations the prefactor for the combined interval is $\sqrt{\mechanical m/(2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}(n+1)\epsilon)}$ and the exponent is $i\mechanical m(x_{n+1}-x_0)^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}(n+1)\epsilon)$. After $N-1$ integrations, $T=N\epsilon$ gives

$$
\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}(x_f,T;x_i,0)
=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}T}}\,
\exp\left[\frac{i\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2}{2\mechanical{\hbar}T}\right].
\tag{5.4}
$$

The phase is $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}/\mechanical{\hbar}$, where $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}=\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2/(2T)$ is the action along the free trajectory that joins the endpoints at constant velocity.

> [!theorem] Proposition 5.2. Free-particle propagation
> The propagator obeys the free Schrödinger equation in the final variables and its backward form in the initial variables. It is invariant under common translations of the endpoints in space or time.

To verify the forward equation, compute $i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_T\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}$ and compare it with $-\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}\partial_{x_f}^2\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}/(2\mechanical m)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Free propagator | The kernel for one-dimensional free evolution. |
| $x_i,x_f,x_k$ | $\unit{m}$ | Positions | The initial, final, and intermediate coordinates. |
| $T,\epsilon$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time intervals | The full propagation time and the time step. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}},\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action and reduced Planck constant | Their ratio is the propagator phase. |
