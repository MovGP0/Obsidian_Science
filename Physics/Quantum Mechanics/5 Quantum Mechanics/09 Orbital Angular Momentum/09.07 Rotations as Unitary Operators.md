---
title: "Rotations as Unitary Operators"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A spatial rotation acts as a unitary operator. The component of angular momentum along the rotation axis generates it.

Rotations about the $z$ axis are generated by $\mechanical{\hat L}_z$.

$$
\hat R_z(\alpha)=e^{-i\mechanical{\hat L}_z\alpha/\mechanical{\hbar}}.
$$

Acting on a spherical harmonic gives $Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi-\alpha)=e^{-im\alpha}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)$. For an arbitrary unit axis $\hat{\mathbf n}$,

$$
\hat R_{\hat{\mathbf n}}(\alpha)=e^{-i\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\alpha/\mechanical{\hbar}}.
$$

Spatial rotations form the group $\mathrm{SO}(3)$. In quantum mechanics they act as unitary operators on the Hilbert space. At fixed $\ell$, the $(2\ell+1)\times(2\ell+1)$ Wigner matrices $D^\ell_{mm'}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$, parameterized by Euler angles, mix the spherical harmonics within that subspace. See [[17.00 Symmetries and Conservation Laws]] for the representation structure.

## Physical meaning

The angular momentum algebra gives the spectrum from commutation relations and the single-valuedness condition. The same algebraic method applies to [[10.00 Spin|spin]]. Extending it to the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gives quark multiplets. These examples show how continuous symmetries organize states into representations.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat R_z,\hat R_{\hat{\mathbf n}}$ | $1$ | Rotation operators | The unitary maps for rotations about the specified axis. |
| $\mechanical{\hat L}_z,\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Angular momentum operators | The generators of rotations. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale that makes the rotation exponent dimensionless. |
| $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Rotation angles | The rotation parameter or the three Euler angles. |
| $\hat{\mathbf n}$ | $1$ | Rotation axis | A unit vector along the axis of rotation. |
| $D^\ell_{mm'},Y_{\ell m}$ | $1$ | Rotation matrix and spherical harmonic | The matrix element and angular basis function at fixed $\ell$. |
