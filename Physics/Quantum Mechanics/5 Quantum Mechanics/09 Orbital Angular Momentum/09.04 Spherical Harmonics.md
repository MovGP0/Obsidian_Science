title: "Spherical Harmonics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Spherical harmonics are the angular factors of the simultaneous eigenfunctions of squared orbital angular momentum and its $z$ component.

In spherical coordinates $(r,\theta,\phi)$, the angular state $\lvert\ell,m\rangle$ has position dependence of the form $\langle\mathbf r\mid\ell,m\rangle=f(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)$. The Hamiltonian determines the radial factor $f(r)$. The **spherical harmonics** have the form

$$
Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)
=(-1)^m\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}\,
P_\ell^m(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi}.
$$

This convention takes the associated Legendre functions $P_\ell^m$ without the Condon–Shortley phase. The formula is for $m\ge0$, and $Y_{\ell,-m}=(-1)^mY_{\ell m}^*$.

The spherical harmonics are simultaneous eigenfunctions of $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}$ and $\mechanical{\hat L}_z$. They form a complete orthonormal basis of $L^2(S^2)$. With $\Omega=(\theta,\phi)$ and $d\Omega=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$,

$$
\int_{S^2}Y_{\ell'm'}^*(\Omega)Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)\,d\Omega
=\delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'},\qquad
\sum_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}^*(\Omega)Y_{\ell m}(\Omega')
=\delta^{(2)}(\Omega-\Omega').
$$

## First spherical harmonics

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y_{00}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi}},\\
Y_{10}&=\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi}}\cos\theta,
&Y_{1,\pm1}&=\mp\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\pi}}\sin\theta\,e^{\pm i\phi},\\
Y_{20}&=\sqrt{\frac{5}{16\pi}}(3\cos^2\theta-1),
&Y_{2,\pm1}&=\mp\sqrt{\frac{15}{8\pi}}\sin\theta\cos\theta\,e^{\pm i\phi},\\
Y_{2,\pm2}&=\sqrt{\frac{15}{32\pi}}\sin^2\theta\,e^{\pm2i\phi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Chemistry uses the orbital labels $s$, $p$, and $d$ for $\ell=0,1,2$, respectively.

## Parity

Spatial inversion sends $\mathbf r\mapsto-\mathbf r$, or $(\theta,\phi)\mapsto(\pi-\theta,\phi+\pi)$. The harmonics transform as

$$
Y_{\ell m}(-\hat{\mathbf r})=(-1)^\ell Y_{\ell m}(\hat{\mathbf r}).
$$

Even $\ell$ gives even parity and odd $\ell$ gives odd parity. Electric dipole transitions with $\ell\mapsto\ell\pm1$ change parity.

## Example. Shapes of the angular densities

- $\lvert Y_{00}\rvert^2$ is constant. The $s$ state is spherically symmetric.
- $\lvert Y_{10}\rvert^2\propto\cos^2\theta$. The $p_z$ state has two lobes along $z$.
- $\lvert Y_{1,\pm1}\rvert^2\propto\sin^2\theta$. The complex states have an equatorial ring about $z$. The real combination $(Y_{1,-1}-Y_{11})/\sqrt2\propto\sin\theta\cos\phi$ gives $p_x$, and $(Y_{1,-1}+Y_{11})/(i\sqrt2)\propto\sin\theta\sin\phi$ gives $p_y$ up to an overall sign.
- $\lvert Y_{20}\rvert^2$ has two lobes along $\pm z$ and a smaller equatorial ring. This is the $d_{z^2}$ state.
