---
title: "Raising and Lowering Operators"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The angular momentum ladder operators change the magnetic quantum number by one while preserving the squared angular momentum.

Define

$$
\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm=\mechanical{\hat L}_x\pm i\mechanical{\hat L}_y.
$$

They are adjoints, $\mechanical{\hat L}_+^\dagger=\mechanical{\hat L}_-$. Their commutators are

$$
[\mechanical{\hat L}_z,\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm]=\pm\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm,\qquad
[\mechanical{\hat L}_+,\mechanical{\hat L}_-]=2\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat L}_z.
\tag{9.1}
$$

The algebra also gives

$$
\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}
=\mechanical{\hat L}_\mp\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm
+\mechanical{\hat L}_z^{\,2}\pm\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat L}_z.
$$

If $\mechanical{\hat L}_z\lvert\ell,m\rangle=m\mechanical{\hbar}\lvert\ell,m\rangle$, then Eq. (9.1) gives

$$
\mechanical{\hat L}_z(\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm\lvert\ell,m\rangle)
=(m\pm1)\mechanical{\hbar}(\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm\lvert\ell,m\rangle).
$$

When the result is nonzero, $\mechanical{\hat L}_+$ raises $m$ by one and $\mechanical{\hat L}_-$ lowers it by one. Both commute with $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}$, so its eigenvalue stays the same.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Ladder operators | The combinations that raise or lower the magnetic quantum number. |
| $\mechanical{\hat L}_x,\mechanical{\hat L}_y,\mechanical{\hat L}_z$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Angular momentum components | The three generators of rotations about Cartesian axes. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}$ | $\unit{J^2\,s^2}$ | Squared angular momentum | The operator whose eigenvalue is unchanged by the ladder. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The step size of the $z$ component. |
| $\ell,m$ | $1$ | Angular quantum numbers | The labels for the squared magnitude and $z$ component. |
