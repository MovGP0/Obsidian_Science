---
title: "L̂² as the Angular Laplace Operator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Squared orbital angular momentum is proportional to the angular Laplacian. In a central potential it contributes the centrifugal term to the radial equation.

In spherical coordinates,

$$
\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}=-\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}
\left[\frac1{\sin\theta}\partial_\theta(\sin\theta\,\partial_\theta)
+\frac1{\sin^2\theta}\partial_\phi^2\right].
$$

The three-dimensional Laplacian separates into radial and angular parts.

$$
\nabla^2=\frac1{r^2}\partial_r(r^2\partial_r)
-\frac{\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}}{\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}r^2}.
$$

For a central potential $\energy V(r)$,

$$
\energy{\hat H}
=-\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical m}\frac1{r^2}\partial_r(r^2\partial_r)
+\frac{\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical m r^2}+\energy V(r).
$$

The second term is the quantum centrifugal energy, corresponding to the classical $\mechanical L^{\,2}/(2\mechanical m r^2)$. For the separated state $\signal{\psi}=R(r)Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\phi)$, replace $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}$ by $\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}\ell(\ell+1)$. This gives a radial equation for $R(r)$, which is used in [[12.00 Hydrogen Atom]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}$ | $\unit{J^2\,s^2}$ | Squared angular momentum | The angular Laplacian multiplied by $-\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}$. |
| $r$ | $\unit m$ | Radius | The distance from the center of the potential. |
| $\theta,\phi$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Polar angle and azimuth | The independent variables of the angular part. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy V$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and potential energy | The energy operator and its central potential. |
| $R(r),Y_{\ell m}$ | $\unit{m^{-3/2}},1$ for normalized separated factors | Radial and angular factors | The two parts of the position wave function. |
| $\ell,m$ | $1$ | Angular quantum numbers | The labels of the squared magnitude and axial projection. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass in the kinetic operator. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale of the angular kinetic term. |
