> [!TLDR]
> The five postulates cover states, observables, measurement probabilities, state updates, and time evolution.

| Postulate | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| I. State | A normalized vector $\lvert\psi\rangle\in\mathcal H$ represents the state up to global phase. |
| II. Observable | A self-adjoint operator $\hat A$ represents the observable. |
| III. Measurement | The outcomes belong to $\sigma(\hat A)$. For a nondegenerate eigenvalue $a_n$, the probability is $\lvert\langle a_n\mid\psi\rangle\rvert^2$. |
| IV. Collapse | Given outcome $a$, the state becomes $\hat P_a\lvert\psi\rangle/\lVert\hat P_a\lvert\psi\rangle\rVert$ for an ideal projective measurement. |
| V. Time evolution | $i\hbar\,d\lvert\psi\rangle/dt=\energy{\hat H}\lvert\psi\rangle$. |

The **composition postulate** assigns $\mathcal H_1\otimes\mathcal H_2$ to a system of two subsystems. See [[14.00 Many-Particle Systems and Identical Particles|Chapter 14]]. Some accounts number composition as a sixth postulate.

## Physical significance

The five postulates describe the operations used throughout this book. The Copenhagen, many-worlds, and pilot-wave interpretations differ in how they explain the measurement update. The operational rule gives the predictions used here.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a,a_n$ | — | Measurement value | An eigenvalue with the unit of the observed quantity. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the evolution. |
| $\energy{\hat H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The generator of time evolution. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale in the equation of motion. |
