---
title: "First Complete Example – Spin ½"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A spin-½ system shows how state preparation, measurement, and Hamiltonian evolution fit together.

The simplest Hilbert space that gives more than one state is $\mathbb C^2$. Choose $\lvert+z\rangle$ and $\lvert-z\rangle$ as the basis. The observables for the spin components are

$$
\mechanical{\hat S}_x=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_x,\qquad
\mechanical{\hat S}_y=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_y,\qquad
\mechanical{\hat S}_z=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sigma_z,
$$

where $\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z$ are the Pauli matrices. See [[10.00 Spin]]. The components obey $[\mechanical{\hat S}_i,\mechanical{\hat S}_j]=i\hbar\sum_k\varepsilon_{ijk}\mechanical{\hat S}_k$.

The operator $\mechanical{\hat S}_z$ has eigenvalues $\pm\hbar/2$ with eigenstates $\lvert\pm z\rangle$. Prepare the state

$$
\lvert\psi(0)\rangle=\lvert+x\rangle=\frac{\lvert+z\rangle+\lvert-z\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

A measurement of $\mechanical{\hat S}_z$ gives

$$
P(+\hbar/2)=\lvert\langle+z\mid\psi\rangle\rvert^2=\tfrac12,\qquad
P(-\hbar/2)=\tfrac12.
$$

Thus $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_z\rangle=0$ and $\Delta\mechanical{S}_z=\hbar/2$. A measurement of $\mechanical{\hat S}_x$ on the same preparation gives $+\hbar/2$ with probability one and zero standard deviation because $\lvert+x\rangle$ is its eigenstate.

## Time evolution in a magnetic field

For $\magnetic{\vec B}=\magnetic{B}\hat z$, the interaction Hamiltonian is $\energy{\hat H}=-\gamma\magnetic{\vec B}\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\vec S}}=-\gamma\magnetic{B}\mechanical{\hat S}_z$, with gyromagnetic ratio $\gamma$. The eigenstates $\lvert\pm z\rangle$ have energies $\energy{E}_\pm=\mp\gamma\magnetic{B}\hbar/2$. Thus

$$
\lvert\psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}
\left(e^{-i\energy{E}_+t/\hbar}\lvert+z\rangle+e^{-i\energy{E}_-t/\hbar}\lvert-z\rangle\right).
$$

Define the signed Larmor frequency $\signal{\omega}_L=-\gamma\magnetic{B}$. The spin points along a direction in the $xy$ plane at angle $\signal{\omega}_Lt$ from the $x$ axis. The expectation values are $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_x(t)\rangle=(\hbar/2)\cos(\signal{\omega}_Lt)$ and $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_y(t)\rangle=(\hbar/2)\sin(\signal{\omega}_Lt)$. The mean spin precesses about $\magnetic{\vec B}$, like a classical magnetic moment. See [[10.04 Larmor Precession]].

The example uses all five postulates. It starts with a state and noncommuting observables, gives measurement probabilities and the corresponding state update, and describes unitary time evolution under $\energy{\hat H}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat S}_i,\Delta\mechanical{S}_z$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Spin and uncertainty | The spin components and the spread of the $z$ measurement. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale in the spin algebra and the dynamical phase. |
| $P$ | $1$ | Measurement probability | The probability of the indicated spin outcome. |
| $\magnetic{\vec B},\magnetic{B}$ | $\unit{T}$ | Magnetic flux density | The field vector and its component along the $z$ axis. |
| $\gamma$ | $\unit{s^{-1}\,T^{-1}}$ | Gyromagnetic ratio | The coupling between spin and magnetic flux density. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy{E}_\pm$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energy levels | The magnetic interaction operator and its two eigenvalues. |
| $\signal{\omega}_L$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Larmor angular frequency | The signed precession rate about the field. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The time since the spin was prepared. |
