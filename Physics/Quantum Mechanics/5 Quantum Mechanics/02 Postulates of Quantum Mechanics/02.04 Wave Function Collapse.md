title: "Wave Function Collapse"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An ideal projective measurement leaves the system in the normalized projection onto the eigenspace of the observed result.

> [!definition] Definition 2.4 | Postulate IV. Wave Function Collapse
> Immediately after an ideal projective measurement of $\hat A$ yields $a$, the state is
>
> $$
> \lvert\psi'\rangle=\frac{\hat P_a\lvert\psi\rangle}{\lVert\hat P_a\lvert\psi\rangle\rVert}.
> $$
>
> The projector $\hat P_a$ selects the eigenspace of $\hat A$ for outcome $a$. The denominator normalizes the state.

If $a$ is nondegenerate, $\hat P_a=\lvert a\rangle\langle a\rvert$, and the resulting state is $\lvert a\rangle$ up to a global phase. If $a$ is degenerate, the resulting state depends on the component of the initial state in $\mathcal H_a$. The measurement selects that component.

## Repeat measurements

Two successive ideal measurements of the same observable give the same result if the intervening time evolution is negligible. After the first result $a$, the state is already in the corresponding eigenspace. A second measurement yields $a$ with probability one. In the nondegenerate case this probability is $\lvert\langle a\mid a\rangle\rvert^2=1$.

## Example. Stern–Gerlach filter

Prepare an atom in the spin state $\lvert\psi\rangle=\cos(\theta/2)\lvert+z\rangle+\sin(\theta/2)\lvert-z\rangle$. Measure the spin component along $z$. The states $\lvert\pm z\rangle$ are eigenstates of $\mechanical{\hat S}_z$ with eigenvalues $\pm\hbar/2$. See [[10.00 Spin]] for the spin operators. The probabilities are

$$
P(+z)=\cos^2(\theta/2),\qquad P(-z)=\sin^2(\theta/2).
$$

If the outcome is $+z$, the state becomes $\lvert+z\rangle$. Immediate repeats of the $\mechanical{\hat S}_z$ measurement give $+\hbar/2$.

Rotate the apparatus to measure $\mechanical{\hat S}_x$. Its eigenbasis is $\lvert\pm x\rangle=(\lvert+z\rangle\pm\lvert-z\rangle)/\sqrt2$. The input state $\lvert+z\rangle=(\lvert+x\rangle+\lvert-x\rangle)/\sqrt2$ gives $P(+x)=P(-x)=1/2$. The measurement projects onto $\lvert+x\rangle$ or $\lvert-x\rangle$. The two spin components do not commute, and neither new state is an eigenstate of $\mechanical{\hat S}_z$. The previously definite $z$ component is now uncertain.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a$ | — | Measurement result | The eigenvalue selected by the measurement. Its unit depends on the observable. |
| $P$ | $1$ | Measurement probability | The probability of the indicated outcome. |
| $\theta$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Preparation angle | The angle that sets the amplitudes in the spin state. |
| $\mechanical{\hat S}_x,\mechanical{\hat S}_z$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Spin components | The spin observables along the two measurement axes. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Twice the magnitude of a spin-½ projection. |
