---
title: "Wannier Functions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A Fourier transform of the Bloch states in one band gives Wannier functions localized near individual cells. Matrix elements between these functions describe hopping.

Bloch states $\signal\psi_{nk}$ are delocalized across the crystal. For processes such as hopping between neighboring sites, use the Wannier basis defined by a Fourier transform within a band.

$$
\signal w_{n,R}(x)=\frac{a}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/a}^{\pi/a}e^{-ikR}\signal\psi_{nk}(x)\,\mathrm dk,
\qquad R=ja,\quad j\in\mathbb Z.
$$

With consistent Bloch normalization, $\langle\signal w_{n,R}\mid\signal w_{n,R'}\rangle=\delta_{RR'}$. A suitable smooth choice of Bloch phases localizes the functions near their respective cells. The phase choice affects their localization and relates to the [[18.00 Geometric Phases|geometric phase]] of the band.

The projected Hamiltonian has hopping matrix elements given by the Fourier coefficients of $\energy E_n(k)$. With the convention $\energy E(k)=\energy E_0-2\energy t\cos(ka)$ for a nearest-neighbor band, the hopping energy is

$$
\energy t=-\frac{a}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/a}^{\pi/a}\energy E_n(k)e^{ika}\,\mathrm dk.
$$

This gives the relation between dispersion and the matrix element for hopping between adjacent cells.

> [!note] Range of hopping
> The source describes the projected Hamiltonian as a nearest-neighbor model. In general it can also contain longer-range hopping from higher Fourier harmonics of $\energy E_n(k)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a,R$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lattice period and cell label | $R=ja$ identifies the cell of a Wannier function. |
| $k$ | $\unit{rad\,m^{-1}}$ | Bloch wave number | The variable of the Fourier transform. |
| $\signal w_{n,R}$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Wannier wave function | A normalized localized state in band $n$. |
| $\energy E_n(k),\energy t$ | $\unit{J}$ | Band energy and hopping energy | The dispersion and the sign-adjusted nearest-neighbor Fourier coefficient. |
