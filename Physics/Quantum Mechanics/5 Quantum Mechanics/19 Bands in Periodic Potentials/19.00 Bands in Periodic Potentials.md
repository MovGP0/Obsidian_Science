> [!TLDR]
> Discrete translation symmetry gives Bloch states in a periodic potential. Their energies form allowed bands separated by gaps, which help explain the electrical properties of crystals.

Consider a particle in a one-dimensional potential $\energy V(x+a)=\energy V(x)$ with lattice period $a>0$. Discrete translation symmetry makes the energy eigenstates products of plane waves and periodic factors. In an infinite lattice, the spectrum typically forms allowed energy bands separated by gaps. The arrangement and filling of the bands help distinguish metals, insulators, and semiconductors.

This chapter develops the Bloch theorem, the exactly solvable Kronig–Penney model, and the origin of band gaps. It also introduces effective mass, the density of states, and Wannier functions.

## Chapter summary

- In a potential of period $a$, translation by $a$ commutes with the Hamiltonian. The two operators share a generalized spectral basis.
- Bloch eigenfunctions have the form $\signal\psi_{nk}(x)=e^{ikx}\signal u_{nk}(x)$ with $\signal u_{nk}$ periodic and $k\in[-\pi/a,\pi/a)$.
- The band energies $\energy E_n(k)$ are continuous and $2\pi/a$-periodic in $k$.
- In the delta-barrier Kronig–Penney model, real solutions for $k$ of $\cos(ka)=\cos(qa)+(\mechanical m\alpha/\mechanical{\hbar}^2q)\sin(qa)$ determine the allowed bands.
- Bragg reflection opens a gap at the first zone boundary. In the nearly free limit, the first gap is $2\lvert\energy V_{2\pi/a}\rvert$.
- The local band curvature gives $1/\mechanical m^*=\mechanical{\hbar}^{-2}\partial_k^2\energy E$. It sets the response to a weak slowly varying force near an extremum.
- In one dimension, a quadratic band edge gives a van Hove singularity proportional to $1/\sqrt{\lvert\energy E-\energy E_{\mathrm{edge}}\rvert}$ on the allowed side.
- Fourier transforms of Bloch states give localized Wannier functions. The dispersion determines the hopping matrix elements in this basis.
- Partially filled bands give metallic behavior in the basic band model. Filled bands with a gap account for band insulators and semiconductors.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lattice period | The spacing of equivalent cells. |
| $k,q$ | $\unit{rad\,m^{-1}}$ | Wave numbers | The Bloch label and the free-region wave number in the delta-barrier model. |
| $\energy E_n(k)$ | $\unit{J}$ | Band energy | The dispersion of band $n$. |
| $\mechanical m^*$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Effective mass | The mass inferred from the local band curvature. |
