---
title: "Density of States in One Dimension"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The one-dimensional density of states is large where a band disperses slowly. A quadratic band edge gives an integrable inverse-square-root divergence.

For a system of length $L$ with periodic boundary conditions, count one state per $\Delta k=2\pi/L$ for each band and spin. To obtain the density per unit energy and length, change variables from $k$ to $\energy E$. For two symmetric branches $\pm k$ at the same energy,

$$
g_n(\energy E)=\frac1\pi\left\lvert\frac{\mathrm dk}{\mathrm d\energy E_n}\right\rvert,
$$

where the derivative is evaluated on one branch. For an arbitrary number of branches, sum $\lvert\mathrm dk/\mathrm d\energy E_n\rvert/(2\pi)$ over all distinct solutions $k$ of $\energy E_n(k)=\energy E$. Multiply by the spin degeneracy if it is included in the count.

At a quadratic band edge $\energy E_{\mathrm{edge}}$, the derivative $\partial_k\energy E_n$ vanishes and $g_n(\energy E)$ diverges as $1/\sqrt{\lvert\energy E-\energy E_{\mathrm{edge}}\rvert}$ from inside the band. This is a **van Hove singularity**. The divergence is integrable. The density of states vanishes in a gap. These features affect transport and optical properties.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $g_n(\energy E)$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,m^{-1}}$ | Density of states | The number of states per unit energy and length for band $n$ and one spin. |
| $L$ | $\unit{m}$ | System length | Sets the discrete wave-number spacing before the infinite-system limit. |
| $\energy E_{\mathrm{edge}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Band edge | The energy of a local extremum of the band. |
