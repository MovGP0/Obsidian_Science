title: "Effective Mass"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The curvature of a band near an extremum defines the effective mass. Positive curvature gives positive effective mass, and negative curvature gives negative effective mass.

Near a smooth band extremum at $k_0$, expand the energy to second order.

$$
\energy E_n(k)\simeq\energy E_n(k_0)+
\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m_n^*}(k-k_0)^2.
$$

The effective mass is defined by

$$
\frac{1}{\mechanical m_n^*}
=\frac{1}{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}
\left.\frac{\partial^2\energy E_n}{\partial k^2}\right\rvert_{k=k_0}.
$$

A wave packet with $k$ near $k_0$ responds to a slowly varying force like a free particle of mass $\mechanical m_n^*$ within this approximation. Near a band maximum, the negative curvature gives $\mechanical m_n^*<0$. The acceleration is then opposite to the force on the electron. This sign is the basis of the hole description near the top of a nearly filled band. Figure 19.3 compares the exact and parabolic dispersions.

> [!theorem] Proposition 19.1 · Effective mass in a tight-binding band
> For $\energy E(k)=\energy E_0-2\energy t\cos(ka)$ with hopping energy $\energy t>0$, the mass at $k=0$ is $\mechanical m^*=\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\energy t a^2)$. The mass at $k=\pi/a$ is $-\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\energy t a^2)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical m_n^*$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Effective mass | Its inverse is the band curvature divided by $\mechanical{\hbar}^2$. |
| $k,k_0$ | $\unit{rad\,m^{-1}}$ | Wave numbers | The Bloch wave number and its value at the band extremum. |
| $\energy E_n(k),\energy t$ | $\unit{J}$ | Band energy and hopping energy | The dispersion and the coupling scale in the tight-binding example. |
| $a$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lattice period | The distance between neighboring sites. |
