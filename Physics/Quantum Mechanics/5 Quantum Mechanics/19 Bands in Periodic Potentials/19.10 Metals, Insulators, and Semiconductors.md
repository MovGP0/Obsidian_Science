---
title: "Metals, Insulators, and Semiconductors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In the independent-electron band model, a partially filled band gives metallic behavior. A full set of bands separated from empty bands by a gap gives insulating behavior at zero temperature.

Consider $N$ cells with periodic boundary conditions on a ring of length $L=Na$. Each band has $N$ distinct Bloch wave numbers and accommodates $2N$ electrons when spin is included and the bands are spin degenerate. Let $\nu$ be the number of electrons per cell. At zero temperature, an odd integer $\nu$ leaves the last occupied band half filled in this model. The Fermi level lies inside the band, with unoccupied states at arbitrarily small excitation energies above it. This gives metallic behavior.

If $\nu$ is even and all bands below the Fermi level are full, consider the gap between the last filled band, the **valence band**, and the first empty band, the **conduction band**. When there is a positive gap, the undoped material has no mobile band carriers at zero temperature and is a band insulator. If the gap is small enough, thermal excitation can populate the conduction band and leave holes in the valence band. Doping can also supply electrons or holes. This is the semiconductor regime. Band filling and the size of the gap give the three-way classification in the basic model.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $N,\nu$ | $1$ | Cell count and electrons per cell | The quantities that set the available states and their filling. |
| $L,a$ | $\unit{m}$ | Ring length and lattice period | The model uses $L=Na$. |
| $\energy E_F$ | $\unit{J}$ | Fermi energy | Its position relative to the bands determines the available low-energy excitations. |
