---
title: "Discrete Translation Symmetry"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Translation by one lattice period commutes with the Hamiltonian. Its eigenvalue labels a Bloch wave number modulo the reciprocal-lattice period.

Consider the one-dimensional Hamiltonian

$$
\energy{\hat H}=\frac{\mechanical{\hat p}^2}{2\mechanical m}+\energy V(\hat x).
\tag{19.1}
$$

The potential has period $a$, so $\energy V(x+a)=\energy V(x)$ for all $x\in\mathbb R$. Define the unitary translation $\hat T_a$ on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ by

$$
(\hat T_a\signal{\psi})(x)=\signal{\psi}(x-a).
$$

Translations commute with the second derivative, and $\energy V(\hat x-a)=\energy V(\hat x)$ by periodicity. Therefore

$$
[\hat T_a,\energy{\hat H}]=0.
\tag{19.2}
$$

The two operators can be diagonalized together, using generalized eigenstates for the infinite lattice. A translation eigenvalue has magnitude one and can be written $e^{-ika}$ with $k\in\mathbb R$. Changing $k$ by $2\pi/a$ leaves this eigenvalue unchanged, so choose

$$
k\in\left[-\frac{\pi}{a},\frac{\pi}{a}\right).
$$

This interval is the **first Brillouin zone**. The Bloch wave number $k$ labels the irreducible unitary representations of the Abelian group generated by $\hat T_a$. The associated quasimomentum is $\mechanical{\hbar}k$ modulo $2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}/a$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lattice period | The smallest positive spatial period of the potential. |
| $k$ | $\unit{rad\,m^{-1}}$ | Bloch wave number | Labels translation eigenvalues modulo $2\pi/a$. |
| $\mechanical{\hat p},\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}},\unit{kg}$ | Momentum operator and mass | The quantities that give the kinetic term. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy V$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and periodic potential energy | The total energy operator and its potential term. |
