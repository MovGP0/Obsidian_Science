> [!TLDR]
> One-dimensional potentials show how boundary conditions quantize bound states and how wave functions behave in scattering and classically forbidden regions.

One-dimensional systems give exact solutions for basic quantum effects. These include quantization of bound levels, penetration into classically forbidden regions, scattering states, and the role of symmetry. The simpler equations make these effects easier to isolate before treating three-dimensional systems.

Solve the [[04.02 Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation|time-independent Schrödinger equation]] with the appropriate boundary conditions.

$$
-\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical m}\signal{\psi}''(x)+\energy V(x)\signal{\psi}(x)
=\energy E\signal{\psi}(x).
\tag{6.1}
$$

The shape of $\energy V(x)$ determines the spectrum. For real, piecewise continuous potentials, require continuity of $\signal{\psi}$ and $\signal{\psi}'$ at finite jumps in $\energy V$. A delta-function potential is a singular case. There $\signal{\psi}$ remains continuous and $\signal{\psi}'$ has a finite jump that follows from Eq. (6.1).

## Chapter summary

- A [[06.01 Free Particle|free particle]] has a continuous spectrum $\energy E\ge0$ with distributionally normalized plane waves.
- A [[06.02 Potential Step|potential step]] causes partial reflection even for $\energy E>\energy V_0$. Below the step, the amplitude falls exponentially with $\signal{\kappa}=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V_0-\energy E)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$.
- The [[06.03 Infinite Potential Well|infinite well]] on $[0,L]$ gives $\energy E_n=n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical mL^2)$ and $\signal{\psi}_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$.
- The [[06.04 Finite Potential Well|finite well]] has finitely many bound states. The even and odd conditions are $z\tan z=\sqrt{z_0^2-z^2}$ and $-z\cot z=\sqrt{z_0^2-z^2}$, respectively.
- The [[06.05 Attractive Delta Potential|attractive delta potential]] has one bound state with $\energy E_0=-\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2})$.
- The [[06.06 General Theorems in One Dimension|one-dimensional theorems]] give nondegenerate bound states, $n-1$ nodes in $\signal{\psi}_n$, and definite parity for even potentials.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x,L$ | $\unit m$ | Position and well width | The coordinate and the distance between the infinite-well walls. |
| $\energy E,\energy E_n,\energy V,\energy V_0$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The particle energy, discrete levels, potential, and step height or well depth. |
| $\signal{\psi}_n$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Bound eigenfunction | The normalized position-space amplitude of level $n$. |
| $\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Decay constant | The exponential decay rate in a classically forbidden region. |
| $z,z_0$ | $1$ | Scaled wavenumber and well parameter | The variables of the finite-well quantization conditions. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Delta strength | The magnitude of the integrated delta potential. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the kinetic energy operator. |
