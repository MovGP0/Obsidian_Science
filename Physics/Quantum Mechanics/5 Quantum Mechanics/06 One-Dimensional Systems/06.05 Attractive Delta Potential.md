title: "Attractive Delta Potential"

> [!TLDR]
> An attractive delta potential is the zero-width limit of a narrow well at fixed integrated strength. It gives a finite jump in the derivative of the continuous wave function.

Take $\energy V(x)=-\mechanical{\alpha}\delta(x)$ with $\mechanical{\alpha}>0$. This models a very narrow, deep well at finite $\energy V_0a$. Integrate the time-independent Schrödinger equation over $[-\epsilon,\epsilon]$ and let $\epsilon\to0$. The finite terms vanish and the delta function gives

$$
\signal{\psi}'(0^+)-\signal{\psi}'(0^-)
=-\frac{2\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha}}{\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}}\signal{\psi}(0).
$$

The derivative has a finite jump, while the wave function is continuous.

## Bound state

For $\energy E<0$, let $\signal{\kappa}=\sqrt{-2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. The decaying solutions on the two sides must have the same value $\signal A$ at zero, so

$$
\signal{\psi}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\signal A e^{\signal{\kappa}x}, & x<0,\\
\signal A e^{-\signal{\kappa}x}, & x>0.
\end{cases}
$$

The jump is $-2\signal A\signal{\kappa}=-2\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha}\signal A/\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}$, which gives $\signal{\kappa}=\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha}/\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}$. There is exactly one bound state with

$$
\energy E_0=-\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}}.
$$

Any attractive delta potential in one dimension supports one bound state. Its wave function is even with a characteristic length $1/\signal{\kappa}=\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}/(\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha})$.

Its derivative jumps at $x=0$ because of the singular potential. The jump $\signal{\psi}'(0^+)-\signal{\psi}'(0^-)=-2\signal{\kappa}\signal{\psi}(0)$ gives the matching condition.

## Scattering states

For $\energy E>0$, there are scattering states. Apply continuity and the derivative jump to the incident, reflected, and transmitted waves. With $\signal k=\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$, this gives

$$
\signal T=\frac{1}{1+(\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha}/(\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}\signal k))^2},
\qquad
\signal R=1-\signal T.
$$

Transmission tends to one as $\signal k\to\infty$ and tends to zero as $\signal k\to0$. At low energies the delta potential reflects almost all of the incident current, even though the potential is attractive.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy V,\energy E,\energy E_0$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The potential, particle energy, and bound-state energy. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Delta strength | The positive magnitude of the integrated attractive potential. |
| $\delta(x)$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Dirac delta | The distribution that localizes the potential at zero. |
| $\signal{\psi},\signal A$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ for the bound state | Wave function and amplitude | The bound-state wave function and its value at the origin. |
| $\signal k,\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber and decay constant | The spatial frequency in scattering and the exponential decay rate in the bound state. |
| $x,1/\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit m$ | Position and decay length | The position coordinate and characteristic extent of the bound state. |
| $\signal R,\signal T$ | $1$ | Reflection and transmission coefficients | The fractions of incident current reflected and transmitted. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the jump and scattering equations. |
