---
title: "One-Dimensional Systems Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Find energy levels, probability currents, and matching conditions for one-dimensional potentials.

1. For an electron in an infinite well of width $L=1\,\unit{nm}$, calculate (a) the energies of $\signal{\psi}_1$, $\signal{\psi}_2$, and $\signal{\psi}_3$, and (b) the wavelength of the photon emitted in the transition $2\to1$.  
   **Result.** (a) $0.376$, $1.50$, and $3.38\,\unit{eV}$. (b) $\signal{\lambda}\simeq1.10\,\unit{\mu m}$.
2. Calculate $\signal R$ and $\signal T$ for a step with $\energy V_0=3\energy E$.  
   **Result.** $\signal R=1$ and $\signal T=0$. The current is totally reflected, while the wave penetrates the forbidden region exponentially.
3. Derive the bound-state energy of the attractive delta potential by evaluating $\int_{-\epsilon}^{+\epsilon}\signal{\psi}''\,dx$ in the limit $\epsilon\to0$.
4. Verify that the eigenfunctions of the infinite well are orthonormal.
5. For the finite well with $\energy V_0a^2=5\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}/\mechanical m$, determine the number of bound states graphically and estimate the ground-state energy.
6. A particle in the infinite well starts in $\signal{\psi}(x,0)=(\signal{\psi}_1(x)+\signal{\psi}_2(x))/\sqrt2$. Calculate $\langle\hat x(t)\rangle$ and the oscillation period.  
   **Result.** $\langle\hat x(t)\rangle=L/2-[16L/(9\pi^2)]\cos[(\energy E_2-\energy E_1)t/\mechanical{\hbar}]$ and $T=2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}/(\energy E_2-\energy E_1)=4\mechanical mL^2/(3\pi\mechanical{\hbar})$.
7. Show that $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle=0$ for every infinite-well eigenstate. Calculate $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}^{\,2}\rangle$ for $\signal{\psi}_n$.  
   **Result.** $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}^{\,2}\rangle=n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}/L^2=2\mechanical m\energy E_n$.
8. Find the transmission coefficient of the attractive delta potential for $\energy E>0$. Verify that $\signal T(k)\to1$ as $\signal k\to\infty$.  
   **Result.** $\signal T(k)=[1+(\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha}/(\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}\signal k))^2]^{-1}$.
9. Show that the infinite-well ground state has $\Delta x\,\Delta\mechanical p=\mechanical{\hbar}\sqrt{\pi^2-6}/(2\sqrt3)\simeq0.568\mechanical{\hbar}>\mechanical{\hbar}/2$. The product lies above the Heisenberg lower bound.
10. **Repulsive delta barrier.** Calculate $\signal T$ and $\signal R$ for $\energy V(x)=+\mechanical{\alpha}\delta(x)$ with $\mechanical{\alpha}>0$ and $\energy E>0$. Show that there are no bound states.  
    **Result.** $\signal T(k)=[1+(\mechanical m\mechanical{\alpha}/(\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}\signal k))^2]^{-1}$ and $\signal R=1-\signal T$. The transmission depends only on $\mechanical{\alpha}^{\,2}$.
11. **Asymmetric well.** Consider a well with $\energy V(x)=-\energy V_0$ for $-a<x<a$, $\energy V(x)=\infty$ for $x<-a$, and $\energy V(x)=0$ for $x>a$. Determine the quantization condition. Does an arbitrarily shallow well still have at least one bound state?

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x,L,a$ | $\unit m$ | Position and well dimensions | The coordinate, infinite-well width, and finite-well half-width. |
| $\energy E,\energy E_n,\energy V,\energy V_0$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The particle energy, eigenvalues, potential, and positive step height or well depth. |
| $\signal{\psi}_n,\signal{\psi}(x,t)$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Wave functions | An eigenstate and the evolving superposition in position space. |
| $\signal{\lambda}$ | $\unit m$ | Wavelength | The wavelength of the emitted photon. |
| $\signal R,\signal T$ | $1$ | Reflection and transmission coefficients | The fractions of incident current. |
| $\signal k$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | The oscillation rate of an incident wave. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Delta strength | The magnitude of the integrated delta potential. |
| $\mechanical{\hat p},\Delta\mechanical p$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum operator and uncertainty | The momentum observable and its standard deviation. |
| $\Delta x$ | $\unit m$ | Position uncertainty | The standard deviation of position. |
| $t,T$ | $\unit s$ | Time and period | The elapsed time and the oscillation period. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the energy, momentum, and time evolution formulas. |
