---
title: "Finite Potential Well"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A finite square well has a finite number of bound states. Matching the interior standing waves to exterior decaying tails gives separate conditions for even and odd states.

Take $\energy V(x)=-\energy V_0$ for $\lvert x\rvert<a$ and $\energy V(x)=0$ outside, with $\energy V_0>0$. For bound states, $-\energy V_0<\energy E<0$. Define $\signal{\kappa}=\sqrt{-2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ and $\signal k=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E+\energy V_0)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. The solutions in the three regions are

$$
\signal{\psi}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\signal A e^{\signal{\kappa}x}, & x<-a,\\
\signal B\cos(\signal kx)+\signal C\sin(\signal kx), & \lvert x\rvert<a,\\
\signal D e^{-\signal{\kappa}x}, & x>a.
\end{cases}
$$

Discard the growing exponential on each side. Because the potential is even, use definite parity to match the two independent solutions.

- **Even states.** Set $\signal C=0$. Continuity at $x=a$ gives $\signal B\cos(\signal ka)=\signal D e^{-\signal{\kappa}a}$ and $-\signal B\signal k\sin(\signal ka)=-\signal D\signal{\kappa}e^{-\signal{\kappa}a}$. Divide the derivative condition by the value condition to obtain $\signal k\tan(\signal ka)=\signal{\kappa}$.
- **Odd states.** Set $\signal B=0$. The corresponding conditions give $-\signal k\cot(\signal ka)=\signal{\kappa}$.

With $z=\signal ka$ and $z_0^2=2\mechanical m\energy V_0a^2/\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}$, the conditions become

$$
z\tan z=\sqrt{z_0^2-z^2},\qquad
-z\cot z=\sqrt{z_0^2-z^2}.
$$

Find the roots at the intersections of $z\tan z$ or $-z\cot z$ with the quarter circle $\sqrt{z_0^2-z^2}$. The number of bound states is finite.

- For $0<z_0<\pi/2$, there is one bound state, which is even.
- For $\pi/2<z_0<\pi$, there are two bound states, one even and one odd.
- In general, there are $\lceil 2z_0/\pi\rceil$ bound states. At a threshold, a new zero-energy solution is outside the bound spectrum.

In one dimension an attractive square well has at least one bound state, however shallow the well. In three dimensions a finite spherical well needs a minimum depth to bind a particle. Figure 6.2 shows a bound eigenfunction that oscillates inside the well and decays exponentially outside it.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x,a$ | $\unit m$ | Position and half-width | The coordinate and the distance from the center to an edge. |
| $\energy V,\energy V_0,\energy E$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential, depth, and energy | The well profile, its positive depth, and a negative bound-state energy. |
| $\signal{\psi}$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Position wave function | The normalized bound-state amplitude. |
| $\signal A,\signal B,\signal C,\signal D$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Wave coefficients | The amplitudes of the interior and exterior solutions. |
| $\signal k,\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber and decay constant | The interior oscillation rate and exterior exponential decay rate. |
| $z,z_0$ | $1$ | Scaled wavenumber and depth parameter | The variables of the graphical quantization conditions. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the quantization conditions. |
