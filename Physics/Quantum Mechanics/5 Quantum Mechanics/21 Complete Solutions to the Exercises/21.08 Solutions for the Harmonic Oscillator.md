## Exercise 1 · Trap frequency for carbon-12

The ground energy is $\energy E_0=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega/2=\quantity{0.1}{eV}$. Hence $\mechanical{\hbar}\omega=\quantity{0.2}{eV}$ and $\omega\simeq\quantity{3.04\times10^{14}}{rad\,s^{-1}}$. The ordinary frequency is $\nu=\omega/(2\pi)\simeq\quantity{4.84\times10^{13}}{Hz}$, in the infrared range.

## Exercise 2 · Position second moment in an energy eigenstate

Set $x_0=\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}/(\mechanical m\omega)}$ and $\hat x=(x_0/\sqrt2)(\hat a+\hat a^\dagger)$. In $\langle n\rvert\hat x^2\lvert n\rangle$, the terms $\hat a^2$ and $\hat a^{\dagger2}$ have zero expectation because they change $n$. The terms $\hat a\hat a^\dagger$ and $\hat a^\dagger\hat a$ give $n+1$ and $n$ respectively. Thus

$$
\langle n\rvert\hat x^2\lvert n\rangle=\frac{x_0^2(2n+1)}2
=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}(2n+1)}{2\mechanical m\omega}.
$$

## Exercise 3 · Ground-state uncertainty product

The real ground-state Gaussian has $\Delta x=\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}/(2\mechanical m\omega)}$. Its Fourier transform gives $\Delta\mechanical p=\sqrt{\mechanical m\omega\mechanical{\hbar}/2}$. Therefore $\Delta x\,\Delta\mechanical p=\mechanical{\hbar}/2$.

## Exercise 4 · Squared Hamiltonian as a function of occupation

Since $\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega(\hat N+1/2)$, its square is $\energy{\hat H}^2=\mechanical{\hbar}^2\omega^2(\hat N^2+\hat N+1/4)$. On $\lvert n\rangle$, the second moment is $\mechanical{\hbar}^2\omega^2(n+1/2)^2$. This equals $\langle\energy{\hat H}\rangle^2$ and gives $\Delta\energy H=0$, as expected for an energy eigenstate.

## Exercise 5 · Position mean and spread of a coherent state

Use $\hat a\lvert\alpha\rangle=\alpha\lvert\alpha\rangle$ and $\langle\alpha\rvert\hat a^\dagger=\alpha^*\langle\alpha\rvert$. Then

$$
\langle\hat x\rangle=(x_0/\sqrt2)(\alpha+\alpha^*)
=x_0\sqrt2\,\operatorname{Re}\alpha.
$$

Normal ordering gives $\langle\hat x^2\rangle=(x_0^2/2)(\alpha^2+\alpha^{*2}+2\lvert\alpha\rvert^2+1)$. Subtract $(x_0^2/2)(\alpha+\alpha^*)^2$. The variance is $x_0^2/2$ for every $\alpha$. Hence $\Delta x=x_0/\sqrt2=\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}/(2\mechanical m\omega)}$, the same spread as the ground state.

## Exercise 6 · Displacement operator

The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff identity gives $e^{A+B}=e^Ae^Be^{-[A,B]/2}$ when $[A,B]$ commutes with $A$ and $B$. For $A=\alpha\hat a^\dagger$ and $B=-\alpha^*\hat a$, the commutator is $\lvert\alpha\rvert^2$. Thus

$$
\hat D(\alpha)=e^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert^2/2}
e^{\alpha\hat a^\dagger}e^{-\alpha^*\hat a}.
$$

The last factor leaves $\lvert0\rangle$ fixed. Expand the creation exponential and use $(\hat a^\dagger)^n\lvert0\rangle=\sqrt{n!}\lvert n\rangle$. The result is

$$
\hat D(\alpha)\lvert0\rangle
=e^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\lvert n\rangle
=\lvert\alpha\rangle.
$$

## Exercise 7 · Poisson statistics

The occupation probability is $P_n=e^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert^2}\lvert\alpha\rvert^{2n}/n!$. Its mean is $\langle\hat N\rangle=\lvert\alpha\rvert^2$, and $\langle\hat N^2\rangle=\lvert\alpha\rvert^4+\lvert\alpha\rvert^2$. Thus $(\Delta N)^2=\langle\hat N\rangle$ and, for nonzero mean, $\Delta N/\langle\hat N\rangle=1/\sqrt{\langle\hat N\rangle}$. This is the shot-noise scaling for photodetection with coherent light.

## Exercise 8 · Degeneracy of the isotropic two-dimensional oscillator

The Hamiltonian is $\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega(\hat N_1+\hat N_2+1)$, with levels $\energy E_n=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega(n+1)$ for $n=n_1+n_2$. For each $n_1\in\{0,\ldots,n\}$, there is one $n_2=n-n_1$. Therefore the degeneracy is $n+1$.

## Exercise 9 · Mehler propagator

Insert the oscillator eigenstates into $\langle x\rvert e^{-i\energy{\hat H}t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert x'\rangle$. The Hermite-polynomial sum is

$$
\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{H_n(\xi)H_n(\xi')w^n}{2^n n!}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-w^2}}
\exp\left[\frac{2\xi\xi'w-(\xi^2+\xi'^2)w^2}{1-w^2}\right].
$$

Use $\xi=x/x_0$, $\xi'=x'/x_0$, and $w=e^{-i\omega t}$ as a boundary value from $\lvert w\rvert<1$. With the oscillator normalization,

$$
K(x,t;x',0)=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m\omega}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}\sin(\omega t)}}
\exp\left[\frac{i\mechanical m\omega}{2\mechanical{\hbar}\sin(\omega t)}
\big((x^2+x'^2)\cos(\omega t)-2xx'\big)\right].
$$

The exponent is $i\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ for the classical path between the endpoints. The square root follows the continuous propagator branch away from the caustic times.

## Exercise 10 · Squeezed states

With $\hat S(r)=\exp[(r/2)(\hat a^2-\hat a^{\dagger2})]$, the transformation is $\hat S^\dagger\hat a\hat S=\hat a\cosh r-\hat a^\dagger\sinh r$. Thus $\hat S^\dagger\hat x\hat S=e^{-r}\hat x$ and $\hat S^\dagger\mechanical{\hat p}\hat S=e^r\mechanical{\hat p}$. In $\hat S(r)\lvert0\rangle$,

$$
\Delta x=\frac{x_0}{\sqrt2}e^{-r},
\qquad
\Delta\mechanical p=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}}{x_0\sqrt2}e^r.
$$

For $r>0$, the Gaussian narrows in position and broadens in momentum while $\Delta x\,\Delta\mechanical p=\mechanical{\hbar}/2$.

## Exercise 11 · Thermal ensemble

The partition function is $\thermal Z=e^{-\thermal\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega/2}/(1-e^{-\thermal\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega})$. The zero-point factor cancels from the ratio that gives the mean occupation.

$$
\langle\hat N\rangle_T=\frac{\sum_{n=0}^\infty n e^{-\thermal\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega n}}
{\sum_{n=0}^\infty e^{-\thermal\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega n}}
=\frac1{e^{\thermal\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega}-1}.
$$

This is the Bose–Einstein occupation of a single photon mode. At high temperature, $\langle\hat N\rangle_T\simeq k_B\thermal T/(\mechanical{\hbar}\omega)$. At low temperature, $\langle\hat N\rangle_T\simeq e^{-\mechanical{\hbar}\omega/(k_B\thermal T)}$ and tends to zero.
