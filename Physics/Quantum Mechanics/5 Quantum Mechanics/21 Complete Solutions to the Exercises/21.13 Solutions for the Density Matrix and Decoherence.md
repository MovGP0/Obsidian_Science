> [!TLDR]

Problems are in [[13.09 Density Matrix and Decoherence Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Density matrix of a pure state

Let $\lvert\signal\psi\rangle=c\lvert0\rangle+s\lvert1\rangle$ with $c=\cos(\theta/2)$ and $s=\sin(\theta/2)$. Its density operator is

$$
\hat\rho=\lvert\signal\psi\rangle\langle\signal\psi\rvert
=\begin{pmatrix}c^2&cs\\cs&s^2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Since $c^2+s^2=1$, $\hat\rho^2=\hat\rho$ and $\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho^2=c^4+2c^2s^2+s^4=1$.

## Exercise 2 · Maximally mixed state

The equal mixture of $\lvert0\rangle,\lvert1\rangle$ gives $\hat\rho=\hat I/2$. In the $\pm$ basis, the sum of $\lvert+\rangle\langle+\rvert$ and $\lvert-\rangle\langle-\rvert$ is also $\hat I$. Expanding in the computational basis makes the off-diagonal terms cancel. The equal mixture gives the same density operator, so both preparations have the same statistics for every measurement.

## Exercise 3 · Purity

For $\hat\rho=\operatorname{diag}(3/4,1/4)$, $\hat\rho^2=\operatorname{diag}(9/16,1/16)$. Thus $\mathcal P=\operatorname{Tr}\hat\rho^2=5/8$.

## Exercise 4 · Bloch vector (1/2, 0, 0)

Here $\hat\rho=(\hat I+\sigma_x/2)/2$. In general, $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_i\rangle=(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)\operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho\sigma_i)=(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)a_i$. Thus $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_x\rangle=\mechanical{\hbar}/4$ and the other two components have zero mean.

## Exercise 5 · Eigenvalues of a mixture of orthogonal states

In $\hat\rho=p_1\lvert\signal\psi_1\rangle\langle\signal\psi_1\rvert+p_2\lvert\signal\psi_2\rangle\langle\signal\psi_2\rvert$, orthonormality gives $\hat\rho\lvert\signal\psi_i\rangle=p_i\lvert\signal\psi_i\rangle$. The nonzero eigenvalues come from $p_1,p_2$. Any orthogonal complement has eigenvalue zero.

## Exercise 6 · Partial trace of the Bell state Φ⁺

Expand $\lvert\signal\Phi^+\rangle\langle\signal\Phi^+\rvert$ in the computational basis. In the trace over $B$, $\operatorname{Tr}(\lvert0\rangle\langle1\rvert)=0$, so the two off-diagonal terms vanish. The result is

$$
\hat\rho_A=\tfrac12(\lvert0\rangle\langle0\rvert+\lvert1\rangle\langle1\rvert)=\hat I/2.
$$

Subsystem $A$ is maximally mixed.

## Exercise 7 · Reduced state of a product

For $\lvert\signal\psi\rangle=\lvert+\rangle_A\otimes\lvert0\rangle_B$, the joint density operator factors as $\lvert+\rangle\langle+\rvert\otimes\lvert0\rangle\langle0\rvert$. The trace of the second factor is one. Thus $\hat\rho_A=\lvert+\rangle\langle+\rvert$, $\hat\rho_A^2=\hat\rho_A$, and the purity is one.

## Exercise 8 · Entropy of a three-level mixture

The diagonal weights are $1/2,1/3,1/6$. With natural logarithms,

$$
\thermal S=-\sum_i\lambda_i\ln\lambda_i
=\frac{\ln2}{2}+\frac{\ln3}{3}+\frac{\ln6}{6}
\simeq1.011\ \text{nats}.
$$

Divide by $\ln2$ to get about $1.459$ bits.

## Exercise 9 · Entropy along the z axis of the Bloch ball

For $\hat\rho=(\hat I+a\sigma_z)/2$, the eigenvalues are $(1\pm a)/2$. Hence

$$
\thermal S(a)
=-\frac{1+a}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+a}{2}\right)
-\frac{1-a}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1-a}{2}\right).
$$

At $a=0$, the entropy is maximal and equals $\ln2$. At $a=1$, the state is pure and the entropy is zero, using $0\ln0=0$ by continuity.

## Exercise 10 · Liouville–von Neumann evolution in a z field

The Hamiltonian is $\energy{\hat H}=-\magnetic\gamma\magnetic B\mechanical{\hat S}_z=-\mechanical{\hbar}\omega\sigma_z/2$, with signed $\omega=\magnetic\gamma\magnetic B$. Under $\hat\rho(t)=\hat U(t)\hat\rho(0)\hat U^\dagger(t)$, an initial $x$ component evolves as $a_x(t)=a_x(0)\cos(\omega t)$ and $a_y(t)=-a_x(0)\sin(\omega t)$. The $z$ component is constant because $\sigma_z$ commutes with $\energy{\hat H}$.

## Exercise 11 · Lindblad equation with a dephasing jump operator

For $\hat L=\sqrt\gamma\,\sigma_z$ with $\gamma\ge0$ and $\energy{\hat H}=0$, the evolution is $\dot{\hat\rho}=\gamma(\sigma_z\hat\rho\sigma_z-\hat\rho)$. Multiplication by $\sigma_z$ on both sides preserves the diagonal entries and changes the sign of the off-diagonal entries. Thus

$$
\dot\rho_{00}=\dot\rho_{11}=0,\qquad
\dot\rho_{01}=-2\gamma\rho_{01},\qquad
\rho_{01}(t)=\rho_{01}(0)e^{-2\gamma t}.
$$

For $\gamma>0$, $T_\phi=1/(2\gamma)$. The populations stay constant, which is pure dephasing.
