> [!TLDR]

Problems are in [[07.07 Barriers and Quantum Tunneling Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Rectangular barrier

For $\energy V_0-\energy E=\quantity{4}{eV}$ and $a=\quantity{0.5}{nm}$,

$$
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{2\mechanical m_e c^2(\energy V_0-\energy E)}}{\mechanical{\hbar}c}
\simeq\frac{\sqrt{2(5.11\times10^5)(4)}}{197}\unit{nm^{-1}}
\simeq\quantity{10.25}{nm^{-1}}.
$$

Then $\kappa a\simeq5.12$ and $e^{-2\kappa a}\simeq3.5\times10^{-5}$. The prefactor $16\energy E(\energy V_0-\energy E)/\energy V_0^2=16\cdot1\cdot4/25=2.56$ gives $T\simeq9.0\times10^{-5}$.

## Exercise 2 · Electron through a 1 nm barrier

For $\energy E=\quantity{1}{eV}$, $\energy V_0=\quantity{2}{eV}$, and $a=\quantity{1}{nm}$, $\kappa\simeq\quantity{5.13}{nm^{-1}}$ and $e^{-2\kappa a}\simeq3.5\times10^{-5}$. The prefactor is $4$, so $T\simeq1.4\times10^{-4}$.

## Exercise 3 · Resonances above the barrier

For $\energy E>\energy V_0$, the internal wave number is $k'=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E-\energy V_0)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$, and

$$
T=\left[1+\frac{\energy V_0^2}{4\energy E(\energy E-\energy V_0)}\sin^2(k'a)\right]^{-1}.
$$

Transmission is one at $k'a=n\pi$, when the barrier width equals an integer multiple of the internal half-wavelength. The reflected components from the interfaces then interfere destructively, as in a Fabry–Pérot interferometer.

## Exercise 4 · STM sensitivity

For a work function $\energy\phi=\quantity{4}{eV}$, the decay constant is $\kappa=\sqrt{2\mechanical m_e\energy\phi}/\mechanical{\hbar}\simeq\quantity{10.25}{nm^{-1}}$. Increasing the gap by $\Delta a=\quantity{0.1}{nm}$ gives

$$
\frac{T(a+\Delta a)}{T(a)}=e^{-2\kappa\Delta a}=e^{-2.05}\simeq0.13.
$$

A distance change of one ångström changes the current by about a factor of eight in this model. This strong distance dependence enables atomic spatial resolution.

## Exercise 5 · Exact transmission with V₀ = 2E

With $\energy V_0-\energy E=\energy E$, $\kappa=k$. At $\kappa a=1$ the exact result becomes

$$
T=\frac{1}{1+\dfrac{\energy V_0^2}{4\energy E(\energy V_0-\energy E)}\sinh^2(\kappa a)}
=\frac1{1+\sinh^2 1}=\frac1{\cosh^2 1}\simeq0.420.
$$

Thus about $42\%$ of the incident flux is transmitted below the barrier.

## Exercise 6 · WKB for a triangular barrier

For $\energy V(x)=\energy V_0(1-x/a)$ over $0\le x\le a$ and $\energy E=0$, use the forbidden interval between the entry boundary and the turning point.

$$
\mechanical S
=\int_0^a\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy V_0(1-x/a)}\,\mathrm dx
=\frac{2a}{3}\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy V_0}.
$$

The tunneling factor is $T\simeq e^{-2\mechanical S/\mechanical{\hbar}}=\exp[-4a\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy V_0}/(3\mechanical{\hbar})]$. This is the exponent used in the Fowler–Nordheim treatment of field emission.

## Exercise 7 · Alpha decay of uranium-238

The charge product in units of $\electric e^2$ is $Z'=2\cdot90=180$. For $\energy E_\alpha=\quantity{4.27}{MeV}$, the outer turning point is $r_2=Z'\electric e^2/(4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0\energy E_\alpha)\simeq\quantity{60.7}{fm}$. The nuclear radius gives $R/r_2\simeq0.153$. Integrating the Coulomb barrier from $R$ to $r_2$ gives

$$
G=\frac{Z'\electric e^2}{4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0\mechanical{\hbar}}
\sqrt{\frac{2\mechanical m_\alpha}{\energy E_\alpha}}
\left[\arccos\sqrt{R/r_2}-\sqrt{R/r_2-(R/r_2)^2}\right].
$$

Numerically $G\simeq54.9\cdot0.808\simeq44.4$, so the transmission factor is $T\simeq e^{-2G}\simeq3\times10^{-39}$. The alpha speed is $v_\alpha=c\sqrt{2\energy E_\alpha/(\mechanical m_\alpha c^2)}\simeq\quantity{1.44\times10^7}{m\,s^{-1}}$. Use an attempt frequency $\nu=v_\alpha/(2R)\simeq\quantity{7.7\times10^{20}}{Hz}$. The decay rate is of order $\nu T$, giving a mean lifetime of order $\quantity{5\times10^{17}}{s}$, or $10^{10}$ years. The source compares this with the measured half-life of about $4.5\times10^9$ years. The estimate is within an order of magnitude. For exponential decay, the half-life is $\ln 2$ times the mean lifetime.

## Exercise 8 · Gamow approximation

For a Coulomb barrier, integrate

$$
\mechanical S=\int_R^{r_2}
\sqrt{2\mechanical m\left(\frac{Z'\electric e^2}{4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0 r}-\energy E\right)}\,\mathrm dr.
$$

With $u=r/r_2$ and $r_2=Z'\electric e^2/(4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0\energy E)$,

$$
\frac{\mechanical S}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
=\frac{Z'\electric e^2}{4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0\mechanical{\hbar}}
\sqrt{\frac{2\mechanical m}{\energy E}}\int_{R/r_2}^1\sqrt{1/u-1}\,\mathrm du.
$$

As $R/r_2\to0$, the integral tends to $\pi/2$. Then $\log T=-2\mechanical S/\mechanical{\hbar}$ is proportional to $-1/\sqrt{\energy E}$, which gives the energy dependence in the Geiger–Nuttall law.

## Exercise 9 · Gamow peak

Keep the two leading exponential factors in the fusion integrand, $e^{-\energy E/(k_B\thermal T)}e^{-b/\sqrt{\energy E}}$, with $b=\pi Z_1Z_2\electric e^2\sqrt{2\mechanical\mu}/(4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0\mechanical{\hbar})$ and reduced mass $\mechanical\mu$. The maximum satisfies

$$
-\frac1{k_B\thermal T}+\frac{b}{2\energy E^{3/2}}=0,\qquad
\energy E_G=\left(\frac{bk_B\thermal T}{2}\right)^{2/3}.
$$

The Gaussian expansion about $\energy E_G$ gives a full $1/e$ width $\energy\Delta_G=4\sqrt{\energy E_Gk_B\thermal T/3}\propto\thermal T^{5/6}$. In the regime $\energy E_G\gg k_B\thermal T$, fusion comes from the high-energy tail of the thermal distribution.

## Exercise 10 · Ramsauer–Townsend effect

Model the atom by a one-dimensional well of depth $\energy V_0$ and width $a$. In the transmission analogy, the internal wave number is $k'=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E+\energy V_0)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ and reflection vanishes at $k'a=n\pi$. The positive resonance energies are

$$
\energy E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m a^2}-\energy V_0.
$$

The source uses this transparency to illustrate the cross-section minimum observed for slow electrons in noble gases such as argon, krypton, and xenon, on the scale of $\quantity{0.7}{eV}$ in its example. For an actual spherical potential, the $s$-wave phase shift controls the low-energy minimum.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $T$ | $1$ | Transmission factor | The transmitted flux fraction or the approximate tunneling factor. |
| $a,R,r_2$ | $\unit{m}$ | Barrier width and radii | The spatial scales used in barrier and nuclear examples. |
| $k,k',\kappa$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wave numbers and decay constant | Control propagation or evanescence in each region. |
| $\mechanical S,G$ | $\unit{J\,s},1$ | Barrier action and scaled action | The tunneling exponent is twice the action in units of $\mechanical{\hbar}$. |
| $\nu,\tau$ | $\unit{Hz},\unit{s}$ | Attempt frequency and lifetime | The rates of barrier encounters and the mean decay time. |
| $\energy E_G,\energy\Delta_G$ | $\unit{J}$ | Gamow peak energy and width | The location and full $1/e$ width of the fusion integrand. |
