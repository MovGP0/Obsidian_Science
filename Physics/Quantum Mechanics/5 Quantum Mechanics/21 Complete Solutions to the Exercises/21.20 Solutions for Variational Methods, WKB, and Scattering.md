## Exercise 1 · Gaussian trial state for the oscillator

Use $\signal\phi(x)=(2\alpha/\pi)^{1/4}e^{-\alpha x^2}$, which is normalized. The kinetic expectation is $\mechanical{\hbar}^2\alpha/(2\mechanical m)$ and $\langle x^2\rangle=1/(4\alpha)$ gives the potential expectation $\mechanical m\omega^2/(8\alpha)$. Therefore

$$
\energy E(\alpha)=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2\alpha}{2\mechanical m}
+\frac{\mechanical m\omega^2}{8\alpha}.
$$

Set its derivative to zero to obtain $\alpha^*=\mechanical m\omega/(2\mechanical{\hbar})$. At this value the trial function is the exact ground state and $\energy E_{\min}=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega/2$.

## Exercise 2 · Trial state for an infinite well

On $[0,L]$ use $\signal\phi(x)=Nx(L-x)$. The norm integral $\int_0^L x^2(L-x)^2\,\mathrm dx=L^5/30$ gives $N^2=30/L^5$. With $\signal\phi'=N(L-2x)$ and $\int_0^L(L-2x)^2\,\mathrm dx=L^3/3$,

$$
\langle\energy{\hat H}\rangle
=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m}\int_0^L\lvert\signal\phi'\rvert^2\,\mathrm dx
=\frac{5\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{\mechanical m L^2}
=\frac{10}{\pi^2}\energy E_1.
$$

This is about $1.3\%$ above the exact energy. The trial function satisfies both hard-wall boundary conditions.

## Exercise 3 · Bohr–Sommerfeld rule for the infinite well

The classical round trip gives $2L\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E}$. For two hard walls, set this action equal to $nh$, with $n=1,2,\ldots$. Then

$$
2L\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E_n}=nh
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\energy E_n=\frac{n^2h^2}{8\mechanical m L^2}
=\frac{n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m L^2}.
$$

This reproduces the exact well spectrum. The half-integer correction of two smooth turning points is replaced by the hard-wall boundary conditions.

## Exercise 4 · WKB for a quartic oscillator

For $\energy V=\lambda x^4$ with $\lambda>0$, the turning points are $\pm x_t$ with $x_t=(\energy E/\lambda)^{1/4}$. Set $u=x/x_t$ in the closed-orbit action.

$$
\oint\mechanical p\,\mathrm dx
=2x_t\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E}\int_{-1}^1\sqrt{1-u^4}\,\mathrm du
=(n+1/2)h.
$$

The integral is $C=2\int_0^1\sqrt{1-u^4}\,\mathrm du\simeq1.748$. Thus

$$
\energy E_n\simeq
\left(\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m}\right)^{2/3}
\lambda^{1/3}\left[\frac{\pi(n+1/2)}{C}\right]^{4/3}.
$$

## Exercise 5 · Hard sphere at low energy

The radial wave function vanishes at radius $a$. With spherical Bessel and Neumann functions, the matching condition is $\tan\delta_\ell=j_\ell(ka)/n_\ell(ka)$. For $\ell=0$, $j_0(z)=\sin z/z$ and $n_0(z)=-\cos z/z$, so $\tan\delta_0=-\tan(ka)$. Choose $\delta_0=-ka$ near $k=0$. The scattering length $a_s=-\lim_{k\to0}\delta_0/k$ is $a$. The low-energy cross section tends to $4\pi a^2$, four times the geometric area.

## Exercise 6 · First Born approximation for a Gaussian potential

For $\energy V(\mathbf r)=\energy V_0e^{-r^2/b^2}$, the three-dimensional Fourier transform of the Gaussian is $(\pi b^2)^{3/2}e^{-q^2b^2/4}$. Thus

$$
f^{\mathrm B}(\theta)=-\frac{\mechanical m\energy V_0}{2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}^2}
(\pi b^2)^{3/2}e^{-q^2b^2/4},\qquad q=2k\sin(\theta/2).
$$

The amplitude is largest at small scattering angles and decreases as $qb$ increases. The decay occurs when the momentum transfer resolves the spatial scale $b$.

## Exercise 7 · Variational helium calculation

Use $\signal\phi_{1s}(r;Z')=(Z'^3/(\pi a_0^3))^{1/2}e^{-Z'r/a_0}$ for both electrons and the spin singlet. Each electron contributes kinetic energy $Z'^2\unit{Ry}$ and nuclear attraction $-4Z'\unit{Ry}$ for true charge number $Z=2$. The repulsion expectation is $(5Z'/8)\cdot2\unit{Ry}$. Thus

$$
\energy E(Z')=2\unit{Ry}\left[Z'^2-4Z'+\frac58 Z'\right].
$$

The derivative gives $2Z'-4+5/8=0$, so $Z'=27/16=1.6875$. The minimum $-2(27/16)^2\unit{Ry}\simeq\quantity{-77.5}{eV}$ differs by about $1.9\%$ from the experimental ground energy near $\quantity{-79}{eV}$.

## Exercise 8 · Spherical well for an s state

Use $\signal u(r)=r\signal\psi(r)$ and $\signal u''+(2\mechanical m/\mechanical{\hbar}^2)(\energy E-\energy V)\signal u=0$. Inside the well, $\signal u_{\mathrm{in}}=A\sin(\kappa r)$ with $\kappa=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E+\energy V_0)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. Outside, $\signal u_{\mathrm{out}}=Be^{-\gamma r}$ with $\gamma=\sqrt{-2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. Match $\signal u$ and its derivative at $r=a$.

$$
\kappa\cot(\kappa a)=-\gamma.
$$

The first threshold occurs at $\gamma=0$ and $\kappa a=\pi/2$. A normalizable $s$ state requires $\energy V_0>\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(8\mechanical m a^2)$. Equality gives the zero-energy threshold. The source includes equality in its bound-state criterion.

## Exercise 9 · Total Born cross section for Yukawa scattering

For $\energy V(r)=V_0e^{-\mu r}/r$, its transform is $4\pi V_0/(\mu^2+q^2)$ in this convention. Thus $f^{\mathrm B}=-2\mechanical m V_0/[\mechanical{\hbar}^2(\mu^2+q^2)]$, and

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\sigma}{\mathrm d\Omega}
=\frac{4\mechanical m^2 V_0^2}{\mechanical{\hbar}^4(\mu^2+q^2)^2}.
$$

Set $q^2=2k^2(1-\cos\theta)$ and integrate with $\mathrm d\Omega=2\pi\sin\theta\,\mathrm d\theta$.

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{16\pi\mechanical m^2 V_0^2}
{\mechanical{\hbar}^4\mu^2(\mu^2+4k^2)}.
$$

As $k\to0$, this approaches $16\pi\mechanical m^2V_0^2/(\mechanical{\hbar}^4\mu^4)$ for $\mu>0$.

## Exercise 10 · WKB for the sextic potential

For $\energy V(x)=\energy V_0(x/a)^6$, the turning points are $\pm x_t$ with $x_t=a(\energy E/\energy V_0)^{1/6}$. Set $u=x/x_t$. Then

$$
2x_t\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E}\,C_6=(n+1/2)h,\qquad
C_6=2\int_0^1\sqrt{1-u^6}\,\mathrm du\simeq1.82149.
$$

Define $\energy E_*=(\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\mechanical m a^2))^{3/4}\energy V_0^{1/4}$. The WKB energies are $\energy E_n/\energy E_*=[\pi(n+1/2)/C_6]^{3/2}$.

| $n$ | WKB $\energy E_n/\energy E_*$ | Numerical $\energy E_n/\energy E_*$ | WKB error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0$ | $0.80083$ | $1.14480$ | $-30.0\%$ |
| $1$ | $4.16123$ | $4.33859$ | $-4.1\%$ |

The numerical values come from a centered finite-difference discretization of $-\mathrm d^2/\mathrm dy^2+y^6$ on $[-5,5]$ with zero boundary values and step $0.0025$. The coordinate is $y=(x/a)[2\mechanical m a^2\energy V_0/\mechanical{\hbar}^2]^{1/8}$. Halving the step from $0.005$ changes the first two eigenvalues by less than $3\times10^{-5}$.

> [!note] Error estimate in the source
> The source gives a typical ground-state WKB error of $5$–$10\%$. The numerical comparison requested in the exercise gives about $30\%$ for this sextic ground state.

## Exercise 11 · Regge trajectories

Analytically continue the angular momentum label $\ell$ in partial-wave amplitudes $f_\ell(\energy E)$. Their poles trace curves $\ell=\alpha(\energy E)$ in the complex angular momentum plane. In the Regge picture, resonances of different spins and energies can lie on the same trajectory. In high-energy scattering at fixed momentum-transfer invariant $t$, a Regge-pole contribution has the schematic behavior $A(s,t)\sim\beta(t)s^{\alpha(t)}$ as $s\to\infty$.

The source connects these trajectories to meson families such as the rho trajectory and to Veneziano's amplitude. It identifies the Pomeron as the leading trajectory in strong elastic scattering.
