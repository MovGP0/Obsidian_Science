> [!TLDR]
> Use Pauli matrices, spin projections, and unitary rotations to solve spin-$1/2$ measurement and dynamics problems.

Problems are in [[10.07 Spin Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Pauli matrix algebra

Direct multiplication gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_x\sigma_y
&=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\0&-i\end{pmatrix}
=i\sigma_z,\\
\sigma_y\sigma_z
&=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}
=i\sigma_x.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Exercise 2 · Measurement of z spin in the positive y state

Since $\lvert+y\rangle=(\lvert+z\rangle+i\lvert-z\rangle)/\sqrt2$, the probabilities of $\pm\mechanical{\hbar}/2$ along $z$ are both $1/2$. The eigenbases for these orthogonal directions are mutually unbiased.

## Exercise 3 · Square of a spin component

Use $\mechanical{\hat S}_i=(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)\sigma_i$. Each Pauli matrix squares to $\hat I$, so $\mechanical{\hat S}_i^2=(\mechanical{\hbar}^2/4)\hat I$. Summing over the three directions gives $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^2=(3\mechanical{\hbar}^2/4)\hat I=s(s+1)\mechanical{\hbar}^2\hat I$ with $s=1/2$.

## Exercise 4 · Positive z spin on the Bloch sphere

In $\lvert\signal\psi\rangle=\cos(\theta/2)\lvert+z\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2)\lvert-z\rangle$, the state $\lvert+z\rangle$ has $\theta=0$. The azimuth is arbitrary at this pole. The Bloch vector is $\langle\boldsymbol\sigma\rangle=(0,0,1)$ on the unit sphere.

## Exercise 5 · Bloch vector of a given spinor

The spinor $(3\lvert+\rangle+4i\lvert-\rangle)/5$ has coefficients $a=3/5$, $b=4i/5$ in the $z$ basis. Then $\langle\sigma_x\rangle=2\operatorname{Re}(a^*b)=0$, $\langle\sigma_y\rangle=2\operatorname{Im}(a^*b)=24/25$, and $\langle\sigma_z\rangle=\lvert a\rvert^2-\lvert b\rvert^2=-7/25$. The length is $\sqrt{(24/25)^2+(7/25)^2}=1$, as for a pure state.

## Exercise 6 · Exponential of the z Pauli matrix

Since $\sigma_z\lvert+\rangle=\lvert+\rangle$, every power of $\sigma_z$ acts as one on this state. The exponential series gives $e^{-i\sigma_z\alpha/2}\lvert+\rangle=e^{-i\alpha/2}\lvert+\rangle$. The rotation gives only a phase on the $z$ eigenstate.

## Exercise 7 · Larmor precession

Initially $\lvert\signal\psi(0)\rangle=(\lvert+z\rangle+\lvert-z\rangle)/\sqrt2$. With $\energy{\hat H}=-\magnetic\gamma\magnetic B\mechanical{\hat S}_z=-(\mechanical{\hbar}\omega_L/2)\sigma_z$ and signed $\omega_L=\magnetic\gamma\magnetic B$,

$$
\lvert\signal\psi(t)\rangle
=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(e^{i\omega_Lt/2}\lvert+z\rangle+e^{-i\omega_Lt/2}\lvert-z\rangle\right).
$$

The expectations are $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_x\rangle=(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)\cos(\omega_Lt)$, $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_y\rangle=-(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)\sin(\omega_Lt)$, and $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_z\rangle=0$. The Bloch vector precesses in the $xy$ plane with angular speed $\lvert\omega_L\rvert$.

## Exercise 8 · Rotation by π/2 about y

The rotation is $\hat R_y(\pi/2)=e^{-i\sigma_y\pi/4}=(\hat I-i\sigma_y)/\sqrt2$. Since $\sigma_y\lvert+z\rangle=i\lvert-z\rangle$,

$$
\hat R_y(\pi/2)\lvert+z\rangle
=(\lvert+z\rangle+\lvert-z\rangle)/\sqrt2
=\lvert+x\rangle.
$$

The rotation takes the positive $z$ direction to positive $x$.

## Exercise 9 · Rotation by π about x

Here $\hat R_x(\pi)=-i\sigma_x$, so $\hat R_x(\pi)\lvert+z\rangle=-i\lvert-z\rangle$. The phase has no effect on the probability. Measuring $-\mechanical{\hbar}/2$ along $z$ has probability one.

## Exercise 10 · Squared total spin on triplet and singlet

For two spins $1/2$,

$$
\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^2
=\frac32\mechanical{\hbar}^2\hat I
+2\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^{(1)}\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^{(2)}.
$$

In the triplet, $s=1$ gives $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^2=2\mechanical{\hbar}^2$ on the subspace, so the dot product has eigenvalue $\mechanical{\hbar}^2/4$. For the singlet, $s=0$ gives zero total spin squared and the dot product has eigenvalue $-3\mechanical{\hbar}^2/4$.

## Exercise 11 · Berry phase for spin in a conical field

Let $\magnetic{\mathbf B}(t)=\magnetic B(\sin\theta\cos\omega t,\sin\theta\sin\omega t,\cos\theta)$. The instantaneous positive spinor is $\lvert+,\hat{\mathbf n}(t)\rangle=\cos(\theta/2)\lvert+\rangle+e^{i\omega t}\sin(\theta/2)\lvert-\rangle$. Its Berry connection has $A_\phi=i\langle+,\hat{\mathbf n}\rvert\partial_\phi\lvert+,\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle=-\sin^2(\theta/2)$. One positive circuit gives

$$
\gamma_B=\oint A_\phi\,\mathrm d\phi
=-2\pi\sin^2(\theta/2)
=-\pi(1-\cos\theta)=-\frac\Omega2,
$$

where $\Omega=2\pi(1-\cos\theta)$ is the enclosed solid angle. In the adiabatic limit the phase depends on the path and is independent of the traversal speed.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat S}_i$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Spin component | The observable $\mechanical{\hbar}\sigma_i/2$. |
| $\magnetic B,\magnetic\gamma$ | $\unit{T},\unit{rad\,s^{-1}\,T^{-1}}$ | Magnetic field and gyromagnetic ratio | Their product gives the signed Larmor frequency. |
| $\omega_L,\omega$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The Larmor precession and external field rotation rates. |
| $\gamma_B,\Omega$ | $\unit{rad},\unit{sr}$ | Berry phase and solid angle | The spin geometric phase and the solid angle of the conical path. |
