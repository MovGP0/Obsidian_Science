> [!TLDR]
> Apply normalization, Hermitian conjugation, and the measurement postulates to simple quantum states.

Problems are in [[02.09 Postulates of Quantum Mechanics Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · State normalization

The squared norm of the given vector is $\lVert\signal\psi\rVert^2=4+1+1=6$. Divide by $\sqrt6$.

$$
\lvert\signal\psi\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt6}(2\lvert1\rangle+i\lvert2\rangle-\lvert3\rangle).
$$

## Exercise 2 · Reality of a Hermitian expectation value

Conjugating the expectation value gives

$$
\langle\signal\psi\rvert\hat A\lvert\signal\psi\rangle^*
=\langle\hat A\signal\psi\mid\signal\psi\rangle
=\langle\signal\psi\rvert\hat A^\dagger\lvert\signal\psi\rangle
=\langle\signal\psi\rvert\hat A\lvert\signal\psi\rangle.
$$

A complex number equal to its conjugate is real.

## Exercise 3 · Expectation and spread of A

Each of the outcomes $1,2,3$ has probability $1/3$. Therefore

$$
\langle\hat A\rangle=\frac{1+2+3}{3}=2,\qquad
\langle\hat A^2\rangle=\frac{1+4+9}{3}=\frac{14}{3}.
$$

The variance is $(\Delta A)^2=14/3-4=2/3$ and the standard deviation is $\Delta A=\sqrt{2/3}$.

## Exercise 4 · Minimum and maximum spread

Each eigenstate has $\Delta A=0$. An equal-weight superposition of eigenstates with eigenvalues $a_i$ and $a_j$ has variance $(a_i-a_j)^2/4$, independent of the relative phase. The largest spread comes from the extreme eigenvalues $1$ and $3$ with equal weights and gives $\Delta A=1$. To see the bound for any state, use $\langle(\hat A-2\hat I)^2\rangle\le1$ and subtract $(\langle\hat A\rangle-2)^2$.

## Exercise 5 · Unitarity of time evolution

Since $\energy{\hat H}=\energy{\hat H}^\dagger$, $\hat U^\dagger=e^{+i\energy{\hat H}t/\mechanical{\hbar}}$. The two exponentials commute and cancel. Thus $\hat U^\dagger\hat U=\hat U\hat U^\dagger=\hat I$.

## Exercise 6 · Spin probabilities along z

Use $\lvert+z\rangle=(\lvert+x\rangle+\lvert-x\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and $\lvert+y\rangle=(\lvert+z\rangle+i\lvert-z\rangle)/\sqrt2$. The corresponding Born probabilities are

$$
P_{+x\to+z}=\frac12,\qquad
P_{+y\to+z}=\frac12,\qquad
P_{-z\to+z}=0.
$$

## Exercise 7 · Projection onto a degenerate eigenspace

The projector is $\hat P_{a_1}=\lvert u_1\rangle\langle u_1\rvert+\lvert u_2\rangle\langle u_2\rvert$. The projected vector is $(\lvert u_1\rangle+2\lvert u_2\rangle)/3$. Its squared norm gives $P(a_1)=5/9$. The normalized state after that outcome is $(\lvert u_1\rangle+2\lvert u_2\rangle)/\sqrt5$.

## Exercise 8 · Change from the computational basis to the ± basis

The overlaps are $\langle\pm\mid\signal\psi\rangle=(\alpha\pm\beta)/\sqrt2$. Thus $P(+)=\lvert\alpha+\beta\rvert^2/2$ and $P(-)=\lvert\alpha-\beta\rvert^2/2$. The sum is $\lvert\alpha\rvert^2+\lvert\beta\rvert^2=1$.

## Exercise 9 · Rabi oscillation in a two-level system

The Hamiltonian $\energy{\hat H}=\energy\epsilon\sigma_x$ has eigenvalues $\pm\energy\epsilon$ with $\lvert\pm x\rangle=(\lvert0\rangle\pm\lvert1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Resolve $\lvert0\rangle$ in this basis and apply the energy phases.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert\signal\psi(t)\rangle
&=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(e^{-i\energy\epsilon t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert+x\rangle
+e^{i\energy\epsilon t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert-x\rangle\right)\\
&=\cos(\energy\epsilon t/\mechanical{\hbar})\lvert0\rangle
-i\sin(\energy\epsilon t/\mechanical{\hbar})\lvert1\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $P_1(t)=\sin^2(\energy\epsilon t/\mechanical{\hbar})$ and the Rabi angular frequency is $\Omega=2\lvert\energy\epsilon\rvert/\mechanical{\hbar}$.

## Exercise 10 · Spectral form of time evolution

For $\lvert\signal\psi(0)\rangle=\sum_n c_n\lvert n\rangle$, use $\hat U(t)=\sum_n e^{-i\energy E_n t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert n\rangle\langle n\rvert$.

$$
\lvert\signal\psi(t)\rangle=\sum_n c_n e^{-i\energy E_n t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert n\rangle,\qquad
\lvert\langle n\mid\signal\psi(t)\rangle\rvert^2=\lvert c_n\rvert^2.
$$

The populations of the energy levels are constant.

## Exercise 11 · Oscillation with two Hamiltonian components

Let $\energy{\hat H}=\energy\epsilon\sigma_z+\energy\Delta\sigma_x$ and $\energy E=\sqrt{\energy\epsilon^2+\energy\Delta^2}>0$. Define the angle $\eta$ by $\cos(2\eta)=\energy\epsilon/\energy E$ and $\sin(2\eta)=\energy\Delta/\energy E$. The eigenstates at energies $\pm\energy E$ are $\lvert+_H\rangle=\cos\eta\lvert0\rangle+\sin\eta\lvert1\rangle$ and $\lvert-_H\rangle=-\sin\eta\lvert0\rangle+\cos\eta\lvert1\rangle$. Since $\lvert0\rangle=\cos\eta\lvert+_H\rangle-\sin\eta\lvert-_H\rangle$,

$$
\lvert\signal\psi(t)\rangle=\cos\eta\,e^{-i\energy E t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert+_H\rangle
-\sin\eta\,e^{i\energy E t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert-_H\rangle.
$$

Project onto $\lvert1\rangle$ and square the magnitude.

$$
P_1(t)
=\sin^2(2\eta)\sin^2(\energy E t/\mechanical{\hbar})
=\frac{\energy\Delta^2}{\energy\epsilon^2+\energy\Delta^2}
\sin^2\left(\frac{\sqrt{\energy\epsilon^2+\energy\Delta^2}}{\mechanical{\hbar}}t\right).
$$

For $\lvert\energy\Delta\rvert\ll\lvert\energy\epsilon\rvert$, the peak probability is about $(\energy\Delta/\energy\epsilon)^2$ and the angular frequency of the population is about $2\lvert\energy\epsilon\rvert/\mechanical{\hbar}$. For $\lvert\energy\Delta\rvert\gg\lvert\energy\epsilon\rvert$, the peak probability approaches one and the population frequency approaches $2\lvert\energy\Delta\rvert/\mechanical{\hbar}$.

> [!note] Frequency convention
> In its limiting statements, the source gives the coefficient in the sine argument. The angular frequency of the squared sine is twice that coefficient, consistent with Exercise 9.
