> [!TLDR]
> Calculate stationary energy corrections and transition probabilities for weak perturbations and coherent drives.

Problems are in [[16.08 Perturbation Theory Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Harmonic correction proportional to x²

The first-order shift for $\energy{\hat V}=\lambda\hat x^2$ is $\lambda\langle n\rvert\hat x^2\lvert n\rangle$. With $\hat x=x_0(\hat a+\hat a^\dagger)/\sqrt2$ and $x_0=\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}/(\mechanical m\omega)}$, only the two terms that preserve $n$ survive. They give $\langle\hat x^2\rangle_n=x_0^2(n+1/2)$. Thus

$$
\Delta\energy E_n^{(1)}=\frac{\lambda\mechanical{\hbar}}{\mechanical m\omega}\left(n+\frac12\right).
$$

The perturbed potential remains harmonic when its curvature is positive, with $\omega'=\omega\sqrt{1+2\lambda/(\mechanical m\omega^2)}$. Expansion in $\lambda$ gives the same shift.

## Exercise 2 · Infinite well with a central delta potential

For $\signal\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$,

$$
\Delta\energy E_n^{(1)}
=\lambda\int_0^L\lvert\signal\psi_n(x)\rvert^2\delta(x-L/2)\,\mathrm dx
=\frac{2\lambda}{L}\sin^2(n\pi/2).
$$

This is $2\lambda/L$ for odd $n$. For even $n$ there is a node at the delta, so the first-order correction vanishes.

## Exercise 3 · Sign of the second-order ground-state correction

The expression $\energy E_0^{(2)}=\sum_{n\ne0}\lvert\energy V_{n0}\rvert^2/(\energy E_0-\energy E_n)$ has negative denominators for a nondegenerate ground state. Each term is nonpositive. The correction is strictly negative if at least one coupling $\energy V_{n0}$ is nonzero, and zero if they all vanish.

## Exercise 4 · Lifetime of the hydrogen 2p state

The dipole rate is $A=\omega_{fi}^3\lvert\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi}\rvert^2/(3\pi\electric\varepsilon_0\mechanical{\hbar}c^3)$. The energy gap $\quantity{10.2}{eV}$ gives $\omega_{fi}\simeq\quantity{1.55\times10^{16}}{s^{-1}}$. Estimate $\lvert\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi}\rvert\simeq\electric e a_0\simeq\quantity{8.5\times10^{-30}}{C\,m}$. The rate is then of order $\quantity{10^9}{s^{-1}}$. The exact dipole matrix element gives a squared magnitude about $0.55$ times this estimate, a rate of about $\quantity{6.27\times10^8}{s^{-1}}$, and a lifetime of about $\quantity{1.6}{ns}$.

## Exercise 5 · Stark effect on n = 2

Use $\lvert2s\rangle=\signal R_{20}Y_{00}$, $\lvert2p_0\rangle=\signal R_{21}Y_{10}$, and $z=r\cos\theta$. With $Y_{00}=1/\sqrt{4\pi}$ and $Y_{10}=\sqrt{3/(4\pi)}\cos\theta$, the angular factor is $1/\sqrt3$. With $\rho=r/a_0$, the radial factor is $\int_0^\infty\signal R_{20}\signal R_{21}r^3\,\mathrm dr=-3\sqrt3\,a_0$. The product is $-3a_0$. Hence $\energy{\hat V}=\electric e\electric{\mathcal E}z$ gives the block $-3\electric e a_0\electric{\mathcal E}\sigma_x$ on $2s,2p_0$. Its two eigenvalues give the linear shifts $\pm3\electric e a_0\electric{\mathcal E}$.

## Exercise 6 · Normal Zeeman effect on 2p

The perturbation $(\magnetic\mu_B\magnetic B/\mechanical{\hbar})\mechanical{\hat L}_z$ is diagonal in $\lvert n\ell m\rangle$. For $n=2,\ell=1$, the shifts are $\Delta\energy E_m=m\magnetic\mu_B\magnetic B$ for $m=-1,0,1$. The middle level stays fixed, with the other two separated from it by $\lvert\magnetic\mu_B\magnetic B\rvert$. The $\sigma^\pm$ and $\pi$ lines correspond to $\Delta m=\pm1$ and $\Delta m=0$.

## Exercise 7 · Gaussian pulse

For the full pulse $\energy{\hat V}(t)=\energy{\hat V}_0e^{-t^2/\tau^2}$, the first-order amplitude is

$$
c_1(\infty)=\frac{\energy V_{0,10}}{i\mechanical{\hbar}}
\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{i\omega_{10}t-t^2/\tau^2}\,\mathrm dt
=\frac{\energy V_{0,10}}{i\mechanical{\hbar}}
\sqrt\pi\,\tau\,e^{-\omega_{10}^2\tau^2/4}.
$$

Thus $P_{0\to1}(\infty)=(\pi\tau^2/\mechanical{\hbar}^2)\lvert\energy V_{0,10}\rvert^2e^{-\omega_{10}^2\tau^2/2}$. For nonzero $\omega_{10}$ this expression is maximal at $\tau=\sqrt2/\lvert\omega_{10}\rvert$. A short pulse has small area at fixed amplitude. A long pulse has narrow spectral width, which suppresses the off-center component.

## Exercise 8 · Constant perturbation switched on at zero

For $\omega_{fi}\ne0$,

$$
c_f^{(1)}(t)=\frac{\energy V_{0,fi}}{i\mechanical{\hbar}}
\frac{e^{i\omega_{fi}t}-1}{i\omega_{fi}},
\qquad
P_{i\to f}(t)\simeq\frac{4\lvert\energy V_{0,fi}\rvert^2}{\mechanical{\hbar}^2\omega_{fi}^2}
\sin^2(\omega_{fi}t/2).
$$

The probability has period $2\pi/\lvert\omega_{fi}\rvert$. Its long-time average for a fixed nonzero gap is $2\lvert\energy V_{0,fi}\rvert^2/(\mechanical{\hbar}\omega_{fi})^2$. In a continuum, the distributional limit $\sin^2(\omega t/2)/(\omega/2)^2\sim2\pi t\,\delta(\omega)$ yields the golden-rule rate after integration over final states.

## Exercise 9 · Resonant Rabi oscillations

Write the state in the interaction picture with amplitudes $c_g,c_e$ for the ground and excited levels. The drive is $\energy{\hat V}(t)=(\mechanical{\hbar}\Omega_R/2)(e^{-i\omega t}+e^{i\omega t})(\lvert e\rangle\langle g\rvert+\lvert g\rangle\langle e\rvert)$. In the rotating-wave approximation, retain the slowly varying terms. On resonance $\omega=\omega_{eg}$ this gives

$$
i\dot c_e=(\Omega_R/2)c_g,\qquad i\dot c_g=(\Omega_R/2)c_e.
$$

For $c_g(0)=1$ and $c_e(0)=0$, $c_e(t)=-i\sin(\Omega_Rt/2)$. The population $P_e(t)=\sin^2(\Omega_Rt/2)$ oscillates between zero and one with angular frequency $\Omega_R$.

## Exercise 10 · Sudden displacement of the oscillator center

The new ground state is $\lvert0_a\rangle=\hat D(\alpha)\lvert0\rangle$ with $\alpha=a/(x_0\sqrt2)$ and $x_0=\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}/(\mechanical m\omega)}$. Its overlap with the old ground state is $\langle0_a\mid0\rangle=e^{-\lvert\alpha\rvert^2/2}$. Thus

$$
P=\lvert\langle0_a\mid0\rangle\rvert^2
=e^{-a^2/(2x_0^2)}=e^{-\mechanical m\omega a^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar})}.
$$

It approaches one for $\lvert a\rvert\ll x_0$ and is exponentially small for $\lvert a\rvert\gg x_0$. The occupations of the new eigenstates follow a Poisson distribution of mean $\lvert\alpha\rvert^2$.

## Exercise 11 · Quadratic Stark effect on hydrogen 1s

The perturbation $\electric e\electric{\mathcal E}z$ has zero ground-state expectation by parity. The discrete second-order terms are

$$
\Delta\energy E_{100}^{(2)}\big|_{\mathrm{discrete}}
=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\lvert\langle n,1,0\rvert\electric e\electric{\mathcal E}z\lvert100\rangle\rvert^2}{\energy E_1-\energy E_n}.
$$

The dipole selection rules restrict the intermediate angular states to $\ell=1,m=0$. The denominators are $-(1-1/n^2)\unit{Ry}$. The source uses $a_0^2$ as a characteristic squared dipole length. This identifies the scale of the shift. The full hydrogen result is

$$
\Delta\energy E_{100}^{(2)}=-\frac94(4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0)a_0^3\electric{\mathcal E}^2
=-\frac12\alpha_0\electric{\mathcal E}^2,\qquad
\alpha_0=\frac92(4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0)a_0^3.
$$

The field polarizes the atom and lowers its ground energy. The polarizability is $9/2$ in atomic units.

> [!note] Scope of the discrete estimate
> The source states the exact coefficient after estimating the discrete sum. A constant estimate for every squared matrix element would give a divergent sum. The actual matrix elements decrease with $n$, and the exact coefficient includes continuum contributions.
