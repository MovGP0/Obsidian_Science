---
title: "Tunneling and Lifetimes"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A quasibound state can escape through a barrier. Its lifetime is the inverse of the encounter frequency times the transmission probability.

A particle confined by a finite barrier can occupy a **resonance** if there is an energetically allowed region beyond the barrier. This quasibound state has resonance energy $\energy E_0-i\energy{\Gamma}/2$, where $\energy{\Gamma}$ is the energy width. Its amplitude evolves as $e^{-i(\energy E_0-i\energy{\Gamma}/2)t/\mechanical{\hbar}}$, with magnitude $e^{-\energy{\Gamma}t/(2\mechanical{\hbar})}$. The survival probability is $e^{-\energy{\Gamma}t/\mechanical{\hbar}}$, so the mean lifetime is $\tau=\mechanical{\hbar}/\energy{\Gamma}$.

At each encounter with the barrier, the particle has escape probability $\signal T$. If it encounters the wall at frequency $\mechanical{\nu}$ and transmission is small, the rate is $\energy{\Gamma}/\mechanical{\hbar}\simeq\mechanical{\nu}\signal T$. Thus

$$
\tau\simeq\frac{1}{\mechanical{\nu}\signal T}.
$$

This is the estimate used for [[07.04 Application to Alpha Decay|alpha decay]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy E_0,\energy{\Gamma}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Resonance energy and width | The real part of the resonance energy and the width that controls decay. |
| $t,\tau$ | $\unit s$ | Time and lifetime | Elapsed time and the mean survival time. |
| $\mechanical{\nu}$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Encounter frequency | The rate at which the trapped wave reaches the barrier. |
| $\signal T$ | $1$ | Transmission probability | The escape probability per encounter. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant that relates resonance width to decay rate. |
