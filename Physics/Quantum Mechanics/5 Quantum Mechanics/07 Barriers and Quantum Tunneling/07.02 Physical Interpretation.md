> [!TLDR]
> Tunneling current comes from interference between the two evanescent components. The transmission is exponentially sensitive to barrier width, energy deficit, and particle mass.

## Penetration and current

Inside the barrier, the decaying component $e^{-\signal{\kappa}x}$ is evanescent and has no well-defined group velocity. The interior wavenumber is imaginary. The probability current comes from interference between $e^{-\signal{\kappa}x}$ and $e^{+\signal{\kappa}x}$. It is small and nonzero.

## Exponential dependence

For a sufficiently opaque barrier,

$$
\signal T\sim e^{-2\signal{\kappa}a}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\log\signal T\simeq-\frac{2a\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V_0-\energy E)}}{\mechanical{\hbar}}.
$$

Doubling $a$ squares the leading exponential factor. Small changes in the barrier parameters can therefore produce large relative changes in transmission.

## Role of mass

The decay constant satisfies $\signal{\kappa}\propto\sqrt{\mechanical m}$. A heavier particle therefore tunnels less readily. For an electron with an energy deficit of about $1\,\unit{eV}$ across a $1\,\unit{nm}$ barrier, the exponential factor is of order $10^{-4}$. For a proton with $\mechanical m_p\simeq1836\mechanical m_e$, the same parameters give a factor of order $10^{-190}$. Tunneling is more readily observed for light particles.

## Example. Scanning tunneling microscopy

A scanning tunneling microscope measures current between a fine metal tip and the sample. For work function $\energy{\phi}\simeq4\,\unit{eV}$, take $\energy V_0-\energy E\simeq\energy{\phi}$. Then $\signal{\kappa}\simeq\sqrt{2\mechanical m_e\energy{\phi}}/\mechanical{\hbar}\simeq10^{10}\,\unit{m^{-1}}$. The logarithmic slope is

$$
\frac{d\log\signal T}{da}\simeq-2\signal{\kappa}\simeq-2\times10^{10}\,\unit{m^{-1}}.
$$

Increasing the gap by $0.1\,\unit{nm}$ changes transmission by a factor of about $e^{-2}\simeq0.13$. The current responds strongly to changes in distance on the scale of a fraction of an atom. This sensitivity lets the microscope resolve atomic topography.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal T$ | $1$ | Transmission coefficient | The transmitted fraction of the incident probability current. |
| $\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Decay constant | The exponential decay rate inside the barrier. |
| $x,a$ | $\unit m$ | Position and barrier width | The coordinate and the barrier thickness or tip–sample gap. |
| $\energy V_0,\energy E,\energy{\phi}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The barrier height, particle energy, and work function. |
| $\mechanical m,\mechanical m_e,\mechanical m_p$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Masses | The general particle mass and the masses of the electron and proton. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale in the tunneling exponent. |
