> [!TLDR]
> Quantum tunneling gives a finite transmission probability through a barrier higher than the particle's energy. The rectangular barrier gives an exact result, and the WKB approximation extends it to smooth barriers.

A classical particle reflects from a barrier higher than its energy. A quantum particle has a small but nonzero probability of crossing a finite barrier. This effect is **tunneling**. It helps explain radioactive alpha decay, the operation of the scanning tunneling microscope, and nuclear fusion in stars.

This chapter solves the rectangular barrier, extends the result with the WKB approximation, and describes its main physical applications.

## Chapter summary

- For a [[07.01 Rectangular Potential Barrier|rectangular barrier]] with $\energy E<\energy V_0$, the exact result is $\signal T=[1+\energy V_0^{\,2}\sinh^2(\signal{\kappa}a)/(4\energy E(\energy V_0-\energy E))]^{-1}$.
- For $\signal{\kappa}a\gg1$, $\signal T\simeq[16\energy E(\energy V_0-\energy E)/\energy V_0^{\,2}]e^{-2\signal{\kappa}a}$.
- For $\energy E>\energy V_0$, complete transmission occurs at the resonances $\signal k'a=n\pi$.
- The [[07.03 WKB Approximation for Tunneling|WKB approximation]] gives $\signal T\simeq\exp[-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\signal{\kappa}(x)\,dx]$.
- Applications include [[07.02 Physical Interpretation|scanning tunneling microscopy]], [[07.04 Application to Alpha Decay|alpha decay and the Geiger–Nuttall law]], [[07.05 Other Applications#Stellar fusion|stellar fusion]], and [[07.05 Other Applications#Josephson junctions|Josephson junctions]].
- [[07.06 Tunneling and Lifetimes|Quasibound states]] decay exponentially with mean lifetime $\tau\simeq1/(\mechanical{\nu}\signal T)$ when the escape probability is small.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal T$ | $1$ | Transmission coefficient | The fraction of incident current transmitted through a barrier. |
| $\energy E,\energy V_0$ | $\unit{J}$ | Particle energy and barrier height | Their difference determines the type of interior solution. |
| $a,x_1,x_2$ | $\unit m$ | Barrier width and turning points | The thickness of a rectangular barrier and the WKB integration endpoints. |
| $\signal{\kappa},\signal k'$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Decay constant and interior wavenumber | The decay rate below the barrier and the oscillation rate above it. |
| $\mechanical{\nu},\tau$ | $\unit{Hz},\unit s$ | Encounter frequency and lifetime | The barrier encounter rate and the mean time to escape. |
