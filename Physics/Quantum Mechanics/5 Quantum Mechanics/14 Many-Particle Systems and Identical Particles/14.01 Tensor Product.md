---
title: "Tensor Product"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The tensor product combines two state spaces. Operators on distinct factors commute, and a general joint state can be a superposition of product states.

For two systems $A,B$ with Hilbert spaces $\mathcal H_A,\mathcal H_B$,

$$
\mathcal H_{AB}=\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B.
$$

If $\{\lvert a_i\rangle\}$ and $\{\lvert b_j\rangle\}$ are bases, then $\{\lvert a_i\rangle\otimes\lvert b_j\rangle\}$ is a product basis of the joint space. For finite dimensions, $\dim\mathcal H_{AB}=\dim\mathcal H_A\cdot\dim\mathcal H_B$.

The operator $\hat A\otimes\hat I_B$ acts on $A$, $\hat I_A\otimes\hat B$ acts on $B$, and $\hat A\otimes\hat B$ acts on both. The two operators on distinct factors commute.

$$
[\hat A\otimes\hat I_B,\hat I_A\otimes\hat B]=0.
$$

A general joint state need not factor. For distinct basis vectors on each side and $c_1,c_2\ne0$, the state

$$
\lvert\signal{\Psi}\rangle=c_1\lvert a_1,b_1\rangle+c_2\lvert a_2,b_2\rangle
$$

cannot be written as $\lvert\alpha\rangle_A\otimes\lvert\beta\rangle_B$. Such a state is **entangled**.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\lvert\signal{\Psi}\rangle,c_1,c_2$ | $1$ | Joint state and amplitudes | The state of both systems and the coefficients in its product-basis expansion. |
| $\hat A,\hat B$ | Depends on observable | Subsystem operators | Operators acting on their respective tensor factors. |
