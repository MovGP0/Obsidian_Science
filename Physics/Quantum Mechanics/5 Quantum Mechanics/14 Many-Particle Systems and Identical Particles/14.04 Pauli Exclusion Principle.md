> [!TLDR]
> Antisymmetrization gives zero if two fermions occupy the same complete single-particle state. This is the Pauli exclusion principle.

> [!theorem] Theorem 14.1. Pauli exclusion principle
> Two identical fermions cannot occupy the same complete single-particle quantum state.

Indeed, the antisymmetric combination of two copies of $\lvert\alpha\rangle$ is $\lvert\alpha,\alpha\rangle-\lvert\alpha,\alpha\rangle=0$. The zero vector cannot describe a normalized state.

## Slater determinant

For $N$ fermions occupying orthonormal single-particle states $\lvert\signal{\phi}_1\rangle,\ldots,\lvert\signal{\phi}_N\rangle$, the normalized antisymmetric amplitude is

$$
\signal{\Psi}(1,\ldots,N)=\frac1{\sqrt{N!}}
\det\begin{pmatrix}
\signal{\phi}_1(1)&\cdots&\signal{\phi}_1(N)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\signal{\phi}_N(1)&\cdots&\signal{\phi}_N(N)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Interchanging two particle coordinates swaps two columns and changes the sign. If two occupied states coincide, two rows coincide and the determinant vanishes.

## Atomic structure

Atomic electrons are fermions. The independent-particle picture builds the ground-state configuration by filling the lowest available single-electron levels. Each subshell $n\ell$ has $2(2\ell+1)$ states, including the two spin projections. Filling subshells in sequences such as $1s^2,2s^2,2p^6,3s^2,\ldots$ helps explain the periodic table.

Each spatial orbital can contain up to two electrons, with opposite spin. As the nuclear charge and electron number grow, the exclusion principle requires electrons to occupy additional orbitals and shells. This is one reason matter occupies space. ## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}_i(j)$ | $\unit{m^{-3/2}}$ for a three-dimensional position amplitude | Single-particle amplitude | Orbital $i$ evaluated at particle $j$'s coordinates. |
| $\signal{\Psi}$ | $\unit{m^{-3N/2}}$ for $N$ three-dimensional position coordinates | Fermion amplitude | The antisymmetrized many-particle wave function. |
| $N$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of rows and columns of the Slater determinant. |
| $n,\ell$ | $1$ | Atomic orbital quantum numbers | The principal and orbital angular momentum labels of a subshell. |
| $\langle n\rangle$ | $1$ | Mean occupation | The average number of particles in a single-particle state. |
| $\energy{\varepsilon},\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy and chemical potential | Their difference sets the occupation relative to the thermal energy. |
| $\thermal T,\thermal{k_B}$ | $\unit K,\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Temperature and Boltzmann constant | Their product sets the thermal energy scale. |
