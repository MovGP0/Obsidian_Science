---
title: "Identical Particles"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Exchanging the labels of identical particles leaves their physical state invariant. In the symmetric and antisymmetric sectors, the exchange operator has eigenvalue $+1$ or $-1$.

Two electrons, two protons, or two atoms of the same isotope such as $^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ are intrinsically identical. Their labels alone have no observable meaning. The symmetrization postulate restricts the physical states of identical particles.

## Exchange of two particles

For a product state, the exchange operator acts as

$$
\hat P_{12}\lvert\signal{\psi}_1,\signal{\psi}_2\rangle
=\lvert\signal{\psi}_2,\signal{\psi}_1\rangle.
$$

Since $\hat P_{12}^2=\hat I$, its eigenvalues are $\pm1$. Exchanging the labels of identical particles gives the same physical ray. The allowed state is symmetric for eigenvalue $+1$ and antisymmetric for eigenvalue $-1$.

> [!definition] Definition 14.2. Bosons and fermions
> **Bosons** have states symmetric under the exchange of any pair and have integer spin. **Fermions** have states antisymmetric under the exchange of any pair and have half-integer spin.

The spin–statistics connection is a theorem of relativistic quantum field theory, attributed here to Pauli (1940). In nonrelativistic quantum mechanics it is an empirical postulate.

## Symmetric and antisymmetric states

For distinct orthonormal single-particle states $\lvert\alpha\rangle,\lvert\beta\rangle$,

$$
\lvert\signal{\Psi}^{(S)}\rangle
=\frac{\lvert\alpha,\beta\rangle+\lvert\beta,\alpha\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\lvert\signal{\Psi}^{(A)}\rangle
=\frac{\lvert\alpha,\beta\rangle-\lvert\beta,\alpha\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

If the one-particle states coincide, the antisymmetric expression vanishes. This gives the Pauli exclusion principle.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat P_{12}$ | $1$ | Exchange operator | The operator that interchanges the two particle labels. |
| $\lvert\signal{\Psi}^{(S)}\rangle,\lvert\signal{\Psi}^{(A)}\rangle$ | $1$ | Symmetric and antisymmetric states | The normalized two-particle states constructed from orthonormal single-particle states. |
| $s$ | $1$ | Spin quantum number | The integer or half-integer label related to exchange statistics. |
