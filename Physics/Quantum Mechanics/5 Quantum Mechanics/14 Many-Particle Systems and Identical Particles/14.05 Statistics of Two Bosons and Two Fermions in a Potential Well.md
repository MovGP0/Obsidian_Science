---
title: "Statistics of Two Bosons and Two Fermions in a Potential Well"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In an infinite well, two bosons can occupy the lowest orbital. Two fermions with the same spin must occupy different orbitals, raising the ground-state energy.

Consider two noninteracting particles in an infinite well on $[0,L]$, with orbitals $\signal{\phi}_n(x)$ and $\energy E_n=n^2\energy E_1$.

- **Distinguishable particles.** Both can occupy the lowest orbital. The energy is $2\energy E_1$.
- **Bosons.** The same product $\lvert\signal{\phi}_1,\signal{\phi}_1\rangle$ is symmetric and has energy $2\energy E_1$.
- **Fermions with no available spin degeneracy.** The antisymmetric ground state is
  $$
  \lvert\signal{\Psi}_A\rangle
  =\frac{\lvert\signal{\phi}_1,\signal{\phi}_2\rangle-\lvert\signal{\phi}_2,\signal{\phi}_1\rangle}{\sqrt2},
  \qquad \energy E=\energy E_1+\energy E_2=5\energy E_1.
  $$

The fermion ground energy is $5/2$ times the boson result for the two-particle example. For $N$ fermions in a fixed well without spin degeneracy,

$$
\energy E_{\min}=\energy E_1\sum_{n=1}^N n^2
=\energy E_1\frac{N(N+1)(2N+1)}6
\sim\frac{\energy E_1}3N^3.
$$

For $N$ noninteracting bosons, $\energy E_{\min}=N\energy E_1$. If spin $1/2$ is available, two fermions with opposite spin can share each spatial orbital. For even $N$, the lowest $N/2$ orbitals are filled with both spin projections. For odd $N$, the next orbital is singly occupied.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $L,x$ | $\unit m$ | Well width and position | The length of the confining interval and the coordinate within it. |
| $\signal{\phi}_n$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Single-particle orbital | The normalized amplitude of the well level $n$. |
| $\energy E_n,\energy E_{\min}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy level and ground energy | The one-particle energy and the minimum energy at fixed particle count. |
| $N,n$ | $1$ | Particle count and level index | The number of particles and the single-particle excitation label. |
