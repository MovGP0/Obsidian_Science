> [!TLDR]
> Many-particle states belong to a tensor product. For identical particles, exchange symmetry restricts the allowed states and gives the Pauli exclusion principle for fermions.

Combine the Hilbert spaces of the particles with a tensor product. For identical particles, such as two electrons, two photons, or two atoms of the same isotope in the same internal species, exchange symmetry restricts the physical states. This restriction distinguishes bosons from fermions. For fermions it leads to the Pauli exclusion principle, which helps explain the periodic structure of the elements and the properties of matter.

## Chapter summary

- The space for $N$ labeled particles is $\mathcal H_{\mathrm{tot}}=\mathcal H_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal H_N$. Subsystem operators act on individual factors.
- Entangled pure states cannot be factored. Bell states are maximally entangled two-qubit examples and illustrate entanglement as a physical resource.
- Identical bosons have symmetric states and identical fermions have antisymmetric states. The spin–statistics connection associates these with integer and half-integer spin.
- The Pauli exclusion principle forbids two identical fermions from occupying the same complete one-particle state. A Slater determinant implements antisymmetry for $N$ fermions.
- Filling atomic orbitals with the Pauli constraint accounts for the periodic structure.
- Helium has a $1s^2$ singlet ground state. Exchange energy splits singlet and triplet states of singly excited configurations.
- Hund's rules first maximize $S$ and then $L$ for the lowest term of a partially filled subshell. They also specify the lowest $J$ in the usual coupling regime.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $N$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles in the combined system. |
| $\mathcal H_i,\mathcal H_{\mathrm{tot}}$ | — | Hilbert spaces | The individual spaces and their tensor product. |
| $\lvert\Phi^\pm\rangle,\lvert\Psi^\pm\rangle$ | $1$ | Bell states | The four maximally entangled states of two qubits. |
| $S,L,J$ | $1$ | Term quantum numbers | The spin, orbital, and total angular momentum labels used in Hund's rules. |
