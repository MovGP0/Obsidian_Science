---
title: "Separable and Entangled States"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A pure bipartite state is entangled if it cannot be factored into subsystem states. Its reduced density matrix is then mixed.

Let the subsystems $A$ and $B$ have Hilbert spaces $\mathcal H_A$ and $\mathcal H_B$. The joint space is $\mathcal H=\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$. A pure state $\lvert\signal{\Psi}\rangle$ is **separable** if there are states $\lvert\signal{\psi}_A\rangle\in\mathcal H_A$ and $\lvert\signal{\psi}_B\rangle\in\mathcal H_B$ such that

$$
\lvert\signal{\Psi}\rangle=\lvert\signal{\psi}_A\rangle\otimes\lvert\signal{\psi}_B\rangle.
$$

Otherwise the state is **entangled**.

> [!definition] Definition 15.1 · Entanglement of pure states
> A pure bipartite state is entangled if and only if at least two terms in its Schmidt decomposition have nonzero coefficients. Equivalently, the reduced state $\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B(\lvert\signal{\Psi}\rangle\langle\signal{\Psi}\rvert)$ is mixed.

For two qubits, use the computational basis $\{\lvert0\rangle,\lvert1\rangle\}$ for each factor. Both $\lvert\signal{\Psi}_1\rangle=\lvert0\rangle\otimes\lvert0\rangle=\lvert00\rangle$ and $\lvert\signal{\Psi}_2\rangle=(\lvert0\rangle+\lvert1\rangle)\otimes(\lvert0\rangle-\lvert1\rangle)/2$ are separable. Now consider

$$
\lvert\signal{\Phi}^+\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(\lvert00\rangle+\lvert11\rangle).
$$

A putative product $(a\lvert0\rangle+b\lvert1\rangle)\otimes(c\lvert0\rangle+d\lvert1\rangle)$ would require $ac=bd=1/\sqrt2$ and $ad=bc=0$. The first two products require all four coefficients to be nonzero, in conflict with the last two conditions. Thus $\lvert\signal{\Phi}^+\rangle$ is entangled.

A mixed state $\rho$ is separable if it has a decomposition $\rho=\sum_k p_k\rho_A^{(k)}\otimes\rho_B^{(k)}$ with $p_k\ge0$ and $\sum_k p_k=1$. Otherwise it is entangled. A separable mixed state can have correlations. In dimensions above $2\times3$, some entangled states have a positive partial transpose and escape that test for entanglement. The pure-state criterion is sufficient for the applications here.

The **entanglement entropy** of a pure bipartite state is the [[13.05 Von Neumann Entropy|von Neumann entropy]] of either reduced state. With Schmidt coefficients $\lambda_k$,

$$
E(\lvert\signal{\Psi}\rangle)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A\log_2\rho_A)=-\sum_k\lambda_k^2\log_2\lambda_k^2.
$$

The entropy is zero for separable pure states and one bit for maximally entangled two-qubit states.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\rho,\rho_A,\rho_B$ | $1$ | Density operators | The joint state and the reduced states of the two subsystems. |
| $p_k,\lambda_k$ | $1$ | Mixture weights and Schmidt coefficients | The weights of product states and the nonnegative amplitudes in a Schmidt decomposition. |
| $E(\lvert\signal{\Psi}\rangle)$ | bit | Entanglement entropy | The entropy of either reduced state for a pure bipartite state. |
