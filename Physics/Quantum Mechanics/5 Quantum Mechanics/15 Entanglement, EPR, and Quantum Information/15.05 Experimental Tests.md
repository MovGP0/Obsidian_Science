---
title: "Experimental Tests"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Bell tests measure correlations that violate the CHSH inequality. Later experiments closed the main loopholes in a single test.

Aspect and his coworkers performed early convincing tests of CHSH violation in 1981 and 1982. They obtained pairs of polarized photons from atomic calcium cascades and analyzed them with adjustable polarimeters. The results agreed with the quantum prediction to within a few percentage points and conflicted with local hidden-variable predictions under the test assumptions.

Several **loopholes** remained. The detection loophole arises when the fraction of registered pairs is too small to rule out selective sampling. The locality loophole arises when the timing of detector choices leaves time for a signal between the two stations. A third loophole concerns the independence of the settings from the variables carried by the particles.

From 2015 onward, experiments with photons over long distances, pairs of nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond, and other platforms closed the main loopholes simultaneously. They confirmed the violation with high statistical significance.

> [!note] Detection efficiency
> Closing the detection loophole requires the combined efficiency of the source and detectors to exceed a threshold that depends on the prepared state and test. In the ideal symmetric case, the singlet requires about $82.8\%$ efficiency. An optimized test with a nonmaximally entangled pair can approach $2/3$, below $67\%$. This makes a test possible with less efficient detectors.
