> [!TLDR]
> Compatibility determines which observables can be jointly sharp, and the Robertson relation bounds the spreads of incompatible observables.

This chapter asks three questions.

- When can two observables be measured jointly with arbitrarily high precision?
- What does it mean to measure an observable with a broad spectrum that can contain degenerate values?
- What lower bound applies to the spreads of two incompatible observables?

The Heisenberg–Robertson relation answers the last question. It follows from the geometry of Hilbert space and the commutator of the operators. This bound describes uncertainty in the prepared state and applies independently of the measuring apparatus.

## Chapter summary

- Compatible observables with discrete spectra have a common eigenbasis.
- A CSCO specifies the joint eigenstates uniquely through their eigenvalues.
- Robertson gives $\Delta A\Delta B\ge\tfrac12\lvert\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\rangle\rvert$. For position and momentum, $\Delta\mechanical{x}\,\Delta\mechanical{p}\ge\hbar/2$. Gaussian states can attain equality.
- Mandelstam–Tamm gives $\Delta\energy{E}\,\tau_B\ge\hbar/2$, where $\tau_B$ characterizes the change of $\langle\hat B\rangle$.
- Frequent measurements can inhibit the system's time evolution. This is the quantum Zeno effect.
- Ideal measurements use projectors. POVMs describe outcome probabilities in more general settings.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\Delta A,\Delta B$ | — | Standard deviations | The spreads of outcomes for the two observables. |
| $\Delta\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position uncertainty | The standard deviation in position. |
| $\Delta\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum uncertainty | The standard deviation in momentum. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale in the uncertainty relations. |
| $\Delta\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy uncertainty | The standard deviation of the Hamiltonian. |
| $\tau_B$ | $\unit{s}$ | Characteristic time | The time for the mean of the observable to change by one standard deviation at its current rate. |
