---
title: "Ideal and Real Measurements"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Real measurements can have finite resolution or disturb the system. Positive operator-valued measures describe their outcome probabilities.

The measurement postulate in [[02.04 Wave Function Collapse|Chapter 2]] describes an ideal projective measurement, also called a von Neumann measurement. The result selects an eigenspace and updates the state. A real apparatus has finite resolution, interacts with the system for a finite time, and can disturb or destroy the system during readout.

**Quantum nondemolition measurement (QND).** A QND measurement of $\hat A$ preserves the possibility of measuring it again with the same result. For example, a conserved observable can be measured through an interaction that couples the system to a pointer while preserving $\hat A$.

**Weak measurement.** A weak interaction between the apparatus and system gives little information in a single readout and causes little disturbance. The state update is partial. With appropriate calibration, an average over many such readouts recovers the expected value.

**Positive operator-valued measure (POVM).** A discrete POVM is a family of positive operators $\{\hat E_m\}$ with $\sum_m\hat E_m=\hat I$. For a normalized state, the probability of outcome $m$ is $P(m)=\langle\psi\mid\hat E_m\mid\psi\rangle$. A projective measurement is a special case with $\hat E_m=\hat P_m$. POVMs are standard tools in quantum information theory.

The distinction between ideal and real measurements is important in the laboratory. This book generally uses projective measurements as an idealization and returns to the more general description in its later chapters.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $P(m)$ | $1$ | Outcome probability | The probability of the measurement outcome labeled $m$. |
