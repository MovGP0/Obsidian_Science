title: "Robertson Inequality"

> [!TLDR]
> The Robertson inequality bounds the product of two measurement standard deviations by the expectation of their commutator.

> [!theorem] Theorem 3.2 | Robertson Inequality
> For self-adjoint observables $\hat A$ and $\hat B$ and a normalized state $\lvert\psi\rangle$ for which the expressions are defined,
>
> $$
> \Delta A\,\Delta B\ge
> \tfrac12\left\lvert\langle\psi\mid[\hat A,\hat B]\mid\psi\rangle\right\rvert.
> $$

**Proof.** Define the centered operators $\hat a=\hat A-\langle\hat A\rangle\hat I$ and $\hat b=\hat B-\langle\hat B\rangle\hat I$. The variances are $\langle\hat a^2\rangle$ and $\langle\hat b^2\rangle$. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to $\hat a\lvert\psi\rangle$ and $\hat b\lvert\psi\rangle$.

$$
\langle\hat a^2\rangle\langle\hat b^2\rangle
\ge\left\lvert\langle\hat a\hat b\rangle\right\rvert^2.
$$

Write $\hat a\hat b=\tfrac12\{\hat a,\hat b\}+\tfrac12[\hat a,\hat b]$, where $\{\hat a,\hat b\}=\hat a\hat b+\hat b\hat a$. The first term is Hermitian and has a real expectation. The second is anti-Hermitian and has a purely imaginary expectation. Since the squared magnitude of a complex number is at least the square of its imaginary part,

$$
\left\lvert\langle\hat a\hat b\rangle\right\rvert^2
\ge \tfrac14\left\lvert\langle[\hat a,\hat b]\rangle\right\rvert^2.
$$

The scalar subtractions commute, so $[\hat a,\hat b]=[\hat A,\hat B]$. Taking the square root proves the inequality.

## Position and momentum

For $\hat A=\mechanical{\hat x}$ and $\hat B=\mechanical{\hat p}$, the commutator is $i\hbar\hat I$. The bound in a normalized state is

$$
\Delta\mechanical{x}\,\Delta\mechanical{p}\ge\frac{\hbar}{2}.
$$

This is the **Heisenberg uncertainty relation**. A Gaussian state can attain equality.

## Example. A minimum-uncertainty Gaussian

Take $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}e^{-\mechanical{x}^2/(4\sigma^2)}$. Then

$$
\langle\mechanical{x}\rangle=0,\qquad
\langle\mechanical{x}^2\rangle=\sigma^2,\qquad \Delta\mechanical{x}=\sigma.
$$

The Fourier transform is also Gaussian. Its momentum width is $\hbar/(2\sigma)$.

$$
\langle\mechanical{p}\rangle=0,\qquad
\langle\mechanical{p}^2\rangle=\frac{\hbar^2}{4\sigma^2},\qquad
\Delta\mechanical{p}=\frac{\hbar}{2\sigma}.
$$

Thus $\Delta\mechanical{x}\,\Delta\mechanical{p}=\hbar/2$. These **minimum-uncertainty states** appear again for the [[08.00 Harmonic Oscillator|harmonic oscillator]] and are prototypes of coherent states.

The horizontal axes and densities are scaled so that each curve has unit area in its plotted coordinate. Both distributions are symmetric and saturate the Robertson inequality.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\Delta A,\Delta B$ | — | Measurement standard deviations | The spreads of the two outcomes, with the units of their respective observables. |
| $\mechanical{\hat x},\mechanical{x},\Delta\mechanical{x},\sigma$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and position width | The position operator, coordinate, and Gaussian standard deviation. |
| $\mechanical{\hat p},\mechanical{p},\Delta\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum and momentum width | The momentum operator, coordinate, and Gaussian standard deviation. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale in the minimum uncertainty product. |
| $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Position wave function | The Gaussian probability amplitude per square root of length. |
| $\signal{\tilde\psi}(\mechanical{p})$ | $\unit{kg^{-1/2}\,m^{-1/2}\,s^{1/2}}$ | Momentum wave function | The amplitude normalized over the momentum coordinate. |
