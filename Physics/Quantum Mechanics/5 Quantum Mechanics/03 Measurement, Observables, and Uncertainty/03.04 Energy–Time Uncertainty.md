---
title: "Energy–Time Uncertainty"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Mandelstam–Tamm inequality relates energy spread to the rate at which an observable changes.

The heuristic relation $\Delta\energy{E}\,\Delta t\gtrsim\hbar/2$ does not follow from a commutator between energy and time. In ordinary quantum mechanics, $t$ is a parameter. The Mandelstam–Tamm relation instead applies Robertson to the Hamiltonian and a time-independent observable.

> [!theorem] Theorem 3.3 | Mandelstam–Tamm Inequality
> For a state and a time-independent observable $\hat B$ with a nonzero expectation rate,
>
> $$
> \Delta\energy{E}\,\tau_B\ge\frac{\hbar}{2},\qquad
> \tau_B=\frac{\Delta B}{\lvert d\langle\hat B\rangle/dt\rvert}.
> $$
>
> The characteristic time $\tau_B$ is the time for the expectation to change by one standard deviation at its instantaneous rate.

**Proof.** The Heisenberg equation gives $d\langle\hat B\rangle/dt=\langle[\hat B,\energy{\hat H}]\rangle/(i\hbar)$. Therefore Robertson gives

$$
\Delta B\,\Delta\energy{E}\ge\tfrac12\lvert\langle[\hat B,\energy{\hat H}]\rangle\rvert
=\frac{\hbar}{2}\left\lvert\frac{d\langle\hat B\rangle}{dt}\right\rvert.
$$

Divide by the magnitude of the expectation rate.

**Stationary states.** In an eigenstate of a time-independent Hamiltonian, $\Delta\energy{E}=0$. The inequality before division then gives $d\langle\hat B\rangle/dt=0$ for every time-independent observable to which it applies. Fast changes relative to the spread of an observable require a large energy spread.

**Line width.** An excited state of lifetime $\tau$ has a characteristic energy line width $\energy{\Gamma}\simeq\hbar/\tau$ for exponential decay. Long lifetimes give narrow lines, and short lifetimes give broad resonances. For the hydrogen transition $2p\to1s$, the orders of magnitude are $\tau\sim\quantity{10^{-9}}{s}$ and $\energy{\Gamma}\sim\quantity{10^{-7}}{eV}$. The natural line is very narrow.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\Delta\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy standard deviation | The spread of Hamiltonian outcomes in the state. |
| $\energy{\hat H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The generator of time evolution. |
| $\Delta B,\langle\hat B\rangle$ | — | Observable spread and mean | Their unit is the unit of the observable. |
| $t,\Delta t,\tau_B,\tau$ | $\unit{s}$ | Times | The time parameter, heuristic interval, characteristic change time, and excited-state lifetime. |
| $\energy{\Gamma}$ | $\unit{eV}$ | Natural energy line width | The spectral width associated with the lifetime. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale in the energy–time relation. |
