---
title: "Combining Two Spin-½ Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The three triplet states of two spin-$1/2$ particles are symmetric under exchange. The singlet is antisymmetric.

Two spin-$1/2$ particles give four product states. The triangle rule gives $J\in\{0,1\}$. In Eq. (11.8), $\lvert0,0\rangle$ is the antisymmetric **singlet**. The states $\lvert1,M\rangle$ for $M=-1,0,1$ form the symmetric **triplet**. [[10.06 Introduction to Combining Two Spin-½ Systems|Chapter 10]] gives the same states directly. They are a special case of the general Clebsch–Gordan expansion.

For two identical fermions, the complete state is antisymmetric under exchange. Combine the antisymmetric spin singlet with a symmetric spatial state, as discussed in [[14.00 Many-Particle Systems and Identical Particles]]. Two electrons occupying the same bonding orbital have a symmetric spatial factor, so their spin state is the singlet. This is the usual low-energy paired configuration in a bonding orbital.
