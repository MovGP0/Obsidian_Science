---
title: "Clebsch–Gordan Coefficients"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Clebsch–Gordan coefficients form a unitary change of basis. A coefficient can be nonzero only when the individual projections add to the total projection.

The **Clebsch–Gordan coefficients** (CG coefficients) are the overlaps of the two bases.

$$
\lvert j_1j_2;JM\rangle
=\sum_{m_1,m_2}\langle j_1m_1;j_2m_2\mid JM\rangle
\,\lvert j_1m_1;j_2m_2\rangle.
\tag{11.6}
$$

Here $\lvert JM\rangle$ is short for the coupled state at the fixed $j_1,j_2$. Because $\mechanical{\hat J}_z=\mechanical{\hat J}_{1z}+\mechanical{\hat J}_{2z}$, the overlap vanishes if $m_1+m_2\ne M$.

## Properties

In the real phase convention, orthogonality in both directions gives

$$
\sum_{m_1,m_2}\langle j_1m_1;j_2m_2\mid JM\rangle
\langle j_1m_1;j_2m_2\mid J'M'\rangle=\delta_{JJ'}\delta_{MM'},
\tag{11.7}
$$

$$
\sum_{J,M}\langle j_1m_1;j_2m_2\mid JM\rangle
\langle j_1m'_1;j_2m'_2\mid JM\rangle=\delta_{m_1m'_1}\delta_{m_2m'_2}.
$$

The Condon–Shortley convention fixes the coefficients to be real and sets $\langle j_1j_1;j_2(J-j_1)\mid JJ\rangle>0$.

Exchange of the two angular momenta gives

$$
\langle j_1m_1;j_2m_2\mid JM\rangle
=(-1)^{j_1+j_2-J}\langle j_2m_2;j_1m_1\mid JM\rangle.
$$

## Examples

For $j_1=j_2=1/2$, write $\lvert\pm\rangle=\lvert\tfrac12,\pm\tfrac12\rangle$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert1,1\rangle&=\lvert+\rangle\otimes\lvert+\rangle,\\
\lvert1,0\rangle&=\frac{\lvert+\rangle\otimes\lvert-\rangle+\lvert-\rangle\otimes\lvert+\rangle}{\sqrt2},\\
\lvert1,-1\rangle&=\lvert-\rangle\otimes\lvert-\rangle,\\
\lvert0,0\rangle&=\frac{\lvert+\rangle\otimes\lvert-\rangle-\lvert-\rangle\otimes\lvert+\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.8}
$$

For $j_1=1$ and $j_2=1/2$, the coupled space contains a quartet with $J=3/2$ and a doublet with $J=1/2$. Three states are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert\tfrac32,\tfrac32\rangle
&=\lvert1,1\rangle\otimes\lvert\tfrac12,\tfrac12\rangle,\\
\lvert\tfrac32,\tfrac12\rangle
&=\sqrt{\tfrac13}\lvert1,1\rangle\otimes\lvert\tfrac12,-\tfrac12\rangle
+\sqrt{\tfrac23}\lvert1,0\rangle\otimes\lvert\tfrac12,\tfrac12\rangle,\\
\lvert\tfrac12,\tfrac12\rangle
&=\sqrt{\tfrac23}\lvert1,1\rangle\otimes\lvert\tfrac12,-\tfrac12\rangle
-\sqrt{\tfrac13}\lvert1,0\rangle\otimes\lvert\tfrac12,\tfrac12\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.9}
$$

> [!note] Constructing the coefficients
> Start with $\lvert J=j_1+j_2,M=j_1+j_2\rangle=\lvert j_1j_1\rangle\otimes\lvert j_2j_2\rangle$. Repeatedly apply the total lowering operator $\mechanical{\hat J}_-=\mechanical{\hat J}_{1-}+\mechanical{\hat J}_{2-}$ and normalize to obtain the rest of that multiplet. At $M=j_1+j_2-1$, choose a normalized state orthogonal to the state already constructed and set its Condon–Shortley phase. This starts the $J=j_1+j_2-1$ multiplet. Repeat until all multiplets are included.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\langle j_1m_1;j_2m_2\mid JM\rangle$ | $1$ | Clebsch–Gordan coefficient | The amplitude of the uncoupled basis state in the coupled state. |
| $j_1,j_2,J,m_1,m_2,M$ | $1$ | Angular momentum labels | The individual and total representation and projection indices. |
| $\mechanical{\hat J}_-,\mechanical{\hat J}_{1-},\mechanical{\hat J}_{2-}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Lowering operators | The total operator and its two individual contributions. |
