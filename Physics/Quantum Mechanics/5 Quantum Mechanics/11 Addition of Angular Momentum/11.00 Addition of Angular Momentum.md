> [!TLDR]
> Angular momenta add as operators. Clebsch–Gordan coefficients change from the product basis of the two parts to the basis of total angular momentum.

[[09.00 Orbital Angular Momentum|Orbital angular momentum]] and [[10.00 Spin|spin]] act on separate degrees of freedom of the same system. Atoms, nuclei, and molecules have total angular momentum equal to the vector sum of their contributions. The product basis of the separate angular momenta generally differs from the eigenbasis of the total angular momentum.

Clebsch–Gordan coefficients relate the two bases. The Wigner–Eckart theorem uses them to separate the geometric and dynamical parts of matrix elements. This chapter derives the addition rules and applies them to spin–orbit coupling and transition selection rules.

## Chapter summary

- The total $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}}=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}}_1+\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}}_2$ satisfies the angular momentum algebra. The allowed $J$ values run from $\lvert j_1-j_2\rvert$ to $j_1+j_2$ in unit steps.
- The space of dimension $(2j_1+1)(2j_2+1)$ has an uncoupled basis $\{\lvert j_1m_1;j_2m_2\rangle\}$ and a coupled basis $\{\lvert j_1j_2;JM\rangle\}$. Real Clebsch–Gordan coefficients in the Condon–Shortley convention give an orthogonal change of basis.
- The coefficients require $M=m_1+m_2$ and obey exchange symmetry and ladder recurrences.
- Two spin-$1/2$ particles give a singlet with $J=0$ and a triplet with $J=1$. Combining $\ell$ with spin $1/2$ gives $j=\ell\pm1/2$ for $\ell\ge1$ and $j=1/2$ for $\ell=0$.
- Commutators with $\mechanical{\hat J}_z$ and $\mechanical{\hat J}_\pm$ define a spherical tensor. The identity is rank zero and position is rank one.
- The Wigner–Eckart theorem factors each spherical tensor matrix element into an angular coefficient and a reduced matrix element.
- Electric dipole transitions obey $\Delta j=0,\pm1$ with $j=0\to j'=0$ forbidden, and $\Delta m=0,\pm1$. In hydrogen-like orbitals the rank and parity conditions give $\Delta\ell=\pm1$.
