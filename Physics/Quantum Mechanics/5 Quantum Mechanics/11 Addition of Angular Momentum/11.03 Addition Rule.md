title: "Addition Rule"

> [!TLDR]
> The triangle rule gives the allowed total angular momenta from $\lvert j_1-j_2\rvert$ to $j_1+j_2$ in unit steps. Each occurs once.

In the coupled basis, the **triangle rule** is

$$
J\in\{\lvert j_1-j_2\rvert,\lvert j_1-j_2\rvert+1,\ldots,j_1+j_2\}.
\tag{11.5}
$$

Each allowed representation occurs once. Its dimension is $2J+1$. The total dimension agrees with Eq. (11.4).

$$
\sum_{J=\lvert j_1-j_2\rvert}^{j_1+j_2}(2J+1)=(2j_1+1)(2j_2+1).
$$

Dimension alone checks the total. At fixed $M$, the number of uncoupled pairs $(m_1,m_2)$ with $m_1+m_2=M$ equals the number of allowed $J$ values containing that $M$. The number of representations with highest weight $J$ is the difference between the counts at $M=J$ and $M=J+1$. This gives one copy of each $J$ in Eq. (11.5).

Vectors at an intermediate angle give intermediate labels, and antiparallel vectors give the smallest $J$ label. The quantum triangle rule permits the values between these limits in unit steps.

## Physical meaning

The geometry of rotations determines which $J$ values occur and how the states recombine. This information depends only on $j_1,j_2$. The dynamics of a particular system enter through reduced matrix elements. The Wigner–Eckart theorem factors these two kinds of information.
