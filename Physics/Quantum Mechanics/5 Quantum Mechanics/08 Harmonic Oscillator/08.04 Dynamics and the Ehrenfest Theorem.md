---
title: "Dynamics and the Ehrenfest Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The mean position and momentum of the harmonic oscillator obey the classical equations exactly because the force is linear in position.

The [[04.06 Ehrenfest Theorem|Ehrenfest theorem]] gives the classical equations of motion for the means. If $\langle\hat x(0)\rangle=A$ and $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}(0)\rangle=0$, their solutions are

$$
\langle\hat x(t)\rangle=A\cos(\mechanical{\omega}t),\qquad
\langle\mechanical{\hat p}(t)\rangle=-\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}A\sin(\mechanical{\omega}t).
$$

This exact correspondence among the confining potentials treated here follows from the linearity of $\energy{V'}(x)$.

## Example. A superposition of the lowest two levels

Start in $(\lvert0\rangle+\lvert1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. The evolved state is

$$
\lvert\signal{\psi}(t)\rangle=
\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{\omega}t/2}\lvert0\rangle+e^{-3i\mechanical{\omega}t/2}\lvert1\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

The diagonal terms of $\hat x$ vanish. The remaining terms are

$$
\langle\hat x(t)\rangle=\frac12
\left(\langle0\lvert\hat x\rvert1\rangle e^{-i\mechanical{\omega}t}
+\langle1\lvert\hat x\rvert0\rangle e^{i\mechanical{\omega}t}\right)
=x_0'\cos(\mechanical{\omega}t),
$$

where $x_0'=\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}/(2\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega})}$. Thus the mean position oscillates at the classical frequency, as Ehrenfest's theorem gives.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\langle\hat x\rangle,A,x_0'$ | $\unit m$ | Mean position and amplitudes | The expectation value and the amplitudes in the two examples. |
| $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Mean momentum | The expectation value of the momentum operator. |
| $\energy V$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The quadratic potential that gives a linear force. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The oscillator mass. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The classical and mean quantum oscillation frequency. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the position matrix element. |
| $t$ | $\unit s$ | Time | The time after preparation. |
