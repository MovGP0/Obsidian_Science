> [!TLDR]
> The annihilation and creation operators lower and raise oscillator eigenstates. Their commutator determines the equally spaced energy levels.

Define the **annihilation operator** $\hat a$ and its adjoint, the **creation operator** $\hat a^\dagger$.

$$
\hat a=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}{2\mechanical{\hbar}}}
\left(\hat x+\frac{i}{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}\mechanical{\hat p}\right),\qquad
\hat a^\dagger=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}{2\mechanical{\hbar}}}
\left(\hat x-\frac{i}{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}\mechanical{\hat p}\right).
$$

The canonical commutator $[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}]=i\mechanical{\hbar}\hat I$ gives $[\hat a,\hat a^\dagger]=\hat I$.

The inverse relations are

$$
\hat x=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}}{2\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}}(\hat a+\hat a^\dagger),\qquad
\mechanical{\hat p}=-i\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}\mechanical{\hbar}}2}(\hat a-\hat a^\dagger).
$$

Insert these relations into the Hamiltonian and define the **number operator** $\hat N=\hat a^\dagger\hat a$.

$$
\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}\left(\hat a^\dagger\hat a+\frac12\hat I\right)
=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}\left(\hat N+\frac12\hat I\right).
$$

## Spectrum of the number operator

The ladder commutator gives

$$
[\hat N,\hat a]=-\hat a,\qquad [\hat N,\hat a^\dagger]=\hat a^\dagger.
$$

If $\hat N\lvert n\rangle=n\lvert n\rangle$, then

$$
\hat N(\hat a\lvert n\rangle)=(n-1)(\hat a\lvert n\rangle),\qquad
\hat N(\hat a^\dagger\lvert n\rangle)=(n+1)(\hat a^\dagger\lvert n\rangle).
$$

Thus $\hat a$ lowers the eigenvalue by one, while $\hat a^\dagger$ raises it by one. Since $\hat N=\hat a^\dagger\hat a$ is positive semidefinite, $n=\lVert\hat a\lvert n\rangle\rVert^2\ge0$. Successive applications of $\hat a$ must end at a state $\lvert0\rangle$ with $\hat a\lvert0\rangle=0$. It follows that $\hat N\lvert0\rangle=0$. Repeated application of $\hat a^\dagger$ yields $\lvert0\rangle,\lvert1\rangle,\lvert2\rangle,\ldots$, with eigenvalues $n=0,1,2,\ldots$.

Since $\lVert\hat a\lvert n\rangle\rVert^2=n$ and $\lVert\hat a^\dagger\lvert n\rangle\rVert^2=n+1$, choose the phases such that

$$
\hat a\lvert n\rangle=\sqrt n\,\lvert n-1\rangle,\qquad
\hat a^\dagger\lvert n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,\lvert n+1\rangle.
$$

The lowering formula gives zero at $n=0$. The normalized states are

$$
\lvert n\rangle=\frac{(\hat a^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\lvert0\rangle.
$$

## Energy spectrum

Since $\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}(\hat N+\hat I/2)$, the energies are

$$
\energy E_n=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}\left(n+\frac12\right),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

Adjacent levels differ by $\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}$. The ground state has **zero-point energy** $\energy E_0=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}/2$. A state of zero energy would require both the kinetic and potential contributions to vanish. The canonical commutator prevents a state with both position and momentum sharply zero.

## Physical meaning

In the electromagnetic vacuum, changes in the field modes produce observable effects such as the Casimir force. Summing the zero-point energies $\sum_n\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}_n/2$ over all modes gives a divergent formal expression. The source cites a discrepancy of about $10^{120}$ between a high-cutoff vacuum energy estimate and the inferred cosmological energy density. The size of that estimate depends on the cutoff. Explaining the observed value is the cosmological constant problem.
