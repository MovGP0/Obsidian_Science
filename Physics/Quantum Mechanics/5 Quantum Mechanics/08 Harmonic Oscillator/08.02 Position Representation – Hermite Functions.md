---
title: "Position Representation – Hermite Functions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The ground-state equation gives a Gaussian. Applying the creation operator gives higher states as Hermite polynomials times the same Gaussian.

Let $\signal{\psi}_0(x)=\langle x\mid 0\rangle$. In position space the equation $\hat a\lvert0\rangle=0$ becomes

$$
\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}{2\mechanical{\hbar}}}
\left(x+\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}}{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\signal{\psi}_0(x)=0.
$$

This first-order equation gives the normalized Gaussian

$$
\signal{\psi}_0(x)=
\left(\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}{\pi\mechanical{\hbar}}\right)^{1/4}
\exp\left(-\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}x^2}{2\mechanical{\hbar}}\right).
$$

Define the characteristic length $x_0=\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}/(\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega})}$ and the dimensionless coordinate $\xi=x/x_0$. Expressed in $\xi$, the position-space amplitude is

$$
\signal{\psi}_0(x_0\xi)=\frac{e^{-\xi^2/2}}{\pi^{1/4}\sqrt{x_0}}.
$$

Repeated application of $\hat a^\dagger$ gives

$$
\signal{\psi}_n(x)=
\frac{H_n(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2}}{\sqrt{2^n n!}\,\pi^{1/4}\sqrt{x_0}},
$$

where $H_n$ are the **Hermite polynomials**. The first four are

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_0(\xi)&=1,\\
H_1(\xi)&=2\xi,\\
H_2(\xi)&=4\xi^2-2,\\
H_3(\xi)&=8\xi^3-12\xi.
\end{aligned}
$$

They satisfy $H_n''-2\xi H_n'+2nH_n=0$. The recurrence $H_{n+1}=2\xi H_n-2nH_{n-1}$ corresponds to the action of $\hat a^\dagger$.

**Fig. 8.1** Harmonic potential $\energy V(x)=\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}^{\,2}x^2/2$ and the first four eigenfunctions. Each amplitude is scaled for display and plotted at the energy $\energy E_n=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}(n+1/2)$. The levels are equally spaced. The ground-state Gaussian has no nodes, and $\signal{\psi}_n$ has exactly $n$ nodes. The classically forbidden regions are $\lvert\xi\rvert>\sqrt{2n+1}$. The Gaussian factor controls the asymptotic decay there.

## Properties

- The state $\signal{\psi}_n$ has $n$ nodes. The oscillator uses the index $n=0$ for the ground state, in agreement with the [[06.06 General Theorems in One Dimension|node theorem]].
- Parity is $\signal{\psi}_n(-x)=(-1)^n\signal{\psi}_n(x)$.
- Normalization gives $\int_{\mathbb R}\lvert\signal{\psi}_n(x)\rvert^2\,dx=1$.
- Orthonormality gives $\langle m\mid n\rangle=\delta_{mn}$.
