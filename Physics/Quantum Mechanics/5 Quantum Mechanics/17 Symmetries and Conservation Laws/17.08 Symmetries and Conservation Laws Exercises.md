---
title: "Symmetries and Conservation Laws Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Use symmetry operators to test conservation laws, selection rules, and degeneracy.

1. Show that $[\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}},\energy{\hat H}_0]=0$ for $\energy{\hat H}_0=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}^2/(2\mechanical m)$. Interpret the result.
2. Calculate $\hat T^\dagger(\mathbf a)\hat{\mathbf r}\hat T(\mathbf a)$ and $\hat T^\dagger(\mathbf a)\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}\hat T(\mathbf a)$.  
   **Result.** $\hat{\mathbf r}+\mathbf a$ and $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}$, respectively.
3. Verify $\hat\Pi^2=\hat I$ and show that the parity eigenvalues are $\pm1$.
4. Give the parity of the hydrogen orbitals $\lvert n\ell m\rangle$ for $n=1,2,3$.  
   **Result.** The $1s$, $2s$, $3s$, and $3d$ orbitals are even. The $2p$ and $3p$ orbitals are odd.
5. For an even potential, show that if $\signal{\psi}(-x,0)=\pm\signal{\psi}(x,0)$ then $\signal{\psi}(-x,t)=\pm\signal{\psi}(x,t)$ with the same sign for all $t$.
6. Write the $3\times3$ matrix for $\hat R_z(\pi/2)$ in the basis $\lvert\ell=1,m\rangle$ ordered by $m=-1,0,+1$.  
   **Result.** $\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\pi/2},1,e^{-i\pi/2})=\operatorname{diag}(i,1,-i)$.
7. For magnetic translations in a uniform field, show that interchanging $\hat T_{\mathbf a}$ and $\hat T_{\mathbf b}$ gives a relative phase. Relate the nonzero commutator for a nontrivial phase to the Aharonov–Bohm phase.
8. Calculate $\hat R_z^\dagger(\alpha)\mechanical{\hat L}_+\hat R_z(\alpha)$.  
   **Result.** $e^{i\alpha}\mechanical{\hat L}_+$.
9. Let $\energy{\hat H}=\energy{\hat H}_0+\energy{\hat V}$ with $[\energy{\hat H}_0,\mechanical{\hat J}_z]=0$ and $[\energy{\hat V},\mechanical{\hat J}_z]\ne0$. Show that $\mechanical{\hat J}_z$ is no longer conserved. Discuss how its expectation can stay approximately constant for a weak perturbation over a suitable time interval.
10. Show that the hydrogen ground state $\signal{\psi}_{100}$ has $\langle\mechanical{\hat L}_z\rangle=0$, $\langle\mechanical{\mathbf L}^2\rangle=0$, and even parity, with $\ell=0$.
11. Calculate the hydrogen decay-rate ratio $\Gamma(2p\to1s)/\Gamma(3p\to1s)$ using the selection rules and an estimate of $\omega^3\lvert\electric{\mathbf d}\rvert^2$.  
    **Hint.** The transition angular frequency is larger for $3p\to1s$, but its dipole matrix element has smaller magnitude.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}},\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}},\unit{kg}$ | Momentum and mass | The variables in the free-particle Hamiltonian. |
| $\hat{\mathbf r},\mathbf a,\mathbf b$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and displacements | The position operator and translation vectors. |
| $\alpha,\ell,m$ | $\unit{rad},1,1$ | Angle and orbital labels | The rotation angle, orbital angular momentum label, and projection label. |
| $\mechanical{\hat L}_+,\mechanical{\hat L}_z,\mechanical{\hat J}_z$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Angular momentum operators | The orbital raising operator and the orbital and total projections. |
| $\Gamma,\omega,\electric{\mathbf d}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}},\unit{rad\,s^{-1}},\unit{C\,m}$ | Transition quantities | The decay rate, transition angular frequency, and electric dipole matrix element. |
