title: "Symmetries and Conservation Laws"

> [!TLDR]
> Continuous symmetries give conserved generators. Discrete symmetries constrain transitions and can protect degeneracies.

In classical mechanics, Noether's theorem associates a conserved quantity with each continuous symmetry of the action. In quantum mechanics, continuous symmetries act by unitary operators. Their generators are Hermitian observables. The expectation of a generator $\hat G$ is constant when $[\hat G,\energy{\hat H}]=0$ and $\hat G$ has no explicit time dependence.

Discrete symmetries such as parity and time reversal can give selection rules and protected degeneracies, including Kramers degeneracy. This chapter collects the results for translations, rotations, parity, and time reversal in one framework.

## Chapter summary

- A unitary symmetry commutes with $\energy{\hat H}$. It gives a degeneracy if it connects linearly independent states.
- A continuous unitary symmetry has the form $\hat U=e^{-i\hat G\lambda/\mechanical{\hbar}}$. Its Hermitian generator $\hat G$ is conserved when it is time independent and commutes with the Hamiltonian.
- Momentum generates spatial translations. Total angular momentum generates rotations. The Hamiltonian generates time translations.
- Parity has eigenvalues $\pm1$. In an even potential, a nondegenerate energy eigenstate has definite parity.
- The parity of $Y_{\ell m}$ is $(-1)^\ell$. For electric dipole transitions between orbital states, the selection rule is $\Delta\ell=\pm1$.
- Time reversal is antiunitary. Kramers degeneracy occurs when $\hat\Theta^2=-\hat I$ and time reversal preserves the Hamiltonian.
- Perturbations can lift degeneracies by breaking symmetries, as in Zeeman, Stark, and spin–orbit splitting.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat U,\hat\Pi,\hat\Theta$ | $1$ | Symmetry operators | A unitary symmetry, spatial parity, and antiunitary time reversal. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The generator of spatial translations. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf J}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Total angular momentum | The generator of rotations. |
| $\energy{\hat H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The generator of time evolution for a time-independent system. |
