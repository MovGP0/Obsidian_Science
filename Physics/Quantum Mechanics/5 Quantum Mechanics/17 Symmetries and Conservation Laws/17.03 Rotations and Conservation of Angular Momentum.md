---
title: "Rotations and Conservation of Angular Momentum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Total angular momentum generates rotations. Rotational invariance conserves angular momentum and constrains matrix elements through angular-momentum selection rules.

A rotation by angle $\alpha$ about the axis $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is

$$
\hat R_{\hat{\mathbf n}}(\alpha)=e^{-i\alpha\mechanical{\mathbf J}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}/\mechanical{\hbar}},
$$

where $\mechanical{\mathbf J}=\mechanical{\mathbf L}+\mechanical{\mathbf S}$ for a particle with spin. A central potential $\energy V(r)$ is invariant under spatial rotations. Then $[\mechanical{\mathbf J},\energy{\hat H}]=0$, and the energy eigenstates can be labeled by eigenvalues of $\mechanical{\mathbf J}^2$ and $\mechanical{\hat J}_z$, specified by $j,m_j$.

## Selection rules

For a rank-$k$ tensor operator $\hat T^{(k)}$, the Wigner–Eckart theorem factors the matrix element into an angular coefficient and a reduced matrix element, with normalization absorbed into the latter.

$$
\langle j'm'\rvert\hat T_q^{(k)}\lvert jm\rangle
=\langle j'm'\mid jm;kq\rangle
\,\langle j'\rVert\hat T^{(k)}\lVert j\rangle.
$$

The Clebsch–Gordan coefficient imposes $\lvert j-k\rvert\le j'\le j+k$ and $m'=m+q$. For an electric dipole, the position operator is a vector with $k=1$. The angular conditions are $\Delta j=0,\pm1$ with $j=0\to j'=0$ forbidden, and $\Delta m=0,\pm1$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\alpha,\hat{\mathbf n}$ | $\unit{rad},1$ | Rotation angle and axis | The angle and the unit vector that specify the rotation. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf J},\mechanical{\mathbf L},\mechanical{\mathbf S}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Angular momenta | The total, orbital, and spin angular momentum operators. |
| $j,m_j,k,q$ | $1$ | Angular momentum labels | The state labels and the rank and component of a tensor operator. |
