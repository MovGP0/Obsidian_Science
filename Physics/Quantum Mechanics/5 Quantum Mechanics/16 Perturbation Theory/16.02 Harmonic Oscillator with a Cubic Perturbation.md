---
title: "Harmonic Oscillator with a Cubic Perturbation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A cubic perturbation has zero first-order energy shift because it is odd under parity. At second order, only oscillator states with occupation changes of $\pm1$ or $\pm3$ contribute.

For the harmonic oscillator, let $\energy{\hat H}_0=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega(\hat a^\dagger\hat a+1/2)$ and take the perturbation to be $\lambda\hat x^3$. Since $\hat x\propto\hat a+\hat a^\dagger$, each cubic monomial changes the occupation number by $\pm1$ or $\pm3$. Equivalently, the oscillator states are parity eigenstates and $\hat x^3$ is odd. Therefore $\langle n\rvert\hat x^3\lvert n\rangle=0$ and the first-order shift vanishes.

The first contribution is the second-order shift.

$$
\Delta\energy{E}_n^{(2)}
=\lambda^2\sum_{k\ne n}
\frac{\lvert\langle k\rvert\hat x^3\lvert n\rangle\rvert^2}{\energy{E}_n^{(0)}-\energy{E}_k^{(0)}}
=-\frac{\lambda^2}{\mechanical{\hbar}\omega}
\left(\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}}{2\mechanical{m}\omega}\right)^3(30n^2+30n+11).
$$

Only $k-n=\pm1,\pm3$ contribute.

> [!note] Expansion convention
> The source includes $\lambda^2$ in its notation for the second-order correction here. The preceding section separates this factor from the coefficient.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m},\omega$ | $\unit{kg},\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Mass and angular frequency | The parameters of the unperturbed oscillator. |
| $\hat x,\lambda$ | $\unit{m},\unit{J\,m^{-3}}$ | Position operator and cubic coefficient | The product $\lambda\hat x^3$ has units of energy. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Sets the oscillator energy scale with $\omega$. |
| $\Delta\energy{E}_n^{(2)}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Second-order shift | The energy shift including the factor $\lambda^2$. |
