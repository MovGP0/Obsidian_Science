## Choose a formula by the task

For a wave packet, start with complex conjugation before forming a probability density. Use Gaussian integrals to normalize a Gaussian packet, and then use the Fourier convention to move between its coordinate and wave-number forms. Parseval's relation checks that both forms give the same total probability.

For a finite-state system, use the matrix identities to change basis or simplify an expectation value. Use the Pauli formulas only for a two-dimensional state space; use oscillator ladder operators for the number-state basis of a quadratic oscillator. Their operator algebras describe different systems and are not interchangeable.

For a state on a finite interval, the trigonometric identities help check mode normalization and orthogonality. Fourier series describe periodic mode expansions; the Fourier transform describes a continuous wave-number variable. Check the interval and boundary conditions before choosing between them.

The constants page supplies rounded SI values and atomic scales. It does not determine the accuracy of a model. Before substituting numbers, check dimensions and one simple case: a normalized state must still have unit norm after a unitary change of representation.
