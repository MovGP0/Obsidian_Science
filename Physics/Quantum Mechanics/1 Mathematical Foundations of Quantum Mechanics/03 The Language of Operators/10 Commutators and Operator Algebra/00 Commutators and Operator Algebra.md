> [!TLDR]
> A commutator records how the order of two operators changes their product. Commutator algebra connects compatibility, uncertainty, conservation laws, and quantum energy spectra.

Multiplication of numbers is commutative: $ab=ba$. Matrix multiplication generally is not: $AB\ne BA$.

In quantum mechanics, noncommutativity is central to the uncertainty principle, energy quantization, and selection rules for optical transitions. The **commutator** describes the failure of two operators to commute.

> [!note]
> The source describes noncommutativity as the mechanism that produces all these effects. The commutator algebra is essential, but a specific spectrum or selection rule also depends on the Hamiltonian, its representation, and the physical system. The commutator alone does not determine every such result.
