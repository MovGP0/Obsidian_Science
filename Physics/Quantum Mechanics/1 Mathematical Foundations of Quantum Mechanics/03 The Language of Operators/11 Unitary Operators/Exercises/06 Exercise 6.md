> [!TLDR]
> The specified translation moves the Gaussian at constant speed and preserves its width.

Chapter: [[11 Unitary Operators/00 Unitary Operators|Chapter 11 — Unitary Operators]]. Exercise 6. Difficulty: **Intermediate level**.

Back to [[11 Unitary Operators/08 Exercises#Exercise 6|Chapter 11 exercise index]].

## Question

A particle initially has the Gaussian wavefunction

$$
\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x},0)=\signal{A}e^{-\mechanical{x}^2/(4\mechanical{\sigma}^2)},
$$

centered at $\mechanical{x}=0$. For $\signal{t}>0$, a translation acts according to

$$
|\psi(\signal{t})\rangle=\hat T(\mechanical{v}\signal{t})|\psi(0)\rangle.
$$

Calculate the wavefunction $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x},\signal{t})=\langle\mechanical{x}|\psi(\signal{t})\rangle$. Describe the physical state.

**Hint:** This is a rigid translation, generated by $\hat H=\mechanical{v}\hat p$. It differs from free-particle Schrödinger evolution, which also spreads the packet.

## Worked solution

The translation rule is $\langle\mechanical{x}|\hat T(\mechanical{a})|\psi\rangle=\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{a})$. Set the displacement equal to $\mechanical{v}\signal{t}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x},\signal{t})
&=\langle\mechanical{x}|\hat T(\mechanical{v}\signal{t})|\psi(0)\rangle\\
&=\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}\signal{t},0)
=\signal{A}\exp\left[-\frac{(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}\signal{t})^2}{4\mechanical{\sigma}^2}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The probability density is

$$
|\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x},\signal{t})|^2
=|\signal{A}|^2\exp\left[-\frac{(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}\signal{t})^2}{2\mechanical{\sigma}^2}\right].
$$

For a normalized state, the Gaussian integral gives $|\signal{A}|^2\sqrt{2\pi}\mechanical{\sigma}=1$. Thus $|\signal{A}|=(2\pi\mechanical{\sigma}^2)^{-1/4}$; an arbitrary constant overall phase is allowed. With the variable change $u=\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}\signal{t}$, the density is the same even Gaussian as at the start. Its mean and variance are

$$
\mechanical{\langle x\rangle}=\mechanical{v}\signal{t},
\qquad
\mechanical{(\Delta x)^2}=\mechanical{\sigma}^2.
$$

The center moves with the imposed velocity and the width stays fixed. To identify the dynamics, differentiate the translated profile:

$$
\partial_{\signal{t}}\signal{\psi}=-\mechanical{v}\partial_{\mechanical{x}}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_{\signal{t}}\signal{\psi}
=\mechanical{v}(-i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_{\mechanical{x}})\signal{\psi}
=\mechanical{v}\hat p\signal{\psi}.
$$

Therefore the specified evolution is generated by $\hat H=\mechanical{v}\hat p$. It is not free-particle evolution under $\hat H_{\mathrm{free}}=\hat p^2/(2\mechanical{m})$. For the unchirped initial Gaussian in the question, free evolution gives the position width

$$
\mechanical{\sigma_{\mathrm{free}}}(\signal{t})
=\mechanical{\sigma}\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}\signal{t}}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\sigma}^2}\right)^2}.
$$

The distinction is physical: a translation alone does not add the momentum phase of a Galilean boost. For the given real Gaussian up to a constant phase, the mean momentum stays zero, even though the density center moves under the linear-momentum Hamiltonian. Its velocity operator is $(i/\mechanical{\hbar})[\mechanical{v}\hat p,\hat x]=\mechanical{v}\hat I$.

## Answer

$$
\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x},\signal{t})
=\signal{A}e^{-(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}\signal{t})^2/(4\mechanical{\sigma}^2)}.
$$

The Gaussian center is at $\mechanical{v}\signal{t}$ and its standard deviation stays $\mechanical{\sigma}$. This is rigid translation under $\hat H=\mechanical{v}\hat p$; a freely evolving Gaussian also spreads.
