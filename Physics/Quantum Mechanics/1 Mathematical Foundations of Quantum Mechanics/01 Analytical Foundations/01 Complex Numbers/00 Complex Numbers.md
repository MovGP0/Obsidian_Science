> [!TLDR]
> Complex numbers extend real arithmetic with an imaginary unit whose square is −1. They provide a basic mathematical structure for quantum mechanics.

Let us start with a difficult question: What is $\sqrt{-1}$?

The honest answer is: nothing, if we stay within the real numbers. The function $f(x)=x^2$ is always nonnegative. Thus, no real number has a square equal to $-1$.

But mathematicians in the sixteenth century faced a difficult situation. They worked with cubic equations of the form

$$
x^3+px+q=0.
$$

Cardano's solution formula produced square roots of negative numbers during the calculation. The strange feature was that these roots *disappeared* by the end of the calculation. The final result was a valid real number. The roots were an intermediate tool, like scaffolding that is removed after a wall is built.

Rafael Bombelli was the first to take these objects seriously, in 1572. He did not ask whether they existed. He asked which algebraic rules they obeyed. He found that everything worked if those rules were applied consistently.

Three centuries later, quantum mechanics would show that these unusual objects were not temporary scaffolding. They form a permanent structure of physical reality.
