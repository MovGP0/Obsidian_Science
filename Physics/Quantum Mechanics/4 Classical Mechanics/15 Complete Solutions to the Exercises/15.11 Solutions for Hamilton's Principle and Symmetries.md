---
title: "Solutions for Hamilton's Principle and Symmetries"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions derive conservation laws from translations, rotations, and other symmetries of the action.

See [[11.15 Hamilton's Principle and Symmetries Exercises]] for the exercise list.

## Exercise 1. Translation of a free particle

*Show that $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}^{\,2}$ has symmetry $\mechanical{\vec r}\to\mechanical{\vec r}+\varepsilon\hat{\imath}$.*

The Lagrangian is independent of $\mechanical{x}$, so the translation leaves it invariant. The conserved charge is $Q=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot x}=\mechanical{p}_x$.

## Exercise 2. Rotations and axial angular momentum

*Show that $\mechanical{L}_z$ is conserved in a central potential.*

An infinitesimal rotation about $\hat k$ gives $\delta\mechanical{\vec r}=\varepsilon(-\mechanical{y},\mechanical{x},0)$. The conserved charge is $Q=\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{x}\mechanical{\dot y}-\mechanical{y}\mechanical{\dot x})=\mechanical{L}_z$.

## Exercise 3. Energy from time invariance

*Show energy conservation for a Lagrangian with no explicit time dependence.*

From $\partial\energy{L}/\partial t=0$ and the Euler–Lagrange equation, $d\energy{E}/dt=0$, with $\energy{E}=\dot q\,\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q-\energy{L}$ for one coordinate.

## Exercise 4. Addition of a total time derivative

*Show that $\energy{L}'=\energy{L}+df/dt$ gives the same equations of motion.*

The variation is $\delta\int\energy{L}'\,dt=\delta\int\energy{L}\,dt+\delta[f]$. With fixed endpoints, $\delta f$ vanishes at both ends, so the equations are the same.

## Exercise 5. Conserved quantities for central motion

*Find the three components of angular momentum.*

A central potential $\energy{U}(\lvert\mechanical{\vec r}\rvert)$ is invariant under rotations about every axis. All three components of $\mechanical{\vec L}$ are conserved.

## Exercise 6. Galilean boost

*Find the conserved charge $\mechanical{\vec G}$.*

Under $\mechanical{\vec r}\to\mechanical{\vec r}+\varepsilon\mechanical{\vec V}t$, the first-order change is $\delta\energy{L}=\varepsilon\,d(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}\cdot\mechanical{\vec V})/dt$. An equivalent choice of sign for the conserved vector gives $\mechanical{\vec G}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}-\mechanical{\vec P}t$.

## Exercise 7. Two-body symmetries

*Show conservation of total linear and angular momentum.*

The potential $\energy{U}(\lvert\mechanical{\vec r}_1-\mechanical{\vec r}_2\rvert)$ is invariant under simultaneous translations and rotations. The kinetic energy shares these symmetries, giving conservation of $\mechanical{\vec P}$ and $\mechanical{\vec L}$, respectively.

## Exercise 8. Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector

*Give the conserved vector for $\energy{U}=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}$.*

The vector is $\mechanical{\vec A}=\mechanical{\vec p}\times\mechanical{\vec L}-\mechanical{\mu}\alpha\hat r$. Verify $d\mechanical{\vec A}/dt=0$ using $\mechanical{\dot{\vec p}}=-\alpha\hat r/\mechanical{r}^2$, $d\hat r/dt=\dot\phi\hat\phi$, and constant $\mechanical{\vec L}$.

## Exercise 9. General form of Noether's theorem

*Prove conservation for an infinitesimal symmetry.*

If $q_k\to q_k+\varepsilon\eta_k$ and $\delta\energy{L}=\varepsilon\,df/dt$, the Euler–Lagrange equations give $\delta\energy{L}=\varepsilon\,d(\sum_k p_k\eta_k)/dt$ along a solution. Hence $dQ/dt=0$ for $Q=\sum_k p_k\eta_k-f$.

## Exercise 10. Relativistic particle

*Use $\energy{L}=-\mechanical{m}c^2\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/c^2}$.*

The conjugate momentum is $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}/\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/c^2}=\mechanical{\vec p}$. A free particle has $d\mechanical{\vec p}/dt=0$ by the Euler–Lagrange equations.

## Exercise 11. Rotating rail and the Jacobi function

*Calculate the Jacobi function $\energy{h}$ for a bead on the rail.*

For $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}+\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\omega}^2)$,

$$
\energy{h}=\mechanical{\dot r}\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{\dot r}}-\energy{L}
=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}-\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\omega}^2).
$$

It is constant because $\partial\energy{L}/\partial t=0$. In general it differs from the kinetic energy $\energy{T}$.

## Exercise 12. Galilean invariance and the free-particle Lagrangian

*Explain why the usual free-particle Lagrangian is quadratic in velocity.*

Under a Galilean boost, $\energy{L}(\mechanical{\vec v}+\mechanical{\vec V})-\energy{L}(\mechanical{\vec v})$ must be a total derivative of a function of position and time. For a free particle, the difference therefore has at most linear dependence on $\mechanical{\vec v}$ for arbitrary boosts. Differentiation with respect to the boost gives a velocity derivative of $\energy{L}$ that is affine in $\mechanical{\vec v}$. With spatial isotropy and up to a total derivative and a constant, this gives $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2$.

## Exercise 13. Conserved quantities in uniform gravity

*Use $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}$.*

The Lagrangian has no explicit time dependence, so $\energy{E}$ is conserved. It is independent of $\mechanical{x}$ and $\mechanical{y}$, so $\mechanical{p}_x$ and $\mechanical{p}_y$ are conserved. Rotation about $z$ is also a symmetry, so $\mechanical{L}_z$ is conserved.
