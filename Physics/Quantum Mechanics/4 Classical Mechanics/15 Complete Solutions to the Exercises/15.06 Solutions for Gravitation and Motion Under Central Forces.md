> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions use Newtonian gravity, the Kepler laws, and conservation of energy and angular momentum.

See [[06.16 Gravitation and Orbits Exercises]] for the exercise list.

## Exercise 1. Orbital binding energy

*Use orbital radius $\mechanical{r}=\quantity{7\times10^6}{m}$, central mass $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{5.97\times10^{24}}{kg}$, and satellite mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{1000}{kg}$.*

For a circular orbit, $\energy{E}=-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}/(2\mechanical{r})$. Substitution gives $\energy{E}\simeq\quantity{-2.846\times10^{10}}{J}$.

## Exercise 2. Escape speed from the Moon

*Use lunar mass $\mechanical{M}_L=\quantity{7.35\times10^{22}}{kg}$ and radius $\mechanical{R}_L=\quantity{1.74\times10^6}{m}$.*

The escape speed is $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}_L/\mechanical{R}_L}\simeq\quantity{2.375}{km\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 3. Orbital period of Mars

*The semimajor axis is $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{1.524}{au}$. Find the orbital period.*

Relative to Earth's orbit, Kepler's third law gives $\signal{T}=(\mechanical{a}/\quantity{1}{au})^{3/2}\,\unit{yr}\simeq\quantity{1.881}{yr}$.

## Exercise 4. Earth–Moon reduced mass

*Use $\mechanical{m}_T\simeq\quantity{5.97\times10^{24}}{kg}$ and $\mechanical{m}_L\simeq\quantity{7.35\times10^{22}}{kg}$.*

The reduced mass is $\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical{m}_T\mechanical{m}_L/(\mechanical{m}_T+\mechanical{m}_L)\simeq\quantity{7.26\times10^{22}}{kg}$. It is about $0.988\mechanical{m}_L$.

## Exercise 5. Escape speed and circular speed

*Prove $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt2\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{circ}}$ at the same radius.*

The circular speed is $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{circ}}=\sqrt{\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}$. The escape speed is $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}$.

## Exercise 6. Extreme distances of an ellipse

*Use $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{2\times10^{11}}{m}$ and eccentricity $e=0.5$.*

The farthest distance is $\mechanical{r}_{\max}=\mechanical{a}(1+e)=\quantity{3\times10^{11}}{m}$. The nearest is $\mechanical{r}_{\min}=\mechanical{a}(1-e)=\quantity{10^{11}}{m}$.

## Exercise 7. Virial theorem for a circular orbit

*Verify the virial relation.*

For $\mechanical{v}^2=\alpha/(\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r})$, the kinetic energy is $\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}^2=\alpha/(2\mechanical{r})$. Since $\energy{U}=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}$, one has $2\energy{K}=-\energy{U}$.

## Exercise 8. Geostationary orbit

*Use the sidereal period $\signal{T}=\quantity{86164}{s}$.*

The radius is $\mechanical{r}=[\mechanical{G}\mechanical{m}_T\signal{T}^2/(4\pi^2)]^{1/3}\simeq\quantity{4.22\times10^7}{m}$. The orbital speed is $\mechanical{v}=2\pi\mechanical{r}/\signal{T}\simeq\quantity{3.07}{km\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 9. Bertrand's theorem

*Show that bound nonradial orbits in $\energy{U}=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}$ close.*

For $\energy{E}<0$, the orbit equation is $\mechanical{r}(\phi)=\mechanical{p}/(1+e\cos\phi)$ with $0\le e<1$, an ellipse. The radius is periodic in $\phi$ with period $2\pi$, so the orbit closes after one revolution. Bertrand's theorem (1873) identifies two attractive central potentials for which all bound orbits close, the inverse-distance and isotropic harmonic potentials. The general proof analyzes the apsidal angle and the radial and angular frequencies.

## Exercise 10. Rutherford scattering

*Find the magnitude of the scattering angle $\theta$ as a function of impact parameter $\mechanical{b}$ and incoming speed $\mechanical{v}_\infty$.*

Angular momentum and energy are $\mechanical{L}=\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}_\infty\mechanical{b}$ and $\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}_\infty^2$. The orbit is hyperbolic. For positive interaction strength $\alpha$ the Rutherford formula gives

$$
\tan(\theta/2)=\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{L}\mechanical{v}_\infty}
=\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}_\infty^2\mechanical{b}}.
$$

## Exercise 11. Earth–Mars Hohmann transfer

*Calculate the two speed changes and the transfer time.*

The orbit radii are $\mechanical{r}_1=\quantity{1}{au}$ and $\mechanical{r}_2=\quantity{1.524}{au}$. The transfer ellipse has $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{1.262}{au}$. The circular speeds are $\mechanical{v}_1=\quantity{29.78}{km\,s^{-1}}$ and $\mechanical{v}_2=\quantity{24.13}{km\,s^{-1}}$. The vis-viva equation gives departure speed $\mechanical{v}_p\simeq\quantity{32.72}{km\,s^{-1}}$ on the transfer ellipse. The speed changes are $\Delta\mechanical{v}_1\simeq\quantity{2.94}{km\,s^{-1}}$ and $\Delta\mechanical{v}_2\simeq\quantity{2.65}{km\,s^{-1}}$. The transfer takes half an orbital period, $t_H\simeq\quantity{0.709}{yr}$, or about $8.5$ months.

## Exercise 12. Roche limit

*Estimate the rigid-body Roche limit for a moon of density $\mechanical{\rho}_l=\quantity{3000}{kg\,m^{-3}}$ around Saturn.*

The source uses planetary radius $\mechanical{R}_p=\quantity{6\times10^7}{m}$ and density ratio $2\mechanical{\rho}_p/\mechanical{\rho}_l=1400/3000$. The approximate center-to-center limit is $\mechanical{d}_R=\mechanical{R}_p(2\mechanical{\rho}_p/\mechanical{\rho}_l)^{1/3}\simeq\quantity{4.66\times10^7}{m}$. This estimate lies inside the assumed planetary radius.

## Exercise 13. Long-period comet

*Use semimajor axis $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{100}{au}$ and eccentricity $e=0.99$.*

The extreme radii are $\mechanical{r}_{\min}=\mechanical{a}(1-e)=\quantity{1}{au}$ and $\mechanical{r}_{\max}=\mechanical{a}(1+e)=\quantity{199}{au}$. Kepler's third law gives $\signal{T}=\quantity{1000}{yr}$.

## Exercise 14. Gravitational acceleration at 1000 km

*Calculate the gravitational acceleration at altitude $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{1000}{km}$.*

Use the inverse-square law with surface gravity $\mechanical{g}_0$ and Earth radius $\mechanical{R}_T$.

$$
\mechanical{g}(\mechanical{h})=\mechanical{g}_0\frac{\mechanical{R}_T^2}{(\mechanical{R}_T+\mechanical{h})^2}
=9.81\left(\frac{6371}{7371}\right)^2\,\unit{m\,s^{-2}}
\simeq\quantity{7.33}{m\,s^{-2}}.
$$

## Exercise 15. Speed at perihelion and aphelion

*Prove the formulas for the two speeds.*

Conservation of energy gives the vis-viva equation $\mechanical{v}^2=\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}(2/\mechanical{r}-1/\mechanical{a})$. At the apsides, angular momentum conservation gives $\mechanical{r}_p\mechanical{v}_p=\mechanical{r}_a\mechanical{v}_a$. Insert $\mechanical{r}_p=\mechanical{a}(1-e)$ and $\mechanical{r}_a=\mechanical{a}(1+e)$ into vis-viva.

$$
\mechanical{v}_p^2=\frac{\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{a}}\frac{1+e}{1-e},
\qquad
\mechanical{v}_a^2=\frac{\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{a}}\frac{1-e}{1+e}.
$$
