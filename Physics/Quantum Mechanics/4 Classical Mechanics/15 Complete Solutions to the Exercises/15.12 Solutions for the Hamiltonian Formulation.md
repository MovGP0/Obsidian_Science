> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions use Hamilton's equations, Poisson brackets, and Hamiltonian phase-space flow.

See [[12.14 Hamiltonian Formulation Exercises]] for the exercise list.

## Exercise 1. Hamiltonian of the pendulum

*Derive the Hamiltonian and its equations.*

The conjugate momentum is $p_\theta=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\dot\theta$. Therefore $\energy{H}=p_\theta^2/(2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2)-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\theta$. Hamilton's equations are $\dot\theta=p_\theta/(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2)$ and $\dot p_\theta=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\sin\theta$.

## Exercise 2. Harmonic oscillator

*Apply Hamilton's equations.*

The equations are $\dot q=p/\mechanical{m}$ and $\dot p=-\mechanical{k}q$. Thus $\ddot q=-\mechanical{k}q/\mechanical{m}=-\signal{\omega}^2q$.

## Exercise 3. Bracket of angular momentum components

*Calculate $\{\mechanical{L}_x,\mechanical{L}_y\}$.*

The fundamental brackets give $\{\mechanical{L}_x,\mechanical{L}_y\}=\mechanical{x}\mechanical{p}_y-\mechanical{y}\mechanical{p}_x=\mechanical{L}_z$.

## Exercise 4. Bracket of a coordinate and squared momentum

*Calculate $\{q,p^2\}$.*

The definition gives $\{q,p^2\}=1\cdot2p-0=2p$.

## Exercise 5. Angular momentum squared in a central potential

*Show that $\mechanical{L}^2$ is conserved.*

Each component has $\{\mechanical{L}_i,\energy{H}\}=0$. The product rule gives $\{\mechanical{L}^2,\energy{H}\}=2\sum_i\mechanical{L}_i\{\mechanical{L}_i,\energy{H}\}=0$.

## Exercise 6. Free particle in three dimensions

*Find the conserved quantities from the usual space and time symmetries.*

The Hamiltonian is $\energy{H}=\mechanical{\vec p}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical{m})$. The position coordinates are cyclic, so $\mechanical{\vec p}$ is conserved. Rotation symmetry conserves $\mechanical{\vec L}$, and time translation symmetry conserves $\energy{E}$.

## Exercise 7. Liouville's theorem for the oscillator

*Find the area enclosed by a phase-space ellipse.*

The ellipse at energy $\energy{E}$ has semiaxes $\sqrt{2\energy{E}/\mechanical{k}}$ and $\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\energy{E}}$. Its area is $\pi\sqrt{2\energy{E}/\mechanical{k}}\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\energy{E}}=2\pi\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}$. The energy is constant, so the ellipse and its area are invariant.

## Exercise 8. Cyclic coordinates of the double pendulum

The ordinary pendulum angles are both noncyclic.

## Exercise 9. Jacobi identity

*Prove the identity by direct expansion.*

Expand the three nested brackets. Terms containing second derivatives of each function cancel in pairs by equality of mixed partial derivatives and antisymmetry of the Poisson bracket. The sum is zero.

## Exercise 10. Poisson's theorem

*Show that if $f$ and $g$ are conserved, then $\{f,g\}$ is conserved.*

The Jacobi identity gives $\{\{f,g\},\energy{H}\}=\{\{f,\energy{H}\},g\}+\{f,\{g,\energy{H}\}\}$. If both brackets with $\energy{H}$ vanish and $f,g$ are conserved, then $\partial f/\partial t=\partial g/\partial t=0$. Thus $\{f,g\}$ is conserved. More generally, the full time derivative obeys $d\{f,g\}/dt=\{df/dt,g\}+\{f,dg/dt\}$, so the result also holds with explicit time dependence.

## Exercise 11. Electromagnetic Hamiltonian

*Use $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2-\electric{q}\electric{\Phi}+\electric{q}\mechanical{\vec v}\cdot\magnetic{\vec A}$.*

The canonical momentum is $\mechanical{\vec p}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}+\electric{q}\magnetic{\vec A}$. The kinetic momentum is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\vec p}-\electric{q}\magnetic{\vec A}$. Thus

$$
\energy{H}=\frac{\lvert\mechanical{\vec p}-\electric{q}\magnetic{\vec A}\rvert^2}{2\mechanical{m}}+\electric{q}\electric{\Phi}.
$$

## Exercise 12. Superintegrable two-dimensional oscillator

*Find the conserved quantities.*

Three generically independent integrals are $\energy{H}$, $\mechanical{L}_z$, and $\energy{A}_{xy}=\mechanical{p}_x\mechanical{p}_y/\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}\mechanical{y}$. Both $\{\energy{H},\mechanical{L}_z\}$ and $\{\energy{H},\energy{A}_{xy}\}$ are zero. The third independent integral gives superintegrability.

## Exercise 13. Oscillator flow as a rotation

*Prove the rotation property.*

Let $u=p/(\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega})$. The energy is proportional to $q^2+u^2$. Hamilton's equations become $\dot q=\signal{\omega}u$ and $\dot u=-\signal{\omega}q$, which give a clockwise rotation at angular speed $\signal{\omega}$ in the $(q,u)$ plane.

## Exercise 14. Bracket of the Hamiltonian with itself

*Show $\{\energy{H},\energy{H}\}=0$.*

In each term of the defining sum, $(\partial\energy{H}/\partial q_k)(\partial\energy{H}/\partial p_k)$ cancels $(\partial\energy{H}/\partial p_k)(\partial\energy{H}/\partial q_k)$. The bracket is zero.
