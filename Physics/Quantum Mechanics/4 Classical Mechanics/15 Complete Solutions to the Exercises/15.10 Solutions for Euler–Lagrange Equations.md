> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions construct Lagrangians and derive the corresponding equations of motion.

See [[10.17 Euler–Lagrange Exercises]] for the exercise list.

## Exercise 1. Simple pendulum

*Find the Lagrangian and equation of motion.*

Use $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\dot\theta^2$ and $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\theta$ in $\energy{L}=\energy{T}-\energy{U}$. The Euler–Lagrange equation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\ddot\theta+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\sin\theta=0$, or $\ddot\theta+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})\sin\theta=0$.

## Exercise 2. Free particle

*Find the Lagrangian and equations of motion.*

For $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot x}^{\,2}+\mechanical{\dot y}^{\,2}+\mechanical{\dot z}^{\,2})$, each Cartesian coordinate is cyclic. Thus $\mechanical{\ddot x}=\mechanical{\ddot y}=\mechanical{\ddot z}=0$ and $\mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}=0$.

## Exercise 3. Pendulum with prescribed rotation

*Find the Lagrangian.*

Use $\theta$ as the free coordinate and prescribe $\phi(t)=\mechanical{\omega}t$. Then $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2(\dot\theta^2+\mechanical{\omega}^2\sin^2\theta)$ and $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\theta$.

## Exercise 4. Vertical mass and spring

*Use vertical coordinate $\mechanical{z}$, stiffness $\mechanical{k}$, and gravity.*

The Lagrangian is $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot z}^{\,2}-\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{z}^2-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}$. The Euler–Lagrange equation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot z}+\mechanical{k}\mechanical{z}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}=0$, or $\mechanical{\ddot z}+\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{z}=-\mechanical{g}$ with $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$.

## Exercise 5. Cyclic coordinates of a double pendulum

The Lagrangian depends explicitly on both $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$. Neither coordinate is cyclic.

## Exercise 6. Particle on a cylinder

*Find the Lagrangian and equations.*

For radius $\mechanical{R}$, $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{R}^2\dot\phi^2+\mechanical{\dot z}^{\,2})$ and $\energy{U}=0$. The equations are $\ddot\phi=0$ and $\mechanical{\ddot z}=0$. Motion with both rates nonzero is a uniform helix.

## Exercise 7. Particle on a helicoid at fixed radius

*Set $\mechanical{z}=c\phi$ and fix $\mechanical{\rho}$.*

Then $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\rho}^2+c^2)\dot\phi^2$ and $\energy{U}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}c\phi$. Thus $\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\rho}^2+c^2)\ddot\phi=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}c$. The angular coordinate has constant acceleration $\ddot\phi=-\mechanical{g}c/(\mechanical{\rho}^2+c^2)$.

## Exercise 8. Central motion

*Show that $\phi$ is cyclic and $p_\phi=\mechanical{L}_z$ is constant.*

Since $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\phi=0$, $p_\phi=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2\dot\phi=\mechanical{L}_z$ is conserved.

## Exercise 9. Lagrange top

*Use Euler angles.*

$$
\energy{L}=\frac{\mechanical{I}_1}{2}(\dot\phi^2\sin^2\theta+\dot\theta^2)
+\frac{\mechanical{I}_3}{2}(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)^2
-\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}\cos\theta.
$$

The coordinates $\phi,\psi$ are cyclic, so $p_\phi$ and $p_\psi$ are constant.

## Exercise 10. Rotating hoop

*A bead moves on a vertical hoop rotating at angular speed $\mechanical{\omega}$ about its vertical diameter.*

For hoop radius $\mechanical{R}$, use $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{R}^2(\dot\theta^2+\mechanical{\omega}^2\sin^2\theta)$ and $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}\cos\theta$. Then $\ddot\theta+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{R})\sin\theta-\mechanical{\omega}^2\sin\theta\cos\theta=0$. The equilibrium at $\theta=0$ is stable for $\mechanical{\omega}^2<\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{R}$ and unstable above that threshold. The equilibrium at $\theta=\pi$ is always unstable. Above the threshold, two new stable equilibria satisfy $\cos\theta=\mechanical{g}/(\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\omega}^2)$.

## Exercise 11. Two masses with a spring

*Separate center-of-mass and relative motion.*

For equal masses $\mechanical{m}$, the total and reduced masses are $\mechanical{M}=2\mechanical{m}$ and $\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical{m}/2$. The center of mass obeys $\mechanical{M}\mechanical{\ddot R}=0$ and moves uniformly. The relative displacement obeys $\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{\ddot r}=-\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}$, giving $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$.

## Exercise 12. Damped oscillator

*Find the Lagrangian and Rayleigh dissipation function.*

Use $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot x}^{\,2}-\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}^2$ and $\mathcal R=\mechanical{m}\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{\dot x}^{\,2}$, with $\mechanical{b}=2\mechanical{m}\signal{\gamma}$. The Euler–Lagrange equation with dissipation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot x}+2\mechanical{m}\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{\dot x}+\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}=0$.

## Exercise 13. Disk rolling down an incline

*Find the Lagrangian.*

Use the rolling constraint $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{R}\theta$ with $\mechanical{x}$ positive down the slope. Then $\energy{K}=\tfrac12(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2+\mechanical{I})\dot\theta^2$ and $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}\theta\sin\alpha$. The equation is $\ddot\theta=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}\sin\alpha/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2+\mechanical{I})$.

## Exercise 14. Pendulum of varying length

*Find the Lagrangian.*

The kinetic energy is $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot\ell}^{\,2}+\mechanical{\ell}^2\dot\theta^2)$ and the potential is $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\theta$. The constraint is rheonomic because $\mechanical{\ell}$ is a prescribed function of time.
