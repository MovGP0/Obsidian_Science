---
title: "Solutions for Canonical Transformations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions use generating functions, canonical rotations, action variables, and the Hamilton–Jacobi equation.

See [[13.15 Canonical Transformations and Hamilton–Jacobi Exercises]] for the exercise list.

## Exercise 1. Canonical exchange

*Test $Q=p$, $P=-q$.*

The bracket is $\{Q,P\}=\{p,-q\}=1$, so the transformation is canonical.

## Exercise 2. Type 2 generating function

*Use $F_2=qP+\varepsilon q^2P$.*

Then $p=\partial F_2/\partial q=(1+2\varepsilon q)P$ and $Q=\partial F_2/\partial P=q+\varepsilon q^2$. Where the transformation is invertible, it is canonical. Since $F_2$ has no time dependence, $\energy{K}$ is $\energy{H}$ expressed in the new variables.

## Exercise 3. Identity from $F_2=qP$

The derivatives give $p=P$ and $Q=q$, the identity transformation.

## Exercise 4. Action of the oscillator

*Show $J=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p\,dq=\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}$.*

The phase-space ellipse encloses area $2\pi\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}$. Divide by $2\pi$ to obtain $J=\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}$.

## Exercise 5. Hamilton–Jacobi equation for a free particle

*Find $\energy{S}$ explicitly.*

The separated equation is $(\energy{W}'(q))^2=2\mechanical{m}\energy{E}$. Choose the positive momentum branch $P=\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\energy{E}}$, so $\energy{W}=qP$ and $\energy{S}=qP-\energy{E}t$. Then $\beta=\partial\energy{S}/\partial P=q-Pt/\mechanical{m}$. Thus $q(t)=\beta+t\sqrt{2\energy{E}/\mechanical{m}}$, a uniform motion. The negative branch gives the opposite velocity.

## Exercise 6. Canonical rotation

*Test $Q=q\cos\alpha+p\sin\alpha$ and $P=-q\sin\alpha+p\cos\alpha$ in scaled variables.*

The fundamental bracket is $\{Q,P\}=\cos^2\alpha+\sin^2\alpha=1$, so the rotation is canonical.

## Exercise 7. Isochronism in action–angle variables

For $\energy{H}=J\signal{\omega}$ with constant $\signal{\omega}$, the angle rate is $\dot\theta=\partial\energy{H}/\partial J=\signal{\omega}$. The period $2\pi/\signal{\omega}$ is independent of $J$.

## Exercise 8. Nonlinear pendulum

*Find $J(\energy{E})$ at small energy measured from equilibrium.*

The harmonic approximation gives $J=\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}_0+O(\energy{E}^2)$. The higher-order terms give the amplitude dependence of the period.

## Exercise 9. Kepler Hamilton–Jacobi equation

*Separate the equation in spherical coordinates.*

Use $\energy{S}=\energy{W}_r(\mechanical{r})+\energy{W}_\theta(\theta)+\energy{W}_\phi(\phi)-\energy{E}t$. The separated equations give the three action integrals. See [[13.09 Application to the Kepler Problem]].

## Exercise 10. Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector and $SO(4)$ symmetry

At fixed negative energy, the three components of angular momentum and the three components of the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector, rescaled by $1/\sqrt{-2\mechanical{\mu}\energy{E}}$, close under Poisson brackets as the Lie algebra of $SO(4)$. The degeneracy of the bound Kepler motion reflects this hidden symmetry.

## Exercise 11. Transformation generated by $G=p^2/2$

The changes are $\delta q=\varepsilon\{q,p^2/2\}=\varepsilon p$ and $\delta p=\varepsilon\{p,p^2/2\}=0$. This is an infinitesimal canonical transformation.

## Exercise 12. Bohr–Sommerfeld quantization of the oscillator

*Derive the energy levels $\energy{E}_n$.*

Use $\oint p\,dq=2\pi\hbar(n+\tfrac12)$. The oscillator action gives $\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}=\hbar(n+\tfrac12)$, so $\energy{E}_n=\hbar\signal{\omega}(n+\tfrac12)$.

## Exercise 13. Kepler actions

*Express $\energy{E}$ in terms of $J_r$ and $\mechanical{L}$.*

The radial action is $J_r=-\mechanical{L}+\mechanical{\mu}\alpha/\sqrt{2\mechanical{\mu}\lvert\energy{E}\rvert}$. For bound motion,

$$
\energy{E}=-\frac{\mechanical{\mu}\alpha^2}{2(J_r+\mechanical{L})^2}.
$$

The energy depends only on $J_r+\mechanical{L}$. This inverse-distance degeneracy reflects the $SO(4)$ symmetry.

## Exercise 14. Complex oscillator variables

*Use $a=\sqrt{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}/2}\,q+ip/\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}}$.*

The product is $a^*a=(\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}/2)q^2+p^2/(2\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega})$, so $\signal{\omega}a^*a=\energy{H}$. Write $a=Aq+iBp$ with $A=\sqrt{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}/2}$, $B=1/\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}}$, and $AB=1/2$. Then $\{a,ia^*\}=AB+AB=1$. The pair $(a,ia^*)$ is canonical in complex variables.

> [!note] Source convention
> The source identifies the classical expression with $\hbar\signal{\omega}\hat a^\dagger\hat a$. This quantum operator omits the additive zero-point energy $\hbar\signal{\omega}/2$ of the oscillator Hamiltonian.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,p,Q,P$ | — | Canonical variables | The original and transformed coordinates and momenta. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{K},\energy{E},\energy{E}_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonians and energies | The Hamiltonians before and after transformation, the classical energy, and the energy levels. |
| $F_2,J,J_r,\energy{S},\energy{W},\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Actions and generating functions | The generator, phase integrals, action functions, and reduced Planck constant. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass and reduced mass | The particle mass and the Kepler reduced mass. |
| $\mechanical{r},\beta$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial coordinate and initial position | The Kepler distance and the free-particle initial position. |
| $\theta,\phi$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The spherical or action–angle coordinates. |
| $\signal{\omega},\signal{\omega}_0$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The oscillator frequency and the small-amplitude pendulum frequency. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The magnitude of Kepler angular momentum. |
| $\alpha$ | — | Parameter | The rotation angle or the Kepler potential strength, depending on the exercise. |
| $a,a^*$ | $\unit{J^{1/2}\,s^{1/2}}$ | Complex variables | The canonical variables of the oscillator. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
