> [!TLDR]

## Exercise 1. Work by a variable force

*Calculate the work of $\mechanical{F}(\mechanical{x})=3\mechanical{x}^2-2\mechanical{x}$ from $\mechanical{x}_A=0$ to $\mechanical{x}_B=\quantity{4}{m}$, with the force law in SI units.*

$$
\energy{W}=\int_0^4(3\mechanical{x}^2-2\mechanical{x})\,d\mechanical{x}
=[\mechanical{x}^3-\mechanical{x}^2]_0^4=64-16=\quantity{48}{J}.
$$

## Exercise 2. Fall and impact speed

*A mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{2}{kg}$ falls from rest through height $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{5}{m}$.*

Energy conservation gives $\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}$, so $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}=\sqrt{98.1}\simeq\quantity{9.90}{m\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 3. Smooth inclined plane

*Start from rest at height $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{2}{m}$. Find the speed at the bottom.*

Energy conservation gives $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}\simeq\quantity{6.26}{m\,s^{-1}}$, independent of the incline angle.

## Exercise 4. Equilibria of a quartic potential

*Find and classify the equilibria of $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=\mechanical{x}^4-4\mechanical{x}^2$ in the given units.*

The derivative is $\energy{U}'=4\mechanical{x}^3-8\mechanical{x}=4\mechanical{x}(\mechanical{x}^2-2)$. Its zeros are $0$ and $\pm\sqrt2$. Since $\energy{U}''=12\mechanical{x}^2-8$, the curvature is $-8$ at zero and $16$ at the two other points. The first point is an unstable maximum, and the other two are stable minima.

## Exercise 5. Inclined plane with friction

*Use $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{1}{kg}$, $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{3}{m}$, $\alpha=30^\circ$, and $\mechanical{\mu}_d=0.1$.*

The distance is $\mechanical{L}=\mechanical{h}/\sin\alpha=\quantity{6}{m}$. Friction dissipates $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{fric}}=\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\alpha\cdot\mechanical{L}\simeq\quantity{5.10}{J}$. The initial energy is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}=\quantity{29.43}{J}$, so the final kinetic energy is $\energy{K}=\quantity{24.33}{J}$. Thus $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\energy{K}/\mechanical{m}}\simeq\quantity{6.98}{m\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 6. Vehicle braking

*A vehicle of mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{1200}{kg}$ at speed $\mechanical{v}=\quantity{30}{m\,s^{-1}}$ stops over $\mechanical{d}=\quantity{50}{m}$. Find the average braking force and energy dissipated.*

The work is $\energy{W}=\Delta\energy{K}=-\mechanical{F}\mechanical{d}=-\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2$. Thus $\mechanical{F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2/(2\mechanical{d})=\quantity{10800}{N}$. The dissipated energy is $\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2=\quantity{540000}{J}=\quantity{0.54}{MJ}$.

## Exercise 7. Curl-free force

*For $\mechanical{\vec F}=(\mechanical{y},\mechanical{x},0)$ in the given units, decide if the force is conservative and find its potential.*

The only potentially nonzero curl component is $\partial\mechanical{F}_y/\partial\mechanical{x}-\partial\mechanical{F}_x/\partial\mechanical{y}=1-1=0$. The force is conservative on $\mathbb R^3$. The relations $-\partial\energy{U}/\partial\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{y}$ and $-\partial\energy{U}/\partial\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{x}$ give $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{x}\mechanical{y}$ up to a constant.

## Exercise 8. Lifting power

*Lift $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{100}{kg}$ through $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{20}{m}$ in $\Delta t=\quantity{10}{s}$.*

The average power is $\energy{P}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}/\Delta t=100\cdot9.81\cdot20/10=\quantity{1962}{W}$. With one mechanical horsepower approximately $\quantity{745.7}{W}$, this is about $2.6$ horsepower.

## Exercise 9. Morse potential

*For $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=\energy{U}_0(1-e^{-\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{a}})^2$, find equilibrium, the small-oscillation frequency, and the dissociation energy.*

The derivative $\energy{U}'=(2\energy{U}_0/\mechanical{a})(1-e^{-\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{a}})e^{-\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{a}}$ vanishes at $\mechanical{x}=0$ and tends to zero as $\mechanical{x}\to\infty$. Since $\energy{U}''(0)=2\energy{U}_0/\mechanical{a}^2$, the angular frequency is $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{2\energy{U}_0/(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{a}^2)}$. The dissociation energy is $\energy{E}_{\mathrm{diss}}=\energy{U}(\infty)-\energy{U}(0)=\energy{U}_0$.

## Exercise 10. Combined potential

*For $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=-A/\mechanical{x}+B/\mechanical{x}^2$ with $A,B>0$ and $\mechanical{x}>0$, find the equilibrium and describe the motion.*

The derivative is $A/\mechanical{x}^2-2B/\mechanical{x}^3$, which vanishes at $\mechanical{x}_0=2B/A$. Since $\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)>0$, this is a stable minimum. For negative energies above the minimum, the particle oscillates about $\mechanical{x}_0$. For nonnegative energy, it can escape.

## Exercise 11. Spring launch with friction

*A ball of mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{0.2}{kg}$ is released from a spring with $\mechanical{k}=\quantity{800}{N\,m^{-1}}$ and compression $\Delta\mechanical{x}=\quantity{0.05}{m}$. The horizontal surface has $\mechanical{\mu}_d=0.3$. Find the total distance traveled.*

The spring stores $\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}(\Delta\mechanical{x})^2=\quantity{1}{J}$. The energy dissipated is $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{fric}}=\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}$, with friction force $\quantity{0.589}{N}$. Equate the energies to get $\mechanical{d}=\quantity{1.70}{m}$.

## Exercise 12. Conservative force test

*Test $\mechanical{\vec F}=(\mechanical{x}\mechanical{y},\mechanical{x}^2/2,0)$ in the given units.*

The curl has only one potentially nonzero component, $\partial(\mechanical{x}^2/2)/\partial\mechanical{x}-\partial(\mechanical{x}\mechanical{y})/\partial\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}=0$. Thus the force is conservative on $\mathbb R^3$. Integrating $-\partial\energy{U}/\partial\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{x}\mechanical{y}$ gives $\energy{U}=-\tfrac12\mechanical{x}^2\mechanical{y}+f(\mechanical{y})$. The condition $-\partial\energy{U}/\partial\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{x}^2/2$ implies $f'(\mechanical{y})=0$. Thus $\energy{U}=-\tfrac12\mechanical{x}^2\mechanical{y}$ up to a constant.

## Exercise 13. Effective potential and radial oscillations

*Find the small radial oscillations for $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}+\beta/\mechanical{r}^2$.*

Adding the centrifugal potential gives

$$
\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r})=-\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{r}}+\frac{\beta'}{\mechanical{r}^2},
\qquad \beta'=\beta+\frac{\mechanical{L}^2}{2\mechanical{m}}.
$$

For $\alpha,\beta'>0$, the minimum is at $\mechanical{r}_0=2\beta'/\alpha$. The squared frequency is $\signal{\omega}^2=\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}''(\mechanical{r}_0)/\mechanical{m}=\alpha^4/(8\mechanical{m}\beta'^3)$.

## Exercise 14. Smooth inclined rod

*A bead slides from rest down a smooth rod of length $\mechanical{L}=\quantity{2}{m}$ at angle $\alpha=30^\circ$.*

The drop is $\mechanical{h}=\mechanical{L}\sin30^\circ=\quantity{1}{m}$. Energy conservation gives $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}=\sqrt{19.62}\simeq\quantity{4.43}{m\,s^{-1}}$, independent of mass.

## Exercise 15. Virial theorem for an elliptical orbit

*Prove the time-average relations for a Kepler ellipse.*

The virial theorem gives $2\langle\energy{K}\rangle=-\langle\energy{U}\rangle$. Since $\energy{E}=\energy{K}+\energy{U}$ is constant, $\energy{E}=\langle\energy{K}\rangle+\langle\energy{U}\rangle=\tfrac12\langle\energy{U}\rangle$. Thus $\langle\energy{K}\rangle=-\energy{E}$ and $\langle\energy{U}\rangle=2\energy{E}$.

> [!note] Source sign
> The source writes $\energy{E}=-\tfrac12\langle\energy{U}\rangle$ in the intermediate step. Its final averages use the positive coefficient $\tfrac12$ shown above.
