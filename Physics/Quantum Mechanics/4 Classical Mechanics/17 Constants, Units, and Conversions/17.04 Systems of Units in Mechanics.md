---
title: "Systems of Units in Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> SI, CGS, and natural units use different reference scales for mechanics.

- **SI units.** The book uses meters for length, kilograms for mass, and seconds for time.
- **CGS units.** Centimeters, grams, and seconds are common in some treatments of classical electrodynamics and solid-state physics. The energy unit is the erg, $\quantity{1}{erg}=\quantity{10^{-7}}{J}$. The force unit is the dyne, $\quantity{1}{dyn}=\quantity{10^{-5}}{N}$.
- **Natural units.** Relativistic mechanics often sets $c=1$, quantum mechanics often sets $\hbar=1$, and general relativity often sets $c=\mechanical{G}=1$. In classical mechanics, choose the length, mass, and time scales of the problem to simplify the equations.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $c$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | The reference speed for relativistic units. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The reference action for quantum units. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant set to unity in geometrized units. |
