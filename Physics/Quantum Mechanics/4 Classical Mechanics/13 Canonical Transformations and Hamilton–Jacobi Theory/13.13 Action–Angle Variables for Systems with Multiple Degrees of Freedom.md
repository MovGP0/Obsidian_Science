---
title: "Action–Angle Variables for Systems with Multiple Degrees of Freedom"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Integrable systems evolve linearly in their angle variables. Frequency relations determine whether the trajectories close or are dense.

For an integrable system with $n$ degrees of freedom and Hamiltonian $\energy{H}(J_1,\ldots,J_n)$ in the action variables,

$$
\dot J_k=0,\qquad \dot\theta_k=\signal{\omega}_k(J)=\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial J_k}.
$$

Thus $\theta_k(t)=\signal{\omega}_kt+\theta_k(0)$. The trajectories lie on a torus $\mathbb T^n$ in phase space when action–angle coordinates exist.

**Resonances.** If $\sum_k m_k\signal{\omega}_k=0$ for integers $m_k$ that are not all zero, then the trajectories lie on lower-dimensional subtori. If all nonzero frequencies are pairwise commensurate, the nonstationary trajectories close with a finite period. Without resonances, the trajectories are dense in the torus.

**Example. Anisotropic two-dimensional oscillator.** For $\energy{H}=\signal{\omega}_1J_1+\signal{\omega}_2J_2$, a rational frequency ratio gives closed trajectories and closed Lissajous figures in configuration space. An irrational ratio gives a trajectory whose projection is dense in the rectangle bounded by the two amplitudes.

**Integrable and chaotic systems.** In the regular compact regions of an integrable system, trajectories lie on invariant tori. Chaotic trajectories can explore larger invariant regions of an energy surface. Such regions can coexist with surviving KAM tori whose union has positive measure. Exact integrability is a special case among real systems.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source says that most real systems are chaotic and that chaotic trajectories fill regions of positive phase-space volume. Chaotic motion can coexist with regular motion, and its accessible region can be restricted to a fixed energy surface.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $J_k$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action | The constant momentum conjugate to an angle. |
| $\theta_k$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angle | The periodic phase coordinate on the torus. |
| $\signal{\omega}_k$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The advance rate of an angle. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy expressed as a function of the actions. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
