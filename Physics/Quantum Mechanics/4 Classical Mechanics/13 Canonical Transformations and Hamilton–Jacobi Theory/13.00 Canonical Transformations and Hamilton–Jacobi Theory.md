> [!TLDR]
> Canonical transformations preserve the form of Hamilton's equations. Hamilton–Jacobi theory uses them to solve equations of motion.

Hamilton's equations have a symmetric structure in the canonical variables $(q,p)$. **Canonical transformations** use this structure to change the phase-space coordinates and preserve the form of the equations. In an integrable system, action–angle variables reduce the motion to uniform angle evolution. Canonical transformations also lead to the **Hamilton–Jacobi equation**, an alternative integration method and a classical starting point for canonical quantization.

## Chapter summary

- Canonical transformations preserve the form of Hamilton's equations.
- They preserve the fundamental brackets, including $\{Q_k,P_l\}=\delta_{kl}$.
- The four types of generating function $F_1$ to $F_4$ use different independent variables.
- The Hamilton–Jacobi equation is $\energy{H}(q,\partial\energy{S}/\partial q,t)+\partial\energy{S}/\partial t=0$.
- For a time-independent Hamiltonian, set $\energy{S}=\energy{W}-\energy{E}t$. Then $\energy{H}(q,\partial\energy{W}/\partial q)=\energy{E}$.
- The action $J=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p\,dq$ has conjugate angle $\theta$ with frequency $\signal{\omega}=\partial\energy{H}/\partial J$.
- In integrable systems, suitable coordinates can separate the Hamilton–Jacobi equation. Action–angle coordinates give $n$ independent actions on regular compact invariant tori.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,p,Q_k,P_l$ | — | Canonical variables | The original and transformed coordinates and momenta. |
| $F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4,\energy{S},\energy{W},J$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Generating functions and action | The four generators, principal and characteristic functions, and phase integral. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energy | The original Hamiltonian and the separation constant for time. |
| $\theta$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angle | The coordinate conjugate to the action. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The rate of advance of the angle. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
