---
title: "Application to the Kepler Problem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Kepler Hamilton–Jacobi equation separates in spherical coordinates. For bound motion, the energy depends on the sum of three actions.

In spherical coordinates, the Kepler Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{H}=\frac{\mechanical{p}_r^2}{2\mechanical{\mu}}+\frac{p_\theta^2}{2\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r}^2}+\frac{p_\phi^2}{2\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r}^2\sin^2\theta}-\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{r}}.
$$

Use $\energy{W}(\mechanical{r},\theta,\phi)=\energy{W}_r(\mechanical{r})+\energy{W}_\theta(\theta)+\energy{W}_\phi(\phi)$ in the Hamilton–Jacobi equation to obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac{d\energy{W}_\phi}{d\phi}\right)^2 &= \alpha_\phi^2,\\
\left(\frac{d\energy{W}_\theta}{d\theta}\right)^2+\frac{\alpha_\phi^2}{\sin^2\theta} &= \alpha_\theta^2,\\
\left(\frac{d\energy{W}_r}{d\mechanical{r}}\right)^2+\frac{\alpha_\theta^2}{\mechanical{r}^2}-\frac{2\mechanical{\mu}\alpha}{\mechanical{r}}-2\mechanical{\mu}\energy{E} &= 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The actions $J_\phi,J_\theta,J_r$ are constant. Choose the azimuthal action to be nonnegative. For bound orbits,

$$
J_r+J_\theta+J_\phi=\alpha\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{\mu}}{2\lvert\energy{E}\rvert}},\qquad
\energy{E}=-\frac{\mechanical{\mu}\alpha^2}{2(J_r+J_\theta+J_\phi)^2}.
$$

All three frequencies are equal in this convention, $\signal{\omega}_r=\signal{\omega}_\theta=\signal{\omega}_\phi$. This degeneracy of the inverse-distance potential accounts for the closed bound orbits.

> [!info] Analytical mechanics
> With the phase ansatz $\psi=e^{i\energy{S}/\hbar}$, the leading-order Schrödinger equation as $\hbar\to0$ gives the Hamilton–Jacobi equation. The phase is $\energy{S}/\hbar$, and the surfaces $\energy{S}=\mathrm{const.}$ are wavefronts. This is the basis of the WKB approximation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{H},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energy | The Kepler Hamiltonian and its constant value for a trajectory. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Reduced mass | The mass parameter of the relative motion. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial coordinate | The distance between the bodies. |
| $\theta,\phi$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Spherical angles | The polar angle and azimuth. |
| $\mechanical{p}_r$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Radial momentum | The momentum conjugate to the radial coordinate. |
| $p_\theta,p_\phi,\alpha_\theta,\alpha_\phi$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Conjugate momenta and separation constants | The angular momenta and the constants of the angular equations. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Potential strength | The coefficient of the inverse-distance potential. |
| $\energy{W},\energy{W}_r,\energy{W}_\theta,\energy{W}_\phi,\energy{S}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action functions | The characteristic function, its separated parts, and the principal function. |
| $J_r,J_\theta,J_\phi$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Actions | The three phase integrals divided by $2\pi$. |
| $\signal{\omega}_r,\signal{\omega}_\theta,\signal{\omega}_\phi$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The rates of the three angle variables. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale in the phase ansatz. |
