---
title: "Action–Angle Variables"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In action–angle variables, the actions are constant and the angles advance uniformly.

For a periodic system with one degree of freedom, use these canonical variables.

- **Action.** $J=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p\,dq$. Integrate over one full period.
- **Angle.** $\theta=\signal{\omega}t+\theta_0$. This coordinate is conjugate to $J$.

The transformed Hamiltonian $\energy{H}=\energy{H}(J)$ depends only on $J$. The angle evolves at $\dot\theta=\partial\energy{H}/\partial J=\signal{\omega}(J)$.

**Harmonic oscillator.** The integral gives $J=\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}$, so $\energy{H}=J\signal{\omega}$. Therefore $\dot\theta=\signal{\omega}$ is constant and independent of amplitude. This is isochronism.

**Anharmonic oscillator.** For $\energy{H}=p^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\energy{U}(q)$ with a general potential, $J(\energy{E})$ is usually nonlinear. Then $\signal{\omega}=d\energy{E}/dJ$ depends on energy. In an integrable system with several degrees of freedom, action–angle variables give $n$ constant actions $J_k$ and $n$ angles $\theta_k$ that advance linearly with time.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $J,J_k$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Actions | The phase integrals divided by $2\pi$. |
| $q,p$ | — | Canonical variables | The generalized coordinate and conjugate momentum. |
| $\theta,\theta_0,\theta_k$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The angle coordinates and the initial angle. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The rate of advance of the angle. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{E},\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energies | The Hamiltonian, total energy, and potential energy. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The oscillator mass. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
