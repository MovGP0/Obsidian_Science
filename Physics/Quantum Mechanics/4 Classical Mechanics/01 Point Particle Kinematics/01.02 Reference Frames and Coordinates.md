---
title: "Reference Frames and Coordinates"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A reference frame uses an origin, coordinate axes, and a clock to specify where and when motion occurs.

A **reference frame** consists of the following:

- An origin $O$. This is the point from which distances are measured.
- A set of three directed, orthogonal coordinate axes in the most common case.
- A clock to measure time.

In Cartesian coordinates, the position of point $P$ is the vector

$$
\mechanical{\vec r} = \mechanical{x}\hat{\imath} + \mechanical{y}\hat{\jmath} + \mechanical{z}\hat{k},
$$

where $\hat{\imath}$, $\hat{\jmath}$, and $\hat{k}$ are the unit vectors of the $\mechanical{x}$, $\mechanical{y}$, and $\mechanical{z}$ axes.

**Other coordinates.** Use non-Cartesian coordinates for problems with specific symmetries.

- *Polar coordinates* $(\mechanical{r}, \mechanical{\theta})$ in a plane: $\mechanical{x} = \mechanical{r}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\mechanical{y} = \mechanical{r}\sin\mechanical{\theta}$.
- *Cylindrical coordinates* $(\mechanical{\rho}, \mechanical{\phi}, \mechanical{z})$: $\mechanical{x} = \mechanical{\rho}\cos\mechanical{\phi}$, $\mechanical{y} = \mechanical{\rho}\sin\mechanical{\phi}$, and $\mechanical{z} = \mechanical{z}$.
- *Spherical coordinates* $(\mechanical{r}, \mechanical{\theta}, \mechanical{\phi})$: $\mechanical{x} = \mechanical{r}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi}$, $\mechanical{y} = \mechanical{r}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi}$, and $\mechanical{z} = \mechanical{r}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$.

Choose coordinates to suit the symmetry of the problem. For example, polar coordinates describe a circular path in a plane more simply than Cartesian coordinates do.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The position of the point relative to the origin. |
| $\mechanical{x}, \mechanical{y}, \mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Cartesian coordinates | The components of the position vector along the three axes. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance | The distance from the origin in polar or spherical coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Cylindrical radial distance | The perpendicular distance from the vertical axis. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Planar polar angle | The angle from the positive horizontal axis in the plane. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Spherical polar angle | The angle from the positive vertical axis. |
| $\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Azimuth | The angle from the positive horizontal axis in the horizontal plane. |
