title: "Point Particle Kinematics"

> [!TLDR]
> Kinematics uses a reference frame to describe the position, velocity, and acceleration of a point particle.

Kinematics describes motion. First, choose a reference frame. Then examine how the coordinates of a body change with time and introduce the basic quantities: position, velocity, and acceleration. [[02.00 Newton's Three Laws|Chapter 2]] uses dynamics to examine the causes of motion. Kinematics gives the language for formulating problems in dynamics.

This chapter uses the *point particle* approximation. It models the body as a point with no spatial extent. This approximation is sufficient for many practical problems. [[07.00 Rigid Bodies|Chapter 7]] covers the kinematics of extended bodies, for which rotation and deformation have separate roles.

## Chapter Summary

- A [[01.02 Reference Frames and Coordinates|reference frame]] has an origin, a set of directed axes, and a clock.
- The [[01.03 Position, Velocity, and Acceleration|position]] is $\mechanical{\vec r}(t)$, the velocity is $\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}$, and the acceleration is $\mechanical{\vec a}=\mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}$.
- For [[01.04 Motion with Constant Acceleration in One Dimension|constant acceleration along a line]], $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{v}_0+\mechanical{a}_0t$, $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{x}_0+\mechanical{v}_0t+\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{a}_0t^2$, and $\mechanical{v}^2=\mechanical{v}_0^2+2\mechanical{a}_0(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_0)$.
- For [[01.05 Motion in Two and Three Dimensions|parabolic motion]], the range is $\mechanical{x}_g=\mechanical{v}_0^2\sin(2\mechanical{\alpha})/\mechanical{g}$ and the maximum height is $\mechanical{y}_h=\mechanical{v}_0^2\sin^2\mechanical{\alpha}/(2\mechanical{g})$.
- For [[01.06 Circular Motion|uniform circular motion]] with positive angular velocity, $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{\omega}\mechanical{R}$, $\mechanical{a}_c=\mechanical{\omega}^2\mechanical{R}=\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R}$, and $\signal{T}=2\pi/\mechanical{\omega}$.
- [[01.09 Relative Motion and Addition of Velocities|Galilean velocity addition]] gives $\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\vec V}+\mechanical{\vec v}\,'$ for nonrotating frames.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable that describes the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}, \mechanical{x}, \mechanical{x}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The vector position or its one-dimensional coordinate, including its initial value. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}, \mechanical{\vec v}\,', \mechanical{v}, \mechanical{v}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Particle velocity or speed | The rate of change of position, its signed component, or its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\vec V}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Frame velocity | The velocity of the origin of the moving frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}, \mechanical{a}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The rate of change of velocity, or its constant component along the line. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Launch angle | The angle above the horizontal at launch. |
| $\mechanical{x}_g, \mechanical{y}_h$ | $\unit{m}$ | Projectile range and height | The horizontal landing distance and the maximum vertical position. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of angular position in circular motion. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radius | The distance from the center of the circular path. |
| $\mechanical{a}_c$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Centripetal acceleration magnitude | The magnitude of the acceleration toward the center of the circular path. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The time for one revolution. |
