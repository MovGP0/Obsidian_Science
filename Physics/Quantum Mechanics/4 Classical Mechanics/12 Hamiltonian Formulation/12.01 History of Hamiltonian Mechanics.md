---
title: "History of Hamiltonian Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Hamilton adapted methods from geometric optics to mechanics. Jacobi, Poisson, and Liouville developed related tools.

William Rowan Hamilton (1805–1865) published the Hamiltonian formulation of mechanics in two papers in 1834 and 1835. He first developed a similar formulation for geometric optics using the **characteristic**, the integral of the optical path-length element. He applied the same methods to mechanics and obtained the equations that bear his name.

- **Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi** (1804–1851) developed the theory of canonical transformations and the Hamilton–Jacobi equation.
- **Siméon Denis Poisson** (1781–1840) introduced Poisson brackets in 1809.
- **Joseph Liouville** (1809–1882) proved the theorem on phase-space volume conservation in 1838.
- **Vladimir Arnold** (1937–2010) developed the geometric theory of symplectic mechanics in the twentieth century.

Statistical mechanics uses the Hamiltonian formalism directly, as in the work of Boltzmann and Gibbs. Canonical quantization also uses it, as in the work of Heisenberg and Dirac.
