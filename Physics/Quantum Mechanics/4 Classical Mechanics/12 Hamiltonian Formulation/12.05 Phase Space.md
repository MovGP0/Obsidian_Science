---
title: "Phase Space"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Phase space describes the state of a system with its generalized coordinates and conjugate momenta.

The **phase space** of a system with $n$ degrees of freedom is the $2n$-dimensional space of $(q,p)$. Initial conditions determine a phase-space trajectory $(q(t),p(t))$.

## Properties

- **Determinism.** The trajectory from each initial state is unique for a given initial time. For an autonomous system, distinct phase-space trajectories cannot cross.
- **Conservation of the Hamiltonian.** For conservative systems, trajectories lie on surfaces of constant $\energy{H}$.
- **Time-reversal invariance.** The transformations $t\to-t$ and $p\to-p$ preserve Hamilton's equations if the Hamiltonian is even in momentum and has no explicit time dependence.

> [!note] Source conditions
> The source gives non-crossing trajectories and time-reversal invariance for Hamiltonian motion. The statements above specify the autonomous case. A time-dependent Hamiltonian must also have the appropriate symmetry in time for time reversal to apply.

**Example. Harmonic oscillator.** In the $(q,p)$ plane, the trajectories are ellipses

$$
\frac{p^2}{2\mechanical{m}}+\frac{\mechanical{k}q^2}{2}=\energy{E}.
$$

Hamilton's equations determine the direction of motion. For $\energy{E}>0$, the trajectory runs clockwise when $q$ is horizontal and $p$ is vertical.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,p$ | — | Canonical variables | The generalized coordinates and conjugate momenta. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and total energy | The Hamiltonian and its value on an oscillator trajectory. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The restoring force per unit displacement. |
