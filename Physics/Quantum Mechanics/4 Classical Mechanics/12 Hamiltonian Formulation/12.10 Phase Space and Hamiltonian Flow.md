---
title: "Phase Space and Hamiltonian Flow"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Hamiltonian flow maps each initial phase-space point to its evolved state.

The **Hamiltonian flow** $\Phi^t$ takes the phase-space point $(q,p)$ at time $0$ to $(q(t),p(t))$ at time $t$.

## Properties

- **One-parameter group.** For an autonomous Hamiltonian, wherever the flow is defined, $\Phi^{t+s}=\Phi^t\circ\Phi^s$ and $\Phi^{-t}=(\Phi^t)^{-1}$.
- **Volume conservation.** Liouville's theorem gives $\det(d\Phi^t)=1$.
- **Preservation of Poisson brackets.** For any phase-space functions $f,g$, $\{f\circ\Phi^t,g\circ\Phi^t\}=\{f,g\}\circ\Phi^t$.

> [!note] Source formula
> The source equates $\{f,g\}$ at the initial and final points for arbitrary $f,g$. Preservation refers to the bracket of the two pulled-back functions. The value of the original bracket can change along the flow.

**Symplectic form.** The flow preserves the two-form $\omega=\sum_k dq_k\wedge dp_k$, called the **symplectic form**. Canonical transformations are symplectomorphisms, maps that preserve $\omega$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q_k,p_k$ | — | Canonical variables | The generalized coordinates and conjugate momenta. |
| $t,s$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The durations for the flow maps. |
