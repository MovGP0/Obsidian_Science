> [!TLDR]
> Apply pressure balance, mass conservation, and Bernoulli's equation to practical flows.

## Siphon

An inverted U-shaped siphon transfers liquid from a higher container to a lower one. Apply Bernoulli's equation from the free surface in the upper container to a lower outlet open to the same atmosphere. The upper free surface is nearly at rest. If $\mechanical{h}_a$ and $\mechanical{h}_u$ are the heights of the upper free surface and outlet relative to the lower free surface, then

$$
\mechanical{p}_0+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}_a
=\mechanical{p}_0+\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}^2+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}_u.
$$

The outlet speed is $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}(\mechanical{h}_a-\mechanical{h}_u)}$. In this ideal model it is independent of the height of the crest.

**Siphon limit.** The source estimates the maximum height of the crest above the upper surface by requiring positive absolute pressure there. Neglecting the kinetic term and vapor pressure gives $\mechanical{h}_{\max}=\mechanical{p}_{\mathrm{atm}}/(\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g})\approx\quantity{10}{m}$ for water. Flow speed and vapor pressure reduce the practical limit.

## Force on a Dam Wall

A straight dam of width $\mechanical{L}$ holds water to depth $\mechanical{H}$. Gauge pressure at depth $\mechanical{z}$ is $\mechanical{p}(\mechanical{z})=\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}$. The total force is

$$
\mechanical{F}=\int_0^H\mechanical{p}(\mechanical{z})\mechanical{L}\,dz
=\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{L}\mechanical{H}^2.
$$

For $\mechanical{H}=\quantity{50}{m}$ and $\mechanical{L}=\quantity{200}{m}$, $\mechanical{F}\approx\quantity{2.45\times10^9}{N}$. Dams must withstand forces of this scale.

## Lift on an Airfoil

Faster airflow above an airfoil can correspond to lower pressure there than below it, giving a net upward force. A quantitative treatment also accounts for circulation and the wake. The mean lift for an airfoil of area $\mechanical{S}$ in a flow of speed $\mechanical{v}$ is $\mechanical{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{C}_L\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}^2\mechanical{S}$, where $\mechanical{C}_L$ depends on the shape and angle of attack.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p}_0$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Atmospheric pressure | The pressure at the free surface and open outlet. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The mass per unit volume of the liquid. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward gravitational acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{h}_a,\mechanical{h}_u$ | $\unit{m}$ | Height | The upper free surface and outlet heights relative to the lower free surface. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Flow speed | The siphon outlet speed or the speed of air relative to the airfoil. |
| $\mechanical{p}_{\mathrm{atm}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Atmospheric pressure | The ambient pressure available to sustain the siphon column. |
| $\mechanical{h}_{\max}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Maximum crest height | The estimated height limit above the upper free surface. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Gauge pressure | The water pressure above atmospheric pressure at the given depth. |
| $\mechanical{L},\mechanical{H},\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Dam dimensions and depth | The width of the wall, water depth, and depth coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force on the dam | The total hydrostatic force on the wall. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Lift | The mean upward force on the airfoil. |
| $\mechanical{S}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Airfoil area | The reference area in the lift formula. |
| $\mechanical{C}_L$ | 1 | Lift coefficient | The dimensionless coefficient for the shape and angle of attack. |
