---
title: "Pressure and Hydrostatics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Pressure is normal force per unit area. In a static fluid of constant density, pressure increases linearly with depth.

> [!definition] Definition 8.1 — Pressure
> At a point in a fluid in static equilibrium, the **pressure** is
>
> $$
> \mechanical{p}=\frac{d\mechanical{F}_\perp}{d\mechanical{A}},
> $$
>
> where $d\mechanical{F}_\perp$ is the normal force on a small surface element of area $d\mechanical{A}$ through that point.

In equilibrium, pressure is isotropic. It has the same value for all orientations of the surface element. Its SI unit is $\unit{Pa}=\unit{N\,m^{-2}}$. A standard atmosphere is $\quantity{1}{atm}\approx\quantity{1.013\times10^5}{Pa}$.

**Hydrostatic equation.** Force balance on a fluid element in uniform gravity gives

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{p}}{d\mechanical{z}}=-\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g},
\qquad
\mechanical{p}(\mechanical{z})=\mechanical{p}_0+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}(\mechanical{z}_0-\mechanical{z})
\quad\text{for constant density}.
$$

Measure $\mechanical{z}$ upward. At the reference height $\mechanical{z}_0$, the pressure is $\mechanical{p}_0$. For constant density, pressure increases by $\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}$ per unit depth.

**Special cases.**

- **Incompressible liquid.** If $\mechanical{\rho}$ is constant, $\Delta\mechanical{p}=\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}$ for a depth increase $\mechanical{h}$.
- **Isothermal atmosphere.** For an ideal gas at constant temperature, $\mechanical{\rho}\propto\mechanical{p}$. With $\mechanical{p}_0$ at zero height, $\mechanical{p}(\mechanical{z})=\mechanical{p}_0e^{-\mechanical{z}/\mechanical{H}}$. The scale height is $\mechanical{H}=k_B\thermal{T}/(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g})$. The mean molecular mass $\mechanical{m}$ for air is about $\quantity{4.81\times10^{-26}}{kg}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p}_0$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The local and reference pressures. |
| $d\mechanical{F}_\perp$ | $\unit{N}$ | Normal force | The force that the fluid exerts perpendicular to the area element. |
| $d\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Area element | The small surface area used to define pressure. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The mass per unit volume of the fluid. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward gravitational acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{z},\mechanical{z}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Height | The local and reference vertical positions. |
| $\Delta\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure increase | The increase due to a change in depth. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Depth increase | The downward vertical distance between the two points. |
| $\mechanical{H}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Scale height | The height increase over which pressure falls by a factor $e^{-1}$. |
| $k_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant relating temperature to molecular energy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The uniform temperature of the isothermal atmosphere. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mean molecular mass | The mean mass per molecule of the gas. |
