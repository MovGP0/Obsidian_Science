---
title: "Euler Equation for Fluid Flow"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Euler's equation balances the acceleration of an ideal fluid element against pressure and gravity.

For an ideal fluid, apply Newton's second law to a fluid element.

$$
\mechanical{\rho}\left[\frac{\partial\mechanical{\vec v}}{\partial t}
+(\mechanical{\vec v}\cdot\nabla)\mechanical{\vec v}\right]
=-\nabla\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{\vec g}.
$$

The term $(\mechanical{\vec v}\cdot\nabla)\mechanical{\vec v}$ is the **convective acceleration**. It occurs because the fluid element moves through regions with different velocities.

**Equivalent forms.** For irrotational, incompressible flow, write $\mechanical{\vec v}=\nabla\mechanical{\Phi}$ where a velocity potential exists. Euler's equation gives the generalized Bernoulli equation. For compressible fluids, use the mass-conservation equation as well.

$$
\frac{\partial\mechanical{\rho}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{\vec v})=0.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The local mass per unit volume. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity field | The local fluid velocity. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the flow. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The local pressure in the fluid. |
| $\mechanical{\vec g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The external acceleration due to gravity. |
| $\mechanical{\Phi}$ | $\unit{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity potential | The scalar whose gradient gives the velocity in potential flow. |
