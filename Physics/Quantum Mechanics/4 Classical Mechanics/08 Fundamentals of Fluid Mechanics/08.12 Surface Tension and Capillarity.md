---
title: "Surface Tension and Capillarity"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Surface tension creates a pressure difference across curved interfaces and makes a wetting liquid rise in a narrow tube.

Intermolecular interactions give a liquid interface **surface tension** $\mechanical{\gamma}$, the energy required per unit increase in area. For pure water at $20\unit{{}^\circ C}$, $\mechanical{\gamma}\approx\quantity{73\times10^{-3}}{N\,m^{-1}}$.

**Laplace pressure.** Across a single spherical interface of radius $\mechanical{R}$, the pressure inside exceeds the pressure outside by

$$
\Delta\mechanical{p}=\frac{2\mechanical{\gamma}}{\mechanical{R}}.
$$

For a thin soap bubble with two interfaces, $\Delta\mechanical{p}=4\mechanical{\gamma}/\mechanical{R}$. Smaller bubbles have a larger pressure difference.

**Capillary rise.** A liquid that wets a tube of radius $\mechanical{r}$ rises by

$$
\mechanical{h}=\frac{2\mechanical{\gamma}\cos\mechanical{\theta}}
{\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{r}}.
$$

Here $\mechanical{\theta}$ is the contact angle. For water on clean glass, $\mechanical{\theta}\approx0$. A tube of radius $\mechanical{r}=\quantity{1}{mm}$ gives $\mechanical{h}\approx\quantity{1.5}{cm}$. Capillarity contributes to liquid transport in plants and similar biological processes.

### Example — Oil Drop on Water

A drop of oil can spread over water as a thin film. Surface and interfacial tensions determine whether it spreads, and surface tension gradients can drive Marangoni flow. The source gives a film thickness of order $\quantity{10}{nm}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\gamma}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Surface tension | The energy required per unit increase in interfacial area. |
| — | $\unit{{}^\circ C}$ | Water temperature | The temperature for the stated surface tension of pure water. |
| $\mechanical{R},\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radius | The radius of the bubble or capillary tube. |
| $\Delta\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure difference | The pressure inside minus the pressure outside the bubble. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Capillary rise | The height of the liquid in the tube above the free surface. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Contact angle | The angle at the liquid–solid interface measured through the liquid. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Liquid density | The mass per unit volume of the liquid. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| — | $\unit{m}$ | Film thickness | The thickness scale of the oil film in the example. |
