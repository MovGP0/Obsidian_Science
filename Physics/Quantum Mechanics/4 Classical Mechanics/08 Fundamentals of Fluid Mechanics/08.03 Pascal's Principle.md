---
title: "Pascal's Principle"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An applied pressure change transmits through an incompressible liquid. A hydraulic press uses different piston areas to multiply force.

> [!theorem] Theorem 8.1 — Pascal's Principle
> In an incompressible liquid in equilibrium, a pressure change applied at one location transmits undiminished to all other parts of the liquid.

**Hydraulic press.** Two pistons have areas $\mechanical{A}_1$ and $\mechanical{A}_2$. A force $\mechanical{F}_1$ on the smaller piston gives pressure increase $\mechanical{p}=\mechanical{F}_1/\mechanical{A}_1$. At the larger piston, this produces force $\mechanical{F}_2=\mechanical{p}\mechanical{A}_2=\mechanical{F}_1(\mechanical{A}_2/\mechanical{A}_1)$. The force increases by the area ratio.

**Energy conservation.** If the small piston descends by $\mechanical{h}_1$, the volume displaced is $\mechanical{A}_1\mechanical{h}_1=\mechanical{A}_2\mechanical{h}_2$. Thus $\mechanical{h}_2=\mechanical{h}_1\mechanical{A}_1/\mechanical{A}_2$. In the ideal press, the input and output work are equal, $\mechanical{F}_1\mechanical{h}_1=\mechanical{F}_2\mechanical{h}_2$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Applied pressure change | The pressure increase transmitted through the liquid. |
| $\mechanical{A}_1,\mechanical{A}_2$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Piston area | The cross-sectional areas of the two pistons. |
| $\mechanical{F}_1,\mechanical{F}_2$ | $\unit{N}$ | Piston force | The input force and the output force. |
| $\mechanical{h}_1,\mechanical{h}_2$ | $\unit{m}$ | Piston travel | The distances the two pistons move. |
