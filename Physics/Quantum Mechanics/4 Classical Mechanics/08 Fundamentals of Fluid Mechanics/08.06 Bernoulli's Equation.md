---
title: "Bernoulli's Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For steady, inviscid, incompressible flow under gravity, pressure plus kinetic and gravitational energy per unit volume stays constant along a streamline.

Along a streamline in the ideal flow considered here,

$$
\mechanical{p}+\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}^2+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}=\mathrm{const}.
$$

> [!note] Source assumptions
> The source also assumes irrotational flow. The equation holds along an individual streamline without that assumption. Irrotational flow makes the Bernoulli constant common to streamlines in the same connected region.

**Derivation.** Follow a fluid element of volume $\delta\mechanical{V}$ and mass $\delta\mechanical{m}=\mechanical{\rho}\delta\mechanical{V}$ from section 1 to section 2 along a streamline. The energy balance is

$$
(\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_2)\delta\mechanical{V}
+(\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}_1-\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}_2)\delta\mechanical{V}
=\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}_2^2\delta\mechanical{V}
-\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}_1^2\delta\mechanical{V}.
$$

The work done by the pressure forces and gravity equals the change in kinetic energy. Divide by $\delta\mechanical{V}$ and group the terms at the same section.

$$
\boxed{\mechanical{p}+\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}^2+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}=\mathrm{const}.}
$$

**Meaning.** This is an energy balance per unit volume. Pressure accounts for the work done by fluid pressure forces as the element moves through the flow.

### Example — Venturi Tube

A horizontal tube narrows from area $\mechanical{A}_1$ to $\mechanical{A}_2<\mechanical{A}_1$. Continuity gives $\mechanical{v}_2=\mechanical{v}_1\mechanical{A}_1/\mechanical{A}_2>\mechanical{v}_1$. Bernoulli's equation gives $\mechanical{p}_2<\mechanical{p}_1$. The pressure drops where the speed increases. A flowmeter uses this pressure difference to determine the speed.

### Example — Torricelli's Efflux Speed

A hole lies at depth $\mechanical{h}$ below the free surface of a tank. Neglect the speed of the free surface and take the pressure at both points as atmospheric. Apply Bernoulli's equation from the surface to the hole. Then $\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}^2$, so

$$
\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}.
$$

This is the same speed as for free fall through height $\mechanical{h}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The local pressure or its value at one of the sections. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The constant density of the fluid. |
| $\mechanical{v},\mechanical{v}_1,\mechanical{v}_2$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Flow speed | The fluid speed along the streamline. |
| $\mechanical{z},\mechanical{z}_1,\mechanical{z}_2$ | $\unit{m}$ | Height | The vertical position along the streamline. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\delta\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Element volume | The volume of the moving fluid element. |
| $\delta\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Element mass | The mass of the moving fluid element. |
| $\mechanical{A}_1,\mechanical{A}_2$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Cross-sectional area | The areas at the wide and narrow sections of the tube. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Depth below the surface | The vertical distance from the free surface to the hole. |
