---
title: "Kinetic Energy in the Center-of-Mass Frame"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Total kinetic energy is the sum of the energy of center-of-mass motion and the energy of motion relative to it.

> [!theorem] Theorem 5.3 — König's Theorem
> $$
> \energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{M}\mechanical{V}_{\mathrm{CM}}^2+\energy{K}_{\mathrm{rel}},
> $$
>
> where $\energy{K}_{\mathrm{rel}}=\tfrac12\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\lVert\mechanical{\vec v}_i-\mechanical{\vec V}_{\mathrm{CM}}\rVert^2$ is the kinetic energy in the center-of-mass frame.

**Proof.** Write $\mechanical{\vec v}_i=\mechanical{\vec V}_{\mathrm{CM}}+\mechanical{\vec v}'_i$, where $\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec v}'_i=\vec 0$. Substitute into $\energy{K}=\tfrac12\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{v}_i^2$ and expand. The mixed term vanishes.

**Consequences.**

- For an isolated collision, $\mechanical{\vec V}_{\mathrm{CM}}$ is constant. The kinetic energy of center-of-mass motion is then constant, so any change in $\energy{K}$ comes from $\energy{K}_{\mathrm{rel}}$.
- In an elastic two-body collision, the relative speed $\lVert\mechanical{\vec v}_{\mathrm{rel}}\rVert$ stays constant. In the center-of-mass frame, the velocities change direction while keeping their magnitudes.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{K},\energy{K}_{\mathrm{rel}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The total kinetic energy and the kinetic energy in the center-of-mass frame. |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{m}_i$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The total mass and the individual particle masses. |
| $\mechanical{\vec V}_{\mathrm{CM}},\mechanical{V}_{\mathrm{CM}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Center-of-mass velocity | The velocity of the center of mass and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}_i,\mechanical{\vec v}'_i,\mechanical{v}_i$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Particle velocity and speed | The velocities in the laboratory and center-of-mass frames, or the laboratory speed. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}_{\mathrm{rel}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Relative velocity | The velocity of one particle relative to the other. |
