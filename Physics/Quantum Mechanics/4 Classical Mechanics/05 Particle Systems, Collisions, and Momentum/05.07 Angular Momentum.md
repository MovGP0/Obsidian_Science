---
title: "Angular Momentum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Angular momentum about a fixed point changes at the rate given by the net external torque about that point.

> [!definition] Definition 5.3 — Angular Momentum
> For a point particle about a reference point $O$,
>
> $$
> \mechanical{\vec L}=\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec p}
> =\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec v}.
> $$
>
> For a system, $\mechanical{\vec L}=\sum_i\mechanical{\vec L}_i$.

**Change in angular momentum.** Differentiation about a fixed point gives $d\mechanical{\vec L}/dt=\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{\vec\tau}$, where $\mechanical{\vec\tau}$ is the **torque** about $O$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 5.2 — Conservation of Angular Momentum
> If the net external torque about a fixed point is zero, the system's angular momentum about that point is constant.

**Typical cases.**

- A central force is parallel to $\mechanical{\vec r}$, so its torque is zero. Angular momentum is conserved. See [[06.04 Central Forces and Conservation of Angular Momentum]].
- For an isolated system the external torques sum to zero, so total angular momentum is conserved.
- If the net torque component $\mechanical{\tau}_z=0$, the corresponding component $\mechanical{L}_z$ is conserved.

### Example — Skater Pulling in Their Arms

A skater rotates about a vertical axis and pulls in their arms. The angular momentum $\mechanical{L}_z=\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\omega}$ stays constant when the net external torque about that axis is negligible. Pulling the arms closer to the axis decreases the moment of inertia $\mechanical{I}$, so the angular speed $\mechanical{\omega}$ increases.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec L},\mechanical{\vec L}_i,\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The angular momentum about the reference point, its particle contributions, or its axial component. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The displacement from the reference point to the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Linear momentum | The product of the particle mass and velocity. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The time derivative of position. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | The force on the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec\tau},\mechanical{\tau}_z$ | $\unit{N\,m}$ | Torque | The moment of the force about the reference point and its axial component. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the angular momentum derivative. |
| $\mechanical{I}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia | The rotational inertia of the skater about the vertical axis. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular speed | The rotation rate of the skater. |
