---
title: "Full Derivation of the Rocket Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Balance the momentum of the rocket and the expelled gas across a short time interval to derive the rocket equation.

At time $t$, a rocket of mass $\mechanical{m}(t)$ ejects mass $-d\mechanical{m}$. Here $d\mechanical{m}<0$, because the rocket's mass decreases. The exhaust has velocity $\mechanical{\vec u}$ relative to the rocket, directed opposite the rocket's motion. In the absence of external forces, the total momentum of the rocket and the expelled gas is conserved between $t$ and $t+dt$.

Initially $\mechanical{\vec p}(t)=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}$. At $t+dt$, to first order in the changes,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\vec p}(t+dt)
&=(\mechanical{m}+d\mechanical{m})(\mechanical{\vec v}+d\mechanical{\vec v})
+(-d\mechanical{m})(\mechanical{\vec v}+\mechanical{\vec u})\\
&\approx\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}+\mechanical{m}\,d\mechanical{\vec v}-\mechanical{\vec u}\,d\mechanical{m}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $d\mechanical{\vec p}=\vec 0$, $\mechanical{m}\,d\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\vec u}\,d\mechanical{m}$, so

$$
\mechanical{m}\frac{d\mechanical{\vec v}}{dt}=\mechanical{\vec u}\frac{d\mechanical{m}}{dt}.
$$

Since $d\mechanical{m}/dt<0$ and $\mechanical{\vec u}$ points backward, the thrust $\mechanical{\vec u}\,d\mechanical{m}/dt$ points forward. Set $\mechanical{u}=\lVert\mechanical{\vec u}\rVert$. Along the direction of motion, $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot v}=-\mechanical{u}\mechanical{\dot m}>0$ in the absence of external forces.

> [!note] Source sign
> The source writes $-\mechanical{\vec u}\,d\mechanical{m}/dt$ in the explanation of the vector thrust. With $\mechanical{\vec u}$ defined as backward exhaust velocity, the vector formula immediately above has a plus sign. The minus sign applies to the scalar formula with positive exhaust speed $\mechanical{u}$.

With uniform downward gravity, $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot v}=-\mechanical{u}\mechanical{\dot m}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}$. For constant mass consumption $\mechanical{\dot m}=-\mechanical{\dot m}_0$ and launch from rest,

$$
\mechanical{v}(t)=\mechanical{u}\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}_0}{\mechanical{m}_0-\mechanical{\dot m}_0t}-\mechanical{g}t.
$$

The exhaust contribution increases with the logarithm of the mass ratio. The gravity loss increases linearly with time. Large velocity changes require high mass ratios, which is a central engineering challenge of launch vehicles.

### Example — Saturn V

For the first stage, the source gives $\mechanical{m}_0\approx\quantity{2.97\times10^6}{kg}$, $\mechanical{m}_f\approx\quantity{7.5\times10^5}{kg}$, and $\mechanical{u}\approx\quantity{2.6}{km\,s^{-1}}$. The ideal first-stage speed gain is $\Delta\mechanical{v}_1=\mechanical{u}\ln(\mechanical{m}_0/\mechanical{m}_f)\approx\quantity{3.6}{km\,s^{-1}}$. The source estimates a gravity loss of about $\quantity{1}{km\,s^{-1}}$ and an overall speed of order $\quantity{12}{km\,s^{-1}}$ after contributions from the other stages, sufficient for a lunar trajectory.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m},d\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The rocket mass and its negative change as gas is ejected. |
| $t,dt$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The initial time and the small interval used in the balance. |
| $\mechanical{\vec u}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Relative exhaust velocity | The velocity of the gas relative to the rocket. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v},d\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Rocket velocity | The velocity in the inertial frame and its change. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The total momentum of the rocket and gas considered over the interval. |
| $\mechanical{m}_0,\mechanical{m}_f$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Initial and final mass | The vehicle masses at the start and end of the stage. |
| $\mechanical{\dot m},\mechanical{\dot m}_0$ | $\unit{kg\,s^{-1}}$ | Mass change and consumption rates | The negative rate of mass change and its positive constant magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{u}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Relative exhaust speed | The magnitude of the exhaust velocity relative to the rocket. |
| $\mechanical{v},\Delta\mechanical{v}_1$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed and speed gain | The instantaneous speed and ideal first-stage increase. |
| $\mechanical{\dot v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Longitudinal acceleration | The acceleration along the flight direction. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in the uniform-gravity approximation. |
