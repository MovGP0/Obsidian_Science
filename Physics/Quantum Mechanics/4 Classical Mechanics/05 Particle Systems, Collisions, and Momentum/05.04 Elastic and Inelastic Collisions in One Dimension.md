---
title: "Elastic and Inelastic Collisions in One Dimension"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Momentum is conserved during a collision with negligible external impulse. In an elastic collision, kinetic energy is also conserved.

A **collision** is a short interaction during which internal forces dominate. Apply momentum conservation over the whole collision when the external impulse is negligible. Whether kinetic energy is conserved distinguishes elastic from inelastic collisions.

## Elastic Collision in One Dimension

Two bodies of masses $\mechanical{m}_1$ and $\mechanical{m}_2$ have initial velocities $\mechanical{v}_1$ and $\mechanical{v}_2$. Let the primes denote their final velocities. Conservation of momentum and kinetic energy gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{v}_1+\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{v}_2
&=\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{v}'_1+\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{v}'_2,\\
\tfrac12\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{v}_1^2+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{v}_2^2
&=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}_1(\mechanical{v}'_1)^2+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}_2(\mechanical{v}'_2)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Rewrite momentum conservation as $\mechanical{m}_1(\mechanical{v}_1-\mechanical{v}'_1)=\mechanical{m}_2(\mechanical{v}'_2-\mechanical{v}_2)$. Factor the differences of squares in the energy equation. Dividing by the common nonzero momentum transfer gives $\mechanical{v}_1+\mechanical{v}'_1=\mechanical{v}_2+\mechanical{v}'_2$. The relative velocity reverses sign. Together with momentum conservation, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{v}'_1&=\frac{\mechanical{m}_1-\mechanical{m}_2}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}\mechanical{v}_1
+\frac{2\mechanical{m}_2}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}\mechanical{v}_2,\\
\mechanical{v}'_2&=\frac{2\mechanical{m}_1}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}\mechanical{v}_1
+\frac{\mechanical{m}_2-\mechanical{m}_1}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}\mechanical{v}_2.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Limiting cases.**

- For $\mechanical{m}_1=\mechanical{m}_2$, the bodies exchange velocities: $\mechanical{v}'_1=\mechanical{v}_2$ and $\mechanical{v}'_2=\mechanical{v}_1$.
- If $\mechanical{m}_2\gg\mechanical{m}_1$ and the heavier body starts at rest, $\mechanical{v}'_1\to-\mechanical{v}_1$ and $\mechanical{v}'_2\to0$. This is the limit of elastic rebound from a wall.
- If $\mechanical{m}_1\gg\mechanical{m}_2$ and the lighter body starts at rest, $\mechanical{v}'_1\approx\mechanical{v}_1$ and $\mechanical{v}'_2\approx2\mechanical{v}_1$. The heavy body continues, and the light body moves forward at nearly twice the incoming speed.

## Perfectly Inelastic Collision

The bodies stick together, with $\mechanical{v}'_1=\mechanical{v}'_2=\mechanical{v}'$. Momentum conservation gives

$$
\mechanical{v}'=\frac{\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{v}_1+\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{v}_2}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}.
$$

The positive kinetic energy lost to deformation, heat, or sound is

$$
\Delta\energy{K}_{\mathrm{loss}}=\tfrac12\frac{\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{m}_2}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}(\mechanical{v}_1-\mechanical{v}_2)^2.
$$

### Example — Ballistic Pendulum

A projectile of mass $\mechanical{m}$ and speed $\mechanical{v}$ embeds in a suspended block of mass $\mechanical{M}$. Momentum conservation during impact gives $\mechanical{v}'=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}/(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})$. After impact, kinetic energy becomes gravitational potential energy:

$$
\tfrac12(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})(\mechanical{v}')^2=(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}.
$$

Therefore $\mechanical{v}=[(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})/\mechanical{m}]\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}$. Measuring the rise height $\mechanical{h}$ gives the incoming speed.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The masses of the colliding bodies. |
| $\mechanical{v}_1,\mechanical{v}_2,\mechanical{v}'_1,\mechanical{v}'_2$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The initial and final signed velocities along the collision axis. |
| $\mechanical{v}'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Common final velocity | The velocity of the joined bodies just after impact. |
| $\Delta\energy{K}_{\mathrm{loss}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy loss | The positive difference between initial and final kinetic energies. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Projectile and block masses | The separate masses in the ballistic pendulum. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Projectile speed | The speed before impact. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Rise height | The vertical distance the joined bodies rise after impact. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
