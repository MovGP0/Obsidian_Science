---
title: "Motivation for Studying Particle Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Separate the motion of the center of mass from the motion of particles relative to it.

Systems of particles extend the dynamics of a single point particle. They describe car crashes, collisions between balls, explosions, rocket propulsion, satellite dynamics, and elementary particle scattering.

Separate the motion into two parts:

- The motion of the center of mass, determined by the net external force.
- The internal motion relative to the center of mass, influenced by the internal forces.

For an isolated system, the center of mass moves at constant velocity along a straight line. Internal interactions can redistribute motion within the system without changing that center-of-mass motion.

**Many-body systems.** The number of equations increases with the number $N$ of particles. Conservation of momentum, energy, and angular momentum can reduce the problem when the corresponding conditions hold. The standard two-body problem with a central interaction can be solved by reducing it to one-body motion. For $N\ge 3$, the system is generally not integrable, but useful conservation laws still apply.
