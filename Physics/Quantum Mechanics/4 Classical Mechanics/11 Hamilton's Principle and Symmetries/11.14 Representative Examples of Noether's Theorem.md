---
title: "Representative Examples of Noether's Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Global symmetries of a field Lagrangian give conserved Noether charges.

**Conservation of electric charge.** The Lagrangian density of a charged complex scalar or spinor field coupled to the electromagnetic field contains bilinear terms such as $\psi^*\psi$. The density $\mathcal L$ is invariant under the global $U(1)$ transformation $\psi\to e^{i\alpha}\psi$, where $\alpha$ is a real constant. The associated conserved Noether charge is the total electric charge.

**Energy–momentum tensor.** Space-time translations give the conserved energy–momentum tensor. The spatial integral of its component $T^{00}$ gives the conserved total energy. In the units used in this example, the spatial integral of $T^{0i}$ gives the $i$th component of the total momentum.

Noether's theorem puts conservation laws in a common framework. When a classical or quantum system has a continuous symmetry, look for a corresponding conserved quantity. In the other direction, an observed conservation law can suggest an underlying symmetry.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\psi$ | — | Charged field | The field transformed by the global phase rotation. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Phase angle | The constant parameter of the global rotation. |
| $\mathcal L$ | $\unit{J\,m^{-3}}$ | Lagrangian density | The field quantity invariant under the transformation. |
| — | $\unit{C}$ | Electric charge | The conserved Noether charge of the global phase symmetry. |
| $T^{00}$ | $\unit{J\,m^{-3}}$ | Energy density | The component whose spatial integral is the total energy. |
| $T^{0i}$ | — | Momentum component of the tensor | Its spatial integral gives the total momentum component in the source's convention. |
