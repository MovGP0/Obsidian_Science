---
title: "Noether's Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A continuous symmetry of the action gives a quantity that is constant along solutions of the equations of motion.

> [!theorem] Theorem 11.2 — Noether's Theorem
> For an infinitesimal transformation $q_k\to q_k+\varepsilon\eta_k(q,t)$ with $\delta\energy{L}=\varepsilon\,df/dt$, the **Noether charge** is
>
> $$
> Q=\sum_k\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\eta_k-f.
> $$
>
> Along a physical trajectory, $dQ/dt=0$.

**Proof sketch.** Vary the action under the transformation. On a physical path, the Euler–Lagrange equations cancel the interior variation. The variation of the Lagrangian is then the time derivative of $\varepsilon\sum_k(\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q_k)\eta_k$. By the symmetry it also equals $\varepsilon\,df/dt$. Equating these expressions gives $\varepsilon\,dQ/dt=0$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q_k,\dot q_k$ | — | Coordinate and velocity | The generalized coordinate and its time derivative. |
| $\varepsilon,\eta_k$ | — | Transformation parameter and generator | Their product gives the infinitesimal change of $q_k$. |
| $\energy{L},\delta\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian and variation | The dynamical function and its variation under the transformation. |
| $Q,f$ | — | Noether charge and boundary coefficient | The conserved quantity and the coefficient of the symmetry boundary term. Their units depend on $\varepsilon$. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
