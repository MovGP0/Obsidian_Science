---
title: "Stationary Action Principle"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The physical path has zero first variation of the action for variations that fix the endpoint times and configurations.

> [!theorem] Theorem 11.1 — Hamilton's Principle
> Between fixed times $t_1,t_2$ and fixed configurations $q(t_1)=q_1$, $q(t_2)=q_2$, the actual path satisfies $\delta S[q]=0$.

**Derivation.** Vary $q(t)$ to $q(t)+\delta q(t)$ with $\delta q(t_1)=\delta q(t_2)=0$. Then

$$
\delta S=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\sum_k
\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial q_k}\delta q_k
+\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\delta\dot q_k\right)\,dt.
$$

Use $\delta\dot q_k=d(\delta q_k)/dt$ and integrate the velocity term by parts.

$$
\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\delta\dot q_k\,dt
=\left[\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\delta q_k\right]_{t_1}^{t_2}
-\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\right)\delta q_k\,dt.
$$

Substitution gives

$$
\delta S=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\sum_k
\left[\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial q_k}
-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\right)\right]\delta q_k\,dt
+\left[\sum_k\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\delta q_k\right]_{t_1}^{t_2}.
$$

The boundary term vanishes because the endpoints are fixed. The remaining variations are arbitrary, so each coefficient vanishes. This gives the Euler–Lagrange equations.

**Stationarity.** A stationary path can give a minimum, maximum, or saddle point depending on the functional. For a mechanical system with positive-definite kinetic energy, the physical path minimizes the action locally over a sufficiently short interval under suitable regularity conditions. This is the origin of the name *principle of least action*.

$$
\boxed{S=\int\energy{L}\,dt,\qquad\delta S=0.}
$$
