title: "Newton's Three Laws"

> [!TLDR]
> Newton's three laws relate motion to forces. Together with the initial conditions, the forces determine the motion of a classical system.

Kinematics describes motion. Dynamics examines its causes. The **Newtonian laws**, formulated in 1687 in the *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica*, provide the basis for classical dynamics. These three principles together determine the motion of a system once the forces and initial conditions are known. Later developments, from Lagrange's equations to statistical mechanics, build on them.

## Chapter Summary

- The [[02.02 Principle of Inertia|first law]] identifies inertial frames. In such a frame, a body with zero net force keeps a constant velocity.
- The [[02.03 Second Law – F = ma|second law]] gives $\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec a}$ for constant mass. Its momentum form is $\mechanical{\vec F}=d\mechanical{\vec p}/dt$.
- The [[02.04 Third Law – Action and Reaction|third law]] pairs each action with an opposite reaction on a different body.
- [[02.05 Fundamental Forces and Contact Forces|Fundamental forces]] include gravitation and electromagnetism. Contact forces include normal force, friction, tension, and elastic force.
- In [[02.07 Noninertial Reference Frames|noninertial frames]], $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec a}\,'=\mechanical{\vec F}+\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{app}}$. Rotating frames include centrifugal and Coriolis forces.
- A [[02.11 Free-Body Diagram|free-body diagram]] is the standard tool for setting up a dynamics problem.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Net force | The sum of the forces due to physical interactions. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{app}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Apparent force | The sum of the forces due to the acceleration of the frame. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The inertial mass of the body. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}, \mechanical{\vec a}\,'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The acceleration of the body in the inertial or noninertial frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The product of the mass and velocity. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the momentum derivative. |
