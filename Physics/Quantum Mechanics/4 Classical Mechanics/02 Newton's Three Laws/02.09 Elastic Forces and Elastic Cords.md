---
title: "Elastic Forces and Elastic Cords"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An ideal spring produces a force proportional to displacement from its rest position.

An **ideal spring** exerts a force proportional to the displacement $\mechanical{\vec x}$ from its rest position. The force points in the opposite direction.

$$
\mechanical{\vec F}=-\mechanical{k}\mechanical{\vec x}.
$$

The positive coefficient $\mechanical{k}$ is the **spring constant**, measured in $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$. The force is conservative, with potential energy $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{k}\mechanical{x}^2$.

**Spring combinations.**

- In parallel, $\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\mechanical{k}_1+\mechanical{k}_2$.
- In series, $1/\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm{eq}}=1/\mechanical{k}_1+1/\mechanical{k}_2$.

**Elastic limit.** Real springs obey Hooke's law only over a limited range. Beyond the elastic limit, plastic deformation occurs. This section uses the linear elastic regime.

### Example — Mass on a Vertical Spring

A body of mass $\mechanical{m}$ hangs from a spring of constant $\mechanical{k}$ in a uniform gravitational field. Take the $\mechanical{z}$ axis vertically upward, with zero at the unloaded position of the moving end. At equilibrium, $-\mechanical{k}\mechanical{z}_0-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}=0$. Thus $\mechanical{z}_0=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{k}$ lies below the origin, and the extension is $\lvert\mechanical{z}_0\rvert=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{k}$.

With $\mechanical{\xi}=\mechanical{z}-\mechanical{z}_0$, the equation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot\xi}+\mechanical{k}\mechanical{\xi}=0$. This is the equation of a harmonic oscillator. Gravity shifts the equilibrium position.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source places the origin at the attachment point at rest. The equations use the position of the unloaded moving end as zero. The extension is measured from that position.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Spring force | The restoring force exerted by the spring. |
| $\mechanical{\vec x}, \mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Spring displacement | The displacement vector or signed displacement from the rest position. |
| $\mechanical{k}, \mechanical{k}_1, \mechanical{k}_2, \mechanical{k}_{\mathrm{eq}}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The stiffness of an individual spring or the combination. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Elastic potential energy | The energy stored by the displacement of the spring. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the body suspended from the spring. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{z}, \mechanical{z}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Vertical position | The position of the body and its equilibrium value. |
| $\mechanical{\xi}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement from equilibrium | The vertical position relative to the loaded equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\xi}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Vertical acceleration | The second time derivative of the displacement from equilibrium. |
