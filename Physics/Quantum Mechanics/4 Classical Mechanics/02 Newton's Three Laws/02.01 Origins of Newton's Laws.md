---
title: "Origins of Newton's Laws"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Newton united earlier results on motion and gravitation into a system that could explain motion on Earth and in the sky.

Newton published the three laws of motion in 1687 in the *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica*, a foundation of modern physics. He built on Galileo's laws of falling bodies and principle of relativity, Kepler's laws of planetary motion, Huygens's work on centripetal force, and Hooke's ideas about gravitation, which Hooke had anticipated in private letters.

Newton's main innovation was to unite the laws into a coherent deductive system. Three axioms could account for the known results of mechanical dynamics. Applying the law of gravitation to the laws of motion gives Kepler's three laws.

For the first time, the same theory explained phenomena on Earth and in the sky. This removed the Aristotelian distinction between the worlds below and above the Moon.

Newton's laws remain the basis of classical mechanics in technology and engineering. At relativistic speeds and atomic scales, the corresponding extensions are relativity and quantum mechanics.
