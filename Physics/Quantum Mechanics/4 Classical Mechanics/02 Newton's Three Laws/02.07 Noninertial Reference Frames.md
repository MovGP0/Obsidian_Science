---
title: "Noninertial Reference Frames"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In an accelerating or rotating reference frame, add apparent forces to apply Newton's second law in that frame.

In a noninertial frame $S'$, Newton's second law in the form $\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec a}$ requires additional *apparent forces*.

**Translating frame.** If $S'$ translates with acceleration $\mechanical{\vec A}$, the observer in $S'$ finds

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec a}\,'=\mechanical{\vec F}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec A}.
$$

The term $-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec A}$ is the apparent force due to the acceleration of the frame.

**Rotating frame.** In a frame that rotates with angular velocity $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$, the apparent forces include:

- *Centrifugal force*: $\mechanical{\vec F}_c=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times(\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec r}\,')$.
- *Coriolis force*: $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{Co}}=-2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec v}\,'$.

For constant $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ and a fixed origin, the equation of motion in $S'$ is

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec a}\,'=\mechanical{\vec F}
-2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec v}\,'
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times(\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec r}\,').
$$

**Meaning.** Apparent forces arise from the acceleration of the reference frame. An observer in $S'$ experiences their effects, as in a car going around a curve. These terms represent the change of frame rather than interactions between bodies.

### Example — Foucault Pendulum

A long pendulum can swing in any vertical plane. To an observer in a rotating laboratory on Earth's surface, its plane of oscillation turns at angular rate $\mechanical{\omega}_F=\mechanical{\omega}_T\sin\mechanical{\lambda}$, where $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is the geographic latitude. This shows the effect of the Coriolis force on a macroscopic scale. In Paris, at $\mechanical{\lambda}\approx48.85\unit{{}^\circ}$, $\mechanical{\omega}_F\approx\quantity{5.5\times10^{-5}}{rad\,s^{-1}}$, and the period is about $\quantity{32}{h}$.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source describes the plane of oscillation as fixed relative to the stars. This description applies at a pole in the ideal model. At other latitudes the local vertical also changes as Earth turns.

> [!note] Analytical Mechanics
> In the Lagrangian formulation, a Lagrangian written in generalized coordinates for a rotating frame produces the centrifugal and Coriolis terms directly. See [[10.11 Inertial Forces in the Lagrangian Formulation]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Net physical force | The sum of the forces due to physical interactions. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the body. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}, \mechanical{\vec a}\,'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The acceleration of the body in the unprimed and primed frames. |
| $\mechanical{\vec A}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Frame acceleration | The translational acceleration of the reference frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Frame angular velocity | The rate and axis of rotation of the moving frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}\,'$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The position of the body relative to the rotating origin. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}\,'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Relative velocity | The velocity of the body in the rotating frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_c, \mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{Co}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Apparent forces | The centrifugal and Coriolis terms. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}_T$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Earth's angular speed | The magnitude of Earth's angular velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}_F$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Foucault precession rate | The signed rate of rotation of the plane of oscillation relative to the laboratory. |
| $\mechanical{\lambda}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Geographic latitude | The angle north or south of the equator. |
| — | $\unit{s}$ | Precession period | The time for the plane of oscillation to make a full turn relative to the laboratory. |
