---
title: "Nonmechanical Forms of Energy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> When mechanical energy decreases, it can become thermal, acoustic, deformation, or electromagnetic energy.

Nonconservative forces can convert mechanical energy into other forms. The **first law of thermodynamics** extends the energy balance.

$$
\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{tot}}
=\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{mech}}+\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{int}}
=\thermal{Q}+\energy{W}_{\mathrm{ext}},
$$

where $\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{int}}$ is the change in internal energy, which includes thermal and binding energy. The heat exchanged with the surroundings is $\thermal{Q}$, and $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is work done by external forces. Both are positive for energy transferred into the system.

**Total energy conservation.** The energy balance accounts for all forms of energy. This chapter focuses on mechanical energy. Thermodynamics also treats forms such as thermal, chemical, and electromagnetic energy.

### Example — Braking a Car

A car of mass $\quantity{1500}{kg}$ moving at $\quantity{30}{m\,s^{-1}}$ brakes to a stop. Its kinetic energy changes by $\Delta\energy{K}=-\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2=\quantity{-675}{kJ}$. The brakes and tires dissipate this energy as heat. The source notes that brake disc temperatures can exceed $200\unit{{}^\circ C}$. To account for total energy, include the change in internal energy of the car, air, and road.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Change in total energy | The change in all forms of energy of the system. |
| $\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{mech}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Change in mechanical energy | The change in the sum of kinetic and potential energies. |
| $\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{int}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Change in internal energy | The change in thermal, binding, and other internal energy of the system. |
| $\thermal{Q}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Heat | Energy transferred into the system due to a temperature difference. |
| $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | External work | Energy transferred to the system by external forces. |
| $\Delta\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Change in kinetic energy | The decrease in the kinetic energy of the car. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the car. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The speed of the car before braking. |
| — | $\unit{{}^\circ C}$ | Brake disc temperature | The temperature the brake discs can reach during braking. |
