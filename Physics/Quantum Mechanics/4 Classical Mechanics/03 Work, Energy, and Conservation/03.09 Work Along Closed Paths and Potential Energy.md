---
title: "Work Along Closed Paths and Potential Energy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A force is conservative when its work around every closed path is zero. In a simply connected region, this is equivalent to zero curl for a differentiable force field.

Use any of these equivalent conditions to test whether a force $\mechanical{\vec F}$ is conservative:

1. There is a potential energy $\energy{U}$ such that $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\nabla\energy{U}$.
2. The work between two points depends only on those points.
3. For every closed path $\gamma$, $\oint_\gamma\mechanical{\vec F}\cdot d\mechanical{\vec r}=0$.

For a differentiable force field in a simply connected region, the equivalent differential condition is

$$
\nabla\times\mechanical{\vec F}=\vec 0.
$$

### Example — A Nonconservative Force

In the source's scaled coordinates, take $\mechanical{\vec F}=(-\mechanical{y},\mechanical{x},0)$. Its curl is $\nabla\times\mechanical{\vec F}=2\hat{k}\ne\vec 0$. Around a circle of radius $\mechanical{R}$ centered on the origin, traversed counterclockwise, the work is

$$
\oint\mechanical{\vec F}\cdot d\mechanical{\vec r}
=\oint(-\mechanical{y}\,dx+\mechanical{x}\,dy)
=2\pi\mechanical{R}^2\ne 0.
$$

The force is nonconservative. The example omits the dimensional proportionality constant between force and position.

### Example — Central Force and Conservativity

A central force of the form $\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{F}(\mechanical{r})\hat r$ is conservative. A potential is $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})=-\int\mechanical{F}(\mechanical{r})\,dr$. The zero curl also follows directly in spherical coordinates where the field is differentiable.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F},\mechanical{F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | The force field or the signed radial component of the central force. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | A scalar whose negative gradient gives the conservative force. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and radial distance | The position along the path and the distance from the center of force. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Cartesian coordinates | The position components in the plane of the closed path. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radius | The radius of the circular path. |
