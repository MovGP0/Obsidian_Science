---
title: "Power"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Instantaneous power is the rate at which a force does work.

The **power** is

$$
\energy{P}=\frac{d\energy{W}}{dt}=\mechanical{\vec F}\cdot\mechanical{\vec v}.
$$

The unit is the watt, $\unit{W}=\unit{J\,s^{-1}}$.

**Example.** A car moving horizontally at speed $\mechanical{v}$ must overcome drag $\mechanical{\vec F}_r$. The power needed is $\energy{P}=\mechanical{F}_r\mechanical{v}$. For constant drag magnitude $\mechanical{F}_r$, doubling the speed doubles the power needed.

**Aerodynamic drag.** If $\mechanical{F}_r\propto\mechanical{v}^2$, the power scales as $\mechanical{v}^3$. Tripling the speed requires 27 times the power.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{P}$ | $\unit{W}$ | Power | The rate of doing work. |
| $\energy{W}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Work | The energy transferred by a force. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The variable used to calculate the rate of doing work. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | The force that does work. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v},\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity and speed | The velocity of the body and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_r,\mechanical{F}_r$ | $\unit{N}$ | Drag force | The resistive force and its magnitude. |
