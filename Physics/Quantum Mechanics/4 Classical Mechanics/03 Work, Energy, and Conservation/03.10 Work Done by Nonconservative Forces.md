---
title: "Work Done by Nonconservative Forces"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The change in mechanical energy equals the work done by the nonconservative forces.

For nonconservative forces such as friction, include dissipation in the energy balance. The **generalized energy theorem** is

$$
\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{mech}}=\energy{W}_{\mathrm{nc}},
$$

where $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{nc}}$ is the work done by the nonconservative forces only. When $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{nc}}<0$, the mechanical energy decreases. For kinetic friction with constant normal force $\mechanical{N}$, $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{fr}}=-\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{N}\mechanical{s}$, where $\mechanical{s}$ is the distance traveled along the surface.

### Example — Diver Entering a Pool

A diver of mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{70}{kg}$ jumps into a pool from height $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{10}{m}$. With air resistance neglected, mechanical energy is conserved on the way down. Drag slows the diver to rest in the water. The source estimates the work of drag as $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{visc}}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}=\quantity{-6.87}{kJ}$. This energy goes into heat and turbulent flow in the water.

> [!note] Source model
> This estimate uses the energy at water entry and neglects additional work by weight and buoyancy during the underwater part of the motion.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{E}_{\mathrm{mech}},\Delta\energy{E}_{\mathrm{mech}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Mechanical energy | The sum of kinetic and potential energy and its change. |
| $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{nc}},\energy{W}_{\mathrm{fr}},\energy{W}_{\mathrm{visc}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Nonconservative work | Work done by nonconservative forces, kinetic friction, or viscous drag. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}_d$ | 1 | Coefficient of kinetic friction | The ratio of kinetic friction magnitude to normal force. |
| $\mechanical{N}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Normal force | The contact force perpendicular to the surface. |
| $\mechanical{s}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Distance traveled | The length of the path along the friction surface. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the diver. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Initial height | The height above the surface of the pool. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
