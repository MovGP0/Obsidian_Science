> [!TLDR]
> A plot of potential energy and a horizontal line for total energy show where motion is possible.

For a one-dimensional system with potential energy $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})$, conservation of energy gives

$$
\frac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot x}^{\,2}+\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=\energy{E}.
$$

To analyze the motion qualitatively, draw $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})$ and a horizontal line at $\energy{E}$.

- Where $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})<\energy{E}$, the region is accessible.
- Where $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=\energy{E}$, the velocity $\mechanical{\dot x}$ is zero. The intersections can be turning points.
- Where $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})>\energy{E}$, classical motion is forbidden.

**Equilibria.** A point $\mechanical{x}_0$ where $\energy{U}'(\mechanical{x}_0)=0$ is an equilibrium.

- If $\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)>0$, the point is a stable minimum. Small oscillations have angular frequency $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)/\mechanical{m}}$.
- If $\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)<0$, the point is an unstable maximum. A small perturbation can grow exponentially.
- If $\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)=0$, examine the higher-order terms to determine stability.

### Example — Harmonic Oscillator

The potential energy is $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{x}^2$. At energy $\energy{E}$, the turning points are $\mechanical{x}_\pm=\pm\sqrt{2\energy{E}/\mechanical{k}}$. The motion has period $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{k}}$. See [[04.02 Simple Harmonic Oscillator]].

> [!note] Analytical Mechanics
> Energy conservation is an example of [[11.07 Noether's Theorem|Noether's theorem]]. It follows from invariance under time translations. Momentum conservation follows from invariance under spatial translations, and angular momentum conservation follows from invariance under rotations. Chapter 11 develops these relations.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{x}_0,\mechanical{x}_\pm$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The particle coordinate, an equilibrium position, or a turning point. |
| $\mechanical{\dot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The rate of change of position. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The energy associated with the particle's position. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Mechanical energy | The constant sum of kinetic and potential energies. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The angular frequency of small oscillations about a stable minimum. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The stiffness of the harmonic oscillator. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The time for one complete oscillation. |
