---
title: "Gravitational Forces and Potential"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Newtonian gravitation is conservative. With zero potential energy at infinity, the potential energy of two masses is negative.

A mass $\mechanical{M}$ exerts the force $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}\hat r/\mechanical{r}^2$ on a mass $\mechanical{m}$ at displacement $\mechanical{\vec r}$. The force is conservative, with potential energy

$$
\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})=-\frac{\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}}{\mechanical{r}}.
$$

The convention $\energy{U}(\infty)=0$ makes the potential energy negative at all finite distances. In the point-mass model it tends to $-\infty$ as the separation tends to zero.

**Binding energy.** For a mass initially at rest at distance $\mechanical{r}$, the binding energy is $\energy{E}_{\mathrm{bind}}=\lvert\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})\rvert=\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{r}$. This is the minimum energy needed to take the mass to infinity.

**Escape speed.** Set $\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{r}=0$ to obtain

$$
\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{\frac{2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}}}.
$$

The escape speed is approximately $\quantity{11.2}{km\,s^{-1}}$ at Earth's surface and $\quantity{618}{km\,s^{-1}}$ at the Sun's surface.

> [!note] Source analogy
> The source describes a black hole by saying that the escape speed exceeds $c$ and neither matter nor light can escape. The Newtonian escape-speed expression is an analogy. In general relativity, the event horizon is the boundary from inside which light and matter cannot reach a distant observer.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The source and test masses. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The displacement from the source to the test mass and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Gravitational force | The attractive force on the test mass. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant in Newton's law of gravitation. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Gravitational potential energy | The interaction energy of the two masses, measured relative to infinite separation. |
| $\energy{E}_{\mathrm{bind}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Binding energy | The energy required to take the mass from rest at the specified radius to infinity. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Mechanical energy | The sum of the kinetic and gravitational potential energies. |
| $\mechanical{v},\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed and escape speed | The speed of the test mass and the threshold for escape. |
| $c$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | The speed of light in vacuum. |
