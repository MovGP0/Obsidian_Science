---
title: "Damped Oscillator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Linear damping causes a free oscillator to lose amplitude. The damping rate determines whether it returns to equilibrium with or without oscillations.

With a friction force $-\mechanical{b}\mechanical{\dot x}$ proportional to velocity, the equation of motion is

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot x}+\mechanical{b}\mechanical{\dot x}+\mechanical{k}\mechanical{x}=0,
\qquad
\mechanical{\ddot x}+2\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{\dot x}+\signal{\omega}_0^2\mechanical{x}=0,
$$

where $\signal{\gamma}=\mechanical{b}/(2\mechanical{m})$ and $\signal{\omega}_0^2=\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}$. The characteristic equation $\lambda^2+2\signal{\gamma}\lambda+\signal{\omega}_0^2=0$ has roots $\lambda=-\signal{\gamma}\pm\sqrt{\signal{\gamma}^2-\signal{\omega}_0^2}$.

**Underdamping.** For $\signal{\gamma}<\signal{\omega}_0$, the roots are $\lambda=-\signal{\gamma}\pm i\signal{\omega}_d$ with $\signal{\omega}_d=\sqrt{\signal{\omega}_0^2-\signal{\gamma}^2}$. The solution is

$$
\mechanical{x}(t)=\mechanical{A}e^{-\signal{\gamma}t}\cos(\signal{\omega}_dt+\signal{\varphi}).
$$

The amplitude decays exponentially with characteristic time $1/\signal{\gamma}$. The quality factor is $\signal{Q}=\signal{\omega}_0/(2\signal{\gamma})$.

**Critical damping.** For $\signal{\gamma}=\signal{\omega}_0$, the solution is $\mechanical{x}(t)=(\mechanical{A}+\mechanical{B}t)e^{-\signal{\gamma}t}$. Critical damping gives the fastest asymptotic return to equilibrium among the nonoscillatory regimes for a given natural frequency, for typical initial conditions. It is useful in applications such as automatic doors and suspensions.

**Overdamping.** For $\signal{\gamma}>\signal{\omega}_0$, the roots are two distinct negative real numbers. The system returns to equilibrium without oscillating, generally more slowly than in the critically damped case.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical{b}$ | $\unit{kg\,s^{-1}}$ | Linear damping coefficient | The proportionality between friction force magnitude and speed. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The stiffness of the spring. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement | The signed position relative to equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{\dot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The rate of change of displacement. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The rate of change of velocity. |
| $\signal{\gamma}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Damping rate | The exponential decay rate in the underdamped solution. |
| $\signal{\omega}_0$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Undamped angular frequency | The natural angular frequency with zero damping. |
| $\signal{\omega}_d$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Damped angular frequency | The angular frequency in the underdamped regime. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement coefficient | The initial amplitude factor or the constant term in the critically damped solution. |
| $\mechanical{B}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Linear coefficient | The coefficient of time in the critically damped solution. |
| $\signal{\varphi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Initial phase | The phase of the underdamped oscillation at zero time. |
| $\signal{Q}$ | 1 | Quality factor | The ratio of undamped angular frequency to twice the damping rate. |
