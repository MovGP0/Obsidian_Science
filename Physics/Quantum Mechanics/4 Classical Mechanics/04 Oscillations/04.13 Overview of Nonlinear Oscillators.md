---
title: "Overview of Nonlinear Oscillators"
---

> [!TLDR]
> At finite amplitudes, terms beyond the harmonic approximation can make the period depend on amplitude.

At finite amplitudes, real potentials can differ from the harmonic approximation. The period can then change with amplitude, and the oscillator loses isochronism.

**Duffing oscillator.** The equation is $\mechanical{\ddot x}+\signal{\omega}_0^2\mechanical{x}+\beta\mechanical{x}^3=0$. If $\beta>0$, the potential is stiffer at large displacements and the period decreases with amplitude $\mechanical{A}$. If $\beta<0$, it is softer and the period increases with amplitude in the range of bounded oscillation.

**Nonlinear pendulum.** The exact equation is $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})\sin\mechanical{\theta}=0$. The period at amplitude $\mechanical{\theta}_0$ is

$$
\signal{T}(\mechanical{\theta}_0)
=\frac{2\signal{T}_0}{\pi}K\!\left(\sin(\mechanical{\theta}_0/2)\right),
$$

where $K$ is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind, with its argument given as the modulus. As $\mechanical{\theta}_0\to\pi$, the period diverges logarithmically.

### Example — Pendulum at 60 Degrees

For $\mechanical{\theta}_0=60\unit{{}^\circ}=\pi/3$ in radians, $\sin(\mechanical{\theta}_0/2)=0.5$, and $K(0.5)\approx1.686$. The period ratio is $\signal{T}(\pi/3)/\signal{T}_0=2K(0.5)/\pi\approx1.073$. This is a $7.3\%$ increase over the small-amplitude period.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement and amplitude | The Duffing oscillator's position relative to equilibrium and its maximum magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The second time derivative of displacement. |
| $\signal{\omega}_0$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Natural angular frequency | The frequency parameter of the linear term. |
| $\beta$ | $\unit{m^{-2}\,s^{-2}}$ | Cubic coefficient | The coefficient of the cubic restoring acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\theta}_0$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Pendulum angle and amplitude | The angular displacement from the downward vertical and its maximum magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The second time derivative of the pendulum angle. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum length | The distance from the pivot to the bob. |
| $\signal{T},\signal{T}_0$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The period at the stated amplitude and its small-amplitude limit. |
