---
title: "Harmonic Approximation for Small Oscillations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The curvature of the potential at a nondegenerate minimum determines the angular frequency of small oscillations.

For a one-dimensional system with a minimum of $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})$ at $\mechanical{x}_0$, $\energy{U}'(\mechanical{x}_0)=0$. The Taylor expansion is

$$
\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=\energy{U}(\mechanical{x}_0)
+\tfrac12\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_0)^2+\cdots.
$$

For small displacements $\mechanical{\xi}=\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_0$, the equation to first order is

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot\xi}=-\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)\mechanical{\xi}.
$$

When the curvature is positive, this is a harmonic oscillator with $\mechanical{k}=\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)$ and $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)/\mechanical{m}}$. The angular frequency depends on the local curvature of the potential.

### Example — Simple Pendulum

For a pendulum of length $\mechanical{\ell}$ in a uniform gravitational field, let $\mechanical{\theta}$ be the angle from the downward vertical. The potential energy is $\energy{U}(\mechanical{\theta})=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})$. At small angles,

$$
\energy{U}(\mechanical{\theta})\approx\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\mechanical{\theta}^2.
$$

The kinetic energy is $\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}$. The equation of motion is

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\mechanical{\ddot\theta}
=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\sin\mechanical{\theta}
\approx-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\mechanical{\theta}.
$$

The small-angle result is a harmonic oscillator with $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell}}$ and $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{\ell}/\mechanical{g}}$.

**Amplitude effect.** At finite angular amplitude $\mechanical{\theta}_0$, the period depends on amplitude. The expansion gives an anharmonic correction.

$$
\signal{T}(\mechanical{\theta}_0)=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{\ell}}{\mechanical{g}}}
\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{\theta}_0^2}{16}+\frac{11\mechanical{\theta}_0^4}{3072}+\cdots\right).
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The energy associated with the system's position. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{x}_0,\mechanical{\xi}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and displacement | The position, its equilibrium value, and the difference from equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\xi}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The second time derivative of the displacement. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillating body. |
| $\mechanical{k},\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Effective spring constant | The curvature of the potential at the equilibrium position. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Natural angular frequency | The angular frequency in the small-oscillation approximation. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum length | The distance from the pivot to the bob. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\theta}_0$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Pendulum angle and amplitude | The instantaneous and maximum angular displacement from the downward vertical. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The energy associated with the motion of the bob. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of the pendulum angle. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The rate of change of angular velocity. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The time for one complete pendulum oscillation. |
