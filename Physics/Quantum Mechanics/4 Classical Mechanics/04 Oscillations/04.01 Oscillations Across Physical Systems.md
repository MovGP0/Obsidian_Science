---
title: "Oscillations Across Physical Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Near a potential minimum with positive curvature, the quadratic term gives harmonic motion for sufficiently small amplitudes.

The harmonic oscillator is the basic model for dynamics near a stable equilibrium with nonzero restoring stiffness. It describes systems with different physical mechanisms.

**General argument.** Let the potential $\energy{U}(q)$ have a minimum at $q_0$. Its Taylor expansion is

$$
\energy{U}(q)=\energy{U}(q_0)+\tfrac12\energy{U}''(q_0)(q-q_0)^2+\cdots.
$$

Cubic, quartic, and higher-order terms are small at small amplitudes. When the quadratic coefficient is positive, the harmonic term dominates the dynamics.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source applies the harmonic approximation to every stable equilibrium. A minimum with zero quadratic curvature needs a higher-order analysis.

**Examples that reduce to harmonic oscillators.**

- A pendulum at small amplitude.
- Vibrations of a diatomic molecule about its equilibrium separation.
- Vibrations of crystal lattices, described by phonons when quantized.
- Waves on strings, on membranes, and in fluids.
- Oscillations in LC circuits.
- Oscillations of a single mode of the electromagnetic field.

In quantum mechanics, quantizing the harmonic oscillator gives discrete energy levels $\energy{E}_n=\hbar\signal{\omega}(n+1/2)$. These levels form a basis for molecular spectroscopy and quantum field theory.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The energy associated with the generalized coordinate. |
| $q,q_0$ | — | Generalized coordinate | The coordinate that describes the displacement and its value at equilibrium. |
| $\energy{U}''(q_0)$ | — | Potential curvature | The second derivative of potential energy at equilibrium. Its unit depends on the coordinate. |
| $\energy{E}_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy level | The energy of oscillator state $n$. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The quantum of action divided by $2\pi$. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The oscillator's natural angular frequency. |
