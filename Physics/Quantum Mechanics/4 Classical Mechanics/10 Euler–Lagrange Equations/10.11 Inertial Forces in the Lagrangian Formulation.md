---
title: "Inertial Forces in the Lagrangian Formulation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> When the coordinates move with a noninertial frame, their time dependence produces inertial terms in the equations of motion.

Inertial forces arise naturally in the Lagrangian formulation of motion in a noninertial frame.

## Particle in a Rotating Frame

Describe a free particle in rotating planar polar coordinates $(\mechanical{\rho},\mechanical{\phi})$ with constant frame angular speed $\mechanical{\Omega}$. Its laboratory coordinates are $(\mechanical{\rho},\mechanical{\phi}+\mechanical{\Omega}t)$. Thus $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{lab}}^2=\mechanical{\dot\rho}^{\,2}+\mechanical{\rho}^2(\mechanical{\dot\phi}+\mechanical{\Omega})^2$, and

$$
\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}
[\mechanical{\dot\rho}^{\,2}+\mechanical{\rho}^2(\mechanical{\dot\phi}+\mechanical{\Omega})^2].
$$

The radial derivatives are $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{\dot\rho}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot\rho}$ and $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{\rho}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\rho}(\mechanical{\dot\phi}+\mechanical{\Omega})^2$. The radial equation $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot\rho}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\rho}(\mechanical{\dot\phi}+\mechanical{\Omega})^2$ contains the centrifugal term $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{\Omega}^2$ and the Coriolis term $2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{\dot\phi}\mechanical{\Omega}$.

If $\mechanical{\dot\phi}=0$ instantaneously, $\mechanical{\ddot\rho}=\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{\Omega}^2$ is outward. When $\mechanical{\dot\phi}\ne0$, the radial Coriolis term also contributes. The Lagrangian produces both terms directly.

## Particle in an Electromagnetic Field

For charge $\electric{q}$, scalar potential $\electric{\Phi}$, and vector potential $\magnetic{\vec A}$, use

$$
\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2-\electric{q}\electric{\Phi}
+\electric{q}\mechanical{\vec v}\cdot\magnetic{\vec A}.
$$

The Euler–Lagrange equations give the Lorentz force $\mechanical{\vec F}=\electric{q}(\electric{\vec E}+\mechanical{\vec v}\times\magnetic{\vec B})$, where $\electric{\vec E}=-\nabla\electric{\Phi}-\partial\magnetic{\vec A}/\partial t$ and $\magnetic{\vec B}=\nabla\times\magnetic{\vec A}$. The magnetic force follows from a generalized potential that depends on velocity.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\rho},\mechanical{\dot\rho},\mechanical{\ddot\rho}$ | $\unit{m},\unit{m\,s^{-1}},\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Radial quantities | The radial coordinate and its time derivatives. |
| $\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Polar angle | The angle in the rotating frame. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\phi},\mechanical{\Omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular rates | The relative angular rate and the rotation rate of the frame. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for motion and field changes. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{lab}},\mechanical{v},\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed and velocity | The speed or velocity in the inertial frame. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The function used to obtain the equations of motion. |
| $\electric{q}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Electric charge | The charge of the particle. |
| $\electric{\Phi}$ | $\unit{V}$ | Electric potential | The scalar electromagnetic potential. |
| $\magnetic{\vec A}$ | $\unit{T\,m}$ | Magnetic vector potential | The vector potential whose curl is the magnetic field. |
| $\electric{\vec E},\magnetic{\vec B}$ | $\unit{V\,m^{-1}},\unit{T}$ | Electromagnetic fields | The electric field and magnetic flux density. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Lorentz force | The electromagnetic force on the particle. |
