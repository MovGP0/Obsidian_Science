title: "Representative Examples of Lagrangian Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Write kinetic and potential energy in the independent coordinates to obtain the equations of motion.

## Simple Pendulum

Use $q=\mechanical{\theta}$. The position is $\mechanical{\vec r}=(\mechanical{\ell}\sin\mechanical{\theta},-\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta})$, and the velocity is $\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\ell}\mechanical{\dot\theta}(\cos\mechanical{\theta},\sin\mechanical{\theta})$. Thus

$$
\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2},
\quad
\energy{U}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta},
\quad
\energy{L}=\energy{T}-\energy{U}.
$$

The Euler–Lagrange equation gives

$$
\frac{d}{dt}(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\mechanical{\dot\theta})
+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\sin\mechanical{\theta}=0.
$$

Therefore $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})\sin\mechanical{\theta}=0$.

## Central Motion in Polar Coordinates

A particle of mass $\mechanical{m}$ moves in $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})$. In coordinates $(\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\phi})$, the squared speed is $\mechanical{v}^2=\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}+\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}^{\,2}$. Thus

$$
\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}+\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}^{\,2})-\energy{U}(\mechanical{r}).
$$

- The radial derivatives are $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{\dot r}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot r}$ and $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\dot\phi}^{\,2}-\energy{U}'(\mechanical{r})$. Thus $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot r}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\dot\phi}^{\,2}=-\energy{U}'(\mechanical{r})$.
- The angular derivatives are $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{\dot\phi}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}$ and $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{\phi}=0$. Thus $\frac{d}{dt}(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi})=0$.

This gives the radial equation and conservation of $\mechanical{L}_z=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}$ from Chapter 6.

## Planar Double Pendulum

Two point masses $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ are connected by ideal rods of lengths $\mechanical{\ell}_1,\mechanical{\ell}_2$. With angles $\mechanical{\theta}_1,\mechanical{\theta}_2$ from the downward vertical,

$$
\mechanical{\vec r}_1=(\mechanical{\ell}_1\sin\mechanical{\theta}_1,-\mechanical{\ell}_1\cos\mechanical{\theta}_1),
\qquad
\mechanical{\vec r}_2=\mechanical{\vec r}_1+
(\mechanical{\ell}_2\sin\mechanical{\theta}_2,-\mechanical{\ell}_2\cos\mechanical{\theta}_2).
$$

The velocities are $\mechanical{\vec v}_1=\mechanical{\ell}_1\mechanical{\dot\theta}_1(\cos\mechanical{\theta}_1,\sin\mechanical{\theta}_1)$ and $\mechanical{\vec v}_2=\mechanical{\vec v}_1+\mechanical{\ell}_2\mechanical{\dot\theta}_2(\cos\mechanical{\theta}_2,\sin\mechanical{\theta}_2)$. The mixed term in $\mechanical{v}_2^2$ contains $\cos\mechanical{\theta}_1\cos\mechanical{\theta}_2+\sin\mechanical{\theta}_1\sin\mechanical{\theta}_2=\cos(\mechanical{\theta}_1-\mechanical{\theta}_2)$. The energies are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{T}&=\tfrac12(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{\ell}_1^2\mechanical{\dot\theta}_1^2
+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{\ell}_2^2\mechanical{\dot\theta}_2^2\\
&\quad+\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{\ell}_1\mechanical{\ell}_2
\mechanical{\dot\theta}_1\mechanical{\dot\theta}_2\cos(\mechanical{\theta}_1-\mechanical{\theta}_2),\\
\energy{U}&=-(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}_1\cos\mechanical{\theta}_1
-\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}_2\cos\mechanical{\theta}_2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Obtain the two Euler–Lagrange equations by differentiation of $\energy{L}=\energy{T}-\energy{U}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\theta},q$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Pendulum angle | The generalized coordinate measured from the downward vertical. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of the pendulum angle. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The rate of change of angular velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\ell},\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length and position | The cord length and the bob position relative to the pivot. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The velocity of the bob. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the bob. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward gravitational acceleration. |
| $\energy{T},\energy{U},\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energies and Lagrangian | The kinetic energy, potential energy, and their difference. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance | The distance to the center of force. |
| $\mechanical{\phi},\mechanical{\theta}_1,\mechanical{\theta}_2$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The polar angle or the double pendulum angles. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\phi},\mechanical{\dot\theta}_1,\mechanical{\dot\theta}_2$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rates of change of the angles. |
| $\mechanical{\dot r},\mechanical{v},\mechanical{\vec v}_1,\mechanical{\vec v}_2,\mechanical{v}_2$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity and speed | The radial velocity and the speeds or velocities of the particles. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot r}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Radial coordinate acceleration | The second time derivative of the radial distance. |
| $\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The conserved component perpendicular to the plane. |
| $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The two double pendulum masses. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}_1,\mechanical{\ell}_2,\mechanical{\vec r}_1,\mechanical{\vec r}_2$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length and position | The rod lengths and positions of the pendulum masses. |
