---
title: "Lagrangian and Symmetries"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Continuous symmetries of the action give conserved quantities through Noether's theorem.

The Lagrangian formulation makes symmetry and conservation laws explicit. Each continuous variational symmetry gives a conserved quantity through [[11.07 Noether's Theorem]].

**Common symmetries.**

- **Spatial translation.** Translation invariance gives conservation of total momentum $\mechanical{\vec P}$.
- **Rotation.** Rotation invariance gives conservation of total angular momentum $\mechanical{\vec L}$.
- **Time translation.** If $\energy{L}$ does not depend explicitly on $t$, the energy function $\energy{E}$ is conserved.
- **Galilean boost.** If the transformation $\mechanical{\vec r}\to\mechanical{\vec r}+\mechanical{\vec V}t$ changes $\energy{L}$ by at most a total derivative as in an isolated Galilean system, the center of mass moves at constant velocity.

**Cyclic coordinates.** If $\partial\energy{L}/\partial q_k=0$, then $p_k$ is constant.

Look for cyclic coordinates before writing all the equations. Each one gives a first integral.

**Central motion.** For $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}+\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}^{\,2})-\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})$, $\mechanical{\phi}$ is cyclic and $\mechanical{p}_\phi=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}=\mechanical{L}_z$ is constant. The radial equation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot r}=\mechanical{L}_z^2/(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^3)-\energy{U}'(\mechanical{r})$. This is equivalent to one-dimensional motion in $\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\energy{U}+\mechanical{L}_z^2/(2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2)$.

**Symmetric top.** For a symmetric top with a fixed point in vertical gravity, the Euler angles $\mechanical{\phi}$ about the vertical and $\mechanical{\psi}$ about the symmetry axis are cyclic. Their momenta $\mechanical{p}_\phi,\mechanical{p}_\psi$ are conserved. This leaves a one-dimensional integrable equation for $\mechanical{\theta}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{L},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian and energy function | The dynamical function and the conserved quantity for time translation. |
| $\mechanical{\vec P}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Total momentum | The conserved quantity for spatial translation. |
| $\mechanical{\vec L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Total angular momentum | The conserved quantity for rotation. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{\vec V}$ | $\unit{m},\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Position and boost velocity | The position and constant velocity used in a Galilean transformation. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable. |
| $q_k,p_k$ | — | Coordinate and conjugate momentum | The generalized coordinate and the momentum conserved when it is cyclic. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\dot r},\mechanical{\ddot r}$ | $\unit{m},\unit{m\,s^{-1}},\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Radial quantities | The radial coordinate and its first two time derivatives. |
| $\mechanical{\phi},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\psi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The polar or Euler angles for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\phi}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of the polar angle. |
| $\mechanical{p}_\phi,\mechanical{p}_\psi,\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momenta | The conjugate momenta of the cyclic angles. |
| $\energy{U},\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The physical and effective radial potentials. |
