---
title: "Motivation and Overview of Constrained Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Generalized coordinates and virtual work simplify dynamics when direct calculation of constraint forces is difficult.

The earlier chapters described motion in Cartesian coordinates and wrote the constraints as explicit equations. The approach works for simple problems such as an incline, an ideal cord, or a pendulum. With many constraints, the equations become difficult because they include the forces that enforce the constraints.

Analytical mechanics uses two main ideas:

1. Use generalized coordinates adapted to the geometry of the problem that automatically satisfy the constraints.
2. Write the equations of motion so that ideal constraint forces do not appear. Calculate those forces afterward if needed.

This chapter introduces constraints and degrees of freedom, virtual displacements, the principle of virtual work, and d’Alembert’s equation. These concepts prepare for the Euler–Lagrange equations, in which generalized forces appear.
