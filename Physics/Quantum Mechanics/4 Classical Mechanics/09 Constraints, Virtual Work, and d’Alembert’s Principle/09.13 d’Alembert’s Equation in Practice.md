title: "d’Alembert’s Equation in Practice"
---

> [!TLDR]
> d’Alembert’s equation gives the acceleration of a constrained system without solving for ideal reactions.

In practice, the Lagrange equations are used more often than d’Alembert’s equation itself. The direct method is useful for learning to calculate acceleration without writing constraint forces explicitly. See [[10.03 Derivation of Lagrange's Equations]].

### Example — Inclined Atwood Machine

A mass $\mechanical{m}_1$ slides on an incline of angle $\mechanical{\alpha}$. An ideal cord over a pulley at the top connects it to a hanging mass $\mechanical{m}_2$. Use position $\mechanical{s}$ of $\mechanical{m}_1$ along the incline, increasing uphill, and let $\hat u$ point uphill. The constant cord length gives virtual displacements $\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_1=\delta\mechanical{s}\hat u$ and $\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_2=-\delta\mechanical{s}\hat{\jmath}$ with $\hat{\jmath}$ vertically upward.

The active forces are $\mechanical{\vec F}_i^{\,\mathrm{act}}=-\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{g}\hat{\jmath}$. Use $\mechanical{\vec a}_1=\mechanical{\ddot s}\hat u$ and $\mechanical{\vec a}_2=-\mechanical{\ddot s}\hat{\jmath}$ in d’Alembert’s equation. This gives

$$
(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{\ddot s}
=(\mechanical{m}_2-\mechanical{m}_1\sin\mechanical{\alpha})\mechanical{g}.
$$

The normal force and tension do not enter this calculation.

> [!note] Source notation
> The source writes $\mechanical{\vec a}_1=\mechanical{\ddot s}\hat{\jmath}$. The first mass accelerates along the incline, so its acceleration uses the uphill unit vector $\hat u$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}_i,\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The masses on the incline and cord. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Inclination | The angle of the plane above horizontal. |
| $\mechanical{s},\delta\mechanical{s}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Along-slope position and variation | The coordinate of the first mass and its virtual change. |
| $\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_1,\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_2$ | $\unit{m}$ | Virtual displacement | The allowed changes of position of the two masses. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_i^{\,\mathrm{act}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Active force | The weight of mass $i$. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward gravitational acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}_1,\mechanical{\vec a}_2,\mechanical{\ddot s}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The acceleration vectors or their signed value along the cord. |
