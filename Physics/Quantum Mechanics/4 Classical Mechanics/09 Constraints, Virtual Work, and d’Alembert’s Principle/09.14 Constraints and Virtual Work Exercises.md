---
title: "Constraints and Virtual Work Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Identify constraints and use virtual work to calculate equilibria and constrained motion.

## Exercise 1

**Basic.** How many degrees of freedom does a double pendulum have in a plane and in three-dimensional space?

**Result.** Two in a plane and four in space.

## Exercise 2

**Basic.** Apply virtual work to a first-class lever with arm lengths $\mechanical{\ell}_1=\quantity{0.30}{m}$ and $\mechanical{\ell}_2=\quantity{1.20}{m}$. A weight $\mechanical{P}=\quantity{200}{N}$ acts on the short arm. Find the minimum force on the long arm that gives equilibrium.

**Result.** For perpendicular application, $\mechanical{F}=\mechanical{P}\mechanical{\ell}_1/\mechanical{\ell}_2=\quantity{50}{N}$.

## Exercise 3

**Basic.** Does a ball rolling without slipping on a plane have a holonomic or nonholonomic constraint?

**Result.** Nonholonomic. The rolling condition links the horizontal components of $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ to the two components of $\mechanical{\vec V}_{\mathrm{CM}}$ in a nonintegrable way.

The reduced case of a disk constrained to roll along a straight line is holonomic.

## Exercise 4

**Intermediate.** Find the constraints and degrees of freedom of a disk of radius $\mechanical{R}$ rolling without slipping along a horizontal straight line.

**Result.** Use rotation angle $\mechanical{\theta}$ and center position $\mechanical{x}$, with $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\theta}$ for coincident zero values. There is one degree of freedom.

## Exercise 5

**Intermediate.** Two masses are connected by an inextensible cord over a fixed pulley. Give a holonomic constraint, generalized coordinates, and the number of degrees of freedom.

**Result.** $\mechanical{y}_1+\mechanical{y}_2$ is constant. There is one free coordinate and one degree of freedom.

## Exercise 6

**Intermediate.** Calculate the generalized forces $Q_{\theta_1}$ and $Q_{\theta_2}$ for a double pendulum with weights on both rods.

## Exercise 7

**Intermediate.** A particle lies on the helicoid $\mechanical{z}=c\mechanical{\phi}$ in cylindrical coordinates $(\mechanical{\rho},\mechanical{\phi},\mechanical{z})$. Find its degrees of freedom and generalized coordinates.

**Result.** There are two degrees of freedom. Use $q_1=\mechanical{\rho}$ and $q_2=\mechanical{\phi}$. The height follows from these coordinates.

## Exercise 8

**Challenge.** For a system with holonomic constraints and forces derived from a potential, show that equilibrium requires $\partial\energy{U}/\partial q_k=0$ for each independent generalized coordinate.

## Exercise 9

**Intermediate.** A ball starts near rest at the top of a smooth hemisphere of radius $\mechanical{R}$. At what angle from the vertical does it leave the surface in the limit of release at the top?

**Result.** $\cos\mechanical{\theta}=2/3$ and $\mechanical{\theta}\approx48.2\unit{{}^\circ}$.

## Exercise 10

**Intermediate.** Use Lagrange multipliers to find the cord tension of a simple pendulum as a function of its angle.

**Result.** $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\ell}\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}+\mechanical{g}\cos\mechanical{\theta})$.

For ordinary oscillation of the pendulum, the tension is greatest at the bottom.

## Exercise 11

**Intermediate.** Find the constraint force on a particle of mass $\mechanical{m}$ on the smooth surface $\mechanical{z}=a\mechanical{x}^2+b\mechanical{y}^2$ at $(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})=(0,0)$ with velocity $\mechanical{\vec v}$.

**Result.** The normal curvature in the direction of motion is $\kappa=2(a\mechanical{v}_x^2+b\mechanical{v}_y^2)/\mechanical{v}^2$. The upward normal force is $\mechanical{N}=\mechanical{m}[\mechanical{g}+2(a\mechanical{v}_x^2+b\mechanical{v}_y^2)]$. For $\lvert\mechanical{v}_x\rvert=\lvert\mechanical{v}_y\rvert$, this reduces to $\mechanical{N}=\mechanical{m}[\mechanical{g}+(a+b)\mechanical{v}^2]$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\ell}_1,\mechanical{\ell}_2$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lever-arm length | The distance from the pivot to each force application point. |
| $\mechanical{P},\mechanical{F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Weight and force | The load and the balancing force. |
| $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rotation vector of the rolling ball. |
| $\mechanical{\vec V}_{\mathrm{CM}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Center-of-mass velocity | The velocity of the ball's center. |
| $\mechanical{R},\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}_1,\mechanical{y}_2,\mechanical{\rho},\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length and coordinates | The radius or particle coordinates in the stated example. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The rotation, detachment, or azimuthal angle. |
| $Q_{\theta_1},Q_{\theta_2}$ | $\unit{N\,m}$ | Generalized torques | The forces conjugate to the double pendulum angles. |
| $c$ | $\unit{m\,rad^{-1}}$ | Helicoid coefficient | The increase in height per unit azimuth. |
| $q_1,q_2,q_k$ | — | Generalized coordinates | Independent parameters describing the configuration. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The energy whose derivative gives the conservative generalized force. |
| $\mechanical{T}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Tension | The force of the cord on the pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum length | The distance from the pivot to the bob. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of the pendulum angle. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $a,b$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Surface coefficients | The quadratic coefficients of the surface height. |
| $\kappa$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Normal curvature | The surface curvature along the particle's motion at the origin. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v},\mechanical{v},\mechanical{v}_x,\mechanical{v}_y$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity and speed | The velocity vector, its magnitude, and the two horizontal components. |
| $\mechanical{N}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Normal force | The surface reaction at the origin. |
