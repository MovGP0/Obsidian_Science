---
title: "Holonomic and Nonholonomic Constraints"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A holonomic constraint relates positions and possibly time. A nonholonomic constraint cannot be reduced to such a relation.

> [!definition] Definition 9.1 — Holonomic Constraint
> For $N$ points at positions $\mechanical{\vec r}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{\vec r}_N$, a **holonomic constraint** has the form
>
> $$
> f(\mechanical{\vec r}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{\vec r}_N,t)=0.
> $$
>
> A constraint that cannot be reduced to this form is **nonholonomic**.

**Holonomic examples.**

- Simple pendulum: $\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2=\mechanical{\ell}^2$.
- Particle on a surface: $\mechanical{z}=f(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})$.
- Particle on a moving sphere of radius $\mechanical{a}$: $\lVert\mechanical{\vec r}-\mechanical{\vec R}(t)\rVert^2=\mechanical{a}^2$. Here $\mechanical{\vec R}(t)$ is the center position. The constraint is rheonomic because it depends explicitly on time.

**Nonholonomic examples.**

- A ball rolls without slipping on a plane. The rolling conditions relate its angular velocity to the translation of its center and cannot be integrated into configuration equations.
- A floor gives the inequality $\mechanical{z}\ge0$. The body cannot pass through the floor. This is a unilateral constraint, distinct from a nonintegrable velocity constraint.

Holonomic constraints are **scleronomic** when they are time-independent, as in $f(\mechanical{\vec r})=0$, and **rheonomic** when they depend explicitly on time.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}_i,\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{\vec R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The particle positions and the position of the moving sphere's center. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Cartesian coordinates | The components of position in the constraint equations. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable in a time-dependent constraint. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum length | The fixed distance from the pivot to the pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Sphere radius | The fixed distance from the sphere center to its surface. |
