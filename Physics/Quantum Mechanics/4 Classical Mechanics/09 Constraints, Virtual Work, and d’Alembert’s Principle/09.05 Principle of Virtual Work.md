---
title: "Principle of Virtual Work"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In static equilibrium under ideal bilateral constraints, the active forces do zero total work for every admissible virtual displacement.

> [!theorem] Theorem 9.1 — Principle of Virtual Work in Statics
> For a system under ideal constraints with bilateral virtual displacements, the condition for static equilibrium is
>
> $$
> \delta\energy{W}=\sum_i\mechanical{\vec F}_i^{\,\mathrm{act}}\cdot\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_i=0
> $$
>
> for every admissible virtual displacement.

**Ideal constraints.** Reactions $\mechanical{\vec\Phi}_i$ of ideal constraints do zero total virtual work, $\sum_i\mechanical{\vec\Phi}_i\cdot\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_i=0$. Sliding friction generally does virtual work. For ideal rolling without slipping on a fixed surface, the contact point has no allowed displacement relative to that surface while the rolling constraint is active. For unilateral frictionless contact, the reaction is normal to the constraint surface. See [[09.10 Unilateral Constraints and Inequalities]] for virtual displacements that can release contact.

### Example — Lever

For a rigid lever with a fixed pivot, forces $\mechanical{\vec F}_1$ and $\mechanical{\vec F}_2$ act tangentially at distances $\mechanical{\ell}_1$ and $\mechanical{\ell}_2$ with opposite torques. A virtual rotation $\delta\mechanical{\theta}$ gives $\delta\mechanical{s}_i=\mechanical{\ell}_i\delta\mechanical{\theta}$. The virtual work is $(\mechanical{F}_1\mechanical{\ell}_1-\mechanical{F}_2\mechanical{\ell}_2)\delta\mechanical{\theta}=0$. Thus $\mechanical{F}_1\mechanical{\ell}_1=\mechanical{F}_2\mechanical{\ell}_2$.

### Example — Weight on an Incline

A cord holds a body of weight $\mechanical{P}$ on a plane inclined at $\mechanical{\alpha}$. For an uphill virtual displacement $\delta\mechanical{s}$, gravity does virtual work $-\mechanical{P}\sin\mechanical{\alpha}\,\delta\mechanical{s}$. The cord does work $\mechanical{T}\delta\mechanical{s}$. In equilibrium, $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{P}\sin\mechanical{\alpha}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\delta\energy{W}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Virtual work | The work of the active forces along a virtual displacement. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_i^{\,\mathrm{act}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Active force | The applied force excluding the ideal constraint reaction. |
| $\mechanical{\vec\Phi}_i$ | $\unit{N}$ | Constraint reaction | The force enforcing an ideal constraint. |
| $\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_i,\delta\mechanical{s}_i,\delta\mechanical{s}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Virtual displacement | A displacement or its component along the allowed motion. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_1,\mechanical{\vec F}_2,\mechanical{F}_1,\mechanical{F}_2$ | $\unit{N}$ | Lever forces | The forces on the lever and their magnitudes. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}_1,\mechanical{\ell}_2$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lever arms | The distances from the pivot to the force application points. |
| $\delta\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The virtual rotation of the lever and the inclination of the plane. |
| $\mechanical{P},\mechanical{T}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Weight and tension | The weight of the body and the force of the cord. |
