---
title: "d’Alembert’s Equation in Symbolic Form"
---

> [!TLDR]
> d’Alembert’s principle applies virtual work to dynamics by including the inertial terms with the active forces.

> [!theorem] Theorem 9.2 — d’Alembert’s Equation
> For a moving system with ideal constraints,
>
> $$
> \sum_i(\mechanical{\vec F}_i^{\,\mathrm{act}}-\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec a}_i)
> \cdot\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_i=0
> $$
>
> for every virtual displacement compatible with the constraints.

> [!note]
> Subtract $\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec a}_i$ from the active force, or add the inertial term $-\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec a}_i$. The resulting virtual-work balance has the form of static equilibrium at the instantaneous configuration.

With suitable generalized coordinates, this equation gives the Lagrange equations. See [[10.03 Derivation of Lagrange's Equations]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_i^{\,\mathrm{act}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Active force | The applied force on particle $i$ apart from the ideal reaction. |
| $\mechanical{m}_i$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of particle $i$. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}_i$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The actual acceleration of particle $i$. |
| $\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_i$ | $\unit{m}$ | Virtual displacement | A displacement compatible with the constraints at fixed time. |
