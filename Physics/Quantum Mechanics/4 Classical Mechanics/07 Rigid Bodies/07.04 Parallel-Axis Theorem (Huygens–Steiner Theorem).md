---
title: "Parallel-Axis Theorem (Huygens–Steiner Theorem)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> To shift an inertia axis away from the center of mass, add the mass times the square of the distance between the parallel axes.

> [!theorem] Theorem 7.1 — Parallel-Axis Theorem
> Let $\mechanical{I}_G$ be the moment of inertia about an axis through the center of mass. For a parallel axis at distance $\mechanical{d}$,
>
> $$
> \mechanical{I}=\mechanical{I}_G+\mechanical{M}\mechanical{d}^2.
> $$

**Proof.** Let $\mechanical{\vec r}$ be the position relative to the center of mass, and let $\mechanical{\vec d}$ point from the new axis to the old axis. The distance from the new axis is $\lVert\mechanical{\vec r}_\perp+\mechanical{\vec d}\rVert$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{I}
&=\int\mechanical{\rho}\lVert\mechanical{\vec r}_\perp+\mechanical{\vec d}\rVert^2\,dV\\
&=\int\mechanical{\rho}
\bigl(\mechanical{r}_\perp^2+\mechanical{d}^2
+2\mechanical{\vec r}_\perp\cdot\mechanical{\vec d}\bigr)\,dV
=\mechanical{I}_G+\mechanical{M}\mechanical{d}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The mixed term vanishes because $\int\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{\vec r}_\perp\,dV=\vec 0$ by the definition of the center of mass.

### Example — Rod with an Axis Through One End

A uniform rod of length $\mechanical{L}$ has $\mechanical{I}_G=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{L}^2/12$ about the transverse axis through its center. For the parallel axis through one end, $\mechanical{d}=\mechanical{L}/2$ and $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{L}^2/12+\mechanical{M}(\mechanical{L}/2)^2=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{L}^2/3$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{I},\mechanical{I}_G$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia | The moment about the displaced or center-of-mass axis. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the body. |
| $\mechanical{d},\mechanical{\vec d}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Axis displacement | The perpendicular displacement between the axes and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{\vec r}_\perp,\mechanical{r}_\perp$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and projection | The position relative to the center of mass, its transverse projection, and that projection's magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The mass per unit volume. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Rod length | The distance between the rod's ends. |
