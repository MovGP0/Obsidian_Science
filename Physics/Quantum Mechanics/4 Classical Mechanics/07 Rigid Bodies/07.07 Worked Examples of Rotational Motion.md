---
title: "Worked Examples of Rotational Motion"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A reduction in moment of inertia increases angular speed when angular momentum is conserved. An external torque can also change the direction of the rotation axis.

## Skater Pulling in Their Arms

A skater rotates about a vertical axis. With arms extended, $\mechanical{I}_1=\quantity{4}{kg\,m^2}$ and $\mechanical{\omega}_1=\quantity{1}{rad\,s^{-1}}$. With arms pulled in, $\mechanical{I}_2=\quantity{1.5}{kg\,m^2}$. Conservation of $\mechanical{L}_z$ gives $\mechanical{\omega}_2=\mechanical{I}_1\mechanical{\omega}_1/\mechanical{I}_2\approx\quantity{2.67}{rad\,s^{-1}}$. The angular speed almost triples.

The rotational kinetic energy changes from $\energy{K}_1=\tfrac12\mechanical{I}_1\mechanical{\omega}_1^2=\quantity{2}{J}$ to $\energy{K}_2=\tfrac12\mechanical{I}_2\mechanical{\omega}_2^2\approx\quantity{5.33}{J}$. The skater's muscles do the work that supplies $\Delta\energy{K}=\quantity{3.33}{J}$.

## Angular Momentum During Stellar Collapse

In the source model, a star of mass $\mechanical{M}=10\mechanical{M}_\odot$ and radius $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{10^9}{m}$ collapses to a neutron star of radius $\mechanical{R}'=\quantity{10^4}{m}$, conserving mass and angular momentum. With the same inertia factor before and after, $\mechanical{I}/\mechanical{I}'=(\mechanical{R}/\mechanical{R}')^2=10^{10}$, so $\mechanical{\omega}'/\mechanical{\omega}=10^{10}$. An initial period of 30 days, or about $\quantity{2.6\times10^6}{s}$, becomes about $\quantity{2.6\times10^{-4}}{s}$, or $\quantity{0.3}{ms}$. The source compares this scale to a shortest observed pulsar period of about $\quantity{1.4}{ms}$ and attributes part of the difference to magnetic braking neglected by the model.

## Precession of Earth's Rotation Axis

Earth's rotation axis precesses around the normal to the ecliptic with a period of approximately 26,000 years. The Sun and Moon produce tidal torques on Earth's equatorial bulge. This causes the precession of the equinoxes, known since Hipparchus in antiquity. The angular frequency $\signal{\Omega}_{\mathrm{prec}}\approx2\pi/(26{,}000\,\unit{yr})$ can be calculated from rigid-body theory.
