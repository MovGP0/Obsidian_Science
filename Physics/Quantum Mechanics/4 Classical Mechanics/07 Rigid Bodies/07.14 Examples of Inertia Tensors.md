---
title: "Examples of Inertia Tensors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Symmetry often makes the inertia tensor diagonal in a convenient basis. In a rotated basis it can have nonzero products of inertia.

For a rigid body,

$$
\mechanical{I}_{ij}=\int\mechanical{\rho}(\mechanical{\vec r})
(\mechanical{r}^2\delta_{ij}-\mechanical{r}_i\mechanical{r}_j)\,dV.
$$

- **Uniform thin rod along $\hat z$, length $\mechanical{L}$.** Its linear mass density is $\mechanical{\lambda}=\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{L}$. At its center, $\mechanical{I}_{zz}=0$ when the rod thickness is neglected and $\mechanical{I}_{xx}=\mechanical{I}_{yy}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{L}^2/12$. The tensor is diagonal, with the same principal moment about every transverse axis.
- **Uniform disk in the $xy$ plane.** About axes through its center, $\mechanical{I}_{zz}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2/2$ and $\mechanical{I}_{xx}=\mechanical{I}_{yy}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2/4$. The perpendicular-axis theorem for a planar body gives $\mechanical{I}_z=\mechanical{I}_x+\mechanical{I}_y$.
- **Uniform cube of side $\mechanical{L}$.** For centered axes parallel to its edges, $\mechanical{I}_{xx}=\mechanical{I}_{yy}=\mechanical{I}_{zz}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{L}^2/6$. The tensor is a multiple of the identity. As for a uniform sphere, any orthonormal basis gives principal axes.
- **Uniform rectangular block of sides $\mechanical{a}\times\mechanical{b}\times\mechanical{c}$.** For centered axes parallel to the edges, the tensor is diagonal. For example, $\mechanical{I}_{xx}=\mechanical{M}(\mechanical{b}^2+\mechanical{c}^2)/12$. Permute the sides to obtain the other moments.

### Example — Tilted Rod in Laboratory Coordinates

A uniform rod is tilted by $45\unit{{}^\circ}$ to the laboratory axis $\hat z$. In laboratory coordinates, the off-diagonal tensor elements represent products of inertia. When $\mechanical{\vec\omega}=\mechanical{\omega}\hat z$, the angular momentum $\mechanical{\vec L}=\mechanical{\mathbf I}\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ is not parallel to $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf I},\mechanical{I}_{ij}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Inertia tensor | The map from angular velocity to angular momentum and its matrix elements. |
| $\mechanical{I}_x,\mechanical{I}_y,\mechanical{I}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Axial moments | The moments of inertia about the specified axes. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{r}_i$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The position of a mass element, its magnitude, and its components. |
| $\mechanical{\rho},\mechanical{\lambda}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}},\unit{kg\,m^{-1}}$ | Mass density | The mass per unit volume or unit length. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The total mass of the body. |
| $\mechanical{L},\mechanical{R},\mechanical{a},\mechanical{b},\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Body dimensions | The length, radius, or side dimensions. |
| $\mechanical{\vec\omega},\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The angular velocity and its component along the laboratory axis. |
| $\mechanical{\vec L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The angular momentum of the rotating rod. |
| — | $\unit{rad}$ | Tilt angle | The angle between the rod and the laboratory axis. |
