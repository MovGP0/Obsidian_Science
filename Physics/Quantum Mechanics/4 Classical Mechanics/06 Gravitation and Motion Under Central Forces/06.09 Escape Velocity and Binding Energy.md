---
title: "Escape Velocity and Binding Energy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Escape requires enough kinetic energy to bring the total energy to zero. A circular orbit has negative total energy.

**Escape speed.** The minimum speed at radius $\mechanical{r}$ needed to travel arbitrarily far from a central mass $\mechanical{M}$ corresponds to $\energy{E}=0$.

$$
\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}(\mechanical{r})=\sqrt{\frac{2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}}}.
$$

At Earth's surface, $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}\approx\quantity{11.2}{km\,s^{-1}}$.

**Circular orbit speed.** For a circular orbit at radius $\mechanical{r}$, $\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{r}=\alpha/\mechanical{r}^2$. Thus $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{\alpha/(\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r})}$. The total energy is

$$
\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}^2-\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{r}}
=-\frac{\alpha}{2\mechanical{r}}.
$$

It is half the potential energy, as follows from the virial theorem for an inverse-distance potential.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radius | The distance from the central mass. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Central mass | The mass producing the gravitational field. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant in Newton's law of gravitation. |
| $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}},\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The threshold escape speed or the circular orbit speed. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Reduced mass | The effective mass of the relative motion. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Gravitational coupling | The strength of the inverse-distance potential. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Total mechanical energy | The sum of kinetic and potential energies. |
