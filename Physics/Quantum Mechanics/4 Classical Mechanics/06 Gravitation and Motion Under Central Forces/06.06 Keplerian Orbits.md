---
title: "Keplerian Orbits"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An attractive inverse-square force produces elliptical, parabolic, or hyperbolic orbits according to the sign of the total energy.

For the potential $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}$, $\alpha=\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}$ for gravitation and $\alpha=\electric{k}\electric{e}^2$ for the Coulomb model of hydrogen. The effective potential is

$$
\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r})=-\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{r}}
+\frac{\mechanical{L}^2}{2\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r}^2}.
$$

For $\mechanical{L}\ne0$, its minimum occurs at $\mechanical{r}_0=\mechanical{L}^2/(\mechanical{\mu}\alpha)$ with $\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r}_0)=-\mechanical{\mu}\alpha^2/(2\mechanical{L}^2)$. Energy conservation gives

$$
\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}+\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r}).
$$

**Orbit types.** For nonzero angular momentum, the sign of $\energy{E}$ determines the type.

- $\energy{E}<0$: a bound ellipse, including the circular case.
- $\energy{E}=0$: a parabola.
- $\energy{E}>0$: a hyperbola.

To obtain the orbit equation, substitute $u=1/\mechanical{r}$ and convert time derivatives into derivatives with respect to the polar angle $\mechanical{\phi}$.

Use $\mechanical{\dot\phi}=\mechanical{L}/(\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r}^2)$. The radial equation becomes $d^2u/d\mechanical{\phi}^2+u=\mechanical{\mu}\alpha/\mechanical{L}^2$, a driven harmonic equation in $u$. Its general solution is $u=\mechanical{\mu}\alpha/\mechanical{L}^2+C\cos(\mechanical{\phi}-\mechanical{\phi}_0)$. Choose $\mechanical{\phi}_0=0$ so that the periapsis is on the horizontal axis. Then

$$
\mechanical{r}(\mechanical{\phi})=\frac{\mechanical{p}}{1+e\cos\mechanical{\phi}},
\quad \mechanical{p}=\frac{\mechanical{L}^2}{\mechanical{\mu}\alpha},
\quad e=\sqrt{1+\frac{2\energy{E}\mechanical{L}^2}{\mechanical{\mu}\alpha^2}}.
$$

The energy determines the eccentricity $e$. The conic is an ellipse for $0\le e<1$, a parabola for $e=1$, and a hyperbola for $e>1$.

**Elliptical orbits.** The semimajor axis is $\mechanical{a}=-\alpha/(2\energy{E})$. The semiminor axis is $\mechanical{b}=\mechanical{a}\sqrt{1-e^2}$. Divide the ellipse area $\pi\mechanical{a}\mechanical{b}$ by the areal velocity $\mechanical{L}/(2\mechanical{\mu})$ to obtain the period.

$$
\signal{T}=\frac{2\pi\mechanical{a}\mechanical{b}\mechanical{\mu}}{\mechanical{L}}
=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{a}^3}{\alpha}}.
$$

For gravitation with $\mechanical{m}\ll\mechanical{M}$, this gives **Kepler's third law**.

$$
\boxed{\signal{T}^2=\frac{4\pi^2\mechanical{\mu}}{\alpha}\mechanical{a}^3
\approx\frac{4\pi^2}{\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}}\mechanical{a}^3.}
$$

> [!note] Source approximation
> The source writes the last two expressions as equal. For two finite masses, $\alpha/\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical{G}(\mechanical{M}+\mechanical{m})$. The approximation gives the same ratio $\signal{T}^2/\mechanical{a}^3$ for planets orbiting a much more massive Sun.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U},\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The interaction potential and the effective radial potential. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Coupling coefficient | The strength of the attractive inverse-distance potential. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant in Newton's law of gravitation. |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The masses of the gravitating bodies. |
| $\electric{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}$ | Coulomb constant | The proportionality constant in the electrostatic force law. |
| $\electric{e}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Elementary charge | The magnitude of the electron and proton charges. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Reduced mass | The effective mass of the relative motion. |
| $\mechanical{r},\mechanical{r}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance | The separation and its value at the minimum of the effective potential. |
| $\mechanical{\dot r}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Radial velocity | The rate of change of separation. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The conserved component perpendicular to the orbit. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Total energy | The constant sum of radial kinetic energy and effective potential energy. |
| $\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Polar angle | The angle of the relative position vector in the orbital plane. |
| $\mechanical{\phi}_0$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Periapsis direction | The angle of the closest point of the orbit before the axes are aligned. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\phi}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of the polar angle. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Semilatus rectum | The orbital distance at right angles to the periapsis direction. |
| $e$ | 1 | Eccentricity | The parameter that classifies the conic. |
| $\mechanical{a},\mechanical{b}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Orbital semiaxes | The semimajor and semiminor axes of the ellipse. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Orbital period | The time for a complete orbit. |
