title: "Gravitation and Motion Under Central Forces"

> [!TLDR]
> Newton's law of gravitation explains Kepler's laws and predicts the motion of planets, satellites, and comets.

Gravitation was the first fundamental interaction formulated mathematically. Newton's force law $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{M}\hat r/\mechanical{r}^2$, together with the laws of motion, explained Kepler's three empirical laws. It provided one method to predict the motion of planets, satellites, and comets. This chapter treats gravitation and central-force motion, with the Kepler problem as the main example.

## Chapter Summary

- [[06.02 Law of Gravitation|Gravitation]] is conservative, with potential energy $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}$.
- [[06.04 Central Forces and Conservation of Angular Momentum|Central forces]] conserve angular momentum. The orbit lies in a plane and the areal velocity is constant.
- The [[06.05 Two-Body Problem|two-body problem]] reduces to an equivalent one-body problem with $\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{m}_2/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)$. The effective potential is $\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\energy{U}+\mechanical{L}^2/(2\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r}^2)$.
- A [[06.06 Keplerian Orbits|Keplerian orbit]] satisfies $\mechanical{r}(\mechanical{\phi})=\mechanical{p}/(1+e\cos\mechanical{\phi})$, where $e$ is the eccentricity.
- [[06.08 Kepler's Three Laws|Kepler's third law]] gives $\signal{T}^2\approx4\pi^2\mechanical{a}^3/(\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M})$ when the central mass dominates.
- The [[06.09 Escape Velocity and Binding Energy|escape speed]] is $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}$ in the same approximation.
- The [[06.10 Virial Theorem|virial theorem]] gives $2\langle\energy{K}\rangle=n\langle\energy{U}\rangle$ for bounded motion with $\energy{U}\propto\mechanical{r}^n$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Gravitational force | The attractive force between the masses. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant of proportionality in Newton's law. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The two masses in the interaction. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The distance between the masses. |
| $\energy{U},\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The gravitational interaction energy and the effective radial potential energy. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Reduced mass | The effective mass of the relative motion. |
| $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Component masses | The two masses in the reduced-mass formula. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The conserved orbital angular momentum component. |
| $\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Polar angle | The angle of the relative position in the orbital plane. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Semilatus rectum | The orbit parameter in the numerator of the conic equation. |
| $e$ | 1 | Eccentricity | The parameter that describes the shape of the conic. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Orbital period | The time for a complete orbit. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Semimajor axis | Half the longest diameter of an elliptical orbit. |
| $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Escape speed | The threshold speed for escape from the central mass. |
| $\langle\energy{K}\rangle,\langle\energy{U}\rangle$ | $\unit{J}$ | Average energies | The time averages of the kinetic and potential energies. |
