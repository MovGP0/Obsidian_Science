title: "Gravitation and Orbits Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Apply gravitation, the virial theorem, and Kepler's laws to orbital motion, escape, and scattering.

## Exercise 1

**Basic.** Find the total mechanical energy of a body of mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{1000}{kg}$ in a circular orbit of radius $\mechanical{r}=\quantity{7\times10^6}{m}$ around Earth of mass $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{5.97\times10^{24}}{kg}$.

**Result.** $\energy{E}\approx\quantity{-2.85\times10^{10}}{J}$.

## Exercise 2

**Basic.** Find the escape speed from the Moon, with $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{7.35\times10^{22}}{kg}$ and $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{1.74\times10^6}{m}$.

**Result.** $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}\approx\quantity{2.38}{km\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 3

**Basic.** Mars has orbital semimajor axis $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{1.524}{AU}$. Find its period.

**Result.** $\signal{T}=\quantity{(1.524)^{3/2}}{yr}\approx\quantity{1.881}{yr}$.

## Exercise 4

**Basic.** Find the reduced mass of the Earth–Moon system.

**Result.** $\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical{m}_E\mechanical{m}_M/(\mechanical{m}_E+\mechanical{m}_M)\approx\quantity{7.26\times10^{22}}{kg}\approx0.987\mechanical{m}_M$.

## Exercise 5

**Intermediate.** Show that the escape speed is $\sqrt2$ times the speed of a circular orbit at the same distance.

## Exercise 6

**Intermediate.** An elliptical solar orbit has $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{2\times10^{11}}{m}$ and eccentricity $e=0.5$. Find the maximum and minimum distances from the Sun.

**Result.** $\mechanical{r}_{\max}=\mechanical{a}(1+e)=\quantity{3\times10^{11}}{m}$ and $\mechanical{r}_{\min}=\mechanical{a}(1-e)=\quantity{10^{11}}{m}$.

## Exercise 7

**Intermediate.** Verify the virial theorem for circular orbits in a gravitational potential.

**Result.** $\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}^2=\alpha/(2\mechanical{r})$ and $\energy{U}=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}$, so $2\energy{K}=-\energy{U}$.

## Exercise 8

**Intermediate.** Find the speed of a geostationary satellite.

**Result.** $\signal{T}=\quantity{86164}{s}$ and $\mechanical{r}=[\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}_E\signal{T}^2/(4\pi^2)]^{1/3}\approx\quantity{4.22\times10^7}{m}$. Thus $\mechanical{v}=2\pi\mechanical{r}/\signal{T}\approx\quantity{3.07}{km\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 9

**Challenge.** Show that bounded noncollision orbits in $\energy{U}=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}$ close. This is the Kepler case of Bertrand's theorem.

## Exercise 10

**Challenge.** For a hyperbolic scattering orbit, calculate the deflection angle as a function of the impact parameter $\mechanical{b}$ and the speed at infinity $\mechanical{v}_\infty$.

**Result.** $\tan(\mechanical{\theta}/2)=\alpha/(\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}_\infty^2\mechanical{b})$ for the positive magnitude of the deflection.

## Exercise 11

**Intermediate.** Estimate both velocity changes and the transfer time for a Hohmann transfer from Earth to Mars.

**Result.** $\Delta\mechanical{v}_1\approx\quantity{2.94}{km\,s^{-1}}$, $\Delta\mechanical{v}_2\approx\quantity{2.65}{km\,s^{-1}}$, and $t_H\approx\quantity{0.71}{yr}$.

## Exercise 12

**Intermediate.** Estimate the Roche limit for a hypothetical moon of density $\mechanical{\rho}_M=\quantity{3000}{kg\,m^{-3}}$ around Saturn, with $\mechanical{R}_P=\quantity{6\times10^7}{m}$ and $\mechanical{\rho}_P=\quantity{700}{kg\,m^{-3}}$.

**Result.** $\mechanical{d}_R\approx6\times10^7(1400/3000)^{1/3}\,\unit{m}\approx\quantity{4.7\times10^7}{m}$.

> [!note] Source result
> The calculated distance is smaller than the stated radius of Saturn. This simple Roche-limit estimate gives no corresponding orbit outside the planet for these inputs.

## Exercise 13

**Intermediate.** A comet has semimajor axis $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{100}{AU}$ and eccentricity $e=0.99$. Find its closest and farthest distances from the Sun and its period.

**Result.** $\mechanical{r}_{\min}=\mechanical{a}(1-e)=\quantity{1}{AU}$, $\mechanical{r}_{\max}=\mechanical{a}(1+e)=\quantity{199}{AU}$, and $\signal{T}=\quantity{1000}{yr}$.

## Exercise 14

**Basic.** Find the acceleration due to gravity at altitude $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{1000}{km}$ above Earth.

**Result.** $\mechanical{g}(\mechanical{h})=\mechanical{g}_0[\mechanical{R}_E/(\mechanical{R}_E+\mechanical{h})]^2=9.81(6371/7371)^2\,\unit{m\,s^{-2}}\approx\quantity{7.33}{m\,s^{-2}}$.

## Exercise 15

**Challenge.** Prove that the speed in an elliptical orbit is $\mechanical{v}_p=\sqrt{(\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{a})(1+e)/(1-e)}$ at perihelion and $\mechanical{v}_a=\sqrt{(\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{a})(1-e)/(1+e)}$ at aphelion in the dominant central-mass approximation.

| $e$ | 1 | Eccentricity | The parameter that describes the shape of the orbit. |
| $\energy{K},\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic and potential energy | The two contributions to orbital energy. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Gravitational coupling | The strength of the inverse-distance potential. |

| $\mechanical{d}_R$ | $\unit{m}$ | Roche limit | The estimated center-to-center separation at tidal disruption. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Altitude | The height above Earth's surface. |
| $\mechanical{g},\mechanical{g}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitudes at the given altitude and at Earth's surface. |
| $\mechanical{v}_p,\mechanical{v}_a$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Apsis speeds | The speeds at perihelion and aphelion. |
