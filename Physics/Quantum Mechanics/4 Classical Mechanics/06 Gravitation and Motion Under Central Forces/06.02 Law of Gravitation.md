---
title: "Law of Gravitation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Two point masses attract each other with a force proportional to both masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.

> [!definition] Definition 6.1 — Newton's Law of Gravitation
> For point masses $\mechanical{m}$ and $\mechanical{M}$ at separation $\mechanical{r}$, the force on $\mechanical{m}$ is
>
> $$
> \mechanical{\vec F}=-\frac{\mechanical{G}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}^2}\hat r,
> $$
>
> where $\mechanical{G}\approx\quantity{6.674\times10^{-11}}{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ and $\hat r$ points from $\mechanical{M}$ to $\mechanical{m}$.

**Superposition.** For several masses, the total force is the vector sum of the pairwise forces.

**Gauss's law for gravitation.** Outside a spherically symmetric mass distribution, the force at radius $\mechanical{r}$ is the same as if the total mass were concentrated at the center. At an interior point, only the mass enclosed within radius $\mechanical{r}$ contributes to the net field.

**Near Earth's surface.** For $\mechanical{r}\approx\mechanical{R}_E$, $\mechanical{\vec F}\approx-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\hat{\jmath}$, where $\mechanical{g}=\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}_E/\mechanical{R}_E^2\approx\quantity{9.81}{m\,s^{-2}}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Gravitational force | The attractive force exerted by the source mass. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The test and source masses. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The distance between the two masses. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The proportionality constant in Newton's law. |
| $\mechanical{M}_E$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Earth mass | The mass of Earth in the spherical approximation. |
| $\mechanical{R}_E$ | $\unit{m}$ | Earth radius | The distance from Earth's center to its surface in the spherical approximation. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The near-surface magnitude of Earth's gravitational field. |
