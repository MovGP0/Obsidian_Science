---
title: "From Kepler to Newton"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Over a century, observations and studies of motion led from Brahe's measurements and Kepler's laws to Newton's theory of gravitation.

The law of gravitation developed in stages through the work of Brahe, Kepler, Galileo, Hooke, and Newton.

- **Tycho Brahe (1546–1601)** collected unusually precise astronomical data during more than twenty years of nightly observations on the island of Hven, then part of Denmark.
- **Johannes Kepler (1571–1630)** inherited Brahe's data. After years of manual calculation, he formulated his three empirical laws of planetary motion from 1609 to 1619.
- **Galileo Galilei (1564–1642)** used telescope observations and experiments with falling bodies to establish ideas about free fall, inertia, and the independence of free-fall acceleration from mass.
- **Robert Hooke (1635–1703)** proposed an inverse-square force law in 1674 and described the idea in a letter to Newton in 1679.
- **Isaac Newton (1643–1727)** derived Kepler's three laws from the inverse-square force and his laws of motion in the *Principia* (1687). He established the universality of gravitation and applied it to the Moon, planets, and comets.

**Unification.** Before Newton, the Aristotelian view assigned different laws to celestial and terrestrial motion. Newton showed that the force that makes an apple fall also pulls the Moon toward Earth and keeps it in orbit. One theory explains both kinds of motion.
