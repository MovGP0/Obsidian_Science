---
title: "Kepler's Three Laws"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Kepler's laws specify the shape of planetary orbits, the rate at which they sweep area, and the relation between orbital period and size.

> [!theorem] Theorem 6.2 — Kepler's Laws
> 1. A planet's orbit is an ellipse with the Sun at one focus.
> 2. The line from the Sun to a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times.
> 3. The square of a planet's orbital period is proportional to the cube of its orbital semimajor axis.

All three follow from Newton's laws and the potential $\energy{U}=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}$, treating the Sun as much more massive than the planets for the common proportionality constant.

### Example — Earth's Orbital Period

Use $\signal{T}^2/\mechanical{a}^3\approx4\pi^2/(\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}_S)$ with $\mechanical{M}_S=\quantity{1.989\times10^{30}}{kg}$ and $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{1.496\times10^{11}}{m}$, one astronomical unit. This gives $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{a}^3/(\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}_S)}\approx\quantity{3.155\times10^7}{s}\approx\quantity{365.25}{d}$, in agreement with observation at the stated precision.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The interaction energy of the planet and Sun. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Gravitational coupling | The strength of the inverse-distance potential. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The instantaneous distance between the planet and Sun. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Semimajor axis | Half the longest diameter of the orbital ellipse. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Orbital period | The time for the planet to complete one orbit. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant in Newton's law of gravitation. |
| $\mechanical{M}_S$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Solar mass | The mass of the Sun. |
