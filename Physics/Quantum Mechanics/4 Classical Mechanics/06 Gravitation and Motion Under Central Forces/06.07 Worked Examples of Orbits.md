> [!TLDR]
> Orbital measurements and Kepler's laws give the mass of the central body and other properties of the orbit.

## Mass of the Sun from Kepler's Third Law

For Earth, $\signal{T}_T^2\approx4\pi^2\mechanical{a}_T^3/(\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}_S)$. Solve for the solar mass.

$$
\mechanical{M}_S\approx\frac{4\pi^2\mechanical{a}_T^3}{\mechanical{G}\signal{T}_T^2}
=\frac{4\pi^2(1.496\times10^{11})^3}{6.674\times10^{-11}(3.156\times10^7)^2}\,\unit{kg}
\approx\quantity{1.989\times10^{30}}{kg}.
$$

Earth's orbital period and semimajor axis, together with the law of gravitation, determine the solar mass in this approximation.

## Escape-Speed Analogy for a Black Hole

For mass $\mechanical{M}$, the Schwarzschild radius is $\mechanical{r}_s=2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/c^2$. At that radius, the Newtonian escape-speed expression gives $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}_s}=c$. This is a heuristic connection to the relativistic event horizon, from inside which light cannot escape.

For a black hole of one solar mass, $\mechanical{r}_s\approx\quantity{3}{km}$. The source gives $\mechanical{M}\approx4.1\times10^6\mechanical{M}_\odot$ for Sgr A*, which yields $\mechanical{r}_s\approx\quantity{1.2\times10^{10}}{m}\approx\quantity{0.08}{AU}$.

## Gravity Assist

A spacecraft that passes close to a planet orbiting the Sun can gain kinetic energy from the planet and change its own solar orbit. The spacecraft enters and leaves the planet's vicinity with different velocity directions. In the solar frame this can give different speeds, as in an elastic collision with a moving target. The planet slows by a negligible amount because of its much greater mass. Voyager 2, launched in 1977, used gravity assists to visit Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune in one mission.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M}_S$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Solar mass | The mass of the Sun inferred from Earth's orbit. |
| $\mechanical{a}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Earth orbital semimajor axis | The semimajor axis of Earth's orbit. |
| $\signal{T}_T$ | $\unit{s}$ | Earth orbital period | The time for Earth to complete one orbit. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant in Newton's law of gravitation. |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{M}_\odot$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass and solar mass | The mass of the black hole and the solar mass used as a reference. |
| $\mechanical{r}_s$ | $\unit{m}$ | Schwarzschild radius | The event-horizon radius of a nonrotating, uncharged black hole. |
| $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Escape speed | The speed given by the Newtonian escape condition. |
| $c$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | The speed of light in vacuum. |
