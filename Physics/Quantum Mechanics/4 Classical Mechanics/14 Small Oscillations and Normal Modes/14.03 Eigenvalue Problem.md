---
title: "Eigenvalue Problem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The generalized eigenvalue problem gives squared natural frequencies and a mass-orthonormal mode basis.

Seek harmonic solutions $\vec\eta(t)=\vec u e^{-i\signal{\omega}t}$ with constant $\vec u$. Substitution gives

$$
(-\signal{\omega}^2M+V)\vec u=0,\qquad
V\vec u=\signal{\omega}^2M\vec u.
$$

This is a **generalized eigenvalue problem**. The squared natural frequencies $\signal{\omega}_\alpha^2$ are the roots of $\det(V-\signal{\omega}^2M)=0$.

- The $n$ eigenvalues $\signal{\omega}_\alpha^2$ are real because $V$ and $M$ are real symmetric and $M$ is positive definite.
- If $V$ is positive definite, the eigenvalues are positive.
- The eigenvectors $\vec u_\alpha$ can be chosen as an $M$-orthogonal basis. Then $\vec u_\alpha^TM\vec u_\beta=0$ for $\alpha\ne\beta$.

**Normalization.** Choose $\vec u_\alpha^TM\vec u_\alpha=1$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\vec\eta,\vec u,\vec u_\alpha$ | — | Deviation and mode vectors | The coordinate deviations and eigenvectors of the motion. |
| $V,M$ | — | Stiffness and mass matrices | The quadratic coefficients in the eigenvalue problem. |
| $\signal{\omega},\signal{\omega}_\alpha$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The trial and natural frequencies. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the oscillation. |
