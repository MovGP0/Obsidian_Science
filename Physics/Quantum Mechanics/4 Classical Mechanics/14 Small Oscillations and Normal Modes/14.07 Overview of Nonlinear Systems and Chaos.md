---
title: "Overview of Nonlinear Systems and Chaos"
---

> [!TLDR]
> At finite amplitude, nonlinear terms can change frequencies, couple modes, and lead to chaos.

The linear small-oscillation approximation gives independent harmonic oscillators. Each such oscillator is integrable and periodic. At finite amplitudes, nonlinear terms in the potential change the motion.

**Nonlinear pendulum.** The exact equation $\ddot\theta+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})\sin\theta=0$ gives an amplitude-dependent period for libration.

$$
\signal{T}(\theta_0)=4\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{\ell}}{\mechanical{g}}}
\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{d\phi}{\sqrt{1-k^2\sin^2\phi}},\qquad k=\sin(\theta_0/2).
$$

The integral is a complete elliptic integral. The oscillation remains periodic but its period depends on amplitude. Above the energy of the upright equilibrium, the pendulum rotates.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source describes the rotation threshold as the energy at the suspension point. The threshold is the potential energy of the bob at its upright equilibrium above the pivot.

**Resonances and KAM.** Under suitable hypotheses, the Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser theorem guarantees that many invariant tori of a weakly perturbed integrable system survive. Resonant tori can break up and chaotic regions can form.

**Sensitivity to initial conditions.** Nearby trajectories can initially separate as $\lvert\Delta\vec x(t)\rvert\sim\lvert\Delta\vec x(0)\rvert e^{\lambda t}$ when the Lyapunov exponent $\lambda$ is positive. This limits the time over which practical predictions are accurate.

**Double pendulum.** At large amplitudes the double pendulum can be chaotic. Sensitivity to initial conditions makes it a common laboratory demonstration of chaos.

**Logistic map.** For $x_{n+1}=rx_n(1-x_n)$ with $0<r\le4$, increasing $r$ produces a transition to chaos through successive period doublings. The bifurcation values $r_n$ satisfy

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{r_n-r_{n-1}}{r_{n+1}-r_n}=\delta\simeq4.669,
$$

where $\delta$ is the universal Feigenbaum constant for this class of maps.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\theta,\theta_0,\phi$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The pendulum angle, its maximum value, and the integration angle. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The gravitational acceleration magnitude at the pendulum. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length | The pendulum length. |
| $\signal{T},t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period and time | The libration period and the time variable. |
| $k,x_n,r,r_n,\delta$ | $1$ | Dimensionless quantities | The elliptic modulus, logistic-map state, control parameter, bifurcation values, and Feigenbaum constant. |
| $\Delta\vec x$ | — | State separation | The difference between two nearby trajectories. |
| $\lambda$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Lyapunov exponent | The exponential growth rate of a small separation. |
