---
title: "Linearization About an Equilibrium"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Expand the potential and kinetic energy to quadratic order near equilibrium.

Consider generalized coordinates $q_k$ for $k=1,\ldots,n$ and a potential $\energy{U}(q)$. At equilibrium $q_0$, each $\left.\partial\energy{U}/\partial q_k\right|_{q_0}=0$. Set $\eta_k=q_k-q_{0,k}$. The second-order Taylor expansion is

$$
\energy{U}(q_0+\eta)\simeq\energy{U}(q_0)+\frac12\sum_{k,l} V_{kl}\eta_k\eta_l,\qquad
V_{kl}=\left.\frac{\partial^2\energy{U}}{\partial q_k\partial q_l}\right|_{q_0}.
$$

The matrix $V$ is symmetric. At a nondegenerate minimum it is positive definite.

> [!note] Source condition
> The source states positive definiteness for stable equilibria. A degenerate minimum can have zero quadratic stiffness and remain stable through higher-order terms.

To quadratic order near equilibrium, the kinetic energy is

$$
\energy{T}=\frac12\sum_{k,l} M_{kl}(q_0)\dot\eta_k\dot\eta_l.
$$

The mass matrix $M_{kl}=a_{kl}(q_0)$ is symmetric and positive definite. Dropping the constant term, the quadratic Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{L}=\frac12\sum_{k,l}M_{kl}\dot\eta_k\dot\eta_l-\frac12\sum_{k,l}V_{kl}\eta_k\eta_l.
$$

The Euler–Lagrange equations give the linear system with constant coefficients

$$
M\ddot{\vec\eta}+V\vec\eta=0.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q_k,q_0,\eta_k,\dot\eta_k$ | — | Coordinates and deviations | The original coordinates, equilibrium, and deviations and their rates. |
| $M_{kl},a_{kl},V_{kl}$ | — | Mass and stiffness coefficients | The kinetic coefficients and the potential Hessian at equilibrium. |
| $\energy{U},\energy{T},\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy and Lagrangian | The potential, kinetic energy, and quadratic Lagrangian. |
