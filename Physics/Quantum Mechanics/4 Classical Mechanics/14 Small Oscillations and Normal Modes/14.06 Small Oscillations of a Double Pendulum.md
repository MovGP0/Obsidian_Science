---
title: "Small Oscillations of a Double Pendulum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The equal-mass, equal-length double pendulum has two small-oscillation frequencies.

Expanding the double pendulum from [[10.05 Representative Examples of Lagrangian Mechanics]] to second order in $\theta_1,\theta_2$ gives a linear $2\times2$ system with two natural frequencies. The exercises in [[15.00 Complete Solutions to the Exercises]] treat the case $\mechanical{m}_1=\mechanical{m}_2=\mechanical{m}$ and $\mechanical{\ell}_1=\mechanical{\ell}_2=\mechanical{\ell}$. The result is

$$
\signal{\omega}_\pm^2=\frac{\mechanical{g}}{\mechanical{\ell}}(2\pm\sqrt{2}).
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\theta_1,\theta_2$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Pendulum angles | The small angular displacements from equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2,\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Masses | The two bob masses and their common value. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}_1,\mechanical{\ell}_2,\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lengths | The pendulum lengths and their common value. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The magnitude of the gravitational acceleration. |
| $\signal{\omega}_\pm$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The two natural frequencies of the double pendulum. |
