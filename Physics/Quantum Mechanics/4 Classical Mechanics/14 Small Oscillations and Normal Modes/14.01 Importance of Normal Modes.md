---
title: "Importance of Normal Modes"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Normal modes describe vibrations near equilibrium in systems from molecules to stars.

Normal modes give a common description of dynamics near equilibrium. They occur throughout physics.

- **Molecular vibrations.** The normal modes of a molecule determine its infrared and Raman spectra. These spectra are a basis for laboratory spectroscopy.
- **Crystals and phonons.** Phonons are the quantized excitations of the normal modes of a crystal lattice. They transport heat and contribute to specific heat.
- **Stars.** Helioseismology and asteroseismology use stellar normal modes to investigate the internal structure of stars.
- **Civil and mechanical engineering.** Normal modes inform earthquake-resistant design, the analysis of structural vibration, and the selection of dampers.
- **Electrical circuits.** Modes describe resonances in complex circuits, filters, and antennas.
- **Quantum field theory.** The normal modes of a classical free field are harmonic oscillators. Quantization gives particle excitations.

Near stable equilibrium, a conservative linearized system with positive mass and stiffness matrices decomposes into independent oscillators. Diagonalizing the generalized eigenvalue problem $V\vec u=\signal{\omega}^2M\vec u$ gives the frequencies and modes. Their superposition gives each solution.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $V,M$ | — | Stiffness and mass matrices | The matrices in the generalized eigenvalue problem. |
| $\vec u$ | — | Mode vector | The relative displacements of the coordinates in a mode. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The natural frequency of the mode. |
