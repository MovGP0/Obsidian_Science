---
title: "Resonance in Multimode Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A drive couples to modes according to its frequency and spatial pattern. Damping sets the width of each resonance.

In a system with $n$ modes of frequencies $\signal{\omega}_\alpha$, an external force near one mode frequency can selectively excite that mode. For well-separated, weakly damped modes that couple to the force and the measured response, the frequency response can show a peak near each $\signal{\omega}_\alpha$. The damping of the mode determines the peak width.

**Mode selection.** The spatial pattern of the mode determines how the drive couples to it. A force at the center of a symmetric chain couples to symmetric modes that move at the center. Antisymmetric modes have a node there.

**Spectrum of a real system.** Buildings, cars, and bridges have characteristic normal-mode spectra. Structural and mechanical engineers account for excitation from wind, traffic, and motors to avoid dangerous resonances.

> [!note] Source terminology
> The source calls the 1940 Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapse an example of aeroelastic resonance in a torsional mode. The bridge experienced self-excited torsional flutter, in which the interaction with the airflow amplified the twisting motion. [Washington State Department of Transportation](https://wsdot.wa.gov/TNBhistory/bridges-failure.htm)

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
