---
title: "The Casimir Effect"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Casimir force measures a geometry-dependent vacuum-energy difference between conducting plates.

The unsubtracted vacuum energy $\energy{E}_0$ is formally infinite, but differences between geometries can be measured. The Casimir effect (1948) is the attractive force between two parallel conducting plates in vacuum. The plates modify the quantum-vacuum modes relative to free space.

Take plates separated by $\mechanical{d}$ along the z-axis, with $\signal{\phi}=0$ at each plate. The allowed normal momenta are $\mechanical{k}_z=n\pi/\mechanical{d}$ for positive integer n. In 1+1 dimensions,

$$
\energy{E}_0(\mechanical{d})=\frac12\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n\pi}{\mechanical{d}}. \tag{3.42}
$$

The sum diverges. Zeta-function regularization, formally $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{s}\to\zeta(-s)$, gives the finite physical difference

$$
\energy{E}_0(\mechanical{d})=-\frac{\pi}{24\mechanical{d}}. \tag{3.43}
$$

The force is

$$
\mechanical{F}=-\frac{\partial\energy{E}_0(\mechanical{d})}{\partial\mechanical{d}}=-\frac{\pi}{24\mechanical{d}^{2}}. \tag{3.44}
$$

For electromagnetism in 3+1 dimensions, the force per area is

$$
\frac{\mechanical{F}}{\mechanical{A}}=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{240\mechanical{d}^{4}}. \tag{3.45}
$$

The source reports high-precision experimental verification and an attractive force for typical micrometer separations. The geometry-dependent effect demonstrates the physical consequences of vacuum fluctuations.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{E}_0$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Vacuum energy | The formally divergent unrenormalized total vacuum contribution. |
| $\mechanical{d}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Plate separation | The distance between the boundary surfaces. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | — | Confined scalar field | The field satisfying the plate boundary conditions; units depend on dimension. |
| $\mechanical{k}_z$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Normal momentum | The discretized momentum perpendicular to the plates. |
| $\mechanical{F}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Casimir force | The derivative of vacuum energy with respect to separation. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Plate area | The area used to define force per unit area. |
