---
title: "Relations between the Propagators"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Step functions relate the scalar propagation functions, while pole prescriptions distinguish their Fourier forms.

The coordinate-space relations are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x})&=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\Delta}^{+}(\mechanical{x})+\theta(-\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\Delta}^{-}(\mechanical{x}),\\
\signal{\Delta}_{R}(\mechanical{x})&=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x})=-i\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0})[\signal{\Delta}^{+}(\mechanical{x})-\signal{\Delta}^{-}(\mechanical{x})],\\
\signal{\Delta}_{A}(\mechanical{x})&=-\theta(-\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}),\\
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x})-\signal{\Delta}_{F}^{*}(\mechanical{x})&=i\operatorname{sgn}(\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}),\\
\signal{\Delta}_{R}(\mechanical{x})-\signal{\Delta}_{A}(\mechanical{x})&=\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}).
\end{aligned} \tag{3.87}
$$

In Fourier space,

$$
\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_{F}(\mechanical{k})=\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}+i\varepsilon}, \tag{3.88}
$$

$$
\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_{R}(\mechanical{k})=\frac{1}{(\energy{k}^{0}+i\varepsilon)^{2}-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}^{2}}=\frac{1}{(\energy{k}^{0}+i\varepsilon)^{2}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}}, \tag{3.89}
$$

$$
\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_{A}(\mechanical{k})=\frac{1}{(\energy{k}^{0}-i\varepsilon)^{2}-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}^{2}}=\frac{1}{(\energy{k}^{0}-i\varepsilon)^{2}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}}. \tag{3.90}
$$

The extra numerator i in the Feynman function reflects its source of minus i times the delta function, compared with minus one for the retarded and advanced functions.
