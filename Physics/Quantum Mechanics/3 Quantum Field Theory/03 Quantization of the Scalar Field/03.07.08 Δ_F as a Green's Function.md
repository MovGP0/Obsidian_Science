---
title: "Δ_F as a Green's Function"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Klein–Gordon operator acting on the Feynman function produces a point source with a factor of minus i.

With the stated pole prescription, the Feynman propagator is a Klein–Gordon Green's function up to a factor of i:

$$
(\square_x+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=-i\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}). \tag{3.83}
$$

This agrees with $\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_{F}(\mechanical{k})=i/(\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}+i\varepsilon)$.

**Proof.** Since $\square_x e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}=-\mechanical{k}^{2}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\square_x+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
&=\int\frac{d^{4}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{i(-\mechanical{k}^{2}+\mechanical{m}^{2})}{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}+i\varepsilon}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&=-i\int\frac{d^{4}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}}{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}+i\varepsilon}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to0^{+}}-i\int\frac{d^{4}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}=-i\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned} \tag{3.84}
$$

The ratio tends to one in the sense of distributions: subtracting one leaves an infinitesimal-regulator term that vanishes in the distributional limit. Thus

$$
(\square_x+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=-i\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}). \tag{3.85}
$$

This verifies the source convention. Equivalently, multiplying the Feynman function by i gives a Green's function with positive unit delta source.

> [!note]
> The source instead prints minus i in that alternative rescaling. Multiplying (3.85) shows that minus i would give a negative delta source; the positive-source rescaling is corrected here.

## Remark 3.11 — Conventions

Some authors define their Feynman function with a source of minus one times the delta function; others use minus i. The difference is an overall factor tied to the convention for the time-ordered expectation value.
