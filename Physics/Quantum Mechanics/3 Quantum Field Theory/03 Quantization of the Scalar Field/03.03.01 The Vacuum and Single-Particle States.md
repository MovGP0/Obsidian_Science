---
title: "The Vacuum and Single-Particle States"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The vacuum is annihilated by every mode annihilation operator, and creation operators produce covariantly normalized one-particle states.

The mode commutators have the same algebraic structure as the harmonic oscillator. Field states can therefore be built by the same algebraic construction used for oscillator energy eigenstates.

## Definition 3.4 — Vacuum State

The vacuum is the unique state up to a phase satisfying

$$
a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})|0\rangle=0\qquad\text{for every momentum}, \tag{3.24}
$$

with $\langle0|0\rangle=1$.

Although it contains no real particles, the vacuum has field fluctuations and formally infinite zero-point energy. Vacuum effects include the measurable Casimir effect.

A one-particle state is

$$
|\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rangle\equiv\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}\,a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})|0\rangle. \tag{3.25}
$$

Its factor gives covariant normalization:

$$
\langle\mechanical{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{\mathbf k}'\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\,2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}'). \tag{3.26}
$$

The energy factor comes from the Lorentz-invariant mass-shell measure $d\mu(\mechanical{k})=d^{3}\mechanical{k}/[(2\pi)^{3}2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}]$, restricted to $\mechanical{k}^{2}=\mechanical{m}^{2}$ and $\mechanical{k}^{0}>0$.
