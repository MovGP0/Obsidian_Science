> [!TLDR]
> Wightman, commutator, retarded, advanced, and Feynman functions encode different propagation conditions.

Green's functions of the Klein–Gordon equation depend on boundary conditions.

Translation invariance of the free vacuum makes the event difference sufficient. The positive- and negative-frequency Wightman functions below solve the homogeneous Klein–Gordon equation. The retarded, advanced, and time-ordered combinations instead acquire point sources when time derivatives act on their step functions. Thus not every function listed here is a Green function with a delta source.

## Definition 3.10 — Scalar-Field Propagation Functions

All functions depend on the event difference:

$$
\signal{\Delta}^{+}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}=\langle0|\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})|0\rangle, \tag{3.61}
$$

$$
\signal{\Delta}^{-}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}=\langle0|\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})|0\rangle, \tag{3.62}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})&=-i[\signal{\Delta}^{+}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})-\signal{\Delta}^{-}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})]=-i[\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})],\\
\signal{\Delta}_{R}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})&=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}),\\
\signal{\Delta}_{A}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})&=-\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}),\\
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})&=\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.63}
$$

The step function is the Heaviside function, and the script T denotes time ordering.

The minus superscript in (3.62) labels the reversed frequency branch; it is not an overall minus sign. For the real free field that function equals the positive-frequency function at the opposite separation, and also its complex conjugate. Their difference yields the commutator, while time ordering selects one or the other according to time order. All these position-space functions have energy dimension two, the same as a field pair. The c-number equality with the operator commutator in (3.63) suppresses the identity operator.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime positions | The two field events. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The real field in the vacuum correlators. |
| $\signal{\Delta}^{+},\signal{\Delta}^{-},\signal{\Delta},\signal{\Delta}_{R},\signal{\Delta}_{A},\signal{\Delta}_{F}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Propagation functions | The Wightman, commutator, retarded, advanced, and Feynman functions. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The on-shell integration momentum. |
| $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode energy | The positive on-shell energy. |
