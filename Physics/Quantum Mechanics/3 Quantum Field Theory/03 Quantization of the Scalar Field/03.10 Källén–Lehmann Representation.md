title: "Källén–Lehmann Representation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The exact scalar propagator is a positive spectral superposition of free propagators with different invariant masses.

The free propagator has a simple pole at $\mechanical{k}^{2}=\mechanical{m}^{2}$. Interactions give it a richer structure.

The question is what replaces a single free mass pole when a field can excite many interacting states. Insert a complete set of positive-norm energy–momentum states between two fields. Their squared matrix elements give nonnegative spectral weights. Use a Poincaré-invariant vacuum, positive energy, and a scalar field with its vacuum expectation subtracted. This removes a disconnected constant contribution. See [Weigand, section 2.2, pp. 42–46](https://physics.umd.edu/grt/taj/624b/WeigandQFT.pdf#page=42).

## Theorem 3.17 — Källén–Lehmann Representation

The exact interacting scalar propagator has spectral form

$$
\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_{F}(\mechanical{k})^{\mathrm{exact}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\mechanical{\mu}^{2}\,\frac{\signal{\rho}(\mechanical{\mu}^{2})\,i}{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{\mu}^{2}+i\varepsilon}, \tag{3.102}
$$

where the Källén–Lehmann spectral density is nonnegative.

The spectral function describes the excitation structure. It contains a delta pole at the physical mass squared and a multiparticle continuum. The source places the latter above $\mechanical{\mu}^{2}>(2\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}})^{2}$.

> [!note]
> The printed two-particle threshold is model dependent. The lightest allowed multiparticle contribution also depends on the field's quantum numbers.

The representation is nonperturbative and follows from unitarity, causality, and Lorentz invariance. Separate the one-particle pole:

$$
\signal{\rho}(\mechanical{\mu}^{2})=Z\delta(\mechanical{\mu}^{2}-\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}}^{2})+\signal{\rho}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mechanical{\mu}^{2}). \tag{3.103}
$$

The field renormalization constant obeys $0\le Z\le1$. In the free theory, it is one and the continuum term vanishes.

> [!note]
> The bound on Z uses a canonically normalized field and the corresponding unit total spectral weight. Rescaling the field rescales both spectral density and pole residue, so the upper bound is not an invariant statement for an arbitrarily normalized operator. The isolated-pole decomposition also assumes a stable one-particle state with nonzero overlap with this field. Additional stable states can give more poles; the printed one-pole form is a model choice. Compare [Weigand, section 2.2, pp. 45–46](https://physics.umd.edu/grt/taj/624b/WeigandQFT.pdf#page=45).

As a normalization check, the free spectral delta collapses (3.102) to the free propagator. The mass-squared measure and spectral density have cancelling dimensions, leaving the propagator's energy dimension minus two. This is a structural result; no numerical interacting spectral density is calculated here.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momentum | The Fourier momentum of the exact propagator. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass scales | The free-particle mass and spectral invariant-mass variable. |
| $\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_{F}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Exact propagator | The momentum-space interacting two-point function. |
| $\signal{\rho}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Spectral density | The nonnegative distribution over invariant mass squared. |
| $\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Physical mass | The measured one-particle pole mass. |
| $\signal{\rho}_{\mathrm{cont}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Continuum spectral density | The multiparticle part of the spectral distribution. |
| $Z$ | $1$ | Field normalization | The one-particle pole residue in the stated convention. |
