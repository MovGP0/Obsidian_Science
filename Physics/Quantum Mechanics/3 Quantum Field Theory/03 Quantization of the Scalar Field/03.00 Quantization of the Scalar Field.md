> [!TLDR]
> The real scalar quantum field introduces Fock space, Bose–Einstein statistics, propagators, and causality in the simplest spinless setting.

> “The career of a young theoretical physicist consists of treating the harmonic oscillator in ever-increasing levels of abstraction.”
>
> — Sidney Coleman

The real scalar field is the simplest starting point for quantum field theory. It has no spin or charge and describes bosonic particles of mass $\mechanical{m}$. Despite its simplicity, it contains central QFT concepts: Fock space, Bose–Einstein statistics, propagators, and causality. It is the natural starting point before fields with richer spin structure are considered.

The question is how a classical wave becomes a theory with discrete particle occupation numbers. Work first with a free field in flat spacetime. The quadratic dynamics make each momentum mode an oscillator; quantization changes the mode coefficients into operators, rather than changing the classical dispersion relation. Natural units set the reduced Planck constant and the speed of light to one.

Read [[03.01 Canonical Quantization]] for the postulate, then [[03.02 Quantization of the Real Scalar Field]] for its mode realization and [[03.03 Fock Space and Bose–Einstein Statistics]] for the states. [[03.04 Hamiltonian Operator and Normal Ordering]] tests energy positivity. The complex field then separates particle number from charge. Finally, compare [[03.06 Microcausality]] with [[03.07 Propagators of the Scalar Field]]: a spacelike vacuum correlation is allowed, but a spacelike commutator must vanish. This distinction prevents a propagator from being read as a signal speed.
