---
title: "The Feynman Propagator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Feynman propagator is the vacuum expectation value of a time-ordered field pair.

Use the free translation-invariant vacuum and the normalization of (3.61)–(3.62). The definition below includes no extra factor of minus i outside the expectation value. This convention later gives the Fourier numerator i and the Green-function source minus i times a delta distribution.

For the scalar field,

$$
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})\equiv\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\signal{\Delta}^{+}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\Delta}^{-}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}). \tag{3.69}
$$

This is central to perturbation theory: internal scalar lines in Feynman diagrams carry this propagator.

For positive time difference only the positive-frequency correlation remains; for negative time difference only the reversed correlation remains. Interchanging the two events leaves the time-ordered real-scalar function unchanged. These checks reconstruct (3.69) without interpreting it as a retarded response. In particular, time ordering does not impose zero support at spacelike separation. Internal-line use is a perturbative role for this correlation, not a probability for a detected localized particle.
