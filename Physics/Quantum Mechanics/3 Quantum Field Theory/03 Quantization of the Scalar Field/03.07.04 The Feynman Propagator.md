---
title: "The Feynman Propagator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Feynman propagator is the vacuum expectation value of a time-ordered field pair.

For the scalar field,

$$
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})\equiv\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\signal{\Delta}^{+}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\Delta}^{-}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}). \tag{3.69}
$$

This is central to perturbation theory: internal scalar lines in Feynman diagrams carry this propagator.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\Delta}_{F},\signal{\Delta}^{+},\signal{\Delta}^{-}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Correlation functions | The time-ordered and positive/negative-frequency scalar functions. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The field in the vacuum expectation value. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime positions | The two field events. |
