> [!TLDR]
> The scalar Hamiltonian is a collection of mode-oscillator energies with a vacuum contribution that normal ordering separates.

The next test of the quantization is energetic: does adding a quantum increase the energy, and what energy remains when no modes are occupied? The mode Hamiltonian answers both questions. Its occupation term has positive mode energies; its constant term is the sum of oscillator zero-point contributions.

Read the derivation before the divergence and subtraction. Normal ordering sets a reference for the free vacuum in a fixed setup. The Casimir note then changes the boundary conditions, so it compares different mode spectra rather than measuring that discarded constant in isolation. Keep the distinction between total energy and energy density throughout.
