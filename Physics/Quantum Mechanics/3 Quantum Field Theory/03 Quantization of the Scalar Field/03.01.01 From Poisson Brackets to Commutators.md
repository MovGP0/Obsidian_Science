---
title: "From Poisson Brackets to Commutators"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Canonical quantization builds a quantum field theory directly from a classical Hamiltonian description.

Canonical quantization is the most direct route from a classical theory to a quantum field theory. Its basic idea is formally analogous to the transition from classical mechanics to quantum mechanics.

Use natural units, so the reduced Planck constant is one in (3.2). With units restored, its right-hand side includes the inverse reduced Planck constant. The bracket prescription fixes the basic canonical algebra; it is not a rule that maps every product of classical functions to a unique operator. Noncommuting factors can require a separate ordering choice. The fermion statement also requires the graded canonical formalism; this note applies the ordinary bracket rule to the bosonic scalar field.

Canonical coordinates and momenta become operators. Poisson brackets become commutators, or anticommutators for fermions. For generalized coordinates and conjugate momenta,

$$
\{\mechanical{q}_i,\mechanical{p}_j\}_{\mathrm{PB}}=\delta_{ij},\qquad\{\mechanical{q}_i,\mechanical{q}_j\}_{\mathrm{PB}}=0,\qquad\{\mechanical{p}_i,\mechanical{p}_j\}_{\mathrm{PB}}=0. \tag{3.1}
$$

The prescription is

$$
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathrm{PB}}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{i}[\cdot,\cdot], \tag{3.2}
$$

so the coordinate–momentum relation becomes $[\mechanical{\hat q}_i,\mechanical{\hat p}_j]=i\delta_{ij}$.

For a field, the values at each spatial point are the generalized coordinates. The field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})$ takes the role of a coordinate labeled continuously by position. Its conjugate momentum density is

$$
\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\dot\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})}. \tag{3.3}
$$

The equal-time functional bracket is

$$
\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}_{\mathrm{PB}}=\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}), \tag{3.4}
$$

and canonical quantization imposes

$$
\boxed{[\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})]=i\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}).} \tag{3.5}
$$

The delta distribution replaces the Kronecker delta because the coordinate label is continuous. Integrate (3.5) against smooth spatial test functions to obtain finite smeared operators; the formula at coincident points is not an ordinary finite number. As a dimensional check in four spacetime dimensions, field times momentum density has energy dimension three, the same as a spatial delta distribution. Equal time is essential here: unequal-time commutators follow from this algebra and the equations of motion.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{q}_i,\mechanical{\hat q}_i$ | — | Generalized coordinates | The classical and quantum mechanical coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{p}_i,\mechanical{\hat p}_i$ | — | Conjugate momenta | The momenta paired with the selected generalized coordinates. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The canonical field coordinate in four dimensions. |
