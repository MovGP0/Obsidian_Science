---
title: "Bose–Einstein Statistics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Commuting creation operators make scalar multiparticle states symmetric under particle exchange.

Bose–Einstein statistics follows directly from (3.20). Since creation operators commute,

$$
|\mechanical{\mathbf k},\mechanical{\mathbf k}'\rangle=|\mechanical{\mathbf k}',\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rangle. \tag{3.32}
$$

More generally, (3.31) is symmetric under every permutation of its momentum labels. This is the Bose–Einstein principle: scalar particles are bosons.

The spin–statistics theorem connects Lorentz structure and microcausality with this statistics. Integer-spin bosons use commutators; half-integer-spin fermions use anticommutators and obey Fermi–Dirac statistics.

## Remark 3.5 — Occupation Numbers

A bosonic momentum state can contain zero, one, two, or any nonnegative integer number of particles. A fermionic state can contain at most one, by the Pauli exclusion principle. The distinction is encoded in commutators versus anticommutators.
