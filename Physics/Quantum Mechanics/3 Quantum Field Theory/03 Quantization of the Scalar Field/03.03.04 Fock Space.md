---
title: "Fock Space"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Fock space combines all symmetric fixed-particle-number spaces and permits particle creation and annihilation.

States obtained by applying finite products of creation operators to the vacuum generate Fock space:

$$
\mathcal F=\mathcal H_0\oplus\mathcal H_1\oplus\mathcal H_2\oplus\cdots, \tag{3.33}
$$

where the bosonic n-particle space is

$$
\mathcal H_n=\operatorname{Sym}^{n}(\mathcal H_1). \tag{3.34}
$$

The zero-particle sector is the one-dimensional span of the vacuum. The one-particle space contains normalizable momentum wave packets; higher sectors are symmetric tensor products of that space. The direct sum also includes limits of finite-particle superpositions whose total norm is finite, so finite creation products generate a dense subspace rather than every vector without completion.

This structure describes variable particle number, allowing creation and annihilation processes.

Sectors with different particle counts are orthogonal. A creation operator maps one sector to the next, while the free Hamiltonian acts within each sector. Thus the availability of many sectors is a kinematic structure; an interaction that couples sectors is needed for dynamical particle production. This free Fock construction is not an assertion that every interacting vacuum has the same representation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $n$ | $1$ | Particle number | The number of particles in a Fock-space sector. |
