---
title: "Time Ordering"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Time ordering places the later bosonic field to the left.

In an evolution product, the earlier operation acts first on a ket and therefore appears farther to the right. This gives the order used in perturbative time evolution. Time ordering is a prescription for an operator product, not the physical time-evolution operator itself, and it differs from normal ordering by frequency components.

The time-ordering operator arranges operators chronologically from right to left:

$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}=\begin{cases}\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y}),&\mechanical{x}^{0}>\mechanical{y}^{0},\\\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}),&\mechanical{y}^{0}>\mechanical{x}^{0}.\end{cases} \tag{3.67}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}). \tag{3.68}
$$

If the two events are spacelike, different frames can select different lines of (3.67), but microcausality makes the two products equal. This is why time ordering of local bosonic scalar fields is compatible with Lorentz covariance. At equal times away from coincidence the products also agree. Coincident products and derivatives of their time ordering require a distributional prescription; differentiating the step functions generates the contact terms needed for Green-function equations.
