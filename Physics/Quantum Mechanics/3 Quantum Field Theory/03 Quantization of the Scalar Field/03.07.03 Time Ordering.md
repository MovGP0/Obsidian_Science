---
title: "Time Ordering"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Time ordering places the later bosonic field to the left.

The time-ordering operator arranges operators chronologically from right to left:

$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}=\begin{cases}\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y}),&\mechanical{x}^{0}>\mechanical{y}^{0},\\\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}),&\mechanical{y}^{0}>\mechanical{x}^{0}.\end{cases} \tag{3.67}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}). \tag{3.68}
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The bosonic field ordered by its time argument. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime positions | The two events whose temporal order is compared. |
