---
title: "Lagrangian Density and U(1) Symmetry"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Global phase invariance of the complex scalar Lagrangian gives a conserved current and charge.

The real field $\signal{\phi}=\signal{\phi}^{\dagger}$ describes particles that coincide with their antiparticles, such as the neutral pion in an effective description. Charged particles, such as charged pions, require a complex scalar field.

The free complex scalar Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{*})(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})-\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{*}\signal{\phi}. \tag{3.46}
$$

Writing $\signal{\phi}=(\signal{\phi}_1+i\signal{\phi}_2)/\sqrt2$ expresses it as the sum of two real-field Lagrangians. Treating the field and its conjugate as independent variables gives

$$
(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\phi}=0,\qquad(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\phi}^{*}=0. \tag{3.47}
$$

The global phase symmetry is

$$
\signal{\phi}\to e^{i\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\phi},\qquad\signal{\phi}^{*}\to e^{-i\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\phi}^{*}. \tag{3.48}
$$

The conserved current, with the source's sign convention, is

$$
\electric{j}^{\mu}=i(\signal{\phi}^{*}\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}-\signal{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{*})
=i\signal{\phi}^{*}\overleftrightarrow{\partial}^{\mu}\signal{\phi}, \tag{3.49}
$$

with $\partial_{\mu}\electric{j}^{\mu}=0$. Its charge is

$$
\electric{Q}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\electric{j}^{0}=i\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,(\signal{\phi}^{*}\signal{\dot\phi}-\signal{\dot\phi}^{*}\signal{\phi}). \tag{3.50}
$$
