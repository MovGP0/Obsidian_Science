title: "Fourier Representation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Feynman function can be calculated explicitly from the real scalar mode expansion.

We now calculate the Feynman function explicitly for a real scalar field.

From (3.61)–(3.62) and (3.69),

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})={}&\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.70}
$$

Spatial momentum reversal leaves the mode energy unchanged. Combining the integrals gives

$$
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}[\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}]. \tag{3.71}
$$

Use the integral representation

$$
\theta(\mechanical{t})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to0^{+}}\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\signal{\omega}\,\frac{e^{-i\signal{\omega}\mechanical{t}}}{\signal{\omega}+i\varepsilon}. \tag{3.72}
$$

The equivalent four-dimensional Fourier form is

$$
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=\lim_{\varepsilon\to0^{+}}\int\frac{d^{4}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{i e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}+i\varepsilon}. \tag{3.73}
$$

## Derivation by Complex-Energy Integration

Prove the equality by evaluating

$$
I(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\energy{k}^{0}}{2\pi}\frac{i e^{-i\energy{k}^{0}(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})}}{(\energy{k}^{0})^{2}-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}^{2}+i\varepsilon}, \tag{3.74}
$$

where the positive mode energy is the square root of momentum squared plus mass squared. The source factorizes the denominator in the infinitesimal-regulator limit as

$$
(\energy{k}^{0})^{2}-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}^{2}+i\varepsilon
=(\energy{k}^{0}-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}+i\varepsilon')(\energy{k}^{0}+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}-i\varepsilon'). \tag{3.75}
$$

Here $\varepsilon'=\varepsilon/(2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k})\to0^{+}$. The poles are

$$
\energy{k}^{0}=+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}-i\varepsilon'\quad\text{(lower half-plane)},\qquad
\energy{k}^{0}=-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}+i\varepsilon'\quad\text{(upper half-plane)}. \tag{3.76}
$$

Fig. 3.1 illustrates their positions. For $\mechanical{\tau}=\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0}>0$, close the contour clockwise in the lower half-plane. The exponential decreases as the imaginary energy tends to negative infinity. The positive-energy pole gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
I(\mechanical{\tau}>0)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}(-2\pi i)\operatorname*{Res}_{k^{0}=\omega_{\mathbf k}-i\varepsilon'}\left[\frac{i e^{-i\energy{k}^{0}\mechanical{\tau}}}{(\energy{k}^{0}-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}+i\varepsilon')(\energy{k}^{0}+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}-i\varepsilon')}\right]\\
&=(-i)\frac{i e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{\tau}}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}=\frac{e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{\tau}}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.77}
$$

The negative residue-theorem factor comes from clockwise orientation; the measure supplies the factor of inverse two pi.

For negative time difference, close counterclockwise in the upper half-plane. The exponential decreases as the imaginary energy tends to positive infinity. The negative-energy pole gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
I(\mechanical{\tau}<0)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}(+2\pi i)\operatorname*{Res}_{k^{0}=-\omega_{\mathbf k}+i\varepsilon'}\left[\frac{i e^{-i\energy{k}^{0}\mechanical{\tau}}}{(\energy{k}^{0}-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}+i\varepsilon')(\energy{k}^{0}+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}-i\varepsilon')}\right]\\
&=(+i)\frac{i e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{\tau}|}}{-2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}=\frac{e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{\tau}|}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}=\left.\frac{e^{+i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{\tau}}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}\right|_{\tau<0}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.78}
$$

At the negative-energy pole, $e^{-i\energy{k}^{0}\mechanical{\tau}}=e^{+i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{\tau}}=e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{\tau}|}$ for negative time difference.

Both time orders give $e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{\tau}|}/(2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k})$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{d^{4}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{i e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}+i\varepsilon}
&=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}\int\frac{d\energy{k}^{0}}{2\pi}\frac{i e^{-i\energy{k}^{0}(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})}}{(\energy{k}^{0})^{2}-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}^{2}+i\varepsilon}\\
&=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}\frac{e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0}|}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}\\
&=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}\\
&=\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned} \tag{3.79}
$$

The result is

$$
\boxed{\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=\int\frac{d^{4}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{i e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}+i\varepsilon},} \tag{3.80}
$$

or in momentum space,

$$
\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_{F}(\mechanical{k})=\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}+i\varepsilon}. \tag{3.81}
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\Delta}_{F}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Feynman function | The time-ordered coordinate-space propagator. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinates | The field events and time in the step-function representation. |
| $\mechanical{k},\mechanical{\mathbf k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The Fourier four-momentum and spatial mode momentum. |
| $\energy{k}^{0},\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Energies | The integration energy and positive on-shell energy. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fourier angular frequency | The frequency conjugate to time in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The scalar-particle rest mass. |
| $\mechanical{\tau}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time difference | The observation time minus the source time. |
| $\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_{F}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Momentum-space Feynman propagator | The Fourier function with the Feynman pole prescription. |
