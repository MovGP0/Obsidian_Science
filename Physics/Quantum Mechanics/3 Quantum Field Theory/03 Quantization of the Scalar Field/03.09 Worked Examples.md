## Example 3.13 — Large-Mass Feynman Propagator and the Nonrelativistic Limit

Consider the nonrelativistic limit of the Feynman propagator at large mass.

For the positive-time branch, the source starts from

$$
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})+i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}. \tag{3.91}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (3.91) displays only the positive-time branch of the full Feynman function. The following nonrelativistic calculation uses that branch.

Expand $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\approx\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{\mathbf k}^{2}/(2\mechanical{m})$ and separate the rest-mass phase:

$$
e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})}\approx e^{-i\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})}e^{-i\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf k}^{2}}{2\mechanical{m}}(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})}. \tag{3.92}
$$

Approximate the denominator by twice the mass:

$$
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})|_{\mathrm{NR}}\approx\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})}}{2\mechanical{m}}\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf k}^{2}}{2\mechanical{m}}(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})+i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}. \tag{3.93}
$$

The Gaussian integral is

$$
\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{-i\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf k}^{2}}{2\mechanical{m}}\mechanical{\tau}+i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf r}}=\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}}{2\pi i\mechanical{\tau}}\right)^{3/2}e^{i\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf r}^{2}/(2\mechanical{\tau})}, \tag{3.94}
$$

with time difference $\mechanical{\tau}=\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0}$ and spatial displacement $\mechanical{\mathbf r}=\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}$. Apart from the rest-mass factor, this is the free Schrödinger propagator.

## Example 3.14 — Vacuum Expectation of the Squared Field

Translation invariance makes the coincident expectation independent of position:

$$
\langle\signal{\phi}^{2}(\mechanical{x})\rangle\equiv\langle0|\signal{\phi}^{2}(\mechanical{x})|0\rangle=\lim_{y\to x}\signal{\Delta}^{+}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=\signal{\Delta}^{+}(0)=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}. \tag{3.95}
$$

At large momentum the energy approaches its magnitude. The cutoff integral grows as $\int_0^{\mechanical{\Lambda}}k^{2}dk/k\propto\mechanical{\Lambda}^{2}$, a quadratic ultraviolet divergence.

The squared-field observable requires regularization and renormalization. The source describes its renormalized value as a parameter fixed experimentally or incorporated in the physical-field definition. In $d=4-2\varepsilon$ dimensions,

$$
\langle\signal{\phi}^{2}(\mechanical{x})\rangle_{\mathrm{reg}}=-\frac{\mechanical{m}^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left(\frac1\varepsilon-\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^{2}}{\energy{\mu}^{2}}+1+O(\varepsilon)\right). \tag{3.96}
$$

The renormalization scale is the energy scale in the logarithm. In the modified minimal-subtraction convention, the printed pole stands for $1/\varepsilon-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi$. The negative sign follows from the negative pole of $\Gamma(-1+\varepsilon)$.

## Example 3.15 — Wick Decomposition and Normal Ordering

Wick's lemma expands a time-ordered product into normal-ordered products with all possible contractions. For two fields,

$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}+\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}). \tag{3.97}
$$

A direct proof is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
&=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\\
&=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})[\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}+[\signal{\phi}^{+}(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^{-}(\mechanical{y})]]\\
&\quad+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})[\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\}+[\signal{\phi}^{+}(\mechanical{y}),\signal{\phi}^{-}(\mechanical{x})]]\\
&=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}+\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})[\signal{\phi}^{+}(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^{-}(\mechanical{y})]\\
&\quad+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})[\signal{\phi}^{+}(\mechanical{y}),\signal{\phi}^{-}(\mechanical{x})].
\end{aligned} \tag{3.98}
$$

The normal-ordered two-field product is symmetric under exchange of its arguments.

The extra c-number term is

$$
\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0})\langle0|[\signal{\phi}^{+}(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^{-}(\mechanical{y})]|0\rangle+\theta(\mechanical{y}^{0}-\mechanical{x}^{0})\langle0|[\signal{\phi}^{+}(\mechanical{y}),\signal{\phi}^{-}(\mechanical{x})]|0\rangle=\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}). \tag{3.99}
$$

Thus the contraction of two scalar fields is

$$
\operatorname{contr}[\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})]=\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}). \tag{3.100}
$$

## Example 3.16 — Free-Field Spectrum and Dispersion Relation

The normal-ordered energy and momentum are $\energy{H}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{k}\,\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}a^{\dagger}a/(2\pi)^{3}$ and $\mechanical{\mathbf P}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{k}\,\mechanical{\mathbf k}a^{\dagger}a/(2\pi)^{3}$. Therefore

$$
\energy{H}|\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rangle=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rangle,\qquad
\mechanical{\mathbf P}|\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rangle=\mechanical{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rangle. \tag{3.101}
$$

The four-momentum $\mechanical{p}^{\mu}=(\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k},\mechanical{\mathbf k})$ obeys $\mechanical{p}^{2}=\mechanical{m}^{2}$. The on-shell scalar particle has mass $\mechanical{m}$ and spin zero, in agreement with Wigner's classification of Poincaré representations.
