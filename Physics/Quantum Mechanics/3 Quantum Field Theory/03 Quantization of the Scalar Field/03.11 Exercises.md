## Exercise 3.1 — Mode-Operator Commutators

Starting from the canonical field and momentum commutators, derive $[a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}')$ directly from (3.18)–(3.19). Also verify that the two annihilation operators commute.

## Exercise 3.2 — Hamiltonian in Mode Operators

Show in detail that the density $\energy{\mathcal H}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\pi}^{2}+\tfrac12(\nabla\signal{\phi})^{2}+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}$, integrated over space and normal ordered, gives

$$
\energy{H}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}). \tag{3.104}
$$

**Hint:** use $\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,e^{i(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}')\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}')$.

## Exercise 3.3 — Momentum Observable

Express $\mechanical{P}^{i}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{x})\partial^{i}\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$ in mode operators. Show that normal ordering gives

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf P}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\mechanical{\mathbf k}a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}). \tag{3.105}
$$

Verify momentum conservation, $[\energy{H},\mechanical{\mathbf P}]=0$.

## Exercise 3.4 — Noether Current for Spatial Translations

Use $\mechanical{x}^{\mu}\to\mechanical{x}^{\mu}+\varepsilon^{\mu}$ and the energy–momentum tensor to derive the conserved momentum $\mechanical{P}^{i}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{T}^{0i}$. Verify agreement with Exercise 3.3.

## Exercise 3.5 — Complex Scalar Charge Observable

Use the Lagrangian (3.46).

**(a)** Calculate the global U(1) Noether current $\electric{j}^{\mu}$.

**(b)** Verify its conservation using the field equations.

**(c)** Insert the mode expansion into $\electric{Q}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\electric{j}^{0}$ and obtain (3.55).

**(d)** Verify that charge commutes with the Hamiltonian.

## Exercise 3.6 — Explicit Microcausality

Show explicitly that the Pauli–Jordan function vanishes at spacelike separation. First prove

$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{x}^{0}=0)=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\sin(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x})}{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}=0. \tag{3.106}
$$

Use momentum reversal to evaluate (3.106), then verify that every spacelike separation can be transformed to equal time by a proper orthochronous transformation.

**Hint:** up to an overall sign,

$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x})\propto\operatorname{sgn}(\mechanical{x}^{0})\left[\delta(\mechanical{x}^{2})-\frac{\mechanical{m}\theta(\mechanical{x}^{2})}{2\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^{2}}}J_1(\mechanical{m}\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^{2}})\right],
$$

which vanishes outside the light cone.

> [!note]
> The source describes the sine integrand as invariant under momentum reversal. The sine is odd; the integration domain and measure are invariant.

## Exercise 3.7 — Massless Feynman Propagator

Calculate the configuration-space massless scalar propagator using spherical coordinates and the pole regulator. Obtain

$$
\signal{\Delta}_{F}(\mechanical{x})|_{m=0}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to0^{+}}\frac{-1}{4\pi^{2}(\mechanical{x}^{2}-i\varepsilon)}, \tag{3.107}
$$

where $\mechanical{x}^{2}=(\mechanical{x}^{0})^{2}-|\mechanical{\mathbf x}|^{2}$. **Hint:** integrate first over energy by residues, then over spatial momentum angles, and finally over its magnitude.

## Exercise 3.8 — Configuration-Space Retarded Propagator

Calculate the massive scalar retarded function and show the expression printed in the source:

$$
\signal{\Delta}_{R}(\mechanical{x})=\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0})\frac{1}{2\pi}\delta(\mechanical{x}^{2})-\theta(\mechanical{x}^{0})\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^{2}}}J_1(\mechanical{m}\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^{2}}),\qquad \mechanical{x}^{2}>0. \tag{3.108}
$$

It vanishes for spacelike arguments. The function with subscript one is the Bessel function of the first kind. Discuss the causal interpretation.

> [!note]
> The source formula (3.108) has the opposite overall sign to the negative-source retarded convention in (3.64). The printed exercise formula is retained; Solution 3.8 gives the form consistent with (3.64).

## Exercise 3.9 — Noether's Theorem and Lorentz Invariance

A scalar field is invariant under $\mechanical{x}^{\mu}\to\mechanical{x}^{\mu}+\omega^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\mechanical{x}^{\nu}$ with antisymmetric lowered parameters.

**(a)** Determine the field variation, using $\signal{\phi}'(\mechanical{x}')=\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$.

**(b)** Derive the angular-momentum current and charges $\mechanical{L}^{\mu\nu}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\mechanical{\mathcal M}^{0\mu\nu}$.

**(c)** Verify the Lorentz algebra of the conserved charges.

## Exercise 3.10 — Particle Number in a Coherent State

For a complex function of momentum, define a coherent state by $a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})|\alpha\rangle=\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})|\alpha\rangle$.

**(a)** Show that

$$
|\alpha\rangle=\exp\left[\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}(\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})-\signal{\alpha}^{*}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}))\right]|0\rangle.
$$

**(b)** Calculate the mean and variance of particle number in this state.

**(c)** Show that $\langle\signal{\hat\phi}(\mechanical{x})\rangle_{\alpha}$ is a classical Klein–Gordon solution. This supports the interpretation of coherent states as quasiclassical states.

## Source solutions

The following solutions are supplied in Appendix D of the source.

### Solution 3.1 — Mode-operator commutators

At $\mechanical{t}=0$, use the inverse-mode formulas
$$
a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}
\left[
\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2}\,\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})
+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}}\,\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})
\right],
$$
$$
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf y}\,e^{-i\mechanical{\mathbf k}'\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf y}}
\left[
\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}2}\,\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf y})
-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}\,\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf y})
\right].
$$
Their commutator is
$$
\begin{aligned}
[a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]
={}&\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,d^3\mechanical{\mathbf y}\,
e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}
-i\mechanical{\mathbf k}'\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf y}}\\
&\times\Biggl[
\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2}
\frac{-i}{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}
[\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}),\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf y})]\\
&\qquad+
\frac{i}{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}}
\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}2}
[\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}),\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf y})]
\Biggr].
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the equal-time canonical commutators,
$$
[\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}),\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf y})]
=i\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}),
$$
and the reversed commutator with the opposite sign, this becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
[a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]
={}&\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,d^3\mechanical{\mathbf y}\,
e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}
-i\mechanical{\mathbf k}'\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf y}}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})\\
&\times\frac12
\left(
\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}
+\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
After integrating over $\mechanical{\mathbf y}$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
[a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]
={}&\frac12
\left(
\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}
+\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}}
\right)\\
&\times\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
e^{i(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}')\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last integral is $(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}')$.
Its support enforces equal momenta and equal mode energies, so the numerical coefficient is one:
$$
[a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}').
$$

For two annihilation operators, the mixed field-momentum commutators have opposite signs.
Their coefficient is proportional to
$$
\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}
-\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}}.
$$
The spatial integral gives a delta function supported at
$\mechanical{\mathbf k}'=-\mechanical{\mathbf k}$, where the two energies agree.
Thus
$$
[a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]=0.
$$

### Solution 3.2 — Hamiltonian in mode operators

Start from
$$
\energy{H}
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}
\left[
\frac12\mechanical{\pi}^2
+\frac12(\boldsymbol\nabla\signal{\phi})^2
+\frac12\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\phi}^2
\right].
$$
The conjugate momentum is
$$
\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
(-i)\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2}
\left[
a_{\mathbf k}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
-a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
\right],
$$
where $a_{\mathbf k}\equiv a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})$ and
$\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}$.

The momentum contribution is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\frac12\mechanical{\pi}^2
={}&\frac12\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}
\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}'}{(2\pi)^3}
(-1)\frac{\sqrt{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}{2}\\
&\times\Bigl[
a_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k'}e^{-i(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{k}')\cdot\mechanical{x}}
-a_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k'}^\dagger
e^{-i(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{k}')\cdot\mechanical{x}}\\
&\qquad-a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k'}
e^{+i(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{k}')\cdot\mechanical{x}}
+a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k'}^\dagger
e^{+i(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{k}')\cdot\mechanical{x}}
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
The spatial integral gives
$(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\pm\mechanical{\mathbf k}')$.
On its support, $\mechanical{\mathbf k}'=\mp\mechanical{\mathbf k}$ for the corresponding phase and the mode energies agree.
After the primed integration,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\frac12\mechanical{\pi}^2
=\frac14\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\Bigl[
&-a_{\mathbf k}a_{-\mathbf k}e^{-2i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{t}}
+a_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\\
&+a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}
-a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger
e^{+2i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{t}}
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$

Treat the gradient and mass terms in the same way.
For the gradient term, the oscillating terms contain
$\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf k}'=-\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2$
on the opposite-momentum delta function.
The mass term contributes $\mechanical{m}^2$.
The total coefficient of the $aa$ and $a^\dagger a^\dagger$ terms is
$$
\frac{-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}^2
+\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2+\mechanical{m}^2}
{4\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}=0,
$$
using $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}^2=\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2+\mechanical{m}^2$.

The coefficient of the nonoscillating terms is
$$
\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}^2
+\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2+\mechanical{m}^2}
{4\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}
=\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2.
$$
Hence
$$
\energy{H}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
\left[
a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
+a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
\right].
$$
Normal ordering removes the vacuum constant and gives
$$
:\!\energy{H}\!:
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}).
$$

### Solution 3.3 — Momentum operator

With $\partial^i=-\partial_i$, the physical contravariant momentum is
$$
\mechanical{P}^i
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{x})\,\partial^i\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
=-\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
\mechanical{\pi}\,\partial_i\signal{\phi}.
$$

> [!note] Correction to the source
> The opening line of the source solution has the opposite sign. The sign here follows from $P^i=\int T^{0i}$ and agrees with the positive-momentum mode sum given later in the same solution.

Insert the expansions
$$
\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
(-i)\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2}
\left[
a_{\mathbf k}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
-a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
\right],
$$
$$
\partial_i\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}'}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{i\mechanical{k}'_i}{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}
\left[
a_{\mathbf k'}e^{-i\mechanical{k}'\cdot\mechanical{x}}
-a_{\mathbf k'}^\dagger e^{+i\mechanical{k}'\cdot\mechanical{x}}
\right].
$$
Here $\mechanical{k}'_i$ in the spatial Fourier coefficient denotes a Cartesian component of $\mechanical{\mathbf k}'$.
In particular,
$$
\partial_i e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
=i\mechanical{k}_i e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}},
\qquad
\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}
=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}.
$$
Perform the spatial integrals and use the momentum delta functions.
The oscillating terms cancel under $\mechanical{\mathbf k}\to-\mechanical{\mathbf k}$, leaving
$$
\mechanical{\mathbf P}
=\frac12\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\mechanical{\mathbf k}
\left[
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
+a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
\right].
$$
The commutator term is proportional to an odd momentum integral and vanishes with a symmetric momentum regulator.
Thus the normal-ordered form is
$$
\mechanical{\mathbf P}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\mechanical{\mathbf k}\,a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}).
$$

#### Conservation of momentum

Using the normal-ordered Hamiltonian,
$$
\begin{aligned}
[\energy{H},\mechanical{\mathbf P}]
={}&\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}'}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{\mathbf k}'\\
&\times
[a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')].
\end{aligned}
$$
Expand the product commutator with the mode-operator rules.
The delta functions make the exchanged terms cancel, giving
$$
[\energy{H},\mechanical{\mathbf P}]=0.
$$

### Solution 3.4 — Spatial translations

Under $\mechanical{x}^\mu\to\mechanical{x}^\mu+\mechanical{\varepsilon}^\mu$, the intrinsic scalar variation is $\Delta\signal{\phi}=0$.
The Noether translation current is
$$
\energy{T}^\mu{}_\nu
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})}
\partial_\nu\signal{\phi}
-\delta^\mu{}_\nu\energy{\mathcal L}
=\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}\,\partial_\nu\signal{\phi}
-\delta^\mu{}_\nu\energy{\mathcal L}.
$$
After raising the spatial charge index,
$$
\mechanical{P}^i
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\energy{T}^{0i}
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
\partial^0\signal{\phi}\,\partial^i\signal{\phi}
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\mechanical{\pi}\,\partial^i\signal{\phi}.
$$

Using $\partial^i=-\partial_i$,
$$
\mechanical{P}^i=-\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\mechanical{\pi}\,\partial_i\signal{\phi}.
$$
Inserting the mode expansions gives
$$
\mechanical{P}^i
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\mechanical{k}^i\,a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),
$$
in agreement with Solution 3.3.

### Solution 3.5 — Complex-scalar U(1) charge

**(a)** For
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi}^*)(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi})
-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\phi}^*\signal{\phi},
$$
use the infinitesimal transformation
$$
\delta\signal{\phi}=i\signal{\phi},
\qquad
\delta\signal{\phi}^*=-i\signal{\phi}^*,
$$
with its constant phase parameter suppressed.
The direct Noether expression is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{j}_{\mathrm{can}}^\mu
&=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})}
(i\signal{\phi})
+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi}^*)}
(-i\signal{\phi}^*)\\
&=(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^*)(i\signal{\phi})
+(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi})(-i\signal{\phi}^*)\\
&=i(\signal{\phi}\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^*
-\signal{\phi}^*\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The source chooses the opposite overall charge convention:
$$
\electric{j}^\mu
=i(\signal{\phi}^*\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}
-\signal{\phi}\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^*).
$$

**(b)** Its divergence is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\mu\electric{j}^\mu
=i\Bigl[
&(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi}^*)(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi})
+\signal{\phi}^*\Box\signal{\phi}\\
&-(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^*)
-\signal{\phi}\Box\signal{\phi}^*
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
The derivative products cancel.
The field equations then give
$$
\partial_\mu\electric{j}^\mu
=i\left[
\signal{\phi}^*(-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\phi})
-\signal{\phi}(-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\phi}^*)
\right]=0.
$$

**(c)** Insert the mode expansions (3.52)–(3.53) into
$$
\electric{Q}
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\electric{j}^0
=i\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}
(\signal{\phi}^*\dot{\signal{\phi}}-\signal{\phi}\dot{\signal{\phi}}^*).
$$
The spatial integral supplies momentum delta functions, and the oscillating terms cancel.
After normal ordering,
$$
\electric{Q}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\left[
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
-b^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})b(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
\right]
=N_a-N_b.
$$
Normal ordering removes the vacuum contribution proportional to $\delta^{(3)}(0)$.

**(d)** The normal-ordered Hamiltonian is
$$
\energy{H}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}
\left[a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}
+b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k}\right].
$$
Occupation-number operators commute with themselves, and the two species commute with one another.
Therefore
$$
[\energy{H},\electric{Q}]=0.
$$

### Solution 3.6 — Explicit microcausality

At equal times, choose $\mechanical{x}^0=\mechanical{y}^0=0$.
Using
$e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}-e^{-i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}
=2i\sin(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x})$,
$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},0)
=-i\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{2i\sin(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x})}
{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{\sin(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x})}
{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}.
$$
The sine is odd under $\mechanical{\mathbf k}\to-\mechanical{\mathbf k}$, while
$\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}=\energy{\omega}_{-\mathbf k}$ is even.
The integral vanishes:
$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},0)=0.
$$

> [!note]
> The source excludes the coincident point from the displayed zero and adds a possible distributional caveat. The equal-time field-field commutator is zero as a distribution, including coincidence. A delta distribution appears in the field-momentum commutator, or the corresponding time derivative.

Any spacelike separation can be boosted to a frame in which its time component vanishes.
In that frame, the preceding argument gives zero.
Since the Pauli–Jordan function is a Lorentz scalar, it vanishes in every frame for spacelike separation.

For $\mechanical{m}>0$, the individual Wightman functions do not vanish there.
They can be expressed with the modified Bessel function $K_1$:
$$
\left.\signal{\Delta}^+(\mechanical{x})\right|_{\mechanical{x}^2<0}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi^2\sqrt{-\mechanical{x}^2}}\,
K_1\!\left(\mechanical{m}\sqrt{-\mechanical{x}^2}\right).
$$
At spacelike separation,
$$
\signal{\Delta}^-(\mechanical{x})=\signal{\Delta}^+(\mechanical{x}),
$$
so their commutator difference vanishes.

### Solution 3.7 — Massless Feynman propagator

Start from the momentum representation
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i\,e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}}{\mechanical{k}^2+i\varepsilon}
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i\,e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}}
{(\mechanical{k}^0)^2-\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2+i\varepsilon}.
$$
Integrating over $\mechanical{k}^0$ by residues gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})
={}&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0)
\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{e^{-i\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert\mechanical{x}^0
+i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}}
{2\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert}\\
&+\theta(-\mechanical{x}^0)
\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{e^{+i\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert\mechanical{x}^0
-i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}}
{2\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Reverse $\mechanical{\mathbf k}$ in the negative-time term to combine the two contributions:
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{
e^{-i\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert
+i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}}
{2\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert}.
$$

Use spherical coordinates with
$$
\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert=\mechanical{k},
\qquad
\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}=\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}\cos\theta,
\qquad
\mechanical{r}=\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf x}\rvert.
$$
The angular integrals give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})
&=\frac1{(2\pi)^3}
\int_0^\infty \mechanical{k}^2d\mechanical{k}
\int_0^\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta
\int_0^{2\pi}d\varphi\,
\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{k}\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert+i\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}\cos\theta}}
{2\mechanical{k}}\\
&=\frac1{(2\pi)^2}
\int_0^\infty\frac{\mechanical{k}\,d\mechanical{k}}2
\int_{-1}^1du\,
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert+i\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}u}\\
&=\frac1{(2\pi)^2}
\int_0^\infty\frac{\mechanical{k}\,d\mechanical{k}}2
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert}
\frac{e^{i\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}}-e^{-i\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}}}{i\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}}\\
&=\frac1{(2\pi)^2\,2i\mechanical{r}}
\int_0^\infty d\mechanical{k}\,
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert}
\left[e^{i\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}}-e^{-i\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r}}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

For convergence, take
$\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert\to\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert-i\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon>0$.
Then
$$
\int_0^\infty d\mechanical{k}\,
e^{-i\mechanical{k}(\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert-\mechanical{r}-i\varepsilon)}
=\frac{-i}{\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert-\mechanical{r}-i\varepsilon},
$$
$$
\int_0^\infty d\mechanical{k}\,
e^{-i\mechanical{k}(\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert+\mechanical{r}-i\varepsilon)}
=\frac{-i}{\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert+\mechanical{r}-i\varepsilon}.
$$
Therefore, before taking the regulator limit,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_{F,\varepsilon}(\mechanical{x})
&=-\frac1{(2\pi)^2\,2\mechanical{r}}
\left[
\frac1{\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert-\mechanical{r}-i\varepsilon}
-\frac1{\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert+\mechanical{r}-i\varepsilon}
\right]\\
&=-\frac1{4\pi^2}
\frac1{(\lvert\mechanical{x}^0\rvert-i\varepsilon)^2-\mechanical{r}^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
With $\mechanical{x}^2=(\mechanical{x}^0)^2-\mechanical{r}^2$, the distributional limit has the usual Feynman form
$$
\left.\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})\right|_{\mechanical{m}=0}
=\lim_{\varepsilon\to0^+}
\frac{-1}{4\pi^2(\mechanical{x}^2-i\varepsilon)}.
$$

> [!note]
> The finite coordinate-time convergence regulator and the infinitesimal in the final Lorentz-invariant shorthand are not literally the same finite parameter. The last formula denotes the Feynman distributional boundary value.

### Solution 3.8 — Retarded scalar propagator

With the chapter's negative-source convention,
$$
\widetilde{\signal{\Delta}}_R(\mechanical{k})
=\frac1{(\energy{k}^0+i\varepsilon)^2
-\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2-\mechanical{m}^2}.
$$
Both poles lie in the lower energy half-plane:
$$
\energy{k}^0=\pm\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}-i\varepsilon.
$$
For $\mechanical{x}^0>0$, close the contour below.
The clockwise contour encloses both poles.
Its residue sum gives
$$
\signal{\Delta}_R(\mechanical{x})
=-i\theta(\mechanical{x}^0)
\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}
\left[
e^{-i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{x}^0+i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}
-e^{+i\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\mechanical{x}^0+i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}
\right].
$$
For $\mechanical{x}^0<0$, close above. No poles are enclosed, so $\signal{\Delta}_R=0$.

The momentum representation implies
$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{\Delta}_R(\mechanical{x})
=-\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}).
$$
The corresponding configuration-space expression is
$$
\signal{\Delta}_R(\mechanical{x})
=\theta(\mechanical{x}^0)
\left[
-\frac1{2\pi}\delta(\mechanical{x}^2)
+\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^2}}
J_1\!\left(\mechanical{m}\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^2}\right)
\theta(\mechanical{x}^2)
\right].
$$
The Bessel tail is understood on the timelike region $\mechanical{x}^2>0$.
The light-cone term gives propagation on the cone; the massive term has support inside the future cone.
For spacelike separation, $\mechanical{x}^2<0$, the function vanishes.
Its support therefore respects causality.

> [!note] Correction to the source
> The source's final configuration-space bracket has the opposite overall sign, although it uses the momentum denominator above and states a negative delta source. That printed bracket solves the positive-source equation. The expression here has the sign required by (3.64) and the source's own residue formula. Reversing the complete propagator sign gives the alternative positive-source convention.

### Solution 3.9 — Lorentz Noether current

**(a)** For a scalar,
$$
\signal{\phi}'(\mechanical{x}')=\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}),
\qquad
\Delta\signal{\phi}=0.
$$
At a fixed coordinate point, the variation is
$$
\delta\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
=\signal{\phi}'(\mechanical{x})-\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
=-\omega^\mu{}_\nu\mechanical{x}^\nu\partial_\mu\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}).
$$

**(b)** For $\delta\mechanical{x}^\mu=\omega^\mu{}_\nu\mechanical{x}^\nu$ and intrinsic variation $\Delta\signal{\phi}=0$, the Noether current is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\mathcal J}^\mu
&=-\energy{T}^\mu{}_\rho\,\omega^\rho{}_\nu\mechanical{x}^\nu\\
&=\frac12\omega_{\rho\sigma}
\left(
\mechanical{x}^\rho\energy{T}^{\mu\sigma}
-\mechanical{x}^\sigma\energy{T}^{\mu\rho}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Identify the angular-momentum current
$$
\mechanical{\mathcal M}^{\mu\rho\sigma}
=\mechanical{x}^\rho\energy{T}^{\mu\sigma}
-\mechanical{x}^\sigma\energy{T}^{\mu\rho}.
$$
The conserved charges are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{L}^{\rho\sigma}
&=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\mechanical{\mathcal M}^{0\rho\sigma}\\
&=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}
\left(
\mechanical{x}^\rho\energy{T}^{0\sigma}
-\mechanical{x}^\sigma\energy{T}^{0\rho}
\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

**(c)** Use the canonical equal-time commutator
$$
[\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}),\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf y})]
=i\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})
$$
and integrate spatial derivatives by parts.
The charge commutator is
$$
[\mechanical{L}^{\mu\nu},\mechanical{L}^{\rho\sigma}]
=i\left(
g^{\nu\rho}\mechanical{L}^{\mu\sigma}
-g^{\mu\rho}\mechanical{L}^{\nu\sigma}
-g^{\nu\sigma}\mechanical{L}^{\mu\rho}
+g^{\mu\sigma}\mechanical{L}^{\nu\rho}
\right).
$$
This is the Lorentz algebra $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)$.

> [!note] Correction to the source
> The source identifies the angular-momentum current and charges with the opposite overall sign, while keeping the commutator displayed above. Those choices do not agree. The current and charges here use the sign required by that algebra and by the standard spatial rotation generator; equivalently, reverse the source's charges.

### Solution 3.10 — Coherent-state particle number

**(a)** The displacement state is
$$
\lvert\alpha\rangle
=\exp\!\left[
\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\left(
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
-a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})\signal{\alpha}^*(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
\right)
\right]\lvert0\rangle.
$$
Use the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff identity
$$
e^{A+B}=e^Ae^Be^{-[A,B]/2}
$$
when $[A,B]$ is a scalar multiple of the identity.
Set
$$
A=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),
\qquad
B=-\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\signal{\alpha}^*(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}).
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
[A,B]
&=-\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}\,d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}'}{(2\pi)^6}
\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})\signal{\alpha}^*(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')
[a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]\\
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\lvert\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})\rvert^2
\equiv\lVert\signal{\alpha}\rVert^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Because $e^B\lvert0\rangle=\lvert0\rangle$,
$$
\lvert\alpha\rangle
=e^{-\lVert\signal{\alpha}\rVert^2/2}e^A\lvert0\rangle.
$$
Finally,
$$
\begin{aligned}
a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})\lvert\alpha\rangle
&=e^{-\lVert\signal{\alpha}\rVert^2/2}
a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})e^A\lvert0\rangle\\
&=e^{-\lVert\signal{\alpha}\rVert^2/2}
\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})e^A\lvert0\rangle\\
&=\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})\lvert\alpha\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last step uses
$$
[a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),e^A]
=\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})e^A,
$$
which follows from the mode commutator.

**(b)** The mean particle number is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\hat N\rangle_\alpha
&=\left\langle\alpha\left|
\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
\right|\alpha\right\rangle\\
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\lvert\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})\rvert^2
=\lVert\signal{\alpha}\rVert^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the second moment, write
$$
\hat N^2
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}'}{(2\pi)^3}
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k})a(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
a^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}').
$$
Commute the middle annihilation operator past the creation operator and use the coherent-state eigenvalue property:
$$
\langle\hat N^2\rangle_\alpha
=\lVert\signal{\alpha}\rVert^4+\lVert\signal{\alpha}\rVert^2.
$$
Thus
$$
(\Delta N)^2
=\langle\hat N^2\rangle_\alpha-\langle\hat N\rangle_\alpha^2
=\lVert\signal{\alpha}\rVert^2
=\langle\hat N\rangle_\alpha.
$$
The occupation number has a Poisson distribution.
Its relative fluctuation is
$$
\frac{\Delta N}{\langle\hat N\rangle_\alpha}
=\frac1{\sqrt{\langle\hat N\rangle_\alpha}}
\longrightarrow0
$$
at large mean occupation.

**(c)** The field expectation is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\rangle_\alpha
&=\left\langle\alpha\left|
\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{
a_{\mathbf k}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
+a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}}
{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}}
\right|\alpha\right\rangle\\
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{
\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
+\signal{\alpha}^*(\mechanical{\mathbf k})e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}}
{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
It is a superposition of on-shell plane waves, so
$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\langle\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\rangle_\alpha=0.
$$
The expectation value evolves as a classical free field.
Coherent states also saturate the uncertainty relation for each free oscillator mode, which supports their quasiclassical interpretation.
