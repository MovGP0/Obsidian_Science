---
title: "The Pauli–Jordan Function"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Pauli–Jordan function is the momentum integral that determines the scalar-field commutator.

For two events, substitute (3.13):

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})]
&=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}'}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}\bigl[[a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}+i\mechanical{k}'\cdot\mechanical{y}}+[a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}),a(\mechanical{\mathbf k}')]e^{i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}-i\mechanical{k}'\cdot\mechanical{y}}\bigr]\\
&=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}-e^{i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}\\
&\equiv i\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned} \tag{3.57}
$$

Define the Pauli–Jordan, or commutator, function by

$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=-i\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}-e^{i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}. \tag{3.58}
$$

The covariant form is

$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x})=-i\int\frac{d^{4}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{4}}(2\pi)\delta(\mechanical{k}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2})\operatorname{sgn}(\mechanical{k}^{0})e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}. \tag{3.59}
$$

It is invariant under proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations, which preserve the energy sign. It is also odd: $\signal{\Delta}(-\mechanical{x})=-\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x})$.
