---
title: "The Real Scalar Field and the Klein–Gordon Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The free real scalar Lagrangian contains a relativistic kinetic term and a quadratic mass term.

This model contains one real degree of freedom at each spatial point and no self-interaction. Use flat spacetime, the metric stated below, natural units, and a nonnegative mass squared. The field is a classical real function at this stage; after quantization its reality condition becomes Hermiticity. A real scalar quantum is its own antiparticle.

For a free real scalar field of mass $\mechanical{m}$,

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=\frac12(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})-\frac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}
=\frac12\signal{\dot\phi}^{2}-\frac12(\nabla\signal{\phi})^{2}-\frac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}. \tag{3.6}
$$

The Euler–Lagrange equations give

$$
(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\phi}=(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\phi}=0, \tag{3.7}
$$

with $\square=\partial_{\mechanical{t}}^{2}-\nabla^{2}$ in signature $(+,-,-,-)$. The conjugate momentum is

$$
\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\dot\phi}}=\signal{\dot\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}). \tag{3.8}
$$

In the Euler–Lagrange step, integration by parts transfers one derivative from the field variation to the field. The boundary variation is taken to vanish. The factor one half cancels the factor two from variation of each quadratic term. The momentum equals the time derivative because the kinetic term is canonically normalized; derivative interactions or a different normalization can change this relation.

The Legendre transformation gives

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=\mechanical{\pi}\signal{\dot\phi}-\energy{\mathcal L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\pi}^{2}+\tfrac12(\nabla\signal{\phi})^{2}+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}. \tag{3.9}
$$

The total Hamiltonian is $\energy{H}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{\mathcal H}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})$.

For nonnegative mass squared, every term in (3.9) is nonnegative for a classical real field. In the massless limit the mass term disappears, but the gradient term still costs energy for an inhomogeneous field. The Lagrangian density has energy dimension four, so the field has energy dimension one and the integrated Hamiltonian has energy dimension one. These checks test both the metric signs and the normalization.
