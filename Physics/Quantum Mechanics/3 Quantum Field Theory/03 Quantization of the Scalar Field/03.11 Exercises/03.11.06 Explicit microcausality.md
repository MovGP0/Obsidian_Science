## Exercise 3.6 — Explicit Microcausality

Show explicitly that the Pauli–Jordan function vanishes at spacelike separation. First prove

$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{x}^{0}=0)=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\sin(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x})}{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}=0. \tag{3.106}
$$

Use momentum reversal to evaluate (3.106), then verify that every spacelike separation can be transformed to equal time by a proper orthochronous transformation.

**Hint:** up to an overall sign,

$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x})\propto\operatorname{sgn}(\mechanical{x}^{0})\left[\delta(\mechanical{x}^{2})-\frac{\mechanical{m}\theta(\mechanical{x}^{2})}{2\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^{2}}}J_1(\mechanical{m}\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^{2}})\right],
$$

which vanishes outside the light cone.

> [!note]
> The source describes the sine integrand as invariant under momentum reversal. The sine is odd; the integration domain and measure are invariant.

## Solution 3.6 — Explicit microcausality

At equal times, choose $\mechanical{x}^0=\mechanical{y}^0=0$.
Using
$e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}-e^{-i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}
=2i\sin(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x})$,
$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},0)
=-i\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{2i\sin(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x})}
{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{\sin(\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x})}
{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}.
$$
The sine is odd under $\mechanical{\mathbf k}\to-\mechanical{\mathbf k}$, while
$\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}=\energy{\omega}_{-\mathbf k}$ is even.
The integral vanishes:
$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},0)=0.
$$

> [!note]
> The source excludes the coincident point from the displayed zero and adds a possible distributional caveat. The equal-time field-field commutator is zero as a distribution, including coincidence. A delta distribution appears in the field-momentum commutator, or the corresponding time derivative.

Any spacelike separation can be boosted to a frame in which its time component vanishes.
In that frame, the preceding argument gives zero.
Since the Pauli–Jordan function is a Lorentz scalar, it vanishes in every frame for spacelike separation.

For $\mechanical{m}>0$, the individual Wightman functions do not vanish there.
They can be expressed with the modified Bessel function $K_1$:
$$
\left.\signal{\Delta}^+(\mechanical{x})\right|_{\mechanical{x}^2<0}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi^2\sqrt{-\mechanical{x}^2}}\,
K_1\!\left(\mechanical{m}\sqrt{-\mechanical{x}^2}\right).
$$
At spacelike separation,
$$
\signal{\Delta}^-(\mechanical{x})=\signal{\Delta}^+(\mechanical{x}),
$$
so their commutator difference vanishes.
