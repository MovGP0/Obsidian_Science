---
title: "Crossing Rule for Fermions and Photons"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Fermion crossing exchanges particle and antiparticle spinors while reversing the external four-momentum.

The source writes the formal fermion replacements
$$
u(\mechanical{p})\longleftrightarrow v(-\mechanical{p}),
\qquad
\bar u(\mechanical{p})\longleftrightarrow\bar v(-\mechanical{p}).
$$
These are analytic crossing prescriptions with compatible spinor-phase conventions.

The source states that a photon polarization vector is unchanged under crossing.

> [!note]
> The photon is its own antiparticle, but an incoming and an outgoing physical photon use complex-conjugate polarization factors. The source's next example uses $\signal{\epsilon}\to\signal{\epsilon}^*$ explicitly.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Crossed momentum | The fermion four-momentum whose sign changes. |
| $u,\bar u,v,\bar v$ | $\unit{MeV^{1/2}}$ | Crossed spinors | The particle and antiparticle external-state spinors. |
| $\signal{\epsilon}$ | $1$ | Photon polarization | The external vector that is conjugated when the photon changes between in and out states. |
