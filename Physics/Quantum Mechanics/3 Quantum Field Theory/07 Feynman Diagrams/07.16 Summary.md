> [!TLDR]
> Feynman diagrams give systematic rules for scattering amplitudes and show the structure of loop corrections.

This chapter introduced Feynman diagrams as a systematic tool for the calculation of scattering amplitudes in quantum field theory.

The main points are:

- Feynman diagrams represent Wick contractions. Each diagram corresponds to a precise term in the perturbative expansion of the S-matrix.
- The Feynman rules in Table 7.1 convert each diagram element into an algebraic factor: propagators for internal lines, spinors or polarization vectors for external lines, and $-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu$ for QED vertices.
- Symmetry factors come from permutations of identical fields in Wick's theorem. They require care and must be distinguished from the combinatorial factors in the perturbative expansion.
- Fermions require special rules in QED. Gamma matrices are multiplied from right to left along the fermion lines. Each closed fermion loop contributes a factor $-1$. Exchange of identical final-state fermions gives a relative sign.
- Trace methods and spinor completeness relations give a systematic calculation of $\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}$.
- The Mandelstam variables $\energy{s},\mechanical{t},\mechanical{u}$ describe the scattering channels in a Lorentz-invariant form.
- Loop diagrams generally have ultraviolet divergences. In QED, their superficial degree is $D=4-E_\gamma-\tfrac32 E_e$, where $E_\gamma$ and $E_e$ count external photon and fermion legs.
- Renormalization, treated in Chapter 9, is the systematic method used to extract finite physical predictions from divergent loop diagrams.

Feynman diagrams form the perturbative language of quantum field theory. The results in this chapter provide the basis for the loop calculations and renormalization program in Chapter 9.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{e}$ | $1$ | Electromagnetic coupling | The QED charge parameter in natural units. |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}$ | $1$ | Scattering amplitude | The invariant two-to-two amplitude used in the spin average. |
| $\energy{s}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Squared centre-of-mass energy | The energy invariant for two-to-two scattering. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{u}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Mandelstam transfers | The momentum-transfer invariants for crossed channels. |
