> [!TLDR]
> Electron–positron annihilation into a muon pair proceeds through one virtual photon at tree level.

Consider
$$
\mathrm e^+(\mechanical{q}_1)\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{q}_2)
\to\mu^+(\mechanical{p}_1)\mu^-(\mechanical{p}_2).
$$
At tree level there is one s-channel diagram. The electron and positron annihilate into a virtual photon, which produces the muon–antimuon pair.

![[QFT-07-11-Lepton-Annihilation.svg|**Fig. 7.11** Tree-level s-channel electron–positron annihilation into a muon pair. The virtual photon carries the total four-momentum q = q₁ + q₂.]]

The incoming positron contributes $\bar v(\mechanical{q}_1)$. Applying the rules gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\signal{\mathcal M}
={}&\bar v(\mechanical{q}_1)(-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu)u(\mechanical{q}_2)
\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{(\mechanical{q}_1+\mechanical{q}_2)^2+i\epsilon}\\
&\times\bar u(\mechanical{p}_2)(-i\electric{e}\gamma^\nu)v(\mechanical{p}_1).
\end{aligned}
$$
With $\energy{s}=(\mechanical{q}_1+\mechanical{q}_2)^2$,
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}
=\frac{\electric{e}^{\,2}}{\energy{s}}
[\bar v(\mechanical{q}_1)\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{q}_2)]
[\bar u(\mechanical{p}_2)\gamma_\mu v(\mechanical{p}_1)].
$$

> [!note]
> The recreated incoming-positron fermion-flow arrow points away from the annihilation vertex, consistent with the $\bar v$ external factor. The arrow on that leg in the source appears to point toward the vertex.

## Spin average and trace method

For an unpolarized experiment, average the two initial spin states of each beam particle and sum the final spin states:
$$
\overline{\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2}
=\frac14\sum_{\mathrm{spin}}\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2.
$$
The initial-state factor is $1/4=(1/2)^2$. Use the spinor completeness relation
$$
\sum_su^s(\mechanical{p})\bar u^s(\mechanical{p})
=\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m}.
$$

For antiparticles,
$$
\sum_sv^s(\mechanical{p})\bar v^s(\mechanical{p})
=\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}.
$$
Use independent Lorentz indices for the amplitude and its complex conjugate:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2
=\frac{\electric{e}^{\,4}}{\energy{s}^{\,2}}
&[\bar v(\mechanical{q}_1)\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{q}_2)]
[\bar v(\mechanical{q}_1)\gamma^\nu u(\mechanical{q}_2)]^*\\
&\times[\bar u(\mechanical{p}_2)\gamma_\mu v(\mechanical{p}_1)]
[\bar u(\mechanical{p}_2)\gamma_\nu v(\mechanical{p}_1)]^*.
\end{aligned}
$$
For a general bilinear, $(\bar u\Gamma v)^*=\bar v\bar\Gamma u$, with $\bar\Gamma=\gamma^0\Gamma^\dagger\gamma^0$. Since $\bar{\gamma^\mu}=\gamma^\mu$,
$$
[\bar v(\mechanical{q}_1)\gamma^\nu u(\mechanical{q}_2)]^*
=\bar u(\mechanical{q}_2)\gamma^\nu v(\mechanical{q}_1),
$$
$$
[\bar u(\mechanical{p}_2)\gamma_\nu v(\mechanical{p}_1)]^*
=\bar v(\mechanical{p}_1)\gamma_\nu u(\mechanical{p}_2).
$$
The spin sums become
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{\mathrm{spin}}
[\bar v(\mechanical{q}_1)\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{q}_2)]
[\bar u(\mechanical{q}_2)\gamma^\nu v(\mechanical{q}_1)]\\
&\qquad=\operatorname{Tr}
[(\not{\mechanical{q}}_1-\mechanical{m}_e)\gamma^\mu
(\not{\mechanical{q}}_2+\mechanical{m}_e)\gamma^\nu],
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{\mathrm{spin}}
[\bar u(\mechanical{p}_2)\gamma_\mu v(\mechanical{p}_1)]
[\bar v(\mechanical{p}_1)\gamma_\nu u(\mechanical{p}_2)]\\
&\qquad=\operatorname{Tr}
[(\not{\mechanical{p}}_2+\mechanical{m}_\mu)\gamma_\mu
(\not{\mechanical{p}}_1-\mechanical{m}_\mu)\gamma_\nu].
\end{aligned}
$$
The source lists
$$
\operatorname{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu]=4g^{\mu\nu}, \tag{7.3}
$$
$$
\operatorname{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma]
=4(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}
+g^{\sigma\rho}g^{\mu\nu}), \tag{7.4}
$$
and a zero trace for an odd number of gamma matrices.

> [!note]
> The source reuses $\mu$ in both an amplitude and its conjugate and then changes a free index under conjugation. Independent $\mu,\nu$ indices are used above to make the contractions consistent.
>
> Equation (7.4) is retained as printed, but its last term repeats the first metric pairing. The correct last term is $+g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}$.

Neglecting the electron mass when $\sqrt{\energy{s}}\gg\mechanical{m}_e$, the electron tensor is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{L}_e^{\mu\nu}
&=\operatorname{Tr}
[\not{\mechanical{q}}_1\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{q}}_2\gamma^\nu]\\
&=4(\mechanical{q}_1^\mu\mechanical{q}_2^\nu
+\mechanical{q}_1^\nu\mechanical{q}_2^\mu
-g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_1\cdot\mechanical{q}_2).
\end{aligned}
$$

At high energy, also neglect the muon mass. The muon tensor is
$$
\signal{L}_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}
=4(\mechanical{p}_{2\mu}\mechanical{p}_{1\nu}
+\mechanical{p}_{2\nu}\mechanical{p}_{1\mu}
-g_{\mu\nu}\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2).
$$
The label $(m)$ denotes the muon sector, rather than a Lorentz index. Tensor contraction gives
$$
\signal{L}_e^{\mu\nu}\signal{L}_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}
=16[2(\mechanical{q}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)
(\mechanical{q}_2\cdot\mechanical{p}_1)
+2(\mechanical{q}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_1)
(\mechanical{q}_2\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)]. \tag{7.5}
$$
In the CM frame, $\sqrt{\energy{s}}=\energy{E}_{\mathrm{CM}}$. Let $\mechanical{\theta}$ be the angle of the outgoing negative muon relative to the incoming electron:
$$
\mechanical{q}_2\cdot\mechanical{p}_2
=\mechanical{q}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_1
=\frac{\energy{s}}4(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}),
$$
$$
\mechanical{q}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2
=\mechanical{q}_2\cdot\mechanical{p}_1
=\frac{\energy{s}}4(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
Thus
$$
\signal{L}_e^{\mu\nu}\signal{L}_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}
=8\energy{s}^{\,2}
\frac{(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2+(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2}{4}
=4\energy{s}^{\,2}(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
The averaged squared amplitude is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2}
&=\frac14\frac{\electric{e}^{\,4}}{\energy{s}^{\,2}}
\,4\energy{s}^{\,2}(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})\\
&=\electric{e}^{\,4}(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})
=16\pi^2\electric{\alpha}^{\,2}(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}).
\end{aligned} \tag{7.6}
$$

## Differential cross section

In the negligible-mass two-body CM limit,
$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac1{64\pi^2\energy{s}}
\,\overline{\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2}. \tag{7.7}
$$

Substitution gives
$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^{\,2}}{4\energy{s}}
(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})}.
$$
Integrate over $d\mechanical{\Omega}=\sin\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\varphi}$:
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}
=\frac{4\pi\electric{\alpha}^{\,2}}{3\energy{s}}. \tag{7.7}
$$
This is the high-energy QED result for muon-pair production. The $1/\energy{s}$ behavior follows from dimensional analysis when the lepton masses are negligible.

> [!note]
> The source uses equation label (7.7) for both the general differential formula above and this total cross section. The repeated source label is retained.

## Example 7.3 — Numerical QED estimate

At $\sqrt{\energy{s}}\approx90\,\unit{GeV}$, use $\electric{\alpha}\approx1/137$:
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}
\approx\frac{4\pi(1/137)^2}{3\times8100\,\unit{GeV^2}}
\approx2.8\times10^{-8}\,\unit{GeV^{-2}}.
$$
With $1\,\unit{GeV^{-2}}\approx0.389\,\unit{mb}$, this is about $11\,\unit{pb}$.

The source compares this pure-QED estimate with about $2\,\unit{nb}$ for muon-pair production near the LEP $Z$ peak. The resonant $Z$-boson exchange dominates the difference and is not included in this QED calculation.
