---
title: "Clifford Algebra in d = 4 Dimensions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The four-dimensional Clifford algebra gives contraction rules that shorten products of gamma matrices.

With metric signature $(+,-,-,-)$,
$$
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2g^{\mu\nu}. \tag{7.12}
$$
It gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu&=4,\\
\gamma^\mu\not a\gamma_\mu&=-2\not a,\\
\gamma^\mu\not a\not b\gamma_\mu&=4(a\cdot b),\\
\gamma^\mu\not a\not b\not c\gamma_\mu&=-2\not c\not b\not a.
\end{aligned}
$$
These identities follow from repeated use of the anticommutator, without selecting a gamma-matrix representation. The coefficient four in the first identity counts the four contracted spacetime directions. To check the second, move the left gamma matrix through the slash factor once; the anticommutator gives twice that slash factor, while the contracted pair removes four times it.

The resulting coefficient minus two is specific to four dimensions. If dimensional regularization continues the Lorentz contractions away from four dimensions, these numerical contractions must be rederived in that dimension. Keeping a four-dimensional identity inside a dimensionally regulated divergent expression can lose finite terms.

The vectors $a,b,c$ are arbitrary algebraic arguments, and $\not a=\gamma^\mu a_\mu$. Scalar right-hand sides multiply the identity matrix.
