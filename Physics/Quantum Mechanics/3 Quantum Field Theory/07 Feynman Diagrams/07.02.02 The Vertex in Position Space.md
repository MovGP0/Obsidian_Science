---
title: "The Vertex in Position Space"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A quartic interaction gives a four-leg vertex with factor −ig integrated over its spacetime position.

The interaction $-g\signal{\phi}^4/4!$ gives a four-leg vertex. At position $\mechanical{z}$, its rule is
$$
(-ig)\int d^4\mechanical{z}.
$$
Permutations of the four fields in Wick's theorem cancel the factor $1/4!$.

The factor of four factorial counts the ways to attach four distinct external labels to the four fields at one insertion. For that contact graph, every assignment gives the same vertex factor, so the interaction normalization removes the duplicate count. If some fields contract with each other, the remaining multiplicity can differ; the tadpole symmetry factor is an example.

The integral over the vertex position is essential. The interaction does not occur at a prescribed experimental coordinate. When plane waves are attached, this integral produces momentum conservation. The sign and the factor of the imaginary unit follow from inserting the negative interaction density into the Dyson expansion; they must be retained consistently when several diagrams interfere.
