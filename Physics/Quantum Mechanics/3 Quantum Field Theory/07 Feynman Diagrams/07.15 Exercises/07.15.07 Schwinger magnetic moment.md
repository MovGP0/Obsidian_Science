## Exercise 7.7 — Schwinger magnetic moment

At vanishing transfer, $\mechanical{q}\to0$, the one-loop vertex correction gives $\magnetic{F}_2(0)=\electric{\alpha}/(2\pi)$. Interpret this with the Gordon decomposition:
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')
\left[
\frac{(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{p}')^\mu}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
$$
The mass in this decomposition is the fermion mass.

Interpret $\magnetic{F}_2(0)$ as a correction to the electron's magnetic $\magnetic{g}$ factor and derive
$$
\magnetic{g}=2\left(1+\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}+\cdots\right).
$$

## Solution 7.7 — Schwinger magnetic moment

The on-shell vertex has the form
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\Gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\electric{F}_1(\mechanical{q}^2)\gamma^\mu
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\magnetic{F}_2(\mechanical{q}^2)
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
$$
Charge normalization gives $\electric{F}_1(0)=1$, and at tree level
$\magnetic{F}_2(0)=0$. The Gordon decomposition separates
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\frac{(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\right]u(\mechanical{p})
$$
into a convective term and a spin magnetic term.

The Gordon identity shows that even the Dirac form factor contributes to the spin term. The anomaly is the additional contribution after charge normalization, not the full spin magnetic coupling. Consequently the factor two in the relation between the magnetic factor and the two form factors is essential.

This exercise accepts the one-loop Pauli form factor as input and derives its low-field interpretation. It does not evaluate the vertex loop integral. In the zero-coupling limit the anomalous part vanishes and the Dirac magnetic factor tends to two, which checks the interpretation without using the quoted decimal value.

In a weak external magnetic field, the spin couples through the interaction energy
$$
\energy{H}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B},
\qquad
\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}=\magnetic{g}\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}}\mechanical{\mathbf S}.
$$
> [!note] Electron charge sign
> The printed vector-moment formula above uses a positive charge magnitude with a positive magnetic factor. For an electron, the physical moment has the opposite sign relative to spin: use the electron charge, which is the negative of that magnitude. The positive magnetic factor and anomaly below describe magnitudes. [NIST's electron magnetic moment](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/wall_2022.pdf) has the corresponding negative sign. The source formula is retained here so this convention issue remains visible.

The sign of the moment follows the charge convention; the source uses $\electric{e}$ in this relation. The coefficient of the magnetic vertex term is $\electric{F}_1(0)+\magnetic{F}_2(0)$. Therefore
$$
\magnetic{g}=2[\electric{F}_1(0)+\magnetic{F}_2(0)]
=2[1+\magnetic{F}_2(0)].
$$
With Schwinger's result,
$$
\magnetic{g}=2\left[1+\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
+\mathcal O(\electric{\alpha}^2)\right],
$$
$$
\magnetic{a}_e=\frac{\magnetic{g}-2}{2}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}+\mathcal O(\electric{\alpha}^2)
\approx0.00116
$$
at first order.
