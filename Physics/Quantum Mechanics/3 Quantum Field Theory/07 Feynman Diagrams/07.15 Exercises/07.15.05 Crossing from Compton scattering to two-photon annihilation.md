## Exercise 7.5 — Crossing from Compton to two-photon annihilation

Start from the Compton amplitude with momenta $(\mechanical{p},\mechanical{k};
\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{k}')$. Use crossing to obtain the amplitude for $\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\gamma\gamma$. Identify each momentum and draw both Feynman diagrams.

## Solution 7.5 — Crossing from Compton scattering to two-photon annihilation

For Compton scattering
$\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p})\gamma(\mechanical{k})\to
\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p}')\gamma(\mechanical{k}')$, the amplitude is analytically continued in the Mandelstam variables
$$
\energy{s}=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})^2,\qquad
\mechanical{t}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')^2,\qquad
\mechanical{u}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}')^2.
$$
Crossing replaces an outgoing particle by its incoming antiparticle with reversed four-momentum, or an incoming particle by its outgoing antiparticle. To obtain
$\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p})\mathrm e^+(\bar{\mechanical{p}})
\to\gamma(\mechanical{k}_1)\gamma(\mechanical{k}_2)$, use
$$
\mechanical{k}\to-\mechanical{k}_2,\qquad
\mechanical{p}'\to-\bar{\mechanical{p}},\qquad
\mechanical{k}'\to\mechanical{k}_1.
$$

The incoming Compton photon becomes outgoing photon 2 with reversed momentum. The outgoing electron becomes the incoming positron. The original outgoing photon remains photon 1. The diagram channels map as follows:

| Compton channel | Annihilation channel |
| --- | --- |
| s channel: the electron absorbs $\gamma(\mechanical{k})$; internal numerator $\not{\mechanical{p}}+\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e$ | u channel: the electron emits $\gamma(\mechanical{k}_2)$; numerator $\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}_2+\mechanical{m}_e$ |
| u channel: the electron emits $\gamma(\mechanical{k}')$; internal numerator $\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}'+\mechanical{m}_e$ | t channel: the electron emits $\gamma(\mechanical{k}_1)$; numerator $\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}_1+\mechanical{m}_e$ |

With subscripts $\mathrm C$ for Compton scattering and $\mathrm A$ for annihilation, the invariants map to
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})^2
&\longrightarrow(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}_2)^2=\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm A},\\
\mechanical{t}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')^2
&\longrightarrow(\mechanical{p}+\bar{\mechanical{p}})^2=\energy{s}_{\mathrm A},\\
\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}')^2
&\longrightarrow(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}_1)^2=\mechanical{t}_{\mathrm A}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the massless limit, the Compton squared amplitude is
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm C}|^2}
=-2\electric{e}^4
\left(\frac{\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}}{\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}}
+\frac{\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}}{\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}}\right).
$$
Crossing the fermion spin sum gives an additional sign:
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}'+\mechanical{m}_e
\longrightarrow-(\not{\bar{\mechanical{p}}}-\mechanical{m}_e)
=-\sum_s v(\bar{\mechanical{p}},s)\bar v(\bar{\mechanical{p}},s).
$$
Both processes have a factor $1/4$ for averaging over the initial spin or polarization states. With final states summed, one obtains
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\gamma\gamma}|^2}
=2\electric{e}^4\left(\frac{\mechanical{u}}{\mechanical{t}}+\frac{\mechanical{t}}{\mechanical{u}}\right).
$$
This is positive in the physical region and symmetric under
$\mechanical{t}\leftrightarrow\mechanical{u}$, the exchange of the two final photons.
