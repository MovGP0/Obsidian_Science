## Exercise 7.6 — Vacuum-polarization tensor structure

Show that (7.9) satisfies $\mechanical{q}_\mu\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})=0$ as a consequence of gauge invariance.

*Hint:* Use the Ward–Takahashi identity and the fermion-propagator properties.

## Solution 7.6 — Transversality of the vacuum-polarization tensor

The one-loop tensor is
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=-(-i\electric{e})^2\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\operatorname{Tr}\left[
\gamma^\mu
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e)}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2+i\epsilon}
\gamma^\nu
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}+\mechanical{m}_e)}
{(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2+i\epsilon}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
The leading minus sign is the closed-fermion-loop sign.

Contract with $\mechanical{q}_\mu$ and use
$$
\not{\mechanical{q}}
=(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e)
-(\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e).
$$
This is the difference of the two inverse fermion propagators. With overall factors $i$ suppressed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e}
\not{\mechanical{q}}
\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e}
={}&
\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e}
\left[
(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e)
-(\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e)
\right]
\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e}\\
={}&\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e}
-\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Cyclicity of the trace puts the contracted loop into this form. The two integrated terms cancel after a shift $\mechanical{k}\to\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q}$ in the second term. This shift requires a regulator that preserves the Ward identity, such as dimensional regularization. Hence
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})=0.
$$

### Ward–Takahashi identity

With the charge factor removed from the vertex and the overall propagator factor $i$ removed from $\signal{S}$, the full identity is
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\signal{S}^{-1}(\mechanical{p}')-\signal{S}^{-1}(\mechanical{p}),
\qquad \mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}.
$$
Applied inside the closed loop, it gives the same cancellation. It expresses current conservation in the time-ordered correlation functions, with their required contact terms.

Lorentz covariance and transversality give
$$
\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=(\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2).
$$
In perturbative QED with a gauge-preserving regulator and renormalization, the Ward identity forbids a local photon mass term.

> [!note] Scope of the mass statement
> The source says that the transverse tensor form alone protects a zero photon mass at every order. The perturbative QED conclusion also uses the absence of a mass-generating $1/\mechanical{q}^2$ pole in the scalar coefficient. Transversality by itself is not sufficient to exclude such a nonlocal contribution.
