### Solution 7.1 — Symmetry factors in quartic scalar theory

At order $n$, the Dyson expansion gives $1/n!$, and each quartic vertex gives $1/4!$. If $N$ Wick contractions produce a specified graph with fixed external labels, its combinatorial weight is
$$
\frac{N}{n!(4!)^n}=\frac1S.
$$
The source calls the quartic coupling $g$; here it is denoted by $\signal{\lambda}$, so the interaction density is $-\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi}^4/4!$ and the vertex factor is $-i\signal{\lambda}$. The symmetry factor $S$ is the order of the graph automorphism group with the required external labels fixed.

**(a) Figure-eight vacuum diagram.** One quartic vertex has two tadpole loops. Its four fields have $\binom42/2=3$ pairings, giving
$$
\frac3{4!}=\frac18,\qquad S=8.
$$
The automorphisms are exchange of the two loops, and reversal of each loop:
$$
S=2\cdot2\cdot2=8.
$$

**(b) “Trampoline” with three connecting lines.** Two quartic vertices joined by three internal propagators leave one external leg at each vertex. This is the two-point setting-sun topology.

For fixed external labels, assign the two external legs to the two vertices in two ways. At each vertex choose one of its four fields for the external attachment, then pair the remaining three fields across the vertices in $3!$ ways. Thus
$$
N=2\cdot4\cdot4\cdot3!=192,
\qquad
\frac{N}{2!(4!)^2}=\frac16.
$$
The three identical internal lines can be permuted, giving
$$
S=3!=6.
$$

> [!note] Correction to the source counting
> The source identifies this topology with two-to-two scattering, although it has only two external legs. Its counting paragraph also chooses three ordered legs at each vertex with $4\cdot3\cdot2$, adds another $3!$ permutation, and refers to two unused legs at each vertex. That explanation overcounts the contractions and has the wrong remaining-leg count. The corrected count above gives the source's final factor $S=6$ for the two-point topology.

The factor $3!$ in part (b) is the permutation of the three parallel identical internal propagators.

**(c) s-channel bubble.** Two quartic vertices joined by two identical internal propagators have
$$
S=2.
$$
Exchanging these two propagators leaves the graph unchanged. The loop amplitude is
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm{bubble}}
=\frac{(-i\signal{\lambda})^2}{2}
\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
\frac{i}{(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$

### Solution 7.2 — Scalar propagator in position space

Start with the Minkowski integral
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i\,e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
A Wick rotation gives the four-dimensional Euclidean propagator
$$
\signal{\Delta}_{\mathrm E}(\mechanical{x}_{\mathrm E})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{e^{i\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}\cdot\mechanical{x}_{\mathrm E}}}
{\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}.
$$
In radial coordinates, set $\mechanical{k}=|\mechanical{\mathbf k}_{\mathrm E}|$ for the magnitude of the four-dimensional Euclidean momentum and $\mechanical{r}=|\mechanical{x}_{\mathrm E}|$. Angular integration gives
$$
\signal{\Delta}_{\mathrm E}(\mechanical{r})
=\frac1{(2\pi)^2\mechanical{r}}
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\mechanical{k}^2J_1(\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r})}
{\mechanical{k}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}\,d\mechanical{k}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi^2\mechanical{r}}K_1(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}).
$$
Here $J_1$ is the ordinary Bessel function and $K_1$ is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. For large positive argument,
$$
K_1(z)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}e^{-z}.
$$
Therefore, for $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}\to\infty$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_{\mathrm E}(\mechanical{r})
&\sim\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi^2\mechanical{r}}
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}}
e^{-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{\pi\mechanical{m}}}{4\pi^2\sqrt2}
\frac{e^{-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}}{\mechanical{r}^{3/2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The massive four-dimensional Euclidean propagator decays exponentially with length scale $1/\mechanical{m}$. This supplied solution has the correct $\mechanical{r}^{-3/2}$ prefactor, unlike the three-dimensional static Yukawa form printed in the exercise question.

Separations much larger than $1/\mechanical{m}$ are suppressed by $e^{-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}$. Integrating over the time separation gives the three-dimensional static Yukawa kernel. With the source's unit attractive coupling convention,
$$
\energy{V}(\mechanical{r})=-\frac{e^{-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}}{4\pi\mechanical{r}}.
$$
Exchange of a massive field therefore gives a short-range interaction. As $\mechanical{m}\to0$, the radial dependence becomes $1/(4\pi\mechanical{r})$, the Coulomb form for massless exchange. The interaction couplings and charges determine the potential's overall coefficient and sign.

### Solution 7.3 — Gamma-matrix traces

Use the four-dimensional Clifford algebra
$$
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf1)=4,
$$
and the fact that the trace of an odd product of gamma matrices vanishes.

**(a)** First, cyclicity and the anticommutator give
$\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu)=4g^{\mu\nu}$.
Move the first gamma matrix through the four-matrix trace:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma)
={}&2g^{\mu\nu}\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma)
-\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma)\\
={}&8g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}
-2g^{\mu\rho}\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\nu\gamma^\sigma)
+\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\mu\gamma^\sigma)\\
={}&8g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}
-8g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}
+2g^{\mu\sigma}\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho)\\
&-\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^\mu).
\end{aligned}
$$
The last trace equals the original trace by cyclicity. Therefore
$$
2\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma)
=8(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}
-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}
+g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}),
$$
and hence
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma)
=4(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}
-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}
+g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}).
$$

**(b)** For an arbitrary algebraic four-vector $a$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^\mu\not a\gamma_\mu
&=\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu a_\nu\gamma_\mu\\
&=(2g^{\mu\nu}-\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu)a_\nu\gamma_\mu\\
&=2\not a-\not a\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu
=2\not a-4\not a=-2\not a.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(c)** For arbitrary algebraic four-vectors $a,b$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^\mu\not a\not b\gamma_\mu
&=\gamma^\mu\not a(2b_\mu-\gamma_\mu\not b)\\
&=2\not b\not a-\gamma^\mu\not a\gamma_\mu\not b\\
&=2\not b\not a+2\not a\not b
=4(a\cdot b)\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Solution 7.4 — Compton cross section in the Thomson limit

The Klein–Nishina formula is
$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^2}{2\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\left(\frac{\energy{\omega}'}{\energy{\omega}}\right)^2
\left[
\frac{\energy{\omega}'}{\energy{\omega}}
+\frac{\energy{\omega}}{\energy{\omega}'}
-\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}
\right].
$$
In the initial electron rest frame, the Compton relation gives
$$
\energy{\omega}'
=\frac{\energy{\omega}}
{1+(\energy{\omega}/\mechanical{m}_e)(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})}.
$$
For $\energy{\omega}\ll\mechanical{m}_e$,
$\energy{\omega}'/\energy{\omega}\to1$, with corrections of order
$\energy{\omega}/\mechanical{m}_e$. Hence the sum of the two energy ratios tends to two:
$$
\left.\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}\right|_{\mathrm{Thomson}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^2}{2\mechanical{m}_e^2}(2-\sin^2\mechanical{\theta})
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\frac{1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}2.
$$
For the full angular integral, set $c=\cos\mechanical{\theta}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\sigma}_T
&=\int\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}\,d\mechanical{\Omega}\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\,2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1+c^2}{2}\,dc\\
&=\frac{\pi\electric{\alpha}^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\left[c+\frac{c^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{1}
=\frac{8\pi\electric{\alpha}^2}{3\mechanical{m}_e^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Numerically,
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_T\approx6.65\times10^{-25}\unit{cm^2}.
$$

### Solution 7.5 — Crossing from Compton scattering to two-photon annihilation

For Compton scattering
$\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p})\gamma(\mechanical{k})\to
\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p}')\gamma(\mechanical{k}')$, the amplitude is analytically continued in the Mandelstam variables
$$
\energy{s}=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})^2,\qquad
\mechanical{t}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')^2,\qquad
\mechanical{u}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}')^2.
$$
Crossing replaces an outgoing particle by its incoming antiparticle with reversed four-momentum, or an incoming particle by its outgoing antiparticle. To obtain
$\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p})\mathrm e^+(\bar{\mechanical{p}})
\to\gamma(\mechanical{k}_1)\gamma(\mechanical{k}_2)$, use
$$
\mechanical{k}\to-\mechanical{k}_2,\qquad
\mechanical{p}'\to-\bar{\mechanical{p}},\qquad
\mechanical{k}'\to\mechanical{k}_1.
$$

The incoming Compton photon becomes outgoing photon 2 with reversed momentum. The outgoing electron becomes the incoming positron. The original outgoing photon remains photon 1. The diagram channels map as follows:

| Compton channel | Annihilation channel |
| --- | --- |
| s channel: the electron absorbs $\gamma(\mechanical{k})$; internal numerator $\not{\mechanical{p}}+\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e$ | u channel: the electron emits $\gamma(\mechanical{k}_2)$; numerator $\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}_2+\mechanical{m}_e$ |
| u channel: the electron emits $\gamma(\mechanical{k}')$; internal numerator $\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}'+\mechanical{m}_e$ | t channel: the electron emits $\gamma(\mechanical{k}_1)$; numerator $\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}_1+\mechanical{m}_e$ |

With subscripts $\mathrm C$ for Compton scattering and $\mathrm A$ for annihilation, the invariants map to
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})^2
&\longrightarrow(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}_2)^2=\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm A},\\
\mechanical{t}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')^2
&\longrightarrow(\mechanical{p}+\bar{\mechanical{p}})^2=\energy{s}_{\mathrm A},\\
\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}')^2
&\longrightarrow(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}_1)^2=\mechanical{t}_{\mathrm A}.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the massless limit, the Compton squared amplitude is
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm C}|^2}
=-2\electric{e}^4
\left(\frac{\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}}{\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}}
+\frac{\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}}{\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}}\right).
$$
Crossing the fermion spin sum gives an additional sign:
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}'+\mechanical{m}_e
\longrightarrow-(\not{\bar{\mechanical{p}}}-\mechanical{m}_e)
=-\sum_s v(\bar{\mechanical{p}},s)\bar v(\bar{\mechanical{p}},s).
$$
Both processes have a factor $1/4$ for averaging over the initial spin or polarization states. With final states summed, one obtains
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\gamma\gamma}|^2}
=2\electric{e}^4\left(\frac{\mechanical{u}}{\mechanical{t}}+\frac{\mechanical{t}}{\mechanical{u}}\right).
$$
This is positive in the physical region and symmetric under
$\mechanical{t}\leftrightarrow\mechanical{u}$, the exchange of the two final photons.

### Solution 7.6 — Transversality of the vacuum-polarization tensor

The one-loop tensor is
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=-(-i\electric{e})^2\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\operatorname{Tr}\left[
\gamma^\mu
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e)}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2+i\epsilon}
\gamma^\nu
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}+\mechanical{m}_e)}
{(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2+i\epsilon}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
The leading minus sign is the closed-fermion-loop sign.

Contract with $\mechanical{q}_\mu$ and use
$$
\not{\mechanical{q}}
=(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e)
-(\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e).
$$
This is the difference of the two inverse fermion propagators. With overall factors $i$ suppressed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e}
\not{\mechanical{q}}
\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e}
={}&
\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e}
\left[
(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e)
-(\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e)
\right]
\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e}\\
={}&\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}_e}
-\frac1{\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}-\mechanical{m}_e}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Cyclicity of the trace puts the contracted loop into this form. The two integrated terms cancel after a shift $\mechanical{k}\to\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q}$ in the second term. This shift requires a regulator that preserves the Ward identity, such as dimensional regularization. Hence
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})=0.
$$

#### Ward–Takahashi identity

With the charge factor removed from the vertex and the overall propagator factor $i$ removed from $\signal{S}$, the full identity is
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\signal{S}^{-1}(\mechanical{p}')-\signal{S}^{-1}(\mechanical{p}),
\qquad \mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}.
$$
Applied inside the closed loop, it gives the same cancellation. It expresses current conservation in the time-ordered correlation functions, with their required contact terms.

Lorentz covariance and transversality give
$$
\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=(\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2).
$$
In perturbative QED with a gauge-preserving regulator and renormalization, the Ward identity forbids a local photon mass term.

> [!note] Scope of the mass statement
> The source says that the transverse tensor form alone protects a zero photon mass at every order. The perturbative QED conclusion also uses the absence of a mass-generating $1/\mechanical{q}^2$ pole in the scalar coefficient. Transversality by itself is not sufficient to exclude such a nonlocal contribution.

### Solution 7.7 — Schwinger magnetic moment

The on-shell vertex has the form
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\Gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\electric{F}_1(\mechanical{q}^2)\gamma^\mu
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\magnetic{F}_2(\mechanical{q}^2)
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
$$
Charge normalization gives $\electric{F}_1(0)=1$, and at tree level
$\magnetic{F}_2(0)=0$. The Gordon decomposition separates
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\frac{(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\right]u(\mechanical{p})
$$
into a convective term and a spin magnetic term.

In a weak external magnetic field, the spin couples through the interaction energy
$$
\energy{H}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B},
\qquad
\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}=\magnetic{g}\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}}\mechanical{\mathbf S}.
$$
The sign of the moment follows the charge convention; the source uses $\electric{e}$ in this relation. The coefficient of the magnetic vertex term is $\electric{F}_1(0)+\magnetic{F}_2(0)$. Therefore
$$
\magnetic{g}=2[\electric{F}_1(0)+\magnetic{F}_2(0)]
=2[1+\magnetic{F}_2(0)].
$$
With Schwinger's result,
$$
\magnetic{g}=2\left[1+\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
+\mathcal O(\electric{\alpha}^2)\right],
$$
$$
\magnetic{a}_e=\frac{\magnetic{g}-2}{2}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}+\mathcal O(\electric{\alpha}^2)
\approx0.00116
$$
at first order.

### Solution 7.8 — Electron–muon scattering amplitude

**(a)** Electrons and muons are distinct particles. At tree level, $\mathrm e^-\mu^-\to\mathrm e^-\mu^-$ has one diagram: t-channel photon exchange. There is no second diagram from exchanging identical final fermions, unlike Møller scattering.

**(b)** Let $\mechanical{p},\mechanical{q}$ be the incoming electron and muon momenta and $\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{q}'$ their outgoing momenta. Then
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}
=\bar u_e(\mechanical{p}')(-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu)u_e(\mechanical{p})
\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{t}}
\bar u_\mu(\mechanical{q}')(-i\electric{e}\gamma^\nu)u_\mu(\mechanical{q}),
$$
so
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}
=\frac{\electric{e}^2}{\mechanical{t}}
[\bar u_e(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u_e(\mechanical{p})]
[\bar u_\mu(\mechanical{q}')\gamma_\mu u_\mu(\mechanical{q})].
$$
Here $\energy{s}=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{q})^2$,
$\mechanical{t}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')^2$, and
$\mechanical{u}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{q}')^2$.

**(c)** In the ultrarelativistic limit, neglect both lepton masses in the spin traces. With the initial spins averaged, define
$$
\signal{L}_e^{\mu\nu}
=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}
[\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\nu],
\qquad
\signal{L}_{\mu,\mu\nu}
=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}
[\not{\mechanical{q}}'\gamma_\mu\not{\mechanical{q}}\gamma_\nu].
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}
&=\frac{\electric{e}^4}{\mechanical{t}^2}\signal{L}_e^{\mu\nu}\signal{L}_{\mu,\mu\nu}\\
&=\frac{8\electric{e}^4}{\mechanical{t}^2}
\left[
(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q})(\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{q}')
+(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q}')(\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{q})
\right]\\
&=\frac{2\electric{e}^4(\energy{s}^2+\mechanical{u}^2)}{\mechanical{t}^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The massless scalar products used in the last line are
$$
\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{q}'=\frac{\energy{s}}2,
\qquad
\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q}'=\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{q}=-\frac{\mechanical{u}}2,
\qquad
\energy{s}+\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{u}=0.
$$
For Møller scattering of identical electrons, the result is instead
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm{Moller}}|^2}
=2\electric{e}^4\left[
\frac{\energy{s}^2+\mechanical{u}^2}{\mechanical{t}^2}
+\frac{\energy{s}^2+\mechanical{t}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}
+\frac{2\energy{s}^2}{\mechanical{t}\mechanical{u}}
\right].
$$
The first term is the electron–muon result. The extra u-channel diagram and its interference term arise from the identical final electrons.

### Solution 7.9 — Tadpole in quartic scalar theory

At first order in the quartic coupling $\signal{\lambda}$, the scalar self-energy receives a tadpole contribution. One external leg enters the vertex, one leaves it, and the other two legs close into a loop:
$$
-i\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{-i\signal{\lambda}}2
\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
The factor one half accounts for exchange of the two ends of the loop at the vertex. This scalar self-energy has mass dimension two.

After a Wick rotation, introduce a Euclidean momentum cutoff $\energy{\Lambda}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}
&=\frac{\signal{\lambda}}2
\int_{|\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}|\le\energy{\Lambda}}
\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac1{\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}\\
&=\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{2(2\pi)^4}\,2\pi^2
\int_0^{\energy{\Lambda}}
\frac{\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}^3\,d\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}}
{\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}\\
&=\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{32\pi^2}
\left[
\energy{\Lambda}^2
-\mechanical{m}^2\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
For $\energy{\Lambda}\gg\mechanical{m}$,
$$
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}
=\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{32\pi^2}
\left[
\energy{\Lambda}^2-\mechanical{m}^2\ln\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}
+\cdots
\right].
$$
The leading ultraviolet divergence is quadratic in the cutoff.

In dimensional regularization with $d=4-2\epsilon$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}
&=\frac{\signal{\lambda}\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}}2
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0}\\
&=\frac{\signal{\lambda}\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}}2
\frac{(\mechanical{m}^2)^{d/2-1}\Gamma(1-d/2)}
{(4\pi)^{d/2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The Euler gamma function has the expansion
$$
\Gamma(-1+\epsilon)
=-\frac1\epsilon+\gamma_E-1+\mathcal O(\epsilon).
$$

Define the usual modified pole combination
$$
\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
=\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln(4\pi).
$$
Expanding the dimensional-regulator expression gives
$$
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}
=-\frac{\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{m}^2}{32\pi^2}
\left[
\frac1{\bar\epsilon}+1-\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{\energy{\mu}^2}
\right]+\mathcal O(\epsilon).
$$
The negative pole follows from $\Gamma(-1+\epsilon)$ as $\epsilon\to0^+$. The logarithmic mass-dependent term corresponds to the logarithmic part of the cutoff expression. A mass counterterm proportional to $\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{m}^2/\epsilon$ is required.

> [!note] Regulator dependence
> The source says that dimensional regularization “does not see quadratic divergences.” More precisely, its expansion near four dimensions has no explicit $\energy{\Lambda}^2$ term; it gives the mass-dependent pole above instead. The cutoff calculation still has a quadratic leading term, and the graph's superficial degree of divergence is unchanged. The regulator representation does not remove the need for mass renormalization.

### Solution 7.10 — Power counting in quartic scalar theory

For a connected diagram with $V$ quartic vertices, $I$ internal lines, $E$ external legs, and $L$ loops,
$$
4V=2I+E,\qquad L=I-V+1.
$$
Each loop integration contributes four powers of momentum and each scalar propagator removes two. Thus
$$
D=4L-2I.
$$
Eliminate the internal counts:
$$
I=2V-\frac E2,\qquad L=V-\frac E2+1,
$$
$$
D=4\left(V-\frac E2+1\right)
-2\left(2V-\frac E2\right)
=4-E.
$$
The superficial degree depends only on the number of external legs:

| External legs | Superficial degree | Overall ultraviolet behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $E=2$ | $D=2$ | Quadratic self-energy divergence, as in Solution 7.9. |
| $E=4$ | $D=0$ | Logarithmic two-to-two amplitude divergence. |
| $E=6$ | $D=-2$ | No overall divergence by this power count. |
| $E\ge6$ | $D<0$ | No overall divergence, but subdivergences can remain. |

The negative degree for higher-point graphs does not by itself remove ultraviolet-divergent subgraphs.

Only a finite set of divergent nonvacuum structures is required: the two-point and four-point functions. Their divergences are absorbed by field-normalization, mass, and quartic-coupling counterterms. This is the power-counting basis for renormalizability. The statement concerns a finite set of counterterm structures, not a finite number of divergent graphs; vacuum diagrams also require a vacuum-energy counterterm if retained.
