---
title: "Counting Divergences"
---

> [!TLDR]
> QED power counting gives a superficial divergence degree, while gauge identities can reduce the actual divergence.

For (7.9), naive ultraviolet counting gives a quadratic divergence:

- Two numerator slash momenta supply two powers of $\mechanical{k}$.
- The two quadratic denominators supply four inverse powers.
- The integration measure supplies four powers.

Thus the superficial degree is $D=4+2-4=2$. With gauge invariance preserved, transversality (7.10) removes the leading divergence and leaves a logarithmic divergence.

> [!note] Remark 7.6 — QED power counting
> For a one-particle-irreducible QED diagram with $E_e$ external electron legs and $E_\gamma$ external photon legs, the superficial divergence degree is
> $$
> D=4-E_\gamma-\frac32E_e.
> $$
> These symbols count legs; they are not energies.

A graph with $D\ge0$ is potentially divergent. The source lists:

| External legs | Superficial degree | Case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $E_e=0,\ E_\gamma=2$ | $D=2$ | Vacuum polarization; gauge invariance reduces the surviving divergence to logarithmic. |
| $E_e=2,\ E_\gamma=0$ | $D=1$ | Electron self-energy; the actual primitive divergence is logarithmic. |
| $E_e=2,\ E_\gamma=1$ | $D=0$ | Electron–photon vertex; logarithmic. |
| $E_e=0,\ E_\gamma=4$ | $D=0$ | Light-by-light scattering; the complete one-loop amplitude is UV finite. |

The degree counts the region where all loop momenta become large together while masses and external momenta are held fixed. It does not test soft or collinear regions, and it does not prove that the leading numerator coefficient survives integration. Symmetry constraints must be applied to the regulated expression before a physical divergence is inferred.

For vacuum polarization, a divergent tensor term proportional only to the metric would have the dimensions of a photon mass squared but would fail the transverse contraction. The allowed tensor already contains two external momentum powers. Its scalar coefficient can therefore have a logarithmic divergence even though the unconstrained tensor count was quadratic.

For the electron, chiral symmetry protects the massless limit: the mass correction is proportional to $\mechanical{m}$, rather than to a cutoff $\energy{\Lambda}$. For light-by-light scattering, gauge identities require external-momentum factors; the source describes an effective count $D_{\mathrm{eff}}=-4$.

Other amplitudes with more external legs have negative superficial degree.

> [!note]
> The source equates negative superficial degree with ultraviolet finiteness. A graph with $D<0$ can still contain ultraviolet-divergent subgraphs. Its overall divergence and its subdivergences must be treated separately.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Loop momentum | The large integration momentum used in ultraviolet power counting. |
| $\mechanical{m},\delta\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fermion mass and correction | The electron mass and its chirally protected mass shift. |
