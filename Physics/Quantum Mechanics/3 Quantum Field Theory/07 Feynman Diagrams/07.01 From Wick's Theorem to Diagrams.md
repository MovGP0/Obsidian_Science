> [!TLDR]

## Chapter introduction

> “The diagrams were just a convenient way to keep track of what you were doing. They were not a new way of doing physics; they were a new way of organizing physics.”
>
> — Freeman Dyson

The preceding chapter expresses each perturbative S-matrix order as a vacuum expectation value of time-ordered interaction operators. Wick's theorem evaluates it, but explicit contraction counting becomes expensive even at low order.

In the 1940s, Richard Feynman associated simple drawings with perturbative terms. This turns the algebraic bookkeeping into graphical rules.

Feynman diagrams are precise encodings of perturbative algebra. Each diagram supplies a prescription for its scattering-amplitude contribution. This chapter derives the rules from Wick's theorem, starting with scalar φ⁴ theory and then treating QED.

## From Wick's theorem to diagrams

The chapter writes the scalar quartic density as
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\frac{g}{4!}\signal{\phi}^4.
$$
The first-order contribution to $\signal{\phi}\signal{\phi}\to\signal{\phi}\signal{\phi}$ is proportional to
$$
\langle\mechanical{p}'_1,\mechanical{p}'_2|
\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}^4(\mechanical{x})\}
|\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2\rangle.
$$
Wick's theorem replaces the time-ordered field product with normal-ordered terms and contractions. A scalar contraction is
$$
\overbrace{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})}
\equiv\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i\,e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}
{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
The central correspondence is that **each contraction pattern has a diagram**. A contracted pair gives an internal line between two points. Uncontracted fields attach to external lines for incoming or outgoing real particles. Points where lines meet are vertices.

The coupling is called $g$ in this chapter; the earlier scalar chapter used $\lambda$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Interaction density | The quartic scalar Lagrangian interaction. |
| $g,\lambda$ | $1$ | Quartic coupling | The current and earlier symbols for the scalar self-coupling. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The real field in the quartic interaction. |
| $\mechanical{p}_i,\mechanical{p}'_i$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | External momenta | The incoming and outgoing scalar four-momenta. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime points | The field and vertex positions. |
| $\signal{\Delta}_F$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Scalar propagator | The free position-space contraction. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Internal four-momentum | The Fourier momentum in the propagator. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar mass | The mass in the internal scalar line. |
