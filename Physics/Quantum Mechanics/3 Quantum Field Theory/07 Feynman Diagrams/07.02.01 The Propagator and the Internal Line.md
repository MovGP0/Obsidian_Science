---
title: "The Propagator and the Internal Line"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An internal scalar line represents the Feynman propagator between its endpoint positions.

The position-space scalar propagator is
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
Use a thin solid line for the scalar, joining its endpoint positions. A dashed line is another common scalar-line convention.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\Delta}_F$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Scalar propagator | The position-space contraction of two scalar fields. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The real field in the time-ordered two-point function. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Endpoint positions | The spacetime positions joined by the internal line. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Internal momentum | The Fourier integration four-momentum. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar mass | The mass of the propagating field. |
