---
title: "Symmetry Factors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A diagram's symmetry factor removes repeated Wick contractions that leave its labeled external structure unchanged.

The symmetry factor $S$ is the order of the diagram's automorphism group. It counts permutations of internal lines and vertices that preserve the graph, with the external labels held fixed. Its correct value gives the required numerical coefficient.

The physical requirement is to count every distinct contribution once. A picture alone is insufficient unless particle species, arrow directions, and external labels are specified. Exchanging identical internal scalar lines can preserve a graph; exchanging an electron line with a photon line cannot.

Use the explicit Wick count in the general rule to establish the coefficient, then use the graphical examples to identify the transformations that reproduce it. As a boundary check, the labeled four-point contact tree has symmetry factor one even though its unlabeled drawing can be rotated.
