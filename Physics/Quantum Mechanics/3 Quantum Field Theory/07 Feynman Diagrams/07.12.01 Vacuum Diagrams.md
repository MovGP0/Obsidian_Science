---
title: "Vacuum Diagrams"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Vacuum diagrams have no external legs and contribute to the vacuum-to-vacuum amplitude.

Vacuum diagrams have no external lines. They contribute to $\langle0|\mathcal S|0\rangle$ and represent vacuum quantum fluctuations.

Vacuum diagrams contribute to the vacuum energy. Their sum exponentiates:
$$
\langle0|\mathcal S|0\rangle=\exp\left(\sum_iV_i\right),
$$
where $V_i$ denotes a connected vacuum-graph contribution.

The vacuum factor cancels in normalized transition quantities such as $\langle f|\mathcal S|i\rangle/\langle0|\mathcal S|0\rangle$. This cancellation removes separate vacuum bubbles; selecting the connected scattering part remains a distinct step.

![[QFT-07-18-QED-Vacuum-Graph.svg|**Fig. 7.18** A QED vacuum graph with one closed fermion loop and an internal photon exchanged between two points on it. Separate vacuum factors exponentiate and cancel from normalized correlators.]]

> [!note]
> The source calls this a one-loop vacuum diagram. It has two independent momentum loops: three internal edges and two vertices give $L=I-V+1=2$. “One closed fermion loop” counts the fermionic trace, rather than the full loop order.
