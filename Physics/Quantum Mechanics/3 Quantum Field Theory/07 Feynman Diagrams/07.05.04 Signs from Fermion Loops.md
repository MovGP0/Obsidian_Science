---
title: "Signs from Fermion Loops"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Every closed fermion loop contributes a minus sign and a trace over its ordered spinor factors.

Fermionic statistics gives each closed loop of fermion propagators a factor $-1$. This follows from the anticommutation of Dirac field operators and appears as a relative sign in Wick contractions.

A closed chain has no external spinors to absorb its open spinor indices. The last propagator index must join the first vertex index, which gives a trace. The minus sign has a different origin: closing the fermionic Wick contraction requires an odd exchange relative to the corresponding open-chain ordering.

Count closed fermion chains, not all momentum loops. A scalar bubble has a loop integral but no fermion-loop minus sign. Conversely, a more complicated graph can have several independent momentum integrations but only one closed fermion chain.

The loop rule is therefore:

- Multiply by $-1$ for each closed fermion loop.
- Take the spinor trace of the ordered propagator numerators and gamma matrices around that loop.
