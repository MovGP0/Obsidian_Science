---
title: "Regularization Methods"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Regularization introduces a parameter that controls divergent loop integrals before systematic subtraction.

A regulator makes intermediate amplitudes defined; it does not by itself identify measured mass or charge. Compare [[09.02.01 Cutoff Regularization]], which limits loop momentum, [[09.02.02 Pauli–Villars Regularization]], which subtracts heavy auxiliary propagators, and [[09.02.03 Dimensional Regularization]], which continues the integration dimension. All three organize the ultraviolet problem, but they need not preserve the same symmetries manifestly.

After regularization, choose subtraction conditions as described in [[09.04 Renormalization Procedure]]. Those conditions define a scheme and fix its finite counterterms. A useful consistency test is to calculate the same observable in two regulators, using parameter values matched to the same physical input. The removed-regulator results must agree to the calculated order, with any difference assigned to omitted orders. A disagreement at the retained order indicates a missing term, inconsistent matching, or a broken identity.

The first practical step is to regularize the divergences. Introduce a parameter that controls the ultraviolet behavior and makes the integrals finite or well-defined. Systematic subtraction can then be performed.
