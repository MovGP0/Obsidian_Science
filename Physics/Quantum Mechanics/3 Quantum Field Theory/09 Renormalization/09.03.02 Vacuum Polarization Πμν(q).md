> [!TLDR]
> The one-loop photon two-point function describes electric-charge screening by virtual electron–positron pairs.

## Tensor structure and Ward identity

Figure 9.2 shows the one-loop photon two-point diagram. It is called **vacuum polarization** because fluctuations of virtual electron–positron pairs screen electric charge.

> [!note]
> The source says “screening of the photon charge.” The photon is electrically neutral. The screened quantity is the electric charge probed through photon exchange.

![[QFT-09-02-Vacuum-Polarization-Momenta.svg|**Fig. 9.2** One-loop vacuum-polarization insertion $i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})$. Momentum labels and arrows follow the source; see the routing note.]]

> [!note]
> With the displayed clockwise fermion flow, the source's loop labels $\mechanical{k}$ and $\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q}$ require the external momentum $\mechanical{q}$ to point left. The source instead draws both external guides to the right. Reverse those guides, or use $\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q}$ on the lower arc, to obtain consistent momentum conservation. The integral below keeps the source's $\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q}$ convention.

Gauge invariance, through the Ward–Takahashi identity, requires the tensor to be transverse:
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})=0.
\tag{9.38}
$$
Therefore,
$$
\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=(\mechanical{q}^{\,2}g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^{\,2}).
\tag{9.39}
$$
The scalar coefficient $\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^{\,2})$ is the **vacuum-polarization function**. Checking this structure tests the consistency of the regulator. Dimensional regularization preserves it.

## Starting integral

The Feynman rules, including $-1$ for the closed fermion loop, give
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
&=(-1)(-i\electric{e})^2\energy{\mu}^{\,2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}\,
\operatorname{tr}\Biggl[
\gamma^\mu\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})}
{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}\\
&\hspace{4em}\times
\gamma^\nu
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}+\mechanical{m})}
{(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}
\Biggr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.40}
$$
The minus sign is the closed-loop sign from Fermi statistics. Combine the factors of $i$:
$$
i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=-\electric{e}^{\,2}\energy{\mu}^{\,2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{N^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k},\mechanical{q})}
{[\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}]
[(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}]}.
\tag{9.41}
$$

The numerator is a trace over spinor indices:
$$
N^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k},\mechanical{q})
=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[
\gamma^\mu(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})
\gamma^\nu(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\not{\mechanical{q}}+\mechanical{m})
\right].
\tag{9.42}
$$

Use the gamma-matrix trace identities
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu]&=4g^{\mu\nu},\\
\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma]
&=4(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}
+g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}),\\
\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho]&=0
\qquad\text{(for even }d\text{).}
\end{aligned}
$$
The result is
$$
\begin{aligned}
N^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k},\mechanical{q})
&=\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{k}}\gamma^\nu\not{\mechanical{q}}]
+\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{k}}\gamma^\nu\not{\mechanical{k}}]
+\mechanical{m}^2\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu]\\
&=4\left[
\mechanical{k}^\mu(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^\nu
+(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu
-g^{\mu\nu}\left(\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})
-\mechanical{m}^2\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.43}
$$

### Feynman parametrization and momentum shift

Combine the two denominators with a Feynman parameter $x\in[0,1]$:
$$
\frac{1}{(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)
((\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2)}
=\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\,\frac{1}{D_\Pi^2},
\tag{9.44}
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_\Pi
&=\mechanical{k}^2+2x\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{q}
+x\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{m}^2\\
&=(\mechanical{k}+x\mechanical{q})^2
+x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{m}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.45}
$$
Define $\mechanical{\ell}=\mechanical{k}+x\mechanical{q}$. Then
$D_\Pi=\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta_\Pi}$, with
$$
\energy{\Delta_\Pi}=\mechanical{m}^2-x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2.
\tag{9.46}
$$
The momenta in the numerator become
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{k}^\mu&=\mechanical{\ell}^\mu-x\mechanical{q}^\mu,\\
(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{q})^\mu
&=\mechanical{\ell}^\mu+(1-x)\mechanical{q}^\mu.
\end{aligned}
$$

The numerator in (9.43) becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
N^{\mu\nu}=4\Big[
&(\mechanical{\ell}^\mu-x\mechanical{q}^\mu)
(\mechanical{\ell}^\nu+(1-x)\mechanical{q}^\nu)\\
&+(\mechanical{\ell}^\mu+(1-x)\mechanical{q}^\mu)
(\mechanical{\ell}^\nu-x\mechanical{q}^\nu)\\
&-g^{\mu\nu}\big(
(\mechanical{\ell}-x\mechanical{q})\cdot
(\mechanical{\ell}+(1-x)\mechanical{q})-\mechanical{m}^2
\big)\Big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.47}
$$

Terms linear in $\mechanical{\ell}$ vanish by symmetric integration. Inside the integral, use
$\mechanical{\ell}^\mu\mechanical{\ell}^\nu
\longrightarrow g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{\ell}^2/d$.
The trace terms proportional to $\mechanical{\ell}^2g^{\mu\nu}$ partially cancel the terms from $\mechanical{\ell}^\mu\mechanical{\ell}^\nu$. Before this replacement, the source writes
$$
\begin{aligned}
N^{\mu\nu}\big|_{\text{integrated}}
=4\Big[
&2\mechanical{\ell}^\mu\mechanical{\ell}^\nu
-2x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu\\
&-g^{\mu\nu}\big(\mechanical{\ell}^2
-x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{m}^2\big)
\Big]+\text{terms linear in }\mechanical{\ell}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.48}
$$
After the symmetric tensor replacement, its expression is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int N^{\mu\nu}\ \longrightarrow\
4\Big[
&\left(\frac2d-1\right)g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{\ell}^2
-2x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu\\
&+g^{\mu\nu}\big(x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2+\mechanical{m}^2\big)
\Big].
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.49}
$$

> [!note]
> The “integrated” label in (9.48) and the integral sign in (9.49) indicate a replacement inside the loop integral. The right-hand sides are still numerator expressions; they have not yet been integrated. The odd terms in (9.48) integrate to zero.

### Evaluation of the integral

Combine (9.49) with the denominator
$(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta_\Pi})^2$.
Two scalar integrals remain:
$$
I_1=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{\mechanical{\ell}^2}
{(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta_\Pi})^2},
\tag{9.50}
$$
$$
I_2=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{1}{(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta_\Pi})^2}.
\tag{9.51}
$$

> [!note]
> The source keeps $\mathrm{d}^d\mechanical{k}$ after the shift. Its unit Jacobian permits the replacement by $\mathrm{d}^d\mechanical{\ell}$ in (9.50) and (9.51).

Use (9.12) with $a=1$, $b=2$ for $I_1$, and $a=0$, $b=2$ for $I_2$. The printed results are
$$
I_2=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\Gamma(2-d/2)\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{\,d/2-2},
\tag{9.52}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac d2
\Gamma(1-d/2)\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{\,d/2-1}\\
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac d2
\frac{\Gamma(2-d/2)}{1-d/2}
\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{\,d/2-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.53}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (9.53) is reproduced as printed. Equation (9.12) gives $(-1)^{a-b}=-1$ for $a=1$, $b=2$, so both lines of (9.53) need an overall minus sign with the stated Minkowski convention. This also follows by writing $\mechanical{\ell}^2=(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta_\Pi})+\energy{\Delta_\Pi}$ and evaluating the two scalar terms.

Using $(d/2)/(1-d/2)=d/(2-d)$, the source rewrites (9.53) as
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1
&=-\frac d2\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\frac{\Gamma(2-d/2)}{d/2-1}
\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{\,d/2-1}\\
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac d2
\Gamma(1-d/2)\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{\,d/2-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.54}
$$

> [!note]
> This is the same printed sign as (9.53). The missing overall minus sign identified there also applies to (9.54).

Combine the numerator (9.49) with the denominator
$(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta_\Pi})^2$.
To display the transverse structure required by the Ward identity, write
$$
\begin{aligned}
g^{\mu\nu}\big(x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2+\mechanical{m}^2\big)
=g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\Delta_\Pi}
+2x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mu\nu}
=4\Big[
&\left(\frac2d-1\right)g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{\ell}^2
+g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\Delta_\Pi}\\
&-2x(1-x)\big(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu
-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}\big)
\Big].
\end{aligned}
$$
Insert this expression into $i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
&=-\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{N_{\mathrm{tot}}^{\mu\nu}}
{(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta_\Pi})^2}\\
&=-4\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\,\Big[
\left(\frac2d-1\right)g^{\mu\nu}I_1
+g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\Delta_\Pi}I_2\\
&\hspace{4em}
-2x(1-x)\big(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu
-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}\big)I_2
\Big].
\end{aligned}
$$

The source states $I_1=d\,\energy{\Delta_\Pi}I_2/(2-d)$ and gives
$$
\left(\frac2d-1\right)I_1
=\frac{2-d}{d}\frac{d}{2-d}\energy{\Delta_\Pi}I_2
=-\energy{\Delta_\Pi}I_2.
\tag{9.55}
$$

> [!note]
> The displayed middle product in (9.55) is positive, so the printed equality is not valid. With the corrected sign of (9.53), the relation is $I_1=d\,\energy{\Delta_\Pi}I_2/(d-2)$. It gives the stated final result $(2/d-1)I_1=-\energy{\Delta_\Pi}I_2$ and permits the required cancellation.

The terms proportional to $g^{\mu\nu}$ therefore cancel:
$-\energy{\Delta_\Pi}I_2+\energy{\Delta_\Pi}I_2=0$.
Only the transverse part remains:
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=8\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\,x(1-x)
\big(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu
-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}\big)I_2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.56}
$$
This explicitly gives the transverse structure in (9.39). To identify its scalar coefficient, use
$i\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}
=i(\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)$
and
$\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}
=-(\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)$.

The scalar coefficient obeys
$$
i\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=-8\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\,x(1-x)I_2.
$$
Insert $I_2$ from (9.52) and cancel $i$:
$$
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=-\frac{8\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\Gamma(2-d/2)\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\,x(1-x)
\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{\,d/2-2}.
$$
For $d=4-2\epsilon$,
$\Gamma(2-d/2)=\Gamma(\epsilon)$ and
$\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{\,d/2-2}=\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{-\epsilon}$.
Combine the scale factor with this power as
$\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}\energy{\Delta_\Pi}^{-\epsilon}
=\exp[-\epsilon\ln(\energy{\Delta_\Pi}/\energy{\mu}^2)]$.
The source writes
$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
&=-\frac{8\electric{e}^2}{(4\pi)^2}\Gamma(\epsilon)
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\,x(1-x)
\left(\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\energy{\Delta_\Pi}}\right)^\epsilon\\
&=-\frac{8\electric{e}^2}{(4\pi)^2}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\,x(1-x)
\left[
\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E
+\ln\left(\frac{4\pi\energy{\mu}^2}{\energy{\Delta_\Pi}}\right)
\right]+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon).
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> The first line omits $(4\pi)^\epsilon$ from $(4\pi)^{-d/2}$. Including this factor gives the $\ln(4\pi)$ term in the second line. The scale and mass powers must be combined before taking the logarithm of their dimensionless ratio.

With $\electric{\alpha}=\electric{e}^2/(4\pi)$, the result is
$$
\boxed{
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=-\frac{2\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\,x(1-x)
\left[
\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
-\ln\left(
\frac{\mechanical{m}^2-x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2}
{\energy{\mu}^2}
\right)
\right].
}
\tag{9.57}
$$
Here $1/\bar\epsilon=1/\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln(4\pi)$.
Since $\int_0^1x(1-x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1/6$, the divergent part is
$-\electric{\alpha}/(3\pi\bar\epsilon)$.
The source relates this pole to the photon renormalization constant $\signal{Z_3}$.

The source attributes the overall sign to the Peskin–Schroeder convention, with
$\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}
=(\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)$.
The geometric Dyson sum then gives a propagator denominator
$\mechanical{q}^2[1-\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)]$.

### Renormalized charge and evolution of α

Sum the polarization bubbles with the photon Dyson equation. In Feynman gauge, the modified propagator is
$$
\signal{D_F}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}
{\mechanical{q}^2[1-\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)]}
+\text{terms proportional to }\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu.
\tag{9.58}
$$
The omitted longitudinal terms do not contribute to physical amplitudes when contracted with conserved currents, for which
$\partial_\mu\electric{J}^\mu=0$.

The Ward–Takahashi identities make these longitudinal current contractions zero. For small momentum transfer
$\mechanical{q}^2\ll\mechanical{m}^2$, the source calls the following expression a Compton-scattering amplitude:
$$
\signal{\mathcal{M}}\sim
\frac{\electric{e}^2}
{\mechanical{q}^2[1-\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)]}
\equiv\frac{\electric{e_{\mathrm{eff}}}^2(\mechanical{q}^2)}
{\mechanical{q}^2},
\tag{9.59}
$$
where
$$
\electric{e_{\mathrm{eff}}}^2(\mechanical{q}^2)
=\frac{\electric{e_R}^2}{1-\electric{\Pi_R}(\mechanical{q}^2)}.
\tag{9.60}
$$
Here $\electric{e_R}$ is the renormalized charge at the chosen subtraction point, for example $\mechanical{q}^2=0$, and $\electric{\Pi_R}$ is the renormalized part of $\electric{\Pi}$.

> [!note]
> Equation (9.59) displays the photon-exchange factor between charged currents, with the current and spinor factors suppressed. It is not the full Compton amplitude, whose tree diagrams exchange an electron. Thus this schematic factor alone does not have the unit of a complete scattering amplitude.

The running fine-structure constant is
$$
\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}
{1-\dfrac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi}
\ln\dfrac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m_e}^2}},
\qquad -\mechanical{q}^2\gg\mechanical{m_e}^2.
\tag{9.61}
$$
This is the high-energy, spacelike leading-logarithm expression.
The source gives the low-energy value
$\electric{\alpha}=\electric{\alpha}(0)\approx1/137.036$.
The coupling increases with energy. With only the electron loop, the source obtains
$\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)\approx134.5$.
After adding the other Standard Model fermions—muons, tau leptons, and quarks—it quotes the experimental value
$\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)\approx128.9$.
These numerical values are reproduced from the source. The spacelike logarithm in (9.61) requires analytic continuation for a timelike argument.
This scale evolution is developed systematically in
[[10.01 Motivation - The Scale Problem]].

### Example 9.1: Physical photon mass

The Ward identity requires a transverse polarization tensor:
$\mechanical{q}_\mu\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}=0$.
Its structure
$\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}
=(\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)$
leaves the photon-propagator pole at $\mechanical{q}^2=0$ in perturbative QED.
Thus vacuum polarization does not give the photon a mass. This follows from $U(1)$ gauge invariance in this setting.

For on-shell subtraction of (9.57), impose
$\electric{\Pi_R}(0)=0$.
At $\mechanical{q}^2=0$, the logarithm contains
$\energy{\Delta_\Pi}(x,0)=\mechanical{m}^2$, independent of $x$.
Thus the subtraction makes $\electric{\Pi_R}(0)$ identically zero.

> [!note]
> The massless-pole argument assumes that the scalar polarization has no singularity that cancels the explicit factor of $\mechanical{q}^2$. It applies here to perturbative QED with massive charged fermions; transversality alone is not a general proof for every gauge theory.

The propagator
$\signal{D}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
\propto[\mechanical{q}^2(1-\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2))]^{-1}$
retains its pole at $\mechanical{q}^2=0$.
In particular, the divergent tensor
$\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu$
does not generate a photon mass term
$\mechanical{m_\gamma}^2g^{\mu\nu}$.
