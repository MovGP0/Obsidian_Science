title: "Physical Origin"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Electron self-energy, vacuum polarization, and magnetic vertex corrections shift atomic levels by different amounts.

The Lamb shift has three main QED contributions:

1. **Electron self-energy.** This is the dominant contribution, quoted by the source as about $1010\,\unit{MHz}$ for $2S_{1/2}$. In the nuclear Coulomb field, the electron interacts with its virtual photons. The energy change depends on the quantum state. It differs between $S$ states, which have more probability near the nucleus, and $P$ states.
2. **Vacuum polarization.** The source quotes about $-27\,\unit{MHz}$ for $2S_{1/2}$. Virtual electron–positron pairs partially screen the proton Coulomb field. The leading short-range effect is proportional to $\lvert\signal{\psi}(0)\rvert^2$ and mainly changes $S$-state energies.
3. **Vertex corrections and anomalous moment.** These modify spin–orbit and spin–spin interactions.

The state dependence is essential. A short-range correction samples the wavefunction near the nucleus; a contact approximation samples its probability density exactly at the origin. In the nonrelativistic Coulomb model, an orbital with nonzero angular momentum vanishes there, whereas an S-state wavefunction does not. This explains why the leading contact term distinguishes the levels. It is not a claim that every radiative correction to a P level vanishes.

The quoted positive self-energy and negative vacuum-polarization shifts partly cancel. They describe contributions to the specified S level, rather than a complete numerical budget for the measured S–P splitting. To compare with experiment, form the difference of the two corrected level energies and include all contributions at the chosen accuracy. The signs, state labels, and reference energy must stay attached to each quoted number.
