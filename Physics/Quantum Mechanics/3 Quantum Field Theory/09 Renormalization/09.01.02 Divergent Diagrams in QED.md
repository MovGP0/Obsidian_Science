title: "Divergent Diagrams in QED"
---

> [!TLDR]
> QED symmetries leave three divergent one-loop structures: photon self-energy, electron self-energy, and the electron–photon vertex.

Insert the external-leg combinations allowed by QED symmetries, including charge and parity, into (9.3). Only a finite number of potentially divergent structures occur.

| Function | $E_F$ | $E_B$ | $D$ | Type in the source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Photon self-energy, or vacuum polarization | 0 | 2 | 2 | Quadratic* |
| Electron self-energy | 2 | 0 | 1 | Linear* |
| $\mathrm e\mathrm e\gamma$ vertex | 2 | 1 | 0 | Logarithmic |
| Light-by-light scattering, $\gamma\gamma\to\gamma\gamma$ | 0 | 4 | 0 | Finite* |

The asterisks mark reductions due to gauge and chiral symmetry. Vacuum polarization is effectively logarithmic, $D_{\mathrm{eff}}=0$, and electron self-energy is also logarithmic. The source describes the full light-by-light sum as finite after combinatorial cancellations.

The table classifies functions before all symmetry constraints have been used. For the photon two-point function, current conservation requires a transverse tensor: contraction with the external photon momentum vanishes. The allowed tensor therefore supplies two external-momentum powers, leaving a logarithmic divergence in its scalar coefficient. A photon mass counterterm would violate this constraint. This argument requires a regulator and subtraction procedure that preserve, or consistently restore, the Ward identity.

The light-by-light entry concerns the complete amplitude, including all required loop orderings. One cannot infer that an individual ordering is finite from the finiteness of the sum. Likewise, the electron entry does not permit the leading odd large-momentum contribution to be kept as a physical linear divergence. Use the table to select counterterm structures, then use the symmetry identities to test the actual result.

At one loop, only three divergent structures remain: electron self-energy, vacuum polarization, and vertex correction.
