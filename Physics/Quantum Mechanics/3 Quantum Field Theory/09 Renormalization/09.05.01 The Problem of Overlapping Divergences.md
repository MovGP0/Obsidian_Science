---
title: "The Problem of Overlapping Divergences"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Overlapping divergent subgraphs share internal lines. Their subtractions must be organized together to avoid incorrect repeated subtraction.

Diagrams with two or more loops can contain **overlapping divergences**: divergent subgraphs that share internal propagators.
Their divergences cannot be removed by treating each subgraph as an independent parameter renormalization. Their subtraction requires a joint analysis.

As an example, consider the two-loop vacuum-polarization diagram in Fig. 9.4.
The source describes its overall UV divergence as logarithmic, $D=0$, and says that it contains two overlapping electron self-energy subgraphs, each with $D=0$.

![[QFT-09-04-Overlapping-Divergences.svg|**Fig. 9.4** Example of a two-loop diagram with overlapping divergences.]]

> [!note]
> In the drawn graph, each triangular subgraph contains one external photon attachment and the internal photon chord. These are vertex-correction subgraphs, with superficial degree $D=0$, and they overlap in the internal photon line. They are not electron self-energy subgraphs. The QED power-counting formula gives the full photon two-point graph superficial degree $D=2$; its transverse tensor structure reduces the remaining scalar divergence to a logarithm. The source's description above combines these distinct statements.
