(a) Show that the theory is renormalizable and identify its divergent structures.

(b) Calculate the one-loop four-point diagram, the “fish” or “bubble” diagram.

## Exercise 9.4: Vacuum polarization for q² > 0

For external momentum $\mechanical{q}^2>4\mechanical{m}^2$, real pair production gives the vacuum-polarization function an imaginary part.
Starting from (9.57), in the limit $\epsilon\to0$:

(a) Show that
$\energy{\Delta}_\Pi=\mechanical{m}^2-x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2<0$
for $\mechanical{q}^2>4\mechanical{m}^2$ and suitable $x$.

(b) Calculate the imaginary part, using the source branch
$\ln(-z)=\ln\lvert z\rvert+i\pi$ for $z>0$:
$$
\operatorname{Im}\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}3
\left(1+\frac{2\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right)
\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}}\,
\theta(\mechanical{q}^2-4\mechanical{m}^2).
$$

> [!note]
> The logarithm must specify which side of its cut is approached: $\ln(-z+i0)=\ln z+i\pi$, while $\ln(-z-i0)=\ln z-i\pi$. The displayed positive imaginary part follows the branch requested by this exercise. It must be matched consistently to the causal prescription and polarization-tensor sign convention used elsewhere.

(c) Verify that for $\mechanical{q}^2\gg\mechanical{m}^2$,
$\operatorname{Im}\electric{\Pi}\to\electric{\alpha}/3$.
Relate this result to the electron–positron pair-production cross section through the optical theorem.

## Exercise 9.5: Order-of-magnitude estimate of the Lamb shift

(a) Use nonrelativistic quantum-mechanical perturbation theory and the Uehling potential, the vacuum-polarization correction to the Coulomb potential,
$$
\energy{V_{\mathrm{Uehling}}}(\mechanical{r})
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}\frac{2\electric{\alpha}}{3\mechanical{r}}
\int_1^\infty\mathrm{d}u\,e^{-2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}u}
\left(1+\frac1{2u^2}\right)
\frac{\sqrt{u^2-1}}{u^2},
$$
to show that the energy correction satisfies
$\energy{\Delta E}\propto\lvert\signal{\psi}_n(0)\rvert^2$.

(b) Estimate
$\lvert\energy{\Delta E_{2S}}\rvert/
\lvert\energy{\Delta E_{1S}}\rvert$
and compare it with the exact result $1/8$ stated by the source.

(c) Discuss why the Uehling potential does not change the $2P$ level at this order.

> [!note]
> The source potential and normalization are reproduced above. The proportionality to the wavefunction density at the origin, the exact $1/n^3$ ratio, and the zero $P$-state correction apply to the leading short-range contact approximation. A finite-range Uehling potential generally gives a nonzero $P$-state expectation value and corrections to the simple ratio.

## Source solutions

The following solutions are supplied in Appendix D of the source.

### Solution 9.1 — QED superficial degree of divergence

**(a)** Each QED vertex joins two fermion legs and one photon leg. Counting fermionic line ends gives
$$
2I_F+E_F=2V.
$$
Each internal photon contributes two photon ends, and each external photon contributes one:
$$
2I_B+E_B=V.
$$
For a connected graph, the number of independent loop momenta is
$$
L=I_F+I_B-V+1.
$$

**(b)** Each loop integration gives four powers of momentum. A photon propagator scales as inverse momentum squared, and a fermion propagator as inverse momentum. Thus
$$
D=4L-2I_B-I_F.
$$
The topological relations give
$$
I_F=V-\frac{E_F}{2},\qquad
I_B=\frac{V-E_B}{2},
\qquad
L=\frac V2-\frac{E_F}{2}-\frac{E_B}{2}+1.
$$
Substituting,
$$
\begin{aligned}
D
&=4\left(\frac V2-\frac{E_F}{2}-\frac{E_B}{2}+1\right)
-(V-E_B)-\left(V-\frac{E_F}{2}\right)\\
&=4-\frac32E_F-E_B.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(c)** For the one-loop photon self-energy, $E_F=0$ and $E_B=2$, so $D=2$. Gauge invariance requires
$$
\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=(\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2).
$$
Extracting the two external momentum powers leaves the scalar coefficient with effective degree
$$
D_{\mathrm{eff}}=D-2=0.
$$
The remaining divergence is logarithmic. Schematically it is proportional to
$\electric{\alpha}\ln(\energy{\Lambda}^2/\energy{\mu}^2)/(3\pi)$, rather than a photon mass term.

### Solution 9.2 — Quartic scalar theory in four dimensions

**(a)** For a connected quartic graph,
$$
4V=2I+E,\qquad L=I-V+1,\qquad D=4L-2I.
$$
It follows that
$$
I=2V-\frac E2,\qquad L=V-\frac E2+1,\qquad D=4-E.
$$
The potentially divergent nonvacuum structures are the two-point function with $D=2$ and the four-point function with $D=0$. Field-normalization, mass, and quartic-coupling counterterms absorb these divergences. Vacuum graphs also need a vacuum-energy subtraction if retained.

**(b)** The supplied solution evaluates the **two-point tadpole**:
$$
-i\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}
=\frac{-i\signal{\lambda}}2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0},
\qquad d=4-2\epsilon.
$$
The factor one half is the tadpole symmetry factor. Formula (9.13) at denominator power one gives
$$
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0}
=-\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\Gamma(1-d/2)(\mechanical{m}^2)^{d/2-1}.
$$

> [!note] Diagram scope
> The question asks for a four-point bubble, but the supplied solution proceeds with a two-point tadpole. They are distinct diagrams. This part transcribes the tadpole calculation; the two-propagator scalar bubble is treated in Solution 9.10.

For $d=4-2\epsilon$,
$$
\Gamma(1-d/2)=\Gamma(-1+\epsilon)
=-\frac1\epsilon+\gamma_E-1+\mathcal O(\epsilon).
$$
Combining the loop factors $i(-i)=1$ gives
$$
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}
=\frac{\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{m}^2}{32\pi^2}
\left(\frac{4\pi\energy{\mu}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)^\epsilon
\Gamma(-1+\epsilon).
$$
With $1/\bar\epsilon=1/\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi$,
$$
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}
=\frac{\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{m}^2}{32\pi^2}
\left[
-\frac1{\bar\epsilon}-1
+\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{\energy{\mu}^2}
+\mathcal O(\epsilon)
\right].
$$
The divergent part is $-\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{m}^2/(32\pi^2\bar\epsilon)$. In the convention with inverse propagator
$\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2-\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tad}}-\delta\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ct}}^2$,
it is canceled by
$$
\delta\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ct}}^2
=+\frac{\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{m}^2}{32\pi^2\bar\epsilon}.
$$

**(c)** A Euclidean cutoff gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{cutoff}}
&=\frac{\signal{\lambda}}2
\int_{|\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}|\le\energy{\Lambda}}
\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac1{\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}\\
&=\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{32\pi^2}
\left[
\energy{\Lambda}^2
-\mechanical{m}^2\ln\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}
\right]
\sim\frac{\signal{\lambda}\energy{\Lambda}^2}{32\pi^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Dimensional regularization instead gives the pole multiplied by $\mechanical{m}^2$. Scaleless integrals such as $\int d^d\mechanical{k}\,1$ vanish in that prescription.

> [!note] Interpretation of the comparison
> The source describes the dimensional result as only logarithmic. This is its pole representation near four dimensions, not a change of the tadpole's superficial degree. A regulator without an explicit cutoff squared does not remove sensitivity to physical heavy masses or the associated naturalness question.

### Solution 9.3 — Ward–Takahashi identity at one loop

**(a)** Define $\Gamma^\mu=\gamma^\mu+\Lambda^\mu$ with the overall QED vertex factor removed. Using the same convention as Solution 8.12,
$$
\Lambda^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=-i\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{N^\mu}
{(\mechanical{k}^2+i0)
[(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0]
[(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0]},
$$
where
$$
N^\mu
=\gamma^\alpha(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})
\gamma^\mu(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma_\alpha.
$$
For $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}$,
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu N^\mu
=\gamma^\alpha(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})
\not{\mechanical{q}}(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma_\alpha.
$$

> [!note] Vertex sign convention
> The source again prints $+i\electric{e}^2$ for $\Lambda^\mu$. With $\Gamma^\mu=\gamma^\mu+\Lambda^\mu$, the $-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu$ Feynman vertex, and the propagators used here, the overall factor is $-i\electric{e}^2$. The corresponding self-energy convention must match it in the Ward identity.

**(b)** Insert
$$
\not{\mechanical{q}}
=(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m})
-(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}).
$$
The numerator between the outer gamma matrices becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
&(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})
\not{\mechanical{q}}
(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})\\
={}&(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})
(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m})
(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})\\
&-(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})
(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m})
(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Using $(\not a+\mechanical{m})(\not a-\mechanical{m})=(a^2-\mechanical{m}^2)\mathbf1$, each term cancels one denominator. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{q}_\mu\Lambda^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=-i\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{\gamma^\alpha}{\mechanical{k}^2+i0}
\left[
\frac{\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}}
{(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0}
-\frac{\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}}
{(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0}
\right]\gamma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(c)** The self-energy is defined by
$$
-i\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p})
=(-i\electric{e})^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{-ig_{\alpha\beta}}{\mechanical{k}^2+i0}
\gamma^\alpha
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})}
{(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0}
\gamma^\beta.
$$
Comparing the two integrals,
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\Lambda^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p})-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}').
$$
For the reduced inverse propagator
$$
\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p})=\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}),
$$
where the propagator's overall factor $i$ is removed, its one-loop correction is $-\energy{\Sigma}$. Hence
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\Lambda^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p}')\big|_{\text{one loop}}
-\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p})\big|_{\text{one loop}}.
$$
Adding the tree vertex gives (9.72):
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p}')-\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p}).
$$
The source's final self-energy difference agrees with this convention once the overall vertex-integral sign identified above is corrected.

### Solution 9.4 — Vacuum-polarization imaginary part above threshold

**(a)** The denominator parameter is
$$
\energy{\Delta}_\Pi=\mechanical{m}^2-x(1-x)\mechanical{q}^2.
$$
The maximum of $x(1-x)$ is $1/4$ at $x=1/2$. For $\mechanical{q}^2>4\mechanical{m}^2$,
$$
\energy{\Delta}_\Pi(1/2)=\mechanical{m}^2-\frac{\mechanical{q}^2}{4}<0.
$$
The interval where the parameter is negative is
$$
x\in(x_-,x_+),\qquad
x_\pm=\frac{1\pm\mechanical{\beta}}2,\qquad
\mechanical{\beta}=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}}.
$$

**(b)** On the interval $(x_-,x_+)$, the inherited causal prescription gives
$$
\ln(\energy{\Delta}_\Pi-i0)=\ln|\energy{\Delta}_\Pi|-i\pi.
$$
The supplied solution uses a **negative** coefficient for this logarithm. Denote its scalar polarization convention by $\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm{sol}}$. Its imaginary part is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Im}\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm{sol}}(\mechanical{q}^2)
&=-\frac{2\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}(-\pi)
\int_{x_-}^{x_+}x(1-x)\,dx\\
&=2\electric{\alpha}\int_{x_-}^{x_+}x(1-x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$
Set $x=1/2+\mechanical{\beta}y/2$. Then $dx=\mechanical{\beta}\,dy/2$ and
$x(1-x)=(1-\mechanical{\beta}^2y^2)/4$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Im}\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm{sol}}
&=2\electric{\alpha}\frac{\mechanical{\beta}}2
\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1-\mechanical{\beta}^2y^2}{4}\,dy\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{\beta}}4
\left(2-\frac{2\mechanical{\beta}^2}{3}\right)\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}3
\left(1+\frac{2\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right)
\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}}\,
\theta(\mechanical{q}^2-4\mechanical{m}^2).
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note] Source polarization-sign mismatch
> Equation (9.57) has an overall minus sign outside a bracket containing minus the logarithm, so its logarithmic coefficient is positive. This supplied solution instead uses a negative logarithmic coefficient with the same $-i0$ prescription. Thus $\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm{sol}}=-\electric{\Pi}_{(9.57)}$ for that common prescription. The exercise statement also requests the opposite side of the logarithm cut. The positive absorptive weight derived here must be converted consistently before combining it with the propagator convention of (9.57).

**(c)** For $\mechanical{q}^2\gg\mechanical{m}^2$, $\mechanical{\beta}\to1$ and
$$
\operatorname{Im}\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm{sol}}\to\frac{\electric{\alpha}}3.
$$
With this positive spectral-weight convention, the optical-theorem relation for the corresponding lepton-pair channel is
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}(\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\ell^+\ell^-)
=\frac{4\pi\electric{\alpha}}{\mechanical{q}^2}
\operatorname{Im}\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm{sol}}(\mechanical{q}^2).
$$
It gives $4\pi\electric{\alpha}^2/(3\mechanical{q}^2)$ at high energy, as in Solution 8.4.

### Solution 9.5 — Lamb-shift order-of-magnitude estimate

**(a)** The Uehling correction is short ranged, on the electron Compton scale. In its leading contact approximation, the bound-state wavefunction can be replaced by its value at the origin:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Delta E}_n
&=\langle n|\energy{V}_{\mathrm{Ue}}|n\rangle\\
&\simeq|\signal{\psi}_n(0)|^2
\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf r}\,\energy{V}_{\mathrm{Ue}}(\mechanical{r}).
\end{aligned}
$$
With the conventional QED normalization of the Uehling correction,
$$
\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf r}\,\energy{V}_{\mathrm{Ue}}
=-\frac{4\electric{\alpha}^2}{15\mechanical{m}_e^2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\energy{\Delta E}_n
=-\frac{4\electric{\alpha}^2}{15\mechanical{m}_e^2}
|\signal{\psi}_n(0)|^2.
$$

> [!note] Potential normalization
> The potential printed in Exercise 9.5 has an extra factor $1/(4\pi)$ relative to the conventional Uehling normalization used by this supplied solution. Its stated integral and the result above use the conventional coefficient, consistent with Solution 8.9.

**(b)** For hydrogen with an infinitely heavy nucleus,
$$
|\signal{\psi}_{nS}(0)|^2
=\frac1{\pi n^3\mechanical{a}_0^3},
\qquad \mechanical{a}_0=\frac1{\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{m}_e}.
$$
Therefore, within the contact approximation,
$$
\frac{|\energy{\Delta E}_{2S}|}{|\energy{\Delta E}_{1S}|}
=\frac{|\signal{\psi}_{2S}(0)|^2}{|\signal{\psi}_{1S}(0)|^2}
=\frac{1/8}{1}=\frac18.
$$

**(c)** A nonrelativistic P-state wavefunction vanishes at the origin:
$\signal{\psi}_{nP}(0)=0$. The Uehling range is much smaller than the Bohr radius. Its P-state effect is suppressed relative to the S-state shift by
$$
(\mechanical{a}_0\mechanical{m}_e)^{-2}=\electric{\alpha}^2.
$$

> [!note] Scope of the contact approximation
> The source calls the ratio $1/8$ exact and says that one-loop vacuum polarization does not change P levels. Both statements need the leading contact approximation. The finite-range potential gives corrections to the simple ratio and a nonzero, more suppressed P-state shift. One loop alone does not make the finite-range P-state expectation value zero.

### Solution 9.6 — Electron self-energy on shell

**(a)** The source starts from the logarithmic finite piece of (9.34):
$$
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{log,source}}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}
\int_0^1dx\,(-2x\not{\mechanical{p}}+4\mechanical{m})
\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma(x,\mechanical{p}^2)}.
$$
At $\mechanical{p}^2=\mechanical{m}^2$,
$$
\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma(x,\mechanical{m}^2)
=(1-x)(\mechanical{m}^2-x\mechanical{m}^2)
=\mechanical{m}^2(1-x)^2.
$$

**(b)** Setting $\not{\mechanical{p}}=\mechanical{m}$ on an external on-shell spinor,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{log,source}}(\mechanical{m})
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}
\int_0^1dx\,(-2x\mechanical{m}+4\mechanical{m})
\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2(1-x)^2}\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{m}}{4\pi}
\int_0^1dx\,(-2x+4)\,
2\left[\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{m}}-\ln(1-x)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
Use
$$
\int_0^1x\,dx=\frac12,\qquad
\int_0^1dx=1,\qquad
\int_0^1\ln(1-x)\,dx=-1,\qquad
\int_0^1x\ln(1-x)\,dx=-\frac34.
$$
The result is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{log,source}}(\mechanical{m})
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{m}}{2\pi}
\left[
3\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{m}}
-\left(-2\left(-\frac34\right)+4(-1)\right)
\right]\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{m}}{2\pi}
\left[3\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{m}}+\frac52\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note] Missing finite numerator and scheme distinction
> As noted in [[09.03.01 Electron Self-Energy Σ(p)]], (9.34) omits the finite term $(\electric{\alpha}/4\pi)(\not{\mechanical{p}}-2\mechanical{m})$ from the dimension-dependent numerator. On shell this subtracts $\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{m}/(4\pi)$ from the result above. The corrected ultraviolet-subtracted finite self-energy is therefore $(\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{m}/4\pi)[3\ln(\energy{\mu}^2/\mechanical{m}^2)+4]$ in the stated modified-minimal-subtraction convention.
>
> The source calls its finite integral an “on-shell scheme” result after removing the ultraviolet pole. Evaluating an integral on shell is not the same as imposing on-shell renormalization conditions. The fully renormalized on-shell self-energy also includes its mass and residue counterterms.

**(c)** The source defines its loop mass shift by evaluating the self-energy on shell. Restoring the finite numerator term identified above gives
$$
\frac{\Delta\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{loop}}}{\mechanical{m}}
=\frac{\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{loop}}(\mechanical{m})}{\mechanical{m}}
=\frac{3\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}
\left[
\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
+\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}
+\frac43
\right]
$$
at this order. The source instead has $5/3$ in the bracket, inherited from its incomplete logarithmic integral.

For the kinetic counterterm, the source uses the residue condition on the derivative of the total self-energy and quotes
$$
\signal{\delta}_{2,\mathrm{source}}
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}
\left[
\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
+2\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}
+4+\cdots
\right].
$$

> [!note] Incomplete on-shell residue formula
> The displayed source expression does not separate ultraviolet and infrared singularities and does not specify the omitted terms. The full on-shell wavefunction correction is infrared divergent and depends on the infrared prescription. This expression therefore does not give a complete finite on-shell residue counterterm. Its ultraviolet pole is $-\electric{\alpha}/(4\pi\bar\epsilon)$.

In modified minimal subtraction, only the ultraviolet pole is subtracted:
$$
\signal{\delta}_2=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi\bar\epsilon},
\qquad
\signal{\delta}_m=\signal{Z}_m-1
=-\frac{3\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi\bar\epsilon}.
$$
The loop mass shift $\Delta\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{loop}}$ is distinct from the mass counterterm. With the convention below, the on-shell mass renormalization coefficient cancels that loop shift.

### Solution 9.7 — Counterterm Feynman rules

The counterterm density from (9.81) is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{ct}}
={}&(\signal{Z}_2-1)\bar{\signal{\psi}}\,i\not{\partial}\signal{\psi}
-(\signal{Z}_2\signal{Z}_m-1)\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}\\
&-\frac{\signal{Z}_3-1}{4}\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
-(\signal{Z}_1-1)\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\not{\electric{A}}\signal{\psi}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(a)** Define $\tilde{\signal{\delta}}_m=\signal{Z}_2\signal{Z}_m-1$. At one loop,
$$
\tilde{\signal{\delta}}_m=\signal{\delta}_2+\signal{\delta}_m,
\qquad \signal{\delta}_m=\signal{Z}_m-1.
$$
The fermion-line counterterm insertion is
$$
i\left[\signal{\delta}_2\not{\mechanical{p}}
-\tilde{\signal{\delta}}_m\mechanical{m}\right].
$$
Thus the mass coefficient is the full product correction, not just $\signal{Z}_m-1$.
In modified minimal subtraction,
$$
\tilde{\signal{\delta}}_m
=-\frac{4\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi\bar\epsilon}
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi\bar\epsilon}.
$$

**(b)** The photon counterterm is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{ct}}^{(\gamma)}
&=-\frac{\signal{\delta}_3}{4}\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}\\
&=-\frac{\signal{\delta}_3}{2}
\left[
(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu)(\partial^\mu\electric{A}^\nu)
-(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu)(\partial^\nu\electric{A}^\mu)
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
Its momentum-space insertion is
$$
-i\signal{\delta}_3
(g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu).
$$
The tensor is transverse, as required by gauge invariance.

**(c)** The total self-energy insertion is
$$
-i\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
=-i\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{loop}}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
+i(\signal{\delta}_2\not{\mechanical{p}}-\tilde{\signal{\delta}}_m\mechanical{m}).
$$
The loop ultraviolet pole is
$$
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{div}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi\bar\epsilon}
(-\not{\mechanical{p}}+4\mechanical{m}).
$$
In the total self-energy itself, the counterterm contributes
$-\signal{\delta}_2\not{\mechanical{p}}+\tilde{\signal{\delta}}_m\mechanical{m}$.
The coefficients
$$
\signal{\delta}_2=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi\bar\epsilon},
\qquad
\tilde{\signal{\delta}}_m=-\frac{4\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi\bar\epsilon}
$$
cancel both poles. Therefore
$$
\signal{\delta}_m=\tilde{\signal{\delta}}_m-\signal{\delta}_2
=-\frac{3\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi\bar\epsilon}.
$$
The total is ultraviolet finite. An on-shell infrared singularity still needs its separate infrared prescription.

### Solution 9.8 — Beta function and evolution of α

**(a)** The vacuum-polarization pole is
$$
\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm{div}}
=-\frac{2\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}\frac16\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi\bar\epsilon}.
$$
With the field definition
$$
\electric{A}_0^\mu=\signal{Z}_3^{1/2}\electric{A}^\mu,
$$
modified minimal subtraction gives
$$
\signal{\delta}_3=\signal{Z}_3-1
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi\bar\epsilon}.
$$
The Ward identity gives the bare-charge relation
$$
\electric{e}_0=\signal{Z}_e\electric{e}\energy{\mu}^\epsilon,
\qquad
\signal{Z}_e=\signal{Z}_3^{-1/2}.
$$
At fixed bare charge,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{\beta}_e
=\left.\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\electric{e}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}\right|_{\electric{e}_0}
=-\epsilon\electric{e}
+\electric{e}\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\ln\signal{Z}_3^{1/2}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the four-dimensional limit for one Dirac species of unit charge,
$$
\electric{\beta}_e=\frac{\electric{e}^3}{12\pi^2}+\mathcal O(\electric{e}^5).
$$
This corresponds to $\beta_0=4/3$ if
$\electric{\beta}_e=\beta_0\electric{e}^3/(4\pi)^2+\cdots$.
Equivalently,
$$
\electric{\beta}_\alpha
=\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\electric{\alpha}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
=\frac{2\electric{\alpha}^2}{3\pi}+\mathcal O(\electric{\alpha}^3).
$$

**(b)** At one loop,
$$
\frac{d(\electric{\alpha}^{-1})}{d\ln\energy{\mu}}
=-\frac{2}{3\pi},
$$
so
$$
\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\energy{\mu})
=\electric{\alpha}_0^{-1}
-\frac{2}{3\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\energy{\mu}_0}.
$$
Inverting and writing the scale argument as energy squared,
$$
\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu}^2)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}_0}
{1-\dfrac{\electric{\alpha}_0}{3\pi}
\ln(\energy{\mu}^2/\energy{\mu}_0^2)}.
$$

The one-loop denominator vanishes at the Landau-pole scale
$$
\energy{\mu}_L=\energy{\mu}_0
\exp\left(\frac{3\pi}{2\electric{\alpha}_0}\right).
$$
The positive beta function means that QED is not asymptotically free. The pole signals failure of this perturbative extrapolation, not a controlled prediction at the pole.

**(c)** For one Dirac species with $\electric{\alpha}_0=1/137$ at
$\energy{\mu}_0=\mechanical{m}_e\approx0.5\unit{MeV}$,
$$
\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}_L}{\mechanical{m}_e}
=\frac{3\pi\cdot137}{2}\approx645,
\qquad
\energy{\mu}_L\sim10^{277}\unit{GeV}.
$$
This is far above the Planck scale, of order $10^{19}\unit{GeV}$.
The source also quotes a scale near $10^{34}\unit{GeV}$ after including all Standard Model charged fermions.

> [!note] Extrapolation scope
> The quoted scales are formal source estimates. Thresholds and new interactions change the running long before either extrapolated pole. In particular, isolated QED with all Standard Model fermions is not the full high-energy electroweak theory. These numbers are not physically accessible pole predictions.

### Solution 9.9 — Magnetic anomaly at order α²

**(a)** The two-loop contributions include two virtual photons attached to the electron line, nested or crossed photon arrangements, electron self-energy subgraphs with their counterterms, and a vacuum-polarization insertion in the internal photon. These categories must be counted without counting the same graph twice.

> [!note] Correction to the source loop orders
> The source lists a light-by-light subdiagram among the two-loop contributions, then claims 72 diagrams and about ten distinct topologies. It mixes loop orders: the universal one-flavor two-loop anomaly has seven diagrams; the 72-diagram set is at three loops, where light-by-light contributions occur. See Jegerlehner and Nyffeler, [The Muon g-2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.3360), Section 3 and Figs. 9–10.

**(b)** The source prints an insertion kernel proportional to
$$
\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1dx\,x(1-x)
\electric{\Pi}_R\left(\frac{4\mechanical{m}^2x(1-x)}{(1-x)^2}\right).
$$
Its timelike argument crosses the pair threshold, and it is not the standard real magnetic-anomaly insertion formula.

A representation with an explicit positive Euclidean polarization avoids the sign ambiguity. Define
$$
\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm E}(\mechanical{Q}^2)
=\frac{2\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1dz\,z(1-z)
\ln\left[1+\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}z(1-z)\right],
\qquad \mechanical{Q}^2\ge0.
$$
For an electron loop with the same mass as the external electron,
$$
\magnetic{a}_e^{(2)}\big|_{\mathrm{VP}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1dx\,(1-x)
\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm E}\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}^2x^2}{1-x}\right).
$$
This samples spacelike Minkowski momentum
$\mechanical{q}^2=-\mechanical{m}^2x^2/(1-x)$.
The measure and momentum argument differ from the printed source kernel. This form follows from the vacuum-polarization insertion representation in Jegerlehner and Nyffeler, [Eq. (69)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.3360), with their polarization sign converted to the positive Euclidean function defined here.

For large Euclidean momentum, the positive polarization used above has the asymptotic form
$$
\electric{\Pi}_{\mathrm E}(\mechanical{Q}^2)
\simeq\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi}
\left[\ln\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}-\frac53\right],
\qquad \mechanical{Q}^2\gg\mechanical{m}^2.
$$
The source writes this as its spacelike renormalized polarization and motivates the insertion by an effective photon-mass replacement. This asymptotic expression is not valid over the entire insertion integral.

**(c)** The source quotes a vacuum-polarization contribution near
$0.0156(\electric{\alpha}/\pi)^2$. The equal-mass coefficient is
$$
\magnetic{a}_e^{(2)}\big|_{\mathrm{VP}}
=\left(\frac{119}{36}-\frac{\pi^2}{3}\right)
\left(\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}\right)^2
\approx0.0156874\left(\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}\right)^2.
$$
See Jegerlehner and Nyffeler, [Eq. (78)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.3360). It is small and positive.

The complete one-flavor two-loop coefficient quoted by the source is
$$
C_2=\frac{197}{144}+\frac{\pi^2}{12}
-\frac{\pi^2}{2}\ln2+\frac34\zeta(3)
\approx-0.32848,
$$
$$
\magnetic{a}_e^{(2)}=C_2\left(\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}\right)^2.
$$
The other two-loop vertex contributions give the larger negative net contribution. The source credits the 1957 calculations of Sommerfield and Petermann.

### Solution 9.10 — Dimensional regularization of the scalar bubble

**(a)** The two-propagator scalar integral is
$$
B(\mechanical{p}^2)
=\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int_0^1dx\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{
\left[
x(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)
+(1-x)((\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{p})^2-\mechanical{m}^2)+i0
\right]^2}.
$$
Complete the square:
$$
x\mechanical{k}^2+(1-x)(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{p})^2-\mechanical{m}^2
=\mechanical{k}^2+2(1-x)\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{p}
+(1-x)\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2.
$$
With
$$
\mechanical{\ell}=\mechanical{k}+(1-x)\mechanical{p},
$$
the denominator is $(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta}+i0)^2$, where
$$
\energy{\Delta}
=(1-x)^2\mechanical{p}^2-(1-x)\mechanical{p}^2+\mechanical{m}^2
=\mechanical{m}^2-x(1-x)\mechanical{p}^2.
$$

**(b)** Formula (9.13) at numerator power zero and denominator power two gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{\ell}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta}+i0)^2}
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\frac{\Gamma(2-d/2)}{\Gamma(2)}
(\energy{\Delta}-i0)^{d/2-2}\\
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon}}
\Gamma(\epsilon)(\energy{\Delta}-i0)^{-\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note] Diagram name
> This supplied heading calls the calculation a two-point “box,” while the exercise asks for a four-point function. The displayed integral has two internal propagators. In quartic scalar scattering it is the bubble contribution to the four-point function, not a box topology.

**(c)** Restore the scale factor:
$$
\begin{aligned}
B(\mechanical{p}^2)
&=\frac{i\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon}}
\Gamma(\epsilon)\int_0^1dx\,(\energy{\Delta}-i0)^{-\epsilon}\\
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^2}\int_0^1dx
\left(\frac{4\pi\energy{\mu}^2}{\energy{\Delta}-i0}\right)^\epsilon
\Gamma(\epsilon)\\
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^2}\int_0^1dx
\left[
\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E
+\ln\frac{4\pi\energy{\mu}^2}{\energy{\Delta}-i0}
+\mathcal O(\epsilon)
\right]\\
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^2}
\left[
\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
-\int_0^1dx\,
\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^2-x(1-x)\mechanical{p}^2-i0}{\energy{\mu}^2}
\right]+\mathcal O(\epsilon).
\end{aligned}
$$
The divergent part is independent of $\mechanical{p}^2$, so it is local. In the quartic four-point amplitude, the coupling counterterm removes this pole. The finite part depends on the dimensionless ratio $\mechanical{p}^2/\mechanical{m}^2$. For spacelike $\mechanical{p}^2<0$, the logarithm argument is positive. Above $\mechanical{p}^2=4\mechanical{m}^2$, the pair-production cut opens.

> [!note] Corrections to the source interpretation
> The source calls this a scalar-propagator divergence even though it is treating the two-propagator four-point bubble and its quartic-coupling counterterm.
>
> It also writes $\operatorname{Im}B=-\sqrt{1-4\mechanical{m}^2/\mechanical{p}^2}/(16\pi)$ above threshold. The displayed definition of $B$ has an overall factor $i$. With the causal prescription shown here, the threshold term $-\sqrt{1-4\mechanical{m}^2/\mechanical{p}^2}/(16\pi)$ is the real part of $B$. For the reduced function $\widehat B=B/i$, the absorptive part is
> $$
> \operatorname{Im}\widehat B
> =\frac1{16\pi}\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{p}^2}}\,
> \theta(\mechanical{p}^2-4\mechanical{m}^2).
> $$
> The full amplitude's vertex and propagator factors must be retained when applying the optical theorem.
