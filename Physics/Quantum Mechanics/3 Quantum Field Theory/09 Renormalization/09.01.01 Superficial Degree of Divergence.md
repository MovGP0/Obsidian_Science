---
title: "Superficial Degree of Divergence"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Power counting estimates a diagram's superficial ultraviolet divergence from the large-momentum powers in its loop integral.

Consider a generic Feynman diagram $\Gamma$ in a four-dimensional quantum field theory. Its integral has the form
$$
I\sim\int
\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}_1}{(2\pi)^4}\cdots
\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}_L}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}.
\tag{9.1}
$$
Here $L$ is the number of loops. The denominator comes from the product of propagators. To estimate the behavior as internal momenta become large, count the powers of momentum in the numerator and denominator.

Let $D$ be the **superficial degree of divergence**. If $D\ge0$, power counting classifies the integral as superficially divergent in the region $|\mechanical{k}|\to\infty$.

Power counting gives these cases:

- $D>0$: polynomial divergence, stronger than a logarithm.
- $D=0$: logarithmic divergence.
- $D<0$: superficial convergence. Divergent subdiagrams can still remain.

For $L$ loops, $I_F$ internal fermion propagators, and $I_B$ internal boson propagators,
$$
D=4L-2I_B-I_F.
\tag{9.2}
$$
Each loop measure contributes four momentum powers. A scalar or vector boson propagator scales as $\mechanical{k}^{-2}$, while a fermion propagator scales as $\mechanical{k}^{-1}$.

> [!note]
> Equation (9.2) assumes the stated propagator scaling and no additional momentum powers from the vertices. Derivative interactions require their numerator powers to be included. The nonderivative QED vertex satisfies this assumption.

In four-dimensional QED, topology expresses $D$ in terms of external lines alone. The vertex $\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}\electric{A}_\mu$ has two fermion legs and one boson leg. For a connected diagram with $V$ vertices,
$$
2I_F+E_F=2V,
$$
$$
2I_B+E_B=V,
$$
$$
L=I_F+I_B-V+1.
$$
Here $E_F$ and $E_B$ count external fermion and photon legs. Substitution gives
$$
D=4-E_B-\frac32E_F.
\tag{9.3}
$$

The result depends only on the external legs, not on the loop count. At a fixed number of external legs, additional loops do not worsen the superficial degree of divergence. The source identifies this behavior as a characteristic of renormalizability.
