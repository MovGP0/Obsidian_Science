---
title: "The Bogoliubov–Parasiuk Subtraction Formula"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Bogoliubov–Parasiuk subtraction procedure organizes the removal of ultraviolet divergences from Feynman diagrams.

The BPHZ scheme—Bogoliubov–Parasiuk–Hepp–Zimmermann—provides a systematic procedure to subtract Feynman-diagram divergences in each perturbative order.

The procedure makes each perturbative order finite, including diagrams with overlapping divergences.
Its central idea is the Bogoliubov subtraction operation $R$. The source writes
$$
R[\Gamma]=\Gamma+\sum_\gamma C[\gamma]\cdot(\Gamma/\gamma),
\tag{9.86}
$$
where the sum runs over proper divergent subgraphs $\gamma$ of $\Gamma$.
The reduced diagram $\Gamma/\gamma$ results from contracting $\gamma$ to a point.
The corresponding counterterm is $C[\gamma]$.
The source gives the recursion
$$
C[\Gamma]=-T\cdot R[\Gamma],
\tag{9.87}
$$
and calls $T$ the Taylor operator that extracts the divergent part, described there in terms of a Laurent expansion in $\epsilon$.

> [!note]
> Equations (9.86) and (9.87) suppress distinctions needed in a full definition. The graph with its subdivergences removed is conventionally denoted $\bar R[\Gamma]$; then $C[\Gamma]=-T_\Gamma\bar R[\Gamma]$ and $R[\Gamma]=(1-T_\Gamma)\bar R[\Gamma]$. Simultaneous disjoint subgraph counterterms must also be included. A sum over individual subgraphs alone does not describe every graph.
>
> In momentum-subtraction BPHZ, $T_\gamma$ takes a Taylor polynomial in external momenta through the superficial degree of divergence. A Laurent pole projection is the subtraction operation used in dimensional minimal subtraction; it is not the same operator, although a corresponding recursive organization is possible.

The source credits Hepp (1966) with a convergence proof for the subtracted integrals and Zimmermann (1969) with an explicit formula that avoids recursion: the **Zimmermann forest formula**,
$$
R[\Gamma]=\Gamma+
\sum_{\mathcal F}\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal F}(-T_\gamma)\cdot\Gamma.
\tag{9.88}
$$
A Zimmermann forest is a set of divergent 1PI subgraphs that are pairwise disjoint or nested. For a pair $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$, the allowed relations are
$\gamma_1\cap\gamma_2=\varnothing$,
$\gamma_1\subseteq\gamma_2$, or
$\gamma_2\subseteq\gamma_1$.

> [!note]
> With the separate $\Gamma$ term in (9.88), the forest sum must exclude the empty forest. An equivalent standard form sums over all forests, including the empty forest, without adding another $\Gamma$. The overall graph subtraction must be included when required, and nested subtraction operators act from the inner subgraph outward.
