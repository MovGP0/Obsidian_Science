> [!TLDR]
> Lamb and Retherford measured a hydrogen-level splitting in 1947. The result provided direct evidence of QED radiative effects.

In 1947, Willis Lamb and Robert Retherford measured transition frequencies in hydrogen with high precision.
They found that the $2S_{1/2}$ and $2P_{1/2}$ levels, which the Dirac equation predicts to be degenerate, are split by about $1058\,\unit{MHz}$.

This splitting is called the **Lamb shift**.
The Dirac theory alone could not explain it.
It provided the first direct experimental evidence of QED radiative effects.

The comparison is with a specific model: a Dirac electron in the ideal Coulomb field, before radiative and nuclear corrections are added. That model's degeneracy supplies the zero-splitting prediction to be tested. The reported nonzero frequency therefore identifies missing physics in that approximation. The magnitude and state dependence then provide a more selective test than merely observing that some correction exists.

The number above is the source's approximate historical benchmark. It is a frequency for the separation of two levels, not the radiative displacement of either level measured from an absolute zero of energy. Comparing it with a calculation requires the same pair of states and the same frequency-to-energy convention. This distinction is needed when reading the separate contributions in [[09.06.02 Physical Origin]].

Bethe (1947) calculated the Lamb shift with a semi-relativistic method.

Dyson, French, Weisskopf, and Kroll later used fully relativistic methods. These calculations confirmed the QED prediction.
