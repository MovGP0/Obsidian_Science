---
title: "The MS and MS-Bar Schemes"
---

> [!TLDR]
> MS subtracts dimensional poles, while MS-bar also removes the standard universal constants. Their parameters depend on the renormalization scale.

Minimal-subtraction schemes provide alternatives to the on-shell scheme:

- **MS (minimal subtraction):** subtract only the $1/\epsilon$ poles.
- **$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ (modified minimal subtraction):** subtract the poles together with the universal constants, using $1/\bar\epsilon=1/\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln(4\pi)$ as in (9.19).

In $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$, the renormalized quantities depend in a calculable way on the scale $\energy{\mu}$. The source also describes the renormalization constants as scale dependent.
Relations to on-shell quantities contain finite logarithmic terms. The source gives
$$
\mechanical{m}_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}(\energy{\mu})
=\mechanical{m_e}\left[
1+\frac{3\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
\ln\frac{\mechanical{m_e}}{\energy{\mu}}
+\mathcal{O}(\electric{\alpha}^2)
\right].
\tag{9.85}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (9.85) displays the one-loop logarithmic scale dependence. A complete one-loop conversion between the pole mass and the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ mass also contains a finite matching constant; it is not given in this expression. In minimal subtraction, the pole coefficients depend on the renormalized couplings and regulator. Their scale dependence is generally induced through the running couplings, rather than through additional explicit mass-to-scale logarithms in those coefficients.

Setting the scale equal to the electron mass in (9.85) removes its displayed logarithm. It does not prove equality of the two mass definitions, because the finite matching term identified in the note is absent from that formula. This is a useful test of what a logarithmic approximation can and cannot determine.

For a physical prediction, first infer the minimal-subtraction parameters from specified physical inputs, then use those parameters consistently in the amplitude. Varying the arbitrary scale changes explicit loop logarithms and the parameters together. Their cancellation holds through the computed order; residual higher-order scale dependence is not a new measurable effect. The subtraction prescription is supported by [Beisert, QFT II, Sec. 5.2, pp. 5.8–5.9](https://people.phys.ethz.ch/~nbeisert/lectures/QFT2-17FS-Notes.pdf#page=95).

Renormalization-group equations govern the scale dependence of the renormalized mass and charge; see [[10.01 Motivation - The Scale Problem]].

> [!note]
> **Remark 9.3.** The source prefers $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ for perturbative high-energy calculations, such as QCD calculations, because the dependence on the renormalization scale $\energy{\mu}$ is easy to control systematically. The on-shell scheme is a natural choice for low-energy precision calculations, such as atomic QED, because it relates Lagrangian parameters directly to observable quantities.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The scale used to define the running renormalized parameters. |
| $\mechanical{m}_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}},\mechanical{m_e}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | MS-bar mass and electron pole mass | The running mass and the physical mass in the source conversion formula. |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The electromagnetic coupling in the one-loop mass relation. |
