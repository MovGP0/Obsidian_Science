> [!TLDR]
> The one-loop vertex correction modifies the electron–photon interaction and gives the electron an anomalous magnetic moment.

## Diagram and general structure

Figure 9.3 shows the one-loop vertex correction. The electron emits a virtual photon of momentum $\mechanical{k}$ and then absorbs it. This changes the bare QED vertex $\gamma^\mu$.

![[QFT-09-03-Vertex-Correction-Momenta.svg|**Fig. 9.3** One-loop diagram for the vertex correction $-i\electric{e}\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})$. The external photon carries $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}$.]]

> [!note]
> The source draws the external $\mechanical{q}$ guide away from the vertex. With the electron momenta shown, $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}$ instead requires an incoming photon momentum. Reverse the $\mechanical{q}$ guide, or define the outgoing momentum as $\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}'$, for consistent momentum conservation. The figure preserves the source arrow.

The source gives the contribution as
$$
\begin{aligned}
-i\electric{e}\Lambda^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
&=(-i\electric{e})^3\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{-ig_{\alpha\beta}}{\mechanical{k}^2+i\epsilon}\\
&\quad\times
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})}
{(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
(-i)\gamma^\mu\\
&\quad\times
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})}
{(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\beta,
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.62}
$$
followed by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=i\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{\gamma^\alpha
(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})
\gamma^\mu
(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})
\gamma_\alpha}
{\mechanical{k}^2
[(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2]
[(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2]}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.63}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (9.62) is reproduced as printed. Its gamma-matrix order and extra $(-i)$ before $\gamma^\mu$ do not give (9.63). For the usual open fermion line, the matrices must occur in the order $\gamma^\alpha(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^\mu(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^\beta$, with no additional $(-i)$ at the middle matrix after the three vertex factors have been extracted. Equation (9.63) has that contracted numerator order. The caption uses $\Gamma^\mu$ for the vertex, while these integrals use $\Lambda^\mu$ for its loop correction.

## Feynman parametrization with three propagators

For three denominators, use
$$
\frac1{ABC}
=2\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\int_0^{1-x}\mathrm{d}y\,
\frac1{[xA+yB+(1-x-y)C]^3}.
\tag{9.64}
$$
Set
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2,\\
B&=(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2,\\
C&=\mechanical{k}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The combined denominator is
$$
\begin{aligned}
D&=xA+yB+(1-x-y)C\\
&=\mechanical{k}^2
-2\mechanical{k}\cdot(x\mechanical{p}+y\mechanical{p}')
+x\mechanical{p}^2+y\mechanical{p}'^2
-(x+y)\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.65}
$$
Complete the square with
$\mechanical{\ell}=\mechanical{k}-(x\mechanical{p}+y\mechanical{p}')$:
$$
D=\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta_\Lambda},
\tag{9.66}
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Delta_\Lambda}
&=(x\mechanical{p}+y\mechanical{p}')^2
-x\mechanical{p}^2-y\mechanical{p}'^2+(x+y)\mechanical{m}^2\\
&=x(x-1)\mechanical{p}^2+y(y-1)\mechanical{p}'^2
+2xy\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{p}'
+(x+y)\mechanical{m}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The causal $i\epsilon$ from (9.65) is implicit in the shifted denominator.

Apply the on-shell conditions
$\mechanical{p}^2=\mechanical{p}'^2=\mechanical{m}^2$
and define $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}$.
The source states $2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{p}'=2\mechanical{m}^2+\mechanical{q}^2$ and obtains
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Delta_\Lambda}
&=x(x-1)\mechanical{m}^2+y(y-1)\mechanical{m}^2
+xy(2\mechanical{m}^2+\mechanical{q}^2)
+(x+y)\mechanical{m}^2\\
&=\mechanical{m}^2[x^2+y^2+2xy-x-y+x+y]
+xy\mechanical{q}^2\\
&=\mechanical{m}^2(x+y)^2+xy\mechanical{q}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.67}
$$
It then says that spacelike momentum transfer
$\mechanical{q}^2<0$, as in small-transfer scattering, makes
$xy\mechanical{q}^2$ negative while $\energy{\Delta_\Lambda}>0$.

> [!note]
> The source has a sign error. From $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}$, one gets $\mechanical{q}^2=2\mechanical{m}^2-2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{p}'$. Thus the consistent denominator parameter is $\energy{\Delta_\Lambda}=\mechanical{m}^2(x+y)^2-xy\mechanical{q}^2$. For spacelike transfer its second term is nonnegative. The parameter is positive in the interior of the integration region and vanishes at the soft-photon corner $x=y=0$. Equation (9.67) and the following source statement are retained above for comparison.

At sufficiently small $\mechanical{q}^2$, the source uses the positive parameter discussed above. In the limit $\mechanical{q}\to0$,
$\energy{\Delta_\Lambda}=\mechanical{m}^2(x+y)^2$.

In the numerator of (9.63), substitute
$\mechanical{k}=\mechanical{\ell}+x\mechanical{p}+y\mechanical{p}'$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}
&=(1-x)\not{\mechanical{p}}-y\not{\mechanical{p}}'
-\not{\mechanical{\ell}},\\
\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}
&=-x\not{\mechanical{p}}+(1-y)\not{\mechanical{p}}'
-\not{\mechanical{\ell}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The source writes the numerator as
$$
\begin{aligned}
N^\mu=\gamma^\alpha
\big[(1-x)\not{\mechanical{p}}-y\not{\mechanical{p}}'
+\mechanical{m}-\not{\mechanical{\ell}}\big]\gamma^\mu
\big[-x\not{\mechanical{p}}+(1-y)\not{\mechanical{p}}'
+\mechanical{m}-\not{\mechanical{\ell}}\big]\gamma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.68}
$$

> [!note]
> The two bracketed factors in (9.68) occur in the opposite order to (9.63). In (9.63), the factor that contains $\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}$ is to the left of $\gamma^\mu$. Matrix factors cannot in general be interchanged. The same reversal appears in (9.71).

Expand by powers of $\mechanical{\ell}$:

- Terms quadratic in $\mechanical{\ell}$ contribute a loop integral of the $I_1$ type.
- Terms linear in $\mechanical{\ell}$ vanish under symmetric integration, $\mechanical{\ell}\to-\mechanical{\ell}$.
- Terms independent of $\mechanical{\ell}$ form the reduced numerator $\bar N^\mu$.

The quadratic term is
$$
\gamma^\alpha(-\not{\mechanical{\ell}})\gamma^\mu
(-\not{\mechanical{\ell}})\gamma_\alpha
=\gamma^\alpha\not{\mechanical{\ell}}\gamma^\mu
\not{\mechanical{\ell}}\gamma_\alpha.
\tag{9.69}
$$
Use $\mechanical{\ell}^\rho\mechanical{\ell}^\sigma
\to g^{\rho\sigma}\mechanical{\ell}^2/d$ inside the integral:
$$
\frac{\mechanical{\ell}^2}{d}
\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\rho\gamma^\mu\gamma_\rho\gamma_\alpha
=\frac{\mechanical{\ell}^2}{d}(2-d)^2\gamma^\mu
\ \longrightarrow\
\frac{\mechanical{\ell}^2}{d}(2-d)^2\gamma^\mu.
\tag{9.70}
$$
For the part independent of $\mechanical{\ell}$, the source gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar N^\mu=\gamma^\alpha
\big[(1-x)\not{\mechanical{p}}-y\not{\mechanical{p}}'
+\mechanical{m}\big]\gamma^\mu
\big[-x\not{\mechanical{p}}+(1-y)\not{\mechanical{p}}'
+\mechanical{m}\big]\gamma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.71}
$$
It then lists these gamma-matrix identities in $d$ dimensions:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^\alpha\not A\gamma^\mu\not B\gamma_\alpha
&=(2-d)\not A\gamma^\mu\not B
+2\not B\gamma^\mu\not A+\mathcal{O}(d-4),\\
\gamma^\alpha\not A\gamma^\mu\gamma_\alpha
&=(2-d)\not A\gamma^\mu.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> These last two printed identities are not valid for general vectors $A$ and $B$. The Clifford algebra instead gives
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \gamma^\alpha\not A\gamma^\mu\not B\gamma_\alpha
> &=(4-d)\not A\gamma^\mu\not B-2\not B\gamma^\mu\not A,\\
> \gamma^\alpha\not A\gamma^\mu\gamma_\alpha
> &=4A^\mu+(d-4)\not A\gamma^\mu.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> The source formulas above are retained to make the discrepancy visible.

The source continues its list with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\mu\not B\gamma_\alpha
&=(2-d)\gamma^\mu\not B,\\
\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\mu\gamma_\alpha
&=(2-d)\gamma^\mu.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> The first of these continued identities also needs correction: $\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\mu\not B\gamma_\alpha=4B^\mu+(d-4)\gamma^\mu\not B$. The second identity, with three gamma matrices, is valid.

For the calculation of $\magnetic{g}-2$, focus on the finite part.
The divergent contribution is proportional to $\gamma^\mu$ and determines $\signal{Z_1}$.
The finite Pauli structure $\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu$ gives the anomalous moment.

### Ward identity: Z₁ = Z₂

The Ward–Takahashi identity for the renormalized vertex is
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\signal{S_F}^{-1}(\mechanical{p}')
-\signal{S_F}^{-1}(\mechanical{p}),
\tag{9.72}
$$
where $\signal{S_F}$ is the full electron propagator.
This identity is the quantum-field form of current conservation. It implies
$$
\signal{Z_1}=\signal{Z_2}.
\tag{9.73}
$$
The vertex renormalization constant equals that of the electron field.

> [!note]
> In (9.72), the inverse-propagator notation means the inverse Dirac denominator with the overall propagator factor $i$ removed. If $\signal{S_F}=i/K$, this notation denotes $K$, whereas the literal matrix inverse of $\signal{S_F}$ is $K/i$.

The general charge relation is
$$
\electric{e_0}
=\frac{\signal{Z_1}}{\signal{Z_2}\signal{Z_3}^{1/2}}\electric{e}.
$$
Using $\signal{Z_1}=\signal{Z_2}$ gives
$$
\electric{e_0}=\frac{\electric{e}}{\signal{Z_3}^{1/2}}.
$$
Thus charge renormalization is determined by vacuum polarization through $\signal{Z_3}$. The separate vertex and electron-field factors cancel in the charge relation.
The source writes
$\electric{e_R}^2=\electric{e_0}^2\signal{Z_3}$ and
$\signal{Z_3}\approx1+\electric{\Pi}(0)+\cdots$.
It then states $\signal{Z_3}<1$ because $\electric{\Pi}(0)<0$ in its expression.
The cancellation $\signal{Z_1}=\signal{Z_2}$ follows from $U(1)$ gauge symmetry and provides a basic consistency test.

> [!note]
> These charge relations are written in the four-dimensional convention. In $d=4-2\epsilon$, the relation between a dimensionful bare charge and a dimensionless renormalized charge also includes $\energy{\mu}^\epsilon$. Statements about the sign or size of the divergent unrenormalized $\electric{\Pi}(0)$ and $\signal{Z_3}$ depend on the regulator and subtraction convention.

### Anomalous magnetic moment: Schwinger's result

For on-shell electrons, write the general form of the renormalized vertex as follows.

Lorentz invariance and parity give the on-shell form
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\electric{F_1}(\mechanical{q}^2)\gamma^\mu
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\magnetic{F_2}(\mechanical{q}^2)
\right]u(\mechanical{p}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.74}
$$
where $\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac i2[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]$.
The functions $\electric{F_1}$ and $\magnetic{F_2}$ are the electron form factors.
The Gordon decomposition is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\frac{(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{p}')^\mu}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.75}
$$
The electron magnetic moment is related to these form factors by
$$
\magnetic{g}=2[\electric{F_1}(0)+\magnetic{F_2}(0)].
\tag{9.76}
$$
At tree level, $\electric{F_1}(0)=1$ and $\magnetic{F_2}(0)=0$.
Thus $\magnetic{g}=2$, as predicted by the Dirac equation.
The anomalous moment is
$\magnetic{a_e}=(\magnetic{g}-2)/2=\magnetic{F_2}(0)$,
with the charge normalized by $\electric{F_1}(0)=1$.

To extract $\magnetic{F_2}(0)$, find the coefficient of
$i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu/(2\mechanical{m})$
in the numerator $\bar N^\mu$, between the on-shell spinors
$\bar u(\mechanical{p}')$ and $u(\mechanical{p})$.
Introduce a third Feynman variable $z=1-x-y$.
The parameter integral has the symmetric form
$$
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\int_0^{1-x}\mathrm{d}y
=\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\int_0^1\mathrm{d}y
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}z\,\delta(x+y+z-1).
$$
Gamma-matrix algebra, the Dirac equation
$\not{\mechanical{p}}u(\mechanical{p})=\mechanical{m}u(\mechanical{p})$
and its adjoint, and the Gordon identities give the coefficient of
$i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu/(2\mechanical{m})$
at $\mechanical{q}^2=0$ as
$2\mechanical{m}^2z(1-z)$.
The denominator parameter becomes
$\energy{\Delta_\Lambda}(\mechanical{q}^2=0)
=(1-z)^2\mechanical{m}^2$.
The source gives the Peskin–Schroeder formula
$$
\begin{aligned}
\magnetic{F_2}(0)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\int_0^1\mathrm{d}y\int_0^1\mathrm{d}z\,
\delta(x+y+z-1)
\frac{2\mechanical{m}^2z(1-z)}
{\mechanical{m}^2(1-z)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.77}
$$
To evaluate it, integrate the delta function over $y$.
This sets $y=1-x-z$ with $0\le y\le1$, hence $0\le x\le1-z$.

Equivalently, the domain is $0\le z\le1-x$. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\magnetic{F_2}(0)
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\int_0^{1-x}\mathrm{d}z\,
\frac{z(1-z)}{(1-z)^2}\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\int_0^{1-x}\mathrm{d}z\,\frac z{1-z}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Reverse the integration order. The triangular domain
$0\le z\le1-x$ is also $0\le x\le1-z$. Integrate over $x$ first:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\magnetic{F_2}(0)
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}z\,\frac z{1-z}
\int_0^{1-z}\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}z\,\frac{z(1-z)}{1-z}\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}\int_0^1z\,\mathrm{d}z
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}\frac12
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result, called the **Schwinger correction**, is
$$
\boxed{
\magnetic{a_e}\equiv\frac{\magnetic{g_e}-2}{2}
=\magnetic{F_2}(0)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
\approx1.16\times10^{-3}.
}
\tag{9.78}
$$
Schwinger first obtained this result in 1948.
It shows how perturbation theory predicts the electron magnetic anomaly.
The source states that higher-order corrections, calculated through
$\electric{\alpha}^5$, agree with experiment at the $10^{-12}$ level.
This precision statement is reproduced from the source.

### Example 9.2: Alternative check of the F₂(0) integral

Start with the symmetric parameter form of (9.77).
Integrate the delta function and set $z=1-x-y$:
$$
\magnetic{F_2}(0)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}x\int_0^{1-x}\mathrm{d}y\,
\frac{1-x-y}{x+y}.
$$
Substitute $u=x+y$ and $v=x$.
The Jacobian determinant has magnitude one, and the domain is
$0\le v\le u\le1$. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\magnetic{F_2}(0)
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1\mathrm{d}u\int_0^u\mathrm{d}v\,\frac{1-u}{u}\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}
\int_0^1(1-u)\,\mathrm{d}u
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the first-order electron magnetic anomaly is the Schwinger term
$\magnetic{a_e}^{(1)}=\electric{\alpha}/(2\pi)$.
