## Exercise 9.1

Verify the formula (9.3) for the superficial degree of divergence in QED, using the topological relations between vertices and internal and external lines.

(a) Derive the three topological relations given in the text.

(b) Insert them into the definition (9.2) and derive (9.3).

(c) Check the one-loop photon self-energy diagram, with $E_F=0$ and $E_B=2$. Find $D=2$ and explain why the effective result is $D_{\mathrm{eff}}=0$.

## Solution 9.1 — QED superficial degree of divergence

The counting proof uses a connected graph and the large-momentum propagator powers of four-dimensional QED. The loop relation can be understood by first choosing a spanning tree: it connects the vertices without an independent cycle, and each remaining internal line supplies one independent loop momentum. External lines do not create loops. This gives the stated relation without assuming a particular drawing of the graph.

After part (c), distinguish the tensor degree from its scalar coefficient. The two explicit external momenta do not come from changing the loop measure; they are forced by the Ward identity. The coefficient can therefore be logarithmically divergent even though the original tensor has superficial degree two. A nontransverse quadratic remainder would fail that identity rather than predict a physical photon mass.

**(a)** Each QED vertex joins two fermion legs and one photon leg. Counting fermionic line ends gives
$$
2I_F+E_F=2V.
$$
Each internal photon contributes two photon ends, and each external photon contributes one:
$$
2I_B+E_B=V.
$$
For a connected graph, the number of independent loop momenta is
$$
L=I_F+I_B-V+1.
$$

**(b)** Each loop integration gives four powers of momentum. A photon propagator scales as inverse momentum squared, and a fermion propagator as inverse momentum. Thus
$$
D=4L-2I_B-I_F.
$$
The topological relations give
$$
I_F=V-\frac{E_F}{2},\qquad
I_B=\frac{V-E_B}{2},
\qquad
L=\frac V2-\frac{E_F}{2}-\frac{E_B}{2}+1.
$$
Substituting,
$$
\begin{aligned}
D
&=4\left(\frac V2-\frac{E_F}{2}-\frac{E_B}{2}+1\right)
-(V-E_B)-\left(V-\frac{E_F}{2}\right)\\
&=4-\frac32E_F-E_B.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(c)** For the one-loop photon self-energy, $E_F=0$ and $E_B=2$, so $D=2$. Gauge invariance requires
$$
\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=(\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2).
$$
Extracting the two external momentum powers leaves the scalar coefficient with effective degree
$$
D_{\mathrm{eff}}=D-2=0.
$$
The remaining divergence is logarithmic. Schematically it is proportional to
$\electric{\alpha}\ln(\energy{\Lambda}^2/\energy{\mu}^2)/(3\pi)$, rather than a photon mass term.
