> [!TLDR]
> Work through the chapter's exercises with the question, source solution, and correction notes together in each linked article.

Use Exercises 9.1–9.2 to separate power counting from the actual counterterm structure. Then use 9.3 and 9.7 to check that the vertex, self-energy, and counterterm conventions satisfy the same Ward identity. Exercises 9.4 and 9.10 test analytic continuation: state the causal prescription and whether an overall factor of $i$ has been removed before assigning an imaginary part.

Exercises 9.5–9.6 distinguish a bound-state approximation from a full on-shell subtraction. Finish with 9.8–9.9 to connect renormalization to running charge and magnetic response. Before using a numerical result, identify its perturbative order and the source correction beside it. The source solutions contain several convention and normalization conflicts; agreement with a printed answer alone is therefore not a sufficient check.

## Bibliographical notes

The source says that its treatment of renormalization mainly follows Peskin and Schroeder, Chapters 7, 10, and 12, and Schwartz, Chapters 18–21. Its citation keys are unresolved in the supplied text, shown there as “[?]”.

For the electron magnetic anomaly, the source points to Schwinger's classic 1948 article and the pedagogical discussion in Itzykson and Zuber, Chapter 7.
For the Lamb shift, it refers to Weinberg, Chapter 14, and Bethe and Salpeter's *Quantum Mechanics of One- and Two-Electron Atoms*.
For the BPHZ formalism, it cites Collins, *Renormalization* (Cambridge, 1984), and Zimmermann.
The corresponding unresolved “[?]” citation markers are not replaced with invented bibliographic details.
