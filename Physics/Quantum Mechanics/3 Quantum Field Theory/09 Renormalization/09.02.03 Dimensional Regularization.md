title: "Dimensional Regularization"

> [!TLDR]
> Dimensional regularization continues loop integrals to a variable dimension and represents ultraviolet divergences as poles near four dimensions.

The source introduces **dimensional regularization** through the work of 't Hooft and Veltman (1972) on Yang–Mills renormalizability and presents it as the standard method in particle physics.

Treat the number of dimensions $d$ as a continuous parameter:
$$
\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\longrightarrow
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
=\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}.
\tag{9.7}
$$
The source prints the last integral twice.

The dimensionally continued integrals are meromorphic functions of $d$, with poles associated with divergences at $d=4$. The physical limit is obtained by expansion in
$$
\epsilon=\frac{4-d}2\to0.
$$

The source lists these advantages:

- Preservation of Lorentz and gauge symmetry, with additional care needed for axial and chiral symmetry.
- No auxiliary regulator fields.
- Automatic satisfaction of the nonanomalous QED Ward identities.
- A systematic algebraic procedure.

## Integrals in d dimensions — Wick rotation

After Feynman parametrization, the scalar loop integrals reduce to the form
$$
I=\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{(\mechanical{k}^{\,2})^a}{(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\energy{\Delta})^b}.
\tag{9.8}
$$
The scale $\energy{\Delta}>0$ depends on external momenta and Feynman parameters. The source also describes it as containing a small $+i\epsilon$ part and calls the Euclidean denominator positive definite.

The Minkowski propagator has poles near
$$
\mechanical{k}_M^0=\pm\sqrt{|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|^2+\energy{\Delta}}.
$$
With the causal $+i\epsilon$ prescription in the denominator, the positive-energy pole lies below the real axis in the fourth quadrant, and the negative-energy pole lies above it in the second quadrant.

The source describes the Wick rotation as $\mechanical{k}_E^0=i\mechanical{k}_M^0$ and a clockwise contour rotation by $90^\circ$.

> [!note]
> These rotation and sign statements are inconsistent with the stated pole positions and the factor $+i$ in (9.9). The standard allowed contour deformation uses $\mechanical{k}_M^0=i\mechanical{k}_E^0$, counterclockwise through the first and third quadrants. Then $\mechanical{k}_M^2=-\mechanical{k}_E^2$, and the positive Euclidean denominator is $\mechanical{k}_E^2+\energy{\Delta}$. The causal denominator is $\mechanical{k}_M^2-\energy{\Delta}+i0$; if the prescription is included in the mass-squared parameter, it is $\energy{\Delta}\to\energy{\Delta}-i0$.

The resulting integral relation printed by the source is
$$
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}_M}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{f(\mechanical{k}_M^2)}
{(\mechanical{k}_M^2-\energy{\Delta})^b}
=i(-1)^b
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}_E}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{f(-\mechanical{k}_E^2)}
{(\mechanical{k}_E^2+\energy{\Delta})^b}.
\tag{9.9}
$$
Here
$$
\mechanical{k}_E^2=(\mechanical{k}_E^0)^2+|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|^2\ge0
$$
is the Euclidean momentum squared. The remaining integral is evaluated with $d$-dimensional spherical coordinates and the surface area of the unit sphere $S^{d-1}$.

The unit-sphere surface factor is
$$
\Omega_d=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)},
\tag{9.10}
$$
where $\Gamma$ is the Euler gamma function. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}_E}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{(\mechanical{k}_E^2)^a}{(\mechanical{k}_E^2+\energy{\Delta})^b}
&=\frac{\Omega_d}{(2\pi)^d}
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\mechanical{k}_E^{\,d-1+2a}\,d\mechanical{k}_E}
{(\mechanical{k}_E^2+\energy{\Delta})^b}\\
&=\frac1{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\frac{\Gamma(a+d/2)\Gamma(b-a-d/2)}
{\Gamma(d/2)\Gamma(b)}
\,\energy{\Delta}^{\,a+d/2-b}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.11}
$$

The general Minkowski formula is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{(\mechanical{k}^{\,2})^a}
{(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\energy{\Delta}+i\epsilon)^b}
&=\frac{i(-1)^{a-b}}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\\
&\quad\times
\frac{\Gamma(a+d/2)\Gamma(b-a-d/2)}
{\Gamma(d/2)\Gamma(b)}
\,\energy{\Delta}^{\,a+d/2-b}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{9.12}
$$

The main scalar and rank-two cases are
$$
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\energy{\Delta})^b}
=\frac{i(-1)^b}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\frac{\Gamma(b-d/2)}{\Gamma(b)}
\,\energy{\Delta}^{\,d/2-b},
\tag{9.13}
$$
$$
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}
{(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\energy{\Delta})^b}
=\frac{i(-1)^{b-1}}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}2
\frac{\Gamma(b-1-d/2)}{\Gamma(b)}
\,\energy{\Delta}^{\,1+d/2-b}.
\tag{9.14}
$$
Equation (9.14) follows from symmetry under the integral:
$$
\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu
\longrightarrow \frac{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}d\,g^{\mu\nu}.
$$

## Gamma-function properties and poles at d = 4

The gamma function satisfies
$$
\Gamma(z+1)=z\Gamma(z)
$$
and has simple poles at nonpositive integers:
$$
\Gamma(-n+\epsilon)
=\frac{(-1)^n}{n!}
\left[
\frac1\epsilon+\psi(n+1)+\mathcal O(\epsilon)
\right],
\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.
\tag{9.15}
$$
The symbol $\psi$ in this formula is the digamma function, not a Dirac field.

For nonnegative integer $n$,
$$
\psi(n+1)=-\gamma_E+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1k,
$$
where $\gamma_E=0.5772\ldots$ is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. In particular,
$$
\Gamma(\epsilon)=\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E+\mathcal O(\epsilon),
\tag{9.16}
$$
$$
\Gamma(-1+\epsilon)
=-\frac1\epsilon+\gamma_E-1+\mathcal O(\epsilon).
\tag{9.17}
$$

## The scale μ and expansion near four dimensions

Set $d=4-2\epsilon$. Couplings change their mass dimension when the dimension changes. The bare QED charge has mass dimension $\epsilon$, written schematically by the source as $[\electric{e}]=\energy{\mu}^{\,\epsilon}$. Introduce an arbitrary mass scale $\energy{\mu}$ so the new coupling parameter is dimensionless:
$$
\electric{e}\to\electric{e}\,\energy{\mu}^{\,\epsilon}.
\tag{9.18}
$$
The scale $\energy{\mu}$ is called the **renormalization scale**. Physical predictions cannot depend on this arbitrary scale, while renormalized parameters have logarithmic scale dependence. This is the basic idea of the renormalization group.

As $\epsilon\to0$, divergences appear as $1/\epsilon$ poles. A frequent combination is
$$
\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln(4\pi)
\equiv\frac1{\bar\epsilon}.
\tag{9.19}
$$
The source also collects the constants into a scale defined schematically by
$$
\ln\bar{\energy{\mu}}^{\,2}
=\ln\energy{\mu}^{\,2}-\gamma_E+\ln(4\pi).
$$
Equivalently, without separate logarithms of dimensionful scales,
$$
\bar{\energy{\mu}}^{\,2}
=4\pi e^{-\gamma_E}\energy{\mu}^{\,2}.
$$
This motivates the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme: minimal subtraction with the additional constants $-\gamma_E+\ln(4\pi)$ removed.

> [!note]
> The dimensional regulator $\epsilon=(4-d)/2$ is independent of the positive infinitesimal in the causal $i\epsilon$ prescription. The same letter is used for two different mathematical roles.
