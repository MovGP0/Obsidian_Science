---
title: "Self-Energy Estimate — The Bethe Method"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Bethe's estimate subtracts the free-electron self-energy from the bound-electron self-energy to isolate the atomic radiative shift.

Bethe's calculation starts with mass renormalization: the divergent free-electron self-energy is absorbed into the electron mass.
The difference between the bound- and free-electron self-energies gives the atomic shift.
The source states that at high virtual-photon frequencies, their self-energies agree “according to the equivalence principle.”
Only the low-frequency modes, $\signal{\omega}\lesssim\mechanical{m}$, remain sensitive to the atomic potential and give a finite contribution. It writes
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Delta E_{nS}}
&=\frac{2\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi\mechanical{m}^2}
\lvert\signal{\psi_n}(0)\rvert^2
\int_0^{\energy{\Lambda}}\mathrm{d}\signal{\omega}\,
\signal{\omega}
\frac{2\mechanical{m}}{2\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}
+\signal{\omega}^2}
\ln\frac{2\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}\\
&\approx\frac{\electric{\alpha}(Z\electric{\alpha})^4\mechanical{m}}
{6\pi n^3}
\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}}{\bar{\signal{\omega}}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.89}
$$

> [!note]
> The source's “equivalence principle” wording refers here to the common ultraviolet part removed by free-electron mass renormalization. It should not be read as an application of the gravitational equivalence principle.
>
> The printed integral in (9.89) has a dimension mismatch. With the normalized position-space wavefunction, $\lvert\signal{\psi_n}(0)\rvert^2$ has mass dimension three. Its prefactor divided by $\mechanical{m}^2$ has dimension one, while the displayed integral has dimension one as well. Their product has dimension two rather than energy dimension one. Thus the first expression cannot provide a dimensionally consistent derivation of the final estimate as printed.

The subtraction compares the bound and free electron using the same measured mass. Otherwise the shift would count the free mass correction twice. Atomic binding introduces a much smaller scale than the electron rest energy when the nuclear charge times the fine-structure constant is small. The ratio of those scales explains why a logarithm can be large even when the radiative correction remains perturbative.

The final estimate in (9.89), unlike its printed integral, has energy dimension one: its coupling powers, principal-quantum-number factor, and logarithm are dimensionless, leaving the electron mass as the energy scale. It predicts a rapid reduction with increasing principal quantum number within this estimate. These checks support the scaling, but cannot repair the missing dimensional factor in the first line or establish its normalization. Retain that source issue when using the result for an order-of-magnitude comparison.

The characteristic atomic excitation frequency is
$\bar{\signal{\omega}}\sim
(Z\electric{\alpha})^2\mechanical{m}/n^2$.
The source calls the following the complete relativistic hydrogen Lamb-shift result:
$$
\energy{\Delta E_{2S_{1/2}}}-\energy{\Delta E_{2P_{1/2}}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^5\mechanical{m}}{6\pi}
\left[\ln\frac1{\electric{\alpha}}+C\right]
\approx1058\,\unit{MHz},
\tag{9.90}
$$
where $C$ is a numerical constant calculated in QED.
The source states that theory and measurement agree at the $\unit{kHz}$ level, which supports QED as the fundamental theory of electromagnetic interactions.

> [!note]
> Equation (9.90) is reproduced with its source wording and numerical value. A frequency in $\unit{MHz}$ denotes the transition energy divided by Planck's constant $h$, rather than an energy in $\unit{MeV}$ directly. The compact logarithm-plus-constant expression is an approximate form; a complete precision prediction also includes higher-order, recoil, and nuclear-structure contributions. The agreement statement is the source's claim.

> [!note] Leading-logarithm normalization
> The final estimate in (9.89) also has a coefficient error; dimensions alone cannot detect it. For a hydrogenic S state in the nonrelativistic, fixed-nucleus leading-logarithm approximation, the coefficient of $\ln(\mechanical{m}/\bar{\signal{\omega}})$ is $4\electric{\alpha}(Z\electric{\alpha})^4\mechanical{m}/(3\pi n^3)$. The printed coefficient in (9.89) is smaller by a factor of eight. See [Bethe (1947), Eq. (11)](https://www.physics.umd.edu/grt/taj/624c/Bethe1947.pdf#page=3) and [Branson, The Lamb Shift](https://quantummechanics.ucsd.edu/ph130a/130_notes/node476.html).
>
> For hydrogen with $n=2$, the leading S–P logarithmic splitting is $\electric{\alpha}^5\mechanical{m}\ln(1/\electric{\alpha}^2)/(6\pi)$, or $\electric{\alpha}^5\mechanical{m}\ln(1/\electric{\alpha})/(3\pi)$. The P state has no corresponding leading logarithm in this approximation. The coefficient of $\ln(1/\electric{\alpha})$ printed in (9.90) is therefore smaller by a factor of two. Its unspecified constant cannot establish the correct logarithmic dependence on the coupling. Both source equations and the quoted frequency remain above for comparison; neither printed normalization is validated by the dimensional check.

> [!note]
> **Remark 9.4.** The Lamb shift is sensitive to both ultraviolet and infrared QED effects. Electron self-energy requires electron mass renormalization, while vacuum polarization gives short-range charge screening. Precision measurements in hydrogen, helium, and hydrogen-like ions provide sensitive QED tests.
