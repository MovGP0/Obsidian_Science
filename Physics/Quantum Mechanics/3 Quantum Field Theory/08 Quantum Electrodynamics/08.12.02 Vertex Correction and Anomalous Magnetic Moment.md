---
title: "Vertex Correction and Anomalous Magnetic Moment"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The one-loop Pauli form factor produces the electron's anomalous magnetic moment, with the leading correction α/(2π).

The one-loop correction changes the vertex matrix element $\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})u(\mechanical{p})$. For an on-shell electron, its parity-even Lorentz- and gauge-compatible structure is
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')
\left[
\gamma^\mu\electric{F}_1(\mechanical{q}^{\,2})
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}_e}
\magnetic{F}_2(\mechanical{q}^{\,2})
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
\tag{8.17}
$$
Here
$$
\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p},\qquad
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac i2[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu].
$$
The functions $\electric{F}_1$ and $\magnetic{F}_2$ are the Dirac and Pauli form factors. The on-shell structure follows from Lorentz invariance, parity, and the Ward identity.

The source writes the electron magnetic moment as
$$
\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}
=\magnetic{g}\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}_e}\mechanical{\mathbf S},
\qquad
\magnetic{g}=2[\electric{F}_1(0)+\magnetic{F}_2(0)].
$$
At tree level, $\magnetic{g}=2$, $\electric{F}_1(0)=1$, and $\magnetic{F}_2(0)=0$.

> [!note]
> For the physical electron, the signed magnetic-moment vector uses $\electric{Q}_e=-|\electric{e}|$ in place of a positive charge magnitude. The source's displayed $\electric{e}$ formula must be read with a consistent charge-sign convention.

Schwinger's first-order result (1948) is
$$
\magnetic{a}_e
=\frac{\magnetic{g}-2}2
=\magnetic{F}_2(0)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}}{2\pi}
\approx0.00116.
\tag{8.18}
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\boxed{
\magnetic{a}_e=\frac{\magnetic{g}-2}2
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}}{2\pi}
\approx0.00116
\quad\text{(Schwinger, 1948)}}.
$$

The source reports excellent agreement with experiment. Including QED terms through fifth order in $\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}$, calculated by Kinoshita and collaborators, and small hadronic and electroweak contributions, it quotes
$$
\magnetic{a}_e^{\mathrm{theor}}
\simeq0.001\,159\,652\,182\,032(720).
$$
It compares this with the Harvard measurement (2008):
$$
\magnetic{a}_e^{\mathrm{exp}}
=0.001\,159\,652\,180\,73(28).
$$
These are the numerical values quoted by the source.

The source also gives the more recent Northwestern measurement (2023):
$$
\magnetic{a}_e^{\mathrm{exp}}
=0.001\,159\,652\,180\,59(13).
$$
It describes agreement to eleven decimal places and identifies the electron anomaly as one of the most precise predictions in physics.
