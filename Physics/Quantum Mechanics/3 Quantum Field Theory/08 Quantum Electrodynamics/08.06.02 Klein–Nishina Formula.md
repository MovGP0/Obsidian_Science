---
title: "Klein–Nishina Formula"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Klein–Nishina result follows from the spin and polarization sums for Compton scattering, with the electron initially at rest.

In the laboratory frame, the initial electron is at rest, $\mechanical{p}^\mu=(\mechanical{m},\mathbf0)$. Evaluation of the traces and sums over external photon polarizations gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}_{\mathrm{Compton}}
=2\electric{e}^{\,4}\Biggl[
&\frac{\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'}{\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}}
+\frac{\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}}{\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'}\\
&+2\mechanical{m}^{\,2}
\left(\frac1{\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}}-\frac1{\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'}\right)\\
&+\mechanical{m}^{\,4}
\left(\frac1{\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}}-\frac1{\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'}\right)^2
\Biggr].
\end{aligned}
$$
Here
$$
\energy{s}=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})^2,\qquad
\mechanical{u}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}')^2,
$$
and
$$
\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}=\frac{\energy{s}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}}2,\qquad
\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'=\frac{\mechanical{m}^{\,2}-\mechanical{u}}2.
$$
In the high-energy limit $\mechanical{m}\to0$,
$$
\left.\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}\right|_{\mechanical{m}=0}
=-2\electric{e}^{\,4}\left(\frac{\mechanical{u}}{\energy{s}}+\frac{\energy{s}}{\mechanical{u}}\right).
$$

In the laboratory frame, denote the energies of the incoming and scattered photons by $\energy{\omega}$ and $\energy{\omega}'$.

The complete **Klein–Nishina formula** is
$$
\boxed{
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}^2}{2\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\left(\frac{\energy{\omega}'}{\energy{\omega}}\right)^2
\left[
\frac{\energy{\omega}'}{\energy{\omega}}
+\frac{\energy{\omega}}{\energy{\omega}'}
-\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}
\right]}.
\tag{8.13}
$$
Here $\mechanical{m}=\mechanical{m}_e$, and the scattered photon energy satisfies the Compton relation
$$
\energy{\omega}'=
\frac{\energy{\omega}}
{1+(\energy{\omega}/\mechanical{m}_e)(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})}.
$$

In the low-energy limit $\energy{\omega}\ll\mechanical{m}_e$, one has $\energy{\omega}'\approx\energy{\omega}$. The result reduces to the **Thomson formula**:
$$
\left.\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}\right|_{\energy{\omega}\ll\mechanical{m}_e}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\frac{1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}2
=\mechanical{r}_0^2\,\frac{1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}2,
$$
where the classical electron radius is
$$
\mechanical{r}_0=\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}}{\mechanical{m}_e}
\approx2.82\times10^{-13}\,\unit{cm}.
$$

## Example 8.4 — Thomson cross section

Integrate the Thomson formula over all angles:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{Thomson}}
&=2\pi\int_0^\pi
\mechanical{r}_0^2\,\frac{1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}2
\sin\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\theta}\\
&=\frac{8\pi}3\mechanical{r}_0^2
\approx6.65\times10^{-25}\,\unit{cm^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This agrees with the classical electromagnetic result for scattering of electromagnetic waves by a free electron. It confirms the correct classical limit of QED.
