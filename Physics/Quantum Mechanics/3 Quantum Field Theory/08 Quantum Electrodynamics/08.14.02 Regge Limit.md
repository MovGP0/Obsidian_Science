---
title: "Regge Limit"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Regge limit holds momentum transfer fixed while collision energy increases and describes amplitudes through exchange trajectories.

The **Regge limit** is the high-energy, small-angle limit $\energy{s}\to\infty$ at fixed $\mechanical{t}$. The source gives
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}(\energy{s},\mechanical{t})
\sim\beta(\mechanical{t})\,\energy{s}^{\,\alpha_R(\mechanical{t})},
$$
and, using the optical theorem,
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}
\sim\energy{s}^{\,\alpha_R(0)-1}.
$$
Here $\alpha_R(\mechanical{t})$ is the dominant Regge trajectory and $\alpha_R(0)$ is its intercept at zero transfer. The residue $\beta$ and an implicit fixed reference scale supply the normalization of the energy power.

For a single tree-level photon exchange, spin one gives $\alpha_R(0)=1$. The source therefore states a constant high-energy cross-section scaling. It also describes radiative corrections as producing **electron Reggeization** (Gell-Mann and collaborators, 1964). Such analyses test the analytic structure of scattering amplitudes and were later extended to QCD and the Pomeron.

> [!note]
> The unregulated total cross section for massless Coulomb exchange has a forward divergence. A finite QED total cross section requires an infrared prescription or angular acceptance. The source's constant-cross-section statement is a formal Regge scaling argument, not a finite unregulated tree-level Coulomb result.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}$ | $1$ | Invariant amplitude | The high-energy two-to-two amplitude. |
| $\energy{s}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Squared collision energy | The invariant taken to infinity in the Regge limit. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Momentum transfer | The invariant held fixed in the limit. |
| $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Total cross section | The scattering area in the formal optical-theorem scaling. |
