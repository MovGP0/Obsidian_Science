## Exercise 8.1 — Gauge invariance of the QED Lagrangian

(a) Verify explicitly that (8.2) is invariant under the local gauge transformation
$$
\signal{\psi}\to e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x})}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
\electric{A}_\mu\to\electric{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}.
$$

(b) Show that the field-strength tensor
$$
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}
=\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu-\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\mu
$$
is gauge invariant.

(c) Which Lorentz-invariant but gauge-noninvariant term could be added to the Lagrangian? What is its physical interpretation?

## Exercise 8.9 — Vacuum polarization at low energy

(a) Start from the full expression for $\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^{\,2})$ over the complete kinematic range. Its spectral representation has a cut at $\mechanical{q}^{\,2}\ge4\mechanical{m}_e^2$. Transform the corrected photon propagator to position space and obtain the Coulomb-potential correction called the **Uehling potential**.

The regime of large distance, $\mechanical{r}\gg1/\mechanical{m}_e$, corresponds to small $|\mechanical{q}^{\,2}|\ll\mechanical{m}_e^2$, where the source gives
$$
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^{\,2})
\simeq-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{15\pi}
\frac{\mechanical{q}^{\,2}}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}.
$$

(b) Show that for $\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r}\gg1$,
$$
\energy{V}_{\mathrm{Uehling}}(\mechanical{r})
\simeq-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\mechanical{r}}\,
\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\sqrt\pi}\,
\frac{e^{-2\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r}}}{(\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r})^{3/2}}.
$$
This is an exponentially suppressed correction with range of order $1/(2\mechanical{m}_e)$. The source also refers to a local $\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf r})$ term relevant to s states.

> [!note]
> A local contact term comes from the low-momentum expansion of the same vacuum-polarization correction. It must not be counted as an independent addition to the exact Uehling potential.

(c) Estimate the Uehling correction to the hydrogen $2s$ energy level. Compare it with the experimental Lamb-shift frequency, approximately $1057\,\unit{MHz}$. Note that vacuum polarization is only one of the one-loop contributions and has the opposite sign to the dominant vertex/self-energy contribution described by the source.

## Source solutions

The following solutions are supplied in Appendix D of the source.

### Solution 8.1 — Gauge invariance of the QED Lagrangian

**(a)** Under
$$
\signal{\psi}\to e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x})}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
\electric{A}_\mu\to\electric{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha},
$$
the covariant derivative transforms as
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_\mu\signal{\psi}
&=(\partial_\mu+i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu)\signal{\psi}\\
&\longrightarrow
(\partial_\mu+i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu+i\electric{e}\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha})
(e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi})\\
&=e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}
[\partial_\mu-i\electric{e}\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}
+i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu+i\electric{e}\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}]\signal{\psi}\\
&=e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}D_\mu\signal{\psi}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The adjoint carries the inverse phase. Hence the fermion density
$\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not D-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}$ is invariant.

**(b)** The field strength transforms as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}
&=\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu-\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\mu\\
&\longrightarrow
\partial_\mu(\electric{A}_\nu+\partial_\nu\signal{\alpha})
-\partial_\nu(\electric{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha})\\
&=\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}
+(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu-\partial_\nu\partial_\mu)\signal{\alpha}
=\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(c)** A photon mass term
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{mass}}
=\frac12\mechanical{m}_\gamma^2\electric{A}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu
$$
is Lorentz invariant but is not invariant under this gauge transformation, since
$$
\electric{A}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu\longrightarrow
\electric{A}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu
+2\electric{A}^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}
+(\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha})(\partial^\mu\signal{\alpha}).
$$
The unbroken QED gauge symmetry excludes this mass term. A massless photon gives an infinite-range electromagnetic interaction in vacuum and a static Coulomb potential proportional to $1/\mechanical{r}$.

### Solution 8.2 — QED Noether current

For a constant phase parameter,
$$
\signal{\psi}\to e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}\to
e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
Use the standard first-order Dirac density. The coefficient of the infinitesimal variation is $\delta\signal{\psi}=-i\electric{e}\signal{\psi}$, and
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{\psi})}
=i\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu.
$$
There is no derivative of the adjoint field in this first-order form. The Noether current is therefore
$$
\electric{j}^\mu
=i\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu(-i\electric{e}\signal{\psi})
=\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$

#### Conservation

For interacting QED, use the covariant Dirac equations
$$
\gamma^\mu D_\mu\signal{\psi}=-i\mechanical{m}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
(D_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}})\gamma^\mu=i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}},
$$
where
$D_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}}=\partial_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}}-i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}}$.
The gauge-connection terms cancel between the two fields:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\mu\electric{j}^\mu
&=\electric{e}\left[
(D_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}})\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}
+\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu D_\mu\signal{\psi}\right]\\
&=\electric{e}\left[i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}
-i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}\right]=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> The supplied proof writes the free Dirac equation with ordinary derivatives. In QED with nonzero $\electric{A}_\mu$, the covariant equations above give the full proof. The additional gauge terms cancel, so the stated current remains conserved.

### Solution 8.3 — Trace calculations

**(a)** Contract
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma)
=4(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}
-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}
+g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})
$$
with $a_\mu b_\nu c_\rho d_\sigma$ to obtain
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\not a\not b\not c\not d)
=4[(a\cdot b)(c\cdot d)
-(a\cdot c)(b\cdot d)
+(a\cdot d)(b\cdot c)].
$$
These vectors are algebraic arguments.

**(b)** With $\gamma^5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ and $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$,
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^5)
=-4i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
$$
The trace of three gamma matrices with $\gamma^5$ vanishes, as does $\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^5)$.

**(c)** Expand
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}[(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^\mu
(\not{\mechanical{p}}'+\mechanical{m})\gamma_\mu]
={}&\operatorname{Tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma_\mu)
+\mechanical{m}^2\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu)\\
&+\mechanical{m}\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma_\mu)
+\mechanical{m}\operatorname{Tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu).
\end{aligned}
$$
The odd-gamma traces vanish. Since
$\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma_\mu=-2\not{\mechanical{p}}'$ and
$\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu=4\mathbf1$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Tr}[(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^\mu
(\not{\mechanical{p}}'+\mechanical{m})\gamma_\mu]
&=-8\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{p}'+16\mechanical{m}^2\\
&=8(2\mechanical{m}^2-\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{p}').
\end{aligned}
$$

**(d)** Write the two trace factors as
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_1^{\mu\nu}
&=\operatorname{Tr}[(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^\mu
(\not{\mechanical{q}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^\nu]\\
&=4[\mechanical{p}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu+\mechanical{p}^\nu\mechanical{q}^\mu
-g^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q}-\mechanical{m}^2)],
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
T_{2,\mu\nu}
=4[\mechanical{k}_\mu\mechanical{k}'_\nu+\mechanical{k}_\nu\mechanical{k}'_\mu
-g_{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{k}'-\mechanical{m}^2)].
$$
Contracting and using $g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu}=4$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_1^{\mu\nu}T_{2,\mu\nu}
=32\Bigl[
&(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k})(\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{k}')
+(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}')(\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{k})\\
&-\mechanical{m}^2(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{q}+\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{k}')
+2\mechanical{m}^4
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
This trace structure occurs in fermion-pair production such as $\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$.

### Solution 8.4 — Muon-pair angular distribution

**(a)** In the massless CM limit,
$$
\mechanical{t}=-\frac{\energy{s}}2(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}),
\qquad
\mechanical{u}=-\frac{\energy{s}}2(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}),
$$
so
$$
\mechanical{t}^2+\mechanical{u}^2=\frac{\energy{s}^2}{2}(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
The spin-averaged amplitude is
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}
=\frac{2\electric{e}^4(\mechanical{t}^2+\mechanical{u}^2)}{\energy{s}^2}
=\electric{e}^4(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
Thus
$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}}{64\pi^2\energy{s}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^2}{4\energy{s}}(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}),
$$
and
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^2}{4\energy{s}}\,2\pi\,\frac83
=\frac{4\pi\electric{\alpha}^2}{3\energy{s}}.
$$
The source's numerical check at $\sqrt{\energy{s}}=10\unit{GeV}$ is
$\mechanical{\sigma}\approx0.87\unit{nb}$.

**(b)** Define
$$
\mechanical{A}_{FB}
=\frac{\mechanical{\sigma}_F-\mechanical{\sigma}_B}
{\mechanical{\sigma}_F+\mechanical{\sigma}_B},
$$
with the forward and backward regions given by $\cos\mechanical{\theta}>0$ and $\cos\mechanical{\theta}<0$. The tree-level unpolarized photon-exchange distribution is even in $\cos\mechanical{\theta}$, so
$$
\mechanical{A}_{FB}=0.
$$
Vector–axial interference from weak interactions, including photon–Z interference, can produce a nonzero asymmetry.

> [!note] Correction to the source symmetry explanation
> The source calls the vector current C invariant. The Dirac vector current is C odd; the photon field is also C odd, so their interaction is C even. The zero asymmetry here follows from the even tree-level angular distribution with purely vector couplings. It is not a general claim about all radiative corrections to QED.

**(c)** Neglect the electron mass but retain the muon mass. With incoming momenta $\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p}'$ and outgoing momenta $\mechanical{k},\mechanical{k}'$, the spin traces give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}
=\frac{8\electric{e}^4}{\energy{s}^2}\Bigl[
&(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k})(\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{k}')
+(\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}')(\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{k})\\
&+\mechanical{m}_\mu^2\,\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{p}'
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
Define the CM muon speed
$$
\mechanical{\beta}=\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}_\mu^2}{\energy{s}}}.
$$
For the angle between the incoming electron and outgoing negative muon, the exact Born distribution under the stated zero-electron-mass approximation is
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}
=\electric{e}^4\left[
(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})
+\frac{4\mechanical{m}_\mu^2}{\energy{s}}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}
\right],
$$
$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^2\mechanical{\beta}}{4\energy{s}}
\left[
(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})
+\frac{4\mechanical{m}_\mu^2}{\energy{s}}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}
\right].
$$
The massless limit returns part (a). The total cross section is
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}
=\frac{4\pi\electric{\alpha}^2}{3\energy{s}}
\mechanical{\beta}\left(1+\frac{2\mechanical{m}_\mu^2}{\energy{s}}\right).
$$
The factor $\mechanical{\beta}$ gives the threshold suppression.

> [!note]
> The source appends an $\mathcal O(\mechanical{m}_\mu^4/\energy{s}^2)$ remainder to the angular bracket. At tree level with the electron mass neglected, the bracket displayed above is exact. Further mass dependence in the cross section also enters through $\mechanical{\beta}$.

### Solution 8.5 — Bhabha scattering

**(a)** Let $\mechanical{p},\bar{\mechanical{p}}$ be the incoming electron and positron momenta, and $\mechanical{k},\bar{\mechanical{k}}$ the outgoing momenta.

The s-channel diagram annihilates the incoming pair into a virtual photon with
$\mechanical{q}_s=\mechanical{p}+\bar{\mechanical{p}}$ and $\mechanical{q}_s^2=\energy{s}$:
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}_s
=\frac{\electric{e}^2}{\energy{s}}
[\bar v(\bar{\mechanical{p}})\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})]
[\bar u(\mechanical{k})\gamma_\mu v(\bar{\mechanical{k}})].
$$
The t-channel diagram exchanges a virtual photon with
$\mechanical{q}_t=\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}$ and $\mechanical{q}_t^2=\mechanical{t}$:
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}_t
=\frac{\electric{e}^2}{\mechanical{t}}
[\bar u(\mechanical{k})\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})]
[\bar v(\bar{\mechanical{p}})\gamma_\mu v(\bar{\mechanical{k}})].
$$
With these channel definitions,
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}=\signal{\mathcal M}_s-\signal{\mathcal M}_t.
$$
The relative minus sign comes from the odd permutation of the external fermionic operators between the two contractions.

**(b)** In the massless limit, average over the initial spins and sum over the final spins:
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_s|^2}
=\frac{\electric{e}^4}{4\energy{s}^2}
\operatorname{Tr}(\not{\bar{\mechanical{p}}}\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\nu)
\operatorname{Tr}(\not{\mechanical{k}}\gamma_\mu\not{\bar{\mechanical{k}}}\gamma_\nu).
$$
The traces are
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\not{\bar{\mechanical{p}}}\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\nu)
=4[\bar{\mechanical{p}}^\mu\mechanical{p}^\nu+\bar{\mechanical{p}}^\nu\mechanical{p}^\mu
-g^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}\cdot\bar{\mechanical{p}})],
$$
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\not{\mechanical{k}}\gamma_\mu\not{\bar{\mechanical{k}}}\gamma_\nu)
=4[\mechanical{k}_\mu\bar{\mechanical{k}}_\nu+\mechanical{k}_\nu\bar{\mechanical{k}}_\mu
-g_{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k}\cdot\bar{\mechanical{k}})].
$$
Contracting gives
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_s|^2}
=\frac{2\electric{e}^4(\mechanical{t}^2+\mechanical{u}^2)}{\energy{s}^2}.
$$
Similarly,
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_t|^2}
=\frac{2\electric{e}^4(\energy{s}^2+\mechanical{u}^2)}{\mechanical{t}^2},
\qquad
\overline{\signal{\mathcal M}_s\signal{\mathcal M}_t^*}
=-\frac{2\electric{e}^4\mechanical{u}^2}{\energy{s}\mechanical{t}}.
$$
With the relative minus sign in the full amplitude, the result is
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}_{\mathrm{Bhabha}}
=2\electric{e}^4\left[
\frac{\mechanical{t}^2+\mechanical{u}^2}{\energy{s}^2}
+\frac{\energy{s}^2+\mechanical{u}^2}{\mechanical{t}^2}
+\frac{2\mechanical{u}^2}{\energy{s}\mechanical{t}}
\right],
$$
in agreement with (8.11).

> [!note] Correction to the source trace prefactors
> In its expanded intermediate line, the source prints a factor 16 in front of each trace bracket. Each trace gives a factor four; the product gives 16 in total. The corrected factors above reproduce the source's final squared amplitude.

**(c)** Muon-pair production has only the s-channel annihilation diagram at this order. Bhabha scattering also has t-channel exchange and interference. The t-channel term gives a strong forward enhancement proportional to $1/\mechanical{t}^2$.

Since $\mechanical{t}\propto-\sin^2(\mechanical{\theta}/2)$ in the massless CM limit, the forward Bhabha enhancement behaves as $1/\sin^4(\mechanical{\theta}/2)$. The muon-pair Born distribution stays finite in the forward direction. The source notes the use of Bhabha scattering to determine luminosity at electron–positron colliders.

### Solution 8.6 — Compton scattering with polarized photons

**(a)** For an incoming right-circular photon,
$$
\signal{\epsilon}^{(+)\mu}=\frac1{\sqrt2}(0,-1,-i,0).
$$
For an initially stationary electron and an incoming photon along the positive z direction,
$$
\mechanical{p}^\mu=(\mechanical{m}_e,\mathbf0),
\qquad
\mechanical{k}^\mu=\energy{\omega}(1,0,0,1),
$$
$$
\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}=\mechanical{m}_e\energy{\omega},
\qquad
\signal{\epsilon}\cdot\mechanical{p}=0,
\qquad
\signal{\epsilon}\cdot\mechanical{k}=0.
$$
Transversality gives the useful Clifford identity
$$
\not{\signal{\epsilon}}\not{\mechanical{k}}
=-\not{\mechanical{k}}\not{\signal{\epsilon}}
+2\mechanical{k}\cdot\signal{\epsilon}
=-\not{\mechanical{k}}\not{\signal{\epsilon}}.
$$
In the Thomson limit, the leading spin-independent amplitude is proportional to the scalar product of the outgoing conjugate and incoming spatial polarizations. With suitable helicity phases, define the reduced angular factors
$$
\signal{f}_{RR}
=\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(+)*}(\hat{\mathbf k}')
\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(+)}(\hat{\mathbf k})
=\frac{1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}}2,
$$
$$
\signal{f}_{RL}
=\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(-)*}(\hat{\mathbf k}')
\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(+)}(\hat{\mathbf k})
=\frac{1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}}2.
$$
The physical amplitudes have the corresponding common Thomson prefactor; the labels give the incoming and outgoing photon helicities.

> [!note] Scope of the supplied amplitudes
> The source presents these overlap factors in part (a) as if they were complete Compton amplitudes at arbitrary energy. They describe the leading Thomson limit. At finite energy, recoil and electron-spin terms are required. The factors above have the common amplitude normalization removed; they are not the complete invariant amplitudes.

**(b)** The reduced squared factors are
$$
|\signal{f}_{RR}|^2=\frac{(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2}{4},
\qquad
|\signal{f}_{RL}|^2=\frac{(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2}{4}.
$$
For forward scattering, $\mechanical{\theta}=0$, these are one and zero: the photon helicity is preserved. For backward scattering, $\mechanical{\theta}=\pi$, they are zero and one: the helicity reverses. Summing over the two final helicities gives the Thomson angular factor
$$
|\signal{f}_{RR}|^2+|\signal{f}_{RL}|^2
=\frac{1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}2.
$$

**(c)** For an unpolarized initial electron, the complete finite-energy spin and photon-polarization sums give the Klein–Nishina result
$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}^2}{2\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\left(\frac{\energy{\omega}'}{\energy{\omega}}\right)^2
\left[
\frac{\energy{\omega}'}{\energy{\omega}}
+\frac{\energy{\omega}}{\energy{\omega}'}
-\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}
\right],
$$
$$
\energy{\omega}'
=\frac{\energy{\omega}}
{1+(\energy{\omega}/\mechanical{m}_e)(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})}.
$$
The source quotes this formula and refers to the full spin-sum calculation; it does not supply that trace calculation on this page. It cannot be obtained at finite energy from the two Thomson overlap factors alone.

### Solution 8.7 — Symmetry of Møller scattering

**(a)** The final electrons are identical. In the CM frame, the replacement
$\mechanical{\theta}\to\pi-\mechanical{\theta}$ exchanges their momenta. A cross section that treats the two final electrons equivalently must be invariant under this exchange.

**(b)** For massless kinematics,
$$
\energy{s}+\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{u}=0,\qquad
\mechanical{t}(\mechanical{\theta})=-\frac{\energy{s}}2(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}),
\qquad
\mechanical{u}(\mechanical{\theta})=-\frac{\energy{s}}2(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
The angle replacement changes $\cos\mechanical{\theta}$ to $-\cos\mechanical{\theta}$ and exchanges $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{u}$. The squared amplitude
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}_{\mathrm{Moller}}
=2\electric{e}^4\left[
\frac{\energy{s}^2+\mechanical{u}^2}{\mechanical{t}^2}
+\frac{\energy{s}^2+\mechanical{t}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}
+\frac{2\energy{s}^2}{\mechanical{t}\mechanical{u}}
\right]
$$
is manifestly symmetric under that exchange.

**(c)** In the laboratory frame with one initial electron at rest,
$$
\energy{s}=2\mechanical{m}_e(\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}_e),
\qquad
\mechanical{t}=-2\mechanical{m}_e(\energy{E}-\energy{E}').
$$
Here $\energy{E}$ and $\energy{E}'$ are the total energies of the incident and selected outgoing electron. In the nonrelativistic limit, the spin-averaged identical-electron Coulomb angular shape in the **CM frame** is
$$
\left(\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}\right)_{\mathrm{NR,CM}}
\propto
\frac1{\sin^4(\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}/2)}
+\frac1{\cos^4(\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}/2)}
-\frac1{\sin^2(\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}/2)
\cos^2(\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}/2)}.
$$
The third term is quantum exchange interference and is absent for distinguishable classical particles.

> [!note] Laboratory-frame limitation of the supplied solution
> The source places this proportional angular expression in its laboratory discussion without an angle conversion or Jacobian. The symmetric half-angle expression uses the CM angle. For a nonrelativistic projectile scattering from an equal-mass target at rest, the selected outgoing branch obeys $\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}=2\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{lab}}$. A laboratory differential cross section also requires the solid-angle Jacobian. The supplied page does not give a complete normalized laboratory formula.

### Solution 8.8 — Tree-level Compton Ward identity

Write
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}
=\signal{\epsilon}^\mu(\mechanical{k})\signal{\epsilon}^{*\nu}(\mechanical{k}')
\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mu\nu},
$$
with
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{e}^2\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\gamma_\nu\frac{\not{\mechanical{p}}+\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e}
{2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}}\gamma_\mu
+\gamma_\mu\frac{\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}'+\mechanical{m}_e}
{-2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'}\gamma_\nu
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
\end{aligned}
$$
**(a)** Replace the incoming polarization by $\mechanical{k}^\mu$. Use the on-shell equations
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}u(\mechanical{p})=\mechanical{m}_eu(\mechanical{p}),
\qquad
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\not{\mechanical{p}}'=\mechanical{m}_e\bar u(\mechanical{p}'),
$$
the photon conditions $\mechanical{k}^2=\mechanical{k}'^2=0$, and momentum conservation
$\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k}=\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{k}'$. The contraction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{k}^\mu\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{e}^2\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\gamma_\nu\frac{(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e)\not{\mechanical{k}}}
{2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}}
+\frac{\not{\mechanical{k}}(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}'+\mechanical{m}_e)}
{-2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'}
\gamma_\nu
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
\end{aligned}
$$

For the first numerator, use
$\not{\mechanical{p}}\not{\mechanical{k}}=2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}-\not{\mechanical{k}}\not{\mechanical{p}}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e)\not{\mechanical{k}}u(\mechanical{p})
&=[2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}
-\mechanical{m}_e\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{k}^2
+\mechanical{m}_e\not{\mechanical{k}}]u(\mechanical{p})\\
&=2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}\,u(\mechanical{p}).
\end{aligned}
$$
After division by $2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}$, the first diagram contributes
$-\electric{e}^2\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma_\nu u(\mechanical{p})$.

For the second numerator, momentum conservation and the outgoing Dirac equation give
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\not{\mechanical{k}}
=-\bar u(\mechanical{p}')(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}'-\mechanical{m}_e).
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\not{\mechanical{k}}
(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}'+\mechanical{m}_e)
&=-\bar u(\mechanical{p}')
[(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}')^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2]\\
&=-\bar u(\mechanical{p}')[(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}')^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2]\\
&=2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'\,\bar u(\mechanical{p}').
\end{aligned}
$$
Division by the denominator $-2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'$ gives the negative reduced matrix element. Thus the two diagrams cancel:
$$
\mechanical{k}^\mu\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{e}^2\bar u(\mechanical{p}')
(\gamma_\nu-\gamma_\nu)u(\mechanical{p})=0.
$$

**(b)** Replacing the outgoing polarization by $\mechanical{k}'^\nu$ gives the analogous cancellation, using the same momentum conservation and outgoing on-shell equation:
$$
\mechanical{k}'^\nu\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mu\nu}=0.
$$

**(c)** Each separate diagram has a nonzero contraction. Only their sum is gauge invariant. This tree example of the Ward identity requires all diagrams contributing to the process at that order.

### Solution 8.9 — Low-energy vacuum polarization and the Uehling potential

**(a)** In the source's sign convention for the dimensionless vacuum-polarization coefficient, the photon propagator is written in Dyson form as
$$
\signal{D}'^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{q}^2[1-\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)]}.
$$
Using the one-loop polarization in this denominator and expanding to first order gives the one-loop correction. The static interaction potential for the source's attractive unit-charge pair is
$$
\energy{V}(\mechanical{r})
=-\electric{e}^2\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf q}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf q}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf r}}}
{|\mechanical{\mathbf q}|^2[1-\electric{\Pi}(-|\mechanical{\mathbf q}|^2)]}.
$$

For $|\mechanical{q}^2|\ll\mechanical{m}_e^2$, the on-shell subtraction gives $\electric{\Pi}_R(0)=0$ and
$$
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{15\pi}\frac{\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
+\mathcal O\left(\frac{\mechanical{q}^4}{\mechanical{m}_e^4}\right).
$$
Thus
$$
\frac1{1-\electric{\Pi}}
\simeq1+\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=1-\frac{\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{q}^2}{15\pi\mechanical{m}_e^2}+\cdots.
$$
For a static transfer, $\mechanical{q}^2=-|\mechanical{\mathbf q}|^2$, so the correction factor is
$1+\electric{\alpha}|\mechanical{\mathbf q}|^2/(15\pi\mechanical{m}_e^2)$.
The first term gives the Coulomb potential $-\electric{\alpha}/\mechanical{r}$. In the correction, the factor $|\mechanical{\mathbf q}|^2$ cancels the photon denominator, leaving a constant Fourier integrand. The leading contact approximation is
$$
\delta\energy{V}_{\mathrm{contact}}(\mechanical{\mathbf r})
=-\frac{4\electric{\alpha}^2}{15\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf r}).
$$
It shifts S levels because their wavefunctions do not vanish at the origin.

**(b)** Retaining the full momentum dependence gives the total one-loop static potential
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{V}_{\mathrm{total}}(\mechanical{r})
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\mechanical{r}}\left[
1+\frac{2\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi}
\int_1^\infty du\,
e^{-2\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r}u}
\left(1+\frac1{2u^2}\right)
\frac{\sqrt{u^2-1}}{u^2}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
The Uehling correction $\energy{V}_{\mathrm{Uehling}}$ is the part beyond
$-\electric{\alpha}/\mechanical{r}$.

For $\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r}\gg1$, the integral is dominated by $u$ near one. Set
$u=1+\xi/(2\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r})$ to obtain
$$
\energy{V}_{\mathrm{total}}(\mechanical{r})
\simeq-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\mechanical{r}}\left[
1+\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\sqrt\pi}
\frac{e^{-2\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r}}}{(\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r})^{3/2}}
\right].
$$
Therefore
$$
\energy{V}_{\mathrm{Uehling}}(\mechanical{r})
\simeq-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\mechanical{r}}
\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\sqrt\pi}
\frac{e^{-2\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r}}}{(\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r})^{3/2}}.
$$
The range is $1/(2\mechanical{m}_e)=\bar{\signal{\lambda}}_C/2$, where
$\bar{\signal{\lambda}}_C=1/\mechanical{m}_e$ is the reduced electron Compton wavelength.

> [!note] Potential notation
> The source labels the expression including the Coulomb term as “Uehling potential.” Here $\energy{V}_{\mathrm{total}}$ includes that term and $\energy{V}_{\mathrm{Uehling}}$ denotes only the correction, as in Exercise 8.9. The contact term is a low-momentum approximation to the same correction, not an independent addition to the exact potential.

**(c)** For a hydrogenic S state with an infinitely heavy nucleus,
$|\signal{\psi}_{nS}(0)|^2=(\electric{\alpha}\mechanical{m}_e)^3/(\pi n^3)$.
The contact term gives
$$
\Delta\energy{E}_{nS}
=-\frac{4\electric{\alpha}^5\mechanical{m}_e}{15\pi n^3}.
$$
For $n=2$, this corresponds to a frequency shift of approximately
$-27\unit{MHz}$, about $-3\%$ of the total Lamb-shift frequency quoted by the source, approximately $1058\unit{MHz}$.

> [!note] Correction to the source energy formula
> The printed expression is $-\electric{\alpha}^5\mechanical{m}_e/(15\pi n^3)$, which is missing a factor four. The corrected contact coefficient above gives the source's quoted frequency shift near $-27\unit{MHz}$.
>
> The source separately quotes an electron self-energy contribution of $+1010\unit{MHz}$ and says that vacuum-polarization and vertex effects complete the result. These are source estimates; this page does not supply a complete calculation or numerical decomposition of the Lamb shift.

### Solution 8.10 — R ratio and number of colors

**(a)** At QED tree level, for quark masses neglected well above the relevant production thresholds,
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}(\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to q\bar q)
=N_c\electric{Q}_q^2\,
\mechanical{\sigma}(\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-).
$$
Thus
$$
\mechanical{R}=N_c\sum_{\text{accessible }q}\electric{Q}_q^2.
$$
The quark charges in units of the positive elementary charge are
$$
\electric{Q}_u=\electric{Q}_c=\electric{Q}_t=\frac23,
\qquad
\electric{Q}_d=\electric{Q}_s=\electric{Q}_b=-\frac13.
$$

For three colors, the tree-level plateau values are:

| Accessible quark flavors | Charge-squared sum | Predicted ratio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $u,d,s$ | $4/9+1/9+1/9=6/9$ | $\mechanical{R}=3(6/9)=2$ |
| $u,d,s,c$ | $6/9+4/9=10/9$ | $\mechanical{R}=10/3\approx3.33$ |
| $u,d,s,c,b$ | $10/9+1/9=11/9$ | $\mechanical{R}=11/3\approx3.67$ |

**(b)** With only one color, the corresponding values would be $2/3$, $10/9$, and $11/9$. The source cites electron–positron measurements at SPEAR, ADONE, MARK-I, and CESR as historical evidence for the three-color normalization.

**(c)** Away from thresholds and resonances, the leading perturbative QCD correction is
$$
\mechanical{R}=\mechanical{R}_0
\left[1+\frac{\signal{\alpha}_s}{\pi}
+\mathcal O(\signal{\alpha}_s^2)\right].
$$
Hard gluon radiation produces multijet final states, including three-jet events. The source cites PETRA, PEP, and LEP in this context. Renormalization-group evolution also gives logarithmic scale dependence, schematically
$$
\frac{\signal{\alpha}_s}{\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{s}}{\energy{\mu}^2}.
$$
Near heavy-quark thresholds, bound-state resonances such as
$J/\psi(c\bar c)$ and $\Upsilon(b\bar b)$ modify the simple plateau picture.

> [!note]
> The constant plateau values assume relativistic accessible quarks and neglect QCD corrections. Threshold masses and resonances require more than the simple massless $1+\signal{\alpha}_s/\pi$ factor; narrow quarkonium peaks are not described by that first perturbative correction alone.

### Solution 8.11 — Crossing symmetry

**(a)** Start with Compton scattering
$\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p})\gamma(\mechanical{k})\to
\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p}')\gamma(\mechanical{k}')$.
For annihilation
$\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p})\mathrm e^+(\bar{\mechanical{p}})
\to\gamma(\mechanical{k}_1)\gamma(\mechanical{k}_2)$, take
$$
\mechanical{k}\to-\mechanical{k}_2,\qquad
\mechanical{p}'\to-\bar{\mechanical{p}},\qquad
\mechanical{k}'\to\mechanical{k}_1.
$$
The source denotes the incoming positron momentum by a barred primed momentum; it is called $\bar{\mechanical{p}}$ here. This assignment crosses each particle into its own antiparticle with reversed momentum, rather than exchanging an electron momentum with a photon momentum.

The invariant mapping is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})^2
&\to(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}_2)^2=\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm A},\\
\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}')^2
&\to(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}_1)^2=\mechanical{t}_{\mathrm A},\\
\mechanical{t}_{\mathrm C}=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')^2
&\to(\mechanical{p}+\bar{\mechanical{p}})^2=\energy{s}_{\mathrm A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(b)** In the zero-mass limit, the Compton result is
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm C}|^2}
=-2\electric{e}^4
\left(\frac{\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}}{\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}}
+\frac{\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}}{\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}}\right).
$$
Apply the crossing substitutions above together with the fermionic crossing sign.

The result is
$$
\overline{|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2}_{\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\gamma\gamma}
=2\electric{e}^4\left(\frac{\mechanical{t}}{\mechanical{u}}+\frac{\mechanical{u}}{\mechanical{t}}\right).
$$
The fermionic crossing sign converts the analytically continued Compton spin trace to the annihilation spin trace.

> [!note]
> This page attributes part of the sign change to different initial spin averages. Both unpolarized processes have an initial average factor $1/4$: electron spin times photon polarization for Compton scattering, and electron times positron spin for annihilation. The extra sign comes from the crossed fermion spin sum.

**(c)** Crossing relates analytic continuations of the amplitude in different physical channels. In the original Compton invariant names, its massless physical region is
$\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}>0$, $\mechanical{t}_{\mathrm C}<0$, $\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}<0$.
The annihilation region of that same continued function has
$\mechanical{t}_{\mathrm C}>0$, $\energy{s}_{\mathrm C}<0$, $\mechanical{u}_{\mathrm C}<0$.
In the annihilation process's own names, this is
$\energy{s}_{\mathrm A}>0$ with negative transfer invariants.

Thresholds and unitarity cuts depend on the channel. The external spinors and polarization vectors must be continued consistently, including the $u\leftrightarrow v$ replacements and fermionic signs. Absorptive parts arise from physically open intermediate channels, in agreement with the optical theorem.

### Solution 8.12 — Schwinger result for (g − 2)/2

**(a)** In Feynman gauge, write the vertex as
$\Gamma^\mu=\gamma^\mu+\Lambda^\mu$. With the overall vertex factor $-i\electric{e}$ removed, the one-loop correction is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Lambda^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=-i\electric{e}^2\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{
\gamma^\alpha(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e)
\gamma^\mu(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m}_e)\gamma_\alpha}
{(\mechanical{k}^2+i0)
[(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2+i0]
[(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2+i0]},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $d=4-2\epsilon$.

> [!note] Vertex convention
> The source prints an overall $+i\electric{e}^2$ and omits the dimensional scale factor. With the $-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu$ vertex and propagators used in this article, removing the external $-i\electric{e}$ gives $-i\electric{e}^2$ as shown. The factor $\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}$ keeps the coupling dimensionless. The definition of the vertex correction and its overall sign must be used consistently.

Set
$$
A=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2,\qquad
B=(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}_e^2,\qquad
C=\mechanical{k}^2.
$$
The Feynman parameters obey $0\le x\le1$, $0\le y\le1-x$, and
$$
\frac1{ABC}
=2\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\,
\frac1{[xA+yB+(1-x-y)C]^3}.
$$
Shift
$$
\mechanical{\ell}=\mechanical{k}-(x\mechanical{p}+y\mechanical{p}').
$$
For on-shell external electrons, the combined denominator is
$(\mechanical{\ell}^2-\energy{\Delta}_\Lambda+i0)^3$, with
$$
\energy{\Delta}_\Lambda
=\mechanical{m}_e^2(x+y)^2-xy\mechanical{q}^2,
\qquad \mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}.
$$
At zero transfer,
$\energy{\Delta}_\Lambda=\mechanical{m}_e^2(x+y)^2$.

**(b)** Extract the coefficient of $i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu/(2\mechanical{m}_e)$ from the numerator after the gamma-matrix reduction. This coefficient is the Pauli form factor $\magnetic{F}_2$.

After the gamma-matrix algebra and loop integration, the Pauli form factor is
$$
\magnetic{F}_2(\mechanical{q}^2)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\,
\frac{2\mechanical{m}_e^2z(1-z)}{\energy{\Delta}_\Lambda},
\qquad z=1-x-y.
$$
The source refers to Peskin and Schroeder, Section 6.3, for the explicit numerator reduction. At zero transfer,
$\energy{\Delta}_\Lambda=\mechanical{m}_e^2(1-z)^2$. Changing from $(x,y)$ to $(x,z)$ has unit Jacobian; integrating $x$ from zero to $1-z$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\magnetic{F}_2(0)
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
\int_0^1dz\,(1-z)
\frac{2z(1-z)}{(1-z)^2}\\
&=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\pi}\int_0^1z\,dz
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The integral is finite. The one-loop vertex's ultraviolet divergence is in the Dirac form factor $\electric{F}_1$ and its vertex counterterm, not in $\magnetic{F}_2$.

**(c)** The magnetic anomaly is
$$
\magnetic{a}_e=\frac{\magnetic{g}-2}{2}
=\magnetic{F}_2(0)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
\approx1.161\times10^{-3}
$$
at one loop. This is Schwinger's 1948 result, of first order in the electromagnetic coupling parameter. The source quotes an experimental benchmark
$\magnetic{a}_e^{\mathrm{exp}}=1.159652\times10^{-3}$.

> [!note]
> The quoted experimental number is retained as a source benchmark. The one-loop result gives the leading correction; higher-order terms are needed for a precision comparison. Ultraviolet finiteness of $\magnetic{F}_2(0)$ does not mean that every part of the full on-shell vertex is infrared finite.
