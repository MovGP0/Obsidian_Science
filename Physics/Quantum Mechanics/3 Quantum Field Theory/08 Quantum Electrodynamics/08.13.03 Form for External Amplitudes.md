---
title: "Form for External Amplitudes"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For a real external photon, gauge invariance makes the amplitude vanish when its polarization is replaced by its momentum.

For scattering amplitudes with real external photons, the Ward–Takahashi identity has the simpler form called the **Ward identity**:
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu(\mechanical{q})=0.
\tag{8.20}
$$

The quantity $\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu$ is the amplitude with the external-photon polarization vector removed. With several real external photons, contraction with the momentum of each photon vanishes:
$$
\mechanical{q}_{i\mu}
\signal{\mathcal M}^{\mu\nu\cdots}
(\mechanical{q}_i,\mechanical{q}_j,\ldots)=0.
$$

## Example 8.7 — Compton Ward identity

Let $\signal{\mathcal M}^{\mu\nu}$ be the Compton amplitude with both polarization vectors removed. Verify
$$
\mechanical{k}_\mu\signal{\mathcal M}^{\mu\nu}=0.
$$
Replace $\signal{\epsilon}_\mu\to\mechanical{k}_\mu$ in (8.12). Use the Dirac equation
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}u(\mechanical{p})=\mechanical{m}u(\mechanical{p})
$$
and the kinematic relation
$$
2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}.
$$
The s-channel and u-channel contributions cancel exactly. Thus the physical observables do not depend on longitudinal photon components.

The Ward identity also justifies use of $-g_{\mu\nu}$ in place of the sum over physical polarizations in gauge-invariant contractions. The longitudinal terms vanish, so both procedures give the same result.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{q}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon four-momenta | The momentum of a real external photon; q_i and q_j label individual photons. |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu,\signal{\mathcal M}^{\mu\nu\cdots}$ | — | Photon-index amplitude | The amplitude before contraction with the photon polarization; its mass dimension depends on the process. |
| $\mechanical{k},\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Compton momenta | The incoming photon and electron four-momenta. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron mass | The mass in the external Dirac equation. |
| $\signal{\epsilon}_\mu$ | $1$ | Photon polarization | The external-state vector replaced by momentum in the Ward check. |
| $u$ | $\unit{MeV^{1/2}}$ | Electron spinor | The incoming electron mode function. |
