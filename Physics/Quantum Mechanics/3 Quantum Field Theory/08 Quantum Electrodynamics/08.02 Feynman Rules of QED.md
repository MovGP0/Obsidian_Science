---
title: "Feynman Rules of QED"
---

> [!TLDR]
> QED Feynman rules assign propagators, vertex factors, and external-state factors to each diagram.

Chapter 7 derived the QED Feynman rules systematically from (8.2). This compact list supports the calculations below.

These rules construct the complex amplitude for specified external momenta and spins. Choose a momentum direction on every internal line, impose four-momentum conservation at each vertex, and keep the order of matrices along each fermion chain. A fermion arrow records charge flow; for an antiparticle it need not point in the same direction as the physical momentum.

## Propagators

For a fermion, electron or positron:
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})}
{\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}.
$$
For a photon in Feynman gauge, $\xi=1$:
$$
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}.
$$

## Vertex

Each QED vertex contributes
$$
-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu.
$$
The Lorentz index $\mu$ contracts with the photon line at the vertex.

## External lines

An incoming electron contributes $u^s(\mechanical{p})$.

The remaining external-state factors are:

| External state | Factor |
| --- | --- |
| Outgoing electron | $\bar u^s(\mechanical{p})$ |
| Incoming positron | $\bar v^s(\mechanical{p})$ |
| Outgoing positron | $v^s(\mechanical{p})$ |
| Incoming photon | $\signal{\epsilon}_\lambda^\mu(\mechanical{k})$ |
| Outgoing photon | $\signal{\epsilon}_\lambda^{*\mu}(\mechanical{k})$ |

## Additional rules

- Each closed fermion loop gives a factor $-1$ and a trace over spin indices.
- Each loop requires an integral $\int d^4\mechanical{k}/(2\pi)^4$ over its internal momentum.
- Divide by the symmetry factor of the diagram.
- Add all diagrams for the same process with their relative signs.

Before forming a cross section, sum all diagrams coherently. Only then take the squared modulus, sum unobserved final spins or polarizations, and average over the populated initial states. Squaring diagrams separately would discard interference. Two electromagnetic vertices give an amplitude of second order in the charge parameter and a leading cross section of fourth order.

The Feynman-gauge propagator contains unphysical components. Their cancellation in a complete physical amplitude is a gauge-invariance check; selecting Feynman gauge does not add longitudinal real photons to the final state.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{S}_F$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Fermion propagator | The momentum-space electron or positron propagator. |
| $\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Photon propagator | The momentum-space propagator in Feynman gauge. |
| $\mechanical{p},\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momenta | The fermion and photon line momenta. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fermion mass | The mass in the fermion propagator. |
| $\electric{e}$ | $1$ | Electromagnetic coupling | The charge parameter at each vertex. |
| $u^s$ | $\unit{MeV^{1/2}}$ | Electron spinor | The incoming electron mode function. |
| $\bar u^s,v^s,\bar v^s$ | $\unit{MeV^{1/2}}$ | External spinors | The outgoing electron and positron mode factors. |
| $\signal{\epsilon}_\lambda^\mu,\signal{\epsilon}_\lambda^{*\mu}$ | $1$ | Photon polarizations | The incoming and outgoing normalized polarization vectors. |
