---
title: "Physical Content of the Lagrangian"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The QED Lagrangian contains free charged fermions, free photons, and their interaction, whose strength is set by the fine-structure constant.

To predict a process, first distinguish free propagation from changes in particle content. The quadratic terms determine free modes and propagators. The term containing two fermion fields and one photon field determines the elementary interaction vertex. A diagram combines these ingredients; a vertex alone is not an allowed isolated on-shell scattering process.

The density (8.2) has three parts:

1. **Free electrons and positrons**, described by $\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not\partial-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}$, with Dirac equation $(i\not\partial-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}=0$.
2. **Free photons**, described by $-\tfrac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$, with equation $\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=0$. These are Maxwell's equations in vacuum.
3. **The interaction between these sectors**, $-\electric{e}\signal{j}^\mu\electric{A}_\mu$, which describes photon emission, absorption, and scattering by fermions.

The coupling is related to the **fine-structure constant**:
$$
\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}
=\frac{\electric{e}^{\,2}}{4\pi}
\approx\frac1{137.036}.
$$

The fine-structure constant measures the relative strength of the electromagnetic interaction. Since $\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}\ll1$, diagrams with few loops can give very precise predictions.

> [!note]
> The source says that perturbation theory “converges quickly.” Small coupling supports a useful low-order expansion, but it does not establish convergence of the full perturbation series.

The small coupling organizes corrections, but the relevant expansion can also contain large logarithms or threshold effects. Thus the size of the coupling alone does not set the error of a fixed-order prediction. At zero coupling, emission and absorption vanish, while the free electron, positron, and photon equations remain.

### Remark 8.1

Minimal coupling does not modify the tensor structure $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$. The pure photon sector remains gauge invariant in the full theory. A mass term
$$
\frac12\mechanical{m}_\gamma^2\electric{A}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu
$$
would be Lorentz invariant but not invariant under the stated gauge transformation. The source therefore relates the massless photon to local $\mathrm U(1)$ symmetry.

> [!note]
> This conclusion uses the field content and transformation law of minimal QED. Gauge-invariant mass mechanisms require additional fields or structure.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi},\bar{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac fields | The charged fermion field and adjoint. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fermion mass | The mass in the Dirac equation. |
| $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu},\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Electromagnetic field tensor | The free photon field strength. |
| $\electric{A}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-potential | The photon field in the interaction and hypothetical mass term. |
| $\signal{j}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Fermion number current | The current coupled to the photon. |
| $\electric{e},\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}$ | $1$ | Electromagnetic coupling parameters | The charge parameter and fine-structure constant. |
| $\mechanical{m}_\gamma$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Hypothetical photon mass | The mass parameter in the forbidden Proca term. |
