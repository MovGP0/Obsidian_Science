> [!TLDR]
> Compton scattering combines two tree diagrams and leads to the Klein–Nishina photon-scattering cross section.

Compton scattering asks how a photon changes direction and energy when it transfers momentum to an electron. In this chapter the electron is free; binding and collective material effects are excluded. Recoil becomes important when the incident photon energy is comparable to the electron rest energy.

Follow [[08.06.01 Diagrams and Kinematics]] to combine the two electron-exchange contributions before squaring. Then use [[08.06.02 Klein–Nishina Formula]] for an unpolarized beam and an electron initially at rest. The low-energy Thomson limit checks the angular normalization; the recoil relation checks that the scattered photon cannot gain energy from an initially stationary electron.
