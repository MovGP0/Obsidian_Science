---
title: "Consequences for Renormalization Factors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Ward–Takahashi identity makes the QED vertex and fermion-field renormalization factors equal.

The vertex changes the coupling of a fermion to the photon, while field renormalization changes the normalization of its external state. Gauge symmetry relates these two changes. In a consistent gauge-preserving renormalization convention, the identity implies equality of the vertex renormalization factor $\signal{Z}_1$ and fermion-field renormalization factor $\signal{Z}_2$:
$$
\signal{Z}_1=\signal{Z}_2.
$$
The source describes this as the result needed for the renormalized electric charge to receive no matter-dependent correction. All fermion charges are renormalized universally, as required by electromagnetic universality.

> [!note]
> Equality of the vertex and fermion-field factors cancels their ratio in charge renormalization. It does not remove the photon-field renormalization. Vacuum polarization still makes the running charge depend on the charged matter content, as described in Section 8.12.1.

The equality is a relation between factors defined in the same scheme and with the same regulator. It is not a statement that either factor is one, finite before subtraction, or separately observable. Their ratio cancels from charge renormalization, while the photon factor remains.

As a consistency check, adding a charged species changes vacuum polarization and hence charge running without contradicting the equality. This distinguishes universality of the electromagnetic charge assignment from independence of the running coupling on matter content.
