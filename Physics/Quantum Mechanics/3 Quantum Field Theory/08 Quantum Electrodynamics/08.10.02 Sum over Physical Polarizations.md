---
title: "Sum over Physical Polarizations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Current conservation permits replacement of the physical photon-polarization sum by the negative metric inside a gauge-invariant squared amplitude.

For a real external photon, the source writes the physical-polarization sum as
$$
\sum_{\lambda=1,2}
\signal{\epsilon}_\mu^*(\mechanical{k},\lambda)
\signal{\epsilon}_\nu(\mechanical{k},\lambda)
=-g_{\mu\nu}
+\frac{\mechanical{k}_\mu n_\nu+\mechanical{k}_\nu n_\mu}{\mechanical{k}\cdot n},
$$
where $n^\mu$ is a reference vector and $\mechanical{k}\cdot n\ne0$.

> [!note]
> This displayed form assumes a null reference vector, $n^2=0$. For a general reference vector, add $-n^2\mechanical{k}_\mu\mechanical{k}_\nu/(\mechanical{k}\cdot n)^2$. The source states only $\mechanical{k}\cdot n\ne0$.

The reference vector selects representatives of the two transverse states; it is not an additional physical direction in the event. Contracting the full projector with the photon momentum must give zero. A negative metric by itself fails that free-tensor test, which is why the replacement requires the amplitude's conservation condition.

In practical calculations, the Ward identity gives conservation of the electromagnetic current, $\mechanical{k}^\mu\electric{j}_\mu=0$. The reference-vector dependence then drops out, so one can use
$$
\sum_{\lambda=1,2}
\signal{\epsilon}_\mu^*(\mechanical{k},\lambda)
\signal{\epsilon}_\nu(\mechanical{k},\lambda)
\longrightarrow -g_{\mu\nu}
$$
after contraction with the conserved currents.

This matches the numerator of the Feynman-gauge propagator. It explains why earlier calculations could use the negative metric in place of the physical-polarization sum. The replacement is valid in the conserved-current contraction, not as a free tensor identity.

Use the complete amplitude at a fixed perturbative order in this test. In Compton scattering the two diagrams cancel the momentum-contracted terms jointly; replacing the physical sum in an isolated diagram can give an incorrect answer. For a measured single polarization, retain that polarization vector instead of making any unpolarized sum.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\epsilon}_\mu,\signal{\epsilon}_\nu$ | $1$ | Photon polarization vectors | The physical external-state polarization factors. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon four-momentum | The null momentum of the real external photon. |
| $\electric{j}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Electromagnetic current | The conserved current contracted with the polarization sum. |
