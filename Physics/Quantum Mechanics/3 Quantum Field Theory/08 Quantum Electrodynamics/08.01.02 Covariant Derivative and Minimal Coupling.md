---
title: "Covariant Derivative and Minimal Coupling"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The covariant derivative cancels the derivative of the local phase and introduces the electromagnetic interaction by minimal coupling.

The aim is to compare charged-field values at nearby spacetime points without making the comparison depend on the local phase convention. The potential supplies the compensating term. The sign of that term is tied to the sign of the phase transformation; it cannot be changed on its own.

Introduce the gauge-covariant derivative
$$
\boxed{D_\mu=\partial_\mu+i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})}.
$$
Here $\electric{A}_\mu$ is the electromagnetic four-potential. Require
$$
\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})\to
\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})+\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x}).
$$
Then the derivative of the fermion field transforms covariantly:
$$
D_\mu\signal{\psi}\to
e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x})}D_\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$

Direct calculation gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
D'_\mu\signal{\psi}'
&=(\partial_\mu+i\electric{e}\electric{A}'_\mu)
(e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi})\\
&=\partial_\mu(e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi})
+i\electric{e}(\electric{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha})
(e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi})\\
&=e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}
(-i\electric{e}\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha})\signal{\psi}
+e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}
+i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi}
+i\electric{e}\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}\,
e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi}\\
&=e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}
(\partial_\mu+i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu)\signal{\psi}
=e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}D_\mu\signal{\psi}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.1}
$$
The terms with $-i\electric{e}\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}\,\signal{\psi}$ and $+i\electric{e}\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}\,\signal{\psi}$ cancel exactly. Gauge invariance is restored.

The replacement $\partial_\mu\to D_\mu$ in the Dirac Lagrangian gives the matter part of the QED Lagrangian.

Add the photon kinetic term to obtain
$$
\boxed{
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{QED}}
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not D-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}
-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}}.
\tag{8.2}
$$
Expansion of the covariant derivative gives
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{QED}}
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not\partial-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}
-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
\underbrace{-\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}\electric{A}_\mu}
_{\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}}.
$$
Thus
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}
=-\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}\electric{A}_\mu
=-\electric{e}\signal{j}^\mu\electric{A}_\mu,
\qquad
\signal{j}^\mu=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$

This is **minimal coupling**. For the stated fields and the minimal kinetic term, gauge covariance fixes this interaction once the charge parameter is chosen. Gauge symmetry alone does not exclude all additional gauge-invariant interactions. The claim here concerns minimal QED, not an unrestricted effective field theory. The Pauli spin–field coupling is an example of an additional allowed interaction; see [Gies, Tam, and Ziebell, Section 2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjc/s10052-023-12080-y).

As a dimensional check in natural units, the potential has energy dimension one, as does a derivative. The coupling is dimensionless, so both parts of the covariant derivative have the same dimension. Setting the coupling to zero separates the free Dirac and Maxwell sectors.

> [!note]
> The source calls $\electric{e}$ positive and assigns negative charge to the electron. Charge signs must also agree with the covariant derivative and the identification of particle states. Quantization gives opposite particle and antiparticle charges; it does not by itself select which state has the name “electron.” With the displayed interaction and the usual convention $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}=-J^\mu A_\mu$, the source current is $J^\mu=\electric{e}\signal{j}^\mu$. Keep this convention explicit when assigning charges.

| $J^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Electromagnetic source current | The current in the stated interaction convention. |
