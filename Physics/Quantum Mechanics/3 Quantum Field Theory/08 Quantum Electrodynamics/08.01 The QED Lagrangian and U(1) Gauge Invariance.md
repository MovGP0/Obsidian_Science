## Chapter introduction

> “QED is our most precise theory. It agrees with experiment to better than one part in a billion. It is, in a certain sense, the jewel of physics.”
>
> — Richard P. Feynman, as attributed in the source

Quantum electrodynamics (QED) is the quantum field theory of the interaction between charged matter and the electromagnetic field. The source states that its prediction for the electron's anomalous magnetic moment agrees with experiment to more than ten decimal places. Its conceptual structure follows almost entirely from the requirement of local symmetry, the gauge symmetry $\mathrm U(1)$, and the Dirac Lagrangian.

This chapter derives the Lagrangian from the gauge principle, summarizes the Feynman rules from Chapter 7, and explicitly calculates the tree-level cross sections for the most important processes. It introduces trace methods and the Mandelstam variables for these calculations. The chapter ends with an introduction to infrared divergences and a preview of loop corrections.
