---
title: "Four-Vectors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Four-vectors combine temporal and spatial components in relativistic coordinates, momenta, and electromagnetic quantities.

The following definitions use the mostly-minus metric and natural units.

Use [[A.03.01 Spacetime Coordinate]] for events and displacements, [[A.03.02 Four-Momentum]] for particle kinematics, and [[A.03.03 Derivative Operator]] for wave equations. [[A.03.04 Electromagnetic Current and Potential]] applies the same component rules to sources and fields.

A list of four numbers is a four-vector only when it transforms with the Lorentz four-vector rule. The benefit is that contraction with the metric produces a scalar which all inertial observers agree on. In a wave calculation, check the momentum-coordinate phase first: its time contribution and spatial contribution have opposite signs, and the full phase is dimensionless. This one check links the definitions of coordinates, momentum, and derivatives used below.
