---
title: "Wilson Operators and ’t Hooft Loops"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Wilson and ’t Hooft loops probe electric and magnetic sectors. Their area and perimeter behavior can distinguish idealized gauge-theory phases.

In addition to the Wilson loop $\signal{W}(C)$, there is the **’t Hooft loop** $\signal{B}(C)$, a magnetic disorder operator associated with monopole or magnetic-flux insertion.
The source organizes phases through the duality between these operators:

- **Higgs phase:** $\signal{W}$ has a perimeter law; $\signal{B}$ has an area law.
- **Confining phase:** $\signal{W}$ has an area law; $\signal{B}$ has a perimeter law.
- **Coulomb phase:** Both have perimeter behavior.

> [!note]
> This is an idealized classification for suitable electric and magnetic probes. The gauge group, matter screening, and allowed line operators affect the criteria. Coulomb phases can have additional long-range, contour-dependent contributions beyond a simple perimeter term.

A Wilson probe transports an external electric color source around a closed contour. Path ordering retains the order of the noncommuting matrices; the trace removes the conjugation at the base point. The magnetic disorder probe instead specifies magnetic-flux behavior along its contour. The probes are different operators, not interchangeable formulas for one local field.

The global gauge group determines which electric and magnetic probes are allowed. Two theories with the same local algebra can therefore have different line-operator content; see [Tong, sections 2.6.1–2.6.2](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gaugetheory/gt.pdf#page=100). Apply the listed phase test only after that choice and matter screening are fixed. An asymptotic area term measures an unscreened probe's energy cost; a perimeter term alone leaves additional long-range information unresolved.
