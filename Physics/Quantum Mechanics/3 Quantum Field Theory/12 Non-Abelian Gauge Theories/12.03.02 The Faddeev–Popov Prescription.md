---
title: "The Faddeev–Popov Prescription"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Faddeev–Popov prescription inserts a gauge condition and its Jacobian into the path integral. The resulting determinant depends on the non-Abelian gauge field.

Choose a gauge condition $G^a[\signal{A}]=0$ intended to select one representative per orbit.
For Lorenz gauge (or the Landau-gauge condition),
$$
G^a[\signal{A}]=\partial^\mu\signal{A}_\mu^a.
$$
Insert the Faddeev–Popov identity:
$$
1=\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[\signal{A}]
\int\mathcal{D}\alpha\,
\delta\!\left(G^a[\signal{A}^{\alpha}]\right).
$$
Here $\signal{A}^{\alpha}$ is the gauge transform with parameter $\alpha^a(\mechanical{x})$, and
$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[\signal{A}]
=\left.
\det\left(
\frac{\delta G^a[\signal{A}^{\alpha}]}{\delta\alpha^b}
\right)\right|_{G=0}.
$$
For Lorenz gauge,
$$
\begin{aligned}
G^a[\signal{A}^{\alpha}]
&=\partial^\mu\signal{A}_\mu^{a\alpha}\\
&=\partial^\mu\signal{A}_\mu^a
+\frac{1}{\signal{g}}\partial^2\alpha^a
+f^{abc}\partial^\mu(\signal{A}_\mu^b\alpha^c)
+\cdots.
\end{aligned}
$$
The source writes the derivative as
$$
\left.
\frac{\delta G^a[\signal{A}^{\alpha}]}{\delta\alpha^b}
\right|_{\alpha=0}
=\frac{1}{\signal{g}}\partial^2\delta^{ab}
+f^{abc}\partial^\mu\signal{A}_\mu^c
\equiv\frac{1}{\signal{g}}\partial^\mu(D_\mu^{\mathrm{adj}})^{ab}.
$$
It then gives the FP determinant as
$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[\signal{A}]
=\det\left(\frac{1}{\signal{g}}
\left(-\partial^\mu D_\mu^{\mathrm{adj}}\right)\right).
$$

> [!note]
> The middle expression in the source's derivative formula is incomplete as a differential operator and has an index-order sign mismatch with the preceding field transformation. With $(D_\mu^{\mathrm{adj}}\alpha)^a=\partial_\mu\alpha^a+\signal{g}f^{acb}\signal{A}_\mu^c\alpha^b$, the Jacobian acts on a test function as
> $$
> M^{ab}=\frac{1}{\signal{g}}\partial^\mu
> \left(\delta^{ab}\partial_\mu
> +\signal{g}f^{acb}\signal{A}_\mu^c\right).
> $$
> The derivative acts on the product with the test function. The conventional FP operator is $-\partial^\mu D_\mu^{\mathrm{adj}}$; field-independent overall determinant factors can be absorbed into the normalization. The identity is used locally, with residual zero modes and copies treated separately.

The corrected operator in the callout has two distinct contributions when it acts on a test function: one differentiates the background connection, and one differentiates the function itself. Imposing the Lorenz condition can remove the former term but cannot remove the latter. Thus setting the divergence of the background field to zero does not turn the non-Abelian determinant into a field-independent determinant.

As a check, set the structure constants to zero. The operator then reduces to a color-diagonal wave operator, apart from its constant normalization, and its determinant can be factored out of normalized observables. In the non-Abelian case its remaining field dependence is precisely what the ghost–gluon interaction must reproduce.
