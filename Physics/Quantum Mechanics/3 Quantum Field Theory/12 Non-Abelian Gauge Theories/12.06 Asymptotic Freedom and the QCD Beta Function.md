---
title: "Asymptotic Freedom and the QCD Beta Function"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The one-loop QCD beta function is negative for fewer than 33/2 quark flavors. The strong coupling then decreases at high energy, giving asymptotic freedom.

Gross, Politzer, and Wilczek discovered asymptotic freedom in 1973 and received the Nobel Prize in 2004.
This result changed the understanding of the strong interaction.

The one-loop QCD beta function is
$$
\signal{\beta}(\signal{g}_s)
=\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\signal{g}_s}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
=-\frac{\beta_0}{16\pi^2}\signal{g}_s^3
+\mathcal{O}(\signal{g}_s^5).
$$

> [!note]
> **One-loop QCD coefficient**
> $$
> \beta_0=\frac{11C_A-4T_FN_f}{3}
> =\frac{11N_c-2N_f}{3}.
> \tag{12.13}
> $$

For $N_c=3$ and $N_f<33/2$, $\beta_0>0$, so $\signal{\beta}(\signal{g}_s)<0$ at weak positive coupling.
The coupling decreases as the scale increases.
This is **asymptotic freedom**.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g}_s$ | $1$ | Strong gauge coupling | The running coupling in the QCD covariant derivative. |
| $\signal{\beta}$ | $1$ | QCD beta function | The logarithmic scale derivative of the strong coupling. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The scale at which the running coupling is defined. |
