---
title: "General Structure"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A Lie algebra describes infinitesimal group transformations through generators, commutators, and structure constants.

A **Lie group** $G$ is a group that is also a differentiable manifold.
Its elements depend continuously on real parameters.
Near the identity, a generic element has the form
$$
U(\theta)=1+i\theta^aT^a+\mathcal{O}(\theta^2).
$$
Here $\theta^a$, with $a=1,\ldots,\dim G$, are infinitesimal parameters and $T^a$ are the group generators.
The generators obey the Lie-algebra commutation relations:
$$
[T^a,T^b]=if^{abc}T^c.
\tag{12.1}
$$
The **structure constants** $f^{abc}$ are antisymmetric in $a,b$, as required by the commutator.
They obey the Jacobi identity:
$$
f^{abe}f^{ecd}+f^{bce}f^{ead}+f^{cae}f^{ebd}=0.
$$
For a compact semisimple group such as $SU(N)$, choose Hermitian generators,
$T^{a\dagger}=T^a$, with a suitable normalization.
The source uses
$$
K^{ab}=f^{acd}f^{bcd}
$$
as a positive-definite invariant metric for a compact semisimple algebra.
It allows adjoint indices to be raised and lowered.
In an orthonormal basis, $K^{ab}\propto\delta^{ab}$.

> [!note]
> A Lie group also requires smooth multiplication and inversion; continuous dependence alone is not sufficient. In the usual real Lie-algebra convention, the Killing form of a compact semisimple algebra is negative definite. The source's positive form $f^{acd}f^{bcd}$ corresponds to the negative Killing form, up to the generator convention and normalization.
