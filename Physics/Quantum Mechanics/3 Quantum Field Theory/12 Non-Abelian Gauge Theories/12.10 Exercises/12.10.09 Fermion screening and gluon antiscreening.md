## Exercise 12.9: Qualitative estimate of the one-loop beta function

Use the QED analogy to explain qualitatively why the gluon contribution to the QCD beta function has the opposite sign to the quark contribution.
The source quotes $\beta_0^{\mathrm{QED}}=-4/3$ per fermion flavor in the convention
$\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})=-\beta_0\signal{g}^3/(16\pi^2)+\cdots$.

> [!note]
> **Hint from the source:** Compare charge screening by fermions with antiscreening from gauge-boson color self-interactions in the vacuum.
>
> The coefficient $-4/3$ applies to one unit-charge Dirac fermion. A QED quark contribution also includes the electric-charge square and color multiplicity; “per quark flavor” in the source suppresses these factors.

## Solution 12.9 — Fermion screening and gluon antiscreening

For one charged Dirac species, QED has
$$
\signal{\beta}_{\mathrm{QED}}(\electric{\alpha})
=\frac{2}{3\pi}\electric{\alpha}^2>0.
$$
The fermion vacuum polarization screens charge: the measured coupling increases toward higher energies.

In QCD, quark loops give a screening contribution. The source describes the induced quark–antiquark pairs as a chromoelectric dielectric response. Gluons also carry color and interact with each other. Their contribution is antiscreening; the source uses a paramagnetic analogy in which the gauge-field response strengthens the distant field of the source.

The one-loop result is
$$
\signal{\beta}_{\mathrm{QCD}}(\signal{g}_s)
=-\frac{\signal{g}_s^3}{16\pi^2}
\left(\frac{11}{3}C_A-\frac43T_FN_f\right)
=-\frac{\signal{g}_s^3}{16\pi^2}
\left(\frac{11}{3}N_c-\frac23N_f\right).
$$
The fermion term contributes with a positive sign to the beta function, whereas the gauge-sector term contributes with a negative sign. Antiscreening dominates when
$$
N_f<\frac{11C_A}{4T_F}=\frac{11N_c}{2},
$$
giving asymptotic freedom.

> [!note] Scope of the analogy
> The dielectric and paramagnetic descriptions explain the sign qualitatively. The coefficient $11C_A/3$ is the complete one-loop gauge-sector result, including the ghost and gauge-fixing contributions required by a consistent gauge calculation; it is not an isolated gauge-independent gluon-loop contribution.

The source attributes the different coefficients $11/3$ and $4/3$ to the complete count of three-gluon, four-gluon, and ghost contributions. Ghosts remove unphysical gauge-state contributions from physical quantities. This does not mean that the covariant loop integrals can be replaced directly by a count of two on-shell transverse polarizations.
