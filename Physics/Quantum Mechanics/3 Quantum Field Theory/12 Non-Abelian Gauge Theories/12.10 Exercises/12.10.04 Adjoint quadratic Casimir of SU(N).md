## Exercise 12.4: Casimir in the adjoint representation

Verify that the quadratic Casimir of the adjoint representation of $SU(N)$ is $C_A=N$.
Use
$$
(T_{\mathrm{adj}}^a)_{bc}=-if^{abc},
\qquad
f^{acd}f^{bcd}=C_A\delta^{ab}.
$$

> [!note]
> **Hint from the source:** For $SU(2)$, $f^{abc}=\varepsilon^{abc}$. Check directly that
> $$
> \varepsilon^{1cd}\varepsilon^{1cd}
> =\sum_{c,d}(\varepsilon^{1cd})^2=2=N.
> $$

## Solution 12.4 — Adjoint quadratic Casimir of SU(N)

By definition,
$$
\sum_aT_{\mathrm{adj}}^aT_{\mathrm{adj}}^a=C_A\,1.
$$
In components, with the repeated $a,e,c$ indices summed where present,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(T_{\mathrm{adj}}^aT_{\mathrm{adj}}^a)_{bd}
&=(-if^{abe})(-if^{aed})
=-f^{abe}f^{aed}\\
&=f^{aeb}f^{aed}
=f^{acb}f^{acd}
=C_A\delta^{bd}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $SU(2)$, $f^{abc}=\varepsilon^{abc}$ and
$$
\sum_{a,c}\varepsilon^{acb}\varepsilon^{acd}=2\delta^{bd},
\qquad C_A=2.
$$
For general $SU(N)$, use the fundamental completeness relation with
$\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)=\delta^{ab}/2$:
$$
\sum_a(T^a)_{ij}(T^a)_{kl}
=\frac12\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}
-\frac1N\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\right).
$$
The source states that this gives $C_A=N$ after algebraic rearrangement. An explicit check is
$$
\sum_aT^aXT^a=\frac12\operatorname{tr}(X)\,1-\frac{X}{2N},
\qquad
\sum_aT^aT^a=C_F\,1,\quad C_F=\frac{N^2-1}{2N}.
$$
For traceless $T^b$,
$$
\sum_a[T^a,[T^a,T^b]]
=2C_FT^b-2\sum_aT^aT^bT^a
=\left(2C_F+\frac1N\right)T^b
=NT^b.
$$
The same double commutator equals $C_AT^b$, so $\boxed{C_A=N}$.
