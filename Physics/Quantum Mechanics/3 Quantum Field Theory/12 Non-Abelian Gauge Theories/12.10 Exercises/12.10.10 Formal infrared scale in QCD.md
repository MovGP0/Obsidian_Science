## Exercise 12.10: QCD Landau scale

Use (12.14), $N_c=3$, and $N_f=6$, with all flavors treated as active.
The source supplies
$$
\signal{\alpha}_s(\mechanical{M}_Z^2)=0.118,
\qquad
\mechanical{M}_Z=91.2\,\unit{GeV}.
$$

(a) Calculate $\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}$.

(b) At what energy $\energy{\mu}^*$ is $\signal{\alpha}_s(\energy{\mu}^*)=1$, used here as the boundary of perturbativity?

(c) Compare the resulting scale with the proton mass,
$\mechanical{m}_p\approx938\,\unit{MeV}$.

## Solution 12.10 — Formal infrared scale in QCD

### (a) One-loop scale

Use the fixed-flavor relation
$$
\signal{\alpha}_s(\mechanical{M}_Z)
=\frac{2\pi}{\beta_0\ln(\mechanical{M}_Z/\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}})},
\qquad \beta_0=\frac{33-2N_f}{3}=7
$$
for $N_f=6$. With the supplied values
$\signal{\alpha}_s(\mechanical{M}_Z)=0.118$ and
$\mechanical{M}_Z=91.2\,\unit{GeV}$,
$$
\ln\frac{\mechanical{M}_Z}{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}}
=\frac{2\pi}{7(0.118)}\simeq7.606,
$$
so
$$
\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}
=\mechanical{M}_Ze^{-7.606}
\simeq45\,\unit{MeV}.
$$
The source also quotes
$\energy{\Lambda}_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}^{(5)}\simeq210\,\unit{MeV}$
from a treatment with different flavor and higher-loop conventions. This is a separate supplied benchmark, not the result of the fixed-six-flavor calculation.

### (b) Coupling equal to one

Within the same formal one-loop model,
$$
\signal{\alpha}_s(\energy{\mu}^*)=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}^*}{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}}
=\frac{2\pi}{7}\simeq0.898,
$$
hence
$$
\energy{\mu}^*
=\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}e^{0.898}
\simeq110\,\unit{MeV}.
$$
Using $210\,\unit{MeV}$ merely as an alternative scale in this estimate gives about $510\,\unit{MeV}$.

> [!note] Perturbative limit
> The fixed-six-flavor model ignores thresholds. The condition $\signal{\alpha}_s=1$ is a formal marker of strong coupling, where the one-loop expansion is no longer reliable. It is not a precision prediction for a physical infrared singularity.

### (c) Proton mass and dimensional transmutation

The source compares $\mechanical{m}_p\simeq938\,\unit{MeV}$ with the strong-interaction scale. A dimensionless high-energy coupling gives rise to a dimensionful scale through running: dimensional transmutation. Most of the proton mass comes from nonperturbative QCD dynamics, rather than the small up- and down-quark mass parameters, which the source quotes as being of order $5\,\unit{MeV}$.

> [!note] Numerical and physical scope
> The source states $\mechanical{m}_p/\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}\simeq4$–$5$. This fits the quoted $210\,\unit{MeV}$ benchmark; the $45\,\unit{MeV}$ estimate instead gives about $21$. Its statement that “99%” is binding energy and gluon fluctuations “not matter” is a qualitative claim, not a mass decomposition established here. The quark and gluon energy and interactions are part of the hadron; a precise split requires specified operator and renormalization conventions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\alpha}_s$ | $1$ | Strong coupling | The perturbative order parameter for the annihilation amplitude. |
| $\mechanical{M}_Z$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Z-boson mass | The 91.2 GeV reference scale supplied in Exercise 12.10. |
| $\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}},\energy{\mu}^*$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | QCD and strong-coupling scales | The formal infrared pole scale and the energy at which α_s reaches one. |
| $\mechanical{m}_p$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Proton mass | The hadronic mass used for comparison. |
