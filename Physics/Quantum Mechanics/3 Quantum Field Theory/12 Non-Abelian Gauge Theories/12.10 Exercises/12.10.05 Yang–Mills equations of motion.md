## Exercise 12.5: Equation of motion from the variational principle

Starting from the Yang–Mills Lagrangian density (12.5), derive the Euler–Lagrange equations for $\signal{A}_\mu^a$.
Show that
$$
(D^\nu\signal{F}_{\nu\mu})^a=0.
$$

## Solution 12.5 — Yang–Mills equations of motion

Start with
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}
=-\frac14\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}^a\signal{F}^{a\mu\nu},
\qquad
\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}^a
=\partial_\mu\signal{A}_\nu^a-\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\mu^a
+\signal{g}f^{abc}\signal{A}_\mu^b\signal{A}_\nu^c.
$$
The Euler–Lagrange equation is
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}}{\partial\signal{A}_\mu^a}
-\partial_\nu
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}}
{\partial(\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\mu^a)}=0.
$$

The derivatives are
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}}
{\partial(\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\mu^a)}
=-\signal{F}^{a\nu\mu}=\signal{F}^{a\mu\nu},
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}}{\partial\signal{A}_\mu^a}
&=-\frac12\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}^b
\frac{\partial\signal{F}^{b\rho\sigma}}{\partial\signal{A}_\mu^a}\\
&=-\frac12\signal{g}f^{bac}\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}^b
\left(g^{\rho\mu}\signal{A}^{c\sigma}
-g^{\sigma\mu}\signal{A}^{c\rho}\right)\\
&=-\signal{g}f^{bac}\signal{F}^{b\mu}{}_\nu\signal{A}^{c\nu}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Antisymmetry of the field tensor combines the two contributions. Reordering the color indices in the Euler–Lagrange equation gives
$$
\boxed{
(D_\nu\signal{F}^{\nu\mu})^a
=\partial_\nu\signal{F}^{a\nu\mu}
+\signal{g}f^{abc}\signal{A}_\nu^b\signal{F}^{c\nu\mu}=0.
}
$$
In matrix notation this is $[D_\nu,\signal{F}^{\nu\mu}]=0$, where the bracket means the adjoint covariant derivative acting on the matrix-valued field strength. It is the non-Abelian counterpart of the source-free Maxwell equation.
