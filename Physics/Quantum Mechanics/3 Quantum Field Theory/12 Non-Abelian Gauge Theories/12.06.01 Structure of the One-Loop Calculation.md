---
title: "Structure of the One-Loop Calculation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> One-loop gluon, ghost, and quark corrections determine the renormalization factors that give the QCD beta function. Gauge identities constrain the transverse polarization tensor.

The calculation involves the gluon two-point function at one loop.
The source describes the requirement as independence of the physical coupling $\signal{g}_s(\energy{\mu})$ from the arbitrary scale $\energy{\mu}$.
The gluon self-energy is written as
$$
\signal{\Pi}_{\mu\nu}^{ab}(\mechanical{k})
=\delta^{ab}(\mechanical{k}^2g_{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{k}_\mu\mechanical{k}_\nu)
\signal{\Pi}(\mechanical{k}^2).
$$
Transversality fixes this tensor structure.
The scalar function $\signal{\Pi}(\mechanical{k}^2)$ collects the one-loop corrections.

> [!note]
> The running coupling itself depends on the renormalization scale. Physical observables are independent of that arbitrary scale when all orders are included. In an ordinary covariant gauge, the gluon two-point function alone does not determine the coupling counterterm: the relevant vertex and field renormalizations are related by Slavnov–Taylor identities. A background-field formulation can extract the coupling renormalization from the background-gluon two-point function alone.

There are three main types of contribution, corresponding to the loop classes inserted into the gluon propagator.

## Gluon loop

The loop in Fig. 12.8 is made by joining two three-gluon vertices.
It contributes a factor proportional to $C_A$.
Together with the tadpole diagram containing a four-gluon vertex, it has the form
$$
\signal{\Pi}_{gg+\mathrm{tad}}(\mechanical{k}^2)
=\frac{\signal{g}_s^2}{16\pi^2}\,
C_A\,c_g(\xi)\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{-\mechanical{k}^2}\right)
+\cdots,
$$
where $c_g(\xi)$ is gauge dependent.
The source describes the separate contributions as gauge dependent and states that the tadpole is required for a transverse self-energy.

> [!note]
> In massless Yang–Mills theory with dimensional regularization, the one-loop gluon tadpole is a scaleless integral and vanishes. It must be included in the diagram classification, but a nonzero tadpole is not universally required to obtain transversality. The full gluon-plus-ghost result obeys the gauge identity. The two external arrows in the source diagram both point inward and both carry the label $\mechanical{k}$; for a two-point function, the all-incoming external momenta must instead be opposite. The diagram preserves the source's labels.

## Ghost loop

The ghost loop, shown in Fig. 12.9, is needed for unitarity and BRST consistency.
Anticommuting ghosts give a factor $-1$ relative to a bosonic loop.
Its contribution has the form
$$
\signal{\Pi}_{\bar cc}(\mechanical{k}^2)
=\frac{\signal{g}_s^2}{16\pi^2}\,
C_A\,c_{\bar cc}(\xi)
\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{-\mechanical{k}^2}\right)
+\cdots.
$$

![[QFT-12-09-Ghost-Loop.svg|**Fig. 12.9** Ghost loop: a negative contribution proportional to the adjoint Casimir. The arrows indicate the circulation direction.]]

The source gives the purely gluonic result, after combining the gluon loop, four-gluon tadpole, and ghost loop, as
$$
\signal{\Pi}_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mechanical{k}^2)
=\frac{\signal{g}_s^2}{16\pi^2}\,
C_A\,\frac{11}{6}
\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{-\mechanical{k}^2}\right)
+\text{finite terms}.
$$
It calls the coefficient $11/6$ gauge invariant and stresses that the gluon, tadpole, and ghost contributions must be treated consistently.

> [!note]
> This displayed coefficient is retained from the source. The ordinary quantum-gluon two-point function in a covariant gauge is gauge dependent; its coefficient cannot in general be identified with the universal beta-function coefficient without specifying the field and vertex renormalization convention. The universal pure Yang–Mills coefficient in $\beta_0$ is $11C_A/3$, as in (12.13). The massless tadpole qualification and the external-momentum convention noted for Fig. 12.8 also apply here.

![[QFT-12-10-Quark-Loop.svg|**Fig. 12.10** Quark-loop contribution to gluon polarization for each quark flavor.]]

## Quark loop

Each of the $N_f$ quark flavors gives a fermion loop, shown in Fig. 12.10, analogous to QED vacuum polarization.

The source gives the quark contribution as
$$
\signal{\Pi}_q(\mechanical{k}^2)
=-\frac{\signal{g}_s^2}{16\pi^2}\,
\frac{2}{3}T_FN_f
\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{-\mechanical{k}^2}\right)
+\cdots.
$$
The negative sign reflects the fermion-loop factor $-1$ relative to a bosonic loop and the screening contribution, as in QED.
The factor $T_F=1/2$ comes from the quark-loop color trace.

> [!note]
> The displayed $2T_FN_f/3$ coefficient follows the source's normalization of the scalar polarization. For the conventional transverse one-loop quark polarization defined directly from the two-point graph, the logarithmic coefficient has magnitude $4T_FN_f/3$ in units of $\signal{g}_s^2/(16\pi^2)$. Keep this normalization issue separate from the universal beta-function coefficient quoted below.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g}_s$ | $1$ | Strong gauge coupling | The scale-dependent renormalized QCD coupling. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The arbitrary scale whose dependence cancels in physical observables. |
| $\mechanical{k},\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | External and loop momenta | The gluon momentum and the integration momentum in Fig. 12.8. |
| $\signal{\Pi}_{\mu\nu}^{ab}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Gluon polarization tensor | The transverse two-point self-energy tensor. |
| $\signal{\Pi}(\mechanical{k}^2),\signal{\Pi}_{gg+\mathrm{tad}},\signal{\Pi}_{\bar cc}$ | $1$ | Scalar gluon polarization contributions | The total scalar coefficient and the schematic gluon and ghost terms. |
| $\signal{\Pi}_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ | $1$ | Pure-gauge polarization coefficient | The source's scalar gluon-plus-ghost coefficient. |
| $\signal{\Pi}_q$ | $1$ | Quark polarization contribution | The source's scalar quark-loop coefficient. |
