\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{ann}}

## Exercise 12.12: Symmetries of the four-gluon vertex

Verify that the four-gluon vertex (12.8) is invariant under simultaneous exchange of the complete labels $(a,\mu)\leftrightarrow(b,\nu)$ and $(c,\rho)\leftrightarrow(d,\sigma)$.
This is the Bose symmetry required for identical gluons.

> [!note]
> **Historical note from the source:** The source traces the gauge-symmetry idea to Hermann Weyl's work in the 1920s in the context of general relativity and local phase redefinition. Yang and Mills developed the non-Abelian generalization in 1954. Faddeev and Popov gave a consistent quantization with ghost fields in 1967. Gross and Wilczek at Princeton and Politzer at Harvard discovered asymptotic freedom in 1973, which supported the identification of QCD as the strong-interaction theory. The three received the 2004 Nobel Prize. Becchi, Rouet, and Stora (1974), and independently Tyutin (1975), formulated BRST symmetry.
>
> The source's Weyl attribution concerns the development of gauge ideas more broadly. Local phase redefinition by itself describes an Abelian symmetry; it does not supply the non-Abelian Yang–Mills structure.

## Source solutions

These solutions follow the supplied Appendix D pages. Corrections and convention changes are marked explicitly.

### Solution 12.1 — Structure constants of SU(2)

Use $T^a=\sigma^a/2$. Then
$$
[T^1,T^2]=\frac14[\sigma^1,\sigma^2].
$$
Direct matrix multiplication gives
$$
\sigma^1\sigma^2
=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\0&-i\end{pmatrix}
=i\sigma^3.
$$
Since $\sigma^2\sigma^1=-i\sigma^3$,
$$
[\sigma^1,\sigma^2]=2i\sigma^3.
$$

Thus
$$
[T^1,T^2]=\frac14(2i\sigma^3)=iT^3,
\qquad f^{123}=1.
$$
Antisymmetry and cyclic permutations give $f^{abc}=\varepsilon^{abc}$.

### Solution 12.2 — Covariance of the covariant derivative

With
$$
D_\mu=\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\signal{A}_\mu,
\qquad
\signal{A}'_\mu
=U\signal{A}_\mu U^\dagger+\frac{i}{\signal{g}}U\partial_\mu U^\dagger,
\qquad
\signal{\psi}'=U\signal{\psi},
$$
expand
$$
\begin{aligned}
D'_\mu(U\signal{\psi})
&=(\partial_\mu U)\signal{\psi}+U\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}
-i\signal{g}
\left[U\signal{A}_\mu U^\dagger
+\frac{i}{\signal{g}}U\partial_\mu U^\dagger\right]U\signal{\psi}\\
&=(\partial_\mu U)\signal{\psi}+U\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}
-i\signal{g}U\signal{A}_\mu\signal{\psi}
+U(\partial_\mu U^\dagger)U\signal{\psi}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Differentiating $UU^\dagger=1$ gives
$$
(\partial_\mu U)U^\dagger=-U(\partial_\mu U^\dagger),
\qquad
U(\partial_\mu U^\dagger)U=-\partial_\mu U.
$$
The two derivative-of-$U$ terms cancel:
$$
D'_\mu(U\signal{\psi})
=U(\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\signal{A}_\mu)\signal{\psi}
=U(D_\mu\signal{\psi}).
$$

### Solution 12.3 — Jacobi identity for the structure constants

Start with the operator identity
$$
[[T^a,T^b],T^c]+[[T^b,T^c],T^a]+[[T^c,T^a],T^b]=0.
$$
Using $[T^a,T^b]=if^{abe}T^e$ gives
$$
[[T^a,T^b],T^c]
=if^{abe}[T^e,T^c]
=-f^{abe}f^{ecd}T^d.
$$
The cyclic sum is therefore
$$
-\left(f^{abe}f^{ecd}+f^{bce}f^{ead}+f^{cae}f^{ebd}\right)T^d=0.
$$

In a faithful representation the generators are linearly independent, so
$$
\boxed{f^{abe}f^{ecd}+f^{bce}f^{ead}+f^{cae}f^{ebd}=0.}
$$
This also establishes that $(T_{\mathrm{adj}}^a)_{bc}=-if^{abc}$ obey the Lie-algebra commutation relations.

### Solution 12.4 — Adjoint quadratic Casimir of SU(N)

By definition,
$$
\sum_aT_{\mathrm{adj}}^aT_{\mathrm{adj}}^a=C_A\,1.
$$
In components, with the repeated $a,e,c$ indices summed where present,
$$
\begin{aligned}
(T_{\mathrm{adj}}^aT_{\mathrm{adj}}^a)_{bd}
&=(-if^{abe})(-if^{aed})
=-f^{abe}f^{aed}\\
&=f^{aeb}f^{aed}
=f^{acb}f^{acd}
=C_A\delta^{bd}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $SU(2)$, $f^{abc}=\varepsilon^{abc}$ and
$$
\sum_{a,c}\varepsilon^{acb}\varepsilon^{acd}=2\delta^{bd},
\qquad C_A=2.
$$
For general $SU(N)$, use the fundamental completeness relation with
$\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)=\delta^{ab}/2$:
$$
\sum_a(T^a)_{ij}(T^a)_{kl}
=\frac12\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}
-\frac1N\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\right).
$$
The source states that this gives $C_A=N$ after algebraic rearrangement. An explicit check is
$$
\sum_aT^aXT^a=\frac12\operatorname{tr}(X)\,1-\frac{X}{2N},
\qquad
\sum_aT^aT^a=C_F\,1,\quad C_F=\frac{N^2-1}{2N}.
$$
For traceless $T^b$,
$$
\sum_a[T^a,[T^a,T^b]]
=2C_FT^b-2\sum_aT^aT^bT^a
=\left(2C_F+\frac1N\right)T^b
=NT^b.
$$
The same double commutator equals $C_AT^b$, so $\boxed{C_A=N}$.

### Solution 12.5 — Yang–Mills equations of motion

Start with
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}
=-\frac14\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}^a\signal{F}^{a\mu\nu},
\qquad
\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}^a
=\partial_\mu\signal{A}_\nu^a-\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\mu^a
+\signal{g}f^{abc}\signal{A}_\mu^b\signal{A}_\nu^c.
$$
The Euler–Lagrange equation is
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}}{\partial\signal{A}_\mu^a}
-\partial_\nu
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}}
{\partial(\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\mu^a)}=0.
$$

The derivatives are
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}}
{\partial(\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\mu^a)}
=-\signal{F}^{a\nu\mu}=\signal{F}^{a\mu\nu},
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{YM}}}{\partial\signal{A}_\mu^a}
&=-\frac12\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}^b
\frac{\partial\signal{F}^{b\rho\sigma}}{\partial\signal{A}_\mu^a}\\
&=-\frac12\signal{g}f^{bac}\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}^b
\left(g^{\rho\mu}\signal{A}^{c\sigma}
-g^{\sigma\mu}\signal{A}^{c\rho}\right)\\
&=-\signal{g}f^{bac}\signal{F}^{b\mu}{}_\nu\signal{A}^{c\nu}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Antisymmetry of the field tensor combines the two contributions. Reordering the color indices in the Euler–Lagrange equation gives
$$
\boxed{
(D_\nu\signal{F}^{\nu\mu})^a
=\partial_\nu\signal{F}^{a\nu\mu}
+\signal{g}f^{abc}\signal{A}_\nu^b\signal{F}^{c\nu\mu}=0.
}
$$
In matrix notation this is $[D_\nu,\signal{F}^{\nu\mu}]=0$, where the bracket means the adjoint covariant derivative acting on the matrix-valued field strength. It is the non-Abelian counterpart of the source-free Maxwell equation.

### Solution 12.6 — Non-Abelian Bianchi identity

Use
$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]=-i\signal{g}\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}
$$
in the Jacobi identity
$$
[D_\mu,[D_\nu,D_\rho]]
+[D_\nu,[D_\rho,D_\mu]]
+[D_\rho,[D_\mu,D_\nu]]=0.
$$
It follows that
$$
[D_\mu,\signal{F}_{\nu\rho}]
+[D_\nu,\signal{F}_{\rho\mu}]
+[D_\rho,\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}]=0.
$$
Contracting the cyclic identity with the Levi-Civita tensor gives the equivalent dual form
$$
\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}[D_\nu,\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}]=0.
$$

Define the dual tensor by
$$
\tilde{\signal{F}}^{\mu\nu}
=\frac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}.
$$
The identity is $\boxed{D_\mu\tilde{\signal{F}}^{\mu\nu}=0}$.
It follows from the definition of the field strength as a curvature, without use of the equations of motion.

### Solution 12.7 — Three-gluon vertex with two equal color indices

The three-gluon vertex is proportional to $f^{abc}$. Setting $a=b$ gives
$$
f^{aac}=0
$$
by antisymmetry. Thus a vertex with two identical adjoint color labels vanishes.

> [!note] Color selection
> The source describes this as requiring three different “color types.” Distinct adjoint indices are a necessary condition for a nonzero structure constant, but they are not sufficient: some structure constants with three distinct indices also vanish. This is a statement about components in a chosen Lie-algebra basis.

### Solution 12.8 — Annihilation color factor for quark–antiquark scattering

For an $s$-channel gluon, strip off the kinematic spinor currents and write the color factor for fixed external colors as
$$
\mathcal C_{ji;kl}=\sum_a(T^a)_{ji}(T^a)_{kl}.
$$
Average over the two incoming colors and sum over the outgoing colors:
$$
\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_{i,j,k,l}
\lvert\mathcal C_{ji;kl}\rvert^2
=\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_{i,j,k,l,a,b}
(T^a)_{ji}(T^a)_{kl}
(T^b)^*_{ji}(T^b)^*_{kl}.
$$
Hermiticity gives $(T^b)^*_{ji}=(T^b)_{ij}$. Therefore
$$
\sum_{i,j}(T^a)_{ji}(T^b)_{ij}
=\operatorname{tr}(T^bT^a)=T_F\delta^{ab},
$$
and the second pair of color sums gives the same trace. Hence
$$
\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_{\mathrm{colors}}\lvert\mathcal C\rvert^2
=\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_{a,b}T_F^2\delta^{ab}\delta^{ab}
=\frac{T_F^2(N_c^2-1)}{N_c^2}.
$$

For $N_c=3$ and $T_F=1/2$,
$$
\boxed{\frac{T_F^2(N_c^2-1)}{N_c^2}=\frac29.}
$$

> [!note] Color-factor mismatch
> The exercise states $16/9$. The supplied solution obtains $2/9$ and suggests a different normalization or a combination of channels. Simply omitting the initial $1/N_c^2$ average gives $2$, not $16/9$. Thus $16/9$ does not follow from the stated single-channel color sum with the stated generator normalization. A different channel structure would need a separate calculation. The colorless QED factor is one.

### Solution 12.9 — Fermion screening and gluon antiscreening

For one charged Dirac species, QED has
$$
\signal{\beta}_{\mathrm{QED}}(\electric{\alpha})
=\frac{2}{3\pi}\electric{\alpha}^2>0.
$$
The fermion vacuum polarization screens charge: the measured coupling increases toward higher energies.

In QCD, quark loops give a screening contribution. The source describes the induced quark–antiquark pairs as a chromoelectric dielectric response. Gluons also carry color and interact with each other. Their contribution is antiscreening; the source uses a paramagnetic analogy in which the gauge-field response strengthens the distant field of the source.

The one-loop result is
$$
\signal{\beta}_{\mathrm{QCD}}(\signal{g}_s)
=-\frac{\signal{g}_s^3}{16\pi^2}
\left(\frac{11}{3}C_A-\frac43T_FN_f\right)
=-\frac{\signal{g}_s^3}{16\pi^2}
\left(\frac{11}{3}N_c-\frac23N_f\right).
$$
The fermion term contributes with a positive sign to the beta function, whereas the gauge-sector term contributes with a negative sign. Antiscreening dominates when
$$
N_f<\frac{11C_A}{4T_F}=\frac{11N_c}{2},
$$
giving asymptotic freedom.

> [!note] Scope of the analogy
> The dielectric and paramagnetic descriptions explain the sign qualitatively. The coefficient $11C_A/3$ is the complete one-loop gauge-sector result, including the ghost and gauge-fixing contributions required by a consistent gauge calculation; it is not an isolated gauge-independent gluon-loop contribution.

The source attributes the different coefficients $11/3$ and $4/3$ to the complete count of three-gluon, four-gluon, and ghost contributions. Ghosts remove unphysical gauge-state contributions from physical quantities. This does not mean that the covariant loop integrals can be replaced directly by a count of two on-shell transverse polarizations.

### Solution 12.10 — Formal infrared scale in QCD

#### (a) One-loop scale

Use the fixed-flavor relation
$$
\signal{\alpha}_s(\mechanical{M}_Z)
=\frac{2\pi}{\beta_0\ln(\mechanical{M}_Z/\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}})},
\qquad \beta_0=\frac{33-2N_f}{3}=7
$$
for $N_f=6$. With the supplied values
$\signal{\alpha}_s(\mechanical{M}_Z)=0.118$ and
$\mechanical{M}_Z=91.2\,\unit{GeV}$,
$$
\ln\frac{\mechanical{M}_Z}{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}}
=\frac{2\pi}{7(0.118)}\simeq7.606,
$$
so
$$
\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}
=\mechanical{M}_Ze^{-7.606}
\simeq45\,\unit{MeV}.
$$
The source also quotes
$\energy{\Lambda}_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}^{(5)}\simeq210\,\unit{MeV}$
from a treatment with different flavor and higher-loop conventions. This is a separate supplied benchmark, not the result of the fixed-six-flavor calculation.

#### (b) Coupling equal to one

Within the same formal one-loop model,
$$
\signal{\alpha}_s(\energy{\mu}^*)=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}^*}{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}}
=\frac{2\pi}{7}\simeq0.898,
$$
hence
$$
\energy{\mu}^*
=\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}e^{0.898}
\simeq110\,\unit{MeV}.
$$
Using $210\,\unit{MeV}$ merely as an alternative scale in this estimate gives about $510\,\unit{MeV}$.

> [!note] Perturbative limit
> The fixed-six-flavor model ignores thresholds. The condition $\signal{\alpha}_s=1$ is a formal marker of strong coupling, where the one-loop expansion is no longer reliable. It is not a precision prediction for a physical infrared singularity.

#### (c) Proton mass and dimensional transmutation

The source compares $\mechanical{m}_p\simeq938\,\unit{MeV}$ with the strong-interaction scale. A dimensionless high-energy coupling gives rise to a dimensionful scale through running: dimensional transmutation. Most of the proton mass comes from nonperturbative QCD dynamics, rather than the small up- and down-quark mass parameters, which the source quotes as being of order $5\,\unit{MeV}$.

> [!note] Numerical and physical scope
> The source states $\mechanical{m}_p/\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}\simeq4$–$5$. This fits the quoted $210\,\unit{MeV}$ benchmark; the $45\,\unit{MeV}$ estimate instead gives about $21$. Its statement that “99%” is binding energy and gluon fluctuations “not matter” is a qualitative claim, not a mass decomposition established here. The quark and gluon energy and interactions are part of the hadron; a precise split requires specified operator and renormalization conventions.

### Solution 12.11 — Gluon propagator in covariant gauges

#### (a) Feynman and Landau gauges

The free propagator is
$$
\signal{D}_{\mu\nu}^{ab}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-i\delta^{ab}}{\mechanical{k}^2+i0}
\left[g_{\mu\nu}-(1-\xi)\frac{\mechanical{k}_\mu\mechanical{k}_\nu}{\mechanical{k}^2}\right].
$$
Feynman gauge, $\xi=1$, gives
$$
\signal{D}_{\mu\nu}^{ab}
=\frac{-i\delta^{ab}g_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^2+i0}.
$$
This form is manifestly covariant but contains unphysical polarization components. Landau gauge, $\xi=0$, gives the transverse projector:
$$
\signal{D}_{\mu\nu}^{ab}
=\frac{-i\delta^{ab}}{\mechanical{k}^2+i0}
\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\mechanical{k}_\mu\mechanical{k}_\nu}{\mechanical{k}^2}\right).
$$

#### (b) Landau-gauge contraction

For nonzero $\mechanical{k}^2$, the numerator satisfies
$$
\mechanical{k}^\mu\signal{D}_{\mu\nu}^{ab}
=\frac{-i\delta^{ab}}{\mechanical{k}^2+i0}
\left(\mechanical{k}_\nu
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^2\mechanical{k}_\nu}{\mechanical{k}^2}\right)=0.
$$
The singular mass-shell expression requires the consistent propagator prescription.

#### (c) Self-energy and gauge dependence

The full polarization tensor is transverse:
$$
\signal{\Pi}_{ab}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=(\mechanical{k}^2g^{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu)
\delta_{ab}\signal{\Pi}(\mechanical{k}^2,\xi).
$$
Ghost and gauge contributions must be combined to satisfy the Slavnov–Taylor identities.

> [!note] Off-shell gauge dependence
> The supplied solution claims $\partial\signal{\Pi}/\partial\xi=0$ because physical observables are gauge independent. This is incorrect for the ordinary off-shell gluon two-point function. Its transverse scalar coefficient can depend on the gauge parameter. The Slavnov–Taylor identities constrain gauge-dependent Green functions; they do not make each such function an observable. Gauge dependence cancels in suitable physical quantities after all required contributions are combined.

### Solution 12.12 — Symmetry of the four-gluon vertex

With all external legs in the same momentum convention, the vertex is
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{4,abcd}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
=-i\signal{g}^2\big[&
f^{abe}f^{cde}(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})\\
&+f^{ace}f^{bde}(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})\\
&+f^{ade}f^{bce}(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma})
\big].
\end{aligned}
$$
Check the exchange of complete external labels $(a,\mu)\leftrightarrow(b,\nu)$.

In the first term, $f^{abe}\mapsto-f^{abe}$ and
$$
g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}
\mapsto
g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma}-g^{\nu\sigma}g^{\mu\rho},
$$
which is the negative of the original tensor. These signs cancel. The second and third terms exchange places. Thus the full vertex is invariant.

The exchange $(c,\rho)\leftrightarrow(d,\sigma)$ works in the same way. Complete external-leg permutations leave the vertex unchanged, as required by Bose symmetry. For this momentum-independent vertex, the paired labels are the color and Lorentz indices; a general momentum-dependent vertex also requires exchange of the associated momenta.
