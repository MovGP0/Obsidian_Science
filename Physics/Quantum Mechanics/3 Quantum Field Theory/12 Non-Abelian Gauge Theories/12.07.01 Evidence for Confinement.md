> [!TLDR]
> Strong infrared coupling and the approximately linear static quark potential support confinement. Perturbation theory fails at hadronic scales.

## Growth of the coupling

The preceding one-loop expression gives
$\signal{\alpha}_s(\energy{\mu})\to\infty$ as
$\energy{\mu}\to\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ from above.
At low energies, perturbation theory is not useful and cannot calculate the hadron spectrum.

## Confining potential

Lattice QCD calculations show an approximately linear static quark–antiquark potential at large separation in the confining regime:
$$
\energy{V}(\mechanical{r})
\approx-\frac{C_F\signal{\alpha}_s}{\mechanical{r}}
+\energy{\sigma}\mechanical{r},
\qquad \mechanical{r}\to\infty.
$$
The source gives the **string tension**
$$
\energy{\sigma}\approx(420\,\unit{MeV})^2
\approx0.18\,\unit{GeV^2}.
$$
The term $-C_F\signal{\alpha}_s/\mechanical{r}$ is the color Coulomb potential and dominates at short distances.
The term $\energy{\sigma}\mechanical{r}$ is the confining contribution at large distances.

> [!note]
> The one-loop infrared divergence signals the failure of perturbation theory; it does not determine a physical infinite coupling. The linearly rising potential at arbitrarily large distance is the idealized pure-gauge or quenched result. With dynamical light quarks, pair creation can break the string, so the asymptotic static potential does not rise without limit.

## Color string

Chromodynamic field lines between a quark and an antiquark concentrate in a thin **color string**, or **flux tube**, rather than spreading as electromagnetic field lines do in QED.
The tube's energy grows linearly with its length.
When enough energy is supplied to separate the original quarks, the vacuum can produce a new quark–antiquark pair.
This is part of **hadronization**: isolated color charges do not emerge.

## Lattice QCD

Wilson introduced the lattice formulation of QCD in 1974.
It permits numerical calculations of vacuum properties and the hadron spectrum.
The source states that lattice calculations confirm confinement and predict hadron masses in close agreement with experiment.
