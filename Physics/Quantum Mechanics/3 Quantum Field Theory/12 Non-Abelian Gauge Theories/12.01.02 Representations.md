---
title: "Representations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A representation assigns a linear transformation to each group element while preserving the group multiplication law.

A **representation** of $G$ is a homomorphism
$$
\rho:G\to GL(V).
$$
It assigns an invertible linear transformation on a vector space $V$ to each group element.
The following representations are important in gauge theories.

## Fundamental representation

For $SU(N)$, the fundamental representation has dimension $N$.
Its generators $T^a$ are Hermitian, traceless $N\times N$ matrices.
Their normalization is
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(T^aT^b)=T_F\delta^{ab},
\qquad T_F=\frac{1}{2}.
\tag{12.2}
$$
QCD quarks transform in the fundamental representation of $SU(3)$.

## Adjoint representation

The adjoint representation has dimension $\dim G$.
Its generators are given by the structure constants:
$$
(T_{\mathrm{adj}}^a)_{bc}=-if^{abc}.
$$
The Jacobi identity ensures that these matrices obey algebra (12.1).
QCD gluons transform in the adjoint representation of $SU(3)$.

The same abstract generator has different matrices in different representations. In QCD, a fundamental matrix acts on a three-component quark, whereas an adjoint matrix acts on eight color components. These are not competing gauge groups. The inhomogeneous derivative term in a local connection transformation must still be included; saying that gluons carry adjoint color does not make the connection transform homogeneously under a local transformation.

A useful normalization check comes from taking the trace of the summed Casimir relation below. In the fundamental representation the left side is the number of generators times the trace normalization; the right side is the representation dimension times its Casimir. With the stated normalization this gives the quoted fundamental Casimir. Changing the generator normalization changes these numbers and requires a corresponding coupling convention.

## Quadratic Casimir

For a representation $r$, the source defines the quadratic Casimir by
$$
T^aT^a=C_2(r)\,\mathbf{1},
$$
with a sum over all generators.
For the fundamental representation of $SU(N)$,
$$
C_F\equiv C_2(F)=\frac{N^2-1}{2N}.
$$
For the adjoint representation,
$$
C_A\equiv C_2(A)=N.
$$
Thus, for $SU(3)$,
$$
C_F=\frac{4}{3},
\qquad C_A=3.
$$

> [!note]
> The Casimir operator is proportional to a single identity matrix on an irreducible representation. A reducible representation can have different Casimir eigenvalues on its irreducible components.
