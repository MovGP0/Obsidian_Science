---
title: "Transformation of Gauge Fields"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The non-Abelian gauge field transforms with an inhomogeneous derivative term and a commutator term. This makes the matter covariant derivative transform covariantly.

Require the matter derivative to transform covariantly:
$$
D_\mu\signal{\psi}\to U(\mechanical{x})(D_\mu\signal{\psi}).
$$
Then the gauge field must transform as
$$
\signal{A}_\mu\to
U(\mechanical{x})\signal{A}_\mu U^\dagger(\mechanical{x})
+\frac{i}{\signal{g}}U(\mechanical{x})\partial_\mu U^\dagger(\mechanical{x}).
\tag{12.3}
$$
For an infinitesimal transformation
$$
U(\mechanical{x})=1+i\alpha^a(\mechanical{x})T^a+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2),
$$
this becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{A}_\mu^a\to
\signal{A}_\mu^a+\frac{1}{\signal{g}}\partial_\mu\alpha^a
+f^{abc}\signal{A}_\mu^b\alpha^c
=\signal{A}_\mu^a+\frac{1}{\signal{g}}(D_\mu^{\mathrm{adj}}\alpha)^a.
\end{aligned}
$$
The adjoint covariant derivative is defined here by
$$
(D_\mu^{\mathrm{adj}}\alpha)^a
=\partial_\mu\alpha^a
+\signal{g}f^{abc}\signal{A}_\mu^b\alpha^c.
$$
Unlike QED, the transformation contains a term proportional to the gauge field itself.
This commutator term is associated with gauge-boson self-interactions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Matter spinor | The field whose covariant derivative transforms with U. |
| $\signal{A}_\mu,\signal{A}_\mu^a$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Gauge-field matrix and components | The non-Abelian connection in matrix and component notation. |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Gauge coupling | The coupling in the field transformation and covariant derivative. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinate | The position at which the local transformation acts. |
