---
title: "Theoretical Criteria for Confinement"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Wilson-loop area behavior is a criterion for confinement of static color sources in suitable gauge theories. Flux-tube formation can also be described through proposed monopole-condensation mechanisms.

## Wilson criterion

A confinement diagnostic is the **Wilson loop**:
$$
\signal{W}(C)=\frac{1}{N_c}\operatorname{Tr}\,
\mathcal{P}\exp\left(
i\signal{g}\oint_C\signal{A}_\mu\,d\mechanical{x}^\mu
\right).
$$
Here $\mathcal{P}$ denotes path ordering, and $C$ is a closed contour in spacetime.
For a rectangular contour with side lengths $\mechanical{R}$ and $\mechanical{T}$:

- **Area law:** If
  $$
  \langle\signal{W}(C)\rangle\sim e^{-\energy{\sigma}\mechanical{A}(C)},
  \qquad \mechanical{A}(C)=\mechanical{R}\mechanical{T},
  $$
  the theory is in a confining phase.
- **Perimeter law:** The source associates
  $$
  \langle\signal{W}(C)\rangle\sim e^{-\energy{\kappa}\mechanical{P}(C)},
  \qquad \mechanical{P}(C)=2(\mechanical{R}+\mechanical{T}),
  $$
  with a Higgs or Coulomb phase, rather than confinement.

> [!note]
> The decaying exponentials refer to Euclidean expectation values. The area-law criterion has its simplest form in a pure gauge theory with unscreened external fundamental sources. Dynamical matter can break the string and change the asymptotic Wilson-loop behavior. A perimeter term alone does not distinguish a Higgs phase from a Coulomb phase; Coulomb interactions can also give contour-shape-dependent terms.

## Monopole-condensation mechanism

A frequently discussed scenario is associated with Nambu, ’t Hooft, and Mandelstam.
It interprets confinement through monopole condensation.

The scenario is analogous to type-II superconductivity.
Abrikosov vortices confine magnetic flux in a superconductor.
In the proposed QCD picture, color monopoles condense in the vacuum and confine chromodynamic flux into stringlike tubes.
This is an electric–magnetic duality picture.

## Millennium Prize problem

The source connects confinement to the Clay Mathematics Institute's Yang–Mills Millennium Prize problem.
It describes the task as a rigorous construction of four-dimensional Yang–Mills theory with a positive mass gap and states that the problem remains open.

> [!note]
> The precise existence-and-mass-gap problem is distinct from a proof of every aspect of confinement. A mass gap and confinement are related physical questions, but the terms are not interchangeable. The monopole-condensation account above is a proposed confinement mechanism.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{W}(C)$ | $1$ | Wilson loop | The normalized trace of parallel transport around a closed contour. |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Gauge coupling | The coupling in the Wilson-loop exponent. |
| $\signal{A}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Gauge-field matrix | The non-Abelian connection along the contour. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Contour coordinate | The spacetime position along the closed path. |
| $\mechanical{R},\mechanical{T}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Rectangle side lengths | The spatial separation and Euclidean time extent in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{A}(C)$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Contour area | The area enclosed by the rectangular loop. |
| $\mechanical{P}(C)$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Contour perimeter | The boundary length of the rectangular loop. |
| $\energy{\sigma}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | String tension | The coefficient of the area term. |
| $\energy{\kappa}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Perimeter coefficient | The coefficient in the source's perimeter-law expression. |
