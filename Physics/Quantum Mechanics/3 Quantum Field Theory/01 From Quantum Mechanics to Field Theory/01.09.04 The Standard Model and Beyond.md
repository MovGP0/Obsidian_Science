---
title: "The Standard Model and Beyond"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Standard Model describes the electromagnetic, weak, and strong interactions with a product gauge group.

The Standard Model is the field theory of the fundamental interactions other than gravity. Its gauge group is

$$
G_{\mathrm{SM}}=SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y. \tag{1.73}
$$

The Higgs mechanism spontaneously breaks this group to $SU(3)_C\times U(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$. The Higgs particle was discovered at CERN in 2012, completing the experimental particle content of the Standard Model.

Despite its experimental success, the Standard Model leaves major questions open: unification with gravity, the nature of dark matter and dark energy, and the matter–antimatter asymmetry of the universe. The source names supersymmetry, string theory, and loop quantum gravity as approaches in the search for a more fundamental theory. Quantum field theory supplies the theoretical framework for subnuclear physics.
