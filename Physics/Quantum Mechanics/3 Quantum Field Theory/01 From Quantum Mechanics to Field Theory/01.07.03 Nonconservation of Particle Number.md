---
title: "Nonconservation of Particle Number"
---

> [!TLDR]
> High-energy processes can create many particles, so a theory with a fixed particle number is insufficient.

At sufficient energy, particle number need not be conserved. A high-energy collision between two protons can produce dozens or hundreds of particles. A Schrödinger equation with a fixed number of particles cannot describe this behavior.

This does not mean every collision changes particle number. The relevant interactions, conserved charges, and available center-of-momentum energy determine the allowed channels. Charge and four-momentum can remain conserved while the number of excitations changes. A boost that raises an individual particle's energy cannot open a forbidden channel by itself.

Particle-number conservation can be an approximate rule in a low-energy sector when all number-changing channels under consideration are energetically or dynamically suppressed. It is not a universal consequence of small speeds; even low-energy systems can emit massless quanta. The relativistic equations, Klein–Gordon and Dirac, must be incorporated into a description that permits particle number to change with time. The next section develops that field-based description.
