---
title: "The Klein Regime"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Above the Klein threshold, the retained momentum choice gives signed current coefficients; their interpretation depends on the incoming and outgoing boundary conditions.

The potential-step problem has three regimes as the step energy $\energy{V}_0$ changes.

## Case 1 — Ordinary Transmission

For $\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}$, the momentum $\mechanical{k}_2=\sqrt{(\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0)^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}}$ is real and positive. There is an ordinary transmitted flux with $0<\signal{T}<1$ for a nonzero step in this range. Partial reflection is a wave effect; a classical particle above this step would not split into reflected and transmitted parts.

## Case 2 — Evanescent Wave

For $\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}<\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}$, one has $(\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0)^{2}<\mechanical{m}^{2}$. Thus $\mechanical{k}_2$ is imaginary. The wave in Region II is evanescent, and reflection is total. The wave penetrates a finite distance and decays with position, but it carries no transmitted asymptotic flux into the semi-infinite Region II. This is total reflection with an evanescent tail. A barrier of finite width would be a different problem and can permit tunneling.

## Case 3 — The Klein Paradox

For $\energy{V}_0>\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}$, one has $\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0<-\mechanical{m}$. Thus $(\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0)^{2}>\mechanical{m}^{2}$, and $\mechanical{k}_2$ is real again: the particle penetrates the barrier.

Since $\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0<-\mechanical{m}<0$, the ratio $q_2=\mechanical{k}_2/(\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0+\mechanical{m})$ is negative. With $q_1>0$ and $q_2<0$, their sum can be small or negative. The current coefficients then satisfy

$$
\signal{R}>1,\qquad\signal{T}<0. \tag{1.62}
$$

A reflection coefficient above one and a negative transmission coefficient are unacceptable as ordinary single-particle probabilities. They are nevertheless consistent signed flux coefficients for the source's chosen mode.

> [!note]
> Positive right-hand momentum does not mean rightward propagation here. Differentiating the negative shifted-energy dispersion gives a group velocity with the sign of momentum divided by the negative shifted energy, so this mode carries flux toward the step from the right. The source's coefficients must not be identified with ordinary one-particle outgoing scattering probabilities. Choosing the other momentum sign changes the boundary condition. This distinction is treated in [Calogeracos and Dombey, sections 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9905076).

Quantum field theory resolves the paradox. A field description can include pair creation by an external potential and the associated particle and antiparticle channels. The branch overlap identifies available channels; a production rate also needs a specified vacuum, preparation, and incoming/outgoing conditions. The external source supplies energy for production. The algebra above alone does not establish a pair-production rate or uniquely identify an outgoing antiparticle beam.

## Example 1.3 — Numerical Calculation of the Klein Paradox

Choose mass units with $\mechanical{m}=1$, and set $\energy{E}=1.5\mechanical{m}$ and $\energy{V}_0=4\mechanical{m}$. This lies in the Klein regime because $\energy{V}_0>\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}=2.5\mechanical{m}$.

The momenta are

$$
\mechanical{k}_1=\sqrt{\energy{E}^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}}
=\sqrt{2.25-1}\,\mechanical{m}=\sqrt{1.25}\,\mechanical{m}
\approx1.118\mechanical{m},
$$

$$
\mechanical{k}_2=\sqrt{(\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0)^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}}
=\sqrt{(-2.5\mechanical{m})^{2}-\mechanical{m}^{2}}
=\sqrt{5.25}\,\mechanical{m}\approx2.291\mechanical{m}.
$$

The spinor ratios are

$$
q_1=\frac{\mechanical{k}_1}{\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}}
=\frac{1.118}{2.5}\approx0.447,
$$

$$
q_2=\frac{\mechanical{k}_2}{\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0+\mechanical{m}}
=\frac{2.291}{-1.5}\approx-1.527.
$$

The current coefficients become

$$
\signal{R}=\left(\frac{0.447-(-1.527)}{0.447+(-1.527)}\right)^{2}
=\left(\frac{1.974}{-1.080}\right)^{2}\approx3.34,
$$

$$
\signal{T}=\frac{4\times0.447\times(-1.527)}{(0.447+(-1.527))^{2}}
=\frac{-2.730}{1.166}\approx-2.34.
$$

The check gives $\signal{R}+\signal{T}=3.34-2.34=1$. Reflection exceeds one for the retained signed-flux convention. The numerical example checks the current identity, not a probability greater than one or experimental pair-production data. Reversing the right-hand momentum would change the signs of its group current and q ratio; explain that boundary choice before reusing these numbers.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{V}_0$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Step energy | The barrier height that selects the scattering regime. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Incident energy | The positive energy of the incident particle. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The particle rest mass, used as the example's energy scale. |
| $\mechanical{k}_2$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Transmitted momentum | The real or imaginary momentum in Region II. |
| $\mechanical{k}_1$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Incident momentum | The momentum in Region I. |
| $\signal{R}$, $\signal{T}$ | $1$ | Current coefficients | The reflected and transmitted currents relative to the incident current. |
