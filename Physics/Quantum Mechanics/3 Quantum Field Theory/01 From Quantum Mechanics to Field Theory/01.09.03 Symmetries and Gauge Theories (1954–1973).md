---
title: "Symmetries and Gauge Theories (1954–1973)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Yang and Mills extended electromagnetic gauge invariance to non-Abelian groups, creating the framework used by the Standard Model.

In 1954, Yang and Mills generalized electromagnetic gauge invariance from the Abelian group $U(1)$ to non-Abelian symmetry groups. Yang–Mills theories based on groups such as $SU(2)$ and $SU(3)$ form the basis of the Standard Model.

These theories initially appeared to describe massless gauge bosons, in apparent conflict with the short range of the weak interaction. The strong interaction is different: the QCD Lagrangian has no explicit gluon mass term. The absence of isolated colored states and the finite range of residual nuclear forces cannot be explained by assigning a weak-boson-like mass to each gluon. They involve nonperturbative QCD and color-neutral composite states.

Spontaneous symmetry breaking supplied the electroweak solution. Contributions came from Nambu (1960), Goldstone (1961), Anderson (1963), and Higgs, Brout–Englert, and Guralnik–Hagen–Kibble (1964). The mechanism allows gauge bosons to acquire mass in a gauge-theory framework that retains renormalizability. In the electroweak model the scalar kinetic term generates masses for the broken combinations, while the unbroken electromagnetic combination remains massless. This separates a mass-generating model from an arbitrary vector mass term.

Building on Glashow's work from 1961, Weinberg (1967) and Salam (1968) developed the unified electroweak theory. Its gauge group $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ is spontaneously broken to $U(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$. The predicted $W^{\pm}$ and $Z^{0}$ boson masses were about $80\,\unit{GeV}$ and $91\,\unit{GeV}$, respectively. The source associates their 1983 discovery at CERN with Rubbia and van der Meer.

In 1973, Fritzsch, Gell-Mann, and Leutwyler formulated quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the strong-interaction theory based on $SU(3)_C$, with gluons as gauge bosons. Gross, Politzer, and Wilczek discovered asymptotic freedom, work recognized by the 2004 Nobel Prize. It makes the interaction weaker at large momentum transfer and justifies perturbative calculations there.

> [!note]
> The source joins asymptotic freedom and confinement in one explanatory statement. The perturbative decrease of the coupling at short distances does not by itself prove confinement at long distances. Extrapolating a weak-coupling expansion into the strong-coupling regime is not a controlled derivation.

A useful prediction check is that the unbroken electromagnetic field remains massless while the weak vector modes are massive. The quoted boson masses and discovery dates remain source benchmarks; the group structure alone does not determine their numerical masses without model parameters.
