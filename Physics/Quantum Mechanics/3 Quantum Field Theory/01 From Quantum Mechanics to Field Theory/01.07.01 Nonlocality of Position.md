---
title: "Nonlocality of Position"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Localization on the Compton scale requires enough energy for particle creation, which limits a single-particle position description.

In nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, the position operator $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf x}}$ is well defined and Hermitian.

The relativistic situation is more subtle. Referring to the Newton–Wigner theorem, the source describes a Hermitian position operator for massive particles. It states that the wavefunction of a particle localized at $\mechanical{\mathbf x}_0$ extends over a region of order $1/\mechanical{m}$. This is the reduced Compton wavelength,

$$
\signal{\bar\lambda}_{\mathrm C}
=\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}
=\frac{1}{\mechanical{m}}
\quad\text{in natural units}.
$$

> [!note]
> The source also calls the Newton–Wigner position operator covariant. That qualification needs a separate review; it is recorded here as a source claim.

Position measurements with precision better than $\signal{\bar\lambda}_{\mathrm C}$ require energies above the scale $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^{2}$. New particles can then be created. A single particle no longer provides a well-localized object for the measurement.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf x}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Position | The position observable, expressed in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf x}_0$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Localization position | The nominal center of the localized state. |
| $\signal{\bar\lambda}_{\mathrm C}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Reduced Compton wavelength | The characteristic localization length in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The particle mass that sets the localization scale. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action constant in the SI expression for the wavelength. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | The constant in the SI wavelength and rest-energy expressions. |
