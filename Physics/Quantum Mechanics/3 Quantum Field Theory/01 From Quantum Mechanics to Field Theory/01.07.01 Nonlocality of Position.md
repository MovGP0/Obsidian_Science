---
title: "Nonlocality of Position"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Compton-scale localization exceeds a purely nonrelativistic approximation; particle production depends on the complete interacting process.

In nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, the position operator $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf x}}$ is well defined and Hermitian.

The relativistic situation is more subtle. Referring to the Newton–Wigner theorem, the source describes a Hermitian position operator for massive particles. It associates a state centered at $\mechanical{\mathbf x}_0$ with a length scale of order $1/\mechanical{m}$. This scale is the reduced Compton wavelength,

$$
\signal{\bar\lambda}_{\mathrm C}
=\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}}
=\frac{1}{\mechanical{m}}
\quad\text{in natural units}.
$$

> [!note]
> The source calls the Newton–Wigner position operator covariant and describes an unavoidable wavefunction width. These claims need qualification: Newton–Wigner localization permits ideal point-localized generalized states in a chosen frame; it is not a Lorentz-covariant local position four-vector. A Compton-scale profile in another field representation must not be identified with a minimum width in the Newton–Wigner representation. See [Newton and Wigner, original construction](https://journals.aps.org/rmp/abstract/10.1103/RevModPhys.21.400) and the explicit distinction in [Gallardo, Kálnay, and Risemberg](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.158.1484).

To resolve distances shorter than this scale with a scattering probe, the uncertainty estimate requires momentum transfer of order the mass or larger in natural units. A purely nonrelativistic treatment then fails. In a local interacting theory, particle production can become accessible if the complete process has enough invariant energy and satisfies its conservation laws; localization alone does not compel a pair in every measurement.

As a scale check, increasing the mass shortens the Compton wavelength. The formula is not a massless localization theorem: its divergence when mass tends to zero shows that this particular massive-particle estimate no longer supplies a finite length.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf x}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Position | The position observable, expressed in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf x}_0$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Localization position | The nominal center of the localized state. |
| $\signal{\bar\lambda}_{\mathrm C}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Reduced Compton wavelength | The characteristic localization length in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The particle mass that sets the localization scale. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action constant in the SI expression for the wavelength. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | The constant in the SI wavelength and rest-energy expressions. |
