---
title: "Lagrangian Density and Action"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Stationarity of the spacetime integral of the Lagrangian density gives the Euler–Lagrange field equation.

In field theory, the Lagrangian density $\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi},\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})$ takes the role of the mechanical Lagrangian. It depends on the field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$ and its derivatives. The action is

$$
\energy{S}=\int d^{4}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi},\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}), \tag{1.74}
$$

where the integral covers all spacetime or a suitable region. The condition $\delta\energy{S}=0$, with variations that vanish at the boundary, gives

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\phi}}
-\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})}=0. \tag{1.75}
$$

For the real scalar field, the Lagrangian density is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})
-\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}. \tag{1.76}
$$

Substitution into (1.75) gives the Klein–Gordon equation (1.11). For the Dirac field,

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=\overline{\signal{\psi}}(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}, \tag{1.77}
$$

the Euler–Lagrange equations give the Dirac equation (1.32) and its adjoint.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Lagrangian density | The field's local dynamical density in natural units. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The spacetime integral of the Lagrangian density, in units of the reduced Planck constant. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Real scalar field | The canonically normalized field varied in the action. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime position | The integration coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The rest-mass parameter in each free-field equation. |
| $\signal{\psi}$, $\overline{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac field | The fermion field and its adjoint in the Dirac Lagrangian. |
