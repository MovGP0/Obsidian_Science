---
title: "The Quantized Dirac Field"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Anticommutation relations enforce Pauli exclusion and reinterpret the classical negative-energy branch as positive-energy antiparticle creation.

The Dirac field is quantized in an analogous way to the scalar field, with a crucial difference associated with Pauli exclusion.

The fermionic operators must satisfy anticommutation relations. The source relates this requirement to the commutation of observables at spacelike separation. Fermion fields themselves obey the corresponding spacelike anticommutation rule; local physical observables made from an even number of fermion fields can commute. The spin–statistics connection also uses positive energy and the relativistic local field structure, not exclusion alone. In the convention used below,

$$
\{\hat a^{(r)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}),\hat a^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf q})\}
=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q})\delta^{rs},
$$

$$
\{\hat b^{(r)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}),\hat b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf q})\}
=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q})\delta^{rs}.
$$

All other anticommutators vanish. The quantized Dirac field is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\hat\psi}(\mechanical{x})
=\sum_{s=1}^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}}}
\bigl[&\hat a^{(s)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\signal{u}^{(s)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\\
&+\hat b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\signal{v}^{(s)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\bigr].
\end{aligned} \tag{1.71}
$$

Anticommutation prevents the creation of two identical fermions in the same state. It therefore implements the Pauli principle and removes the ad hoc need for a Dirac sea. The negative-energy solutions of the classical Dirac equation become coefficients of creation operators for positive-energy positrons. The vacuum is the quantum-field-theory replacement for the sea.

For one normalized discrete mode, its creation operator anticommutes with itself. Its square therefore vanishes, so a second identical fermion cannot be added to that mode. A fermion in another momentum or spin mode is allowed. This checks the content of exclusion without imposing it on distinguishable mode labels.

Particles are excitation quanta of the field. They are excited states of the quantized classical field, rather than the starting objects of the theory.

> [!note]
> The source-normalization question is resolved: (1.71) is consistent. The spinor norm of twice the energy cancels the square-root factors; the mode anticommutators have no extra energy factor. This differs from the scalar convention (1.65). Compare [Tong, sections 5.1–5.2](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qft.pdf).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\hat\psi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Quantized Dirac field | The canonically normalized operator-valued fermion field. |
| $\signal{u}$, $\signal{v}$ | $\unit{MeV^{1/2}}$ | Mode spinors | The spinors with adjoint norms equal to plus or minus twice the mass. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, $\mechanical{\mathbf q}$, $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The three-momentum labels and four-momentum in the phase. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime position | The event at which the Dirac field is evaluated. |
| $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode energy | The positive on-shell energy in the mode normalization. |
