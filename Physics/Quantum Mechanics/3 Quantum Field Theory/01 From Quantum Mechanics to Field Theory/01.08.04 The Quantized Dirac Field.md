---
title: "The Quantized Dirac Field"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Anticommutation relations enforce Pauli exclusion and reinterpret the classical negative-energy branch as positive-energy antiparticle creation.

The Dirac field is quantized in an analogous way to the scalar field, with a crucial difference associated with Pauli exclusion.

The fermionic operators must satisfy anticommutation relations. The source relates this requirement to the commutation of observables at spacelike separation:

$$
\{\hat a^{(r)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}),\hat a^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf q})\}
=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q})\delta^{rs},
$$

$$
\{\hat b^{(r)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}),\hat b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf q})\}
=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q})\delta^{rs}.
$$

All other anticommutators vanish. The quantized Dirac field is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\hat\psi}(\mechanical{x})
=\sum_{s=1}^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}}}
\bigl[&\hat a^{(s)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\signal{u}^{(s)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\\
&+\hat b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\signal{v}^{(s)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\bigr].
\end{aligned} \tag{1.71}
$$

Anticommutation prevents the creation of two identical fermions in the same state. It therefore implements the Pauli principle and removes the ad hoc need for a Dirac sea. The negative-energy solutions of the classical Dirac equation become coefficients of creation operators for positive-energy positrons. The vacuum is the quantum-field-theory replacement for the sea.

Particles are excitation quanta of the field. They are excited states of the quantized classical field, rather than the starting objects of the theory.

> [!note]
> Equation (1.71) retains the source's field-expansion normalization. Its consistency with the covariant spinor normalization in section 1.5.4 needs review.
