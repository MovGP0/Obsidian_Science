---
title: "Fock Space"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Fock space combines all fixed-particle-number Hilbert spaces into one state space.

For the free particles considered here, Fock space lets one state describe zero, one, or several identical excitations. It is built from symmetrized tensor products of the one-particle space for bosons, or antisymmetrized products for fermions. The zero-particle sector contains the vacuum; it is not the zero vector.

The free-particle state space is

$$
\mathcal F=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal H_n, \tag{1.67}
$$

where $\mathcal H_n$ contains states with exactly $n$ particles. Single-particle quantum mechanics restricts the description to $\mathcal H_1$. A general Fock vector can have components in several sectors. A measurement of number returns an integer, with probabilities set by the norms of these components. The sum in (1.67) is a direct sum of state spaces, not a claim that every state has infinitely many particles. The construction is the free-field and scattering starting point; it is not a general proof that every interacting or curved-spacetime theory admits one global particle basis.

The particle-number operator is

$$
\hat N=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}}
\hat a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\hat a(\mechanical{\mathbf p}). \tag{1.68}
$$

In the free theory, $[\hat N,\energy{\hat H}_0]=0$, so particle number is conserved. With interactions, $[\hat N,\energy{\hat H}_{\mathrm{int}}]\ne0$ can occur. Particle number can then change, as required to describe high-energy processes. An interaction need not change number: inspect its commutator rather than infer this from the word interaction.

Commuting (1.68) through one creation operator gives one extra creation operator, with the factor of twice the mode energy canceled by the invariant integration measure. Thus a state made by n creation operators has number eigenvalue n. This checks the normalization and the counting interpretation together.

> [!note]
> Equation (1.68) preserves the source's k/p notation for the integration measure and mode labels.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $n$ | $1$ | Particle number | The number of particles in a Fock-space sector. |
| $\hat N$ | $1$ | Number observable | The observable that counts particles. |
| $\mechanical{k}$, $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The source integration variable and mode momentum. |
| $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode energy | The positive energy in the invariant measure. |
| $\energy{\hat H}_0$, $\energy{\hat H}_{\mathrm{int}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Hamiltonians | The free energy and interaction energy observables. |
