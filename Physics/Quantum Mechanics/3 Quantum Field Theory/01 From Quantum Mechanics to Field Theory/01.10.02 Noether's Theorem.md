---
title: "Noether's Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A continuous symmetry of the action gives a conserved current and a corresponding conserved quantity.

Noether's theorem (1918) connects each continuous symmetry of a physical system with a conservation law. Suppose the Lagrangian density is invariant up to a total divergence under $\signal{\phi}\to\signal{\phi}+\varepsilon\delta\signal{\phi}$:

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}\to\energy{\mathcal L}+\varepsilon\partial_{\mu}K^{\mu}. \tag{1.78}
$$

For the displayed fixed-coordinate field variation, using the Euler–Lagrange equations converts the change of the Lagrangian into a divergence. Comparing it with (1.78) gives the current below. Its conservation holds on solutions, not for arbitrary field histories. For spacetime transformations the coordinate shift must also be included, which leads to the translation tensor later in this note.

Then the conserved Noether current is

$$
j^{\mu}=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})}\delta\signal{\phi}-K^{\mu},
\qquad\partial_{\mu}j^{\mu}=0. \tag{1.79}
$$

The source writes the associated conserved quantity as

$$
Q=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,j^{0}.
$$

> [!note]
> The displayed source formula has the wrong integration measure for a local position-space density; no Fourier transform is specified. The physical charge is the spatial integral below. Conservation also requires zero net current flux through the boundary.
>
> $$
> Q=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,j^{0}(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x}).
> $$

Basic examples are:

- Time-translation invariance, $\mechanical{t}\to\mechanical{t}+\varepsilon$: conservation of energy, represented by the Hamiltonian.
- Space-translation invariance, $\mechanical{\mathbf x}\to\mechanical{\mathbf x}+\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$: conservation of momentum.
- Global phase invariance of a complex field, $\signal{\phi}\to e^{i\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\phi}$: conservation of charge.

Applying translation invariance to the real-scalar Lagrangian (1.76) gives the energy–momentum tensor:

$$
\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})}\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi}
-g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}, \tag{1.80}
$$

It satisfies $\partial_{\mu}\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}=0$. The source writes the conserved energy and momentum as

$$
\energy{P}^{0}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\energy{T}^{00},\qquad
\mechanical{P}^{i}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\energy{T}^{0i}.
$$

These retained source expressions have the same measure error. For the local tensor in (1.80), use

$$
\energy{P}^{0}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{T}^{00},\qquad
\mechanical{P}^{i}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{T}^{0i}.
$$

This repair has a dimensional check: the tensor has energy dimension four and spatial volume has dimension minus three, leaving dimension one for energy and momentum. Integrating it over momentum volume instead would give dimension seven. Noether's result concerns continuous symmetries of this action; it does not claim a current for every discrete symmetry or for a broken symmetry of an imposed external background.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Lagrangian density | The local dynamical density whose symmetry is examined. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The canonically normalized field in the symmetry transformation. |
| $j^{\mu}$ | — | Noether current | The conserved current; its unit depends on the symmetry. |
| $Q$ | — | Conserved quantity | The charge associated with the chosen continuous symmetry. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The integration variable printed in the source's charge formula. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time | The coordinate shifted by time translations. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Position | The coordinate shifted by spatial translations. |
| $\signal{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Phase angle | The constant phase in a global complex-field rotation. |
| $\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Energy–momentum tensor | The density and flux tensor associated with translations. |
| $\energy{P}^{0}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Total energy | The conserved time-translation charge. |
| $\mechanical{P}^{i}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Total momentum | The conserved space-translation charges. |
