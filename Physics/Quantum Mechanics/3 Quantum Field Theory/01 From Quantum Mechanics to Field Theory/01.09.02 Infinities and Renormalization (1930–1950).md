---
title: "Infinities and Renormalization (1930–1950)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> QED renormalization absorbs divergences into a finite set of physical parameters and gives precise predictions for observable corrections.

During the 1930s, physicists found infinities throughout quantum field theory. Calculations beyond tree level include corrections from virtual-particle loops, which produced divergent integrals.

For example, the electron-mass calculation at order $\electric{\alpha}=\electric{e}^{2}/(4\pi)\approx1/137$ gave an infinite result.

In 1930, Oppenheimer calculated atomic energy shifts due to interaction with the quantized electromagnetic field and obtained an infinite result. After the Shelter Island conference in 1947, Bethe performed the first renormalization calculation of the Lamb shift: the energy difference between the hydrogen states $2s_{1/2}$ and $2p_{1/2}$. His noncovariant subtraction argument gave close agreement with the measurements of Lamb and Retherford.

Schwinger, Feynman, and Tomonaga then independently developed covariant QED renormalization. Their work, recognized by the 1965 Nobel Prize, showed that QED divergences could be absorbed into a redefinition of a finite number of parameters, including electron mass and charge.

Renormalized QED gives very precise predictions. The electron anomalous magnetic moment is

$$
\magnetic{a}_e=\frac{\magnetic{g}_e-2}{2}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}-0.328\frac{\electric{\alpha}^{2}}{\pi^{2}}+\cdots
\approx0.00115965\ldots. \tag{1.72}
$$

The source reports agreement with experiment to more than ten significant figures.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The perturbative expansion parameter of QED. |
| $\electric{e}$ | $1$ | Charge coupling | The charge parameter in Heaviside–Lorentz natural units. |
| $\magnetic{g}_e$ | $1$ | Electron gyromagnetic factor | The factor whose departure from two defines the anomaly. |
| $\magnetic{a}_e$ | $1$ | Magnetic-moment anomaly | Half the difference between the electron gyromagnetic factor and two. |
