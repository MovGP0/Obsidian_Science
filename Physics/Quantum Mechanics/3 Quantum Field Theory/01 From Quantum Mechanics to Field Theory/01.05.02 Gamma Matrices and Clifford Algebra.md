---
title: "Gamma Matrices and Clifford Algebra"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Gamma matrices express the Dirac equation in covariant form and satisfy the Clifford anticommutation algebra.

Define the gamma matrices by

$$
\gamma^{0}=\beta,\qquad
\gamma^{i}=\beta\alpha^{i}=\gamma^{0}\alpha^{i}. \tag{1.31}
$$

Equation (1.26) then becomes

$$
\boxed{(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}=0,} \tag{1.32}
$$

or, with Feynman's slash notation $\not{p}=\gamma^{\mu}\mechanical{p}_{\mu}$,

$$
(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}=0. \tag{1.33}
$$

Relations (1.28)–(1.30) give the Clifford algebra:

$$
\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1_{4\times4}. \tag{1.34}
$$

This relation specifies the gamma-matrix algebra. The source describes the matrices as determined up to unitary transformations. Equation (1.34) immediately gives

$$
(\gamma^{0})^{2}=1,
$$

$$
(\gamma^{i})^{2}=-1,
$$

$$
\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}=-\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}
\qquad\text{for }\mu\ne\nu.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The rest mass in the covariant Dirac equation. |
| $\signal{\psi}$ | — | Dirac wavefunction | The four-component wavefunction. |
| $\mechanical{p}_{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momentum | The momentum contracted with gamma matrices in slash notation. |
