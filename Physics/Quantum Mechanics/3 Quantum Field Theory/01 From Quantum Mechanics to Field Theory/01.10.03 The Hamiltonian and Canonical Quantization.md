title: "The Hamiltonian and Canonical Quantization"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Conjugate momentum and the Hamiltonian density lead to equal-time commutators for bosons and anticommutators for fermions.

Pass from the Lagrangian to the Hamiltonian formalism by defining the momentum conjugate to the field:

$$
\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{x})
\equiv\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\dot\phi}}, \tag{1.81}
$$

and the Hamiltonian density:

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=\mechanical{\pi}\signal{\dot\phi}-\energy{\mathcal L}. \tag{1.82}
$$

For the real-scalar Lagrangian (1.76), $\mechanical{\pi}=\signal{\dot\phi}$, and

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{\pi}^{2}
+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\signal{\phi})^{2}
+\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}. \tag{1.83}
$$

Canonical quantization imposes equal-time commutation relations for bosons:

$$
[\signal{\hat\phi}(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x}),\mechanical{\hat\pi}(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf y})]
=i\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}). \tag{1.84}
$$

All other equal-time canonical commutators vanish. Fermions instead satisfy anticommutation relations:

$$
\{\signal{\hat\psi}_{\alpha}(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x}),
\signal{\hat\psi}_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf y})\}
=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}). \tag{1.85}
$$

The spin–statistics theorem connects the choice of commutators or anticommutators with particle spin. Later chapters discuss this theorem.

## Example 1.4 — Verifying the Klein–Gordon Equation from the Lagrangian

For the Lagrangian density (1.76),

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\phi}}=-\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi},\qquad
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})}=\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}. \tag{1.86}
$$

The Euler–Lagrange equation (1.75) gives

$$
-\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}-\partial_{\mu}(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\square\signal{\phi}+\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}=0, \tag{1.87}
$$

which is exactly the Klein–Gordon equation (1.11).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Lagrangian density | The starting density for the Legendre transformation. |
| $\energy{\mathcal H}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Hamiltonian density | The local energy density of the real scalar field. |
| $\signal{\phi}$, $\signal{\hat\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The classical and quantized scalar amplitude. |
| $\signal{\dot\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Field time derivative | The rate of change of the scalar field. |
| $\mechanical{\pi}$, $\mechanical{\hat\pi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Conjugate momentum density | The canonical momentum of the scalar field. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The scalar-particle rest mass. |
| $\mechanical{x}$, $\mechanical{\mathbf x}$, $\mechanical{\mathbf y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Positions | The spacetime argument and spatial equal-time coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time | The common time in the canonical relations. |
| $\signal{\hat\psi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac field | The fermionic field in the anticommutation relation. |
