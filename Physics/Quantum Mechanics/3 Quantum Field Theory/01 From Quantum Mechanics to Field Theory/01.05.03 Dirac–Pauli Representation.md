---
title: "Dirac–Pauli Representation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Dirac–Pauli representation constructs the gamma matrices from Pauli matrices and is useful in the nonrelativistic limit.

Gamma matrices are not unique. Every unitary transformation $\gamma^{\mu}\to U\gamma^{\mu}U^{-1}$ preserves the Clifford algebra. Common choices are the Dirac–Pauli representation, useful for nonrelativistic physics, and the Weyl or chiral representation, useful for ultrarelativistic physics.

In the Dirac–Pauli representation,

$$
\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma^{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{i}\\-\sigma^{i}&0\end{pmatrix}, \tag{1.35}
$$

where each block has dimension $2\times2$. The Pauli matrices are

$$
\sigma^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\sigma^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
\sigma^{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1.36}
$$

Dirac's original matrices are then

$$
\alpha^{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{i}\\\sigma^{i}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\beta=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1.37}
$$

It is also useful to define $\gamma^{5}$, also written $\gamma_5$:

$$
\gamma^{5}\equiv i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}
=-\frac{i}{4!}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}. \tag{1.38}
$$

This matrix anticommutes with every gamma matrix:

$$
\{\gamma^{5},\gamma^{\mu}\}=0,\qquad(\gamma^{5})^{2}=1. \tag{1.39}
$$

In the Dirac–Pauli representation,

$$
\gamma^{5}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{1.40}
$$

The role of $\gamma^{5}$ in parity and the weak interaction is discussed in the chapters on quantum electrodynamics and electroweak theory.
