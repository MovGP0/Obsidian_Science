---
title: "From Particles to Fields"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A classical field becomes an operator on Fock space, which contains states with arbitrary particle number.

The solution requires a change of viewpoint. Start from classical fields and quantize them, rather than starting from particles. This is the program of quantum field theory (QFT).

A classical field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$ is a function defined at every spacetime point. In QFT, it becomes an operator on Fock space, a Hilbert space that contains states with arbitrary particle number.

Consider a real classical scalar field. Its equation of motion is the Klein–Gordon equation. Apply the reality condition $\signal{\phi}=\signal{\phi}^{*}$ to (1.14). This requires $\signal{b}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})=\signal{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})$, giving

$$
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}}
\left[\signal{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
+\signal{a}^{*}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\right]. \tag{1.64}
$$

> [!note]
> The source retains the k/p integration-variable notation from equation (1.14).

In the quantum theory, the coefficients $\signal{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})$ and $\signal{a}^{*}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})$ become operators $\hat a(\mechanical{\mathbf p})$ and $\hat a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})$, with canonical commutation relation

$$
[\hat a(\mechanical{\mathbf p}),\hat a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}')]
=(2\pi)^{3}\,2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}\,\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf p}'). \tag{1.65}
$$

The operator $\hat a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})$ creates a particle of momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, and $\hat a(\mechanical{\mathbf p})$ annihilates it. The vacuum $|0\rangle$ satisfies $\hat a(\mechanical{\mathbf p})|0\rangle=0$ for every momentum. States with a definite particle number are

$$
|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2,\ldots,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_n\rangle
=\hat a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1)\hat a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2)\cdots
\hat a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}_n)|0\rangle. \tag{1.66}
$$

The factors $e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}$ no longer describe physical states of negative energy. Their coefficients are creation operators that add positive energy $+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}>0$. The vacuum is the minimum-energy state, and the excitation spectrum contains no negative energies.

The quantized field $\signal{\hat\phi}(\mechanical{x})$ is no longer a wavefunction. Acting on the vacuum, it creates a one-particle state associated with the field insertion at $\mechanical{x}$. Position amplitudes and probabilities must be obtained from suitable field-operator expressions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | — | Real scalar field | The classical field that will be quantized. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime position | The event at which the field is evaluated. |
| $\mechanical{p}$, $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The mode momentum and source integration variable. |
| $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode energy | The positive on-shell energy of a scalar mode. |
| $\signal{a}$, $\signal{b}$ | — | Mode amplitudes | The coefficients related by the real-field condition. |
| $n$ | $1$ | Particle number | The number of creation operators in the multiparticle state. |
