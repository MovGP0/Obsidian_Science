> [!TLDR]
> These exercises test natural-unit conversions, gamma-matrix algebra, and the relativistic field concepts developed in Chapter 1.

## Exercise 1.1 — Natural Units and Conversions

**(a)** Verify that, in natural units with $\energy{\hbar}=\mechanical{c}=1$, the electron mass $\mechanical{m}_e\approx0.511\,\unit{MeV}$ gives a reduced Compton wavelength $\signal{\bar\lambda}_{\mathrm C}=1/\mechanical{m}_e\approx386\,\unit{fm}$.

**(b)** The classical electron radius is $\mechanical{r}_e=\electric{\alpha}/\mechanical{m}_e$, where $\electric{\alpha}\approx1/137$. Calculate its value in $\unit{fm}$.

**(c)** Express the reduced Planck constant in units of $\unit{MeV\,fm}$ with the speed of light set to one. Show that $\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\approx197.3\,\unit{MeV\,fm}$.

**(d)** The cosmic microwave background temperature is $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm{CMB}}\approx2.73\,\unit{K}$. Express it as an energy in $\unit{eV}$, using $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}=8.617\times10^{-5}\,\unit{eV\,K^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 1.2 — Properties of Gamma Matrices

**(a)** Use $\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2g^{\mu\nu}$ to prove these contraction identities in four spacetime dimensions:

$$
\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{\mu}=4, \tag{1.88}
$$

$$
\gamma^{\mu}\not{a}\gamma_{\mu}=-2\not{a}, \tag{1.89}
$$

$$
\gamma^{\mu}\not{a}\not{b}\gamma_{\mu}=4a\cdot b, \tag{1.90}
$$

$$
\gamma^{\mu}\not{a}\not{b}\not{c}\gamma_{\mu}=-2\not{c}\not{b}\not{a}. \tag{1.91}
$$

**(b)** Show that $\operatorname{Tr}[\gamma^{\mu}]=0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}[\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}]=4g^{\mu\nu}$.

**(c)** Show that the trace of an odd number of gamma matrices is zero.

**(d)** Calculate $\operatorname{Tr}[\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\rho}\gamma^{\sigma}]$.

## Exercise 1.3 — Klein–Gordon Equation with Complex Mass?

Consider $(\square+\mechanical{\mu}^{2})\signal{\phi}=0$ with $\mechanical{\mu}^{2}<0$. The mass is imaginary: $\mechanical{\mu}^{2}=-\mechanical{\lambda}^{2}$, where $\mechanical{\lambda}>0$ is real.

**(a)** Find the plane-wave solutions. What happens to the dispersion relation?

**(b)** Show that modes with exponential growth in time exist. What does this mean for vacuum stability?

**(c)** This type of field, called a tachyonic field, occurs in the Higgs potential. Give a qualitative explanation of how a stabilizing potential $\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})$ resolves the instability.

## Exercise 1.4 — Noether Current for the Dirac Field

**(a)** The Dirac Lagrangian (1.77) is invariant under the global phase transformation $\signal{\psi}\to e^{i\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi}$ and $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\to e^{-i\signal{\alpha}}\overline{\signal{\psi}}$. Derive the conserved current with Noether's theorem and show that $\electric{j}^{\mu}=\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\signal{\psi}$.

**(b)** Verify explicitly that $\partial_{\mu}\electric{j}^{\mu}=0$, using the Dirac equations.

**(c)** Calculate the conserved charge, printed in the source as

$$
\electric{Q}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\electric{j}^{0},
$$

for the one-particle state $|\mechanical{\mathbf p},s\rangle=\hat a^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})|0\rangle$ and the one-antiparticle state $|\mechanical{\mathbf p},s\rangle=\hat b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})|0\rangle$.

> [!note]
> Part (c) retains the source's momentum-space charge measure. The same notation issue is recorded in [[01.10.02 Noether's Theorem]]. The source also uses the same ket label for the particle and antiparticle states.

## Exercise 1.5 — The Klein Paradox in Detail

Return to the calculation in section 1.6.

**(a)** Sketch the qualitative dependence of $\signal{R}$ and $\signal{T}$ on $\energy{V}_0/\mechanical{m}$ at fixed $\energy{E}=1.5\mechanical{m}$. Identify the three regions: $\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}$, $\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}<\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}$, and $\energy{V}_0>\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}$.

**(b)** Find the asymptotic expressions for $\signal{R}$ and $\signal{T}$ when $\energy{V}_0\gg\energy{E}$.

**(c)** Calculate the exact threshold $\energy{V}_0^{\mathrm{threshold}}$ above which the paradox occurs, for general $\energy{E}>\mechanical{m}$.

**(d)** Use pair creation to explain physically why the current in Region II is negative in the Klein regime.

## Exercise 1.6 — Energy–Momentum Tensor of the Scalar Field

**(a)** Derive $\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}$ for the real scalar field with Lagrangian (1.76), using (1.80).

**(b)** Calculate $\energy{T}^{00}$, $\energy{T}^{0i}$, and $\energy{T}^{ij}$ explicitly.

**(c)** Verify that $\energy{T}^{00}=\energy{\mathcal H}$.

**(d)** Show that

$$
\mechanical{P}^{\mu}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\energy{T}^{0\mu}
$$

is a conserved four-vector by calculating it for the classical field evaluated on the plane-wave solution (1.12).

> [!note]
> Part (d) preserves the source's momentum-space integration measure, as in Exercise 1.4 and [[01.10.02 Noether's Theorem]].

## Exercise 1.7 — Weyl Spinors and Chirality

For a massless theory, $\mechanical{m}=0$, the Dirac equation separates into two equations for two-component spinors.

**(a)** In the Weyl or chiral representation, the gamma matrices are

$$
\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma^{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{i}\\-\sigma^{i}&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Write the Dirac spinor as

$$
\signal{\psi}=\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\psi}_L\\\signal{\psi}_R\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Show that, for $\mechanical{m}=0$, the Dirac equation separates into

$$
i(\partial_{\mechanical{t}}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\nabla)\signal{\psi}_R=0,
$$

$$
i(\partial_{\mechanical{t}}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\nabla)\signal{\psi}_L=0.
$$

**(b)** Show that $\signal{\psi}_L$ and $\signal{\psi}_R$ are eigenstates of $\gamma^{5}$ with eigenvalues $-1$ and $+1$, respectively.

**(c)** For an ultrarelativistic neutrino with $\mechanical{m}_{\nu}\approx0$ and momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ along $\hat{\mathbf z}$, calculate the helicity $\mechanical{h}=\mechanical{\mathbf S}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}/|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|$ for $\signal{\psi}_L$ and $\signal{\psi}_R$.

**(d)** Explain why parity violation in the weak interaction is naturally built into a theory that couples only to left-chiral components.

## Exercise 1.8 — Proca Equation and the Massless Limit

A massive vector field, or Proca field, has Lagrangian density

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac{1}{4}\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{A}_{\mu}\signal{A}^{\mu},\qquad
\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\signal{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\signal{A}_{\mu}.
$$

**(a)** Derive the equations of motion for $\signal{A}^{\mu}$ with the Euler–Lagrange equations. Show that $\partial_{\mu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}+\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{A}^{\nu}=0$.

**(b)** Take the divergence of the result from (a) and derive the automatic Lorenz condition $\partial_{\mu}\signal{A}^{\mu}=0$, valid for $\mechanical{m}\ne0$. Why does this condition not follow automatically in the Maxwell case, $\mechanical{m}=0$?

**(c)** Use the Lorenz condition to show that each component of $\signal{A}^{\mu}$ satisfies $(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{A}^{\mu}=0$.

**(d)** Count the physical degrees of freedom. For $\mechanical{m}>0$, the vector field has three physical polarizations. For $\mechanical{m}=0$, the photon has only two. Explain this reduction using gauge symmetry.
