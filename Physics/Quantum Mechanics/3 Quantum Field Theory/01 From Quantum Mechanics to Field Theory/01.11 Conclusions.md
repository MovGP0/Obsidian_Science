> [!TLDR]
> A fundamental relativistic quantum theory must permit particle creation and annihilation; quantized fields provide that description.

The Klein–Gordon and Dirac equations supply consistent free relativistic dynamics, but a general local interaction theory must do more. Scalar current conservation does not by itself give a positive probability density. Dirac density positivity does not by itself bound the single-particle energy below. A strong step also requires a correct incoming/outgoing flux choice. These are distinct tests, not interchangeable contradictions.

Number-changing processes require states beyond a fixed-particle sector. Available invariant energy and conservation laws decide when such channels open; relativistic speed alone is not a sufficient production criterion. A fixed-number approximation remains useful when the relevant channels are suppressed.

Quantum field theory resolves these problems coherently. Fields are the basic objects, and particles are their excitation quanta. The classical negative-energy modes enter antiparticle creation terms. Particle number can change. Appropriate spacelike commutation or anticommutation relations implement relativistic causality. In particular, causal local response is tested by observables and commutators, not by requiring every two-point propagation amplitude to vanish outside the light cone.

Before continuing, reconstruct three checks without copying a formula: why a neutral pair has positive energy but zero net charge; why a nonnegative Dirac density can coexist with two frequency branches; and why small momentum allows an appropriate fixed-particle sector to recover Schrödinger evolution. These checks connect the new state space, the field interpretation, and the low-energy limit.

The following chapters develop this program in detail. Chapter 3 quantizes the scalar field and introduces its propagator and Feynman rules. Chapter 4 quantizes the Dirac field. Chapters 8 and 12 develop quantum electrodynamics and quantum chromodynamics as gauge theories.

Each chapter contains exercises. Appendix D collects the full solutions with explicit calculation steps.
