> [!TLDR]
> A fundamental relativistic quantum theory must permit particle creation and annihilation; quantized fields provide that description.

Attempts to construct relativistic single-particle quantum mechanics, from the Klein–Gordon equation to the Dirac equation, encounter related problems. The Klein paradox, negative energies, and changes in particle number are different forms of the same basic difficulty. At relativistic energies, particle creation and annihilation cannot be excluded. A theory with fixed particle number therefore cannot be the fundamental description.

Quantum field theory resolves these problems coherently. Fields are the basic objects, and particles are their excitation quanta. The classical negative-energy modes enter antiparticle creation terms. Particle number can change. Appropriate spacelike commutation or anticommutation relations implement relativistic causality.

The following chapters develop this program in detail. Chapter 3 quantizes the scalar field and introduces its propagator and Feynman rules. Chapter 4 quantizes the Dirac field. Chapters 8 and 12 develop quantum electrodynamics and quantum chromodynamics as gauge theories.

Each chapter contains exercises. Appendix D collects the full solutions with explicit calculation steps.
