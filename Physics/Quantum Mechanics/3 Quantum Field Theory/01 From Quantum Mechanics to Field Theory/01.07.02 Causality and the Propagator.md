---
title: "Causality and the Propagator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Particle and antiparticle contributions cancel in spacelike field commutators, which is required for relativistic causality.

A nonrelativistic particle can have a nonzero propagation amplitude outside the light cone. For the free Schrödinger equation, the probability density to find the particle at $\mechanical{\mathbf y}$ at time $\mechanical{t}$, given that it was at $\mechanical{\mathbf x}$ at time zero, is

$$
\left|\langle\mechanical{\mathbf y}|e^{-i\energy{\hat H}\mechanical{t}}|\mechanical{\mathbf x}\rangle\right|^{2}
=\left|\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}}{2\pi i\mechanical{t}}\right)^{3/2}
e^{i\mechanical{m}|\mechanical{\mathbf y}-\mechanical{\mathbf x}|^{2}/(2\mechanical{t})}\right|^{2}\ne0. \tag{1.63}
$$

This holds for any $|\mechanical{\mathbf y}-\mechanical{\mathbf x}|>0$ and $\mechanical{t}>0$, including spacelike separations with $|\mechanical{\mathbf y}-\mechanical{\mathbf x}|>\mechanical{c}\mechanical{t}$. Nonrelativistic mechanics permits this behavior. A relativistic theory must respect the light cone.

In quantum field theory, the scalar-field commutator $[\signal{\hat\phi}(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\hat\phi}(\mechanical{y})]$ vanishes at spacelike separation. This occurs through cancellation between particle and antiparticle contributions. Antiparticles are therefore required by causality.
