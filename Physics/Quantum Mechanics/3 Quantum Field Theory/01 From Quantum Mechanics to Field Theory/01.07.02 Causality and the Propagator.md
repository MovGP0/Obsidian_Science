---
title: "Causality and the Propagator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Particle and antiparticle contributions cancel in spacelike field commutators, which is required for relativistic causality.

A nonrelativistic particle can have a nonzero propagation amplitude outside the light cone. For the free Schrödinger equation, the squared position-space propagation kernel for the particle at $\mechanical{\mathbf y}$ at time $\mechanical{t}$, given that it was at $\mechanical{\mathbf x}$ at time zero, is

$$
\left|\langle\mechanical{\mathbf y}|e^{-i\energy{\hat H}\mechanical{t}}|\mechanical{\mathbf x}\rangle\right|^{2}
=\left|\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}}{2\pi i\mechanical{t}}\right)^{3/2}
e^{i\mechanical{m}|\mechanical{\mathbf y}-\mechanical{\mathbf x}|^{2}/(2\mechanical{t})}\right|^{2}\ne0. \tag{1.63}
$$

This holds for any $|\mechanical{\mathbf y}-\mechanical{\mathbf x}|>0$ and $\mechanical{t}>0$, including spacelike separations with $|\mechanical{\mathbf y}-\mechanical{\mathbf x}|>\mechanical{c}\mechanical{t}$. Nonrelativistic mechanics permits this behavior. Exact position eigenstates are not normalizable preparations, so (1.63) is a kernel statement rather than a normalized conditional probability distribution. A physical calculation smears the kernel with initial and final wave packets. The nonrelativistic approximation has no fundamental light-speed bound; a relativistic causal response must respect the light cone.

For a real free scalar field, the commutator $[\signal{\hat\phi}(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\hat\phi}(\mechanical{y})]$ vanishes at spacelike separation. Positive- and negative-frequency terms cancel there. For a charged complex scalar, the nontrivial test pairs the field with its adjoint; the two terms then involve particle and antiparticle modes. For a real field the particle is its own antiparticle.

> [!note]
> The source does not distinguish real and complex fields. Identical complex charged fields commute for a different algebraic reason; the adjoint test is essential. A spacelike two-point function need not vanish: causal response concerns local commutators. See [Tong, section 2.6.1](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qft.pdf).
