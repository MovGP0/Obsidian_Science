> [!TLDR]
> A Dirac particle incident on an electrostatic step provides a direct test of relativistic single-particle quantum mechanics.

Oskar Klein formulated the paradox in 1929. Consider a Dirac particle incident on an electrostatic potential barrier with step height $\energy{V}_0$.

The step potential is

$$
\energy{V}(\mechanical{x})=\begin{cases}
0,&\mechanical{x}<0\quad\text{(Region I)},\\
\energy{V}_0,&\mechanical{x}>0\quad\text{(Region II)},
\end{cases} \tag{1.54}
$$

Here $\energy{V}_0>0$. A particle with $\energy{E}>0$ travels from left to right, as shown in Fig. 1.1.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{V}_0$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Potential-step energy | The electrostatic barrier height in the scattering problem. |
| $\energy{V}(\mechanical{x})$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Potential energy | The spatially dependent step potential. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Position | The coordinate normal to the step. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Particle energy | The positive energy of the incident particle. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The rest mass in the Klein-regime condition. |
