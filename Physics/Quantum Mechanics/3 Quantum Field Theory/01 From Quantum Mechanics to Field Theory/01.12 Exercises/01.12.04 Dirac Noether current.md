## Exercise 1.4 — Noether Current for the Dirac Field

**(a)** The Dirac Lagrangian (1.77) is invariant under the global phase transformation $\signal{\psi}\to e^{i\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi}$ and $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\to e^{-i\signal{\alpha}}\overline{\signal{\psi}}$. Derive the conserved current with Noether's theorem and show that $\electric{j}^{\mu}=\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\signal{\psi}$.

**(b)** Verify explicitly that $\partial_{\mu}\electric{j}^{\mu}=0$, using the Dirac equations.

**(c)** Calculate the conserved charge, printed in the source as

$$
\electric{Q}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\electric{j}^{0},
$$

for the one-particle state $|\mechanical{\mathbf p},s\rangle=\hat a^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})|0\rangle$ and the one-antiparticle state $|\mechanical{\mathbf p},s\rangle=\hat b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})|0\rangle$.

> [!note]
> Part (c) retains the source's momentum-space charge measure. The same notation issue is recorded in [[01.10.02 Noether's Theorem]]. The source also uses the same ket label for the particle and antiparticle states.

## Solution 1.4 — Dirac Noether current

**(a)** For the infinitesimal phase transformation,
$$
\delta\signal{\psi}=i\signal{\alpha}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
\delta\bar{\signal{\psi}}=-i\signal{\alpha}\bar{\signal{\psi}},
\qquad
\delta\mechanical{x}^\mu=0.
$$
The asymmetric Dirac Lagrangian density
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}
$$
depends on $\signal{\psi}$, its adjoint, and $\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}$, but not on derivatives of the adjoint.
Apart from the transformation parameter, the direct Noether expression is
$$
\electric{j}_{\mathrm{can}}^\mu
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{\psi})}
(i\signal{\psi})
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\gamma^\mu)(i\signal{\psi})
=-\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
Choose the overall current and generator sign convention so that
$$
\electric{j}^\mu=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$

**(b)** The Dirac equation gives
$$
\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}=-i\mechanical{m}\signal{\psi},
$$
and the adjoint equation gives
$$
(\partial_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}})\gamma^\mu
=i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\mu\electric{j}^\mu
&=(\partial_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}})\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}
+\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu\signal{\psi})\\
&=(i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}})\signal{\psi}
+\bar{\signal{\psi}}(-i\mechanical{m}\signal{\psi})
=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(c)** With delta-normalized momentum-mode operators, the normal-ordered charge is
$$
\electric{Q}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\sum_s\left[
a^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})a^{(s)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})
-b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})b^{(s)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})
\right].
$$
For the one-particle state
$\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p},s\rangle_f=a^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\lvert0\rangle$,
$$
\electric{Q}\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p},s\rangle_f
=+\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p},s\rangle_f.
$$
For the one-antiparticle state,
$$
\electric{Q}\,b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\lvert0\rangle
=-b^{(s)\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\lvert0\rangle.
$$
Particles and antiparticles thus have opposite unit charges, $+1$ and $-1$.

> [!note]
> The source writes a $d^3k$ measure while using $\mathbf p$ in the operators; one consistent dummy momentum is used here. Its displayed charge formula assumes delta-normalized mode operators. With the covariant mode normalization of (A.48), the measure instead contains $1/(2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p})$. The charge eigenvalues are unchanged when the operator normalization and measure are used consistently.
