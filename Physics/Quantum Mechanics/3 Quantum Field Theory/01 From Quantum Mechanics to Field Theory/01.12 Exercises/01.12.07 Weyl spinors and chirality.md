## Exercise 1.7 — Weyl Spinors and Chirality

For a massless theory, $\mechanical{m}=0$, the Dirac equation separates into two equations for two-component spinors.

**(a)** In the Weyl or chiral representation, the gamma matrices are

$$
\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma^{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{i}\\-\sigma^{i}&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Write the Dirac spinor as

$$
\signal{\psi}=\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\psi}_L\\\signal{\psi}_R\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Show that, for $\mechanical{m}=0$, the Dirac equation separates into

$$
i(\partial_{\mechanical{t}}+\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\nabla)\signal{\psi}_R=0,
$$

$$
i(\partial_{\mechanical{t}}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\nabla)\signal{\psi}_L=0.
$$

**(b)** Show that $\signal{\psi}_L$ and $\signal{\psi}_R$ are eigenstates of $\gamma^{5}$ with eigenvalues $-1$ and $+1$, respectively.

**(c)** For an ultrarelativistic neutrino with $\mechanical{m}_{\nu}\approx0$ and momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ along $\hat{\mathbf z}$, calculate the helicity $\mechanical{h}=\mechanical{\mathbf S}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}/|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|$ for $\signal{\psi}_L$ and $\signal{\psi}_R$.

**(d)** Explain why parity violation in the weak interaction is naturally built into a theory that couples only to left-chiral components.

Chirality labels a spinor representation component; helicity is spin projected along momentum. They agree in the stated way for positive-energy massless particle modes. The approximate neutrino example uses that limit; it is not an assertion that a nonzero neutrino mass vanishes exactly.

## Solution 1.7 — Weyl spinors and chirality

**(a)** In the Weyl representation, write
$$
\signal{\psi}=
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\psi}_L\\\signal{\psi}_R\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The Dirac equation $(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}=0$ becomes
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
-\mechanical{m}&i(\partial_t+\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla)\\
i(\partial_t-\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla)&-\mechanical{m}
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\psi}_L\\\signal{\psi}_R\end{pmatrix}
=0.
$$
For $\mechanical{m}=0$, the equations decouple:
$$
i(\partial_t+\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla)\signal{\psi}_R=0,
\qquad
i(\partial_t-\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla)\signal{\psi}_L=0.
$$

**(b)** In the standard Weyl convention,
$$
\gamma^5=
\operatorname{diag}(-\mathbf1_2,+\mathbf1_2).
$$
Hence
$$
\gamma^5\signal{\psi}_L=-\signal{\psi}_L,
\qquad
\gamma^5\signal{\psi}_R=+\signal{\psi}_R.
$$
The two chiral components have eigenvalues $-1$ and $+1$. In these eigenvalue statements, each two-component block is understood as embedded in a four-component spinor with the other block zero; the four-by-four gamma-five does not act directly on an isolated two-entry column.

**(c)** Take a positive-energy plane wave
$$
\signal{\psi}_R\propto e^{-i(\energy{E}\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{p}\mechanical{z})},
\qquad
\mechanical{\mathbf p}=\mechanical{p}\,\hat{\mathbf z}.
$$
The right-chiral Weyl equation becomes
$$
(\partial_t+\sigma^3\partial_z)\signal{\psi}_R=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(\energy{E}-\mechanical{p}\sigma^3)\signal{\psi}_R=0.
$$
For $\energy{E}=\mechanical{p}>0$, the spinor is a $\sigma^3$ eigenstate with eigenvalue $+1$.
The helicity operator is
$$
\mechanical{h}=\frac12\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf p}
=\frac12\sigma^3.
$$
Thus the positive-energy massless right-chiral mode has helicity $+1/2$, with spin parallel to its motion.
The left-chiral mode has helicity $-1/2$. A nonzero Dirac mass couples the blocks in part (a), so a freely evolving state need not retain one chirality. For a massive particle an observer can overtake its motion and reverse the momentum direction without reversing its spin; this can reverse helicity. That thought experiment is unavailable for a massless particle.

**(d)** Charged weak currents couple to left-chiral fermion fields.
The corresponding massless antineutrinos have right helicity.
Parity exchanges $\signal{\psi}_L$ and $\signal{\psi}_R$, so an interaction coupled to only one chirality is not parity invariant.

The source identifies this with maximal parity violation, observed by Wu in the 1957 beta-decay experiment with cobalt-60.
The $V-A$ interaction structure makes this asymmetry explicit in the Lagrangian density.

> [!note]
> The source speaks broadly of weak interactions coupling only to the left component. This statement applies to the charged weak current. The neutral Z current also couples to right-chiral charged fermions. For nonzero mass, chirality and helicity are not identical.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac field | The Dirac field whose left and right components are separated. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The momentum direction that defines helicity. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Incident energy | The positive energy of the massless plane-wave mode. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Particle mass | The Dirac mass set to zero for exact chiral decoupling. |
| $\signal{\psi}_L$, $\signal{\psi}_R$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Chiral field components | The left- and right-handed blocks of the Dirac field. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time | The coordinate in the massless chiral equations. |
| $\mechanical{m}_{\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Neutrino mass | The mass neglected in the ultrarelativistic limit. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf S}$ | $1$ | Spin angular momentum | The spin used to define helicity in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $1$ | Helicity | The spin projection along the momentum direction. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Longitudinal momentum | The positive momentum magnitude along the chosen z axis. |
| $\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Longitudinal coordinate | The spatial coordinate along the plane-wave momentum. |
