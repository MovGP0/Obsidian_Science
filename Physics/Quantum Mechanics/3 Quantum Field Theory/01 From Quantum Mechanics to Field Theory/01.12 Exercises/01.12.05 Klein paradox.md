## Exercise 1.5 — The Klein Paradox in Detail

Return to the calculation in section 1.6.

**(a)** Sketch the qualitative dependence of $\signal{R}$ and $\signal{T}$ on $\energy{V}_0/\mechanical{m}$ at fixed $\energy{E}=1.5\mechanical{m}$. Identify the three regions: $\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}$, $\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}<\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}$, and $\energy{V}_0>\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}$.

**(b)** Find the asymptotic expressions for $\signal{R}$ and $\signal{T}$ when $\energy{V}_0\gg\energy{E}$.

**(c)** Calculate the exact threshold $\energy{V}_0^{\mathrm{threshold}}$ above which the paradox occurs, for general $\energy{E}>\mechanical{m}$.

**(d)** Use pair creation to explain physically why the current in Region II is negative in the Klein regime.

Use the source's positive right-hand momentum convention throughout parts (a)–(c). In the Klein regime it gives negative group current, so the listed coefficients are signed flux ratios, not ordinary probabilities for one wave incident only from the left. Predict this sign from the shifted-energy branch before inserting the matching amplitudes.

## Solution 1.5 — Klein paradox

**(a)** For $\energy{E}=1.5\mechanical{m}$:

- If $\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}=0.5\mechanical{m}$, the classical propagation regime has $0<\signal{T}<1$ and $\signal{R}=1-\signal{T}$.
- If $\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}<\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}$, that is, $0.5\mechanical{m}<\energy{V}_0<2.5\mechanical{m}$, the step gives total reflection: $\signal{R}=1$, $\signal{T}=0$, with an evanescent wave in the second region.
- If $\energy{V}_0>\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}=2.5\mechanical{m}$, the Klein regime has $\signal{R}>1$, $\signal{T}<0$, and $\signal{R}+\signal{T}=1$.

**(b)** For $\energy{V}_0\gg\energy{E},\mechanical{m}$,
$$
\mechanical{k}_2\approx\energy{V}_0,
\qquad
\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0+\mechanical{m}\approx-\energy{V}_0,
$$
so
$$
\signal{q}_2\approx
\frac{\mechanical{k}_2}{\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0+\mechanical{m}}
\approx-1.
$$
Meanwhile $\signal{q}_1=\mechanical{k}_1/(\energy{E}+\mechanical{m})$ remains fixed.
Thus
$$
\signal{R}\approx
\left(\frac{\signal{q}_1+1}{\signal{q}_1-1}\right)^2,
\qquad
\signal{T}\approx-\frac{4\signal{q}_1}{(\signal{q}_1-1)^2}.
$$
For $\energy{E}=1.5\mechanical{m}$, $\signal{q}_1\approx0.447$:
$$
\signal{R}\longrightarrow
\left(\frac{1.447}{-0.553}\right)^2
\approx6.85,
\qquad
\signal{T}\approx-5.85,
\qquad
\signal{R}+\signal{T}=1.
$$

**(c)** The threshold is
$$
\energy{V}_0^{\mathrm{threshold}}=\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}.
$$
It is the lower boundary of the regime in which the second-region momentum becomes real again.
Just above this threshold, $\mechanical{k}_2\to0$. The spinor ratio nevertheless diverges because its denominator tends to zero faster than its numerator. The limiting current coefficients approach total reflection at the threshold; evaluate the limit, not the ratio at its singular endpoint.

**(d)** For $\energy{V}_0>\energy{E}+\mechanical{m}$, the effective energy
$\energy{E}-\energy{V}_0$ lies below $-\mechanical{m}$ in the negative-energy continuum of the source's Dirac-sea description.
The barrier can create particle–antiparticle pairs.

The source interprets the negative current in Region II as antiparticle flux. Distinguish that charge-current picture from the Dirac probability current of the retained one-particle spinor: with positive momentum and negative shifted energy, its group and probability currents point left. Opposite charge alone does not reverse a probability current. An outgoing antiparticle interpretation needs specified field-theory modes and vacuum conditions. See the discussion and primary reference in [[01.06.03 The Klein Regime]].
The source calls this Klein scattering and states that a consistent interpretation requires the many-particle formalism of field theory.

> [!note]
> In the Klein regime, $R$ and $T$ are signed current ratios in the stated one-particle matching calculation. They cannot both be interpreted as ordinary probabilities. For the semi-infinite step in the intermediate regime, an evanescent amplitude does not give a nonzero asymptotic transmitted flux.

The large-step limit is a useful contrast with an evanescent interval: the mode momentum becomes real again, so increasing the step does not imply increasing exponential decay. The source values at fixed energy give finite signed coefficients, not a measured amplification factor. Check their sum before interpreting either number separately.
