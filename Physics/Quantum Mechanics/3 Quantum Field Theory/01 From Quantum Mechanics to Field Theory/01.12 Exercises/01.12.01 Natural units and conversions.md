## Exercise 1.1 — Natural Units and Conversions

**(a)** Verify that, in natural units with $\energy{\hbar}=\mechanical{c}=1$, the electron mass $\mechanical{m}_e\approx0.511\,\unit{MeV}$ gives a reduced Compton wavelength $\bar{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm C}=1/\mechanical{m}_e\approx386\,\unit{fm}$.

**(b)** The classical electron radius is $\mechanical{r}_e=\electric{\alpha}/\mechanical{m}_e$, where $\electric{\alpha}\approx1/137$. Calculate its value in $\unit{fm}$.

**(c)** Express the reduced Planck constant in units of $\unit{MeV\,fm}$ with the speed of light set to one. Show that $\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\approx197.3\,\unit{MeV\,fm}$.

**(d)** The cosmic microwave background temperature is $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm{CMB}}\approx2.73\,\unit{K}$. Express it as an energy in $\unit{eV}$, using $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}=8.617\times10^{-5}\,\unit{eV\,K^{-1}}$.

## Solution 1.1 — Natural units and conversions

**(a)** With $\energy{\hbar}=\mechanical{c}=1$, the reduced Compton wavelength is
$$
\bar{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm C}
=\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{c}}
=\frac1{\mechanical{m}_e}.
$$
Using $\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}\approx197.3\,\unit{MeV\,fm}$ and
$\mechanical{m}_e=0.511\,\unit{MeV}$ in natural units,
$$
\bar{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm C}
=\frac{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}{\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{c}^2}
=\frac{197.3\,\unit{MeV\,fm}}{0.511\,\unit{MeV}}
\approx386\,\unit{fm}.
$$

**(b)** The classical electron radius is
$$
\mechanical{r}_e=\electric{\alpha}\bar{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm C}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{\mechanical{m}_e}.
$$
With $\electric{\alpha}\approx1/137$,
$$
\mechanical{r}_e=\frac1{137}\times386\,\unit{fm}
\approx2.82\,\unit{fm}.
$$

**(c)** Use the source's rounded SI values
$$
\energy{\hbar}=1.0546\times10^{-34}\,\unit{J\,s},
\qquad
\mechanical{c}=2.998\times10^8\,\unit{m\,s^{-1}},
$$
and the conversions
$$
1\,\unit{J}=\frac1{1.602\times10^{-13}}\,\unit{MeV},
\qquad
1\,\unit{m}=10^{15}\,\unit{fm}.
$$
Then
$$
\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}
\approx
\frac{1.0546\times10^{-34}\times2.998\times10^8}
{1.602\times10^{-13}}\times10^{15}\,\unit{MeV\,fm}
\approx197.3\,\unit{MeV\,fm}.
$$

**(d)** A cosmic microwave background temperature
$\thermal{T}_{\mathrm{CMB}}=2.73\,\unit{K}$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm{CMB}}
&=(8.617\times10^{-5}\,\unit{eV\,K^{-1}})
(2.73\,\unit{K})\\
&\approx2.35\times10^{-4}\,\unit{eV}
\approx0.235\,\unit{meV}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the typical microwave energy scale.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action constant used in the natural-unit conversions. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | The constant set to one in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{m}_e$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron mass | The mass used in the Compton-wavelength and radius calculations. |
| $\bar{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm C}$ | $\unit{fm}$ | Reduced Compton wavelength | The inverse electron mass converted to a length. |
| $\mechanical{r}_e$ | $\unit{fm}$ | Classical electron radius | The electromagnetic length scale defined in part (b). |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The dimensionless coupling in the radius formula. |
| $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm{CMB}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Background temperature | The cosmic microwave background temperature. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{eV\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The temperature-to-energy conversion factor. |
