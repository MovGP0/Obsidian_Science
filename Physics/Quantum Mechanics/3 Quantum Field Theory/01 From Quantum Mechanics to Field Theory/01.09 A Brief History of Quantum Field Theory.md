title: "A Brief History of Quantum Field Theory"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The main stages in the development of quantum field theory explain the physical meaning of its modern formulation.

The historical route is also a route through physical problems: quantized radiation requires changing occupation numbers, radiative corrections require a controlled definition of parameters, and nuclear interactions require new symmetry structures. The dates organize this route; they do not by themselves establish the validity of a model.

In each stage, identify the theoretical proposal, the observable that could test it, and the assumption that later work had to change. The early field-quantization note prepares the operator picture. The renormalization note explains why a divergent intermediate expression is not an observable. The gauge-theory notes then separate symmetry constraints from experimental identification of particles.
