title: "Derivation"

> [!TLDR]
> Applying the quantum energy and momentum operators to the relativistic dispersion relation gives the Klein–Gordon equation.

Start with the relativistic dispersion relation (1.7). Substitute $\energy{E}\to i\partial_{\mechanical{t}}$ and $\mechanical{\mathbf p}\to-i\nabla$:

$$
(i\partial_{\mechanical{t}})^{2}\signal{\psi}
=\left[(-i\nabla)^{2}+\mechanical{m}^{2}\right]\signal{\psi}. \tag{1.9}
$$

This can be written as

$$
\left(\partial_{\mechanical{t}}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+\mechanical{m}^{2}\right)\signal{\psi}=0, \tag{1.10}
$$

or, in covariant notation,

$$
\boxed{\left(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2}\right)\signal{\phi}=0.} \tag{1.11}
$$

Here $\square=\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}=\partial_{\mechanical{t}}^{2}-\nabla^{2}$ is the d'Alembert operator.

Oskar Klein and Walter Gordon proposed equation (1.11) independently in 1926, at almost the same time as the Schrödinger equation. It was soon abandoned as a single-particle wave equation because of the problems examined in the following subsections.

The equation is Lorentz covariant. It is expressed through the Lorentz-invariant operator $\square$ and therefore has the same form in every inertial frame.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Energy | The energy replaced by the time-derivative operator. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The momentum replaced by the spatial-derivative operator. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The rest mass in the Klein–Gordon equation. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time | The time coordinate in natural units. |
| $\signal{\psi}$, $\signal{\phi}$ | — | Wave amplitude | The amplitude before and after the covariant notation is introduced. |
