title: "Fermionic Functional Integral"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The fermionic generating functional integrates Dirac fields and independent Grassmann sources. Its Gaussian structure produces a determinant and the fermion propagator.

For a Dirac field, define the measure
$\mathcal{D}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\mathcal{D}\signal{\psi}$
as a continuous product of Berezin differentials at each spacetime point.
The generating functional is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z}[\bar{\signal{\eta}},\signal{\eta}]
={}&\int\mathcal{D}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\mathcal{D}\signal{\psi}\\
&\times\exp\Bigg[
i\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\Big(
\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not\partial-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}
+\bar{\signal{\eta}}\signal{\psi}
+\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\eta}
\Big)\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.21}
$$

At finite regulator, the field and its barred partner are independent sets of Grassmann generators. The bar labels the adjoint field in correlators; it does not impose complex conjugacy between integration generators. Boundary conditions and the Feynman prescription define the inverse Dirac operator, just as they did for the scalar Gaussian.

The sources $\bar{\signal{\eta}}(\mechanical{x})$ and
$\signal{\eta}(\mechanical{x})$ are independent Grassmann fields.
Completing the square gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z_0}[\bar{\signal{\eta}},\signal{\eta}]
=\signal{Z_0}[0,0]\exp\Bigg[
-i\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{y}\,
\bar{\signal{\eta}}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{S_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
\signal{\eta}(\mechanical{y})
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.22}
$$
The source defines the inverse Dirac kernel by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{S_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
&=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}
{\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}+i\epsilon}\\
&=(i\not\partial+\mechanical{m})
\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.23}
$$
Here the matrix-inverse notation has the causal prescription inherited from the scalar denominator in $\signal{\Delta_F}$.

Fermionic Green functions follow from source differentiation.
The source prints
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle0|\mathcal{T}\{
\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})
\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
&=\left.
\frac1{\signal{Z_0}}\frac1{i^2}
\frac{\delta^2\signal{Z_0}}
{\delta\bar{\signal{\eta}}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})
\delta\bar{\signal{\eta}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
\right|_{\signal{\eta}=\bar{\signal{\eta}}=0}\\
&=i\signal{S}_{F,\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> The source has two barred-source derivatives in the displayed formula. A fermion–adjoint two-point function instead needs one derivative with respect to $\bar{\signal{\eta}}$ and one with respect to $\signal{\eta}$. With two barred derivatives, (11.22) gives zero at zero source. The derivative order and left/right Grassmann convention must also be specified to fix the sign.

A concrete derivative convention resolves the source error. Take both derivatives to be left derivatives, and let the rightmost derivative act first. Equation (11.22) then gives
$$
\left.\frac{\delta}{\delta\signal{\eta}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
\frac{\delta\signal{Z_0}}{\delta\bar{\signal{\eta}}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})}
\right|_{0}
=-i\signal{Z_0}[0,0]\signal{S}_{F,\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
Multiplying by $1/i^2$ and dividing by the zero-source functional gives $+i\signal{S_F}$, as required. Reversing the two odd derivatives reverses their sign. The same ordering must be used when generating higher correlators.

## Fermion loops and the minus sign

Every closed fermion loop gives a factor $-1$ in an amplitude.
In the functional integral, this follows from the anticommuting Grassmann variables.
Closing the propagator into a loop introduces the negative sign through their antisymmetry.

Multiplying the inverse Dirac kernel by its Dirac operator gives the spacetime delta function. Consequently the time-ordered propagator $i\signal{S_F}$ gives i times that delta function under the same operator. Its position-space mass dimension is three, equal to that of a field–adjoint pair. These checks test the operator, spinor ordering, and numerator i together. The loop minus sign is an exchange sign in amplitudes, not a negative measured probability.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Fermionic generating functional | The vacuum source functional for the Dirac field. |
| $\signal{\psi},\bar{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac field variables | The independent Grassmann integration fields. |
| $\signal{\eta},\bar{\signal{\eta}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{5/2}}$ | Grassmann source fields | The external anticommuting sources coupled to the Dirac fields. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Dirac mass | The mass in the fermion kinetic operator. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinate | The coordinate integrated in the source action. |
| $\signal{Z_0}$ | $1$ | Free fermion generating functional | The Gaussian source functional and its zero-source normalization. |
| $\signal{S_F}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Position-space Dirac inverse kernel | Its multiple iSF is the time-ordered fermion propagator in these definitions. |
| $\signal{\Delta_F}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Position-space scalar inverse kernel | The scalar kernel used to construct the Dirac kernel. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fourier momentum | The momentum integrated in the inverse Dirac operator. |
| $\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Second spacetime coordinate | The second endpoint of the propagator. |
