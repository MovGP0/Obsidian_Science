---
title: "The Free Particle"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For a free particle, splitting each path into its classical straight path and fluctuations reduces the kernel to a Gaussian integral.

For a free particle, $\energy{V}=0$ and
$$
\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]
=\int_{\mechanical{t_i}}^{\mechanical{t_f}}
\mathrm{d}\mechanical{t}\,
\frac{\mechanical{m}\dot{\mechanical{q}}^2}{2}.
$$
Write
$$
\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t})
=\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{t})
+\mechanical{\xi}(\mechanical{t}),
$$
where the classical path is
$$
\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{t})
=\mechanical{q_i}
+(\mechanical{q_f}-\mechanical{q_i})
\frac{\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{t_i}}{\mechanical{T}}.
$$

The classical path is a straight line, and the fluctuation satisfies
$\mechanical{\xi}(\mechanical{t_i})
=\mechanical{\xi}(\mechanical{t_f})=0$.
The mixed term is proportional to the integral of $\dot{\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}}\dot{\mechanical{\xi}}$. Integration by parts gives an endpoint term and a term proportional to the classical acceleration. Both vanish: the fluctuations have zero endpoint values and the free classical path has zero acceleration. No small-fluctuation approximation is needed.

Since the action is quadratic,
$$
\energy{S}[\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}+\mechanical{\xi}]
=\energy{S}[\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}]
+\energy{S}[\mechanical{\xi}],
\qquad
\energy{S}[\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}]
=\frac{\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{q_f}-\mechanical{q_i})^2}
{2\mechanical{T}}.
$$
The fluctuation integral is Gaussian with homogeneous boundary conditions.
It gives a factor that depends only on $\mechanical{T}$ and $\mechanical{m}$.
Comparison with the exact quantum-mechanical result fixes this factor:
$$
\signal{K_0}(\mechanical{q_f},\mechanical{T};
\mechanical{q_i},0)
=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{m}}{2\pi i\mechanical{T}}}
\exp\left[
\frac{i\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{q_f}-\mechanical{q_i})^2}
{2\mechanical{T}}\right].
\tag{11.4}
$$
This formula is exact.
The phase $e^{i\energy{S_{\mathrm{cl}}}}$ is the semiclassical factor.
The prefactor describes Gaussian quantum fluctuations around the classical path.

Two checks fix the physical meaning of this exact Gaussian. For positive elapsed time, differentiating (11.4) gives the free Schrödinger equation in the final coordinate. In the limit of zero elapsed time, its rapidly oscillating phase and growing prefactor together approach the position delta function. The pointwise limit at separated endpoints does not describe this distributional limit.

The prefactor has inverse-length units and decreases in magnitude as the inverse square root of elapsed time. This is consistent with the spreading of a propagated wave packet. The modulus of this kernel alone is not a normalized probability density, because its initial position eigenstate is not normalizable. In an anharmonic potential, cubic and higher fluctuation terms survive and the same Gaussian method is generally an approximation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{V}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Potential energy | Zero for the free particle. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Free-particle action | The kinetic action in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{q},\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}},\mechanical{q_i},\mechanical{q_f},\mechanical{\xi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Path, classical path, endpoints, and fluctuation | The position variables in the decomposition around the straight classical path. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{t_i},\mechanical{t_f},\mechanical{T}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Times and total interval | The time variables, with T equal to tf minus ti. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Particle mass | The mass in the kinetic action. |
| $\dot{\mechanical{q}}$ | $1$ | Particle velocity | The time derivative of the path coordinate. |
| $\signal{K_0}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Free-particle kernel | The exact one-dimensional position-space transition kernel. |
| $\energy{S_{\mathrm{cl}}}$ | $1$ | Classical action | The action evaluated on the straight classical path. |
