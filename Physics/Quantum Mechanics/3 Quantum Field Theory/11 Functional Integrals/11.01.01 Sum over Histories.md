---
title: "Sum over Histories"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Quantum transition amplitudes can be expressed as a sum over possible paths, with each path weighted by its action.

Quantum mechanics describes time evolution with the operator
$e^{-i\hat{\energy{H}}\mechanical{t}}$.
The path-integral construction starts from the corresponding transition amplitude.

For an initial position $\mechanical{q_i}$ at time $\mechanical{t_i}$ and a final position $\mechanical{q_f}$ at time $\mechanical{t_f}$, the transition kernel is
$$
\signal{K}(\mechanical{q_f},\mechanical{t_f};
\mechanical{q_i},\mechanical{t_i})
=\langle\mechanical{q_f}|
e^{-i\hat{\energy{H}}(\mechanical{t_f}-\mechanical{t_i})}
|\mechanical{q_i}\rangle.
\tag{11.1}
$$
Feynman's alternative is to sum the complex weight
$e^{i\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]}$ over all kinematically possible paths
$\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t})$ connecting the endpoints.
The quantity $\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]$ is the classical action functional.
Formally,
$$
\signal{K}(\mechanical{q_f},\mechanical{t_f};
\mechanical{q_i},\mechanical{t_i})
=\int_{\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t_i})=\mechanical{q_i}}^{
\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t_f})=\mechanical{q_f}}
\mathcal{D}\mechanical{q}\,e^{i\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]},
\tag{11.2}
$$
with
$$
\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]
=\int_{\mechanical{t_i}}^{\mechanical{t_f}}
\mathrm{d}\mechanical{t}\,
\energy{L}(\mechanical{q},\dot{\mechanical{q}}).
$$
The source describes the particle as taking all paths at once.
Near a classical path with $\delta\energy{S}=0$, neighboring amplitudes interfere constructively.
Far from stationary paths, their rapidly changing phases cancel.
In the classical limit $\mechanical{\hbar}\to0$, or
$\energy{S}\gg1$ in the convention $\mechanical{\hbar}=1$,
the stationary-path contributions dominate and classical mechanics is recovered.

> [!note]
> “All paths at once” is a description of the amplitude sum, not a statement that a measurement reveals simultaneous classical trajectories. The classical condition is stationary action, which need not be a minimum. Multiple stationary paths can contribute.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat{\energy{H}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy operator generating time evolution. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time interval | The evolution time in natural units with ħ = 1. |
| $\signal{K}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Position-space transition kernel | The one-dimensional propagator between position eigenstates with delta-function normalization. |
| $\mechanical{q},\mechanical{q_i},\mechanical{q_f}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Path coordinate and endpoints | The particle position along the path and its initial and final values. |
| $\mechanical{t_i},\mechanical{t_f}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Endpoint times | The initial and final evolution times. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The classical action divided by ħ in the natural-unit convention. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Lagrangian | The mechanical Lagrangian integrated over time. |
| $\dot{\mechanical{q}}$ | $1$ | Path velocity | The time derivative of position in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $1$ | Reduced Planck constant | Set to one in the displayed natural-unit formulas. |
