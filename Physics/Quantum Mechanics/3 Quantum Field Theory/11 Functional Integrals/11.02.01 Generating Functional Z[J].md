---
title: "Generating Functional Z[J]"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The generating functional introduces sources into the scalar-field path integral so that correlation functions can be obtained by differentiation.

To pass from quantum mechanics to field theory, replace the coordinate
$\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t})$ by a scalar field
$\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$.
Each spatial point $\mechanical{x}$ has a field degree of freedom.
Functional integration then sums over field configurations.

The **generating functional** is
$$
\boxed{
\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]
=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}\,
\exp\left[
i\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\big(\energy{\mathcal{L}}(\signal{\phi},\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})
+\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\big)
\right].
}
\tag{11.6}
$$
The external source $\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})$ is an arbitrary commuting, or c-number, field.
Formally, the measure is
$\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}=\prod_{\mechanical{x}}\mathrm{d}\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$.

Functional differentiation generates time-ordered Green functions:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle0|\mathcal{T}\{
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x_1})\cdots
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x_n})\}|0\rangle\\
\quad=\left.
\frac1{\signal{Z}[0]}\frac1{i^n}
\frac{\delta^n\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]}
{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x_1})\cdots
\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x_n})}
\right|_{\signal{J}=0}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.7}
$$
The source writes the functional expansion as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^n}{n!}
\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x_1}\cdots
\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x_n}\,
\signal{G}^{(n)}(\mechanical{x_1},\ldots,\mechanical{x_n})\\
{}\times\signal{J}(\mechanical{x_1})\cdots
\signal{J}(\mechanical{x_n}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where
$\signal{G}^{(n)}
=\langle0|\mathcal{T}\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x_1})\cdots
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x_n})\}|0\rangle$.

> [!note]
> With the normalized correlators of (11.7), this expansion is for $\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]/\signal{Z}[0]$. For the unnormalized functional on the left, the right side also needs the factor $\signal{Z}[0]$, unless the measure normalization has already set it to one.

> [!note]
> **Remark 11.3: Boundary conditions and the iε prescription.** The path integral needs boundary conditions. Vacuum correlation functions select the vacuum as the initial and final state. The shift $\mechanical{m}^2\to\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon$ adds the damping factor $\exp[-\epsilon\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,\signal{\phi}^2/2]$. This is the Feynman prescription; it suppresses configurations with large field amplitude. Formally, the time contour is tilted slightly toward the imaginary direction.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The scalar field in four-dimensional quantum field theory. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Position | The coordinate labeling field degrees of freedom. |
| $\mechanical{q}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Particle path coordinate | The quantum-mechanical variable replaced by a field configuration. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time | The time argument of the particle path. |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Generating functional | The vacuum functional whose source derivatives generate correlators. |
| $\signal{J}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | External scalar source | The commuting source coupled linearly to the scalar field. |
| $\energy{\mathcal{L}}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Lagrangian density | The density in the scalar-field action. |
| $\signal{G}^{(n)}$ | — | Time-ordered n-point function | The scalar correlator has mass dimension n in four-dimensional position space. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar mass | The mass parameter shifted by the causal prescription. |
