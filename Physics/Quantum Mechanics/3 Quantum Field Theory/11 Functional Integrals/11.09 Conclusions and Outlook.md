> [!TLDR]
> Functional integrals generate full, connected, and 1PI Green functions. Grassmann integration, gauge fixing, and changes of variables extend the method to fermions, gauge fields, and quantum identities.

This chapter develops the following tools:

- The path integral
  $\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}\,
  e^{i(\energy{S}+\int\signal{J}\signal{\phi})}$
  generates all Green functions.
- $\signal{W}[\signal{J}]=-i\ln\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]$ generates connected Green functions.
- The effective action $\energy{\Gamma}[\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}]$, the Legendre transform of $\signal{W}$, generates one-particle-irreducible (1PI) functions. Its expansion in $\mechanical{\hbar}$ gives the loop expansion.
- Fermions require Grassmann variables. A fermionic Gaussian integral gives $\det M$, while a bosonic Gaussian gives $(\det M)^{-1/2}$.
- The Faddeev–Popov procedure handles gauge redundancy. It introduces ghost fields and fixes the gauge covariantly.
- Schwinger–Dyson equations and Ward identities follow from the behavior of the functional measure under changes of variables.

The distinction between these tools is operational. Use source derivatives for full correlators, the logarithm to remove independent clusters, and the Legendre transform to remove single-propagator connections. The proper-vertex factor of i is a convention in addition to the functional derivative. The determinant comparison above is between one real scalar and paired Dirac variables; complex bosons and other fermion reality conditions require their own multiplicities.

The chapter has three levels of checks. The free particle and oscillator compare path integrals with exact operator evolution. Gaussian source derivatives compare kernels and correlation functions, including their normalization and factors of i. Ward identities test that interacting propagators and vertices respect the same symmetry. None of these internal checks is an experimental dataset. Scattering amplitudes constructed from the correlators supply quantities that can be compared with measurements.

Before applying the method to a new theory, identify its state and contour, regulator, physical field content, and gauge redundancy. Then test a free limit and a symmetry identity before trusting an interacting truncation. The exercises retain the source's convention corrections so that an apparent mismatch can be traced to its definition rather than hidden by a changed propagator symbol.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{Z},\signal{W}$ | $1$ | Generating functionals | The full and connected source functionals. |
| $\signal{\phi},\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field and classical field | The integration field and its source-dependent expectation value. |
| $\signal{J}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Scalar source | The external source coupled to the scalar field. |
| $\energy{S},\energy{\Gamma}$ | $1$ | Action and effective action | The classical and quantum actions in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $1$ | Reduced Planck constant | A formal parameter that counts loops; set to one in natural units. |
