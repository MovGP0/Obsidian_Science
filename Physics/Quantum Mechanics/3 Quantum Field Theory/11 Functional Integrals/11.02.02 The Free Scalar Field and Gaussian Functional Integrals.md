---
title: "The Free Scalar Field and Gaussian Functional Integrals"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The free scalar functional integral is Gaussian. Its quadratic kernel determines the propagator and source dependence.

For the free scalar field,
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}_0}
=\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})^2
-\frac12\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\phi}^2.
$$
This is the field-theory test case in which all correlators can be found without an interaction expansion. The quadratic action is the continuum version of a Gaussian matrix integral. Boundary terms are taken to vanish under the vacuum boundary prescription. The small damping term must remain when the quadratic operator is inverted, even where later lines leave it implicit.

After integration by parts,
$$
\energy{S_0}[\signal{\phi}]
=-\frac12\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
(\square+\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}).
$$

Here
$\square=\partial_\mu\partial^\mu
=\partial_{\mechanical{t}}^2-\nabla^2$.
Add the source term:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{S_0}[\signal{\phi}]+\int\signal{J}\signal{\phi}
={}&-\frac12\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
(\square+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\\
&+\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Complete the functional square using the kernel of the inverse operator
$-(\square+\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)$:
$$
-(\square_{\mechanical{x}}+\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)
\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
Its Fourier representation is
$$
\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
\tag{11.8}
$$
Translate the field by
$\signal{\phi}\to\signal{\phi}+\signal{\phi_0}$, where
$$
\signal{\phi_0}(\mechanical{x})
=-\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{y}\,
\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
\signal{J}(\mechanical{y}).
$$
The source writes
$(\square+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{\phi_0}=\signal{J}$
with the causal prescription implicit.
The action becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{S_0}[\signal{\phi}+\signal{\phi_0}]
+\int\signal{J}(\signal{\phi}+\signal{\phi_0})
={}&\energy{S_0}[\signal{\phi}]\\
&-\frac12\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{y}\,
\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
\signal{J}(\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The linear term cancels.

The negative sign in the field shift follows from the chosen kernel: the quadratic action operator is the negative Klein–Gordon operator, whose inverse is $\signal{\Delta_F}$. The stationary field therefore equals minus that inverse acting on the source. This is also a check that the source-dependent action after the shift has the displayed negative one-half coefficient. A retarded inverse would change the boundary problem and would not give these time-ordered vacuum contractions.
The Gaussian integral over $\signal{\phi}$ gives a constant functional determinant.
The source's boxed result is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z_0}[\signal{J}]
=\signal{Z_0}[0]\exp\Bigg[
-\frac12\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{y}\,
\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{11.9}
$$
The normalization is
$\signal{Z_0}[0]=\mathcal{N}
[\det(-\square-\mechanical{m}^2)]^{-1/2}$.
It is usually set to one, corresponding to removal of disconnected vacuum diagrams.

> [!note]
> Equation (11.9) is missing $i$ in its exponent. With the Minkowski weight $e^{i\energy{S}}$ and the definition (11.8), completing the square gives $-\frac{i}{2}\int\signal{J}\signal{\Delta_F}\signal{J}$. The subsequent derivative quoted by the source uses this missing factor.

## Example 11.4: Propagator from the generating functional

Use (11.9) and (11.7) to calculate
$\langle0|\mathcal{T}\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle$.
The source gives the first derivative as
$$
\frac{\delta\signal{Z_0}[\signal{J}]}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})}
=\signal{Z_0}[\signal{J}]
\left[-i\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{z}\,
\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{z}-\mechanical{y})
\signal{J}(\mechanical{z})\right].
$$
Differentiate again with respect to $\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})$ and set the source to zero:
$$
\langle0|\mathcal{T}\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=\left.\frac1{i^2}
\frac{\delta^2\signal{Z_0}[\signal{J}]}
{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})}
\right|_{\signal{J}=0}.
$$
The source calls the surviving contraction
$-i\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})$.

> [!note]
> The factor $-i\signal{\Delta_F}$ quoted here is the second derivative, before multiplication by $1/i^2=-1$. It is not yet the normalized correlator. The source continuation below correctly includes that prefactor and obtains $+i\signal{\Delta_F}$. The source uses $\signal{Z_0}[0]=1$ in the displayed derivative formula.

The next line of the source completes the two-point calculation:
$$
\frac1{i^2}(-i)\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=i\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
Thus its final correlator agrees with the normalization explained above.

For the free field, the **Wick theorem** gives the $n$-point functions.
Sum over all pairwise contractions; each pair contributes a propagator
$i\signal{\Delta_F}$.

The corrected exponent and two-point result pass the free equation check: acting with the Klein–Gordon operator on $i\signal{\Delta_F}$ gives $-i$ times the spacetime delta function. Its mass dimension is two in position space, equal to that of two scalar fields. Odd source derivatives vanish at zero source because the exponent is quadratic and even. Four derivatives give three pair products, whereas four derivatives of its logarithm vanish. This separates a nonzero full four-point function from a zero connected one.

The field-independent determinant cancels in normalized correlators of this fixed free theory. That cancellation does not justify discarding a determinant that depends on a background field, as in the effective-action calculation.
