> [!TLDR]
> The harmonic-oscillator path integral is Gaussian. Its exact kernel follows from the classical action and the determinant of quadratic fluctuations.

For the harmonic oscillator,
$\energy{V}(\mechanical{q})
=\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{q}^2/2$.
The classical path satisfies
$\ddot{\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}}
+\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}=0$,
with endpoints
$\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}(0)=\mechanical{q_i}$ and
$\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{T})=\mechanical{q_f}$:
$$
\mechanical{q_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{t})
=\frac{
\mechanical{q_f}\sin(\signal{\omega}\mechanical{t})
+\mechanical{q_i}\sin[\signal{\omega}(\mechanical{T}-\mechanical{t})]}
{\sin(\signal{\omega}\mechanical{T})}.
$$
The classical action is
$$
\energy{S_{\mathrm{cl}}}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}}
{2\sin(\signal{\omega}\mechanical{T})}
\left[
(\mechanical{q_f}^2+\mechanical{q_i}^2)
\cos(\signal{\omega}\mechanical{T})
-2\mechanical{q_f}\mechanical{q_i}
\right].
$$
Expand the fluctuations $\mechanical{\xi}$ in eigenmodes of
$-\partial_{\mechanical{t}}^2-\signal{\omega}^2$
with homogeneous endpoint conditions:
$$
\xi_n(\mechanical{t})
=\sqrt{\frac2{\mechanical{T}}}
\sin\frac{n\pi\mechanical{t}}{\mechanical{T}},
\qquad
\lambda_n=\left(\frac{n\pi}{\mechanical{T}}\right)^2
-\signal{\omega}^2.
$$
The formal functional determinant is
$$
\det(-\partial_{\mechanical{t}}^2-\signal{\omega}^2)
=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(\frac{n^2\pi^2}{\mechanical{T}^2}-\signal{\omega}^2\right).
$$

Use the infinite-product identity
$$
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}
\left(1-\frac{\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{T}^2}{n^2\pi^2}\right)
=\frac{\sin(\signal{\omega}\mechanical{T})}
{\signal{\omega}\mechanical{T}}.
$$
The full kernel is
$$
\signal{K_{\mathrm{osc}}}(\mechanical{q_f},\mechanical{T};
\mechanical{q_i},0)
=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}}
{2\pi i\sin(\signal{\omega}\mechanical{T})}}\,
e^{i\energy{S_{\mathrm{cl}}}}.
\tag{11.5}
$$
The source calls this result exact for all $\mechanical{T}$.
Its form is semiclassical: a classical-action phase times the Gaussian fluctuation determinant.

> [!note]
> At $\sin(\signal{\omega}\mechanical{T})=0$, the displayed ordinary-function expression is singular and the kernel must be understood as a distributional limit. Across these caustics, the square-root branch must be continued with the correct phase. “All times” therefore requires this continuation, rather than an independent principal square root at each time.

## Example 11.2: Thermal partition function by Wick rotation

Consider
$\signal{Z}=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{H}})$.
Set $\mechanical{t}\to-i\mechanical{\tau}$ and
$\mechanical{T}\to-i\thermal{\beta}$.
The evolution operator becomes $e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{H}}$.
Taking the trace imposes periodic endpoint conditions,
$\mechanical{q}(\thermal{\beta})=\mechanical{q}(0)$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z}
&=\oint\mathcal{D}\mechanical{q_E}\,
e^{-\energy{S_E}[\mechanical{q}]},\\
\energy{S_E}
&=\int_0^{\thermal{\beta}}\mathrm{d}\mechanical{\tau}\,
\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}\dot{\mechanical{q}}^2}{2}
+\energy{V}(\mechanical{q})\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
The subscript $E$ denotes the Euclidean formulation; the dot now denotes differentiation with respect to $\mechanical{\tau}$.
The connection between statistical physics and Euclidean quantum mechanics underlies thermal correspondences and nonperturbative renormalization methods.
