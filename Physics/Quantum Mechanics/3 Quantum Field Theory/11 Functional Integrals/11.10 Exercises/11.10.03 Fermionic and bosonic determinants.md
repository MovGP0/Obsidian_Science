## Exercise 11.3: Fermionic and bosonic determinants

Consider a real scalar field and a Dirac field with the same mass $\mechanical{m}$ in $d=4$ dimensions.
Calculate the ratio of their contributions to the vacuum partition function:
$$
\frac{\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}}{\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{scalar}}}
=\frac{\det(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})}
{[\det(-\Box-\mechanical{m}^2)]^{-1/2}}.
$$

Use
$$
\det(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\,
\det(-i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})
=\det(-\Box-\mechanical{m}^2+\text{spin term})
$$
to show that a fermion loop has the opposite sign to a boson loop, with spinor degeneracy four.
How does this generalize to supersymmetry?

> [!note]
> The displayed determinant identity is copied from the source. For ordinary commuting derivatives, the product of the two displayed Dirac operators gives $\Box+\mechanical{m}^2$, with an identity matrix in spinor space. A spin–field-strength term occurs when covariant derivatives do not commute. The sign and phase of a functional determinant also require a consistent Minkowski or Euclidean prescription.

## Solution 11.3 — Fermionic and bosonic determinants

Use a consistent Euclidean convention and set $\mechanical{\hbar}=1$. For a real scalar, let
$$
K=-\partial_{\mathrm E}^2+\mechanical{m}^2.
$$
The Gaussian integral gives, up to the measure normalization,
$$
\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{scalar}}
=\int\mathcal D\signal{\phi}\,
e^{-\frac12\int\signal{\phi}K\signal{\phi}}
=(\det K)^{-1/2}.
$$
For a complex Dirac field, define
$D_{\mathrm E}=\gamma_{\mathrm E}^\mu\partial_\mu+\mechanical{m}$. The Grassmann integral gives
$$
\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
=\int\mathcal D\bar{\signal{\psi}}\,\mathcal D\signal{\psi}\,
e^{-\int\bar{\signal{\psi}}D_{\mathrm E}\signal{\psi}}
=\det D_{\mathrm E}.
$$
For free commuting derivatives and Hermitian Euclidean gamma matrices,
$$
D_{\mathrm E}^\dagger D_{\mathrm E}
=(-\gamma_{\mathrm E}^\mu\partial_\mu+\mechanical{m})
(\gamma_{\mathrm E}^\nu\partial_\nu+\mechanical{m})
=K\mathbf1_4.
$$
Taking determinants over spinor and spacetime indices,
$$
|\det D_{\mathrm E}|^2
=\det(K\mathbf1_4)=(\det K)^4.
$$
The power four is the Dirac spinor multiplicity.

> [!note] Operator convention
> The supplied solution mixes Euclidean Gaussian notation with Lorentzian operators. In Lorentzian signature its displayed product is $(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})(-i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})=(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\mathbf1_4$, rather than $(-\Box-\mechanical{m}^2)\mathbf1_4$. The Euclidean form above keeps the positive scalar kernel and fermion determinant in one convention. No spin–field-strength term occurs for ordinary commuting derivatives.

For the free Euclidean operator with positive mass and a consistent determinant phase, take the positive result
$$
\det D_{\mathrm E}=(\det K)^2.
$$
Thus the partition-function ratio requested in the exercise is
$$
\frac{\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}}{\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{scalar}}}
=(\det K)^{5/2}.
$$

### Relative loop signs and multiplicity

The logarithms are
$$
\ln\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}=2\operatorname{Tr}\ln K,
\qquad
\ln\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{scalar}}=-\frac12\operatorname{Tr}\ln K.
$$
Their determinant contributions have relative coefficient $-4$: a Dirac field gives four times the magnitude and the opposite sign of one real scalar. The source calls this the “ratio of vacuum contributions”; it is a comparison of logarithmic coefficients, distinct from the ratio of partition functions above.

### Supersymmetry

In unbroken supersymmetry, matched bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom have equal masses and corresponding internal quantum numbers. Their determinant factors cancel when the operators and multiplicities match. Schematically, for a matched pair of factors,
$$
(\det\mathcal O_B)^{-1/2}
(\det\mathcal O_F)^{+1/2}=1
\qquad\text{if }\mathcal O_B=\mathcal O_F.
$$
The equal-mass comparison of one Dirac field and one real scalar alone does not cancel: their multiplicities differ. A Dirac field requires four matching real scalar degrees of freedom for this free determinant count. Supersymmetric cancellations can remove quadratic mass sensitivities; exact cancellation requires the unbroken matching conditions.

> [!note]
> The source asserts reality of the Lorentzian determinant after regularization. Its phase is not fixed by the determinant-modulus identity alone. The free Euclidean positive-mass convention used here makes that choice explicit. Supersymmetry also pairs states of different spin; “same quantum numbers” refers to the corresponding internal quantum numbers, not equal spin.
