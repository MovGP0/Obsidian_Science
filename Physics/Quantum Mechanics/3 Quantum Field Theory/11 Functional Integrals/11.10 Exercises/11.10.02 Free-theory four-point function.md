## Exercise 11.2: Four-point Green function of the free theory

Use Wick's theorem in the functional-integral formalism to calculate the scalar-field function
$$
\signal{G}^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}_1,\mechanical{x}_2,\mechanical{x}_3,\mechanical{x}_4)
=\langle0|T\{
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_2)
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_3)\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_4)
\}|0\rangle.
$$
Show that it has three terms, each a product of two propagators:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G}^{(4)}(1,2,3,4)
={}&\signal{G}^{(2)}(1,2)\signal{G}^{(2)}(3,4)\\
&+\signal{G}^{(2)}(1,3)\signal{G}^{(2)}(2,4)\\
&+\signal{G}^{(2)}(1,4)\signal{G}^{(2)}(2,3).
\end{aligned}
$$
What is the connected four-point function $\signal{G}_c^{(4)}$ in the free theory?

## Solution 11.2 — Free-theory four-point function

In the convention of [[11.02.02 The Free Scalar Field and Gaussian Functional Integrals]], the free normalized generating functional is
$$
\signal{Z}_0[\signal{J}]
=\exp\left[-\frac i2\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,d^4\mechanical{y}\,
\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})\right].
$$

> [!note] Propagator convention
> In this part of Chapter 11, $\signal{\Delta}_F$ denotes the inverse Klein–Gordon kernel with momentum numerator one. The time-ordered two-point correlator is $\signal{G}^{(2)}=i\signal{\Delta}_F$. Earlier chapters sometimes call the correlator itself $\Delta_F$. The factor $i$ is retained explicitly here.

The four-point function is
$$
\signal{G}^{(4)}(1,2,3,4)
=\left.\frac1{i^4}
\frac{\delta^4\signal{Z}_0}
{\delta\signal{J}_1\delta\signal{J}_2\delta\signal{J}_3\delta\signal{J}_4}
\right|_{\signal{J}=0}.
$$
Let $f=\ln\signal{Z}_0=-i\int\signal{J}\signal{\Delta}_F\signal{J}/2$. The source calls this exponent $W_0$; it differs from the chapter's convention $\signal{W}=-i\ln\signal{Z}$.
Since $f$ is quadratic in the source,
$$
\frac{\delta f}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})}
=-i\int d^4\mechanical{y}\,\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})\signal{J}(\mechanical{y}),
\qquad
\frac{\delta^2f}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})}
=-i\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
The first derivative vanishes at zero source, and derivatives of $f$ above second order vanish identically. Applying the product rule to $\signal{Z}_0=e^f$ therefore gives all pairings of the four insertion points, beginning with
$$
(i\signal{\Delta}_F(1,2))(i\signal{\Delta}_F(3,4)).
$$

All three pairings give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G}^{(4)}(1,2,3,4)
={}&(i\signal{\Delta}_F(1,2))(i\signal{\Delta}_F(3,4))\\
&+(i\signal{\Delta}_F(1,3))(i\signal{\Delta}_F(2,4))\\
&+(i\signal{\Delta}_F(1,4))(i\signal{\Delta}_F(2,3)).
\end{aligned}
$$
With $\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})=i\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G}^{(4)}(1,2,3,4)
={}&\signal{G}^{(2)}(1,2)\signal{G}^{(2)}(3,4)\\
&+\signal{G}^{(2)}(1,3)\signal{G}^{(2)}(2,4)\\
&+\signal{G}^{(2)}(1,4)\signal{G}^{(2)}(2,3).
\end{aligned}
$$

### Connected four-point function

The connected function is the full function minus its disconnected products. Each of the three terms above is a product of two two-point functions. Since the free vacuum also has a vanishing one-point function,
$$
\signal{G}_c^{(4)}\big|_{\mathrm{free}}=0.
$$
For a Gaussian free field, all connected correlators of order three and higher vanish. Interactions are required to generate them.
