## Exercise 11.10: Ward identity in φ⁴ theory

Consider scalar $\signal{\phi}^4$ theory with the discrete symmetry
$\signal{\phi}\to-\signal{\phi}$.
The source describes the internal symmetry in terms of field parity.
Instead, consider translation symmetry,
$$
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\to\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}+\mechanical{a}).
$$
Show that its Ward identity expresses conservation of the energy–momentum tensor.
Write the identity explicitly as a relation between a derivative of the propagator and the energy–momentum tensor.
Relate it to the dispersion relation
$$
\mechanical{p}^2=\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}}^2.
$$

> [!note]
> A discrete $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry has no infinitesimal generator and therefore no Noether current of the usual continuous-symmetry type. The source's reference to a Noether current for field parity is imprecise. Continuous translations do have the energy–momentum tensor as their Noether current.

## Solution 11.10 — Translation Ward identity in quartic theory

The action is invariant under constant translations
$\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\mapsto\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}+\mechanical{a})$.
The associated Noether current is the energy–momentum tensor,
$$
\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}
=\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}\,\partial^\nu\signal{\phi}
-g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}.
$$

Conservation of the translation current gives the time-ordered Ward identity
$$
\partial_\mu^x
\left\langle T\,\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\cdots\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_n)\right\rangle
=-i\sum_{k=1}^n
\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_k)
\partial_{x_k}^{\nu}
\left\langle T\,\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\cdots\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_n)\right\rangle.
$$
For one scalar insertion, the right side vanishes in a translation-invariant vacuum because $\langle\signal{\phi}\rangle$ is constant. With two insertions, the contact terms contain derivatives of the scalar two-point function. They express momentum conservation at an energy–momentum-tensor insertion.

Near an isolated stable one-particle pole, the time-ordered propagator has the form
$$
\signal{D}_{\mathrm{exact}}(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i\signal{Z}_{\mathrm{pole}}}
{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}}^2+i0}
+\text{terms regular at the pole}.
$$

> [!note] What translation invariance protects
> The source describes the physical pole mass as protected by translation invariance. Translation symmetry enforces momentum conservation; together with Lorentz symmetry it permits the relativistic one-particle dispersion relation. It does not prevent a radiative mass shift. The pole mass is independent of the choice of renormalization scheme when the same physical theory is held fixed, while the renormalized mass parameter and counterterm can change. The isolated-pole expression also assumes a stable particle.
