## Exercise 11.4: Grassmann Gaussian integral

Prove formula (11.19).
For $n$ pairs of Grassmann variables $\bar{\theta}_i,\theta_j$, with $i,j=1,\ldots,n$,
$$
\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}d\bar{\theta}_i\,d\theta_i\,
\exp\left(-\sum_{i,j}\bar{\theta}_iM_{ij}\theta_j\right)
=\det M.
$$

> [!note]
> **Hint from the source:** Expand the exponential in a Taylor series. Nilpotency makes the series terminate. Only the term of degree $n$ in $\bar{\theta}$ and degree $n$ in $\theta$ survives. It reproduces the Leibniz expansion of the determinant. Use the same Berezin-measure ordering convention as in (11.19).

## Solution 11.4 — Grassmann Gaussian integral

For $n$ pairs of Grassmann variables and the Berezin measure convention of (11.19), the target is
$$
\int\prod_{i=1}^n d\bar\theta_i\,d\theta_i\,
e^{-\bar\theta_iM_{ij}\theta_j}
=\det M.
$$
Expand the exponential:
$$
e^{-\bar\theta_iM_{ij}\theta_j}
=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}
(\bar\theta_iM_{ij}\theta_j)^k.
$$
Nilpotency terminates the series. A nonzero integral needs every variable exactly once, so only the term with $k=n$, containing
$\bar\theta_1\theta_1\cdots\bar\theta_n\theta_n$, can survive.

The surviving Taylor term is
$$
\frac{(-1)^n}{n!}(\bar\theta_iM_{ij}\theta_j)^n.
$$
A nonzero term uses each barred index and each unbarred index once. Let $\sigma,\tau$ denote their permutations. Reordering the Grassmann variables supplies
$\operatorname{sgn}\sigma\,\operatorname{sgn}\tau$.
Choose the measure convention
$$
\int d\bar\theta_i\,d\theta_i\,\bar\theta_i\theta_i=-1,
$$
which makes the one-pair Gaussian equal to its matrix coefficient. The measure sign cancels the Taylor factor $(-1)^n$. Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\prod_i d\bar\theta_i\,d\theta_i\,
e^{-\bar\theta M\theta}
&=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma,\tau}
\operatorname{sgn}\sigma\,\operatorname{sgn}\tau
\prod_{k=1}^nM_{\sigma(k),\tau(k)}\\
&=\sum_\pi\operatorname{sgn}\pi
\prod_{k=1}^nM_{k,\pi(k)}
=\det M.
\end{aligned}
$$
For each relative permutation $\pi=\tau\circ\sigma^{-1}$, the sum has $n!$ choices of $\sigma$, canceling the Taylor factorial.

> [!note] Factorial and measure
> The source drops $1/n!$ in its displayed double-permutation sum and then writes $n!$ times the Leibniz sum as the determinant. The Taylor factorial must remain until the redundant permutation sum cancels it. Reversing the Berezin measure changes its orientation sign; the convention must match the stated Gaussian identity.
