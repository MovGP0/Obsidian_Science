## Exercise 11.1: Harmonic oscillator at finite temperature

Use the Wick rotation $\mechanical{t}\to-i\mechanical{\tau}$ to calculate the harmonic-oscillator partition function
$\signal{Z}=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{H}})$
as a Euclidean path integral over
$\mechanical{\tau}\in[0,\thermal{\beta}]$ with periodic boundary conditions.
Expand the coordinate $\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{\tau})$ in a Fourier series with Matsubara frequencies
$\signal{\omega}_n=2\pi n/\thermal{\beta}$.
Show that
$$
\signal{Z}=\prod_n\frac{1}{\sqrt{\signal{\omega}_n^2+\signal{\omega}^2}},
$$
then use the infinite-product formula to recover
$$
\signal{Z}=\left[2\sinh\left(\frac{\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}}{2}\right)\right]^{-1}.
$$
Check the result against the direct sum
$$
\signal{Z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
e^{-\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}(n+1/2)}.
$$

> [!note]
> The determinant product is formal until the path-integral normalization and the infinite-product regularization are specified. The final partition function uses $\mechanical{\hbar}=1$.

## Solution 11.1 — Harmonic oscillator at finite temperature

Under the Wick rotation $\mechanical{t}=-i\mechanical{\tau}$, $i\energy{S}=-\energy{S}_{\mathrm E}$, where
$$
\energy{S}_{\mathrm E}
=\int_0^{\thermal{\beta}}d\mechanical{\tau}
\left[
\frac{\mechanical{m}}2\dot{\mechanical{q}}^{\,2}
+\frac{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2}{2}\mechanical{q}^2
\right].
$$
The coordinate is periodic over the inverse thermal energy
$\thermal{\beta}$. Expand
$$
\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{\tau})
=\frac1{\sqrt{\thermal{\beta}}}
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\mechanical{q}_ne^{i\signal{\omega}_n\mechanical{\tau}},
\qquad
\signal{\omega}_n=\frac{2\pi n}{\thermal{\beta}},
\qquad
\mechanical{q}_{-n}=\mechanical{q}_n^*.
$$
Then
$$
\energy{S}_{\mathrm E}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}}2\sum_n
(\signal{\omega}_n^2+\signal{\omega}^2)|\mechanical{q}_n|^2.
$$
The formal Gaussian integral gives
$$
\signal{Z}
=\mathcal N\prod_{n\in\mathbb Z}
\frac1{\sqrt{\mechanical{m}(\signal{\omega}_n^2+\signal{\omega}^2)}}.
$$
Separating the zero mode and pairing $n,-n$, the frequency dependence is
$$
\signal{Z}
=\frac{\mathcal N'}{\signal{\omega}}
\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{\signal{\omega}_n^2+\signal{\omega}^2},
$$
where the remaining normalization includes the mass factors and the regulated measure.

> [!note] Zero-mode factor
> The source's second product starts at $n=1$ and puts everything else into $\mathcal N'$. The zero mode contributes $1/\signal{\omega}$, which cannot be absorbed into a frequency-independent normalization. It is retained explicitly here.

The source now explicitly identifies the $n=0$ contribution as $1/\signal{\omega}$.
Use the product identity
$$
\prod_{n=1}^\infty\left(1+\frac{x^2}{n^2}\right)
=\frac{\sinh(\pi x)}{\pi x}
$$
with $x=\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}/(2\pi)$:
$$
\prod_{n=1}^\infty
\frac{\signal{\omega}_n^2+\signal{\omega}^2}{\signal{\omega}_n^2}
=
\prod_{n=1}^\infty
\left(1+\frac{\thermal{\beta}^2\signal{\omega}^2}{4\pi^2n^2}\right)
=\frac{\sinh(\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}/2)}
{\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}/2}.
$$
With the canonically normalized path-integral measure, the frequency-independent factors give
$$
\signal{Z}
=\frac1{\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}}
\prod_{n=1}^\infty
\left(1+\frac{\thermal{\beta}^2\signal{\omega}^2}{4\pi^2n^2}\right)^{-1}
=\frac1{2\sinh(\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}/2)}.
$$
The direct energy-level sum verifies it:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z}
&=\sum_{n=0}^\infty
e^{-\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}(n+1/2)}\\
&=\frac{e^{-\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}/2}}
{1-e^{-\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}}}
=\frac1{e^{\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}/2}
-e^{-\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}/2}}\\
&=\frac1{2\sinh(\thermal{\beta}\signal{\omega}/2)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
