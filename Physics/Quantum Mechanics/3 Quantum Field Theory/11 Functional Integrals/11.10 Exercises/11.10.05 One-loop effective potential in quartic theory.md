## Exercise 11.5: One-loop effective action for φ⁴ theory

Consider a scalar theory with potential
$$
\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})
=\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\signal{\phi}^2
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{4!}\signal{\phi}^4.
$$
Expand the action about a constant configuration $\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}$:
$\signal{\phi}=\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}+\signal{\eta}$,
where $\signal{\eta}$ is the fluctuation field.
Perform the Gaussian functional integral over $\signal{\eta}$ to obtain the one-loop effective potential.
Use dimensional regularization with $d=4-2\varepsilon$.
The source gives
$$
\energy{V}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(1)}
=\frac{[\mechanical{m}^2+\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}^2/2]^2}
{64\pi^2}
\left[
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}-\frac{3}{2}
+\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^2+\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}^2/2}
{\energy{\mu}^2}
+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
\right].
$$
After renormalization in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme, recover the Coleman–Weinberg formula (11.16).

> [!note]
> The expression above preserves the source's signs. In the usual Euclidean determinant convention with $d=4-2\varepsilon$, the unrenormalized scalar contribution has a negative pole when the logarithm has the displayed positive sign. The source also suppresses the usual $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ constants. State the determinant and subtraction conventions when checking this formula.

## Solution 11.5 — One-loop effective potential in quartic theory

Expand about a constant background:
$$
\signal{\phi}=\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}+\signal{\eta},
\qquad
\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})
=\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\signal{\phi}^2
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{4!}\signal{\phi}^4.
$$
The background-dependent squared mass is
$$
\mechanical{M}^2(\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}})
=\energy{V}''(\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}})
=\mechanical{m}^2+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}2\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}^2.
$$
In Lorentzian form, the quadratic fluctuation action is
$$
\energy{S}[\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}+\signal{\eta}]
=\energy{S}[\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}]
+\frac12\int\signal{\eta}(-\Box-\mechanical{M}^2)\signal{\eta}
+\mathcal O(\signal{\eta}^3),
$$
after cancellation of the linear fluctuation term.

> [!note] Background condition
> For an arbitrary constant background, the action alone also has a linear term. In the effective-action construction the chosen source cancels it. Without that source, the displayed expansion requires a stationary background.

The Gaussian fluctuation integral gives
$$
\int\mathcal D\signal{\eta}\,
e^{\frac i2\int\signal{\eta}(-\Box-\mechanical{M}^2)\signal{\eta}}
\propto[\det(-\Box-\mechanical{M}^2)]^{-1/2},
$$
with the causal prescription understood. After Wick rotation, the one-loop potential density is
$$
\energy{V}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(1)}
=\frac12\energy{\mu}^{2\varepsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}}{(2\pi)^d}
\ln\frac{\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}^2+\mechanical{M}^2}{\energy{\mu}^2},
\qquad d=4-2\varepsilon,
$$
up to a field-independent normalization.

Here $\mathcal V$ denotes the spacetime volume. The Wick rotation is $\mechanical{k}^0=i\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}^0$. Dimensional regularization gives
$$
\energy{V}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(1)}
=-\frac{\mechanical{M}^4}{64\pi^2}
\left[
\frac1\varepsilon-\gamma_{\mathrm E}+\ln4\pi
-\ln\frac{\mechanical{M}^2}{\energy{\mu}^2}
+\frac32+\mathcal O(\varepsilon)
\right].
$$
The factor $\energy{\mu}^{2\varepsilon}$ in the preceding integral restores the four-dimensional unit. Subtracting $1/\varepsilon-\gamma_{\mathrm E}+\ln4\pi$ in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme gives
$$
\boxed{
\energy{V}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(1)}
=\frac{[\mechanical{m}^2+\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}^2/2]^2}{64\pi^2}
\left[
\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^2+\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}^2/2}{\energy{\mu}^2}
-\frac32
\right].
}
$$
The full potential through one loop is the classical potential plus this correction. The real logarithmic form assumes $\mechanical{M}^2>0$.

> [!note] Radiative symmetry breaking
> The source identifies this determinant as the Coleman–Weinberg form and states that the massless limit produces radiative symmetry breaking. The formula is the scalar one-loop contribution. For a single massless quartic scalar, a formal nonzero minimum requires large logarithms relative to the tree term, so this fixed-order expression alone does not establish a controlled broken vacuum. Other interactions and a consistent treatment of running couplings can change this conclusion.
