## Exercise 11.7: Schwinger–Dyson equation for the vertex

Use invariance of the QED functional integral under the translation
$\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})\to\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})+\electric{\epsilon}_\mu(\mechanical{x})$
to derive the SD equation for the photon propagator:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\Box g^{\mu\nu}-\partial^\mu\partial^\nu)
\electric{G}^{(2)}_{\mu\alpha}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
+(\text{gauge-fixing term})
=i\delta^\nu_\alpha\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
+(\text{source term}).
\end{aligned}
$$
In momentum space, check transversality of the photon self-energy,
$$
\mechanical{k}_\mu\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})=0,
$$
as required by the Ward identity.

> [!note]
> The source titles this exercise “for the vertex,” but its stated task concerns the photon propagator. The displayed SD equation is schematic: the gauge-fixing and current-insertion terms must be retained. Transversality also uses the gauge-symmetry Ward identity and a compatible regulator.

## Solution 11.7 — Schwinger–Dyson equation for the photon propagator

Keep the gauge-fixed quadratic operator throughout the variation. Its longitudinal part is needed for an inverse even though the physical polarization correction is transverse. The external source is a device for inserting a second photon field; the matter current is the dynamical fermion bilinear. Their roles in the differentiated identity are different.

Translation invariance of the functional measure gives
$$
0=\int\mathcal D\electric{A}\,
\frac{\delta}{\delta\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})}
\exp\left[
i\energy{S}_{\mathrm{QED}}
+i\int\electric{J}\cdot\electric{A}
+i\int(\bar\eta\signal{\psi}+\bar{\signal{\psi}}\eta)
\right],
$$
with the fermion integration understood. Here $\eta,\bar\eta$ are auxiliary Grassmann sources.

For the gauge-fixed Maxwell action,
$$
\energy{S}_{\mathrm{gf}}
=\int d^4\mechanical{x}
\left[-\frac14\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}
-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial\cdot\electric{A})^2\right],
$$
the interacting action derivative is
$$
\frac{\delta\energy{S}_{\mathrm{QED}}}{\delta\electric{A}_\mu}
=
\left[g^{\mu\nu}\Box
-\left(1-\frac1\xi\right)\partial^\mu\partial^\nu\right]\electric{A}_\nu
-\electric{J}_{\mathrm{mat}}^\mu,
\qquad
\electric{J}_{\mathrm{mat}}^\mu
=\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
Differentiate the functional identity once with respect to the photon source, then set the sources to zero. With
$\electric{G}^{(2)}_{\nu\alpha}=\langle T\electric{A}_\nu\electric{A}_\alpha\rangle$,
define the momentum kernel
$$
K^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\mechanical{k}^2g^{\mu\nu}
-\left(1-\frac1\xi\right)\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu.
$$
Absorbing the matter-current insertion into the proper polarization gives
$$
\left[K^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
-\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})\right]
\electric{G}^{(2)}_{\nu\alpha}(\mechanical{k})
=-i\delta^\mu_\alpha.
$$

> [!note] Fourier-kernel sign
> The supplied solution prints $+i\delta^\mu_\alpha$ on the right with the displayed positive-$\mechanical{k}^2$ kernel. For the stated time-ordered correlator and Fourier convention, the free Feynman-gauge propagator is $-ig_{\nu\alpha}/(\mechanical{k}^2+i0)$, so this kernel requires $-i\delta^\mu_\alpha$. The position-space operator in the exercise has the opposite overall Fourier sign.

### Transversality

The matter-current Ward identity implies
$$
\mechanical{k}_\mu\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})=0,
\qquad
\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=(g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu)
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{k}^2),
$$
with a regulator that preserves this identity. Inverting the transverse and longitudinal kernels gives
$$
\electric{G}^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-i}{\mechanical{k}^2[1-\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{k}^2)]}
\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{\mechanical{k}_\mu\mechanical{k}_\nu}{\mechanical{k}^2}\right)
-\frac{i\xi\,\mechanical{k}_\mu\mechanical{k}_\nu}{(\mechanical{k}^2)^2},
$$
with causal prescriptions understood. The longitudinal term is not dressed by the transverse polarization.

> [!note] Massless pole
> The source states that transversality leaves the photon massless at every order. In perturbative QED, the scalar polarization has no mass-generating $1/\mechanical{k}^2$ pole, so the photon pole remains at $\mechanical{k}^2=0$. Transversality alone, without this condition, is not a general proof of masslessness.

The zero-polarization limit of the final expression recombines its transverse and longitudinal pieces into the free covariant propagator (11.27). In Feynman gauge the metric numerator is recovered. Contracting the interacting result with the photon momentum leaves only the longitudinal term, which is proportional to the unchanged gauge parameter. Thus matter polarization dresses the transverse response without renormalizing that gauge-fixing kernel.

The polarization tensor has mass dimension two, while its scalar coefficient is dimensionless. The Fourier propagator has mass dimension minus two; the coordinate-space two-point function has dimension plus two. This distinction is needed when comparing the source-derivative equation with its momentum-space inverse. A truncation that creates a longitudinal polarization term fails the Ward check even if its scalar denominator can be inverted.
