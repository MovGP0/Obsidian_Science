## Exercise 11.6: Faddeev–Popov procedure in Coulomb gauge

Repeat the QED Faddeev–Popov procedure in Coulomb gauge,
$$
G(\electric{A})=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\electric{A}}=0.
$$
Show the following results.

(a) The FP determinant is $\det(-\nabla^2)$ and is independent of $\electric{A}$.

(b) The ghost fields decouple, as in Lorenz gauge.

(c) The Coulomb-gauge photon propagator is
$$
\begin{gathered}
\electric{D}^{00}(\mechanical{k})=\frac{i}{\lvert\mathbf{\mechanical{k}}\rvert^2},
\qquad
\electric{D}^{0i}(\mechanical{k})=0,\\
\electric{D}^{ij}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2+i\epsilon}
\left(\delta^{ij}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^i\mechanical{k}^j}{\lvert\mathbf{\mechanical{k}}\rvert^2}\right).
\end{gathered}
$$
Discuss why physical amplitudes remain Lorentz invariant although the propagator is not manifestly covariant.

## Solution 11.6 — Faddeev–Popov determinant in Coulomb gauge

Choose the gauge condition
$$
G[\electric{A}]=\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\electric{A}}=0.
$$
Under $\electric{A}_\mu\mapsto\electric{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha$,
$$
G[\electric{A}^{\alpha}]
=\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf{\electric{A}}+\nabla^2\alpha.
$$

### (a) Determinant

The Faddeev–Popov factor is
$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}
=\left.\det\frac{\delta G[\electric{A}^{\alpha}]}{\delta\alpha}\right|_{G=0}
=\det(\nabla^2).
$$
It is independent of the photon field and can be absorbed into the normalization. This statement assumes boundary conditions or a residual-gauge prescription that removes zero modes.

### (b) Ghost decoupling

Because $\nabla^2$ is field independent, the Abelian ghost fields have no photon interaction. Ghost loops therefore do not contribute to Green functions of the physical photon fields.

> [!note] Determinant sign
> The exercise uses $\det(-\nabla^2)$, whereas the supplied solution uses $\det(\nabla^2)$. The sign depends on the definition of the gauge function and gauge parameter. Both give a field-independent factor after the same zero-mode prescription.

### (c) Propagator and Lorentz invariance

Invert the quadratic Maxwell operator
$O^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}\Box-\partial^\mu\partial^\nu$
on fields with $\partial_i\electric{A}^i=0$. Its components are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{D}^{00}(\mechanical{k})
&=\frac{i}{\lvert\mathbf{\mechanical{k}}\rvert^2},
&
\electric{D}^{0i}(\mechanical{k})&=0,\\
\electric{D}^{ij}(\mechanical{k})
&=\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2+i0}
\left(\delta^{ij}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^i\mechanical{k}^j}
{\lvert\mathbf{\mechanical{k}}\rvert^2}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
The temporal component is instantaneous and gives the Coulomb interaction. The spatial component propagates transverse photons. The propagator is not manifestly Lorentz covariant. In physical amplitudes, gauge invariance combines the instantaneous and noncovariant transverse terms to give gauge-independent, Lorentz-invariant observables.
