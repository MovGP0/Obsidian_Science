## Exercise 11.8: Relation between Γ⁽²⁾ and the exact propagator

Use the definition of the effective action as the Legendre transform of $\signal{W}[\signal{J}]$ to prove relation (11.15):
$$
\int d^4\mechanical{z}\,
\frac{\delta^2\signal{W}}
{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{z})}
\frac{\delta^2\energy{\Gamma}}
{\delta\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mechanical{z})
 \delta\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mechanical{y})}
=-\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
The source writes the momentum-space relation as
$$
\signal{G}_c^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})\signal{\Gamma}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})=-1.
$$
It identifies the exact connected propagator with the inverse of the 1PI two-point function.

> [!note]
> The last equation uses $\signal{G}_c^{(2)}$ for the Fourier transform of $\delta^2\signal{W}/\delta\signal{J}^2$. With the chapter's Minkowski convention $\signal{W}=-i\ln\signal{Z}$, this derivative is $i$ times the connected time-ordered field correlator. Keep this factor when using “propagator” for the correlator itself.

Use the inverse-kernel relation to show that
$$
\signal{G}_c^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i}{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^2)}.
$$

> [!note]
> In this last formula, the source returns to using $\signal{G}_c^{(2)}$ for the time-ordered field correlator. This differs by a factor of $i$ from its use for the second source derivative of $\signal{W}$ in the preceding formula. The inverse relation must retain this distinction.

## Solution 11.8 — Effective-action Hessian and exact propagator

The Legendre transform is
$$
\energy{\Gamma}[\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}]
=\signal{W}[\signal{J}]
-\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mechanical{x}),
\qquad
\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mechanical{x})
=\frac{\delta\signal{W}}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})}.
$$
It follows that
$$
\frac{\delta\energy{\Gamma}}{\delta\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mechanical{x})}
=-\signal{J}(\mechanical{x}).
$$
Differentiate with respect to $\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})$:
$$
\int d^4\mechanical{z}\,
\frac{\delta^2\energy{\Gamma}}
{\delta\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mechanical{x})
\delta\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mechanical{z})}
\frac{\delta\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mechanical{z})}
{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})}
=-\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
With $\signal{G}^{(2)}_c=\delta^2\signal{W}/\delta\signal{J}^2$ and
$\signal{\Gamma}^{(2)}=\delta^2\energy{\Gamma}/\delta\signal{\phi}_{\mathrm{cl}}^2$,
$$
\boxed{\int d^4\mechanical{z}\,
\signal{\Gamma}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{z})
\signal{G}^{(2)}_c(\mechanical{z},\mechanical{y})
=-\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).}
$$
Translation invariance turns the convolution into
$$
\signal{\Gamma}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})\signal{G}^{(2)}_c(\mechanical{p})=-1.
$$
At tree level, in the stated Hessian convention,
$$
\signal{\Gamma}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})=\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2,
\qquad
\signal{G}^{(2)}_c(\mechanical{p})=-\frac1{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0}.
$$
More generally,
$$
\signal{\Gamma}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})
=\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^2),
\qquad
\signal{G}^{(2)}_c(\mechanical{p})
=-\frac1{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^2)+i0}.
$$
The connected time-ordered field correlator, denoted here by $\signal{D}_{\mathrm{exact}}$, is instead
$$
\signal{D}_{\mathrm{exact}}(\mechanical{p})
=\frac1i\signal{G}^{(2)}_c(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i}{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^2)+i0}.
$$

> [!note] Factors of i in the supplied solution
> The source gives $i(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)$ for the proper two-point function and $i/(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)$ for the propagator. Their product is $-1$, but these are the proper vertex with its extra $i$ and the time-ordered correlator. They are not the two functional derivatives defined in the preceding Legendre-transform proof. With $\signal{W}=-i\ln\signal{Z}$, its second derivative is $i$ times the connected correlator.
