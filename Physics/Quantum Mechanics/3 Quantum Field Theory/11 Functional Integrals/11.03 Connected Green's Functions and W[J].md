---
title: "Connected Green's Functions and W[J]"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The logarithm of the generating functional isolates connected Green functions.

The generating functional $\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]$ produces all Green functions, including disconnected contributions.

The purpose of the logarithm is to remove products of independent clusters without discarding correlations inside each cluster. This is the same combinatorial role as a cumulant generator, with the Lorentzian factors of i kept explicit. If the zero-source functional has not been normalized to one, its logarithm also supplies a source-independent constant. The expansion below omits that constant; source derivatives are unaffected.
Connected Green functions are generated by
$$
\signal{W}[\signal{J}]=-i\ln\signal{Z}[\signal{J}].
\tag{11.11}
$$

This logarithmic relation is analogous to the statistical-mechanical relation between partition function and free energy,
$\energy{F}=-\thermal{k_B}\thermal{T}\ln\signal{Z}$.
The Lorentzian sign convention is fixed by
$\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]=e^{i\signal{W}[\signal{J}]}$.

To see why $\signal{W}$ generates connected functions, expand it:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{W}[\signal{J}]
=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{i^{n-1}}{n!}
\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x_1}\cdots
\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x_n}\,
\signal{G_c}^{(n)}(\mechanical{x_1},\ldots,\mechanical{x_n})\\
{}\times\signal{J}(\mechanical{x_1})\cdots
\signal{J}(\mechanical{x_n}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The coefficients $\signal{G_c}^{(n)}$ are the **connected Green functions**.
The first cases demonstrate the relation.

## Vacuum field

The first derivative is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\delta\signal{W}[\signal{J}]}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})}
&=\frac1i\frac{\delta\ln\signal{Z}}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})}\\
&=\frac1{\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]}
\frac{\delta\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]}{i\,\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})}\\
&=\langle\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\rangle_{\signal{J}}
\equiv\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{x}).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the field expectation value in the presence of the source, called the **classical field**.

The word classical names the mean-field argument; it does not remove quantum corrections from this expectation value. A classical saddle and the exact source-dependent mean field agree at leading order in the loop expansion, but they need not agree beyond that order.

## Connected propagator

The second derivative gives the connected two-point function, as developed below.

Using
$\delta^2\signal{W}/(\delta\signal{J}\delta\signal{J})
=i\signal{G_c}^{(2)}$, the second derivative gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1i\frac{\delta^2\signal{W}}
{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})}
={}&\langle\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\rangle_{\signal{J}}\\
&-\langle\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\rangle_{\signal{J}}
\langle\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\rangle_{\signal{J}}\\
={}&\signal{G_c}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the connected two-point Green function: the correlated part after subtracting the product of one-point expectation values.
Time ordering is understood in these Minkowski functional-integral correlators.

## General relation

The full and connected functions obey the cluster relation
$$
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})
=\signal{G_c}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})
+\signal{G_c}^{(1)}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{G_c}^{(1)}(\mechanical{y}).
$$
More generally, a full $\signal{G}^{(n)}$ is a sum of products of connected
$\signal{G_c}^{(k)}$ with $k\le n$.
Sum over all partitions of the insertion set
$\{\mechanical{x_1},\ldots,\mechanical{x_n}\}$.

## Example 11.5: Free scalar field

For the free field,
$$
\signal{W_0}[\signal{J}]
=-\frac12\int\signal{J}\signal{\Delta_F}\signal{J}.
$$
Consequently,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G_{c,0}}^{(1)}(\mechanical{x})
&=\left.
\frac{\delta\signal{W_0}}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})}
\right|_{\signal{J}=0}=0,\\
\signal{G_{c,0}}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})
&=\left.\frac1i
\frac{\delta^2\signal{W_0}}
{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})}
\right|_{\signal{J}=0}\\
&=i\signal{\Delta_F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
$$
All connected functions of order three or higher vanish in the free theory.
The propagator therefore determines the free field completely.
Interactions generate connected functions of higher order.

Use the free example as a factor-of-i test: the second derivative of $\signal{W_0}$ is $-\signal{\Delta_F}$, and division by i gives the correlator $i\signal{\Delta_F}$. Its fourth derivative is zero even though the full four-point function has three nonzero pair products. The exponential $e^{i\signal{W_0}}$ reconstructs those products. Thus a nonzero full four-point function alone is not evidence of a four-field interaction.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Full generating functional | The source functional containing connected and disconnected contributions. |
| $\signal{W}$ | $1$ | Connected generating functional | Minus i times the logarithm of the full functional in natural units. |
| $\signal{J}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | External scalar source | The source coupled to the four-dimensional scalar field. |
| $\energy{F}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Helmholtz free energy | The statistical-mechanical analogue of the logarithmic generating functional. |
| $\thermal{k_B}$ | $\unit{MeV\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant relating temperature to thermal energy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The temperature in the statistical partition-function relation. |
| $\signal{G}^{(n)},\signal{G_c}^{(n)},\signal{G_c}^{(1)},\signal{G_c}^{(2)},\signal{G_{c,0}}^{(1)},\signal{G_{c,0}}^{(2)}$ | — | Full and connected Green functions | A position-space scalar n-point function has mass dimension n in four dimensions; the subscript 0 denotes the free theory. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{x_i}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinates | The positions of source derivatives and correlation-function insertions. |
| $\signal{\phi},\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field and its expectation value | The quantum field and the source-dependent classical field. |
| $\signal{W_0}$ | $1$ | Free connected generating functional | The quadratic logarithmic source functional of the free scalar field. |
| $\signal{\Delta_F}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Position-space inverse quadratic kernel | Its multiple iΔF is the free two-point correlator. |
