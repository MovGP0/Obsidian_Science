---
title: "Grassmann Variables"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Grassmann generators anticommute and square to zero. A function of one generator has only a constant and a linear term.

Fermion fields in a functional integral require anticommuting numbers, called **Grassmann variables**.
A set of $n$ generators $\{\theta_i\}$ satisfies
$$
\{\theta_i,\theta_j\}
=\theta_i\theta_j+\theta_j\theta_i=0,
\qquad\text{for all }i,j.
\tag{11.17}
$$
In particular, $\theta_i^2=0$: each generator is nilpotent.
A function of one Grassmann variable therefore terminates at first order:
$$
f(\theta)=a+b\theta.
$$

All higher powers vanish.
For two independent Grassmann variables $\theta$ and $\bar\theta$,
$$
f(\theta,\bar\theta)
=a+b\theta+c\bar\theta+d\theta\bar\theta.
$$
