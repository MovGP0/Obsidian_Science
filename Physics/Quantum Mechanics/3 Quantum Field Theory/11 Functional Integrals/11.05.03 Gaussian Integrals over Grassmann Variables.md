---
title: "Gaussian Integrals over Grassmann Variables"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A Gaussian integral over paired Grassmann variables gives a determinant, unlike the inverse determinant produced by commuting Gaussian variables.

Grassmann Gaussian integrals have a different structure from bosonic Gaussian integrals.
For two independent generators $\theta,\bar\theta$ and an ordinary coefficient $a$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\mathrm{d}\bar\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,
e^{-a\bar\theta\theta}
&=\int\mathrm{d}\bar\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,
(1-a\bar\theta\theta)\\
&=a.
\end{aligned}
$$

The paired Grassmann Gaussian is proportional to $a$.
An ordinary real-variable Gaussian instead gives a factor proportional to $a^{-1/2}$.

For $n$ pairs $\bar\theta_i,\theta_j$ and a matrix $M$,
$$
\int\prod_i\mathrm{d}\bar\theta_i\,\mathrm{d}\theta_i\,
\exp(-\bar\theta_iM_{ij}\theta_j)
=\det M.
\tag{11.19}
$$
With Grassmann sources $\bar\eta_i,\eta_j$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\int\prod_i\mathrm{d}\bar\theta_i\,\mathrm{d}\theta_i\,
\exp(-\bar\theta M\theta+\bar\eta\theta+\bar\theta\eta)\\
&\qquad=(\det M)\exp(\bar\eta M^{-1}\eta).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.20}
$$
These are fermionic analogues of the commuting Gaussian formulas.
For paired complex commuting variables, $(\det M)^{-1}$ is replaced by $\det M$.

## Example 11.6: Fermionic versus bosonic determinants

For a real scalar field with quadratic operator $\mathcal{O}$,
$$
\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}\,
\exp\left(-\frac12\signal{\phi}\mathcal{O}\signal{\phi}\right)
=(\det\mathcal{O})^{-1/2},
$$
with normalization and spacetime contractions understood.
The source compares this with a Dirac field, writing
$$
\int\mathcal{D}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\mathcal{D}\signal{\psi}\,
\exp(-\bar{\signal{\psi}}\mathcal{O}\signal{\psi})
=\det\mathcal{O}.
$$
The determinant power is $+1$ rather than $-1/2$.
Fermion integration therefore gives a determinant instead of an inverse determinant.
The source connects these opposite powers with ultraviolet cancellations between supersymmetric partners.

> [!note]
> The comparison concerns the determinant pattern, not identical physical kinetic operators: a scalar and a Dirac field have different operator structures and degrees of freedom. Real and complex bosonic integrals also have different determinant powers. Supersymmetric cancellations require the corresponding matched field content and interactions; the determinant sign alone does not guarantee cancellation of every ultraviolet divergence.
