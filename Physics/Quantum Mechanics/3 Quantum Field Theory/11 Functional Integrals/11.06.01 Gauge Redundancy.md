---
title: "Gauge Redundancy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Gauge-related fields describe the same physical configuration. Directly integrating over all of them introduces redundant gauge volume.

Writing a gauge-field functional integral introduces a structural difficulty. The QED Lagrangian provides the starting example.

For the electromagnetic field,
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}
=-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu},
\qquad
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}
=\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu-\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\mu.
$$
The gauge transformation
$\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})
\to\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})
+\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x})$
leaves the Lagrangian unchanged.

The field tensor is unchanged because partial derivatives commute: the two second derivatives of the gauge function cancel in its antisymmetric curl. This is a redundancy of the potential description. It differs from a global symmetry that can map a physical state to a distinct state with different charge quantum numbers.

Integrating over all potentials counts the same physical configuration repeatedly, once for each gauge function.
Formally, the gauge-group volume is infinite:
$$
\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{G})
=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x})=\infty.
$$
The quadratic operator
$\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}\square-\partial^\mu\partial^\nu$
has zero modes of the form $\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}$.
Consequently, $\det\mathcal{O}=0$, and an ordinary inverse defining the photon propagator does not exist before gauge fixing.

The same derivative cancellation verifies the zero mode directly: applying the quadratic operator to the gradient of a smooth gauge function gives zero. A Gaussian inverse cannot exist on a space containing this nontrivial kernel. Dividing by a common formal gauge volume is therefore not, by itself, a prescription for inverting the operator; one must specify a slice or restrict to a suitable subspace.

Here gauge functions are assumed to preserve the boundary conditions of the problem. Boundary transformations and residual zero modes require separate treatment. This qualification matters in finite regions, where not every transformation of the potential is an allowed redundancy of the same boundary-value problem.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal{L}}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Electromagnetic Lagrangian density | The gauge-invariant quadratic photon Lagrangian. |
| $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Electromagnetic field tensor | The curl of the photon potential. |
| $\electric{A}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon potential | The field with redundant gauge-related configurations. |
| $\signal{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Gauge function | The arbitrary scalar function whose gradient shifts the potential. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinate | The coordinate argument of the gauge fields and transformation. |
