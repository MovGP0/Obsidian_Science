---
title: "Effective Potential"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For a constant mean field, the effective action defines an effective potential. Its minima describe quantum vacuum expectation values.

A homogeneous vacuum has no preferred position or time. Evaluating the effective action on a constant scalar background therefore isolates a potential-energy density. This is the appropriate reduction for comparing homogeneous vacua. It leaves out derivative terms needed for spatially varying fields and propagation.

For a spatially and temporally constant field
$\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{x})
=\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}$,
the effective action factors as
$$
\energy{\Gamma}[\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}]
=-\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\energy{V_{\mathrm{eff}}}(\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}).
$$
The **effective potential** is useful for studying spontaneous symmetry breaking at the quantum level.
Its minimum determines the vacuum field expectation value.

For $\signal{\phi}^4$ theory, the tree-level potential is
$$
\energy{V}^{(0)}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\phi}^2}{2}
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi}^4}{4!}.
$$
The one-loop correction comes from Gaussian fluctuations around the mean field:
$$
\energy{V_{\mathrm{eff}}}^{(1)}
=\frac12\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{k_E}}{(2\pi)^4}
\ln\big(\mechanical{k_E}^2+V''(\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}})\big).
$$
The second derivative of the tree potential is the squared mass in the fluctuation determinant. A positive value gives damped low-momentum Euclidean modes. The real logarithmic formula below assumes this value is positive; continuing it through negative eigenvalues requires a contour and stability analysis, not an ordinary real Gaussian integral.

After regularization and subtraction, the source gives the **Coleman–Weinberg formula**
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\energy{V_{\mathrm{eff}}}^{(1)}(\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}})
={}&\frac{[\mechanical{m}^2+
\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}^2/2]^2}{64\pi^2}\\
&\times\left[
\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^2+
\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}^2/2}{\energy{\mu}^2}
-\frac32\right].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{11.16}
$$

> [!note]
> In the intermediate determinant integral, the logarithm of a dimensionful kernel is shorthand for a ratio to a reference squared-mass scale. Field-independent terms can be absorbed in the vacuum normalization. Equation (11.16) uses the usual finite constant of the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scalar result; other subtraction conditions can change finite terms.

Several checks delimit this result. It is even in the scalar background, as required by field parity. Every term has mass dimension four, and the logarithm has a dimensionless argument. At zero coupling, the one-loop term is independent of the background, so it does not change the free field equation.

A stationary point tests the first derivative; a positive curvature tests local homogeneous stability. Neither test establishes the accuracy of a loop truncation when logarithms or coupling corrections are large. The curvature also differs in general from the pole mass, which requires the momentum-dependent two-point kernel. [[11.10.05 One-loop effective potential in quartic theory]] traces the subtraction and explains the limitation of a radiative-minimum claim for a single quartic scalar.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\Gamma}$ | $1$ | Effective action | The constant-field action whose spacetime density defines the effective potential. |
| $\energy{V_{\mathrm{eff}}},\energy{V_{\mathrm{eff}}}^{(1)},\energy{V}^{(0)}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Effective and tree-level potential densities | The scalar vacuum-energy density and its tree and one-loop contributions. |
| $\signal{\phi},\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field and mean field | The field values in the scalar potential. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar mass | The mass parameter in the field-dependent fluctuation mass. |
| $\signal{\lambda}$ | $1$ | Quartic scalar coupling | The dimensionless interaction coefficient in four dimensions. |
| $\mechanical{k_E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Euclidean loop momentum | The integration momentum in the one-loop determinant. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The reference scale of the logarithm in the renormalized potential. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinate | The coordinate integrated in the constant-field action. |
