---
title: "The Faddeev–Popov Procedure"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Faddeev–Popov procedure inserts a gauge condition and its Jacobian so that the functional integral can be restricted to gauge representatives.

Faddeev and Popov developed the procedure in 1967.
The idea is to integrate over a gauge slice through configuration space, selecting a representative from each gauge orbit.

## Faddeev–Popov identity

Choose a gauge condition $G(A)=0$.
For example, $G(A)=\partial^\mu\electric{A}_\mu$ gives Lorenz gauge.
Define the Faddeev–Popov determinant $\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]$ through
$$
1=\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]
\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x})\,
\delta[G(A^\alpha)].
\tag{11.24}
$$
Here
$\electric{A}_\mu^\alpha
=\electric{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}$
is the gauge-transformed potential.
The functional delta selects the gauge function that brings the potential to the slice.

For the shifted Lorenz condition
$G(A)=\partial^\mu\electric{A}_\mu-\omega(\mechanical{x})$,
$$
G(A^\alpha)=\partial^\mu\electric{A}_\mu+\square\signal{\alpha}-\omega.
$$
Setting this to zero gives
$\signal{\alpha}=\square^{-1}
(\omega-\partial^\mu\electric{A}_\mu)$.
The functional derivative is
$$
\left.
\frac{\delta G(A^\alpha)}{\delta\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{y})}
\right|_{G=0}
=\square_{\mechanical{x}}\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$

> [!note]
> This identity assumes an appropriate local gauge slice and boundary conditions, with residual gauge zero modes handled separately. A globally unique representative is not automatic for a general gauge theory.

Thus, in QED,
$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]=\det\square.
$$
It is independent of the photon field and can be absorbed into the normalization.

## Factor out the gauge volume

Insert (11.24) into the functional integral:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z}
&=\int\mathcal{D}\electric{A}_\mu\,
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]
\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\alpha}\,
\delta[G(A^\alpha)]\,e^{i\energy{S}[A]}\\
&=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\alpha}
\int\mathcal{D}\electric{A}_\mu\,
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]\,
\delta[G(A^\alpha)]\,e^{i\energy{S}[A]}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Change the integration variable by the inverse gauge transformation,
$\electric{A}_\mu\to\electric{A}^{\alpha^{-1}}_\mu
=\electric{A}_\mu-\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}$,
and then rename the field.
The action, measure, and Faddeev–Popov determinant are gauge invariant, so
$$
\signal{Z}
=\underbrace{\left(\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\alpha}\right)}_{
\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{G})}
\int\mathcal{D}\electric{A}_\mu\,
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]\,
\delta[G(A)]\,e^{i\energy{S}[A]}.
$$
The gauge volume factors out.
It is a common constant that cancels from normalized Green functions.
After removing it,
$$
\signal{Z}
=\int\mathcal{D}\electric{A}_\mu\,
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]\,
\delta[G(A)]\,e^{i\energy{S}[A]}.
\tag{11.25}
$$

## Average over gauge conditions

To obtain an explicit propagator, instead of imposing precisely $G(A)=0$, average over choices of the Lorenz condition with a Gaussian weight.
Integrate over $\omega(\mechanical{x})$ with weight
$\exp[-i\int\omega^2/(2\signal{\xi})]$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z}
={}&\int\mathcal{D}\omega\,
\exp\left(-\frac{i}{2\signal{\xi}}\int\omega^2\right)\\
&\times\int\mathcal{D}\electric{A}_\mu\,
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]\,
\delta[\partial^\mu\electric{A}_\mu-\omega]\,
e^{i\energy{S}[A]}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The Gaussian average gives
$$
\signal{Z}
=\int\mathcal{D}\electric{A}_\mu\,
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]\,
\exp\left[
i\energy{S}[A]
-\frac{i}{2\signal{\xi}}\int
(\partial^\mu\electric{A}_\mu)^2
\right].
\tag{11.26}
$$
The resulting gauge-fixed action is
$$
\energy{S_{\mathrm{gf}}}[A]
=\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{x}\,
\left[
-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
-\frac1{2\signal{\xi}}(\partial^\mu\electric{A}_\mu)^2
\right].
$$
Its quadratic operator is
$$
\mathcal{O}^{\mu\nu}
=g^{\mu\nu}\square
-\left(1-\frac1{\signal{\xi}}\right)\partial^\mu\partial^\nu.
$$
The inverse gives the photon propagator in covariant $R_\xi$ gauge:
$$
\signal{D_F}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-i}{\mechanical{k}^2+i\epsilon}
\left[
g^{\mu\nu}
-(1-\signal{\xi})
\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^2}
\right].
\tag{11.27}
$$
Common choices are $\signal{\xi}=1$ for **Feynman gauge** and the
$\signal{\xi}\to0$ limit for **Landau gauge**.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{A}_\mu,\electric{A}_\mu^\alpha$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Original and gauge-transformed photon potentials | The gauge-related fields in the gauge-fixing condition. |
| $\signal{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Gauge function | The variable integrated along the gauge orbit. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinates | The arguments of the gauge condition and its functional derivative. |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Gauge-fixed generating functional | The field integral after removal of the common gauge volume. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Electromagnetic action | The gauge-invariant action in natural units. |
| $\signal{\xi}$ | $1$ | Covariant gauge parameter | The dimensionless parameter in the Gaussian gauge-condition weight. |
| $\energy{S_{\mathrm{gf}}}$ | $1$ | Gauge-fixed electromagnetic action | The action including the covariant gauge-fixing term. |
| $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Electromagnetic field tensor | The tensor in the photon kinetic term. |
| $\signal{D_F}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Momentum-space photon propagator | The inverse quadratic kernel including the conventional factor −i. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon momentum | The Fourier momentum in the covariant propagator. |
