---
title: "Ward Identities from the Functional Integral"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Ward–Takahashi identities relate Green functions through the symmetries of the theory. They follow from symmetry changes of variables in the functional integral.

Ward–Takahashi identities are relations between Green functions that express the consequences of the theory's symmetries. The functional-integral formalism gives them directly.

The physical question is which correlations and amplitudes are allowed by a symmetry, even before their detailed dynamics is solved. Schwinger–Dyson equations use arbitrary field shifts. Ward identities instead exploit a transformation with a known action and measure variation, so they constrain how different correlators fit together.

[[11.08.01 Global Symmetry and the Noether Current]] explains why a conserved current has contact terms when it meets an insertion. [[11.08.02 The Ward–Takahashi Identity for QED]] applies the same principle to the electron vertex and propagator. The transformation, boundary conditions, and regulated measure must belong to the same quantum theory. An invariant classical Lagrangian is not sufficient if its proposed quantum measure fails the symmetry.

Use a Ward identity as a consistency test, rather than as a numerical prediction of every amplitude. In QED, contract the vertex with the photon momentum and compare with the difference of inverse electron propagators. At tree level this reduces to the elementary gamma-matrix identity. At loop level it tests that vertices, self-energies, and subtractions use compatible conventions.
