---
title: "Berezin Integral"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Berezin integration equals Grassmann differentiation. It selects the coefficient of the integrated generator and transforms with an inverse determinant.

Berezin defined differentiation and integration for Grassmann variables so that the formal rules remain similar to ordinary calculus.
The **left derivative** satisfies
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta_j}{\mathrm{d}\theta_i}=\delta_{ij},
\qquad
\frac{\mathrm{d}1}{\mathrm{d}\theta_i}=0,
\qquad
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\theta_i}(\theta_j\theta_k)
=\delta_{ij}\theta_k-\theta_j\delta_{ik}.
$$
Define the integral to reproduce integration by parts without boundary terms:
$$
\int\mathrm{d}\theta\,1=0,
\qquad
\int\mathrm{d}\theta\,\theta=1.
\tag{11.18}
$$
Integration and differentiation therefore give the same operation on one generator.

For several variables, use the ordered exterior-product measure.
With the source's order,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\mathrm{d}\bar\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,
f(\theta,\bar\theta)
&=\int\mathrm{d}\bar\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,
(a+b\theta+c\bar\theta+d\theta\bar\theta)\\
&=d.
\end{aligned}
$$
Only the term containing both generators survives.

## Change of variables

For a scaling $\theta\to c\theta$ with ordinary commuting $c$,
the measure transforms as
$\mathrm{d}\theta\to c^{-1}\mathrm{d}\theta$.
This is inverse to the ordinary commuting-variable Jacobian.

For a linear transformation $\theta_i\to M_{ij}\theta_j$,
$$
\mathrm{d}^n\theta\to(\det M)^{-1}\mathrm{d}^n\theta.
$$
