---
title: "Derivation for the Scalar Field"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Translation invariance of the scalar-field integration measure produces a functional identity relating the action derivative and the source.

The functional identity is an integration-by-parts relation over field configurations. It does not require each fluctuating configuration to satisfy the classical field equation. Work first with a regulated measure and the vacuum convergence prescription, so that the integral of the displayed total derivative has no field-space boundary contribution.

Make an infinitesimal change of integration variable,
$\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
\to\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})+\signal{\epsilon}(\mechanical{x})$.
The measure is translation invariant, so the integral is unchanged:
$$
0=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}\,
\frac{\delta}{\delta\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})}
\left[
\exp\left(i\energy{S}[\signal{\phi}]
+i\int\signal{J}\signal{\phi}\right)
\right].
$$
After differentiating and removing the common factor $i$,
$$
0=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}\,
\left[
\frac{\delta\energy{S}}{\delta\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})}
+\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})
\right]
\exp\left(i\energy{S}[\signal{\phi}]
+i\int\signal{J}\signal{\phi}\right).
$$
Since
$\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]
=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}\,
e^{i(\energy{S}+\int\signal{J}\signal{\phi})}$,
field insertions in the action derivative can be written as source derivatives of $\signal{Z}$.

Thus,
$$
\left[
\left.\frac{\delta\energy{S}}{\delta\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})}
\right|_{\signal{\phi}\to\frac{1}{i}\frac{\delta}{\delta\signal{J}}}
+\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})
\right]\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]=0.
\tag{11.31}
$$
For scalar $\signal{\phi}^4$ theory,
$$
\frac{\delta\energy{S}}{\delta\signal{\phi}}
=-(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{\phi}
-\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{6}\signal{\phi}^3.
$$
The SD equation becomes
$$
\left[
-(\Box_x+\mechanical{m}^2)\frac{1}{i}
\frac{\delta}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})}
-\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{6}
\left(\frac{1}{i}\frac{\delta}{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})}\right)^3
+\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})
\right]\signal{Z}[\signal{J}]=0.
$$
Differentiate with respect to
$\signal{J}(\mechanical{y}_1)\cdots \signal{J}(\mechanical{y}_n)$, then set $\signal{J}=0$.
This gives a hierarchy that relates $n$-point Green functions to functions of lower or higher order.
For the propagator, differentiate once with respect to $\signal{J}(\mechanical{y})$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\Box_x+\mechanical{m}^2)
\langle0|T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{6}
\langle0|T\{\signal{\phi}^3(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=-i\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.32}
$$
The delta function on the right of (11.32) comes from differentiating the explicit source multiplying the functional. One derivative acts on that source and the other terms supply field insertions. Dropping the source before differentiating would lose this contact term. In the operator formulation, it is the corresponding equal-time term generated by differentiating a time-ordered product.

This is the quantum equation of motion.
The interaction modifies the free-field equation
$(\Box_x+\mechanical{m}^2)\langle\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\rangle
=-i\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})$.
The SD equation relates $\signal{G}^{(2)}$ to $\signal{G}^{(4)}$, which in turn relates to higher functions.
The result is an infinite hierarchy of coupled equations.

## Diagrammatic structure

The diagrammatic SD equation expresses the exact propagator as the sum of the free propagator and all self-energy contributions:
$$
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})
=i\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{p})
+\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{p})\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^2)
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p}).
$$
Therefore,
$$
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i}{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^2)}.
$$
This is the standard exact propagator obtained by Dyson resummation; see Fig. 11.5.

> [!note]
> The coincident product $\signal{\phi}^3(\mechanical{x})$ is a composite operator. In the interacting theory, the formal hierarchy requires a regulator and consistent renormalization. The propagator formulas above suppress the usual $i\epsilon$ prescription.

Set the quartic coupling to zero in (11.32). The free correlator $i\signal{\Delta}_F$ then gives exactly the $-i$ delta function of the equation, with the chapter's numerator-one definition of the inverse kernel. The Dyson relation also passes a direct algebraic check: moving the self-energy term to the left gives a denominator shifted by minus the self-energy. Expanding that inverse to first order reproduces one self-energy insertion.

Solving the hierarchy approximately requires stating which higher functions are retained or modeled. A self-consistent two-point truncation can sum infinitely many diagrams while still omit classes of higher vertices. Its self-consistency is not, by itself, a proof of accuracy or gauge-symmetry preservation.

| $\signal{\phi},\signal{\epsilon}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field and infinitesimal field shift | The integration variable and its translation variation. |
| $\signal{J}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | External scalar source | The source coupled to the field in the generating functional. |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Generating functional | The source functional that obeys the Schwinger–Dyson identity. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Scalar action | The action differentiated under the path integral in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinate | The coordinate of the functional variation. |

| $\mechanical{y},\mechanical{y}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{y}_n$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinates | The positions of the other field insertions. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momentum | The momentum of the scalar propagator. |
| $\signal{G}^{(n)}$ | — | Green functions | The units depend on the number of fields and the coordinate or momentum representation. |
| $\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{p})$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Free scalar propagator | The momentum-space propagator without its numerator factor i. |
| $\energy{\Sigma}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Scalar self-energy | The correction to the inverse scalar propagator. |
