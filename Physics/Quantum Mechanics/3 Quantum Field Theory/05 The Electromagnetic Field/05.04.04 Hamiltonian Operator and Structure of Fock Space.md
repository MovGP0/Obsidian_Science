title: "Hamiltonian Operator and Structure of Fock Space"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The transverse photon Hamiltonian is a sum of positive-energy bosonic occupation terms for two physical polarizations.

The normal-ordered Hamiltonian is
$$
\energy{H}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}
\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)\dagger}a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)}.
$$
This has the scalar-field Hamiltonian form, summed over two transverse polarizations. A one-photon state is
$$
|\mechanical{\mathbf k},\lambda\rangle
=a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)\dagger}|0\rangle.
$$
The photon is massless: $\mechanical{p}^{\,2}=\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}_\mu=0$ and $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}=\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert$. It has spin one, with helicities $\pm1$.

> [!note] Remark 5.3 — Covariance problem
> Coulomb-gauge quantization is physically transparent because its Fock space contains only physical states. However, the transverse decomposition depends on the reference frame. The photon propagator contains $P_{ij}^{T}$ and is not manifestly covariant. Covariance of physical results is recovered in the complete calculation and is not generally manifest in each diagram.
