---
title: "Inversion of the Kinetic Operator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The kinetic operator is diagonal in transverse and longitudinal sectors, so its inverse follows by inverting two scalar coefficients.

Integrate (5.13) by parts to obtain the quadratic form
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\frac12\electric{A}_\mu O^{\mu\nu}\electric{A}_\nu
=\frac12\electric{A}_\mu
\left[\Box g^{\mu\nu}
-\left(1-\frac1\xi\right)\partial^\mu\partial^\nu\right]\electric{A}_\nu.
$$
In Fourier space, $\partial^\mu\to i\mechanical{k}^\mu$, so
$$
O^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=-\mechanical{k}^{\,2}g^{\mu\nu}
+\left(1-\frac1\xi\right)\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu.
$$
Use
$$
T^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}},
\qquad
L^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}},
$$
which satisfy
$$
T^{\mu\rho}T_{\rho\nu}=T^\mu{}_\nu,\qquad
L^{\mu\rho}L_{\rho\nu}=L^\mu{}_\nu,\qquad
T^{\mu\rho}L_{\rho\nu}=0.
$$
Then
$$
O^{\mu\nu}
=-\mechanical{k}^{\,2}T^{\mu\nu}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}{\xi}L^{\mu\nu},
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
[O^{-1}]^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
&=-\frac1{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}T^{\mu\nu}
-\frac{\xi}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}L^{\mu\nu}\\
&=-\frac1{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}
\left[g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}\right]
-\frac{\xi}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}
\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Insert the Feynman prescription $\mechanical{k}^{\,2}\to\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon$ and the factor $i$ to obtain (5.21).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Lagrangian density | The gauge-fixed quadratic electromagnetic density. |
| $\electric{A}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-potential | The field on which the kinetic operator acts. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momentum | The momentum used in the projector decomposition. |
