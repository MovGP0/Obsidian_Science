---
title: "The Maxwell Lagrangian"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Maxwell Lagrangian yields the electromagnetic equations of motion and introduces a constrained canonical structure.

The Lagrangian density that reproduces Maxwell's equations is
$$
\boxed{\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Maxwell}}
=-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}}. \tag{5.7}
$$
The fundamental field is $\electric{A}^\mu$. Variation with respect to $\electric{A}_\nu$ gives
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\electric{A}_\nu}
-\partial_\mu
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu)}
=0.
$$
The free density depends only on derivatives of the potential. Since
$$
\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}
=(\partial_\rho\electric{A}_\sigma-\partial_\sigma\electric{A}_\rho)
(\partial^\rho\electric{A}^\sigma-\partial^\sigma\electric{A}^\rho)
$$
and
$$
\frac{\partial(\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma})}
{\partial(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu)}
=4\electric{F}^{\mu\nu},
$$
the Euler–Lagrange equations are $\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=0$.

With sources, add $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\electric{J}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu$. The result is $\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=\electric{J}^\nu$.
