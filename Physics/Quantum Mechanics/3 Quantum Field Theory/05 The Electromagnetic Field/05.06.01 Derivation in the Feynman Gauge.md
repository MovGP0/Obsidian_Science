---
title: "Derivation in the Feynman Gauge"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Inverting the gauge-fixed kinetic operator gives the momentum-space photon propagator and its Feynman-gauge limit.

The photon propagator is the vacuum expectation value of the time-ordered field product:
$$
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\langle0|\mathcal T\{\electric{A}^\mu(\mechanical{x})\electric{A}^\nu(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle.
$$
In momentum space, invert the operator in equation (5.14). For density (5.13), it is
$$
O^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=-\mechanical{k}^{\,2}g^{\mu\nu}
+\left(1-\frac1\xi\right)\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu.
$$
The propagator is the inverse up to a factor $i$:
$$
O^{\mu\rho}(\mechanical{k})\signal{D}_{\rho\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=i\delta^\mu{}_\nu.
$$

Separate the kinetic operator into transverse and longitudinal projectors:
$$
T^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}},
\qquad
T^{\mu\nu}T_{\nu\rho}=T^\mu{}_\rho,
$$
$$
L^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}},
\qquad
L^{\mu\nu}L_{\nu\rho}=L^\mu{}_\rho.
$$
Then $O^{\mu\nu}=-\mechanical{k}^{\,2}T^{\mu\nu}-(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}/\xi)L^{\mu\nu}$. With the Feynman prescription,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
&=\frac{-i}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}[T^{\mu\nu}+\xi L^{\mu\nu}]\\
&=\frac{-i}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}
\left[g^{\mu\nu}-(1-\xi)
\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}\right].
\end{aligned} \tag{5.21}
$$
The $i\epsilon$ prescription implements the Feynman boundary conditions. The projector algebra is defined off shell; singular factors are understood with the limiting prescription.

## Feynman gauge — ξ = 1

The simplest propagator is
$$
\boxed{\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})\big|_{\xi=1}
=\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}}. \tag{5.22}
$$
It is manifestly covariant and convenient for Feynman-diagram calculations.

## Landau gauge — ξ → 0

$$
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})\big|_{\xi=0}
=\frac{-i}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}
\left(g^{\mu\nu}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}}\right).
$$
It projects onto the four-dimensional transverse sector, in agreement with the strict Lorenz condition.
