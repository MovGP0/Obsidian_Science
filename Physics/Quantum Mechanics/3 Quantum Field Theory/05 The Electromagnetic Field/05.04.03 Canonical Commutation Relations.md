---
title: "Canonical Commutation Relations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Coulomb-gauge commutators contain the transverse projector and are equivalent to bosonic commutators for the two physical modes.

For the free field with $\electric{A}^0=0$, the source uses
$$
\signal{\pi}^i=\electric{F}^{i0}=-\dot{\electric{A}}^{\,i}=\electric{E}^{i}.
$$
Transversality imposes $\mechanical{k}^i\electric{A}_i=0$. A naive commutator with the full spatial delta distribution would violate the constraint. The printed canonical relation is
$$
[\electric{A}^{i}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\signal{t}),
\signal{\pi}^{j}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\signal{t})]
=i\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
P_{ij}^{T}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}
\equiv i\delta_T^{ij}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}). \tag{5.11}
$$
The transverse delta distribution satisfies $\partial_i\delta_T^{ij}=0$.

In mode variables the source gives
$$
[a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)},a_{\mathbf k'}^{(\lambda')\dagger}]
=\delta^{\lambda\lambda'}(2\pi)^3
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}'), \tag{5.12}
$$
with
$$
[a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)},a_{\mathbf k'}^{(\lambda')}]=0,
\qquad
[a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)\dagger},a_{\mathbf k'}^{(\lambda')\dagger}]=0.
$$
The positive sign gives positive-norm photon Fock states. The vacuum satisfies $a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)}|0\rangle=0$.

> [!note]
> Equation (5.11) is retained as printed. With the source's $\signal{\pi}^i=-\dot{\electric{A}}^{\,i}$ and the displayed mode expansion, (5.12) gives $[\electric{A}^{i},\signal{\pi}^{j}]=-i\delta_T^{ij}$. The positive canonical sign instead applies to the lower-index coordinate $\electric{A}_i$ paired with $\signal{\pi}^j$, or to an explicitly Euclidean spatial momentum $\dot{\magnetic{\mathbf A}}$. These index conventions must not be mixed.
>
> The source writes the formal occupation density as $N_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)}=a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)\dagger}a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)}$ and describes its eigenvalues as nonnegative. In a continuum normalization this product is distribution-valued. Integer occupation numbers apply to normalized discrete modes or wave packets.
