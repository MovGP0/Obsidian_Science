---
title: "Massive Vector Field (Proca Field)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Proca equation constrains a massive vector field to three spin-one polarizations, including a longitudinal mode.

For a vector field with $\mechanical{m}>0$, the Proca equation implies $\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu=0$. This follows from the equation of motion and is not a gauge choice.

The condition removes one of the four components, leaving **three physical degrees of freedom**: two transverse and one longitudinal.

The little group of a massive four-momentum is $SO(3)$. A spin-one field has $\mechanical{s}=1$, so the representation dimension is $2\mechanical{s}+1=3$.

**Table 5.1** Comparison of massless and massive vector fields.

| Field | Mass | Physical degrees of freedom | Little group |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Photon | $\mechanical{m}=0$ | $2$ | $ISO(2)\supset SO(2)$ |
| Proca field | $\mechanical{m}>0$ | $3$ | $SO(3)$ |

## Discontinuity at m → 0

The polarization count changes from three at nonzero mass to two at zero mass. The source links this to the longitudinal polarization's $1/\mechanical{m}$ behavior and notes that the contribution vanishes when coupled to a conserved current $\electric{J}^\mu$.

> [!note]
> A $1/\mechanical{m}$ factor in the longitudinal polarization does not by itself remove the state from the physical spectrum. The leading current coupling cancels because of current conservation. The exactly massless theory has a gauge redundancy that changes the physical degree count.

This comparison leads to the Higgs mechanism: the Higgs field gives mass to the $W$ and $Z$ bosons and supplies their longitudinal degrees of freedom.

In the Higgs mechanism, the longitudinal mode comes from the Goldstone mode absorbed by the gauge field.
