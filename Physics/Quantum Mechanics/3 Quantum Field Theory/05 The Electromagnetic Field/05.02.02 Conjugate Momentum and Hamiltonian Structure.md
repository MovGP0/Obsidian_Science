---
title: "Conjugate Momentum and Hamiltonian Structure"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The vanishing momentum conjugate to the scalar potential makes Gauss's law a constraint rather than an independent evolution equation.

The momentum density conjugate to the potential is
$$
\signal{\pi}^\mu
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_0\electric{A}_\mu)}
=\electric{F}^{\mu0}.
$$
In particular, $\signal{\pi}^0=\electric{F}^{00}=0$ because the field tensor is antisymmetric. The scalar potential is therefore not an independent dynamical variable. Its equation of motion is the Gauss constraint, rather than a second-order time evolution equation.

The spatial conjugate momenta are
$$
\signal{\pi}^i=\electric{F}^{i0}=\electric{E}^i.
$$
The Hamiltonian density is
$$
\energy{\mathcal H}
=\signal{\pi}^i\dot{\electric{A}}_i-\energy{\mathcal L}
=\frac12(\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}+\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{\,2})
-\electric{A}_0\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\electric{\mathbf E}. \tag{5.8}
$$
The last term follows by integration by parts from $\electric{E}^i\partial_i\electric{A}^0$. It shows that $\electric{A}_0$ is a Lagrange multiplier for the vacuum constraint $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\electric{\mathbf E}=0$. Figure 5.1 summarizes the Lagrangian structure and its consequences.
