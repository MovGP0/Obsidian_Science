---
title: "Vacuum Energy (Cosmological Constant)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Two physical photon polarizations give a divergent zero-point contribution; normal ordering removes that free-field constant.

Before normal ordering, the source writes the electromagnetic vacuum contribution as
$$
\energy{E}_0
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}\frac12
=2\times\frac12\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}
\longrightarrow\infty.
$$
The factor two counts the physical transverse polarizations.

In covariant quantization, each physical mode contributes $+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}/2$, the longitudinal mode contributes $+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}/2$, and the scalar mode contributes $-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}/2$ because of (5.18). Their sum is
$$
\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
+\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
+\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
-\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}.
$$
The scalar and longitudinal contributions cancel, giving the same result as Coulomb gauge. Normal ordering sets the free-field vacuum constant to zero.

> [!note]
> The source calls the displayed integral $\energy{E}_0$ a vacuum energy, but the momentum integral has units of energy density. A total energy also needs a spatial-volume factor. Normal ordering removes this flat-spacetime free-field constant; it does not by itself determine the gravitational cosmological constant.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{E}_0$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Vacuum contribution | The energy-density integral denoted E₀ in the source. |

| $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon energy | The positive massless mode energy. |
