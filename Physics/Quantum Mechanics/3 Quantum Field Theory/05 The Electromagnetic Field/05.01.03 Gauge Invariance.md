---
title: "Gauge Invariance"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An Abelian gauge transformation changes the potential but leaves the electromagnetic field tensor unchanged.

The theory is invariant under the Abelian gauge transformation
$$
\electric{A}^\mu(\mechanical{x})
\longrightarrow\electric{A}^\mu(\mechanical{x})+\partial^\mu\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x}), \tag{5.6}
$$
where $\signal{\alpha}$ is any differentiable scalar function. The field tensor transforms as
$$
\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow
\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\partial^\mu\partial^\nu\signal{\alpha}
-\partial^\nu\partial^\mu\signal{\alpha}
=\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}.
$$
Thus the physics described by $\electric{\mathbf E}$ and $\magnetic{\mathbf B}$ is unchanged. This redundancy causes the complications in quantization.

### Example 5.1 — Common gauges

The source gives three common choices.

(i) **Lorenz gauge:** $\partial_\mu\electric{A}^\mu=0$. This condition is Lorentz covariant and reduces the equation of motion to $\Box\electric{A}^\mu=\electric{J}^\mu$.

(ii) **Coulomb gauge**, or **transverse gauge:** $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf A}=0$. It removes the longitudinal component of the vector potential and makes the two physical transverse polarizations explicit.

(iii) **Axial gauge:** $\electric{A}^{3}=0$. This is useful in some structural analyses.

Lorenz gauge does not fix the potential completely. A residual transformation remains for any $\signal{\alpha}$ that satisfies $\Box\signal{\alpha}=0$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{A}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-potential | The gauge-dependent electromagnetic potential. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime point | The coordinate of the gauge transformation. |
| $\signal{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Gauge function | The arbitrary scalar gauge parameter. |
| $\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Field tensor | The gauge-invariant electromagnetic field strength. |
| $\electric{\mathbf E}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Electric field | The physical electric field. |
| $\magnetic{\mathbf B}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Magnetic field | The physical magnetic field. |
| $\electric{J}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Four-current | The source in the gauge-fixed equation of motion. |
| $\magnetic{\mathbf A}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Vector potential | The spatial potential subject to the Coulomb gauge condition. |
