---
title: "The Physical Polarization Sum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The sum over two real-photon polarizations can be written covariantly with an auxiliary reference vector.

For cross sections with external real photons, sum the squared amplitude over final polarizations and average over initial polarizations as required.

For photon momentum $\mechanical{k}^\mu$ and physical polarizations $\signal{\epsilon}^{\mu(\lambda)}$, $\lambda=1,2$,
$$
\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}
\signal{\epsilon}^{\mu(\lambda)}(\mechanical{k})
\signal{\epsilon}^{\nu(\lambda)*}(\mechanical{k})
=-g^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu n^\nu+\mechanical{k}^\nu n^\mu}{\mechanical{k}\cdot n}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu n^2}{(\mechanical{k}\cdot n)^2}. \tag{5.23}
$$
The auxiliary reference vector $n^\mu$ satisfies $\mechanical{k}\cdot n\ne0$. In Coulomb gauge, choose $n^\mu=(1,\mathbf0)$; the spatial components reduce to the transverse projector.

The expression depends on the reference vector, but physical results do not, because of the Ward identity.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon four-momentum | The null momentum of the external photon. |
| $\signal{\epsilon}^{\mu(\lambda)}$ | $1$ | Polarization vectors | The two physical transverse photon polarizations. |
