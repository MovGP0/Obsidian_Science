---
title: "Structure of the Lagrangian"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Maxwell Lagrangian is the difference of electric and magnetic energy densities; its dual contraction is a total derivative.

Use (5.2)–(5.3) to express the field-tensor contraction in terms of electric and magnetic fields:
$$
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
=-2(\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}-\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{\,2})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Maxwell}}
=\frac12(\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}-\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{\,2}).
$$
This is the difference between the electric and magnetic energy densities. It is not the total energy density, which contains their sum. The relative minus sign is required by the Lorentz contraction with the chosen metric.

A useful check is an ideal vacuum plane wave. Its electric and magnetic fields have equal magnitudes, so this Lagrangian density vanishes even though the wave carries positive energy. A zero value of the Lagrangian on a particular solution does not mean that the field or its energy is zero.

The other quadratic invariant is the pseudoscalar
$$
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}
=-4\electric{\mathbf E}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}.
$$
For the Abelian field with a constant coefficient, it is a total derivative, proportional to $\partial_\mu K^\mu$ for a suitable current $K^\mu$. It does not change the bulk equations of motion when the surface variation vanishes. This statement uses a constant coefficient and a smooth Abelian potential in the region considered. Boundaries, nontrivial global field configurations, or a spacetime-dependent coefficient require a separate analysis; a total derivative cannot simply be discarded in every physical problem.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu},\tilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Field tensors | The electromagnetic tensor and its dual. |

| $\magnetic{\mathbf B}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Magnetic field | The magnetic part of the field strength. |
