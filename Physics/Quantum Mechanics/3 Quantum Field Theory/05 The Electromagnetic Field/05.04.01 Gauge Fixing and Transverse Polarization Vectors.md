---
title: "Gauge Fixing and Transverse Polarization Vectors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Coulomb gauge makes the spatial vector potential perpendicular to its wave vector in Fourier space.

The Coulomb-gauge condition is
$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf A}=0. \tag{5.9}
$$
In Fourier space this is $\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\tilde{\magnetic{\mathbf A}}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})=0$. Thus the vector potential is perpendicular to the wave vector.

The scalar potential is fixed by Gauss's constraint rather than by an independent dynamical equation. Only the two transverse spatial components remain.

For each wave vector $\mechanical{\mathbf k}$, with $\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert=\signal{\omega}_{\mathbf k}$, choose two real polarization vectors $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\lambda)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})$, $\lambda=1,2$, such that
$$
\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\lambda)}\cdot
\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\lambda')}=\delta^{\lambda\lambda'},
\qquad
\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\lambda)}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf k}=0.
$$
Together with $\hat{\mechanical{\mathbf k}}=\mechanical{\mathbf k}/\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert$, they form an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb R^3$. Completeness gives
$$
\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}
\signal{\epsilon}_i^{(\lambda)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
\signal{\epsilon}_j^{(\lambda)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k})
=\delta_{ij}-\frac{\mechanical{k}_i\mechanical{k}_j}{\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2}
\equiv P_{ij}^{T}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}). \tag{5.10}
$$
The transverse projector satisfies $\mechanical{k}^iP_{ij}^{T}=0$.

### Example 5.2 — Explicit polarization vectors

For a photon along the $z$ axis, $\mechanical{\mathbf k}=\mechanical{k}\hat{\mathbf z}$. Choose linear polarizations
$$
\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(1)}=\hat{\mathbf x},
\qquad
\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(2)}=\hat{\mathbf y}.
$$
Circular polarizations with helicity $\pm1$ are
$$
\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\pm)}
=\mp\frac1{\sqrt2}(\hat{\mathbf x}\pm i\hat{\mathbf y}).
$$
They satisfy $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\pm)*}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\pm)}=1$ and $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(+)*}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(-)}=0$. They are helicity eigenvectors of $\hat{\mechanical{h}}=\hat{\mechanical{\mathbf k}}\cdot\mathbf J$ with eigenvalues $\pm1$. Here $\mathbf J$ contains the spin-one rotation generators.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\mathbf A}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Vector potential | The transverse spatial electromagnetic potential. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Wave vector | The spatial Fourier momentum in natural units. |
| $\tilde{\magnetic{\mathbf A}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Fourier-space potential | The spatial Fourier transform with the standard three-dimensional measure. |
| $\signal{\omega}_{\mathbf k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode frequency | The photon frequency, equal to momentum magnitude in natural units. |
| $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\lambda)},\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\pm)}$ | $1$ | Polarization vectors | The normalized linear and circular polarization vectors. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $1$ | Helicity | The spin projection along the photon momentum. |
