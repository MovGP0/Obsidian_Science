---
title: "Massless Vector Field (Photon)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Gauge freedom reduces four photon potential components to two transverse physical polarizations.

## Classical count

The potential $\electric{A}^\mu$ has four components. The Lorenz condition $\partial_\mu\electric{A}^\mu=0$ reduces the independent mode components to three. Residual gauge freedom with $\Box\signal{\alpha}=0$ removes one more, leaving **two physical degrees of freedom**.

## Quantum count

For a nonzero null four-momentum $\mechanical{k}$, with $\mechanical{k}^{\,2}=0$, the little group is $ISO(2)$, the isometry group of the plane.

In finite-dimensional unitary representations of this little group, the translation part acts trivially. The representation reduces to the transverse rotation subgroup $SO(2)$, whose generator is helicity. Its irreducible representations are one-dimensional.

> [!note]
> The source calls the four-momentum “vanishing,” but its stated condition is $\mechanical{k}^{\,2}=0$. The photon momentum is nonzero and null; the little-group statement applies to that case.

The photon helicities are $\mechanical{h}=\pm1$. Together they give two physical polarization states for each photon momentum.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{A}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-potential | The four photon field components before reduction. |
| $\signal{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Residual gauge function | A solution of the wave equation that preserves Lorenz gauge. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon four-momentum | A nonzero null vector that defines the little group. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $1$ | Photon helicity | The two physical spin projections along the momentum. |
