---
title: "Helicity and Circular Polarization"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Circularly polarized photons have helicity +1 or −1; a physical photon has no helicity-zero state.

For $\mechanical{\mathbf k}=\mechanical{k}\hat{\mathbf z}$, the helicity operator is $\hat{\mechanical{h}}=\hat{\mechanical{\mathbf k}}\cdot\mathbf J$, where $\mathbf J$ contains rotation generators. Circular polarization vectors $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\pm)}$ select helicities $\pm1$:
$$
a_{\mathbf k}^{(\pm)\dagger}|0\rangle
=|\mechanical{\mathbf k};\pm1\rangle,
\qquad
\hat{\mechanical{h}}|\mechanical{\mathbf k};\pm1\rangle
=\pm|\mechanical{\mathbf k};\pm1\rangle.
$$
The photon has no physical $\mechanical{h}=0$ state. A massive vector boson instead has helicities $-1,0,+1$. The photon result follows from its zero mass and gauge symmetry.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf k},\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon momentum | The momentum along the z axis. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $1$ | Helicity | The photon spin projection along its momentum. |
| $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(\pm)}$ | $1$ | Circular polarizations | The normalized helicity polarization vectors. |
