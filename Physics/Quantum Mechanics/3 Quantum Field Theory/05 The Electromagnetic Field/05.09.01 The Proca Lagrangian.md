---
title: "The Proca Lagrangian"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Proca mass term breaks the Maxwell gauge symmetry and makes the Lorenz condition a consequence of the field equations.

For a massive vector field,
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Proca}}
=-\frac14\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\signal{A}_\mu\signal{A}^\mu. \tag{5.26}
$$
The mass term explicitly breaks the Maxwell gauge symmetry. The unextended Proca theory is therefore a theory of a massive vector field rather than a Maxwell gauge field.

The Euler–Lagrange equations give
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^\nu=0. \tag{5.27}
$$
Taking the divergence gives
$$
\mechanical{m}^2\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0
\quad(\mechanical{m}\ne0). \tag{5.28}
$$
This is the Lorenz condition as a consequence of the field equation, not a gauge choice. Inserting it into (5.27) gives
$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$
Each component satisfies the massive Klein–Gordon equation, subject to constraint (5.28).

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Proca}}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Proca Lagrangian density | The free massive-vector Lagrangian density. |
| $\signal{A}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Proca field | The massive vector field. |
