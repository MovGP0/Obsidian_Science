> [!TLDR]
> These exercises test Maxwell identities, photon quantization, gauge independence, polarization sums, and the Proca field.

## Exercise 5.1 — Bianchi identity and homogeneous Maxwell equations

Show explicitly that the Bianchi identity gives the homogeneous Maxwell equations.

Use $\partial_{[\mu}\electric{F}_{\nu\rho]}=0$ to obtain
$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}=0,
\qquad
\partial_{\signal{t}}\magnetic{\mathbf B}
+\boldsymbol\nabla\times\electric{\mathbf E}=0.
$$
*Hint:* Express the tensor through the electric and magnetic fields, then calculate the antisymmetrized derivative for the different index choices.

## Exercise 5.2 — Electromagnetic energy–momentum tensor

Derive the canonical tensor from the Maxwell density:
$$
\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\rho)}
\partial^\nu\electric{A}_\rho
-g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}.
$$
Show that it is neither symmetric nor gauge invariant. Construct the symmetric, gauge-invariant Belinfante–Rosenfeld tensor
$$
\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\electric{F}^{\nu}{}_\rho
+\frac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}.
$$
Verify $\energy{T}^{00}=\frac12(\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}+\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{\,2})$ and $\energy{T}^{0i}=(\electric{\mathbf E}\times\magnetic{\mathbf B})^i$, the Poynting vector in natural units.

## Exercise 5.3 — Polarization vectors and completeness

For $\mechanical{\mathbf k}=\mechanical{k}(\sin\mechanical{\theta},0,\cos\mechanical{\theta})$ in the $xz$ plane, construct the two linear polarization vectors $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(1)}$ and $\signal{\boldsymbol\epsilon}^{(2)}$. They must be mutually orthogonal and perpendicular to the momentum. Verify completeness relation (5.10).

Construct the circular polarizations and verify that they are helicity eigenvectors.

## Exercise 5.4 — Transverse Dirac delta

Show that
$$
\delta_T^{ij}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\left(\delta^{ij}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^i\mechanical{k}^j}{\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2}\right)
e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}
$$
satisfies $\partial_i\delta_T^{ij}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})=0$ and
$$
\delta_T^{ij}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})+\delta_L^{ij}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})
=\delta^{ij}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}),
$$
where $\delta_L^{ij}$ is the corresponding longitudinal distribution. Calculate $\delta_T^{ij}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})$ explicitly through derivatives of $1/\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf x}\rvert$.

## Exercise 5.5 — Gupta–Bleuler condition and physical states

Consider
$$
|\Psi\rangle=
\left(
c_{11}a_{\mathbf k}^{(1)\dagger}a_{\mathbf k'}^{(1)\dagger}
+c_{33}a_{\mathbf k}^{(3)\dagger}a_{\mathbf k'}^{(3)\dagger}
+c_{03}a_{\mathbf k}^{(0)\dagger}a_{\mathbf k'}^{(3)\dagger}
\right)|0\rangle.
$$
Apply the Gupta–Bleuler condition and determine the constraints on $c_{11},c_{33},c_{03}$. Calculate the resulting norm and verify that it is nonnegative.

## Exercise 5.6 — Photon propagator in position space

Fourier-transform the Feynman-gauge photon propagator:
$$
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
*Hint:* Use the scalar-propagator method: integrate over $\mechanical{k}^0$ with a contour in the complex-energy plane. Verify
$$
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=-g^{\mu\nu}\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}),
$$
where $\signal{\Delta}_F$ is the massless scalar Feynman propagator.

## Exercise 5.8 — Explicit Ward identity

In tree-level QED, consider Compton scattering
$$
\gamma(\mechanical{k})+\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p})
\to\gamma(\mechanical{k}')+\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p}').
$$
Calculate the electron-current factors with the external photons and show $\mechanical{k}'_\mu\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu=0$. The index $\mu$ refers to the outgoing photon. Use the equations of motion for $u(\mechanical{p})$ and $\bar u(\mechanical{p}')$.

> [!note]
> The Compton Ward cancellation applies to the complete tree amplitude, including both photon orderings on the electron line. It does not hold for a single Compton diagram on its own.

## Exercise 5.9 — Proca Lagrangian and degrees of freedom

Start from (5.26).

(a) Derive the equations of motion and prove that $\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu=0$ follows for $\mechanical{m}\ne0$.

(b) Identify the conjugate momenta $\signal{\pi}^\mu$ and show $\signal{\pi}^0=0$. The source asks you to identify $\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu=0$ as a secondary constraint that follows from the equations of motion.

(c) Calculate the Hamiltonian density and compare it with the electromagnetic result (5.8).

> [!note]
> In a canonical Dirac–Bergmann analysis, preservation of the primary constraint $\signal{\pi}^0=0$ first gives the massive Gauss constraint, $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\signal{\mathbf E}_{\mathrm{Proca}}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^0=0$. The Lorenz condition follows from the Proca equations, but should not be confused with that canonical secondary constraint.

## Exercise 5.10 — Vacuum energy in covariant quantization

Before normal ordering, the Gupta–Bleuler Hamiltonian includes all four modes with commutators (5.17). Show that their signed zero-point contributions sum to the Coulomb-gauge result from the two physical transverse modes.
