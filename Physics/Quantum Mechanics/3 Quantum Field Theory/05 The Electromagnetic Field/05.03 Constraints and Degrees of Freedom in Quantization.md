---
title: "Constraints and Degrees of Freedom in Quantization"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Gauge redundancy and a vanishing conjugate momentum prevent direct canonical quantization of all four electromagnetic potential components.

For the scalar and Dirac fields, the canonical procedure identifies fields and conjugate momenta, imposes commutation or anticommutation relations, and expands the fields into normal modes with creation and annihilation operators.

For the electromagnetic field, this procedure meets two related obstacles.

## First obstacle — Vanishing π⁰

The momentum $\signal{\pi}^0$ vanishes identically. If the canonical relation
$$
[\electric{A}^0(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\signal{t}),
\signal{\pi}^0(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\signal{t})]
=i\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})
$$
were imposed, it would give $0=i\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})$. This is inconsistent.

The scalar potential is not an independent dynamical variable. Its equation of motion is the vacuum Gauss constraint $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\electric{\mathbf E}=0$.

## Second obstacle — Redundant degrees of freedom

A massless vector field in four spacetime dimensions starts with four potential components $\electric{A}^\mu$.

A physical photon has only two transverse polarizations. Gauge transformations (5.6) connect physically equivalent configurations. A naive quantization of all four components therefore adds unphysical states to the state space.

## Two complementary strategies

1. **Coulomb gauge:** Fix the gauge before quantization and remove redundant degrees of freedom explicitly. Only the physical polarizations remain, but Lorentz covariance is not manifest.
2. **Covariant quantization — Gupta–Bleuler method:** Add a gauge-fixing term to the Lagrangian, quantize all four components, and impose the gauge condition on physical states. Covariance is manifest, but the enlarged state space contains negative-norm states.

> [!note]
> The source calls the negative-norm scalar states “scalar ghosts.” These unphysical photon polarizations are distinct from the anticommuting Faddeev–Popov ghost fields used in gauge-fixed path integrals.
