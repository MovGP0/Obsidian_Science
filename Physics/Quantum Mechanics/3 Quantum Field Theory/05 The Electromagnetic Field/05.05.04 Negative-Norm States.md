---
title: "Negative-Norm States"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Covariant photon commutators give a negative norm to the timelike mode, so the enlarged state space cannot itself be the physical Hilbert space.

The covariant canonical relation (5.16) gives
$$
[a_{\mathbf k}^{(\lambda)},a_{\mathbf k'}^{(\lambda')\dagger}]
=-g^{\lambda\lambda'}(2\pi)^3
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}'). \tag{5.17}
$$
The factor $-g^{\lambda\lambda'}$ gives the usual positive sign for $\lambda=\lambda'=1,2,3$. For the timelike mode,
$$
[a_{\mathbf k}^{(0)},a_{\mathbf k'}^{(0)\dagger}]
=-(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}'). \tag{5.18}
$$
The scalar one-photon state is $|\mechanical{\mathbf k},0\rangle=a_{\mathbf k}^{(0)\dagger}|0\rangle$. Its inner product is
$$
\langle\mechanical{\mathbf k},0|\mechanical{\mathbf k}',0\rangle
=\langle0|a_{\mathbf k}^{(0)}a_{\mathbf k'}^{(0)\dagger}|0\rangle
=-(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}').
$$
For a normalized wave packet, this gives a negative norm.

The source calls these modes scalar ghosts and distinguishes them from Faddeev–Popov ghosts in non-Abelian theories. They must be removed from the physical state space to obtain a unitary theory.

> [!note]
> The source appends “$<0$” to the delta-distribution expression. A momentum eigenstate has distributional normalization. The negative-norm statement is precise after smearing with a nonzero square-integrable wave packet.
