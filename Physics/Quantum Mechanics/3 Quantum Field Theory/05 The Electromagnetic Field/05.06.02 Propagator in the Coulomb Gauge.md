---
title: "Propagator in the Coulomb Gauge"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Coulomb-gauge propagator separates an instantaneous scalar part from the transverse spatial photon part.

The Coulomb-gauge propagator is not manifestly covariant:
$$
\signal{D}^{00}(\mechanical{k})=\frac{i}{\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2},
\qquad
\signal{D}^{0i}(\mechanical{k})=\signal{D}^{i0}(\mechanical{k})=0,
$$
$$
\signal{D}^{ij}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}
\left(\delta^{ij}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^{i}\mechanical{k}^{j}}{\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2}\right).
$$

The component $\signal{D}^{00}$ propagates the Coulomb potential. It is instantaneous and has no energy-pole $i\epsilon$ prescription. The source describes this as the nonrelativistic form of the Coulomb interaction in this gauge.

> [!note]
> An instantaneous gauge-dependent potential does not make the full electromagnetic theory nonrelativistic. Physical observables combine the constrained and transverse contributions.

### Example 5.5 — Independence of physical results from ξ

A useful check is that physical amplitudes and cross sections, such as $\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$ in Fig. 5.3, are independent of $\xi$. The Ward identity ensures this.

The gauge-dependent propagator terms are proportional to $\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu$. They give no contribution when coupled to conserved current amplitudes that satisfy $\mechanical{k}_\mu\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu=0$.
