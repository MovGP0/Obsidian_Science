---
title: "The Proca Propagator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Proca propagator includes a longitudinal momentum term whose leading mass singularity cancels between conserved currents.

The Fourier-space Proca kinetic operator is
$$
O^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=-(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2)g^{\mu\nu}
+\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu.
$$
Its inverse, multiplied by $i$, is
$$
\signal{D}_{\mathrm{Proca}}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-i\left(g^{\mu\nu}
-\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu/\mechanical{m}^2\right)}
{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}. \tag{5.30}
$$
The numerator contains $\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu/\mechanical{m}^2$. Although it is singular as $\mechanical{m}\to0$, contraction with conserved currents removes this term and gives finite physical current-exchange contributions away from the usual propagator singularities.

At the massive pole, the numerator is tied to the sum over the three physical polarizations. Away from that pole the full expression is a Green function, not a count of on-shell states. As a massless-limit check, contract both ends with conserved bounded currents first. The inverse-mass-squared numerator disappears exactly, and the remaining denominator approaches the massless current-exchange denominator away from poles. Interchanging these steps with direct componentwise limiting would leave a misleading divergence. More generally, a zero-mass limit needs a specified family of observables and states; this is demonstrated in [Schambach and Sanders, abstract and zero-mass-limit analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01911).

### Example 5.7 — Propagator comparison

Compare the photon in Feynman gauge with the massive vector field:
$$
\signal{D}_\gamma^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon},
$$
$$
\signal{D}_{\mathrm{Proca}}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-i\left(g^{\mu\nu}
-\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu/\mechanical{m}^2\right)}
{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$

At large off-shell momentum, the photon propagator falls as $1/\mechanical{k}^{\,2}$. The Proca propagator also contains
$$
\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}
{\mechanical{m}^2\mechanical{k}^{\,2}},
$$
which does not fall with momentum scale at fixed $\mechanical{m}$ when the off-shell four-momentum is scaled with a fixed non-null direction. This is a statement about the uncontracted tensor. A conserved current can remove this term in a particular amplitude, so this power count is not by itself a proof that every physical process grows. This worsens ultraviolet power counting in generic interacting massive-vector theories. Gauge-theory mechanisms such as the Higgs mechanism can supply the structure needed for renormalizability.

> [!note]
> The source attributes nonrenormalizability to massive-vector theories in general. The conclusion depends on the interactions and their symmetry structure; the free Proca field alone does not establish a nonrenormalizable interaction theory.
