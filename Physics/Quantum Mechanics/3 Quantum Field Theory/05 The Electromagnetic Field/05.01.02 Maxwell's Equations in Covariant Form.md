---
title: "Maxwell's Equations in Covariant Form"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Maxwell's equations form two covariant tensor equations, one with a source and one without a source.

The four Maxwell equations combine into two tensor equations. The equations with sources, Gauss's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law, are
$$
\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=\electric{J}^\nu. \tag{5.4}
$$

The four-current is $\electric{J}^\mu=(\electric{\rho},\electric{\mathbf J})$, with charge density $\electric{\rho}$ and current density $\electric{\mathbf J}$.

The homogeneous equations, Gauss's law for magnetism and Faraday's law, follow from the Bianchi identity:
$$
\partial_\mu\tilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\partial_{[\mu}\electric{F}_{\nu\rho]}=0. \tag{5.5}
$$
This follows directly from (5.1): $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu-\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\mu$ implies the antisymmetrized derivative identity.

Current conservation follows from (5.4):
$$
\partial_\nu\electric{J}^\nu
=\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=0.
$$
The field tensor is antisymmetric, while $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu$ is symmetric.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Field tensor | The electromagnetic field strength. |
| $\electric{J}^\nu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Four-current | The charge and current source. |
| $\electric{\rho}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Charge density | The time component of the four-current. |
| $\electric{\mathbf J}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Current density | The spatial components of the four-current. |
| $\tilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Dual field tensor | The tensor used in the homogeneous equations. |
| $\electric{A}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-potential | The potential whose curl defines the field tensor. |
