---
title: "Maxwell's Equations in Covariant Form"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Maxwell's equations form two covariant tensor equations, one with a source and one without a source.

The four Maxwell equations combine into two tensor equations. Here the source is a prescribed conserved current, or a matter current whose own equations ensure conservation. These are classical field equations in flat spacetime; the inhomogeneous equation is a physical law, whereas the homogeneous equation is an identity for a smooth potential defined on a local region. The equations with sources, Gauss's law and the Ampère–Maxwell law, are
$$
\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=\electric{J}^\nu. \tag{5.4}
$$

The four-current is $\electric{J}^\mu=(\electric{\rho},\electric{\mathbf J})$, with charge density $\electric{\rho}$ and current density $\electric{\mathbf J}$.

The homogeneous equations, Gauss's law for magnetism and Faraday's law, follow from the Bianchi identity:
$$
\partial_\mu\tilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\partial_{[\mu}\electric{F}_{\nu\rho]}=0. \tag{5.5}
$$
This follows directly from (5.1): $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu-\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\mu$ implies the antisymmetrized derivative identity.

Current conservation follows from (5.4):
$$
\partial_\nu\electric{J}^\nu
=\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=0.
$$
The field tensor is antisymmetric, while $\partial_\nu\partial_\mu$ is symmetric. Thus a nonconserved prescribed source is incompatible with this Maxwell equation; it is not repaired by a gauge choice.

For a direct sign check, set $\nu=0$ in (5.4). Since $\electric{F}^{i0}=\electric{E}^{i}$, the result is Gauss's law with positive charge density on the right. This equation constrains the electric field on each time slice. Integrating current conservation over a fixed region shows that its charge changes only by current through the boundary; the charge of an open region need not be constant.

A dimensional check also fixes the source scale: one derivative contributes one power of energy in natural units, so a field tensor with units of energy squared requires a current density with units of energy cubed. The local potential argument for (5.5) assumes commuting derivatives; singular fields and global topology require separate treatment.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Field tensor | The electromagnetic field strength. |
| $\electric{J}^\nu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Four-current | The charge and current source. |
| $\electric{\rho}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Charge density | The time component of the four-current. |
| $\electric{\mathbf J}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Current density | The spatial components of the four-current. |
| $\tilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Dual field tensor | The tensor used in the homogeneous equations. |
| $\electric{A}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-potential | The potential whose curl defines the field tensor. |
