> [!TLDR]
> The path-integral approach must remove repeated integration over gauge-equivalent field configurations.

The Feynman path integral is an alternative quantization of electromagnetic and more general gauge fields. Chapter 11 treats it in detail.

Start with the generating functional
$$
Z[J]=\int\mathcal D\electric{A}^\mu
\exp\left\{
i\int d^4\mechanical{x}
\left[-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\electric{J}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu\right]
\right\}.
$$
The plus sign of the generating source is a convention for functional differentiation; it must not be silently identified with the minus-sign physical interaction convention used earlier. The functional is formal until gauge fixing, boundary conditions and regularization are specified.

The measure integrates over gauge-equivalent configurations and counts the same physical configuration infinitely many times. The Faddeev–Popov method isolates the gauge-orbit contribution by inserting a functional delta constraint.

With the gauge constraint inserted, the functional is schematically
$$
Z[J]=\int\mathcal D\electric{A}^\mu\,\delta(G[A])\,
\det\left(\frac{\delta G[A^{(\alpha)}]}{\delta\alpha}\right)
\exp\left\{i\energy{S}[A]
+i\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,\electric{J}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu\right\}.
$$
Here $G[A]=0$ is the gauge condition, and the determinant is the Faddeev–Popov determinant.

For the Lorenz condition $G[A]=\partial_\mu\electric{A}^\mu-\omega(\mechanical{x})$, integration over the auxiliary function $\omega$ with a Gaussian weight gives the Rξ-gauge Lagrangian.

The auxiliary function $\omega(\mechanical{x})$ in this gauge condition is not a photon frequency. It specifies a gauge slice before the Gaussian average. For the Lorenz gauge, a gradient shift changes the gauge function by the wave operator acting on the gauge parameter. This operator has no dependence on the electromagnetic potential. With boundary conditions that handle residual zero modes, this checks why the Abelian determinant produces no photon interaction vertices.

In Abelian QED, the Faddeev–Popov determinant is independent of $\electric{A}^\mu$ and factors out as a normalization constant. In Yang–Mills theory it depends on the field and is represented by Faddeev–Popov ghost fields.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{A}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-potential | The photon field integrated over in the functional measure. |
| $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Field tensor | The electromagnetic field strength. |
| $\electric{J}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | External source | The source used to generate photon correlation functions. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime point | The integration coordinate in the action. |
| $\energy{S}[A]$ | $1$ | Action | The electromagnetic action in natural units. |
