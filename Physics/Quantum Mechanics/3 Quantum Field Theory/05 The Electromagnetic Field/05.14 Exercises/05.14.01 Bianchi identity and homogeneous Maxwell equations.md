## Exercise 5.1 — Bianchi identity and homogeneous Maxwell equations

Show explicitly that the Bianchi identity gives the homogeneous Maxwell equations.

Use $\partial_{[\mu}\electric{F}_{\nu\rho]}=0$ to obtain
$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}=0,
\qquad
\partial_{\signal{t}}\magnetic{\mathbf B}
+\boldsymbol\nabla\times\electric{\mathbf E}=0.
$$
*Hint:* Express the tensor through the electric and magnetic fields, then calculate the antisymmetrized derivative for the different index choices.

## Solution 5.1 — Bianchi identity and homogeneous Maxwell equations

The Bianchi identity is
$$
\partial_\mu\electric{F}_{\nu\rho}
+\partial_\nu\electric{F}_{\rho\mu}
+\partial_\rho\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}=0.
$$

Equivalently, $\partial_{[\mu}\electric{F}_{\nu\rho]}=0$. From
$$
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu-\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\mu,
$$
the cyclic derivative is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\mu\electric{F}_{\nu\rho}+\text{cyclic}
={}&\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\rho
-\partial_\mu\partial_\rho\electric{A}_\nu
+\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\electric{A}_\mu\\
&-\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\rho
+\partial_\rho\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu
-\partial_\rho\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\mu=0,
\end{aligned}
$$
because partial derivatives commute.

For three spatial indices, use $\electric{F}_{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijl}\magnetic{B}^l$:
$$
\partial_i\electric{F}_{jk}+\partial_j\electric{F}_{ki}+\partial_k\electric{F}_{ij}
=-\epsilon_{jkl}\partial_i\magnetic{B}^l
-\epsilon_{kil}\partial_j\magnetic{B}^l
-\epsilon_{ijl}\partial_k\magnetic{B}^l.
$$
Contract with $\epsilon^{ijk}$:
$$
\epsilon^{ijk}(\partial_i\electric{F}_{jk}+\text{cyclic})
=-3!\,\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}=0.
$$
Thus $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}=0$, the magnetic Gauss law.

For $\mu=0,\nu=i,\rho=j$, the source convention is
$\electric{F}^{0i}=-\electric{E}^i$, $\electric{F}_{0i}=+\electric{E}^i$, and
$\electric{F}_{j0}=-\electric{E}^j$. Hence
$$
\partial_0\electric{F}_{ij}+\partial_i\electric{F}_{j0}+\partial_j\electric{F}_{0i}=0,
$$
$$
-\epsilon_{ijk}\partial_0\magnetic{B}^k
-\partial_i\electric{E}^j+\partial_j\electric{E}^i=0.
$$
Contraction with $\epsilon^{ijm}$ gives
$$
-2\partial_0\magnetic{B}^m-2(\boldsymbol\nabla\times\electric{\mathbf E})^m=0.
$$
Therefore the second homogeneous Maxwell equation is Faraday's law:
$$
\frac{\partial\magnetic{\mathbf B}}{\partial\signal{t}}
+\boldsymbol\nabla\times\electric{\mathbf E}=0.
$$
