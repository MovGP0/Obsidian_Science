## Exercise 5.6 — Photon propagator in position space

Fourier-transform the Feynman-gauge photon propagator:
$$
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
*Hint:* Use the scalar-propagator method: integrate over $\mechanical{k}^0$ with a contour in the complex-energy plane. Verify
$$
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=-g^{\mu\nu}\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}),
$$
where $\signal{\Delta}_F$ is the massless scalar Feynman propagator.

## Solution 5.6 — Photon propagator in position space

In Feynman gauge,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
&=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&=-g^{\mu\nu}
\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&=-g^{\mu\nu}\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})\big|_{\mechanical{m}=0}.
\end{aligned}
$$
To evaluate the scalar integral, split $d^4\mechanical{k}=d\mechanical{k}^0\,d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}$ and write
$\mechanical{k}^2=(\mechanical{k}^0)^2-|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|^2$.
The positive-energy pole is just below the real axis, and the negative-energy pole is just above it:
$$
\mechanical{k}^0=|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|-i0,\qquad
\mechanical{k}^0=-|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|+i0.
$$
For positive time separation, close the contour in the lower half-plane; for negative time separation, close it in the upper half-plane. Writing $\mechanical{x}$ for the separation, the residues give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})\big|_{\mechanical{m}=0}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|}
\Bigl[
&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0)e^{-i|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{x}^0
+i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}\\
+&\theta(-\mechanical{x}^0)e^{+i|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|\mechanical{x}^0
-i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf x}}
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
Changing $\mechanical{\mathbf k}\to-\mechanical{\mathbf k}$ in the second term gives the source's equivalent form with the same spatial exponential in both terms.

Evaluate the remaining integral in spherical coordinates,
$$
d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}
=|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|^2\,d|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|
\sin\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\phi}.
$$
The distributional result is
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})\big|_{\mechanical{m}=0}
=-\frac1{4\pi^2(\mechanical{x}^2-i\epsilon)}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\signal{D}_F^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{4\pi^2[(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})^2-i\epsilon]}
=-g^{\mu\nu}\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})\big|_{\mechanical{m}=0}.
$$
The formulas use the limit $\epsilon\to0^+$; the coordinate-space infinitesimal need not have the same dimensions as the momentum-space regulator.
