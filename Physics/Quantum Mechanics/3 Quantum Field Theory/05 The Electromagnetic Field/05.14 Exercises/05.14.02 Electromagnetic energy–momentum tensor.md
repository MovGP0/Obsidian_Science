## Exercise 5.2 — Electromagnetic energy–momentum tensor

Derive the canonical tensor from the Maxwell density:
$$
\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\rho)}
\partial^\nu\electric{A}_\rho
-g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}.
$$
Show that it is neither symmetric nor gauge invariant. Construct the symmetric, gauge-invariant Belinfante–Rosenfeld tensor
$$
\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\electric{F}^{\nu}{}_\rho
+\frac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}.
$$
Verify $\energy{T}^{00}=\frac12(\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}+\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{\,2})$ and $\energy{T}^{0i}=(\electric{\mathbf E}\times\magnetic{\mathbf B})^i$, the Poynting vector in natural units.

## Solution 5.2 — Electromagnetic energy–momentum tensor

From the Maxwell density
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac14\electric{F}_{\alpha\beta}\electric{F}^{\alpha\beta},
\qquad
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\rho)}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho},
$$
the canonical tensor is
$$
\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial^\nu\electric{A}_\rho
+\frac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\alpha\beta}\electric{F}^{\alpha\beta}.
$$

The canonical tensor is generally not symmetric: its derivative of the potential is not antisymmetrized. It is also not gauge invariant. Under $\electric{A}_\rho\to\electric{A}_\rho+\partial_\rho\chi$,
$$
\delta\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial^\nu\partial_\rho\chi
$$
is generally nonzero.

### Belinfante improvement

Add a total divergence $\partial_\rho K^{\rho\mu\nu}$, with
$$
K^{\rho\mu\nu}=\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\electric{A}^\nu,
\qquad K^{\rho\mu\nu}=-K^{\mu\rho\nu}.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
+\partial_\rho(\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\electric{A}^\nu)
={}&-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial^\nu\electric{A}_\rho
+\frac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\alpha\beta}\electric{F}^{\alpha\beta}\\
&+(\partial_\rho\electric{F}^{\mu\rho})\electric{A}^\nu
+\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial_\rho\electric{A}^\nu.
\end{aligned}
$$
In vacuum, $\partial_\rho\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}=0$. Thus
$$
\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\electric{F}^{\nu}{}_\rho
+\frac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\alpha\beta}\electric{F}^{\alpha\beta}.
$$
The product of field strengths is symmetric in $\mu,\nu$, so
$\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}=\energy{T}^{\nu\mu}$. The expression is gauge invariant because it contains only field strengths.

> [!note] Correction to source signs
> The source writes the improvement with $\electric{F}^{\rho\mu}$ instead of $\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}$, and its first boxed result has a plus sign before the product of field strengths. These signs do not give the stated physical energy density. The expressions above agree with Exercise 5.2 and with the standard tensor that the source itself uses in its component calculation.

Using $\electric{F}^{0i}=-\electric{E}^i$,
$\electric{F}^{ij}=-\epsilon^{ijk}\magnetic{B}^k$, and
$$
\electric{F}_{\alpha\beta}\electric{F}^{\alpha\beta}
=-2(\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}-\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{\,2}),
$$
one obtains
$$
\energy{T}^{00}
=\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}
-\frac12(\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}-\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{\,2})
=\frac12(\electric{\mathbf E}^{\,2}+\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{\,2}),
$$
$$
\energy{T}^{0i}
=(\electric{\mathbf E}\times\magnetic{\mathbf B})^i=\energy{S}^i.
$$
These are the electromagnetic energy density and Poynting vector in natural units.
