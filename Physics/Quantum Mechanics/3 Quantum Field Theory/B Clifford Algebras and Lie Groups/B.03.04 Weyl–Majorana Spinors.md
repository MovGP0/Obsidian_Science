> [!TLDR]
> For Lorentz signature (1, d−1), ordinary Majorana and Weyl conditions can hold together in dimensions congruent to two modulo eight.

For Lorentz signature $(1,d-1)$, the simultaneous **Majorana–Weyl** condition is possible in dimensions
$$
d\equiv2\pmod8,
$$
that is, $d=2,10,18,\ldots$.
The resulting spinor has
$$
2^{d/2-1}
$$
real components.

## Why two separate tests are needed

First, an ordinary Lorentz-invariant real structure must exist. Second, it must preserve a chiral eigenspace. Only then can the two reductions be applied independently. These conditions and the signature dependence are derived in [Van Proeyen, §3.2, especially (3.28) and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9910030#page=22). The statement here assumes one time direction and ordinary Majorana spinors; symplectic reality conditions have different content.

As a count check, a complex Dirac spinor in even $d$ has $2^{d/2}$ complex components. A Weyl projection halves that complex dimension; a compatible real condition replaces the remaining complex components by the same number of real components. For $d=10$, this gives $32$ complex Dirac components, $16$ complex Weyl components, and $16$ real Majorana–Weyl components. For $d=2$, it gives one real component.

Four-dimensional Lorentzian spinors give the useful boundary case: the ordinary Majorana conjugation exchanges the two Weyl spaces. A nonzero spinor cannot satisfy both conditions there. These counts describe representation components before any field equation removes further data.
