> [!TLDR]
> Spinor representations can admit Dirac, chiral, or reality conditions, depending on dimension and signature.

The following sections describe Dirac, Weyl, Majorana, and combined spinor conditions, followed by Fierz identities.

## Three different restrictions

Use [[B.03.01 Dirac Spinors]] to define the full module and its adjoint. Then separate a complex-linear restriction, chirality, from an antilinear restriction, the Majorana condition. The former selects an eigenspace of a volume element; the latter selects fixed points of a compatible conjugation. A component count alone cannot show that both restrictions are compatible.

In four-dimensional Lorentz signature, a Dirac spinor has four complex components. A Weyl spinor has two complex components; a Majorana spinor has four real components. These equal real counts do not make their transformation laws identical. [[B.03.04 Weyl–Majorana Spinors]] gives the dimensions where both conditions can hold at once.

Finally, [[B.03.05 Fierz Identities]] concerns completeness of matrices acting on spinors. First specify whether their components commute or anticommute. A matrix identity does not include the sign from exchanging fermion fields. These definitions organize possible QFT field content; they do not by themselves give equations of motion or measured particle properties.
