---
title: "Definition"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A Clifford algebra is a unital associative algebra generated by a vector space, with the symmetric product of generators fixed by a nondegenerate quadratic form.

Let $V$ be a $d$-dimensional vector space over a field $\mathbb F$, with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form $Q$.
The **Clifford algebra** $\operatorname{Cl}(V,Q)$ is the unital associative algebra generated by the elements of $V$, subject to
$$
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}
=\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu+\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu
=2g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1.
\tag{B.1}
$$
Here $g^{\mu\nu}$ represents the form associated with $Q$ in the chosen basis.

In $d$ Euclidean dimensions,
$$
g^{\mu\nu}=\delta^{\mu\nu}.
$$
For signature $(p,q)$, with $p+q=d$,
$$
g^{\mu\nu}
=\operatorname{diag}(
\underbrace{+1,\ldots,+1}_{p},
\underbrace{-1,\ldots,-1}_{q}).
$$
