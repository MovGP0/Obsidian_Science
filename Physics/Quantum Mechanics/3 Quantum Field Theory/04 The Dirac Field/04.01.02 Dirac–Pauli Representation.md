---
title: "Dirac–Pauli Representation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Dirac–Pauli representation uses Pauli matrices in two-by-two blocks and is convenient at low energies.

Use

$$
\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma^{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{i}\\-\sigma^{i}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad i=1,2,3,
$$

where the blocks are two dimensional and the sigma matrices are the Pauli matrices. Direct multiplication gives $(\gamma^{0})^{2}=1$, $(\gamma^{i})^{2}=-1$, and $\gamma^{0}\gamma^{i}+\gamma^{i}\gamma^{0}=0$. Define

$$
\gamma^{5}\equiv i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

It obeys $(\gamma^{5})^{2}=1$ and anticommutes with every gamma matrix.

Another common choice is the Weyl or chiral representation:

$$
\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma^{i}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{i}\\-\sigma^{i}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma^{5}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4.5}
$$

The last matrix is diagonal, making chiral projection immediate. Most of the following treatment is independent of representation.
