---
title: "Hamiltonian Operator in Mode Variables"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Fermionic reordering gives positive particle and antiparticle energies after subtracting the vacuum constant.

Substitute the mode expansion into the Hamiltonian density and integrate over space. The normal-ordered result is

$$
\energy{H}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\sum_s(b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}b^{s}_{\mathbf p}+d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}d^{s}_{\mathbf p}). \tag{4.37}
$$

Before normal ordering,

$$
\energy{H}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\sum_s(b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}b^{s}_{\mathbf p}-d^{s}_{\mathbf p}d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p})+\text{const.} \tag{4.38}
$$

Anticommutation gives $d^{s}_{\mathbf p}d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}=-d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}d^{s}_{\mathbf p}+(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}(0)$ formally at infinite volume. The vacuum sum has the opposite sign to the bosonic case. Normal ordering subtracts it. Both particles and antiparticles then contribute positive energy, giving a stable free vacuum.

The printed k/p integration labels follow the source convention noted in the field expansion.

For each normalized antiparticle mode, the relation between annihilation followed by creation and the reverse order contains a constant and a minus sign. The minus in (4.38) therefore becomes a plus in front of the antiparticle occupation. This is where anticommutation restores a lower bound; normal ordering alone could not fix a negative coefficient of occupation.

The subtraction chooses the zero of free-vacuum energy. It preserves energy differences and does not remove particle excitations. A one-antiparticle state must raise the normal-ordered energy by its positive on-shell energy, just as a one-particle state does. This check applies to the free Minkowski theory considered here; normal ordering is not a general prescription for the energy of an interacting vacuum.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Hamiltonian | The total free-fermion energy. |
| $\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode energy | The positive energy of either particle branch. |
| $\mechanical{k},\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The source integration variable and mode label. |
