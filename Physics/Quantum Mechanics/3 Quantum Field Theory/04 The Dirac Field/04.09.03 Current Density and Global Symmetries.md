---
title: "Current Density and Global Symmetries"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The free vector current is conserved, while a nonzero mass breaks axial-current conservation.

The vector current $\signal{V}^\mu=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}$ is conserved for the free field. The axial current $\signal{A}^\mu=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\signal{\psi}$ is conserved only in the massless case:
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu
=2i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^5\signal{\psi}. \tag{4.62}
$$
For $\mechanical{m}=0$, the theory has a chiral $U(1)_A$ symmetry. Its field rotation is $\signal{\psi}\to e^{i\signal{\alpha}\gamma^5}\signal{\psi}$.

The vector phase rotation multiplies both chiral components equally, so a Dirac mass is compatible with it. An axial rotation gives the components opposite phases; the mass term links them and breaks that symmetry. This explains the mass factor in (4.62) before doing the derivative calculation.

The divergence has four energy powers: one derivative acting on a dimension-three current matches a mass times a dimension-three bilinear. Setting the mass to zero gives the classical free conservation law exactly. This argument is not by itself a proof of axial-current conservation after quantizing an interacting gauge theory; that requires a separate analysis of the quantum symmetry. For an integrated conserved charge, suitable spatial boundary flux conditions are also required.
