---
title: "Fermionic Normal Ordering"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Normal ordering puts creation operators before annihilation operators and includes a sign for each fermion exchange.

Fermionic normal ordering is defined as in the bosonic case, with the fermionic exchange sign included. Use the frequency-part convention
$$
\signal{\psi}^+\propto b,\qquad
\signal{\psi}^-\propto d^\dagger,\qquad
\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{+}\propto d,\qquad
\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{-}\propto b^\dagger.
$$
For two fields,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal N(\signal{\psi}_\alpha\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta)
={}&\signal{\psi}_\alpha^-\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^-
+\signal{\psi}_\alpha^-\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^+\\
&-\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^-\signal{\psi}_\alpha^+
+\signal{\psi}_\alpha^+\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^+.
\end{aligned} \tag{4.96}
$$
The third term has a minus sign because the annihilation part $\signal{\psi}_\alpha^+$ must pass the creation part $\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^-$. In general, all annihilation operators move to the right of all creation operators. The sign is $(-1)^P$, where $P$ counts the exchanges modulo two.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi},\bar{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac fields | The fields whose frequency parts are normally ordered. |
