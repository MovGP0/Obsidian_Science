---
title: "Constrained Structure of the Hamiltonian Operator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The momentum conjugate to the Dirac adjoint vanishes, so the Dirac theory is a constrained Hamiltonian system.

As in §4.2, the momentum conjugate to $\bar{\signal{\psi}}$ vanishes identically: $\bar{\signal{\pi}}=0$. Thus the Dirac Lagrangian defines a constrained system in the sense of Dirac–Bergmann. The source gives the primary constraint
$$
\phi_\alpha\equiv\bar{\signal{\pi}}_\alpha\approx0. \tag{4.103}
$$
The symbol $\approx$ means “weakly zero”: the equation holds on the constraint surface.

Consistency requires $\dot\phi=\{\phi,\energy{H}_T\}\approx0$, where $\energy{H}_T$ is the total Hamiltonian. The source states that the calculation gives the equation of motion for $\signal{\psi}$ without further constraints and classifies the system as **second class**.

> [!note]
> The vanishing momentum is only one part of the Dirac field's primary constraint set. The momentum conjugate to $\signal{\psi}$ is also fixed by the field, rather than by an invertible velocity relation. The second-class classification and the Dirac bracket require the full constraint set and a consistent Grassmann bracket convention.
