### Exercise 4.1 — Clifford algebra in arbitrary dimension

Show that in $d$ spacetime dimensions the trace of two gamma matrices is $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu)=d\,g^{\mu\nu}$ and the contraction is $\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu=d\,1$. Verify that $d=4$ gives the standard formulas (4.73)–(4.74).

> [!note]
> The trace statement is reproduced from the source. In general, its coefficient is $\operatorname{tr}(1)$, the spinor-representation dimension, rather than the spacetime dimension $d$. For irreducible complex Dirac matrices this is $2^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}$. The contraction $\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu=d\,1$ does use the spacetime dimension.

### Exercise 4.8 — First-order anomalous magnetic moment

In QED, the corrected interaction vertex has the form
$$
\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\gamma^\mu\electric{F}_1(\mechanical{q}^{\,2})
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\magnetic{F}_2(\mechanical{q}^{\,2}), \tag{4.119}
$$
with $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}$. Use the Gordon identity (4.106) to show that as $\mechanical{q}\to0$, the form factor $\magnetic{F}_2(0)$ contributes to the electron magnetic moment as
$$
\magnetic{\mu}=(1+\magnetic{F}_2(0))\magnetic{\mu}_B,
\qquad
\magnetic{\mu}_B=\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}}.
$$
Here $\magnetic{\mu}_B$ is the Bohr magneton. The explicit first-order result $\magnetic{F}_2(0)=\electric{\alpha}/(2\pi)$ is Schwinger's result from 1948.

### Exercise 4.10 — Weyl spinors and chirality

(a) Starting from (4.66)–(4.67), show that for $\mechanical{m}=0$, plane-wave solutions with definite helicity coincide with the eigenstates selected by the chiral projectors $P_L,P_R$.

(b) For $\mechanical{m}\ne0$ and $\mechanical{\mathbf p}\ne0$, the source asks you to show that chirality eigenstates are not helicity eigenstates. Calculate the expectation value of $\mechanical{h}$ in a chiral eigenstate with momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ and mass $\mechanical{m}$.

> [!note]
> The statement in part (b) is too strong. The chirality and helicity operators commute, so simultaneous eigenvectors exist. At nonzero mass, a state of definite chirality alone is not a stationary positive-energy Dirac solution. A helicity expectation value also needs the spin state; chirality, mass, and momentum alone do not determine it. Part (a) must distinguish particle and antiparticle helicity conventions.

(c) Discuss why chirality, unlike massive-particle helicity, is invariant under proper Lorentz transformations.

### Exercise 4.12 — Causality of the propagator

The source defines $\signal{\Delta S}(\mechanical{x})\equiv\signal{S}(\mechanical{x}^+)-\signal{S}(\mechanical{x}^-)$ as the difference of the positive- and negative-energy propagator parts, called the spectral function. It denotes these parts schematically by vacuum expectation values of $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}(0)$.

(a) Show that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
={}&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)
\langle0|\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{y})|0\rangle\\
&-\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)
\langle0|\bar{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})|0\rangle,
\end{aligned}
$$
with the appropriate spinor-component order understood. Emphasize the relative minus sign from fermionic anticommutation.

(b) Prove that for spacelike separation $(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})^2<0$, $\signal{\Delta S}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=0$, which verifies microcausality.

(c) Compare with the bosonic case and discuss the role of signs in the anticommutation relations.

> [!note]
> The source does not give an unambiguous signed definition of both spectral parts. For fermions, the causal distribution is the vacuum expectation of the field anticommutator. A difference of spectral parts represents it only if the negative-energy part includes the required sign. Do not identify it with an unsigned difference of the two Wightman functions.

## Source solutions

The following solutions are supplied in Appendix D of the source.

### Solution 4.1 — Clifford algebra in arbitrary dimension

In $d$ spacetime dimensions with mostly-minus metric,
$$
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1_N.
$$
The minimal complex spinor-representation dimension is
$$
N=2^{\lfloor d/2\rfloor}.
$$
Thus $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf1_N)=N$; in $d=4$, $N=4$.

#### Trace of two gamma matrices

Taking the trace of the Clifford relation and using cyclicity,
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu)
&=\operatorname{tr}\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}\\
&=2g^{\mu\nu}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf1_N)
=2Ng^{\mu\nu}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu)=Ng^{\mu\nu}.
$$
For $N=4$, this is $4g^{\mu\nu}$.

> [!note] Correction to the exercise statement
> The exercise prompt prints $d\,g^{\mu\nu}$ for this trace. The supplied source solution correctly distinguishes the representation dimension $N$ from spacetime dimension $d$. They happen to agree in four dimensions.

#### Contracted gamma matrices

The Clifford relation gives
$$
\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu
=\frac12\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma_\mu\}
=\frac12(2g^\mu{}_\mu)\mathbf1_N
=d\,\mathbf1_N.
$$
Here $g^\mu{}_\mu=\delta^\mu{}_\mu=d$.
For $d=4$, this gives $4\mathbf1_4$, as in (4.73).

> [!note] Dimensional regularization
> In dimensional regularization, $d$ is analytically continued, for example to $d=4-2\varepsilon$. The spinor trace normalization is conventionally kept at $N=4$ in this calculation. This formal trace convention is distinct from the integer-dimensional minimal-representation formula above.

### Solution 4.2 — Canonical anticommutators and inversion

Start from the mode expansion
$$
\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}}}
\sum_s\left[
b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}u_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
+d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}v_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
\right].
$$

> [!note]
> The source uses a $d^3k$ integration measure while labeling the modes by $p$. A single dummy momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ is used here.

The Hermitian-conjugate field obeys
$$
\signal{\psi}_\beta^\dagger=(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^0)_\beta.
$$
Its mode expansion is
$$
\signal{\psi}_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf q}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf q}}}
\sum_{s'}\left[
b_{\mathbf q}^{\,s'\dagger}u_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf q},s')
e^{+i\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{y}}
+d_{\mathbf q}^{\,s'}v_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf q},s')
e^{-i\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{y}}
\right].
$$

Evaluate the field anticommutator at equal times, $\mechanical{x}^0=\mechanical{y}^0=\mechanical{t}$.
Only the particle and antiparticle mode pairs survive:
$$
\{b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s},b_{\mathbf q}^{\,s'\dagger}\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{ss'}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q}),
$$
$$
\{d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger},d_{\mathbf q}^{\,s'}\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{ss'}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q}).
$$
Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{ \signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),
\signal{\psi}_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}
={}&\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}}\sum_s\Bigl[
u_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)u_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)
e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&\qquad+
v_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)v_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)
e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigr]_{\mechanical{x}^0=\mechanical{y}^0}.
\end{aligned}
$$
At equal times,
$$
\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}).
$$
In the second term, change the spatial integration variable
$\mechanical{\mathbf p}\to-\mechanical{\mathbf p}$.
The measure and the positive energy are unchanged.

Use the completeness relations and multiply on the right by $\gamma^0$:
$$
\sum_s u_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)u_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)
=[(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^0]_{\alpha\beta},
$$
$$
\sum_s v_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)v_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)
=[(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m})\gamma^0]_{\alpha\beta}.
$$
After the spatial reflection in the second term, the spatial-gamma and mass terms cancel in the sum:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left[
(\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\gamma^0-\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}+\mechanical{m})
+(\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\gamma^0+\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{m})
\right]\gamma^0\\
&\qquad=2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{ \signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),
\signal{\psi}_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\delta_{\alpha\beta}
e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf p}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}\\
&=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}).
\end{aligned}
$$

### Solution 4.3 — Fermion Fock number

**(a)** In the basis $|0\rangle=(1,0)^T$, $|1\rangle=(0,1)^T$, choose
$$
b=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
b^\dagger=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $b|0\rangle=0$, $b^\dagger|0\rangle=|1\rangle$,
$b|1\rangle=|0\rangle$, and $b^\dagger|1\rangle=0$.
Direct multiplication gives
$$
bb^\dagger=\operatorname{diag}(1,0),\qquad
b^\dagger b=\operatorname{diag}(0,1),
$$
so $\{b,b^\dagger\}=\mathbf1$ and $b^2=(b^\dagger)^2=0$.

**(b)** The number operator is
$$
\hat{\signal{n}}=b^\dagger b=\operatorname{diag}(0,1).
$$
Its eigenvalues are zero and one. More generally,
$$
\hat{\signal{n}}^2
=b^\dagger bb^\dagger b
=b^\dagger(1-b^\dagger b)b
=b^\dagger b-(b^\dagger)^2b^2
=\hat{\signal{n}}.
$$
Thus $\hat{\signal{n}}(\hat{\signal{n}}-1)=0$.

**(c)** With $\energy{H}=\energy{E}\hat{\signal{n}}$,
$$
[\energy{H},b]
=\energy{E}[\hat{\signal{n}},b]
=\energy{E}(b^\dagger bb-bb^\dagger b).
$$
Use $b^2=0$ and $bb^\dagger=1-b^\dagger b$:
$$
[\energy{H},b]
=\energy{E}\bigl[0-(1-b^\dagger b)b\bigr]
=-\energy{E}b.
$$
Similarly,
$$
[\energy{H},b^\dagger]=+\energy{E}b^\dagger.
$$
The creation operator raises the energy by $\energy{E}$, and the annihilation operator lowers it by $\energy{E}$. This agrees with
$$
\energy{H}|1\rangle=\energy{E}|1\rangle,\qquad \energy{H}|0\rangle=0.
$$

### Solution 4.4 — Massless position-space propagator

For $\mechanical{m}=0$, the momentum-space Feynman propagator is
$$
\tilde{\signal{S}}_F(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i\not{\mechanical{p}}}{\mechanical{p}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
Write $\not{\mechanical{p}}=\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p}_\mu$ and use
$$
\mechanical{p}_\mu e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
=i\partial_\mu e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}.
$$
Then
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{p}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p}_\mu}{\mechanical{p}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}.
$$

Define the massless scalar propagator by
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})\big|_{\mechanical{m}=0}
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{p}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{p}^2+i\epsilon}e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}.
$$
Move the derivative outside the Fourier integral. The factor from the derivative gives
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x})
=i\not{\partial}_x\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})\big|_{\mechanical{m}=0}.
$$
In four spacetime dimensions,
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})\big|_{\mechanical{m}=0}
=-\frac1{4\pi^2(\mechanical{x}^2-i\epsilon)}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x})
=\frac{i\not{\mechanical{x}}}{2\pi^2(\mechanical{x}^2-i\epsilon)^2}.
$$
These coordinate-space prescriptions denote the distributional limit $\epsilon\to0^+$. The check is
$$
(i\not{\partial})(i\not{\partial})\signal{\Delta}_F
=-\Box\signal{\Delta}_F
=i\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x})\mathbf1,
$$
and hence
$$
i\not{\partial}\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x})
=i\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x})\mathbf1,
$$
as required by (4.48) at zero mass.

### Solution 4.5 — Noether current and charge

#### On-shell conservation

For $\electric{J}^\mu=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}$,
$$
\partial_\mu\electric{J}^\mu
=(\partial_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}})\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}
+\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
The Dirac equation and its adjoint give
$$
\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}=-i\mechanical{m}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
(\partial_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}})\gamma^\mu
=i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}.
$$
Thus
$$
\partial_\mu\electric{J}^\mu
=i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}
-i\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}=0.
$$

#### Charge in the mode basis

Insert (4.27)–(4.28) into
$$
\electric{Q}=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
\signal{\psi}^\dagger\signal{\psi}.
$$
Spatial integration produces $(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q})$ or $(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}+\mechanical{\mathbf q})$, according to the exponentials. The mixed terms vanish by spinor orthogonality. As in Solution 4.2, the remaining terms are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{Q}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}}
\sum_{s,s'}\Bigl[
&b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s'}
u^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)u(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s')\\
+&d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s'\dagger}
v^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)v(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s')
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$

Before the orthogonality relations are applied, the mixed contribution to the same integrand is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}d_{-\mathbf p}^{\,s'\dagger}
u^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)v(-\mechanical{\mathbf p},s')
e^{2i\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\mechanical{t}}\\
&\quad+
d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}b_{-\mathbf p}^{\,s'}
v^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)u(-\mechanical{\mathbf p},s')
e^{-2i\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\mechanical{t}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here the reflected on-shell four-momentum is $(\energy{E}_{\mathbf p},-\mechanical{\mathbf p})$. Use
$$
u^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)u(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s')
=v^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)v(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s')
=2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\delta_{ss'},
$$
$$
u^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)v(-\mechanical{\mathbf p},s')
=v^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)u(-\mechanical{\mathbf p},s')=0.
$$
The oscillating mixed terms vanish, leaving
$$
\electric{Q}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\sum_s\left(
b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}
+d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}
\right).
$$
The antiparticle anticommutator gives
$$
d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(0)
-d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}.
$$
The delta term is the infinite vacuum constant. Normal ordering removes it:
$$
:\electric{Q}:
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\sum_s\left(
b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}
-d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}
\right)
=\signal{N}_b-\signal{N}_d.
$$
This is (4.40): particles and antiparticles carry opposite charge.

### Solution 4.6 — Charge-conjugation symmetry of the propagator

Let $\mathscr C$ denote charge conjugation on the state space and $C$ the spinor matrix. The field transformations are
$$
\mathscr C\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\mathscr C^{-1}
=\eta_C C\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{\,T}(\mechanical{x}),
$$
$$
\mathscr C\bar{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{x})\mathscr C^{-1}
=-\eta_C^*\signal{\psi}^{\,T}(\mechanical{x})C^{-1}.
$$
The phase obeys $|\eta_C|=1$. Assume the free vacuum is invariant. For the time-ordered two-point function,
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})_{\alpha\beta}
=\langle0|T\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})
\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle,
$$
the transformations give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})_{\alpha\beta}
=-|\eta_C|^2C_{\alpha\gamma}
\langle0|T\{\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\gamma(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\psi}_\delta(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
(C^{-1})_{\delta\beta}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Interchanging the two fermionic fields in the time-ordered product adds a second minus sign:
$$
\langle0|T\{\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\gamma(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\psi}_\delta(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=-\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{y}-\mechanical{x})_{\delta\gamma}.
$$
Consequently,
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=C\signal{S}_F^{\,T}(\mechanical{y}-\mechanical{x})C^{-1}.
$$

> [!note]
> The source writes the time-ordered function as $iS_F$ in this intermediate derivation. Here it is denoted by $\signal{S}_F$, as in Solutions 4.4 and 4.12. The common overall factor cancels from the charge-conjugation identity.

Fourier transformation gives
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{p}}{(2\pi)^4}
\tilde{\signal{S}}_F(\mechanical{p})e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})},
$$
while the reversed separation replaces $\tilde{\signal{S}}_F(\mechanical{p})$ by $\tilde{\signal{S}}_F(-\mechanical{p})$. Thus
$$
\tilde{\signal{S}}_F(\mechanical{p})
=C\tilde{\signal{S}}_F^{\,T}(-\mechanical{p})C^{-1}.
$$
For an explicit check, use
$$
\tilde{\signal{S}}_F(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})}{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
C\tilde{\signal{S}}_F^{\,T}(-\mechanical{p})C^{-1}
&=\frac{iC(-\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})^TC^{-1}}
{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}\\
&=\frac{i[-C(\gamma^\mu)^TC^{-1}\mechanical{p}_\mu+\mechanical{m}]}
{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $C(\gamma^\mu)^TC^{-1}=-\gamma^\mu$, this equals $\tilde{\signal{S}}_F(\mechanical{p})$.

### Solution 4.7 — Gordon identity

The equal-mass on-shell spinors satisfy
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}u(\mechanical{p})=\mechanical{m}u(\mechanical{p}),\qquad
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\not{\mechanical{p}}'
=\mechanical{m}\bar u(\mechanical{p}').
$$
Multiply by $\gamma^\mu$ on the appropriate side and add:
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
+\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}u(\mechanical{p})
=2\mechanical{m}\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p}).
$$
The Clifford algebra gives
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu
=2\mechanical{p}'^\mu-\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}',
\qquad
\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}
=2\mechanical{p}^\mu-\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu,
$$
and hence
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu+\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}
=2\mechanical{p}'^\mu+2\mechanical{p}^\mu
-(\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}'+\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu).
$$
With
$$
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac i2[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu],
\qquad
\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu=g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1-i\sigma^{\mu\nu},
\qquad
\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu=g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1+i\sigma^{\mu\nu},
$$
the subtracted term is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}'+\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu
&=\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\mechanical{p}'_\nu
+\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p}_\nu\\
&=(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu
-i\sigma^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p})_\nu.
\end{aligned}
$$

Combining the terms gives
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu+\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}
=(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu
+i\sigma^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p})_\nu.
$$
For $\mechanical{m}\ne0$, division by $2\mechanical{m}$ gives the Gordon identity (4.106):
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\frac{(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p})_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
$$

### Solution 4.8 — Anomalous magnetic moment

The corrected vertex is
$$
\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\gamma^\mu\electric{F}_1(\mechanical{q}^2)
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\magnetic{F}_2(\mechanical{q}^2),
\qquad \mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}.
$$
Insert the Gordon identity:
$$
\bar u\Gamma^\mu u
=\bar u\left[
\frac{(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu}{2\mechanical{m}}\electric{F}_1
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
(\electric{F}_1+\magnetic{F}_2)
\right]u.
$$
The first, convective term gives the minimal charge coupling. The second gives the magnetic dipole coupling, so
$$
\frac{\magnetic{g}_s}{2}=\electric{F}_1(0)+\magnetic{F}_2(0).
$$
Charge normalization fixes $\electric{F}_1(0)=1$. In the long-wavelength limit $\mechanical{q}\to0$, the spin-one-half magnetic moment is
$$
\magnetic{\mu}
=\magnetic{g}_s\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}}\frac12
=[1+\magnetic{F}_2(0)]\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}}
=[1+\magnetic{F}_2(0)]\magnetic{\mu}_B.
$$
This uses the source's positive moment magnitude; the electron's signed charge fixes the direction relative to its spin. The anomaly is
$$
\magnetic{a}_e=\frac{\magnetic{g}_s-2}{2}=\magnetic{F}_2(0).
$$
At first order in QED, Schwinger's result is
$$
\magnetic{a}_e^{(1)}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
\approx1.16\times10^{-3}.
$$
The calculation evaluates the one-loop vertex diagram with a virtual photon and extracts the coefficient of $i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu/(2\mechanical{m})$ from the renormalized vertex. Chapter 10 gives the details.

### Solution 4.9 — Traces with the Levi-Civita tensor

**(a)** With $\gamma^5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ and $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$,
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^5)
=-4i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.
$$
Cyclicity permits $\gamma^5$ at the beginning or end of the trace. Check the coefficient with $\mu\nu\rho\sigma=0123$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3\gamma^5)
&=i\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3
\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3)\\
&=i(-1)^6\operatorname{tr}
\bigl[(\gamma^0)^2(\gamma^1)^2(\gamma^2)^2(\gamma^3)^2\bigr]\\
&=i(-1)^3\,4=-4i.
\end{aligned}
$$
This uses $(\gamma^0)^2=\mathbf1$ and $(\gamma^i)^2=-\mathbf1$.

**(b)** For arbitrary algebraic four-vectors $a,b$,
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\not a\not b\gamma^5)=0.
$$
Expand it as $a_\mu b_\nu\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^5)$. The trace tensor is antisymmetric because the Clifford anticommutator is proportional to $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^5)=0$. There is no nonzero Lorentz-invariant antisymmetric pseudotensor with only two free indices in four dimensions. Thus this trace vanishes.

> [!note] Correction to the source proof
> The source also attempts to obtain a minus sign by cycling $\gamma^5$ past two gamma matrices. There are two anticommutations, so the sign is positive. That step does not prove the result. The invariant-tensor argument above is the source's valid alternative.

**(c)** Contract the result from part (a) with four algebraic vectors:
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\not a\not b\not c\not d\gamma^5)
=-4i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}a_\mu b_\nu c_\rho d_\sigma.
$$

The $V-A$ weak current $\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma^5)\signal{\psi}$ produces interference terms with Levi-Civita tensors. These terms contribute to parity-sensitive correlations, including forward–backward asymmetries in suitable weak processes. These trace statements use four-dimensional gamma matrices.

### Solution 4.10 — Weyl spinors and chirality

**(a)** For $\mechanical{m}=0$, the Dirac equation separates in the Weyl representation:
$$
i\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}_L=0,
\qquad
i\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}_R=0.
$$

For a positive-energy plane wave
$\signal{\psi}_L(\mechanical{x})\propto\chi_L e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}$ with $\mechanical{p}^2=0$, use
$\sigma^\mu=(\mathbf1,\boldsymbol\sigma)$ and
$\bar\sigma^\mu=(\mathbf1,-\boldsymbol\sigma)$ in the mostly-minus metric. Then
$$
\bar\sigma^\mu\mechanical{p}_\mu\chi_L
=(\energy{E}+\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p})\chi_L=0.
$$
For $\energy{E}=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|>0$,
$$
\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf p}\,\chi_L=-\chi_L,
\qquad
\hat{\mechanical{h}}\signal{\psi}_L=-\frac12\signal{\psi}_L.
$$
Similarly, $\hat{\mechanical{h}}\signal{\psi}_R=+\frac12\signal{\psi}_R$. Thus positive-energy massless particle solutions of definite chirality have definite helicity. The particle statement must be distinguished from the corresponding antiparticle helicity convention.

> [!note] Correction to the source sign
> The source first writes the left-handed equation with $\energy{E}-\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ and obtains positive helicity, then states the negative-helicity result. With the Weyl equations above, the left-handed positive-energy equation contains the plus sign shown here.

**(b)** The source considers the chiral projection
$$
\signal{\psi}_L=P_Lu(\mechanical{p},s)
=\frac{1-\gamma^5}{2}u(\mechanical{p},s).
$$

> [!note] Correction to the massive-state argument
> The source claims $[\hat{\mechanical{h}},\gamma^5]\ne0$ at nonzero mass and assigns $\langle\mechanical{h}\rangle_L=-|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|/(2\energy{E})$ without fixing the spin state. These claims are incorrect. In the Weyl basis, $\hat{\mechanical{h}}=\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf p},\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf p})$ commutes with $\gamma^5=\operatorname{diag}(-\mathbf1,\mathbf1)$ for every mass. If $u$ has definite helicity $\mechanical{h}=\pm\tfrac12$, its nonzero left projection has that same helicity. For a general spin superposition, an expectation value needs the spin amplitudes.
>
> The source also uses $\bar{\signal{\psi}}_L\hat{\mechanical{h}}\signal{\psi}_L$ in its expectation-value numerator. The positive-norm one-particle expression is $\signal{\psi}_L^\dagger\hat{\mechanical{h}}\signal{\psi}_L/(\signal{\psi}_L^\dagger\signal{\psi}_L)$.

The actual massive-particle distinction is that a purely chiral projection is not by itself a stationary positive-energy solution of the massive Dirac equation: the mass couples the left and right components. For a positive-energy helicity eigenstate,
$$
\langle\gamma^5\rangle=2\mechanical{h}\frac{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|}{\energy{E}}.
$$
Thus a helicity eigenstate approaches a chirality eigenstate in the ultrarelativistic limit. This is a chirality expectation in a helicity eigenstate, not the source's claimed helicity expectation in an unspecified chiral state.

**(c)** Chirality is preserved by proper Lorentz transformations because
$$
[\gamma^5,S^{\mu\nu}]=0,
\qquad
S^{\mu\nu}=\frac i4[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu].
$$
For a massive particle, a longitudinal boost can overtake the particle and reverse its momentum relative to its spin. Its helicity then changes sign. Parity interchanges the two chiralities, so the chirality statement applies to proper Lorentz transformations.

### Solution 4.11 — The Majorana field

**(a)** The Majorana condition is
$$
\signal{\psi}^{\,c}=C\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{\,T}=\signal{\psi}.
$$
The charge-conjugated field transforms in the same spinor representation as $\signal{\psi}$ under proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations. The condition is therefore Lorentz covariant.

For a free field with real mass, use $C(\gamma^\mu)^TC^{-1}=-\gamma^\mu$ and the adjoint Dirac equation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}^{\,c}
&=-C\left[i(\partial_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}})\gamma^\mu
+\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\right]^T\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus charge conjugation preserves the free Dirac equation.

If the Majorana condition holds initially, the free evolution preserves it because $\signal{\psi}$ and $\signal{\psi}^{\,c}$ obey the same equation.

**(b)** The correctly normalized Majorana Lagrangian is
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_M
=\frac12\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}
=\frac i2\bar{\signal{\psi}}\not{\partial}\signal{\psi}
-\frac{\mechanical{m}}2\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}.
$$
The factor one half accounts for the fact that the field and its charge conjugate are not independent.

> [!note] Correction to the source normalization
> The source presents the factor one half as if the full Dirac Lagrangian became half of itself plus a total derivative after the Majorana constraint. A total derivative does not produce this overall factor. The Majorana action is defined with the factor one half to give the correct normalization for the independent degrees of freedom.
>
> With $\signal{\psi}=C\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{\,T}$ and $C^T=-C$, transposition gives $\bar{\signal{\psi}}=-\signal{\psi}^{\,T}C^{-1}$. The source omits this minus sign.

**(c)** The vector current becomes
$$
\electric{J}^\mu
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}
=-\signal{\psi}^{\,T}C^{-1}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
The matrix in this bilinear is symmetric:
$$
(C^{-1}\gamma^\mu)^T
=(\gamma^\mu)^T(C^{-1})^T
=-(\gamma^\mu)^TC^{-1}
=C^{-1}\gamma^\mu.
$$
For anticommuting field components, a bilinear $\signal{\psi}^{\,T}A\signal{\psi}$ with symmetric $A$ vanishes. Hence the Majorana vector current, and its associated charge, vanish:
$$
\electric{J}^\mu=0,\qquad
\electric{Q}=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\electric{J}^0=0.
$$
A single Majorana field has no continuous phase symmetry of the Dirac $U(1)$ type.

### Solution 4.12 — Causality of the propagator

**(a)** In component notation, define the two Wightman functions with a fixed external index order:
$$
\signal{S}^+_{\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\langle0|\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})
\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})|0\rangle,
$$
$$
\signal{S}^-_{\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\langle0|\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})
\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})|0\rangle.
$$
The minus sign from fermionic time ordering gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
={}&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)\signal{S}^+(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})\\
&-\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)\signal{S}^-(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
$$

**(b)** The causal combination is the anticommutator:
$$
\langle0|\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x}),
\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=\signal{S}^+_{\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
+\signal{S}^-_{\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
The relative sign here is a plus because $\signal{S}^-$ was defined with the reversed field order and no extra sign.

The mode expansion gives the operator anticommutator
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x}),\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}}\Bigl[
&(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}
e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
+&(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}
e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
Define the scalar Pauli–Jordan combination
$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}}
\left[e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
-e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\right].
$$
Then
$$
\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x}),\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}
=(i\not{\partial}_x+\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
At spacelike separation a Lorentz frame exists with $\mechanical{x}^0=\mechanical{y}^0$. In that frame, the scalar integral is odd under $\mechanical{\mathbf p}\to-\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, so it vanishes. Lorentz invariance makes it zero throughout the spacelike region; its derivatives there also vanish. Therefore
$$
\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x}),\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}=0,
\qquad (\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})^2<0.
$$
This is fermionic microcausality. It also makes the consistently signed spectral combination $\signal{\Delta S}$ vanish there.

**(c)** For a scalar boson, the analogous mode calculation gives a commutator that vanishes at spacelike separation. For fermion fields the anticommutator vanishes. Local observables with even fermion number, such as bilinears, consequently commute at spacelike separation. The two algebraic choices are consistent with the spin–statistics relation.

> [!note] Pauli–Jordan convention
> With the integral definition of $\signal{\Delta}$ above, the scalar-field commutator equals $\signal{\Delta}$ itself. The source writes $i\signal{\Delta}$ in part (c) while keeping that integral unchanged. The alternative convention in which the commutator is $i\signal{\Delta}$ requires an extra factor $1/i$ in the definition of $\signal{\Delta}$.
