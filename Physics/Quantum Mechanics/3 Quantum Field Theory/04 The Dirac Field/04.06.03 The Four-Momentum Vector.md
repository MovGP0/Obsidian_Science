---
title: "The Four-Momentum Vector"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Fermions and antifermions both contribute positive on-shell energy and the momentum carried by their mode label.

The momentum density gives

$$
\mechanical{P}^{\mu}=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\mechanical{p}^{\mu}\sum_s(b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}b^{s}_{\mathbf p}+d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}d^{s}_{\mathbf p}). \tag{4.41}
$$

Both particle types contribute with the momentum direction indicated by their mode label.

This is the normal-ordered free-field translation generator. Its time component reproduces the Hamiltonian, so the same ordering and momentum-normalization conventions must be used in both formulas. The displayed source integration label must be identified with the mode momentum during evaluation.

For a one-antiparticle state the four-momentum eigenvalue is the positive-energy on-shell label, despite the plus sign in that branch's field phase. The creation operator, not the classical frequency sign by itself, fixes the state interpretation. For two excitations their four-momenta add; a pair of opposite spatial momenta has positive total energy and zero total spatial momentum.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{P}^{\mu},\mechanical{p}^{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momenta | The total and single-mode energy–momentum vectors. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The source integration variable, retained alongside the p mode label. |
