---
title: "Wick's Theorem for Fermions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Wick's theorem expands a fermionic time-ordered product into normal-ordered terms with all contractions and their exchange signs.

The time-ordered product of fermionic fields is the sum of the normal-ordered product and all possible contractions:
$$
\mathcal T(\signal{\psi}_1\signal{\psi}_2\cdots\signal{\psi}_n)
=\mathcal N(\signal{\psi}_1\signal{\psi}_2\cdots\signal{\psi}_n)
+\text{all contractions with their signs}. \tag{4.99}
$$
Each term has sign $(-1)^P$. Here $P$ is the number of fermion exchanges needed to put the contracted pairs next to each other.

### Example 4.9 — Four fermionic fields

Consider $\mathcal T(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\signal{\psi}_2\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\signal{\psi}_4)$, where $\bar{\signal{\psi}}_i=\bar{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{x}_i)$ and $\signal{\psi}_j=\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x}_j)$. Apply $\overbrace{\bar{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{y})\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})}=-\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})$ and count the required exchanges.

The complete expansion is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\signal{\psi}_2\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\signal{\psi}_4)
={}&\mathcal N(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\signal{\psi}_2\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\signal{\psi}_4)\\
&-\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}_2-\mechanical{x}_1)\mathcal N(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\signal{\psi}_4)
+\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}_4-\mechanical{x}_1)\mathcal N(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\signal{\psi}_2)\\
&-\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}_4-\mechanical{x}_3)\mathcal N(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\signal{\psi}_2)
+\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}_2-\mechanical{x}_3)\mathcal N(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\signal{\psi}_4)\\
&+\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}_2-\mechanical{x}_1)\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}_4-\mechanical{x}_3)
-\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}_4-\mechanical{x}_1)\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}_2-\mechanical{x}_3).
\end{aligned} \tag{4.100}
$$
The signs come from fermionic statistics. The fully contracted terms on the last line are c-number functions, rather than operators. In perturbative expansions, such terms contribute to vacuum–vacuum diagrams.
