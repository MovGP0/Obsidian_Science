---
title: "Lagrangian Density"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Varying the free Dirac Lagrangian with respect to the independent adjoint field gives the Dirac equation.

The free-field Lagrangian is

$$
\boxed{\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}=\overline{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{x})(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x}),} \tag{4.17}
$$

where the slashed derivative contracts the gamma matrices with the derivative. The density is Hermitian up to a total derivative, sufficient for the action. Treat the field and its adjoint independently. Variation of the adjoint gives

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\overline{\signal{\psi}}}-\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\overline{\signal{\psi}})}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}=0. \tag{4.18}
$$

Variation with respect to the spinor gives its adjoint equation:

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\psi}}-\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\psi})}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\overline{\signal{\psi}}(-i\overleftarrow{\not\partial}-\mechanical{m})=0. \tag{4.19}
$$

The arrow means that the derivative acts on the adjoint field to its left.

The model here is a free spin-half field: the kinetic term fixes propagation and the mass term couples the spinor components. A stationary action is the starting principle; the Dirac equation is its consequence. Independent variation means that the complex field and its conjugate are treated as separate coordinates during the variation, then related by conjugation for physical configurations.

The total derivative can be dropped only with boundary conditions that remove its surface contribution, for example fields or variations that vanish sufficiently fast at the boundary. The dimensions provide a useful check: a derivative or mass contributes one power of energy and the two fields contribute three, giving the required four powers for a four-dimensional Lagrangian density.
