---
title: "Conjugate Momenta"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The first-order Dirac Lagrangian gives a momentum proportional to the field adjoint and a vanishing adjoint momentum.

The momentum conjugate to the spinor is

$$
\mechanical{\pi}(\mechanical{x})=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_0\signal{\psi})}=i\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{0}=i\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}.
$$

For the adjoint field,

$$
\overline{\mechanical{\pi}}(\mechanical{x})=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_0\overline{\signal{\psi}})}=0.
$$

This is a constrained classical Hamiltonian system. Quantization requires care; the canonical treatment proceeds by imposing anticommutation relations between the spinor and its momentum, equivalently its Hermitian adjoint.

The absence of a time derivative in the momentum is the diagnostic feature. One cannot solve the first displayed relation for the field velocity, and the second relation is not an additional evolution equation. Both restrict the allowed phase-space variables.

Consequently the field and both momenta must not be counted as independent initial data. The equation is first order, so the initial spinor determines its later free evolution. [[04.15.02 Dirac Brackets and Quantization]] implements the constraint reduction before quantization. A useful contrast is a scalar kinetic term quadratic in velocity: its momentum can be inverted to recover that velocity.
