---
title: "Time Reversal (T)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Time reversal is antiunitary, reverses time, and reverses the spin projection.

Time reversal changes the sign of time, leaves spatial position fixed, and conjugates complex numbers, including the imaginary unit. For the field,

$$
\mathcal T\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})\mathcal T^{-1}=\eta_T T\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},-\mechanical{t}), \tag{4.58}
$$

where $T=i\gamma^{1}\gamma^{3}$ in the Dirac–Pauli representation. It interchanges the two spin basis states and reverses their z-directed spin projection.

Antiunitarity is required to reverse evolution while preserving positive energies: reversing time changes the sign of a time derivative, and conjugating the imaginary unit compensates it in the evolution equation. The displayed matrix T gives only the spinor part of this antiunitary transformation.

Momentum and spin both reverse. Their projection ratio, helicity, is consequently unchanged for nonzero momentum, in contrast with parity. At rest helicity has no direction to refer to, although the spin-state transformation remains defined. When applying T twice, conjugate the intrinsic phase on the second application; treating it as a unitary phase multiplication would give a wrong result for spin-half states.
