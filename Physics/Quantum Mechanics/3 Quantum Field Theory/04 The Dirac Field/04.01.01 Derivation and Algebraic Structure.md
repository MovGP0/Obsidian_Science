title: "Derivation and Algebraic Structure"

> [!TLDR]
> Dirac's first-order relativistic equation avoids the probability-density difficulty of a second-order wave equation.

The Klein–Gordon equation $(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\phi}=0$ is second order in time, which makes a positive-definite probability-density interpretation difficult. Dirac sought a first-order equation:

$$
(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})=0. \tag{4.1}
$$

Require every spinor component to satisfy the Klein–Gordon equation. Multiplying from the left by the first-order operator with the opposite derivative sign gives

$$
(\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\psi}=0. \tag{4.2}
$$

Symmetrize the indices:

$$
\gamma^{\nu}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}=\frac12\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}. \tag{4.3}
$$

To obtain the Klein–Gordon operator on each component, impose

$$
\boxed{\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1_4,} \tag{4.4}
$$

with signature $(+,-,-,-)$. The smallest representation has four-by-four matrices, so a Dirac spinor has four complex components.

Here the setting is a free field in four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime, with natural units so that time derivatives, spatial derivatives, and mass have the same energy dimension. The matrices are needed because ordinary numbers cannot cancel all mixed derivative terms while keeping opposite time and space signs.

The antisymmetric part of the gamma-matrix product drops out of (4.3) because partial derivatives commute. This step assumes no gauge or gravitational connection; covariant derivatives need not commute. The Clifford algebra is therefore sufficient for the free Klein–Gordon equation, but squaring an interacting Dirac operator also produces interaction terms.

A first-order equation supplies a conserved positive density for a classical one-particle amplitude. It does not by itself remove negative-frequency modes or justify a one-particle interpretation at energies that allow pair creation. In this chapter the quantized Dirac field, rather than that amplitude, is the final dynamical object.
