> [!TLDR]
> Spin sums and gamma-matrix traces give the squared amplitudes for unpolarized fermion processes.

### Example 4.4 — Unpolarized cross section for e⁺e⁻ → μ⁺μ⁻: trace structure

Consider $\mathrm e^-(\mechanical{p}_1)+\mathrm e^+(\mechanical{p}_2)\to
\mu^-(\mechanical{k}_1)+\mu^+(\mechanical{k}_2)$ through photon exchange. In the ultrarelativistic limit, neglect the lepton masses. The spin-averaged squared invariant amplitude is
$$
\overline{\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2}
=\frac{\electric{e}^{\,4}}{\mechanical{q}^{\,4}}
\signal{L}_{\mu\nu}^{(e)}\signal{L}^{(\mu)\mu\nu}. \tag{4.80}
$$

The virtual photon has four-momentum $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}_1+\mechanical{p}_2$. The electron leptonic tensor is
$$
\signal{L}_{\mu\nu}^{(e)}
=\frac14\sum_{\mathrm{spin}}
\bar v(\mechanical{p}_2)\gamma_\mu u(\mechanical{p}_1)
\bar u(\mechanical{p}_1)\gamma_\nu v(\mechanical{p}_2). \tag{4.81}
$$
Use completeness relations (4.13)–(4.14) and neglect the electron mass $\mechanical{m}_e$:
$$
\signal{L}_{\mu\nu}^{(e)}
=\frac14\operatorname{tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}_2\gamma_\mu
\not{\mechanical{p}}_1\gamma_\nu). \tag{4.82}
$$
Apply (4.75):
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}_2\gamma_\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}_1\gamma_\nu)
=4[\mechanical{p}_{2\mu}\mechanical{p}_{1\nu}
+\mechanical{p}_{1\mu}\mechanical{p}_{2\nu}
-g_{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)]. \tag{4.83}
$$
For the muon tensor, the superscript $(\mu)$ denotes the muon species, rather than a Lorentz index:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{L}^{(\mu)\alpha\beta}
&=\operatorname{tr}(\not{\mechanical{k}}_1\gamma^\alpha
\not{\mechanical{k}}_2\gamma^\beta)\\
&=4[\mechanical{k}_1^\alpha\mechanical{k}_2^\beta
+\mechanical{k}_2^\alpha\mechanical{k}_1^\beta
-g^{\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{k}_1\cdot\mechanical{k}_2)].
\end{aligned} \tag{4.84}
$$
The muon mass $\mechanical{m}_\mu$ is generally nonzero, but this example neglects it to show the method. There is no average over final-state spins, so this tensor has no factor $1/4$. In four dimensions,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{L}_{\mu\nu}^{(e)}\signal{L}^{(\mu)\mu\nu}
={}&[\mechanical{p}_{2\mu}\mechanical{p}_{1\nu}
+\mechanical{p}_{1\mu}\mechanical{p}_{2\nu}
-g_{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)]\\
&\cdot4[\mechanical{k}_1^\mu\mechanical{k}_2^\nu
+\mechanical{k}_2^\mu\mechanical{k}_1^\nu
-g^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k}_1\cdot\mechanical{k}_2)]\\
={}&8[(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{k}_1)(\mechanical{p}_2\cdot\mechanical{k}_2)
+(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{k}_2)(\mechanical{p}_2\cdot\mechanical{k}_1)].
\end{aligned} \tag{4.85}
$$
In the center-of-mass frame, $\sqrt{\energy{s}}=\energy{E}_{\mathrm{cm}}$. With scattering angle $\mechanical{\theta}$ and the standard normalization factors, the result is
$$
\overline{\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2}
=\electric{e}^{\,4}(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}). \tag{4.86}
$$
This gives the familiar angular dependence of the differential cross section.

### Example 4.5 — Trace with γ⁵ and axial interference

Consider
$$
T_1=\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^5). \tag{4.87}
$$
By (4.76),
$$
T_1=-4i\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}. \tag{4.88}
$$
This structure occurs in parity-violating processes, such as scattering through a $Z$ boson. The coupling has the form $\gamma^\mu(g_V-g_A\gamma^5)$. The interference term proportional to $g_Vg_A$ contains traces such as $\operatorname{tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}_1\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}_2\gamma^\nu\gamma^5)$. The Levi-Civita contribution produces forward–backward asymmetries in angular distributions.

The source writes explicitly
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{q}}\gamma^\nu\gamma^5)
=-4i\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\mechanical{p}_\alpha\mechanical{q}_\beta. \tag{4.89}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (4.89) is retained as printed. Equation (4.76) gives $-4i\epsilon^{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\mechanical{p}_\alpha\mechanical{q}_\beta$, which becomes $+4i\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\mechanical{p}_\alpha\mechanical{q}_\beta$ after an index exchange. Thus the printed ordering in (4.89) has a sign inconsistency.

### Example 4.6 — Fermionic propagator and spectral function

For $\mechanical{x}^0>0$, the particle contribution in configuration space is
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x})\big|_{\mechanical{x}^0>0}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m}}{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}}
e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}. \tag{4.90}
$$
For timelike separation $\mechanical{x}^2>0$, this oscillates with frequency of order $\mechanical{m}$ in the rest frame and decreases algebraically at large separations. For spacelike separation $\mechanical{x}^2<0$, it decreases exponentially, schematically as $e^{-\mechanical{m}\lvert\mechanical{x}\rvert}$.

A contour-integral proof closes the complex-energy contour according to the sign of $\mechanical{x}^0$.

> [!note]
> The source measure in (4.90) uses $\mechanical{k}$, while its integrand uses $\mechanical{p}$; this mismatch is retained. The spacelike exponential is a schematic massive-field asymptotic. A nonzero spacelike Feynman propagator does not itself prove or violate causality. The causal condition is the vanishing of the appropriate field anticommutators and of commutators of local observables at spacelike separation.

### Example 4.7 — Spin sum calculated in the rest frame

Check completeness relation (4.13) directly at $\mechanical{\mathbf p}=0$. The rest-frame spinors are
$$
u(\mechanical{m},s=1)=\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}}
\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
u(\mechanical{m},s=2)=\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}}
\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4.91}
$$
The completeness sum is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_s u(\mechanical{m},s)\bar u(\mechanical{m},s)
=2\mechanical{m}\Bigg[
&\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\gamma^0\\
+&\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\gamma^0
\Bigg].
\end{aligned} \tag{4.92}
$$
Here $\gamma^0=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)$ in the Dirac–Pauli representation. The first term has only the $(1,1)$ matrix element equal to $2\mechanical{m}$; the second has only $(2,2)$ equal to $2\mechanical{m}$. Thus the sum is $2\mechanical{m}\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0)$.

The right-hand side of (4.13) gives the same result:
$$
(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})\big|_{\mechanical{\mathbf p}=0}
=\mechanical{m}(\gamma^0+1)
=2\mechanical{m}\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0).
$$

### Example 4.8 — Low-energy limit of the Dirac equation

Use the ansatz
$$
\signal{\psi}=e^{-i\mechanical{m}\signal{t}}
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\phi}\\\signal{\chi}\end{pmatrix},
$$
where $\signal{\phi}$ and $\signal{\chi}$ each have two components. The Dirac equation (4.1), separated into these components in the Dirac–Pauli representation, gives
$$
i\partial_{\signal{t}}\signal{\phi}
=\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}\,\signal{\chi}
+\mechanical{m}\signal{\phi}. \tag{4.93}
$$

> [!note]
> The source heading calls this the “Dirac equation as a low-energy limit of KG.” The displayed calculation instead starts from the Dirac equation. Also, the phase $e^{-i\mechanical{m}\signal{t}}$ removes the rest-energy term from the upper-component equation. Equation (4.93), retained as printed, still contains this term and is therefore inconsistent with that ansatz.

The printed lower-component equation is
$$
i\partial_{\signal{t}}\signal{\chi}
=\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}\,\signal{\phi}
-\mechanical{m}\signal{\chi}. \tag{4.94}
$$
For an unshifted energy $i\partial_{\signal{t}}\approx\mechanical{m}+\energy{E}_{\mathrm{NR}}$ with $\energy{E}_{\mathrm{NR}}\ll\mechanical{m}$, it gives
$$
\signal{\chi}\approx
\frac{\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{2\mechanical{m}}\signal{\phi}.
$$
The small component is suppressed by a factor of order $\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert/\mechanical{m}$. Insert this result into the first equation and remove the rest energy. To leading nonrelativistic order,
$$
i\partial_{\signal{t}}\signal{\phi}
=\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical{m}}\signal{\phi}. \tag{4.95}
$$
This is the Schrödinger equation for a free particle.

> [!note]
> Equations (4.93)–(4.94) apply to components before removal of the rest-energy phase. For the stated phase-shifted ansatz, the consistent equations are $i\partial_{\signal{t}}\signal{\phi}=\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}\,\signal{\chi}$ and $i\partial_{\signal{t}}\signal{\chi}=\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}\,\signal{\phi}-2\mechanical{m}\signal{\chi}$. Both routes lead to (4.95).
>
> The source states that the next correction gives spin–orbit and Darwin terms in the Pauli–Schrödinger Hamiltonian. These terms require an external interaction. A free-particle expansion alone gives the relativistic kinetic-energy correction.
