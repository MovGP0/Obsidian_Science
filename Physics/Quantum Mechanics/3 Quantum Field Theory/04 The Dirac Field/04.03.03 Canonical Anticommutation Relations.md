---
title: "Canonical Anticommutation Relations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Equal-time fermionic fields satisfy a field–adjoint anticommutator and vanishing same-type anticommutators.

Impose

$$
\{\signal{\psi}_{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\beta}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}), \tag{4.25}
$$

$$
\{\signal{\psi}_{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),\signal{\psi}_{\beta}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}=0,\qquad
\{\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}_{\beta}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}=0. \tag{4.26}
$$

The spinor labels are matrix-component labels, while the spatial delta function makes the relation local at the common time. Integrating against smooth spatial test functions gives well-defined mode operators; a field at a single point is not a normalized fermionic oscillator.

The units are consistent: two fields have three powers of energy, exactly as a three-dimensional position delta function. The vanishing same-type anticommutators will force a normalized creation operator to square to zero. They do not say that the mixed field–adjoint anticommutator vanishes at coincident points.
