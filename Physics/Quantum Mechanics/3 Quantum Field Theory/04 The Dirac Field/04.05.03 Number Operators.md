---
title: "Number Operators"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Each fermionic mode has occupation zero or one, so its mode Hilbert space is two dimensional.

The particle and antiparticle counts are

$$
N_b=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_s b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}b^{s}_{\mathbf p},\qquad
N_d=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_s d^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}d^{s}_{\mathbf p}. \tag{4.35}
$$

> [!note]
> The source's integration variable k and mode label p are retained.

For one normalized mode, $\hat n=b^{\dagger}b$ obeys

$$
\hat n^{2}=b^{\dagger}bb^{\dagger}b=b^{\dagger}\{b,b^{\dagger}\}b-b^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}bb=b^{\dagger}b=\hat n. \tag{4.36}
$$

Its eigenvalues satisfy $n^{2}=n$, hence occupation is zero or one. The source's unnumbered state diagram gives these transitions:

| Initial state | Operation | Final state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\lvert0\rangle_{\mathbf p}$, $n=0$ | $b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}$ | $\lvert1\rangle_{\mathbf p}$, $n=1$ |
| $\lvert1\rangle_{\mathbf p}$, $n=1$ | $b^{s}_{\mathbf p}$ | $\lvert0\rangle_{\mathbf p}$, $n=0$ |

A second creation gives $(b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p})^{2}|0\rangle=0$. Each fermionic mode therefore has a two-dimensional Hilbert space, unlike a bosonic mode's infinite-dimensional space.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $N_b,N_d$ | $1$ | Particle counts | The total fermion and antifermion numbers. |
| $\hat n,n$ | $1$ | Mode occupation | The occupation observable and its zero-or-one eigenvalue. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The integration variable printed in the number formulas. |
