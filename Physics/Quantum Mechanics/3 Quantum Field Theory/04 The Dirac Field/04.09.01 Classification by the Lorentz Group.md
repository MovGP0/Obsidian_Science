---
title: "Classification by the Lorentz Group"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The sixteen independent Dirac bilinears comprise a scalar, pseudoscalar, vector, axial vector, and antisymmetric tensor.

A fermion bilinear is $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\Gamma\signal{\psi}$, where the matrix is a combination of gamma matrices. The four-by-four matrix space has sixteen independent elements, classified by Lorentz transformation type:

| Bilinear | Definition | Lorentz type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{S}$ | $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}$ | Scalar |
| $\signal{P}$ | $\overline{\signal{\psi}}i\gamma^5\signal{\psi}$ | Pseudoscalar |
| $\signal{V}^{\mu}$ | $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\signal{\psi}$ | Vector, four components |
| $\signal{A}^{\mu}$ | $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^5\signal{\psi}$ | Axial vector, four components |
| $\signal{T}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\signal{\psi}$ | Antisymmetric tensor, six components |

Here $\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{i}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]$. The count is one plus one plus four plus four plus six, matching the dimension of the Dirac matrix algebra.

The count concerns independent matrices, not sixteen independent particle species or separately measurable spinor components. The antisymmetric tensor has six components because its diagonal entries vanish and each off-diagonal pair carries only one independent value.

Under proper Lorentz transformations, scalar and pseudoscalar have the same index structure, as do vector and axial vector. Spatial inversion distinguishes the members of each pair. Thus a mass term built from the scalar is parity even, while a pseudoscalar coupling requires examination of the other field's parity as well. Every displayed bilinear has three energy powers from its two fields; a matrix structure changes transformation type without adding physical units.
