---
title: "Plane-Wave Solutions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> On-shell Dirac plane waves have two spin states for each positive- and negative-frequency branch.

Seek $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})=\signal{u}(\mechanical{p})e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}$ for the positive-energy branch and $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})=\signal{v}(\mechanical{p})e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}$ for the negative-energy branch. Substitution gives

$$
(\not p-\mechanical{m})\signal{u}(\mechanical{p},s)=0,\qquad(\not p+\mechanical{m})\signal{v}(\mechanical{p},s)=0, \tag{4.6}
$$

with $\not p=\gamma^{\mu}\mechanical{p}_{\mu}$ and spin label $s=1,2$. The mass shell gives $\mechanical{p}^{2}=\mechanical{m}^{2}$ and $\energy{p}^{0}=\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}=\sqrt{\mechanical{\mathbf p}^{2}+\mechanical{m}^{2}}$.

## Explicit Positive-Energy Solutions

In the Dirac–Pauli representation, use two-component Pauli spinors $\signal{\chi}_1=(1,0)^{T}$ and $\signal{\chi}_2=(0,1)^{T}$. Then

$$
\signal{u}(\mechanical{p},s)=\sqrt{\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}+\mechanical{m}}\begin{pmatrix}\signal{\chi}_s\\\dfrac{\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}+\mechanical{m}}\signal{\chi}_s\end{pmatrix}. \tag{4.7}
$$

Direct substitution verifies (4.6). At nonrelativistic momenta, the lower small component is suppressed by a factor of momentum magnitude divided by mass relative to the upper large component.

## Explicit Negative-Energy Solutions

$$
\signal{v}(\mechanical{p},s)=\sqrt{\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}+\mechanical{m}}\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}+\mechanical{m}}\signal{\xi}_s\\\signal{\xi}_s\end{pmatrix}, \tag{4.8}
$$

with basis spinors $\signal{\xi}_1=(1,0)^T$ and $\signal{\xi}_2=(0,1)^T$, up to phase conventions.
