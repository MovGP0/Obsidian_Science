---
title: "General Properties of the Trace"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The trace of an odd number of gamma matrices is zero because γ⁵ anticommutes with each gamma matrix.

The trace of an odd number of gamma matrices vanishes:
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^{\mu_1}\gamma^{\mu_2}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n+1}})=0. \tag{4.71}
$$
Use $\gamma^5\gamma^\mu=-\gamma^\mu\gamma^5$, $(\gamma^5)^2=1$, and cyclicity of the trace:
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^{\mu_1}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n+1}})
=\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^5\gamma^{\mu_1}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n+1}}\gamma^5).
$$

Move the final $\gamma^5$ through all $2n+1$ gamma matrices:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^5\gamma^{\mu_1}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n+1}}\gamma^5)
&=(-1)^{2n+1}\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^5\gamma^5\gamma^{\mu_1}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n+1}})\\
&=-\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^{\mu_1}\cdots\gamma^{\mu_{2n+1}}).
\end{aligned} \tag{4.72}
$$
The trace equals its own negative and is therefore zero.

The argument is a matrix identity, independent of the physical state or momentum. It needs an anticommuting gamma-five matrix with square one and a cyclic finite-dimensional trace. The odd number of sign changes is the decisive step; with an even number that same argument would only reproduce the original trace and prove nothing.

For a quick check, set the product to a single temporal gamma matrix in the displayed Dirac–Pauli basis. Its two plus and two minus diagonal entries sum to zero. This is a check of the general identity, not a replacement for the basis-independent proof. A trace that already contains gamma five must be analyzed separately using the identities in [[04.11.03 Traces of Products of Gamma Matrices]].
