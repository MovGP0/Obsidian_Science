## Exercise 4.11 — The Majorana field

Define the Majorana condition $\signal{\psi}^{\,c}=\signal{\psi}$, with $\signal{\psi}^{\,c}=C\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{\,T}$.

(a) Show that the condition is consistent with Lorentz covariance and the Dirac equation.

(b) Write the Dirac Lagrangian for a Majorana field and show that it reduces to
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\frac i2\bar{\signal{\psi}}\not\partial\signal{\psi}
-\frac{\mechanical{m}}2\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}.
$$

(c) Show that the charge $\electric{Q}$ of the Majorana field is identically zero, as expected for a particle without a conserved charge of this type.

## Solution 4.11 — The Majorana field

**(a)** The Majorana condition is
$$
\signal{\psi}^{\,c}=C\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{\,T}=\signal{\psi}.
$$
The charge-conjugated field transforms in the same spinor representation as $\signal{\psi}$ under proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations. The condition is therefore Lorentz covariant.

For a free field with real mass, use $C(\gamma^\mu)^TC^{-1}=-\gamma^\mu$ and the adjoint Dirac equation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}^{\,c}
&=-C\left[i(\partial_\mu\bar{\signal{\psi}})\gamma^\mu
+\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\right]^T\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus charge conjugation preserves the free Dirac equation.

If the Majorana condition holds initially, the free evolution preserves it because $\signal{\psi}$ and $\signal{\psi}^{\,c}$ obey the same equation.

**(b)** The correctly normalized Majorana Lagrangian is
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_M
=\frac12\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}
=\frac i2\bar{\signal{\psi}}\not{\partial}\signal{\psi}
-\frac{\mechanical{m}}2\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}.
$$
The factor one half accounts for the fact that the field and its charge conjugate are not independent.

> [!note] Correction to the source normalization
> The source presents the factor one half as if the full Dirac Lagrangian became half of itself plus a total derivative after the Majorana constraint. A total derivative does not produce this overall factor. The Majorana action is defined with the factor one half to give the correct normalization for the independent degrees of freedom.
>
> With $\signal{\psi}=C\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{\,T}$ and $C^T=-C$, transposition gives $\bar{\signal{\psi}}=-\signal{\psi}^{\,T}C^{-1}$. The source omits this minus sign.

**(c)** The vector current becomes
$$
\electric{J}^\mu
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}
=-\signal{\psi}^{\,T}C^{-1}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
The matrix in this bilinear is symmetric:
$$
(C^{-1}\gamma^\mu)^T
=(\gamma^\mu)^T(C^{-1})^T
=-(\gamma^\mu)^TC^{-1}
=C^{-1}\gamma^\mu.
$$
For anticommuting field components, a bilinear $\signal{\psi}^{\,T}A\signal{\psi}$ with symmetric $A$ vanishes. Hence the Majorana vector current, and its associated charge, vanish:
$$
\electric{J}^\mu=0,\qquad
\electric{Q}=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,\electric{J}^0=0.
$$
A single Majorana field has no continuous phase symmetry of the Dirac $U(1)$ type.

The three parts check compatibility, normalization, and charge separately. Self-conjugacy is preserved by the free equation with real mass; it is not obtained by setting half the components to zero in an arbitrary representation. The one-half action normalization avoids counting the same independent variables twice.

For the current test, the matrix symmetry and the field statistics are both necessary. Interchanging two anticommuting components reverses the bilinear, while transposing the symmetric matrix leaves it unchanged, so it vanishes. With commuting numerical mode spinors this argument has a different sign and does not prove zero. The result excludes the single-field Dirac vector charge; it does not exclude axial interactions or imply that the field has no dynamics.
