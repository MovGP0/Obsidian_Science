## Exercise 4.2 — Inversion of the canonical anticommutation relation

Start from expansion (4.27) and mode-operator anticommutators (4.29)–(4.30). Verify directly that the equal-time relation (4.25) holds.

*Hint:* Use the spinor completeness relations and
$$
\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf p}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}
=\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}).
$$

## Solution 4.2 — Canonical anticommutators and inversion

Start from the mode expansion
$$
\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}}}
\sum_s\left[
b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s}u_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
+d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger}v_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
\right].
$$

> [!note]
> The source uses a $d^3k$ integration measure while labeling the modes by $p$. A single dummy momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ is used here.

The Hermitian-conjugate field obeys
$$
\signal{\psi}_\beta^\dagger=(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^0)_\beta.
$$
Its mode expansion is
$$
\signal{\psi}_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf q}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf q}}}
\sum_{s'}\left[
b_{\mathbf q}^{\,s'\dagger}u_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf q},s')
e^{+i\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{y}}
+d_{\mathbf q}^{\,s'}v_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf q},s')
e^{-i\mechanical{q}\cdot\mechanical{y}}
\right].
$$

Evaluate the field anticommutator at equal times, $\mechanical{x}^0=\mechanical{y}^0=\mechanical{t}$.
Only the particle and antiparticle mode pairs survive:
$$
\{b_{\mathbf p}^{\,s},b_{\mathbf q}^{\,s'\dagger}\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{ss'}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q}),
$$
$$
\{d_{\mathbf p}^{\,s\dagger},d_{\mathbf q}^{\,s'}\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{ss'}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf q}).
$$
Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{ \signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),
\signal{\psi}_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}
={}&\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}}\sum_s\Bigl[
u_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)u_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)
e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&\qquad+
v_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)v_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)
e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigr]_{\mechanical{x}^0=\mechanical{y}^0}.
\end{aligned}
$$
At equal times,
$$
\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}).
$$
In the second term, change the spatial integration variable
$\mechanical{\mathbf p}\to-\mechanical{\mathbf p}$.
The measure and the positive energy are unchanged.

Use the completeness relations and multiply on the right by $\gamma^0$:
$$
\sum_s u_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)u_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)
=[(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^0]_{\alpha\beta},
$$
$$
\sum_s v_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)v_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf p},s)
=[(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m})\gamma^0]_{\alpha\beta}.
$$
After the spatial reflection in the second term, the spatial-gamma and mass terms cancel in the sum:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left[
(\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\gamma^0-\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}+\mechanical{m})
+(\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\gamma^0+\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{m})
\right]\gamma^0\\
&\qquad=2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{ \signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),
\signal{\psi}_\beta^\dagger(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf p}}{(2\pi)^3}
\delta_{\alpha\beta}
e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf p}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}\\
&=\delta_{\alpha\beta}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}).
\end{aligned}
$$
