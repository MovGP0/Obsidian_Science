## Exercise 4.7 — Gordon identity

Complete the proof of (4.106) outlined in §4.16. In particular, use the Clifford algebra to express $[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu]=-2i\sigma^{\mu\nu}$ and collect the terms.

## Solution 4.7 — Gordon identity

The equal-mass on-shell spinors satisfy
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}u(\mechanical{p})=\mechanical{m}u(\mechanical{p}),\qquad
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\not{\mechanical{p}}'
=\mechanical{m}\bar u(\mechanical{p}').
$$
Multiply by $\gamma^\mu$ on the appropriate side and add:
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
+\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}u(\mechanical{p})
=2\mechanical{m}\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p}).
$$
The Clifford algebra gives
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu
=2\mechanical{p}'^\mu-\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}',
\qquad
\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}
=2\mechanical{p}^\mu-\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu,
$$
and hence
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu+\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}
=2\mechanical{p}'^\mu+2\mechanical{p}^\mu
-(\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}'+\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu).
$$
With
$$
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac i2[\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu],
\qquad
\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu=g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1-i\sigma^{\mu\nu},
\qquad
\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu=g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1+i\sigma^{\mu\nu},
$$
the subtracted term is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}'+\not{\mechanical{p}}\gamma^\mu
&=\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\mechanical{p}'_\nu
+\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\mechanical{p}_\nu\\
&=(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu
-i\sigma^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p})_\nu.
\end{aligned}
$$

Combining the terms gives
$$
\not{\mechanical{p}}'\gamma^\mu+\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}
=(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu
+i\sigma^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p})_\nu.
$$
For $\mechanical{m}\ne0$, division by $2\mechanical{m}$ gives the Gordon identity (4.106):
$$
\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\gamma^\mu u(\mechanical{p})
=\bar u(\mechanical{p}')\left[
\frac{(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p})_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\right]u(\mechanical{p}).
$$

The on-shell equations allow the two masses to be added only because the external spinors have the same mass. The proof then separates the gamma product into a metric part and a commutator part. Reversing the definition of momentum transfer would reverse the latter term, so keep the outgoing-minus-incoming convention through every step.

At equal incoming and outgoing momentum the antisymmetric term vanishes. Contracting the general current with the momentum transfer gives zero by the two on-shell equations, a second check of current conservation. The divided form cannot be used at zero mass or as a matrix identity for arbitrary off-shell spinors.
