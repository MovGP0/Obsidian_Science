---
title: "The Dirac Equation in Chiral Form"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The mass term couples the two Weyl components; they satisfy independent Weyl equations when the mass is zero.

Insert the decomposition (4.64) into the Dirac equation and use the Weyl representation:
$$
i\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}_R=\mechanical{m}\signal{\psi}_L, \tag{4.66}
$$
$$
i\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}_L=\mechanical{m}\signal{\psi}_R. \tag{4.67}
$$
Here $\sigma^\mu=(1,\boldsymbol\sigma)$ and $\bar\sigma^\mu=(1,-\boldsymbol\sigma)$. For $\mechanical{m}=0$, the equations decouple. The **Weyl equations** are
$$
i\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}_R=0,
\qquad
i\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}_L=0. \tag{4.68}
$$
They describe right-handed and left-handed massless spinor fields.

> [!note]
> The source uses right-handed and left-handed neutrinos as examples in the ultrarelativistic limit. The Weyl equations alone do not establish the existence or interactions of a right-handed neutrino.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}_L,\signal{\psi}_R$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Weyl fields | The left- and right-chiral components. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The mass that couples the two components. |
