---
title: "The CPT Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> CPT combines the three discrete transformations and relates particle and antiparticle properties.

Write the combined operator as $\Theta=\mathcal C\mathcal P\mathcal T$. The source prints

$$
\Theta\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\Theta^{-1}=\eta_{CPT}\overline{\signal{\psi}}^{T}(-\mechanical{x}), \tag{4.59}
$$

$$
\Theta\electric{J}^{\mu}(\mechanical{x})\Theta^{-1}=-\electric{J}^{\mu}(-\mechanical{x}). \tag{4.60}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (4.59) is retained as printed. Its explicit spinor-matrix factors need comparison with the adopted C, P, and T conventions.

> [!note] Working correction to the spinor transformation
> Composing (4.50), (4.54), and (4.58) fixes the missing matrix. The numerical spinor factors occur in the order $T\gamma^0 C$. With the stated Dirac–Pauli matrices,
> $$
> T\gamma^0 C
> =(i\gamma^1\gamma^3)\gamma^0(i\gamma^2\gamma^0)
> =i\gamma^0\gamma^5.
> $$
> Thus a compatible working transformation, up to an overall phase $\eta$, is
> $$
> \Theta\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\Theta^{-1}
> =\eta\,i\gamma^0\gamma^5\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{\,T}(-\mechanical{x}).
> $$
> In this representation $\bar{\signal{\psi}}^{\,T}=\gamma^0\signal{\psi}^{*}$, so the same law has a gamma-five factor multiplying $\signal{\psi}^{*}(-\mechanical{x})$, up to phase. The nontrivial matrix cannot be absorbed into the scalar phase in printed (4.59). This is a direct composition of the chapter's conventions; the combined operation remains antiunitary.

CPT gives equal masses for particles and antiparticles and equality of total decay rates or lifetimes.

> [!note]
> The source says “total decay amplitudes.” Equal total rates must be distinguished from equality of individual decay amplitudes.

**Table 4.1** Discrete transformations of Dirac bilinears.

| Bilinear | P | C | T | CP | CPT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}$, scalar | + | + | + | + | + |
| $\overline{\signal{\psi}}i\gamma^5\signal{\psi}$, pseudoscalar | − | + | − | − | + |
| $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\signal{\psi}$, vector | $+(\electric{J}^0),-(\electric{\mathbf J})$ | − | $+(\electric{J}^0),-(\electric{\mathbf J})$ | $-(\electric{J}^0),+(\electric{\mathbf J})$ | − |
| $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^5\signal{\psi}$, axial vector | $-(\signal{A}^0),+(\signal{\mathbf A})$ | + | $+(\signal{A}^0),-(\signal{\mathbf A})$ | $-(\signal{A}^0),+(\signal{\mathbf A})$ | − |
| $\overline{\signal{\psi}}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\signal{\psi}$, tensor | ✓ | − | ✓ | ✓ | + |

> [!note]
> The checkmarks in the tensor row are retained from the source. They do not specify component-by-component transformation signs.

The theorem constrains a local relativistic quantum theory with a positive state interpretation and suitable spectral assumptions. It does not assert that each separate discrete transformation is a symmetry. The equality of masses and total widths is a comparison between a particle and its CPT-related antiparticle, including the corresponding spin transformation.

Use the bilinear table as a classification aid, with reversed spacetime arguments understood. It is not a substitute for transforming a whole interaction: coupling constants, other fields, derivatives, and the complex conjugation from T also enter. Use the working spinor correction above in place of printed (4.59). The tensor-row checkmarks still do not give a complete component-level rule; for those entries return to the individually specified C, P, and T transformations.

Equality of total decay widths still allows different partial decay probabilities when the relevant symmetries are violated. Summing over the complete allowed final-state set is therefore part of the stated lifetime comparison.
