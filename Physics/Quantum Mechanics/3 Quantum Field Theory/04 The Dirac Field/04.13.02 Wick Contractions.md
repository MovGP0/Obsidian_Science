---
title: "Wick Contractions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A fermionic Wick contraction equals the time-ordered product minus its normal-ordered product.

Use an overbrace to denote a contraction. For two Dirac fields,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overbrace{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
&=\mathcal T[\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})]
-\mathcal N[\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})]\\
&=\signal{S}_{F\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned} \tag{4.97}
$$
Reversing the order gives
$$
\overbrace{\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})}
=-\signal{S}_{F\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}). \tag{4.98}
$$
The minus sign follows from the antisymmetry of fermionic time ordering.

For free fields in their vacuum, the contraction is a numerical matrix distribution: the operator-valued remainder sits in the normal-ordered product. Its two spinor indices still specify the orientation, even though no Fock-space operator remains in the contraction.

Equation (4.98) reverses the field order while retaining the same external component assignment. It is not permission to reverse the propagator argument or transpose its matrix independently. A useful check is to take the vacuum expectation of (4.97): the normal-ordered term vanishes, leaving exactly the propagator definition. For a non-vacuum state or an interacting field, the simple free-vacuum contraction rules need their own justification.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
