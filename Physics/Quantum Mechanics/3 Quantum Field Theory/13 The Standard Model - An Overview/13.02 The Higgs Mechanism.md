---
title: "The Higgs Mechanism"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In a gauge theory, the would-be Goldstone modes supply the longitudinal polarizations of massive gauge bosons. This is the Higgs mechanism.

The issue is how to reconcile a gauge description with a massive spin-one particle. A massless vector has two physical polarizations; a massive vector has three. A scalar field with a nonzero vacuum magnitude supplies the additional longitudinal mode through its covariant kinetic term. The radial scalar remains a separate possible particle.

Follow [[13.02.01 The Abelian Higgs Model]] to see the gauge-invariant phase-gradient combination before choosing a gauge. Then use [[13.02.02 The Rξ Gauge]] to distinguish physical particle poles from gauge-dependent fields in intermediate calculations. Count physical degrees of freedom in both descriptions as a check. Gauge fixing changes the variables used to calculate an observable; it cannot change a physical decay width or scattering probability.

Goldstone's theorem appears to require massless scalars whenever a continuous internal symmetry is spontaneously broken. No physical massless scalar associated with electroweak breaking is observed. The **Higgs mechanism**, developed by Higgs, Englert and Brout, and Guralnik, Hagen and Kibble in 1964, resolves this in a gauge theory. The would-be Goldstone modes become the longitudinal degrees of freedom of massive gauge bosons.
