---
title: "The Glashow–Weinberg–Salam Electroweak Theory"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The electroweak theory applies the Higgs mechanism to SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y. It combines weak and electromagnetic interactions in one gauge theory.

The construction has three linked tasks. Choose chiral fermion representations that reproduce electric charges, choose a scalar vacuum that preserves electromagnetism, and derive both the vector masses and their couplings from the same covariant derivatives. The gauge group alone does not determine the representations, the two gauge couplings, or the Yukawa matrices.

Read [[13.03.01 The Gauge Group and Vector Bosons]] and [[13.03.02 Fermion Representations]] first. Then follow [[13.03.03 The Higgs Doublet and Electroweak SSB]] into [[13.03.04 Gauge Boson Masses - The Explicit Calculation]]. Complete the construction with [[13.03.05 Fermion Masses and Yukawa Couplings]], [[13.03.06 The CKM Matrix]], and [[13.03.07 Neutral Currents and Charged Currents]]. At each step, check the same electromagnetic charge convention. A massless photon and charge-conserving vertices must follow together.

The Abelian Higgs model shows how a gauge boson acquires mass.
Apply the same idea to the non-Abelian group $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$.
This gives the electroweak theory associated with Glashow (1961), Weinberg (1967), and Salam (1968).
