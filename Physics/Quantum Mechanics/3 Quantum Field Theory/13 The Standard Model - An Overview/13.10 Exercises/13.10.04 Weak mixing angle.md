## Exercise 13.4

Use the source's experimental inputs
$$
\mechanical{m}_W=80.369\,\unit{GeV},
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_Z=91.188\,\unit{GeV},
\qquad
\signal{G}_F=1.1664\times10^{-5}\,\unit{GeV^{-2}}.
$$

1. **(a)** Derive $\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W$ from $\mechanical{m}_W/\mechanical{m}_Z=\cos\signal{\theta}_W$.
2. **(b)** Derive the Higgs vacuum scale $\signal{v}$ from
   $$
   \signal{G}_F
   =\frac{\signal{g}^2}{4\sqrt2\,\mechanical{m}_W^2}
   =\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\signal{v}^2}.
   $$
3. **(c)** Calculate $\signal{g}$ and $\signal{g}'$. Verify $\electric{e}=\signal{g}\sin\signal{\theta}_W$, using $\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}\approx1/128$ at the scale $\mechanical{m}_Z$.

> [!note]
> These are the source's numerical inputs. The mass relation is a tree-level relation; a precision comparison with the running electromagnetic coupling requires a consistent renormalization scheme and radiative corrections.

## Solution 13.4 — Weak mixing angle

There are three supplied low-energy inputs and three tree-level electroweak parameters to reconstruct. First the dimensionless mass ratio fixes an angle; the Fermi constant fixes a mass scale; the charged-vector mass then fixes a dimensionless gauge coupling. The neutral mixing relation gives the second coupling. Keep this order so that a value of the running electromagnetic coupling is a comparison, rather than an extra input used to force agreement.

Predict the scale and consistency checks before inserting numbers. Since the W is lighter than the Z, the inferred squared sine must lie between zero and one. The inverse square root of the Fermi constant has energy units. The tree-level electromagnetic coupling reconstructed from these inputs need not equal the separate running-coupling benchmark once different loop-level definitions are mixed. That difference identifies the limit of the calculation; it is not a license to replace the mass-ratio angle by an effective angle.

### (a) Mass ratio

With the supplied masses,
$$
\cos\signal{\theta}_W
=\frac{80.369}{91.188}\simeq0.8813,
\qquad
\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W
=1-\frac{\mechanical{m}_W^2}{\mechanical{m}_Z^2}
\simeq0.2233.
$$

### (b) Vacuum expectation value

From $\signal{G}_F=1/(\sqrt2\signal{v}^2)$,
$$
\signal{v}^2
=\frac1{\sqrt2(1.1664\times10^{-5}\,\unit{GeV^{-2}})}
\simeq6.062\times10^4\,\unit{GeV^2},
\qquad
\boxed{\signal{v}\simeq246.22\,\unit{GeV}.}
$$

### (c) Gauge couplings

The tree-level mass relations give
$$
\signal{g}=\frac{2\mechanical{m}_W}{\signal{v}}\simeq0.6529,
\qquad
\signal{g}'=\signal{g}\tan\signal{\theta}_W\simeq0.3500.
$$
Thus
$$
\electric{e}=\signal{g}\sin\signal{\theta}_W\simeq0.3085,
\qquad
\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}
=\frac{\electric{e}^2}{4\pi}\simeq0.00757\simeq\frac1{132.1}.
$$

> [!note] Precision comparison
> The source compares this tree-level result with the supplied running-coupling benchmark $\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}(\mechanical{m}_Z)\simeq1/128$ and the effective angle $\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W^{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq0.23122$. These quantities use different definitions beyond tree level. A precision comparison requires one consistent renormalization scheme and the radiative corrections; the mass-ratio angle cannot be replaced silently by the effective angle.
