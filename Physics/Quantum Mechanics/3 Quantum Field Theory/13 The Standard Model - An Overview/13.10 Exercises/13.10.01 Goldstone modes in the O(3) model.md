## Exercise 13.1

Consider three real scalar fields $\signal{\phi}_i$, $i=1,2,3$, with an $O(3)$-invariant potential.

The potential is
$$
\energy{V}(\signal{\boldsymbol\phi})
=-\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{2}\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}4\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^4.
$$

1. **(a)** Find the minimum of the potential and the vacuum manifold.
2. **(b)** Count the Goldstone bosons with Theorem 13.2. What is the unbroken subgroup $H$ after spontaneous symmetry breaking?
3. **(c)** Parametrize the field about the vacuum $\langle\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rangle=(0,0,\signal{v})$. Identify the physical fields in the expanded Lagrangian density. Calculate the radial-field mass.

## Solution 13.1 — Goldstone modes in the O(3) model

The system is a relativistic scalar theory with a global O(3) symmetry and canonical kinetic terms. It has no gauge field that can absorb a Goldstone direction. Before expanding, predict the spectrum from the vacuum sphere: a sphere has two independent tangent directions at a point and one normal direction. The solution should give two massless fields and one massive radial field.

Use the potential Hessian as an independent check of the expansion. At the chosen vacuum its two transverse eigenvalues vanish, and its radial eigenvalue is positive. Counting three broken-looking field components would be incorrect because rotations about the vacuum direction leave the vacuum unchanged. The counting concerns the dimension of the orbit, not the number of scalar components or the disconnected reflections in O(3).

### (a) Vacuum manifold

For
$$
\energy{V}
=-\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{2}\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}4\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^4,
\qquad \energy{\mu}^2>0,\quad\signal{\lambda}>0,
$$
stationarity requires
$$
-\energy{\mu}^2\signal{\phi}_i
+\signal{\lambda}\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2\signal{\phi}_i=0.
$$
The minima obey
$$
\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2
=\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\signal{\lambda}}=\signal{v}^2.
$$
They form a sphere $S^2$ of radius $\signal{v}$ in field space, with a continuous family of minima connected by $O(3)$.

### (b) Unbroken group and Goldstone count

Choose $\langle\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rangle=(0,0,\signal{v})$.
The stabilizer is $O(2)$, acting in the plane perpendicular to the vacuum direction. Since $\dim O(3)=3$ and $\dim O(2)=1$,
$$
N_{\mathrm{GB}}=\dim G-\dim H=2.
$$
The two Goldstone modes are the tangential fluctuations $\signal{\phi}_1,\signal{\phi}_2$.

### (c) Expansion around the vacuum

Write
$$
\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}
=(\signal{\phi}_1,\signal{\phi}_2,\signal{v}+\signal{\sigma}),
\qquad
\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2
=\signal{v}^2+2\signal{v}\signal{\sigma}
+\signal{\sigma}^2+\signal{\phi}_1^2+\signal{\phi}_2^2.
$$
Insert this expression into the potential to identify the quadratic terms.

Using $\energy{\mu}^2=\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^2$, the expansion is
$$
\energy{V}
=\energy{V}_0+\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^2\signal{\sigma}^2
+\text{cubic and quartic terms}.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\mechanical{m}_\sigma^2
=2\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^2=2\energy{\mu}^2.}
$$
There are no quadratic mass terms for $\signal{\phi}_1,\signal{\phi}_2$. These are the two massless Goldstone bosons.
