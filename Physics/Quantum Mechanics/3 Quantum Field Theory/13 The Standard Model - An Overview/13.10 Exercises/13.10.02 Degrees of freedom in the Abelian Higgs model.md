## Exercise 13.2

In the Abelian Higgs model, §13.2.1, verify the degree-of-freedom count before and after spontaneous symmetry breaking.

1. **(a)** Before symmetry breaking, list the physical degrees of freedom of the photon and the complex scalar field. State the type of each mode, such as transverse or longitudinal.
2. **(b)** After symmetry breaking, in unitary gauge, list the degrees of freedom of the massive vector field $\electric{W}_\mu$ and the Higgs field $\signal{h}$.
3. **(c)** Show that the Goldstone field $\signal{\theta}$ supplies the longitudinal degree of freedom of $\electric{W}_\mu$.

## Solution 13.2 — Degrees of freedom in the Abelian Higgs model

Count physical modes after the gauge constraints, rather than counting the four components of a vector potential as four particles. The scalar kinetic term displays the phase and vector mixing explicitly; completing its square explains how one scalar coordinate contributes to a longitudinal vector polarization. The coupling called g in this exercise is the Abelian coupling called e in §13.2.1, not a second independent weak-isospin coupling.

The comparison is a count of variables in equivalent descriptions about the Higgs vacuum. The origin is not a stable particle vacuum for the negative quadratic potential, so the count before symmetry breaking is not a claim of a stable alternative vacuum there. Check that adding a separate physical Goldstone particle after forming the massive vector would incorrectly raise the total from four to five. The zero-coupling limit must be taken in the original fields because the displayed redefinition divides by the coupling.

### (a) Gauge and scalar fields before the Higgs description

A massless gauge field $\electric{A}_\mu$ has two transverse physical modes. The complex scalar
$\signal{\phi}=(\signal{\phi}_1+i\signal{\phi}_2)/\sqrt2$
has two real field components. The total is $2+2=4$ degrees of freedom.

### (b) Fields in unitary gauge about the broken vacuum

The massive vector $\electric{W}_\mu$ has three polarizations: two transverse and one longitudinal. The radial Higgs field $\signal{h}$ has one mode. Thus the total remains $3+1=4$.

### (c) Goldstone mode and longitudinal vector

Parametrize
$$
\signal{\phi}
=\frac{\signal{v}+\signal{h}}{\sqrt2}
e^{i\signal{\theta}/\signal{v}},
\qquad
D_\mu=\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\electric{A}_\mu.
$$
The scalar kinetic term contains
$$
\lvert D_\mu\signal{\phi}\rvert^2
=\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{h})^2
+\frac12\left(1+\frac{\signal{h}}{\signal{v}}\right)^2
(\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}
-\signal{g}\signal{v}\electric{A}_\mu)^2.
$$
Its quadratic terms include
$$
\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{\theta})^2
-\signal{g}\signal{v}\electric{A}^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}
+\frac12\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2\electric{A}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu.
$$
The redefinition
$$
\electric{W}_\mu
=\electric{A}_\mu-\frac1{\signal{g}\signal{v}}\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}
$$
absorbs this Goldstone–vector mixing.

The field redefinition is the unitary-gauge transformation that removes $\signal{\theta}$. The quadratic vector term becomes
$$
\frac12\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2\electric{W}_\mu\electric{W}^\mu
=\frac12\mechanical{m}_{W,\mathrm{Ab}}^2
\electric{W}_\mu\electric{W}^\mu,
\qquad \mechanical{m}_{W,\mathrm{Ab}}=\signal{g}\signal{v}.
$$
The longitudinal polarization accounts for the Goldstone degree of freedom. At energies much larger than the vector mass, the Goldstone equivalence theorem relates amplitudes with external longitudinal vectors to amplitudes with the corresponding Goldstone fields, with the applicable phase, normalization, and mass-suppressed corrections.
