## Exercise 13.8

Study the seesaw mechanism for neutrino masses.

1. **(a)** Write the mass matrix for one generation in the $(\signal{\nu}_L,\signal{\nu}_R)$ sector:
   $$
   \mechanical{\mathcal M}
   =\begin{pmatrix}
   0&\mechanical{m}_D\\
   \mechanical{m}_D&\mechanical{M}_R
   \end{pmatrix},
   \qquad
   \mechanical{m}_D=\frac{\signal{y}_\nu\signal{v}}{\sqrt2},
   \qquad
   \mechanical{M}_R\gg\mechanical{m}_D.
   $$
2. **(b)** Diagonalize the matrix and find its eigenvalues to leading order in $\mechanical{m}_D/\mechanical{M}_R$.
3. **(c)** For $\signal{y}_\nu\sim1$, which value of $\mechanical{M}_R$ gives a light-neutrino mass $\mechanical{m}_\nu\sim0.05\,\unit{eV}$, the atmospheric-oscillation mass scale?
4. **(d)** Discuss the physical meaning. Why is the small neutrino mass said to point to a new high-energy physics scale?

## Solution 13.8 — One-generation seesaw mechanism

### (a) Mass matrix

In the left-chiral basis $(\signal{\nu}_L,\signal{\nu}_R^c)$,
$$
\mechanical{\mathcal M}
=\begin{pmatrix}
0&\mechanical{m}_D\\
\mechanical{m}_D&\mechanical{M}_R
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The zero entry follows from the absence of a gauge-invariant renormalizable Majorana mass for the active neutrino with the stated Standard Model fields. A singlet right-chiral neutrino can have a Majorana mass $\mechanical{M}_R$, which the source takes much larger than the electroweak scale.

> [!note] Renormalizable scope
> The zero active–active entry refers to the renormalizable Lagrangian before the heavy singlet is eliminated. A gauge-invariant dimension-five operator can generate an active Majorana mass after electroweak symmetry breaking.

### (b) Eigenvalues

For real positive $\mechanical{M}_R$ and real $\mechanical{m}_D$, the characteristic equation is
$$
\det(\mechanical{\mathcal M}-\mechanical{m}_{\pm}1)=0,
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_{\pm}^2
-\mechanical{M}_R\mechanical{m}_{\pm}
-\mechanical{m}_D^2=0.
$$
Thus the signed eigenvalues are
$$
\mechanical{m}_{\pm}
=\frac{\mechanical{M}_R
\pm\sqrt{\mechanical{M}_R^2+4\mechanical{m}_D^2}}2.
$$
For $\mechanical{M}_R\gg\mechanical{m}_D$,
$$
\mechanical{m}_+
\simeq\mechanical{M}_R+\frac{\mechanical{m}_D^2}{\mechanical{M}_R},
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_-\simeq-\frac{\mechanical{m}_D^2}{\mechanical{M}_R}.
$$
A Majorana-field phase can remove the negative eigenvalue sign. The physical masses are
$$
\boxed{
\mechanical{m}_{\nu,\mathrm{light}}
\simeq\frac{\mechanical{m}_D^2}{\mechanical{M}_R},
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_{\nu,\mathrm{heavy}}\simeq\mechanical{M}_R.
}
$$

### (c) Heavy mass scale

Using
$$
\mechanical{m}_D=\frac{\signal{y}_\nu\signal{v}}{\sqrt2}
\simeq\signal{y}_\nu(174\,\unit{GeV}),
\qquad \signal{y}_\nu\sim1,
$$
and $\mechanical{m}_\nu=0.05\,\unit{eV}=5\times10^{-11}\,\unit{GeV}$,
$$
\mechanical{M}_R
\simeq\frac{(174\,\unit{GeV})^2}{5\times10^{-11}\,\unit{GeV}}
\simeq6\times10^{14}\,\unit{GeV}.
$$

### (d) Physical interpretation

The seesaw relation explains a small active-neutrino mass through a large Majorana scale, with an order-one Yukawa coupling. A Majorana mass violates lepton number, and hence baryon-minus-lepton number in this sector. The source associates the inferred $10^{14}$–$10^{15}\,\unit{GeV}$ scale with possible grand-unified physics.

> [!note] Scale and Yukawa estimates
> The source calls this an intermediate scale “between unification and quantum gravity.” The computed scale is below the usual grand-unified and Planck benchmarks. Its quoted purely Dirac alternative $\signal{y}_\nu\sim10^{-11}$ also does not follow from the stated $0.05\,\unit{eV}$ target: $\signal{y}_\nu=\sqrt2\mechanical{m}_\nu/\signal{v}\simeq2.9\times10^{-13}$. The mass hierarchy relative to charged fermions depends on which fermion is compared; it is not uniformly ten orders of magnitude.
