---
title: "The Gauge Group and Vector Bosons"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The electroweak gauge sector contains three SU(2)_L fields and one U(1)_Y field, with separate gauge couplings.

The group $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ has generators $T^a$, with $a=1,2,3$, for $SU(2)_L$, and $Y$ for $U(1)_Y$.
Their couplings are $\signal{g}$ and $\signal{g}'$, respectively.
The corresponding gauge fields are $\signal{W}_\mu^a$ and $\signal{B}_\mu$.
Their kinetic Lagrangian density is
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{gauge}}
=-\frac{1}{4}\signal{W}_{\mu\nu}^a\signal{W}^{a\mu\nu}
-\frac{1}{4}\signal{B}_{\mu\nu}\signal{B}^{\mu\nu},
$$
where the non-Abelian field strength is
$$
\signal{W}_{\mu\nu}^a
=\partial_\mu\signal{W}_\nu^a-\partial_\nu\signal{W}_\mu^a
+\signal{g}\varepsilon^{abc}\signal{W}_\mu^b\signal{W}_\nu^c.
$$

The Abelian hypercharge field strength is
$$
\signal{B}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\signal{B}_\nu-\partial_\nu\signal{B}_\mu.
$$
The nonlinear term in the weak field strength follows from the noncommuting generators. Squaring this field strength produces interactions among three and four weak gauge fields, with coefficients fixed by the same weak coupling that appears in the fermion derivative. The hypercharge field has no corresponding self-interaction in its own kinetic term because its group is Abelian.

These are gauge-basis fields. The neutral weak-isospin and hypercharge fields are not yet the physical photon and Z field. Their mass eigenstates are selected by the Higgs vacuum. As a consistency check, set the weak coupling to zero in the displayed field strength: its nonlinear part vanishes and three independent Abelian kinetic terms remain. Each field strength has mass dimension two, so its square has the required density dimension four.

After symmetry breaking, the four initially massless gauge fields give the massive fields
$\signal{W}_\mu^+$, $\signal{W}_\mu^-$, and $\signal{Z}_\mu$, together with the massless photon field $\electric{A}_\mu$.
