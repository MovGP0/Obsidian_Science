---
title: "The Hierarchy Problem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The hierarchy problem concerns the sensitivity of the Higgs mass to much larger energy scales. Proposed mechanisms can reduce this sensitivity.

The Higgs mass receives radiative corrections with quadratic ultraviolet-cutoff dependence:
$$
\delta\mechanical{m}_h^2\sim\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{16\pi^2}\energy{\Lambda}^2.
$$
Here $\energy{\Lambda}$ is the ultraviolet cutoff scale.
If the Standard Model is valid up to the Planck scale,
$\energy{\Lambda}\sim\mechanical{M}_P\approx10^{19}\,\unit{GeV}$,
a physical Higgs mass $\mechanical{m}_h\approx125\,\unit{GeV}$ requires a very precise cancellation between the bare mass parameter and the radiative correction.
This is the **hierarchy problem**, also called the **naturalness problem**.

> [!note]
> The source estimates the cancellation as about one part in $10^{34}$. This is an order-of-magnitude comparison of the squared Higgs and Planck scales. The displayed loop factor and coupling change the numerical estimate.

The source gives these proposed mechanisms:

- **Supersymmetry (SUSY):** Fermions have bosonic partners and bosons have fermionic partners. Their quadratic contributions cancel in the supersymmetric limit and can stabilize the Higgs mass. The source places the desired SUSY scale at no more than a few $\unit{TeV}$.
- **Extra dimensions:** In the proposals of Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos, and Dvali, or Randall and Sundrum, the large apparent Planck scale can result from the geometry of additional dimensions. The fundamental scale could be near the $\unit{TeV}$ scale.

> [!note]
> Equal partner masses apply to unbroken supersymmetry. Broken supersymmetry permits mass splittings. A numerical upper bound from naturalness depends on the spectrum and the accepted degree of cancellation. The geometric mechanisms in large and warped extra dimensions are different; the source groups them as possible explanations of the scale hierarchy.

- **Higgs compositeness:** The Higgs may be a composite particle rather than an elementary particle. The source compares this with pions in chiral symmetry breaking and suggests a compositeness scale of order $\unit{TeV}$. It mentions technicolor and modern composite-Higgs models.

The source reports no direct experimental confirmation of these scenarios at the LHC.

> [!note]
> Technicolor and composite-Higgs models are related proposals for new strong dynamics, but they do not have the same Higgs sector. The statement about experimental confirmation records the source's assessment.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}_h$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Higgs mass | The physical Higgs mass and the mass parameter affected by radiative corrections. |
| $\signal{\lambda}$ | $1$ | Higgs self-coupling | The dimensionless scalar coupling in the schematic correction. |
| $\energy{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Ultraviolet cutoff | The high-energy scale used to estimate the correction. |
| $\mechanical{M}_P$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Planck mass | The gravitational mass scale used as a possible cutoff. |
