---
title: "The Abelian Higgs Model"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A charged complex scalar with a nonzero vacuum value gives its Abelian gauge field a mass. In unitary gauge, the phase mode is absorbed into the vector field.

The Abelian Higgs model is the simplest example of the Higgs mechanism.
Consider a complex scalar $\signal{\Phi}$ with $U(1)$ coupling $\electric{e}$ and gauge field $\electric{A}_\mu$:
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}
=-\frac{1}{4}\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
+(D_\mu\signal{\Phi})^*(D^\mu\signal{\Phi})
-\energy{V}(\signal{\Phi}),
$$
where
$$
D_\mu\signal{\Phi}
=(\partial_\mu-i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu)\signal{\Phi},
\qquad
\energy{V}(\signal{\Phi})
=-\energy{\mu}^2\lvert\signal{\Phi}\rvert^2
+\signal{\lambda}\lvert\signal{\Phi}\rvert^4.
$$
The local $U(1)$ symmetry is
$$
\signal{\Phi}(\mechanical{x})\to e^{i\electric{e}\alpha(\mechanical{x})}\signal{\Phi}(\mechanical{x}),
\qquad
\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})\to
\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})+\partial_\mu\alpha(\mechanical{x}).
$$
For $\energy{\mu}^2>0$, the scalar has vacuum magnitude $\signal{v}/\sqrt{2}$.
Use the polar parametrization (13.1):
$$
\signal{\Phi}(\mechanical{x})
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
(\signal{v}+\signal{h}(\mechanical{x}))e^{i\signal{\theta}(\mechanical{x})/\signal{v}}.
$$
The covariant derivative becomes
$$
D_\mu\signal{\Phi}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{i\signal{\theta}/\signal{v}}
\left[
\partial_\mu\signal{h}
+i\frac{\signal{v}+\signal{h}}{\signal{v}}
(\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}-\electric{e}\signal{v}\electric{A}_\mu)
\right].
$$
The scalar kinetic term is
$$
\lvert D_\mu\signal{\Phi}\rvert^2
=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\signal{h})^2
+\frac{(\signal{v}+\signal{h})^2}{2}
\left(
\frac{\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}}{\signal{v}}-\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu
\right)^2.
$$
Keeping only quadratic terms in the fluctuations,
$$
\left.\lvert D_\mu\signal{\Phi}\rvert^2\right|_{\mathrm{quadratic}}
=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\signal{h})^2
+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}
-\electric{e}\signal{v}\electric{A}_\mu)^2.
$$
Apply a gauge transformation with
$\alpha(\mechanical{x})=-\signal{\theta}(\mechanical{x})/(\electric{e}\signal{v})$.
This gives **unitary gauge**:
$$
\electric{A}_\mu\to
\electric{A}_\mu-\frac{1}{\electric{e}\signal{v}}\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}
\equiv\electric{W}_\mu,
\qquad
\signal{\Phi}\to\frac{\signal{v}+\signal{h}}{\sqrt{2}}.
$$

> [!note]
> In its last display, the source writes $e^{+i\signal{\theta}/\signal{v}}\signal{\Phi}$ for the scalar transformation. With its stated $\alpha=-\signal{\theta}/(\electric{e}\signal{v})$, the multiplying gauge phase is instead $e^{-i\signal{\theta}/\signal{v}}$. This negative phase removes the phase already present in $\signal{\Phi}$.

In unitary gauge, the field $\signal{\theta}$ is absent from the physical scalar spectrum.
The gauge field has absorbed its degree of freedom.
The Lagrangian density becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal{L}}
={}&-\frac{1}{4}\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}[\electric{W}]\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}[\electric{W}]
+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\signal{h})^2
+\frac{1}{2}\electric{e}^2\signal{v}^2\electric{W}_\mu\electric{W}^\mu
-\energy{\mu}^2\signal{h}^2\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\electric{e}^2(2\signal{v}\signal{h}+\signal{h}^2)
\electric{W}_\mu\electric{W}^\mu
-\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}\signal{h}^3-\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{4}\signal{h}^4
+\text{constant}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Gauge-boson mass

The term
$\tfrac12\electric{e}^2\signal{v}^2\electric{W}_\mu\electric{W}^\mu$
is a mass term for the vector field:
$$
\mechanical{m}_W=\electric{e}\signal{v}.
$$

## Higgs-boson mass

The term $-\energy{\mu}^2\signal{h}^2$ gives the radial scalar mass
$$
\mechanical{m}_h^2=2\energy{\mu}^2=2\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^2.
$$

## Counting degrees of freedom

Before symmetry breaking:

- One massless gauge boson has two physical transverse polarizations.
- One complex scalar has two real components.
- The total is four physical degrees of freedom.

After symmetry breaking:

- One massive vector has three polarizations: two transverse and one longitudinal.
- One real Higgs scalar has one degree of freedom.
- The total remains four.

The Goldstone mode supplies the massive vector's longitudinal degree of freedom.
