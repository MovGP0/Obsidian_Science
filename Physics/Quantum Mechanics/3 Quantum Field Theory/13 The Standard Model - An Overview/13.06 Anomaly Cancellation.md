---
title: "Anomaly Cancellation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Gauge anomalies must cancel for a consistent quantum gauge theory. The Standard Model fermion representations satisfy these cancellation conditions.

A quantum gauge theory is consistent only if its gauge anomalies cancel. A **gauge anomaly** is a quantum breaking of a classical gauge symmetry, visible in loop amplitudes.

A chiral anomaly appears in a fermion triangle with three gauge vertices, the **triangle diagram**, associated with the Adler–Bell–Jackiw (ABJ) anomaly.
For gauge group $G$, cancellation requires
$$
\sum_f\operatorname{tr}
\left[T^a\{T^b,T^c\}\right]_f=0,
$$
where left-chiral fermions contribute with a positive sign and right-chiral fermions with a negative sign.

The potentially nonzero Standard Model gauge-anomaly structures include:

- **$[SU(3)_c]^3$.** Leptons carry no color. The quark contribution cancels because color occurs vectorially: the left-chiral $u_L,d_L$ contributions are balanced by $u_R,d_R$.
- **$[SU(2)_L]^3$.** The local perturbative anomaly vanishes identically because
  $\operatorname{tr}[\sigma^a\{\sigma^b,\sigma^c\}]=0$.
- **$[U(1)_Y]^3$.** The condition is
  $$
  \sum_f\electric{Y}_f^3=0,
  $$
  with positive signs for left-chiral fermions and negative signs for right-chiral fermions.

> [!note]
> The signs can equivalently be handled by writing all fermions as left-chiral fields and replacing each right-chiral field by its charge-conjugate representation. The vanishing local $SU(2)^3$ triangle anomaly is separate from the global $SU(2)$ anomaly condition.

For each generation, the cubic-hypercharge sum is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\underbrace{3\cdot2\left(\frac16\right)^3}_{Q_L}
+\underbrace{2\left(-\frac12\right)^3}_{L}
-\underbrace{3\left(\frac23\right)^3}_{u_R}\\
&\qquad
-\underbrace{3\left(-\frac13\right)^3}_{d_R}
-\underbrace{(-1)^3}_{e_R}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13.7}
$$
The factor three counts quark colors.
The factor two counts the components of an $SU(2)_L$ doublet.

- The mixed gravitational–$U(1)_Y$ anomaly requires the corresponding signed sum $\sum_f\electric{Y}_f=0$ for each generation.
- The mixed $[SU(3)_c]^2[U(1)_Y]$ and $[SU(2)_L]^2[U(1)_Y]$ anomalies also cancel because of the hypercharge assignments.

These cancellations reflect the Standard Model group and representation structure.
The source presents the balance between quark and lepton contributions as an argument in favor of grand unification.

## Example 13.6: Check of the SU(2)²U(1) anomaly

Using
$$
\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)_{\mathrm{doublet}}
=\frac12\delta^{ab},
$$
the cancellation condition reduces to
$$
\sum_{\mathrm{doublets}}\electric{Y}=0,
$$
where the sum includes color multiplicity.
For the quark and lepton doublets,
$$
\electric{Y}(Q_L)=\frac16,
\qquad \electric{Y}(L_L)=-\frac12.
$$
Then
$$
N_c\electric{Y}(Q_L)+\electric{Y}(L_L)
=3\cdot\frac16+\left(-\frac12\right)
=\frac12-\frac12=0.
$$
The quark and lepton contributions balance for $N_c=3$.
