---
title: "The Rξ Gauge"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Rξ gauges keep a controlled Goldstone sector and give propagators with useful ultraviolet behavior. Unitary gauge displays the physical fields directly but has poorer propagator power counting.

Unitary gauge has a clear physical interpretation because the Goldstone field is absent.
It also has a technical disadvantage.
The massive-vector propagator is
$$
\signal{G}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}_W^2}
\left(g^{\mu\nu}
-\frac{\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}{\mechanical{m}_W^2}\right).
\tag{13.2}
$$
The usual Feynman $i\epsilon$ prescription is suppressed here, as in the source.

At large momentum, the unitary-gauge propagator does not fall uniformly as $1/\mechanical{k}^2$.
This makes renormalizability harder to prove by power counting.
The **Rξ gauge**, introduced by ’t Hooft in 1971, adds a gauge-fixing term that retains the Goldstone field explicitly and gives better ultraviolet behavior.

The vector propagator is
$$
\signal{G}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
=\frac{-i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}_W^2}
\left(
g^{\mu\nu}
-\frac{(1-\xi)\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\xi\mechanical{m}_W^2}
\right).
$$
For $\xi\to\infty$, this approaches the unitary-gauge expression (13.2).
For $\xi=1$, it becomes the Feynman–’t Hooft propagator
$$
\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}_W^2}.
$$
The Goldstone field has gauge-dependent mass
$$
\mechanical{m}_\theta^2=\xi\mechanical{m}_W^2.
$$
Its mass grows without bound in the formal $\xi\to\infty$ limit.

> [!note]
> The would-be Goldstone field is unphysical for every gauge parameter; it is not a physical scalar that disappears only at $\xi\to\infty$. In Rξ gauges, it remains in intermediate calculations and combines with the other unphysical contributions in gauge-independent observables.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{G}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Massive-vector propagator | The momentum-space unitary-gauge two-point rule. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momentum | The momentum carried by the vector propagator. |
| $\mechanical{m}_W$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Vector mass | The mass in the propagator denominator and longitudinal numerator. |
| $\mechanical{m}_\theta$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Gauge-dependent Goldstone mass | The unphysical scalar mass in the Rξ propagator sector. |
