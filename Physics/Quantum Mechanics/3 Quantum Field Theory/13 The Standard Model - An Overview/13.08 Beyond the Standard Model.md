title: "Beyond the Standard Model"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Neutrino masses and other unresolved questions motivate extensions of the Standard Model despite its successful experimental tests.

Separate a missing observed effect from a theoretical preference. In the standard oscillation interpretation, nonzero neutrino mass differences require a change to the minimal massless-neutrino model. Dark-matter observations motivate additional matter under the gravitational and cosmological assumptions used to infer it. The hierarchy and unification problems instead ask why measured parameters have their observed structure; they do not by themselves establish a new particle.

Follow [[13.08.01 Neutrino Masses]] and [[13.08.03 Dark Matter]] for the observational questions. Compare [[13.08.02 The Hierarchy Problem]] and [[13.08.04 The Grand Unification Problem]] for model-building motivations, then [[13.08.05 Quantum Gravity]] for the gravitational scope. For each proposal, identify the added fields, the approximation that gives its low-energy effect, and an observable that could distinguish it. A successful explanation of one motivation does not confirm the others.

The Standard Model has many experimental successes, but it is not a complete account of fundamental physics. The source gives theoretical and experimental reasons to extend it, including phenomena that require additional fields or interactions.
