---
title: "Breaking a Discrete Symmetry — The Double-Well Potential"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A real scalar field with a double-well potential has two degenerate vacua. Choosing one vacuum breaks the discrete field-reflection symmetry.

Consider a real scalar field $\signal{\phi}$ with Lagrangian density
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})^2
-\energy{V}(\signal{\phi}),
\qquad
\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})
=-\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{2}\signal{\phi}^2
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{4}\signal{\phi}^4,
$$
where $\energy{\mu}^2>0$ and $\signal{\lambda}>0$.
The density is invariant under the discrete transformation
$\signal{\phi}\to-\signal{\phi}$.
The potential has a local maximum at $\signal{\phi}=0$ and two degenerate minima:
$$
\signal{\phi}_\pm=\pm\signal{v},
\qquad
\signal{v}=\sqrt{\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\signal{\lambda}}}.
$$

The parameter $\signal{v}$ is called the **vacuum expectation value** (VEV); see Fig. 13.1.

**Fig. 13.1** Double-well potential V(φ) = −μ²φ²/2 + λφ⁴/4, shifted by a constant so that the two degenerate minima at φ = ±v have zero height. Choosing one vacuum breaks the discrete reflection symmetry. The green point is the unstable stationary point.

> [!note]
> The source's red markers sit above the minima at the tops of dashed guides. The plot places the red points on the actual minima. It uses the dimensionless shape $(x^2-1)^2/4$ with $x=\signal{\phi}/\signal{v}$ and vertical unit $\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^4$; the displayed field labels retain the source's notation. The additive potential shift does not affect the field equations.

Choose the vacuum with $\langle\signal{\phi}\rangle=+\signal{v}$.
Expand about this minimum:
$$
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})=\signal{v}+\signal{\eta}(\mechanical{x}),
$$
where $\signal{\eta}$ describes the quantum fluctuation.
Substitution gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{V}(\signal{v}+\signal{\eta})
&=-\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{2}(\signal{v}+\signal{\eta})^2
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{4}(\signal{v}+\signal{\eta})^4\\
&=\text{constant}+\energy{\mu}^2\signal{\eta}^2
+\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}\signal{\eta}^3
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{4}\signal{\eta}^4,
\end{aligned}
$$
using $\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^2=\energy{\mu}^2$.
The quadratic term corresponds to a scalar mass
$$
\mechanical{m}_\eta^2=2\energy{\mu}^2>0.
$$
The cubic and quartic terms describe interactions.
The original reflection symmetry is no longer manifest in the Lagrangian density written in terms of $\signal{\eta}$; the chosen vacuum breaks it spontaneously.

> [!note]
> **Remark 13.1 from the source:** The symmetry is broken at the level of the vacuum; the Lagrangian density remains invariant. This affects the Fock-space structure. In the thermodynamic limit of infinite volume, the vacua $|\Omega_+\rangle$ and $|\Omega_-\rangle$ are orthogonal, and no local operator connects them.

> [!note]
> **Remark 13.1, continued:** In a finite system, tunneling mixes the two states. The mixing amplitude decreases exponentially with volume.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal{L}},\energy{V}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Lagrangian and potential densities | The scalar action density and its double-well potential. |
| $\signal{\phi},\signal{\phi}_\pm,\signal{v}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field and vacuum values | The field, its two minima, and the positive vacuum magnitude. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Potential mass scale | The positive scale entering the negative quadratic coefficient. |
| $\signal{\lambda}$ | $1$ | Quartic coupling | The positive coupling that stabilizes the potential. |
| $\signal{\eta}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fluctuation field | The scalar displacement from the positive vacuum. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinate | The point at which the scalar field is expanded. |
| $\mechanical{m}_\eta$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fluctuation mass | The mass associated with the quadratic term around the minimum. |
