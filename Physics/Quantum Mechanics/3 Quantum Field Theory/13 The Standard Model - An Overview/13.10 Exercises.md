### Solution 13.1 — Goldstone modes in the O(3) model

#### (a) Vacuum manifold

For
$$
\energy{V}
=-\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{2}\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2
+\frac{\signal{\lambda}}4\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^4,
\qquad \energy{\mu}^2>0,\quad\signal{\lambda}>0,
$$
stationarity requires
$$
-\energy{\mu}^2\signal{\phi}_i
+\signal{\lambda}\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2\signal{\phi}_i=0.
$$
The minima obey
$$
\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2
=\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\signal{\lambda}}=\signal{v}^2.
$$
They form a sphere $S^2$ of radius $\signal{v}$ in field space, with a continuous family of minima connected by $O(3)$.

#### (b) Unbroken group and Goldstone count

Choose $\langle\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rangle=(0,0,\signal{v})$.
The stabilizer is $O(2)$, acting in the plane perpendicular to the vacuum direction. Since $\dim O(3)=3$ and $\dim O(2)=1$,
$$
N_{\mathrm{GB}}=\dim G-\dim H=2.
$$
The two Goldstone modes are the tangential fluctuations $\signal{\phi}_1,\signal{\phi}_2$.

#### (c) Expansion around the vacuum

Write
$$
\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}
=(\signal{\phi}_1,\signal{\phi}_2,\signal{v}+\signal{\sigma}),
\qquad
\lvert\signal{\boldsymbol\phi}\rvert^2
=\signal{v}^2+2\signal{v}\signal{\sigma}
+\signal{\sigma}^2+\signal{\phi}_1^2+\signal{\phi}_2^2.
$$
Insert this expression into the potential to identify the quadratic terms.

Using $\energy{\mu}^2=\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^2$, the expansion is
$$
\energy{V}
=\energy{V}_0+\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^2\signal{\sigma}^2
+\text{cubic and quartic terms}.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\mechanical{m}_\sigma^2
=2\signal{\lambda}\signal{v}^2=2\energy{\mu}^2.}
$$
There are no quadratic mass terms for $\signal{\phi}_1,\signal{\phi}_2$. These are the two massless Goldstone bosons.

### Solution 13.2 — Degrees of freedom in the Abelian Higgs model

#### (a) Gauge and scalar fields before the Higgs description

A massless gauge field $\electric{A}_\mu$ has two transverse physical modes. The complex scalar
$\signal{\phi}=(\signal{\phi}_1+i\signal{\phi}_2)/\sqrt2$
has two real field components. The total is $2+2=4$ degrees of freedom.

#### (b) Fields in unitary gauge about the broken vacuum

The massive vector $\electric{W}_\mu$ has three polarizations: two transverse and one longitudinal. The radial Higgs field $\signal{h}$ has one mode. Thus the total remains $3+1=4$.

#### (c) Goldstone mode and longitudinal vector

Parametrize
$$
\signal{\phi}
=\frac{\signal{v}+\signal{h}}{\sqrt2}
e^{i\signal{\theta}/\signal{v}},
\qquad
D_\mu=\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\electric{A}_\mu.
$$
The scalar kinetic term contains
$$
\lvert D_\mu\signal{\phi}\rvert^2
=\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{h})^2
+\frac12\left(1+\frac{\signal{h}}{\signal{v}}\right)^2
(\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}
-\signal{g}\signal{v}\electric{A}_\mu)^2.
$$
Its quadratic terms include
$$
\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{\theta})^2
-\signal{g}\signal{v}\electric{A}^\mu\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}
+\frac12\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2\electric{A}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu.
$$
The redefinition
$$
\electric{W}_\mu
=\electric{A}_\mu-\frac1{\signal{g}\signal{v}}\partial_\mu\signal{\theta}
$$
absorbs this Goldstone–vector mixing.

The field redefinition is the unitary-gauge transformation that removes $\signal{\theta}$. The quadratic vector term becomes
$$
\frac12\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2\electric{W}_\mu\electric{W}^\mu
=\frac12\mechanical{m}_{W,\mathrm{Ab}}^2
\electric{W}_\mu\electric{W}^\mu,
\qquad \mechanical{m}_{W,\mathrm{Ab}}=\signal{g}\signal{v}.
$$
The longitudinal polarization accounts for the Goldstone degree of freedom. At energies much larger than the vector mass, the Goldstone equivalence theorem relates amplitudes with external longitudinal vectors to amplitudes with the corresponding Goldstone fields, with the applicable phase, normalization, and mass-suppressed corrections.

### Solution 13.3 — Leptonic decay of the W⁺ boson

#### (a) Charged-current vertex and propagator

The charged-current interaction contains
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{cc}}
=\frac{\signal{g}}{\sqrt2}\signal{W}_\mu^+
\bar{\signal{\nu}}_L\gamma^\mu\signal{e}_L+\mathrm{h.c.}
$$
in the source's overall interaction-sign convention. With $P_L=(1-\gamma^5)/2$, the vertex is
$$
V_{We\nu}^\mu=\frac{i\signal{g}}{\sqrt2}\gamma^\mu P_L.
$$
The unitary-gauge propagator is
$$
\signal{D}_W^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q})
=\frac{-i}{\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{m}_W^2+i0}
\left(g^{\mu\nu}
-\frac{\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu}{\mechanical{m}_W^2}\right).
$$

#### (b) Decay amplitude and fermion spin sum

For $W^+(\mechanical{q})\to e^+(\mechanical{p}_1)\nu_e(\mechanical{p}_2)$,
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}
=\frac{i\signal{g}}{\sqrt2}\signal{\varepsilon}_\mu(\mechanical{q})
\bar u(\mechanical{p}_2)\gamma^\mu P_Lv(\mechanical{p}_1).
$$
The overall phase has no effect on the width. With the W polarization held fixed and massless final fermions,
$$
\sum_{\mathrm{fermion\ spins}}\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{2}
\signal{\varepsilon}_\mu\signal{\varepsilon}_\nu^*
\operatorname{tr}[
\not{\mechanical{p}}_2\gamma^\mu P_L
\not{\mechanical{p}}_1\gamma^\nu P_L].
$$
Since $P_L\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}P_L
=\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{p}}P_L$,
the trace reduces to
$$
\operatorname{tr}[
\not{\mechanical{p}}_2\gamma^\mu
\not{\mechanical{p}}_1\gamma^\nu P_L]
=2\left[
\mechanical{p}_2^\mu\mechanical{p}_1^\nu
+\mechanical{p}_2^\nu\mechanical{p}_1^\mu
-g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2
+i\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}
\mechanical{p}_{1\alpha}\mechanical{p}_{2\beta}\right].
$$
The epsilon-term sign follows the source's gamma-five convention; it does not affect the later contraction with the symmetric polarization sum.

Average over the three initial W polarizations using
$$
\sum_{\lambda=1}^3
\signal{\varepsilon}_\mu^{(\lambda)}
\signal{\varepsilon}_\nu^{(\lambda)*}
=-g_{\mu\nu}+\frac{\mechanical{q}_\mu\mechanical{q}_\nu}{\mechanical{m}_W^2}.
$$
For massless final leptons, current conservation removes the term proportional to $\mechanical{q}_\mu\mechanical{q}_\nu$. The antisymmetric trace term also drops out. The contraction with $-g_{\mu\nu}$ gives $4\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2$, so
$$
\overline{\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2}
=\frac13\frac{\signal{g}^2}{2}
\,4\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2\mechanical{m}_W^2}{3},
$$
where $2\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2=\mechanical{m}_W^2$.

#### (c) Partial width, total width, and lifetime

The integrated massless two-body phase space is $1/(8\pi)$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Gamma}_e
&=\frac1{2\mechanical{m}_W}\frac1{8\pi}
\frac{\signal{g}^2\mechanical{m}_W^2}{3}\\
&=\frac{\signal{g}^2\mechanical{m}_W}{48\pi}
=\frac{\signal{G}_F\mechanical{m}_W^3}{6\pi\sqrt2},
\qquad
\frac{\signal{g}^2}{8\mechanical{m}_W^2}
=\frac{\signal{G}_F}{\sqrt2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
With the supplied $\mechanical{m}_W=80.4\,\unit{GeV}$ and
$\signal{G}_F=1.166\times10^{-5}\,\unit{GeV^{-2}}$,
$$
\energy{\Gamma}_e\simeq0.227\,\unit{GeV}.
$$
For a specified hadronic channel,
$$
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to u\bar d)
=3\lvert V_{ud}\rvert^2\energy{\Gamma}_e,
\qquad \lvert V_{ud}\rvert\simeq0.974.
$$
The factor three counts color. Summing three lepton families and two accessible up-type quark families gives, at this massless Born level,
$$
\energy{\Gamma}_W
\simeq3\energy{\Gamma}_e+3(2)\energy{\Gamma}_e
=9\energy{\Gamma}_e\simeq2.05\,\unit{GeV}.
$$
The top channel is closed. The source quotes an experimental width of
$2.085\,\unit{GeV}$ for comparison. The corresponding lifetime estimate is
$$
\mechanical{\tau}_W=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}}{\energy{\Gamma}_W}
\simeq3.2\times10^{-25}\,\unit{s}.
$$

> [!note] Inclusive hadronic sum
> The source labels the two hadronic contributions simply $u\bar d$ and $c\bar s$. The inclusive factor two uses CKM unitarity to sum the accessible down-type partners for each of $u,c$. It also neglects quark-mass and QCD corrections. The quoted experimental number is retained as a source benchmark.

### Solution 13.4 — Weak mixing angle

#### (a) Mass ratio

With the supplied masses,
$$
\cos\signal{\theta}_W
=\frac{80.369}{91.188}\simeq0.8813,
\qquad
\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W
=1-\frac{\mechanical{m}_W^2}{\mechanical{m}_Z^2}
\simeq0.2233.
$$

#### (b) Vacuum expectation value

From $\signal{G}_F=1/(\sqrt2\signal{v}^2)$,
$$
\signal{v}^2
=\frac1{\sqrt2(1.1664\times10^{-5}\,\unit{GeV^{-2}})}
\simeq6.062\times10^4\,\unit{GeV^2},
\qquad
\boxed{\signal{v}\simeq246.22\,\unit{GeV}.}
$$

#### (c) Gauge couplings

The tree-level mass relations give
$$
\signal{g}=\frac{2\mechanical{m}_W}{\signal{v}}\simeq0.6529,
\qquad
\signal{g}'=\signal{g}\tan\signal{\theta}_W\simeq0.3500.
$$
Thus
$$
\electric{e}=\signal{g}\sin\signal{\theta}_W\simeq0.3085,
\qquad
\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}
=\frac{\electric{e}^2}{4\pi}\simeq0.00757\simeq\frac1{132.1}.
$$

> [!note] Precision comparison
> The source compares this tree-level result with the supplied running-coupling benchmark $\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}(\mechanical{m}_Z)\simeq1/128$ and the effective angle $\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W^{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq0.23122$. A precision comparison requires one consistent renormalization scheme and the radiative corrections; the mass-ratio angle cannot be replaced silently by the effective angle.

### Solution 13.5 — Cubic hypercharge anomaly

The cubic anomaly coefficient is the sum of cubed hypercharges, including color and weak-component multiplicities. Left-chiral fields contribute with a plus sign and right-chiral fields with a minus sign:
$$
\mathcal A=\sum_f\operatorname{sgn}(f)\,N_c(f)\,\electric{Y}_f^3,
$$
where the sum includes every weak component.

For this solution the source uses the convention
$Q=T_3+\electric{Y}$, so $\electric{Y}$ is half the hypercharge used in the convention $Q=T_3+\electric{Y}/2$. The cancellation is independent of this common normalization.

The contributions for one generation are:

| Multiplet | Hypercharge | Multiplicity | Signed cubic contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $Q_L$ | $\electric{Y}=1/6$ | $3\times2$ | $+3(2)(1/6)^3=1/36$ |
| $u_R$ | $\electric{Y}=2/3$ | $3$ | $-3(2/3)^3=-8/9$ |
| $d_R$ | $\electric{Y}=-1/3$ | $3$ | $-3(-1/3)^3=1/9$ |
| $L_L$ | $\electric{Y}=-1/2$ | $2$ | $+2(-1/2)^3=-1/4$ |
| $e_R$ | $\electric{Y}=-1$ | $1$ | $-(-1)^3=1$ |

The sum is
$$
\mathcal A=\frac1{36}-\frac89+\frac19-\frac14+1
=\frac{1-32+4-9+36}{36}=0.
$$
Thus the anomaly cancels exactly between the quark and lepton sectors. The source relates this cancellation to an $SO(10)$ embedding in a spinor $\mathbf{16}$.

> [!note] SO(10) field count
> The $\mathbf{16}$ includes the charge-conjugate right-chiral neutrino in addition to the fifteen chiral Standard Model components. Its hypercharge is zero, so it does not change this cubic hypercharge sum.

### Solution 13.6 — Rho parameter and scalar multiplets

#### (a) Tree-level mass contributions

Use the convention $Q=T_3+\electric{Y}$ for this solution. Consider complex scalar multiplets with a neutral vacuum component of isospin weight $T_3$. Write its expectation value as $\signal{v}_{T_3}/\sqrt2$, so that the Standard Model doublet has $\signal{v}\simeq246\,\unit{GeV}$. The tree-level contributions are
$$
\mechanical{m}_W^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{2}
\sum\left[T(T+1)-T_3^2\right]\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2,
$$
and
$$
\mechanical{m}_Z^2
=(\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2)
\sum T_3^2\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W}
\sum T_3^2\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2.
$$
Each sum runs over the neutral vacuum components. Real scalar multiplets require the corresponding real-field normalization factors.

For the Standard Model doublet, $T=1/2$, $\electric{Y}=1/2$, and the neutral component has $T_3=-1/2$. Therefore
$$
\mechanical{m}_W^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{2}\left(\frac34-\frac14\right)\signal{v}^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2}{4},
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_Z^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2}{4\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W}.
$$
It follows that
$$
\boxed{\signal{\rho}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}_W^2}
{\mechanical{m}_Z^2\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W}=1.}
$$
The source relates this tree-level relation to the custodial $SU(2)_c$ structure of the doublet sector, described by $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\supset SU(2)_c$. Hypercharge and unequal Yukawa couplings break the full custodial symmetry, so radiative corrections must be treated separately.

#### (b) Complex triplet

For $T=1$, $\electric{Y}=1$, the neutral component has $T_3=-1$. Then
$$
T(T+1)-T_3^2=1,
\qquad 2T_3^2=2.
$$
A vacuum supplied only by this complex triplet gives $\signal{\rho}=1/2$, rather than one. With an additional doublet vacuum,
$$
\signal{\rho}
=\frac{\signal{v}_d^2+2\signal{v}_t^2}
{\signal{v}_d^2+4\signal{v}_t^2}.
$$

> [!note] Neutral component and multiplet scope
> The source uses $T_3=+1$ for $\electric{Y}=1$; with $Q=T_3+\electric{Y}$ the neutral component is $T_3=-1$. The squared weight, and thus the mass ratio, is unchanged. A single additional complex triplet generally shifts the ratio, but suitably combined and aligned multiplets can preserve custodial symmetry. Triplet field content alone does not exclude that possibility.

#### (c) Constraint from the supplied fit

The source gives $\signal{\rho}_0=1.00038\pm0.00020$ after subtraction of the stated Standard Model corrections. The central value differs from one by $1.9$ quoted standard deviations. The result constrains additional custodial violation at the level of a few parts in $10^4$.

The source concludes that a doublet-dominated scalar sector, or another custodially symmetric structure, is favored. Its estimate
$\lvert\signal{v}_t/\signal{v}_d\rvert\lesssim10^{-2}$
is an order-of-magnitude bound for a simple unaligned triplet extension; an exact limit needs a specified model and confidence interval. Gauge singlets do not contribute directly to these vector masses.

### Solution 13.7 — Higgs couplings to gauge bosons

#### (a) Expansion of the scalar kinetic term

In unitary gauge,
$$
\signal{H}=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}0\\\signal{v}+\signal{h}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The kinetic term gives
$$
(D_\mu\signal{H})^\dagger D^\mu\signal{H}
=\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{h})^2
+\frac{(\signal{v}+\signal{h})^2}{4}
\left[
\signal{g}^2\signal{W}_\mu^+\signal{W}^{-\mu}
+\frac{\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2}{2}\signal{Z}_\mu\signal{Z}^\mu
\right].
$$

Expand $(\signal{v}+\signal{h})^2=\signal{v}^2+2\signal{v}\signal{h}+\signal{h}^2$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal L}\supset{}&
\frac14\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2\signal{W}^+\cdot\signal{W}^-
+\frac12\signal{g}^2\signal{v}\signal{h}\signal{W}^+\cdot\signal{W}^-
+\frac14\signal{g}^2\signal{h}^2\signal{W}^+\cdot\signal{W}^-\\
&+\frac18(\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2)\signal{v}^2\signal{Z}^2
+\frac14(\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2)\signal{v}\signal{h}\signal{Z}^2
+\frac18(\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2)\signal{h}^2\signal{Z}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The vertex factors include the identical-field differentiation factors:

| Vertex | Feynman rule |
| --- | --- |
| $hW^+W^-$ | $i(2\mechanical{m}_W^2/\signal{v})g_{\mu\nu}$ |
| $hZZ$ | $i(2\mechanical{m}_Z^2/\signal{v})g_{\mu\nu}$ |
| $hhW^+W^-$ | $i(2\mechanical{m}_W^2/\signal{v}^2)g_{\mu\nu}$ |
| $hhZZ$ | $i(2\mechanical{m}_Z^2/\signal{v}^2)g_{\mu\nu}$ |

For $V=W,Z$, the compact form is
$$
\signal{g}_{hVV}=\frac{2\mechanical{m}_V^2}{\signal{v}},
\qquad
\signal{g}_{hhVV}=\frac{2\mechanical{m}_V^2}{\signal{v}^2}.
$$
The identical-$Z$ factor is two; the double-Higgs rules also include the factor from the two identical Higgs fields.

#### (b) Coupling ratio

The ratio is
$$
\frac{\signal{g}_{hhVV}}{\signal{g}_{hVV}}=\frac1{\signal{v}}.
$$
At tree level the doublet kinetic term fixes both couplings through the same vacuum scale. Their measurement tests this structure. The source cites single- and multiple-Higgs vector-boson processes as probes; extracting either coupling requires the complete amplitude for the chosen process.

#### (c) Higgs decay into two on-shell W bosons

For $\mechanical{m}_h>2\mechanical{m}_W$, sum physical W polarizations using
$\sum\signal{\varepsilon}_\mu\signal{\varepsilon}_\nu^*
=-g_{\mu\nu}+\mechanical{p}_\mu\mechanical{p}_\nu/\mechanical{m}_W^2$.
Since $2\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2
=\mechanical{m}_h^2-2\mechanical{m}_W^2$,
$$
\sum\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}_{hWW}\rvert^2
=\left(\frac{2\mechanical{m}_W^2}{\signal{v}}\right)^2
\left[
2+\frac{(\mechanical{m}_h^2-2\mechanical{m}_W^2)^2}{4\mechanical{m}_W^4}
\right].
$$

> [!note] Polarization factor
> The supplied intermediate amplitude omits the factor four in the denominator of the second term. The contraction contains $(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2/\mechanical{m}_W^4$, which supplies that factor. The boxed width in the source agrees with the corrected amplitude.

With $\beta=\sqrt{1-4\mechanical{m}_W^2/\mechanical{m}_h^2}$, the width is
$$
\boxed{
\energy{\Gamma}(h\to W^+W^-)
=\frac{\signal{G}_F\mechanical{m}_h^3}{8\pi\sqrt2}
\,\beta
\left[
1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}_W^2}{\mechanical{m}_h^2}
+\frac{12\mechanical{m}_W^4}{\mechanical{m}_h^4}
\right].
}
$$
This is the two-on-shell-vector result; it does not describe a below-threshold decay with an off-shell W.

For the source values $\mechanical{m}_h=125\,\unit{GeV}$ and
$\mechanical{m}_W=80.4\,\unit{GeV}$, the two-on-shell-W channel is closed. The decay proceeds through an off-shell W, schematically
$h\to WW^*\to Wf\bar f'$, followed by the W decays. The source quotes a branching fraction of about $22\%$ and calls this the second-largest Higgs decay channel; these are supplied phenomenological benchmarks.

### Solution 13.8 — One-generation seesaw mechanism

#### (a) Mass matrix

In the left-chiral basis $(\signal{\nu}_L,\signal{\nu}_R^c)$,
$$
\mechanical{\mathcal M}
=\begin{pmatrix}
0&\mechanical{m}_D\\
\mechanical{m}_D&\mechanical{M}_R
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The zero entry follows from the absence of a gauge-invariant renormalizable Majorana mass for the active neutrino with the stated Standard Model fields. A singlet right-chiral neutrino can have a Majorana mass $\mechanical{M}_R$, which the source takes much larger than the electroweak scale.

> [!note] Renormalizable scope
> The zero active–active entry refers to the renormalizable Lagrangian before the heavy singlet is eliminated. A gauge-invariant dimension-five operator can generate an active Majorana mass after electroweak symmetry breaking.

#### (b) Eigenvalues

For real positive $\mechanical{M}_R$ and real $\mechanical{m}_D$, the characteristic equation is
$$
\det(\mechanical{\mathcal M}-\mechanical{m}_{\pm}1)=0,
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_{\pm}^2
-\mechanical{M}_R\mechanical{m}_{\pm}
-\mechanical{m}_D^2=0.
$$
Thus the signed eigenvalues are
$$
\mechanical{m}_{\pm}
=\frac{\mechanical{M}_R
\pm\sqrt{\mechanical{M}_R^2+4\mechanical{m}_D^2}}2.
$$
For $\mechanical{M}_R\gg\mechanical{m}_D$,
$$
\mechanical{m}_+
\simeq\mechanical{M}_R+\frac{\mechanical{m}_D^2}{\mechanical{M}_R},
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_-\simeq-\frac{\mechanical{m}_D^2}{\mechanical{M}_R}.
$$
A Majorana-field phase can remove the negative eigenvalue sign. The physical masses are
$$
\boxed{
\mechanical{m}_{\nu,\mathrm{light}}
\simeq\frac{\mechanical{m}_D^2}{\mechanical{M}_R},
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_{\nu,\mathrm{heavy}}\simeq\mechanical{M}_R.
}
$$

#### (c) Heavy mass scale

Using
$$
\mechanical{m}_D=\frac{\signal{y}_\nu\signal{v}}{\sqrt2}
\simeq\signal{y}_\nu(174\,\unit{GeV}),
\qquad \signal{y}_\nu\sim1,
$$
and $\mechanical{m}_\nu=0.05\,\unit{eV}=5\times10^{-11}\,\unit{GeV}$,
$$
\mechanical{M}_R
\simeq\frac{(174\,\unit{GeV})^2}{5\times10^{-11}\,\unit{GeV}}
\simeq6\times10^{14}\,\unit{GeV}.
$$

#### (d) Physical interpretation

The seesaw relation explains a small active-neutrino mass through a large Majorana scale, with an order-one Yukawa coupling. A Majorana mass violates lepton number, and hence baryon-minus-lepton number in this sector. The source associates the inferred $10^{14}$–$10^{15}\,\unit{GeV}$ scale with possible grand-unified physics.

> [!note] Scale and Yukawa estimates
> The source calls this an intermediate scale “between unification and quantum gravity.” The computed scale is below the usual grand-unified and Planck benchmarks. Its quoted purely Dirac alternative $\signal{y}_\nu\sim10^{-11}$ also does not follow from the stated $0.05\,\unit{eV}$ target: $\signal{y}_\nu=\sqrt2\mechanical{m}_\nu/\signal{v}\simeq2.9\times10^{-13}$. The mass hierarchy relative to charged fermions depends on which fermion is compared; it is not uniformly ten orders of magnitude.

### Solution 13.9 — Photon and Z exchange in electron–positron annihilation

#### (a) Tree amplitudes

The two diagrams are $s$-channel $\gamma^*$ and $Z^*$ exchange. In a consistent overall phase convention,
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}_{\gamma}
=\bar v_e(-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu)u_e
\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{s}+i0}
\bar u_\mu(-i\electric{e}\gamma^\nu)v_\mu,
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\signal{\mathcal M}_{Z}
={}&\bar v_e\frac{-i\signal{g}}{\cos\signal{\theta}_W}
\gamma^\mu(\signal{g}_V^e-\signal{g}_A^e\gamma^5)u_e\\
&\times
\frac{-i(g_{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}_\mu\mechanical{q}_\nu/\mechanical{m}_Z^2)}
{\mechanical{s}-\mechanical{m}_Z^2+i\mechanical{m}_Z\energy{\Gamma}_Z}
\bar u_\mu\frac{-i\signal{g}}{\cos\signal{\theta}_W}
\gamma^\nu(\signal{g}_V^\mu-\signal{g}_A^\mu\gamma^5)v_\mu.
\end{aligned}
$$
For these vertex conventions,
$$
\signal{g}_V^f=\frac{T_3^f}{2}-Q_f\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W,
\qquad
\signal{g}_A^f=\frac{T_3^f}{2}.
$$
For charged leptons $T_3^e=T_3^\mu=-1/2$, $Q_e=Q_\mu=-1$. These are half the couplings used with a vertex prefactor $\signal{g}/(2\cos\signal{\theta}_W)$.

#### (b) Below the Z resonance

For $4\mechanical{m}_\mu^2\ll\mechanical{s}\ll\mechanical{m}_Z^2$, the photon amplitude scales with $1/\mechanical{s}$ while the Z exchange is suppressed by $1/\mechanical{m}_Z^2$. The leading massless QED result is
$$
\boxed{
\mechanical{\sigma}(e^+e^-\to\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-)
=\frac{4\pi\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}^2}{3\mechanical{s}}.
}
$$

#### (c) At the Z resonance

At $\mechanical{s}=\mechanical{m}_Z^2$, the resonant denominator is
$i\mechanical{m}_Z\energy{\Gamma}_Z$. The width regulates the pole, and Z exchange dominates the resonant cross section.

The pure resonant peak formula is
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{peak}}(e^+e^-\to Z\to\mu^+\mu^-)
=\frac{12\pi}{\mechanical{m}_Z^2}
\frac{\energy{\Gamma}_e\energy{\Gamma}_\mu}{\energy{\Gamma}_Z^2}.
$$
With the supplied leptonic partial widths near $0.084\,\unit{GeV}$,
$\energy{\Gamma}_Z=2.50\,\unit{GeV}$, and
$\mechanical{m}_Z=91.2\,\unit{GeV}$,
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{peak}}
=\frac{12\pi(0.084)^2}{(91.2)^2(2.50)^2}\,\unit{GeV^{-2}}
\simeq2\,\unit{nb},
$$
using $1\,\unit{GeV^{-2}}=0.389\,\unit{mb}=3.89\times10^5\,\unit{nb}$.

> [!note] Peak channels
> The source calls $41.5\,\unit{nb}$ the total peak cross section over all final states. This value is the supplied hadronic peak benchmark, $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{had}}^0$, not the sum over all Z decay channels. The formula above is the idealized resonant contribution; a measured line shape also requires the relevant radiation and experimental corrections.

### Solution 13.10 — Accidental Standard Model symmetries

#### (a) Classical baryon and lepton numbers

Assign baryon charge $\signal{B}=1/3$ to quarks and zero to leptons and the Higgs. With the charge-normalized transformation
$$
\signal{q}\mapsto e^{i\alpha/3}\signal{q},
$$
the kinetic, gauge, and renormalizable Yukawa terms preserve baryon number. In particular, $\bar{\signal{Q}}_L\signal{H}\signal{q}_R$ has zero net baryon charge. The source writes $e^{i\alpha}$ for the quark phase, which uses a rescaled group parameter.

Neglecting neutrino masses, a charged-lepton mass basis has separate classical lepton-family symmetries
$U(1)_{L_e}\times U(1)_{L_\mu}\times U(1)_{L_\tau}$.
There is no physical neutrino mixing in this massless-neutrino limit.

A schematic dimension-six baryon-violating interaction is
$$
\mathcal O_{qqq\ell}
\sim\frac1{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{UV}}^2}
\varepsilon^{ijk}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon_{\gamma\delta}
(\signal{Q}_L^{i\alpha}\signal{Q}_L^{j\gamma})
(\signal{Q}_L^{k\delta}\signal{L}_L^\beta),
$$
with Lorentz spinor contractions understood. Color and weak indices are contracted to a gauge singlet. It contains three quarks and one lepton, so its field monomial carries $\signal{B}=\signal{L}=1$.

> [!note] Operator classes
> The source calls $QQQL$ the only dimension-six gauge-invariant operator type that violates baryon number. It is one class. Operators with right-chiral quark and charged-lepton fields also exist, with schematic field content $duq\ell$, $qque$, and $duue$, using the appropriate Lorentz and gauge contractions. Their Hermitian conjugates carry the opposite baryon and lepton charges.

The operator conserves $\signal{B}-\signal{L}$ and allows proton decay such as $p\to e^+\pi^0$ through the appropriate interaction or its Hermitian conjugate. The source estimates
$$
\mechanical{\tau}_p\sim
\frac{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{UV}}^4}{\mechanical{m}_p^5}
$$
in natural units, and quotes about $10^{30}$ years for
$\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{UV}}\sim10^{15}\,\unit{GeV}$.

> [!note] Lifetime estimate
> This scaling suppresses operator coefficients, hadronic matrix elements, and phase-space factors. With only the displayed ratio, $\mechanical{m}_p\simeq0.938\,\unit{GeV}$, and the unit conversion from inverse GeV to years, the estimate is about $3\times10^{28}$ years. The quoted $10^{30}$ years requires additional numerical factors. This dimensional estimate is not an experimental lifetime limit.

#### (b) Mixed anomalies and nonperturbative baryon-plus-lepton violation

For one generation, baryon-minus-lepton charges are $1/3$ for quarks and $-1$ for leptons. The mixed $[SU(2)_L]^2U(1)_{B-L}$ anomaly coefficient is
$$
\frac12\left[3\left(\frac13\right)-1\right]=0,
$$
where $T_F=1/2$ is the weak-doublet trace normalization. The mixed $[U(1)_Y]^2U(1)_{B-L}$ sum, with the half-hypercharge convention used above and right-chiral contributions subtracted, is
$$
\frac1{18}-\frac49-\frac19-\frac12+1=0.
$$
The color mixed anomaly cancels as well. Thus $\signal{B}-\signal{L}$ has no mixed anomaly with the Standard Model gauge fields.

> [!note] Meaning of anomaly free
> These mixed gauge-anomaly cancellations do not require a right-chiral neutrino. A singlet right-chiral neutrino makes the usual baryon-minus-lepton assignment vectorlike and cancels the additional cubic and mixed gravitational anomalies. Without it, the minimal model does not have an entirely anomaly-free gauged $B-L$ symmetry. A Majorana singlet mass would also explicitly break this global charge.

For baryon-plus-lepton number, the weak mixed anomaly is nonzero:
$$
\frac12\left[3\left(\frac13\right)+1\right]=1
$$
per generation. The source displays the bracket as $2$ while suppressing the common $T_F$ factor.

A convention with explicit gauge normalization is
$$
q_{\mathrm{top}}(\mechanical{x})
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{32\pi^2}
\signal{W}_{\mu\nu}^a\tilde{\signal{W}}^{a\mu\nu},
\qquad
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,q_{\mathrm{top}}=n_{\mathrm{inst}}\in\mathbb Z.
$$
Then
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{j}_{B+L}^{\mu}=2N_gq_{\mathrm{top}},
\qquad
\Delta(\signal{B}+\signal{L})=2N_gn_{\mathrm{inst}}.
$$
For three generations and unit topological charge,
$$
\boxed{\Delta\signal{B}=\Delta\signal{L}=3,}
\qquad \Delta(\signal{B}-\signal{L})=0.
$$
The source writes the anomaly schematically as
$N_g\operatorname{Tr}(W\tilde W)/(16\pi^2)$; the trace and inclusion of the gauge coupling must be defined to compare coefficients.

At zero temperature, electroweak instanton effects are exponentially suppressed. The source quotes an $e^{-8\pi^2/\signal{g}^2}$ suppression of order $10^{-80}$; this is a semiclassical instanton weight, not by itself a complete physical transition-rate formula. At sufficiently high temperatures near and above the electroweak scale, thermal sphaleron transitions can efficiently violate $\signal{B}+\signal{L}$ while conserving $\signal{B}-\signal{L}$. The source gives $\thermal{T}\gtrsim100\,\unit{GeV}$ as an order-of-magnitude scale. These processes are important in baryogenesis and leptogenesis scenarios.
