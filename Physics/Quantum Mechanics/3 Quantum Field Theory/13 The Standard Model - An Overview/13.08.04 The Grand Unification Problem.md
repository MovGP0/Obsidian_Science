---
title: "The Grand Unification Problem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Renormalization-group evolution suggests a possible common origin of the strong and electroweak gauge couplings at a very high energy scale.

The three gauge couplings are written as
$$
\signal{\alpha}_s=\frac{\signal{g}_s^2}{4\pi},
\qquad
\signal{\alpha}_2=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{4\pi},
\qquad
\signal{\alpha}_1=\frac53\frac{\signal{g}'^{\,2}}{4\pi}.
$$
The factor $5/3$ gives the conventional grand-unification normalization of hypercharge.

When renormalization-group evolution is included, the couplings approach one another at high energy. The source gives the proposed scale
$$
\mechanical{M}_{\mathrm{GUT}}\sim2\times10^{16}\,\unit{GeV}.
$$
See Fig. 13.5.

> [!note]
> The three couplings do not meet exactly in the minimal Standard Model. Their meeting and its scale depend on the particle content, thresholds, and normalization. The quoted scale is a typical supersymmetric unification benchmark.

In supersymmetric models, additional particle partners change the beta functions. This can improve the convergence substantially.
It motivates a **grand unified theory (GUT)** with one gauge group that contains the Standard Model gauge group as a subgroup.
Examples are $SU(5)$ (Georgi–Glashow), $SO(10)$, and $E_6$.

**Fig. 13.5** Qualitative, uncalibrated evolution of inverse gauge couplings in the Standard Model. The three lines do not meet at one point in the minimal Standard Model. Supersymmetric particle content can improve their convergence.

GUT models generally predict proton decay on very long time scales. The source gives typical lifetimes
$$
\mechanical{\tau}_p\sim10^{34}\text{–}10^{36}\,\unit{yr}.
$$
It states that the absence of a proton-decay signal at Super-Kamiokande excludes the minimal nonsupersymmetric $SU(5)$ model, while many variants remain possible.

> [!note]
> The graph is schematic, as in the source. Its straight-line coordinates and dashed guide do not define a precision running-coupling fit or a measured unification scale. Proton-decay lifetimes depend on the model and decay channel; the quoted range is the source's benchmark.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\alpha}_s,\signal{\alpha}_2,\signal{\alpha}_1$ | $1$ | Gauge coupling strengths | The strong, weak, and grand-unification-normalized hypercharge couplings. |
| $\signal{g}_s,\signal{g},\signal{g}'$ | $1$ | Gauge interaction couplings | The corresponding coefficients before division by four pi. |
| $\mechanical{M}_{\mathrm{GUT}}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Unification scale | The proposed high-energy mass scale where the couplings can meet. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The energy scale on the graph's horizontal axis. |
| $\mechanical{M}_Z$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Z-boson mass | The reference mass in the logarithmic scale ratio. |
| $\mechanical{\tau}_p$ | $\unit{yr}$ | Proton lifetime | The model-dependent lifetime quoted for proton decay. |
