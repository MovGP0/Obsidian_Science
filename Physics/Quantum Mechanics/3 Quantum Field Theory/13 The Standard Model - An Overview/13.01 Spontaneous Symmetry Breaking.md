> [!TLDR]
> Spontaneous symmetry breaking lets a symmetric theory have a vacuum that does not share the full symmetry. This is central to electroweak symmetry breaking in the Standard Model.

## Chapter introduction

> “The Standard Model has been confirmed so many times that it is embarrassing.”
>
> — Steven Weinberg, as quoted in the source

The preceding chapters developed canonical quantization of scalar, fermion, and vector fields, perturbation theory, renormalization, and non-Abelian gauge theory.
These tools provide the basis for the **Standard Model of fundamental interactions**.

The Standard Model describes three of the four fundamental forces: electromagnetic, weak, and strong interactions.
Its gauge-symmetry group is
$$
G_{\mathrm{SM}}=SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y.
$$
The subscript $c$ denotes color, the strong-interaction charge.
The subscript $L$ indicates that the weak $SU(2)$ symmetry acts on left-chiral fermion multiplets.
The subscript $Y$ denotes weak hypercharge.

The physical realization of this symmetry at low energy requires symmetry breaking.

At low energy, $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$ is spontaneously broken to the electromagnetic group $U(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$.
The $W^\pm$ and $Z$ gauge bosons acquire mass.
The **Higgs mechanism** is central to this structure.

This chapter first discusses spontaneous symmetry breaking and the Higgs mechanism.
It then constructs the Glashow–Weinberg–Salam electroweak theory, adds the QCD sector, and presents the full Lagrangian density and an overview of experimental results.

## Spontaneous symmetry breaking

Spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) has applications from condensed-matter physics to cosmology.
Its basic idea is that the Lagrangian density has a symmetry that the ground state, or vacuum, does not share.
