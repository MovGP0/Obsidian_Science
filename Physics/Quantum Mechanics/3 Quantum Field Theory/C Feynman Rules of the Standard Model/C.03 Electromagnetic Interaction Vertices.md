---
title: "Electromagnetic Interaction Vertices"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The electromagnetic fermion vertex is proportional to the fermion's electric charge. Neutral fermions have no direct photon vertex in the minimal Standard Model.

The rule encodes the local electromagnetic current of a Dirac fermion. The index on the gamma matrix contracts with the photon propagator or external polarization. For a quark, an identity in color space is implicit: photon exchange does not change color or fermion flavor. Follow the fermion arrow when assembling the spinor chain and use all-incoming momenta at the vertex.

For a fermion $f$ with charge $\electric{Q}_f$ in units of $\electric{e}>0$, the source's QED vertex is
$$
\gamma\bar f f:\qquad +i\electric{e}\electric{Q}_f\gamma^\mu.
\tag{C.13}
$$
For an electron, $\electric{Q}_e=-1$, giving $-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu$.

The Standard Model charges are:

| Fermions | $e,\mu,\tau$ | $\nu_e,\nu_\mu,\nu_\tau$ | $u,c,t$ | $d,s,b$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{Q}_f$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $+2/3$ | $-1/3$ |

Keep $\electric{Q}_f$ as the charge of the fermion field when an external antifermion is crossed. Its physical charge is opposite, but its external spinor and arrow already encode that crossing; changing the field charge again would count the reversal twice. There is no identical-field factorial for this single photon and fermion–antifermion rule.

The coefficient is dimensionless. It vanishes for $\electric{Q}_f=0$ and when $\electric{e}=0$. These checks explain the absence of a direct tree-level neutrino–photon vertex in the minimal theory; they do not exclude loop-induced electromagnetic form factors. For an on-shell equal-mass fermion current, contracting the photon momentum with the current gives zero by the Dirac equations. This checks charge-current conservation before adding photon exchange to an amplitude.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{Q}_f,\electric{Q}_e$ | $1$ | Fermion electric charge | The charge number in units of the positive elementary charge. |
| $\electric{e}$ | $1$ | Electromagnetic coupling | The positive elementary charge in natural units. |
