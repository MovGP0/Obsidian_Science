---
title: "Discrete CPT Symmetry"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The combined CPT transformation is a symmetry of local Lorentz-invariant theories with the usual spin–statistics connection.

Relativistic field theories generally have three fundamental discrete transformations:

- **Parity P:** $(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x})\to(\mechanical{t},-\mechanical{\mathbf x})$. A scalar field transforms as $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x})\to\eta_P\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{t},-\mechanical{\mathbf x})$, where $\eta_P=\pm1$ is intrinsic parity.
- **Charge conjugation C:** particles become antiparticles; for a complex scalar field, $\signal{\phi}\to\signal{\phi}^{*}$.
- **Time reversal T:** $(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x})\to(-\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x})$. It is antiunitary in quantum mechanics.

The CPT theorem states that every local Lorentz-invariant field theory with normal spin–statistics, integer-spin bosons and half-integer-spin fermions, is invariant under the combined CPT transformation. In particular, a particle and its antiparticle have equal masses and identical lifetimes. Individual discrete symmetries can be violated.

Parity is violated by the weak interaction. CP violation occurs in K- and B-meson transitions. The source associates C violation with matter–antimatter asymmetry.

> [!note]
> A matter–antimatter asymmetry in a state does not by itself prove that the underlying interaction violates C.
