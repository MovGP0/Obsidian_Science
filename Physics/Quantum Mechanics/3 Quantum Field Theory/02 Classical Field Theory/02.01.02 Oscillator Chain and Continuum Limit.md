---
title: "Oscillator Chain and Continuum Limit"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A chain of coupled oscillators becomes a continuous field when the lattice spacing tends to zero at fixed mass density and stiffness.

Consider a chain of $N$ identical harmonic oscillators connected by springs. Each oscillator has mass $\mechanical{m}$, and each spring has constant $\mechanical{k}$. Let $\mechanical{q}_j(\mechanical{t})$ be the longitudinal displacement of oscillator j from equilibrium position $j\mechanical{a}$, where $\mechanical{a}$ is the lattice spacing. The Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{L}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\frac{\mechanical{m}}{2}\mechanical{\dot q}_j^{2}-\frac{\mechanical{k}}{2}(\mechanical{q}_{j+1}-\mechanical{q}_j)^{2}\right]. \tag{2.4}
$$

Introduce a Lagrangian density per lattice site. Each term contributes an amount proportional to the spacing. Define $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_j,\mechanical{t})=\mechanical{q}_j(\mechanical{t})$ and take $\mechanical{a}\to0$, holding fixed the linear mass density $\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{a}$ and stiffness $\mechanical{Y}=\mechanical{k}\mechanical{a}$, the one-dimensional elastic modulus. The ratio $(\mechanical{q}_{j+1}-\mechanical{q}_j)/\mechanical{a}$ becomes $\partial_{\mechanical{x}}\signal{\phi}$, and the sum becomes

$$
\energy{L}\xrightarrow{\mechanical{a}\to0}\int d\mechanical{x}\left[\frac{\mechanical{\mu}}{2}(\partial_{\mechanical{t}}\signal{\phi})^{2}-\frac{\mechanical{Y}}{2}(\partial_{\mechanical{x}}\signal{\phi})^{2}\right]
\equiv\int d\mechanical{x}\,\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi},\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}). \tag{2.5}
$$

The Lagrangian density contains a kinetic term proportional to the squared field time derivative and an elastic term proportional to the squared spatial derivative. This is the basic structure for a real scalar field.

The equation of motion, verified systematically in the next section, is

$$
\partial_{\mechanical{t}}^{2}\signal{\phi}-\frac{\mechanical{Y}}{\mechanical{\mu}}\partial_{\mechanical{x}}^{2}\signal{\phi}=0,
\qquad \mechanical{v}=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{Y}}{\mechanical{\mu}}}.
$$

In three spatial dimensions and natural units, this field description leads to the Klein–Gordon equation. A field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})$ is a system with infinitely many degrees of freedom, one at each spatial point. The continuous coordinate replaces the discrete site index.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Oscillator mass | The mass at each lattice site. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The coupling between adjacent oscillators. |
| $\mechanical{a},\mechanical{x}_j,\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Positions and spacing | The lattice spacing, site coordinate, and continuum coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{q}_j,\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement | The discrete displacement and its continuum field. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The oscillator evolution time. |
| $\mechanical{\dot q}_j$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Displacement rate | The oscillator velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-1}}$ | Linear mass density | The mass per chain length. |
| $\mechanical{Y}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Stiffness | The one-dimensional elastic modulus. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The total mechanical Lagrangian. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{J\,m^{-1}}$ | Lagrangian density | The Lagrangian per unit chain length. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Wave speed | The propagation speed set by stiffness and mass density. |
