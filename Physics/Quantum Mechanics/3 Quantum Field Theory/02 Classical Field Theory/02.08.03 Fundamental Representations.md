title: "Fundamental Representations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Pairs of representation labels distinguish scalars, Weyl and Dirac spinors, vectors, and antisymmetric tensors.

The source summarizes the main representations as follows:

| Representation | Dimension | Spin | Field |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $(0,0)$ | 1 | 0 | Scalar $\signal{\phi}$ |
| $(\tfrac12,0)$ | 2 | $\tfrac12$ | Left-handed Weyl spinor $\signal{\psi}_L$ |
| $(0,\tfrac12)$ | 2 | $\tfrac12$ | Right-handed Weyl spinor $\signal{\psi}_R$ |
| $(\tfrac12,0)\oplus(0,\tfrac12)$ | 4 | $\tfrac12$ | Dirac spinor $\signal{\Psi}$ |
| $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ | 4 | 1 | Vector $\signal{A}^{\mu}$ |
| $(1,0)\oplus(0,1)$ | 6 | 1 | Antisymmetric tensor $\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}$ |

The vector representation is real: $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)^{*}\cong(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$. This explains the four-vector transformation of the vector potential. Weyl spinors are the smallest nontrivial fundamental representations. Parity exchanges the left- and right-handed representations. A chiral gauge theory, such as the electroweak sector of the Standard Model, can therefore violate parity.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The scalar Lorentz representation with canonical normalization. |
| $\signal{\psi}_L,\signal{\psi}_R,\signal{\Psi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Spinor fields | The Weyl and Dirac representations with canonical field normalization. |
| $\signal{A}^{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Vector field | The four-vector representation. |
| $\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Field-strength tensor | The antisymmetric tensor representation. |
