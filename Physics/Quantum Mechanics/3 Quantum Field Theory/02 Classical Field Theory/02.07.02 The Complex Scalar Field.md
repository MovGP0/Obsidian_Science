title: "The Complex Scalar Field"

> [!TLDR]
> A complex scalar field describes charged scalar particles and permits a global charge symmetry.

A complex scalar field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\in\mathbb C$ describes charged scalar particles, such as charged pions $\pi^{\pm}$ in an approximate model. Its Lagrangian density is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{*})(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})-\mechanical{m}^{2}|\signal{\phi}|^{2}-\lambda|\signal{\phi}|^{4}. \tag{2.49}
$$

The model is invariant under global $U(1)$ transformations, $\signal{\phi}\to e^{i\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\phi}$. As in section 2.5.2, its Noether current is $\electric{J}^{\mu}=i[\signal{\phi}^{*}\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}-\signal{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{*}]$, with conserved charge $\electric{Q}$.

Write $\signal{\phi}=(\signal{\phi}_1+i\signal{\phi}_2)/\sqrt2$ in terms of two real fields. Then

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=\tfrac12(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}_1)^{2}+\tfrac12(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}_2)^{2}-\tfrac12\mechanical{m}^{2}(\signal{\phi}_1^{2}+\signal{\phi}_2^{2})-\frac{\lambda}{4}(\signal{\phi}_1^{2}+\signal{\phi}_2^{2})^{2}. \tag{2.50}
$$

The phase transformation becomes a rotation in the two-field plane. The source writes $O(2)\cong U(1)$ for this relation.

> [!note]
> The continuous plane-rotation subgroup is SO(2). The source's O(2) notation also includes reflections, so the displayed group identification needs that qualification.

The construction generalizes to $O(N)$ or $U(N)$ symmetries with multiple scalar fields and provides the basis of nonlinear sigma models.

## Double-Well Potential and Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking

For $\mechanical{m}^{2}<0$, the mass parameter is not the squared mass of a physical particle. The potential becomes

$$
\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})=-|\mechanical{m}^{2}|\,|\signal{\phi}|^{2}+\lambda|\signal{\phi}|^{4}
=\lambda\left(|\signal{\phi}|^{2}-\frac{|\mechanical{m}^{2}|}{2\lambda}\right)^{2}+\text{const.} \tag{2.51}
$$

The potential has a Mexican-hat form. Its minimum is not at zero field, but on a circle satisfying $|\signal{\phi}_0|^{2}=|\mechanical{m}^{2}|/(2\lambda)$. Fig. 2.2 shows a real-field slice.

**Fig. 2.2** Left: the positive-mass-squared scalar potential has one minimum at zero field. Right: a slice of the negative-mass-squared Mexican-hat potential has degenerate minima at ±φ₀. Selecting one vacuum breaks the U(1) symmetry spontaneously.

Choose $\langle\signal{\phi}\rangle=\signal{\phi}_0\in\mathbb R$ and write $\signal{\phi}=\signal{\phi}_0+\signal{\eta}/\sqrt2+i\signal{\xi}/\sqrt2$. The field $\signal{\eta}$ has mass $\mechanical{m}_{\eta}^{2}=2|\mechanical{m}^{2}|>0$, while $\signal{\xi}$ is massless. It is the Goldstone boson associated with the spontaneously broken continuous global symmetry. Chapter 13 returns to this result.
