## Exercise 2.1 — Equations of Motion from the Variational Principle

Consider the massive-vector Proca Lagrangian:

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac14\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}+\frac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{A}_{\mu}\signal{A}^{\mu}.
$$

**(a)** Derive the equations of motion with the Euler–Lagrange equations.

**(b)** Show that the Proca field is not gauge invariant, unlike the Maxwell field. What follows for the number of physical degrees of freedom?

**(c)** Take the divergence of the field equations and show that they imply the Lorenz condition $\partial_{\mu}\signal{A}^{\mu}=0$.

**(d)** Use this condition to show that every component obeys $(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{A}^{\mu}=0$.

## Exercise 2.2 — Noether Current and Charge for O(N)

Use the scalar model (2.69).

**(a)** Calculate the $N(N-1)/2$ Noether currents $J^{\mu}_{ab}$ explicitly.

**(b)** Verify $\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{ab}=0$ with the equations of motion.

**(c)** Calculate the Poisson brackets of the conserved charges $Q_{ab}$ and verify that they form $\mathfrak{so}(N)$.

**(d)** For $N=3$, identify the three charges with isospin angular momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf I}=(\mechanical{I}_1,\mechanical{I}_2,\mechanical{I}_3)$.

## Exercise 2.3 — Maxwell Energy–Momentum Tensor with a Source

Add a conserved external-current coupling:

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}-\electric{J}^{\mu}\electric{A}_{\mu}.
$$

**(a)** Derive the Maxwell equations with a source.

**(b)** Calculate the canonical tensor $\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}$ and show that it is not gauge invariant.

**(c)** Construct the symmetric gauge-invariant Belinfante tensor $\energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}$ by adding a term of the form $\partial_{\rho}B^{\rho\mu\nu}$.

**(d)** Identify $\energy{\Theta}^{00}$ and $\energy{\Theta}^{0i}$ with electromagnetic energy density and the Poynting vector.

## Exercise 2.4 — Virial Theorem for Fields

For a real scalar Lagrangian $\energy{\mathcal L}=\tfrac12(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})^{2}-\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})$, consider static configurations $\signal{\phi}=\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})$.

**(a)** Write the static field equation, a nonlinear Poisson equation.

**(b)** Prove Derrick's theorem: for $d\ge3$ spatial dimensions and $\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})\ge0$, there are no nontrivial static finite-energy solutions. **Hint:** consider $\signal{\phi}_{\lambda}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})=\signal{\phi}(\lambda\mechanical{\mathbf x})$ and examine energy as a function of the scaling parameter.

**(c)** Show that nontrivial solutions can occur for two spatial dimensions if the potential has a suitable form.

## Exercise 2.5 — Chern–Simons Model in 2+1 Dimensions

For $\mu,\nu,\rho=0,1,2$, the gauge-field Chern–Simons term is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{CS}}=\frac{k}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\signal{A}_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\signal{A}_{\rho},
$$

where the Levi-Civita symbol is three dimensional.

**(a)** Verify gauge invariance up to a total divergence. The action is gauge invariant when the boundary contributes nothing.

**(b)** Derive the field equations and show that they give $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\signal{F}_{\nu\rho}=0$, hence $\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}=0$: the gauge field is flat.

**(c)** Combine the term with the three-dimensional Maxwell Lagrangian and derive the equations of motion. Show that they permit a massive photon.

## Exercise 2.6 — Bianchi Identities and Duality

Define the dual tensor $\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}$ with $\varepsilon^{0123}=+1$.

**(a)** Show that $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\electric{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\electric{A}_{\mu}$ implies $\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}=0$.

**(b)** Express these identities in terms of $\electric{\mathbf E}$ and $\magnetic{\mathbf B}$ and identify the homogeneous Maxwell equations.

**(c)** Show that the quadratic Lorentz invariants are $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=2(\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{2}-\electric{\mathbf E}^{2})$ and $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}=-4\electric{\mathbf E}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}$.

**(d)** Electromagnetic duality sends $\electric{\mathbf E}\to\magnetic{\mathbf B}$ and $\magnetic{\mathbf B}\to-\electric{\mathbf E}$. Show that it preserves the vacuum Maxwell equations but not the Lagrangian $\energy{\mathcal L}=-\tfrac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$.

## Source solutions

The following solutions are supplied in Appendix D of the source.

### Solution 2.1 — Proca field equations

**(a)** For
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=-\frac14\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\frac12\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}_\mu\signal{A}^\mu,
$$
the variational derivatives are
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{A}_\nu}
=\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^\nu,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{A}_\nu)}
=-\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}.
$$
Thus
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$

**(b)** Under $\signal{A}_\mu\to\signal{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha$, the field tensor and its kinetic term are invariant.
The mass term instead becomes
$$
\frac12\mechanical{m}^2
(\signal{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha)
(\signal{A}^\mu+\partial^\mu\alpha),
$$
which is not generally equal to $\tfrac12\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}_\mu\signal{A}^\mu$.
For $\mechanical{m}\ne0$, the mass term explicitly breaks this gauge invariance.
The massive Proca field consequently has three physical polarizations, rather than the photon's two.

**(c)** Apply $\partial_\nu$ to the field equation.
Antisymmetry gives
$$
\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}=0,
$$
so that
$$
\mechanical{m}^2\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$
For $\mechanical{m}\ne0$,
$$
\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$

**(d)** With $\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu=0$,
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
=\Box\signal{A}^\nu-\partial^\nu(\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu)
=\Box\signal{A}^\nu.
$$
Hence
$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$

Each component therefore satisfies the Klein–Gordon equation.

### Solution 2.2 — O(N) Noether currents

**(a)** The Lagrangian density
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\frac12\sum_a(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi}^a)^2
-\energy{V}(\signal{\phi}^a\signal{\phi}^a)
$$
is invariant under $O(N)$:
$$
\signal{\phi}^a\longrightarrow O^a{}_b\signal{\phi}^b,
\qquad
O^TO=\mathbf1.
$$
For $\delta\signal{\phi}^a=\omega^a{}_b\signal{\phi}^b$, with
$\omega_{ab}=-\omega_{ba}$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{j}^\mu
&=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi}^a)}
\delta\signal{\phi}^a
=(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^a)(\omega^a{}_b\signal{\phi}^b)\\
&=\frac12\omega_{ab}
\left[
(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^a)\signal{\phi}^b
-(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^b)\signal{\phi}^a
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
For each pair $(a,b)$, the current is
$$
\signal{J}_{ab}^\mu
=(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^a)\signal{\phi}^b
-(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^b)\signal{\phi}^a.
$$

**(b)** Its divergence is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\mu\signal{J}_{ab}^\mu
={}&(\Box\signal{\phi}^a)\signal{\phi}^b
+(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^a)(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi}^b)\\
&-(\Box\signal{\phi}^b)\signal{\phi}^a
-(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi}^b)(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi}^a)\\
={}&(\Box\signal{\phi}^a)\signal{\phi}^b
-(\Box\signal{\phi}^b)\signal{\phi}^a.
\end{aligned}
$$
Write $\signal{R}^2=\signal{\phi}^a\signal{\phi}^a$ and
$\energy{V}'(\signal{R}^2)=\partial\energy{V}/\partial(\signal{R}^2)$.
The field equation gives
$$
\Box\signal{\phi}^a=-2\energy{V}'(\signal{R}^2)\signal{\phi}^a.
$$
Thus
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{J}_{ab}^\mu
=-2\energy{V}'(\signal{R}^2)
(\signal{\phi}^a\signal{\phi}^b-\signal{\phi}^b\signal{\phi}^a)=0.
$$

**(c)** With $\mechanical{\pi}^a=\dot{\signal{\phi}}^{\,a}$, the charges are
$$
\signal{Q}_{ab}
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
(\mechanical{\pi}^a\signal{\phi}^b-\mechanical{\pi}^b\signal{\phi}^a).
$$
Use the canonical Poisson bracket
$$
\{\signal{\phi}^a(\mechanical{\mathbf x}),\mechanical{\pi}^b(\mechanical{\mathbf y})\}
=\delta^{ab}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}).
$$
Direct calculation gives
$$
\{\signal{Q}_{ab},\signal{Q}_{cd}\}
=-\delta_{ac}\signal{Q}_{bd}
+\delta_{ad}\signal{Q}_{bc}
+\delta_{bc}\signal{Q}_{ad}
-\delta_{bd}\signal{Q}_{ac}.
$$
This is the $\mathfrak{so}(N)$ algebra with the chosen charge-sign convention.

**(d)** For $N=3$, the independent charges are
$\signal{Q}_{12},\signal{Q}_{13},\signal{Q}_{23}$.
With the source's definition
$$
\mechanical{I}_k=\frac12\epsilon_{kab}\signal{Q}_{ab},
$$
their bracket is
$$
\{\mechanical{I}_i,\mechanical{I}_j\}=-\epsilon_{ijk}\mechanical{I}_k.
$$
Reversing all three charge signs gives the usual positive-sign rotation algebra.
The three fields $(\signal{\phi}^1,\signal{\phi}^2,\signal{\phi}^3)$ form an isospin triplet.

> [!note] Correction to the source
> The source prints the opposite signs for the charge bracket and a positive-sign bracket for $I_k$. With its displayed $Q_{ab}=\int(\pi^a\phi^b-\pi^b\phi^a)$ and $\{\phi,\pi\}=+\delta$, the brackets have the signs shown here. For example, $\{Q_{12},Q_{23}\}=Q_{13}$. A uniform reversal of the charges restores the source's intended positive-sign generator convention.

### Solution 2.3 — Maxwell equations and energy–momentum tensor

**(a)** For the Maxwell Lagrangian density with an external current,
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
-\electric{J}^\mu\electric{A}_\mu,
$$
the variational derivatives are
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\electric{A}_\nu}
=-\electric{J}^\nu,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu)}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}.
$$

The Euler–Lagrange equation gives
$$
-\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}-(-\electric{J}^\nu)=0,
\qquad
\partial_\mu\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=\electric{J}^\nu.
$$

**(b)** The canonical tensor is
$$
\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\rho)}
\partial^\nu\electric{A}_\rho-g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial^\nu\electric{A}_\rho
-g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}.
$$
Its explicit potential dependence makes it gauge dependent.
For the free field, $\electric{J}^\mu=0$, a gauge transformation
$\electric{A}_\mu\to\electric{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha$ gives
$$
\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
\longrightarrow
\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}
-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial^\nu\partial_\rho\alpha.
$$

**(c)** Add a total divergence $\partial_\rho B^{\rho\mu\nu}$ with
$$
B^{\rho\mu\nu}=\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\electric{A}^\nu,
$$
which is antisymmetric in $\rho,\mu$.
Using $\partial_\rho\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}=-\electric{J}^\mu$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\rho(\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\electric{A}^\nu)
&=(\partial_\rho\electric{F}^{\mu\rho})\electric{A}^\nu
+\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial_\rho\electric{A}^\nu\\
&=-\electric{J}^\mu\electric{A}^\nu
+\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial_\rho\electric{A}^\nu.
\end{aligned}
$$
The derivative term combines with the corresponding canonical term to reconstruct the field tensor.
The improved expression obtained from the full source-coupled Lagrangian is
$$
\energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu}{}_\rho\electric{F}^{\nu\rho}
+\frac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}
+g^{\mu\nu}\electric{J}\cdot\electric{A}
-\electric{J}^\mu\electric{A}^\nu.
$$
For the free field, $\electric{J}^\mu=0$,
$$
\energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}
=-\electric{F}^{\mu}{}_\rho\electric{F}^{\nu\rho}
+\frac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}.
$$
This tensor is symmetric and gauge invariant.

> [!note] External-current qualification
> The source says that the source term itself breaks gauge invariance unless the current is dynamical. A conserved external current does not break gauge invariance of the action: the variation of its coupling is a boundary term when $\partial_\mu\electric{J}^\mu=0$. The explicit source terms above concern this assignment of the stress tensor. The gauge-shift formula in part (b) also suppresses the source-dependent variation, so it is stated there for the free field.

**(d)** In vacuum, with the mostly-minus signature,
$$
\electric{F}^{0i}\electric{F}^{0}{}_{i}=-\lvert\electric{\mathbf E}\rvert^2,
\qquad
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
=-2\left(\lvert\electric{\mathbf E}\rvert^2-\lvert\magnetic{\mathbf B}\rvert^2\right).
$$
Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Theta}^{00}
&=\lvert\electric{\mathbf E}\rvert^2
-\frac12\left(\lvert\electric{\mathbf E}\rvert^2-\lvert\magnetic{\mathbf B}\rvert^2\right)\\
&=\frac12\left(\lvert\electric{\mathbf E}\rvert^2+\lvert\magnetic{\mathbf B}\rvert^2\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the electromagnetic energy density.

For the energy-flux components, use
$\electric{F}^{0j}=-\electric{E}^j$ and
$\electric{F}^{i}{}_{j}=\epsilon_{ijk}\magnetic{B}^k$:
$$
\energy{\Theta}^{0i}
=-\electric{F}^{0j}\electric{F}^{i}{}_{j}
=-(-\electric{E}^j)(\epsilon_{ijk}\magnetic{B}^k)
=( \electric{\mathbf E}\times\magnetic{\mathbf B})_i.
$$
These are the components of the Poynting vector.

### Solution 2.4 — Derrick scaling argument

**(a)** The field equation is
$$
\Box\signal{\phi}+\energy{V}'(\signal{\phi})=0.
$$
For a static field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})$,
$$
-\boldsymbol\nabla^2\signal{\phi}+\energy{V}'(\signal{\phi})=0,
$$
a nonlinear Poisson equation.

**(b)** In $d$ spatial dimensions, the total energy is
$$
\energy{E}[\signal{\phi}]
=\int d^d\mechanical{\mathbf x}
\left[\frac12\lvert\boldsymbol\nabla\signal{\phi}\rvert^2
+\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})\right]
\equiv\energy{E}_\nabla+\energy{E}_V.
$$
Consider the scaled field
$$
\signal{\phi}_\lambda(\mechanical{\mathbf x})
=\signal{\phi}(\lambda\mechanical{\mathbf x}).
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{E}_\nabla[\signal{\phi}_\lambda]
&=\int d^d\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
\frac12\lambda^2
\lvert(\boldsymbol\nabla\signal{\phi})(\lambda\mechanical{\mathbf x})\rvert^2\\
&=\lambda^{2-d}\energy{E}_\nabla[\signal{\phi}],
\end{aligned}
$$
and
$$
\energy{E}_V[\signal{\phi}_\lambda]
=\int d^d\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
\energy{V}(\signal{\phi}(\lambda\mechanical{\mathbf x}))
=\lambda^{-d}\energy{E}_V[\signal{\phi}].
$$
Thus
$$
\energy{E}(\lambda)
=\lambda^{2-d}\energy{E}_\nabla+\lambda^{-d}\energy{E}_V.
$$
A static solution must be stationary under this scaling:
$$
\left.\frac{d\energy{E}}{d\lambda}\right|_{\lambda=1}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(2-d)\energy{E}_\nabla-d\energy{E}_V=0.
$$
For $d\ge3$, both coefficients are negative.
If $\energy{V}\ge0$, both energy terms are nonnegative, so the only possibility is
$$
\energy{E}_\nabla=\energy{E}_V=0.
$$
The field is constant at a vacuum.
This is the source's Derrick no-go result for nontrivial static scalar configurations.

**(c)** For $d=2$, the gradient coefficient vanishes and the scaling condition becomes
$$
-2\energy{E}_V=0.
$$
The potential contribution must vanish, but the gradient contribution need not vanish from this scaling condition alone.

The source cites the two-dimensional $O(3)$ sigma model and Nielsen–Olesen vortices as examples in which nontrivial configurations occur.
Gauge terms, target-space constraints, topology, or higher-derivative terms can change the assumptions of the scalar scaling argument.

> [!note]
> The no-go statement assumes localized finite-energy configurations, a canonical two-derivative scalar energy functional, and a nonnegative potential. The $d=2$ condition is necessary, not a general existence proof. The nonlinear sigma model has constrained fields, while Nielsen–Olesen vortices include gauge fields.

### Solution 2.5 — Chern–Simons gauge variation

**(a)** Under $\signal{A}_\mu\to\signal{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{CS}}
&=\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}
(\partial_\mu\alpha)\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\rho
+\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}
\signal{A}_\mu\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\alpha\\
&=\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}
\partial_\mu(\alpha\,\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\rho)+0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The antisymmetry of $\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}$ gives
$\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu=0$.
Hence the variation is a total divergence,
$$
\delta\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{CS}}
=\partial_\mu\left(
\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\alpha\,\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\rho
\right),
$$
and the action is invariant up to boundary terms.

**(b)** Write
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{CS}}
=\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\signal{A}_\mu\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\rho.
$$
After integration by parts and use of antisymmetry,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{S}_{\mathrm{CS}}
&=\int d^3\mechanical{x}\,\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}
\left[
\delta\signal{A}_\mu\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\rho
+\signal{A}_\mu\partial_\nu\delta\signal{A}_\rho
\right]\\
&=\int d^3\mechanical{x}\,\signal{k}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}
(\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\rho)\delta\signal{A}_\mu.
\end{aligned}
$$
The field equation is
$$
\signal{k}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\rho
=\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\signal{F}_{\nu\rho}
=0.
$$
For nonzero $\signal{k}$, this implies $\signal{F}_{\nu\rho}=0$: the connection is flat and is locally pure gauge.

**(c)** Combine Maxwell and Chern–Simons terms:
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=-\frac14\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\signal{A}_\mu\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\rho.
$$
The field equation becomes
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\signal{k}\epsilon^{\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_\rho\signal{A}_\sigma
=\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{\signal{k}}2\epsilon^{\nu\rho\sigma}\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}
=0.
$$
In $2+1$ dimensions, define the dual vector
$$
\tilde{\signal{F}}^\mu
=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho}\signal{F}_{\nu\rho}.
$$
The field equation is
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}+\signal{k}\tilde{\signal{F}}^\nu=0.
$$
A further derivative, together with the Bianchi identity, gives
$$
(\Box+\signal{k}^2)\tilde{\signal{F}}_\mu=0.
$$
The gauge field acquires a topological mass
$$
\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{CS}}=\lvert\signal{k}\rvert.
$$

> [!note]
> The source writes $m=k$; the physical nonnegative mass is $\lvert\signal{k}\rvert$, or $\signal{k}$ if it is assumed positive. Flatness in the pure Chern–Simons theory implies a pure-gauge form locally; global holonomies can remain on a space with nontrivial topology.

### Solution 2.6 — Electromagnetic duality and Bianchi identity

**(a)** In four dimensions, start from
$$
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}
=\partial_\mu\electric{A}_\nu-\partial_\nu\electric{A}_\mu.
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\mu\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}
&=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\\
&=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
\partial_\mu(\partial_\rho\electric{A}_\sigma-\partial_\sigma\electric{A}_\rho)
=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Symmetric pairs of partial derivatives contract to zero with the antisymmetric Levi-Civita tensor.

**(b)** With $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$,
$$
\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{0i}=-\magnetic{B}^i,
\qquad
\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{ij}=\epsilon^{ijk}\electric{E}_k.
$$
The temporal component of the Bianchi identity gives
$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}=0,
$$
so there are no magnetic monopoles in this formulation.
The spatial components give Faraday's law:
$$
\partial_t\magnetic{\mathbf B}
+\boldsymbol\nabla\times\electric{\mathbf E}=0.
$$

**(c)** Direct component calculation, with $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$, gives
$$
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}
=2\left(\lvert\magnetic{\mathbf B}\rvert^2-\lvert\electric{\mathbf E}\rvert^2\right),
$$
$$
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}
=-4\electric{\mathbf E}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}.
$$

**(d)** Under the discrete duality rotation
$$
\electric{\mathbf E}\longrightarrow\magnetic{\mathbf B},
\qquad
\magnetic{\mathbf B}\longrightarrow-\electric{\mathbf E},
$$
the source-free Maxwell equations map into one another.
For example,
$$
\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\electric{\mathbf E}=0,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\nabla\times\magnetic{\mathbf B}
=\partial_t\electric{\mathbf E}
$$
map to the corresponding magnetic-divergence and Faraday equations.

The invariant $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$ changes sign, so the usual single-potential Maxwell Lagrangian density
$\energy{\mathcal L}=-\tfrac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$
is not invariant under this direct substitution.
The source distinguishes symmetry of the field equations from invariance of this action and compares it with S-duality-type strong–weak dualities.

### Solution 2.7 — Lorentz algebra

**(a)** Start from
$$
[M^{\mu\nu},M^{\rho\sigma}]
=i\left(
g^{\nu\rho}M^{\mu\sigma}
-g^{\mu\rho}M^{\nu\sigma}
-g^{\nu\sigma}M^{\mu\rho}
+g^{\mu\sigma}M^{\nu\rho}
\right).
$$
Define
$$
J^i=\frac12\epsilon^{ijk}M_{jk},
\qquad
K^i=M^{0i}.
$$
For spatial indices, $g^{ij}=-\delta^{ij}$.
Evaluating
$$
[J^i,J^j]
=\left[
\frac12\epsilon^{iab}M_{ab},
\frac12\epsilon^{jcd}M_{cd}
\right]
$$
gives
$$
[J^i,J^j]=i\epsilon^{ijk}J^k.
$$
Likewise,
$$
[J^i,K^j]
=\left[\frac12\epsilon^{iab}M_{ab},M^{0j}\right]
=i\epsilon^{ijk}K^k,
$$
because $g^{0a}=0$ for spatial $a$.
With $g^{00}=+1$,
$$
[K^i,K^j]=[M^{0i},M^{0j}]
=-i\epsilon^{ijk}J^k.
$$
The negative sign reflects the noncompact Lorentz algebra.

**(b)** Define
$$
A^i=\frac12(J^i+iK^i),
\qquad
B^i=\frac12(J^i-iK^i).
$$
Using
$$
[K^i,J^j]=-[J^j,K^i]=i\epsilon^{ijk}K^k,
$$
one obtains
$$
[A^i,A^j]=i\epsilon^{ijk}A^k.
$$
Similarly,
$$
[B^i,B^j]=i\epsilon^{ijk}B^k,
\qquad
[A^i,B^j]=0.
$$

> [!note]
> The covariant commutator in this source solution agrees with the corrected sign in (B.85). The source describes the last result as two $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ factors. Precisely, the complexified Lorentz algebra splits into two $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb C)$ factors with these angular-momentum commutation relations; the real Lorentz algebra is noncompact.

**(c)** In the $(\tfrac12,0)$ representation,
$$
A^i=\frac12\sigma^i,
\qquad
B^i=0,
\qquad
K^i=-i(A^i-B^i)=-\frac{i}{2}\sigma^i.
$$
A finite boost of rapidity $\mechanical{\eta}$ along $z$, with the source's exponential convention, is
$$
\Lambda_L=e^{i\mechanical{\eta}K^3}
=e^{i\mechanical{\eta}(-i\sigma^3/2)}
=e^{+\mechanical{\eta}\sigma^3/2}.
$$

**(d)** In the $(0,\tfrac12)$ representation,
$$
A^i=0,
\qquad
B^i=\frac12\sigma^i,
\qquad
K^i=-i(A^i-B^i)=+\frac{i}{2}\sigma^i.
$$
Thus
$$
\Lambda_R=e^{i\mechanical{\eta}K^3}
=e^{-\mechanical{\eta}\sigma^3/2}.
$$
The two chiralities have opposite boost exponents.
A positive-energy massless particle keeps its helicity under proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations; it cannot be overtaken by a physical boost.
Parity exchanges the two chiralities, so a single Weyl representation is not by itself parity invariant.

### Solution 2.8 — Two-dimensional Thirring model

**(a)** The Lagrangian density
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}
-\frac{\signal{g}}2
(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi})
(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma_\mu\signal{\psi})
$$
is invariant under $\signal{\psi}\to e^{i\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi}$.
Both the scalar mass bilinear and the vector bilinear are invariant.
With the source's current-sign convention, the Noether current is
$$
\signal{j}_T^\mu=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
The subscript $T$ distinguishes this two-dimensional Thirring current from the earlier $O(N)$ current.

**(b)** In two dimensions, gamma matrices are $2\times2$.
One choice is
$$
\gamma^0=\sigma^1,
\qquad
\gamma^1=-i\sigma^3.
$$
The covariant bilinears are the scalar
$\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}$, the vector
$\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}$, and the pseudoscalar
$\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^5\signal{\psi}$.
The candidate quartic Lorentz scalars are
$$
(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi})^2,
\qquad
(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi})
(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma_\mu\signal{\psi}),
\qquad
(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^5\signal{\psi})^2.
$$
For one Grassmann Dirac field in two dimensions, Fierz identities relate the scalar and pseudoscalar squares to the current-current term.
There is one independent local quartic interaction of this type.

> [!note]
> The source's uniqueness statement uses a single fermion species and coincident local bilinears. Additional flavor indices or other field content permit additional invariant interaction channels.

**(c)** For $\mechanical{m}=0$, take
$$
\signal{\psi}\longrightarrow e^{i\signal{\alpha}\gamma^5}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
\gamma^5=\gamma^0\gamma^1.
$$
Because $\gamma^5$ anticommutes with $\gamma^0$, the adjoint transforms with the same phase sign:
$$
\bar{\signal{\psi}}\longrightarrow
\bar{\signal{\psi}}e^{i\signal{\alpha}\gamma^5}.
$$
The kinetic term is invariant, using $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^5\}=0$.
A mass bilinear would transform as
$$
\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}
\longrightarrow
\bar{\signal{\psi}}e^{2i\signal{\alpha}\gamma^5}\signal{\psi},
$$
so it would break this chiral symmetry.

Thus this chiral symmetry requires $\mechanical{m}=0$.
The current-current interaction is invariant, as is the vector current itself.
The classically conserved chiral current is
$$
\signal{j}_{T5}^{\mu}
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\signal{\psi}.
$$
In two dimensions, it is related to the vector current by
$$
\signal{j}_{T5}^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\signal{j}_{T\nu},
$$
when the two-dimensional Levi-Civita convention is chosen as $\epsilon^{01}=-1$.

> [!note]
> The source states this positive-sign duality relation without specifying the two-dimensional epsilon normalization. With $\gamma^5=\gamma^0\gamma^1$ and the mostly-minus metric, it requires $\epsilon^{01}=-1$. If $\epsilon^{01}=+1$ is used instead, the relation carries an overall minus sign.
