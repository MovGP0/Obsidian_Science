> [!TLDR]
> The action integrates a local function of fields and their first derivatives over Minkowski spacetime.

Relativistic field theory uses spacetime $\mathbb R^{1,3}$ with metric $g^{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$. Write $\mechanical{x}^{\mu}=(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x})$ and $\partial_{\mu}=\partial/\partial\mechanical{x}^{\mu}$.

## Definition 2.1 — Classical Field Theory

A classical field theory is specified by:

1. Fields $\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})$, where $a=1,\ldots,n$, which are functions or sections of fiber bundles on spacetime.
2. A Lagrangian density $\energy{\mathcal L}=\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi}^{a},\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})$, depending on fields and their first derivatives.
3. An action functional

$$
\energy{S}[\signal{\phi}^{a}]=\int d^{4}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x}),\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})).
$$

Dependence only on the fields and their first derivatives is the locality hypothesis used here. A Lagrangian with second field derivatives would generally produce higher-order equations of motion and possible instabilities, as in Ostrogradsky's theorem. Controlled exceptions include the Gauss–Bonnet term in gravitation. The first-derivative hypothesis is widely used for fundamental theories.

The action integral extends over all spacetime, or over a region $\Omega$ with suitable boundary conditions.

In natural units, $[d^{4}\mechanical{x}]=\energy{E}^{-4}$, so $[\energy{\mathcal L}]=\energy{E}^{4}$ and $[\energy{S}]=\energy{E}^{0}$; the action is dimensionless because $\energy{\hbar}=1$.

Two densities that differ by a total divergence, $\energy{\mathcal L}'=\energy{\mathcal L}+\partial_{\mu}K^{\mu}$, give the same action up to boundary terms and the same equations of motion. This freedom is important when the energy–momentum tensor is symmetrized.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{x}^{\mu},\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\mathbf x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinates | The temporal and spatial coordinates in natural units. |
| $\signal{\phi}^{a}$ | — | Fields | The field components; units depend on the field type. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Lagrangian density | The local density integrated over spacetime. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The action in units of the reduced Planck constant. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Energy scale | The reference energy used for dimensional counting. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action constant set to one in natural units. |
