title: "Lagrangian Density and Action Functional"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A classical field theory is specified by fields, a local Lagrangian density, and a spacetime action.

Why use a density rather than one Lagrangian for each field value? Locality lets the action collect contributions from small spacetime regions. The density specifies which nearby field variations can influence the dynamics. It is a choice of model; symmetry restricts that choice but does not fix every allowed interaction or coupling.

[[02.02.01 Basic Definitions]] distinguishes the density from its space and spacetime integrals. [[02.02.02 Lorentz Covariance]] then tests whether inertial observers describe the same action. In both steps, keep the integration region and its boundary conditions explicit: a total derivative can leave the bulk equations unchanged while changing a boundary contribution.
