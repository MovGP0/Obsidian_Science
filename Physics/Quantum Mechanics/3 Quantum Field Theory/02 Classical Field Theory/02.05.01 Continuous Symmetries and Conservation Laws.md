> [!TLDR]
> An infinitesimal continuous symmetry can transform both fields and coordinates while leaving the action invariant to first order.

## Definition 2.5 — Continuous Symmetry

A continuous action symmetry is a one-parameter transformation $\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})\to\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})+\varepsilon\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})$, possibly accompanied by $\mechanical{x}^{\mu}\to\mechanical{x}^{\mu}+\varepsilon\delta\mechanical{x}^{\mu}$, under which the action is invariant to first order in the parameter.

Consider a general infinitesimal transformation of coordinates and fields:

$$
\mechanical{x}^{\mu}\to\mechanical{x}'^{\mu}=\mechanical{x}^{\mu}+\varepsilon\delta\mechanical{x}^{\mu}(\mechanical{x}), \tag{2.21}
$$

$$
\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})\to\signal{\phi}'^{a}(\mechanical{x}')=\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})+\varepsilon\Delta\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x}). \tag{2.22}
$$

The total field variation is $\Delta\signal{\phi}^{a}$. The functional variation at a fixed point is

$$
\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})=\signal{\phi}'^{a}(\mechanical{x})-\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})
=\varepsilon[\Delta\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})-\delta\mechanical{x}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a}]. \tag{2.23}
$$

> [!note]
> The source also calls the total variation “on-shell.” This wording needs care: later in the derivation, on-shell means that the equations of motion hold.

Include the measure Jacobian, $d^{4}\mechanical{x}'=(1+\varepsilon\partial_{\mu}\delta\mechanical{x}^{\mu})d^{4}\mechanical{x}$. The action variation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{S}&=\int d^{4}\mechanical{x}'\,\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi}'^{a}(\mechanical{x}'),\partial'_{\mu}\signal{\phi}'^{a}(\mechanical{x}'))-\int d^{4}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x}),\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x}))\\
&=\int d^{4}\mechanical{x}\left[\varepsilon\partial_{\mu}(\energy{\mathcal L}\delta\mechanical{x}^{\mu})+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\phi}^{a}}\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}\partial_{\mu}(\delta\signal{\phi}^{a})\right].
\end{aligned} \tag{2.24}
$$

The functional variation already contains the small parameter. Integration by parts and use of the equations of motion give

$$
\delta\energy{S}=\varepsilon\int d^{4}\mechanical{x}\,\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}, \tag{2.25}
$$

with Noether current

$$
\boxed{J^{\mu}=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}\Delta\signal{\phi}^{a}-\left[\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}\partial_{\nu}\signal{\phi}^{a}-g^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}\right]\delta\mechanical{x}^{\nu}.} \tag{2.26}
$$

## Theorem 2.6 — Noether, 1918

If the action is invariant under (2.21)–(2.22), the current is conserved on solutions of the equations of motion:

$$
\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=0. \tag{2.27}
$$

The associated conserved quantity is $Q=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,J^{0}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{t})$, with $dQ/d\mechanical{t}=0$.

**Proof.** Invariance gives $\delta\energy{S}=0$ for any integration region, hence $\partial_{\mu}J^{\mu}=0$ pointwise on solutions. The integral is conserved because

$$
\frac{dQ}{d\mechanical{t}}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\partial_{\mechanical{t}}J^{0}
=-\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf J
=-\oint_{\partial V}\mathbf J\cdot d\mathbf A\longrightarrow0,
$$

provided the fields decrease sufficiently rapidly at spatial infinity.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}^{a}$ | — | Fields | The components transformed by the symmetry. |
| $\mechanical{x}^{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime position | The coordinates transformed with the fields. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Lagrangian density | The density transformed with the fields and measure. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The invariant functional. |
| $J^{\mu}$ | — | Noether current | The conserved current; units depend on the symmetry. |
| $Q$ | — | Conserved quantity | The integral of the current's temporal component. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time | The evolution coordinate of the conserved charge. |
