> [!TLDR]
> Real scalar, complex scalar, and electromagnetic fields illustrate the classical Lagrangian formalism.

Use these three examples to separate field components from physical degrees of freedom. A real scalar has one real component. A complex scalar has two real components and a continuous internal rotation. The four-potential has four components, but its constraints and gauge freedom leave two propagating vacuum polarizations.

For each example, reconstruct the equation of motion from the density, then compare its energy and symmetry charges. In [[02.07.01 The Real Scalar Klein–Gordon Field]], the free limit permits a mass-shell expansion. In [[02.07.02 The Complex Scalar Field]], the potential geometry explains the fluctuation masses. In [[02.07.03 The Electromagnetic Field]], the field strength identifies which changes of potential have no physical effect.
