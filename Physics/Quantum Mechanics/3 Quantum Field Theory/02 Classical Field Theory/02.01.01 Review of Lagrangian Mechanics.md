---
title: "Review of Lagrangian Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Stationary action gives the Euler–Lagrange equations for generalized coordinates.

Consider a mechanical system with $N$ degrees of freedom, $\mechanical{q}_i(\mechanical{t})$, where $i=1,\ldots,N$. Its Lagrangian is $\energy{L}(\mechanical{q}_i,\mechanical{\dot q}_i,\mechanical{t})$, and its action functional is

$$
\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}_i]=\int_{\mechanical{t}_1}^{\mechanical{t}_2}d\mechanical{t}\,\energy{L}(\mechanical{q}_i(\mechanical{t}),\mechanical{\dot q}_i(\mechanical{t}),\mechanical{t}). \tag{2.1}
$$

The principle of stationary action states that physical paths make the action stationary under variations $\delta\mechanical{q}_i(\mechanical{t})$ that vanish at both endpoints:

$$
\delta\energy{S}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{d}{d\mechanical{t}}\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{\dot q}_i}-\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{q}_i}=0,
\qquad i=1,\ldots,N. \tag{2.2}
$$

These are the Euler–Lagrange equations. The momentum conjugate to $\mechanical{q}_i$ is $\mechanical{p}_i=\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{\dot q}_i$.

A Legendre transformation gives the Hamiltonian:

$$
\energy{H}(\mechanical{q}_i,\mechanical{p}_i,\mechanical{t})=\sum_i\mechanical{p}_i\mechanical{\dot q}_i-\energy{L}. \tag{2.3}
$$

Hamilton's equations, $\mechanical{\dot q}_i=\partial\energy{H}/\partial\mechanical{p}_i$ and $\mechanical{\dot p}_i=-\partial\energy{H}/\partial\mechanical{q}_i$, are equivalent to the Euler–Lagrange equations.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{q}_i$ | — | Generalized coordinate | The coordinate for a mechanical degree of freedom. |
| $\mechanical{\dot q}_i$ | — | Generalized velocity | The time derivative of the generalized coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{p}_i$ | — | Conjugate momentum | The momentum conjugate to the selected coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{t}_1,\mechanical{t}_2$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time | The evolution coordinate and endpoints in natural units. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Lagrangian | The mechanical Lagrangian. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The action measured in units of the reduced Planck constant. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Hamiltonian | The mechanical energy obtained by a Legendre transformation. |
