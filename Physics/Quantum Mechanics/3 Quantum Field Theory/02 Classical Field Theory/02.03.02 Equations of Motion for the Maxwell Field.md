---
title: "Equations of Motion for the Maxwell Field"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Varying the Maxwell Lagrangian gives the vacuum Maxwell equations; the homogeneous equations follow from the field-strength definition.

For an Abelian gauge field $\electric{A}_{\mu}(\mechanical{x})$, the Maxwell Lagrangian density is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Max}}=-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}, \tag{2.12}
$$

with $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\electric{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\electric{A}_{\mu}$. Its derivative is

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\rho}\electric{A}_{\sigma})}=-\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}, \tag{2.13}
$$

while $\partial\energy{\mathcal L}/\partial\electric{A}_{\sigma}=0$. Thus the Euler–Lagrange equations give

$$
\partial_{\rho}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}=0. \tag{2.14}
$$

These are the vacuum Maxwell equations. The Bianchi identities, $\partial_{[\mu}\electric{F}_{\nu\rho]}=0$, follow automatically from the definition of the field strength in terms of the potential.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{A}_{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-potential | The Abelian electromagnetic potential in natural units. |
| $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Field strength | The electromagnetic antisymmetric tensor. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Lagrangian density | The Maxwell dynamical density. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime position | The field coordinate. |
