> [!TLDR]
> Classical field theory combines local actions, canonical dynamics, conserved symmetry currents, and Lorentz representations before quantization.

This chapter extends discrete Lagrangian mechanics to systems with infinitely many degrees of freedom. A Lagrangian density specifies the theory, and stationary action gives its equations of motion. The Hamiltonian formalism supplies conjugate momenta and a symplectic structure. Noether's theorem converts symmetries into conservation laws.

Real and complex scalar fields and the Maxwell field illustrate these principles and provide the starting point for canonical quantization.

Lorentz representations classify scalar, vector, and spinor fields. The classification has consequences for spin–statistics, scattering selection rules, and the structure of fundamental interactions.

> [!note]
> The source abbreviates the Lorentz-group structure as SU(2) × SU(2). Section 2.8.2 establishes a relation between complexified Lie algebras, rather than an identity of the real groups.

## Example 2.11 — Explicit Klein–Gordon Energy–Momentum Tensor

For $\energy{\mathcal L}=\tfrac12(\partial_{\rho}\signal{\phi})(\partial^{\rho}\signal{\phi})-\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})$, equation (2.35) gives

$$
\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})}\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi}-g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}
=(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})(\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi})-g^{\mu\nu}\left[\tfrac12(\partial_{\rho}\signal{\phi})^{2}-\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})\right].
$$

Using the equations of motion,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_{\mu}\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}&=(\square\signal{\phi})(\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi})+(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi}
-g^{\mu\nu}[(\partial^{\rho}\signal{\phi})\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\signal{\phi}-\energy{V}'(\signal{\phi})\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}]\\
&=(\square\signal{\phi})(\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi})+(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})\partial^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}-(\partial^{\rho}\signal{\phi})\partial^{\nu}\partial_{\rho}\signal{\phi}+\energy{V}'(\signal{\phi})\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi}\\
&=(\square\signal{\phi}+\energy{V}'(\signal{\phi}))(\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi})=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last step uses $\square\signal{\phi}+\energy{V}'(\signal{\phi})=0$.

> [!note]
> The source prints a minus sign on the potential-derivative term in the second line, although its first and final lines require a plus sign. The second-line sign is corrected here.

## Example 2.12 — Scalar Theory with O(N) Symmetry

For N real fields,

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=\frac12\sum_{a=1}^{N}(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})^{2}-\energy{V}(\signal{\phi}^{a}\signal{\phi}^{a}), \tag{2.69}
$$

where the potential depends only on $\signal{R}^{2}=\signal{\phi}^{a}\signal{\phi}^{a}$. The symmetry is $\signal{\phi}^{a}\to O^{a}{}_{b}\signal{\phi}^{b}$, with $O^{T}O=1$. For $O^{a}{}_{b}=\delta^{a}{}_{b}+\omega^{a}{}_{b}$ and $\omega_{ab}=-\omega_{ba}$, the variation is $\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}=\omega^{a}{}_{b}\signal{\phi}^{b}$. The $N(N-1)/2$ Noether currents are

$$
J^{\mu}_{ab}=(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})\signal{\phi}^{b}-(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{b})\signal{\phi}^{a}. \tag{2.70}
$$

Their charges generate $\mathfrak{so}(N)$, with $[Q_{ab},Q_{cd}]\propto(\delta_{ac}Q_{bd}-\cdots)$.

For $\energy{V}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{R}^{2}+\tfrac{\lambda}{4}\signal{R}^{4}$ and $\mechanical{m}^{2}<0$, the minima form the sphere $\signal{R}=\signal{v}=\sqrt{|\mechanical{m}^{2}|/\lambda}$. Spontaneous breaking from $O(N)$ to $O(N-1)$ produces $N-1$ Goldstone bosons.

## Example 2.13 — Nonlinear Sigma Lagrangian

The nonlinear sigma model is a scalar theory on a quotient space $G/H$. For $O(3)/O(2)\cong S^{2}$, the field $\signal{\mathbf n}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{t})$ lies on a unit two-sphere:

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=\frac{\energy{f}^{2}}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\mathbf n})^{2},\qquad|\signal{\mathbf n}|^{2}=1. \tag{2.71}
$$

The scale has mass dimension one; in QCD it is the pion decay constant. The unit-vector constraint makes the field nonlinear. Parameterize it as

$$
\signal{\mathbf n}=\left(\frac{\signal{\boldsymbol\pi}}{\energy{f}},\sqrt{1-\frac{\signal{\boldsymbol\pi}^{2}}{\energy{f}^{2}}}\right).
$$

Expansion in powers of the pion field divided by the scale gives interactions of arbitrary order, all determined by one parameter. This is the structure of effective chiral theory.
