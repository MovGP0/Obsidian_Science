---
title: "Noether's Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Continuous symmetries of the action determine conservation laws such as energy, momentum, and electric charge conservation.

Noether's theorem (1918) connects action symmetries with conservation laws. Energy conservation follows from time-translation invariance, electric-charge conservation from $U(1)$ phase invariance, and momentum conservation from spatial homogeneity.

The useful question is which transformation changes the description without changing the action. Start with [[02.05.01 Continuous Symmetries and Conservation Laws]] to distinguish the change at a fixed point from the change caused by moving that point. The phase example then isolates an internal symmetry; translations and rotations add spacetime transport.

A current is locally conserved on solutions, but its integral is constant only for a region with no net outward flux. Also distinguish a global symmetry with a constant parameter from gauge freedom with a point-dependent parameter. The charge interpretation in this sequence concerns global transformations.
