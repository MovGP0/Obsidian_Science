> [!TLDR]
> The electromagnetic field is described by a four-potential and its antisymmetric derivative tensor.

## The Maxwell Lagrangian

The electromagnetic four-potential is $\electric{A}^{\mu}=(\electric{A}^{0},\magnetic{\mathbf A})$, where $\electric{A}^{0}=\electric{\phi}$ is the electric scalar potential and $\magnetic{\mathbf A}$ is the vector potential. The field tensor is

$$
\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\electric{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\electric{A}_{\mu}. \tag{2.52}
$$

The tensor is antisymmetric: $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}=-\electric{F}_{\nu\mu}$. Its components are $\electric{F}_{0i}=\electric{E}_i$ and $\electric{F}_{ij}=-\varepsilon_{ijk}\magnetic{B}^{k}$. The Maxwell Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Max}}=-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=\frac12(\electric{\mathbf E}^{2}-\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{2}). \tag{2.53}
$$

The Euler–Lagrange equations give $\partial_{\mu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=0$ in vacuum. Adding the source coupling $-\electric{J}^{\mu}\electric{A}_{\mu}$ gives $\partial_{\mu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=\electric{J}^{\nu}$. The Bianchi equations $\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}=0$ are identities, with dual tensor $\widetilde{\electric{F}}^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}$.

## Gauge Invariance

The Lagrangian is invariant under

$$
\electric{A}_{\mu}(\mechanical{x})\to\electric{A}_{\mu}(\mechanical{x})+\partial_{\mu}\alpha(\mechanical{x}) \tag{2.54}
$$

for any scalar gauge function. The field strength is unchanged because partial derivatives commute.

Gauge invariance organizes the theory and removes unphysical components: photons have two transverse physical polarizations, although the four-potential has four components. Fig. 2.3 illustrates this. A gauge condition makes this reduction explicit. Common choices include

$$
\partial^{\mu}\electric{A}_{\mu}=0\qquad\text{(Lorenz gauge)}, \tag{2.55}
$$

$$
\electric{A}^{0}=0\qquad\text{(temporal gauge)}. \tag{2.56}
$$

A third choice is

$$
\nabla\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf A}=0\qquad\text{(Coulomb gauge)}. \tag{2.57}
$$

All gauges describe the same physics, although their mathematical forms can differ greatly.

## Coupling to Matter

Minimal substitution couples electromagnetism to a charged complex scalar field:

$$
\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}\to D_{\mu}\signal{\phi}\equiv(\partial_{\mu}-i\electric{e}\electric{A}_{\mu})\signal{\phi}. \tag{2.58}
$$

The resulting scalar-electrodynamics Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{SED}}=(D_{\mu}\signal{\phi})^{*}(D^{\mu}\signal{\phi})-\mechanical{m}^{2}|\signal{\phi}|^{2}-\lambda|\signal{\phi}|^{4}-\frac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}. \tag{2.59}
$$

The source prints the local $U(1)$ transformation as

$$
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\to e^{i\electric{e}\alpha(\mechanical{x})}\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}),\qquad
\electric{A}_{\mu}(\mechanical{x})\to\electric{A}_{\mu}(\mechanical{x})+\frac{1}{\electric{e}}\partial_{\mu}\alpha(\mechanical{x}). \tag{2.60}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (2.60) retains the source's phase and potential factors. With the covariant derivative in (2.58), their gauge-parameter normalizations are inconsistent: the displayed phase pairs with a potential shift without the inverse-charge factor.

The electromagnetic source current is $\electric{J}^{\mu}=-i\electric{e}[\signal{\phi}^{*}D^{\mu}\signal{\phi}-\signal{\phi}(D^{\mu}\signal{\phi})^{*}]$. Varying the potential gives $\partial_{\mu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}=\electric{J}^{\nu}$, the Maxwell equations with a source.
