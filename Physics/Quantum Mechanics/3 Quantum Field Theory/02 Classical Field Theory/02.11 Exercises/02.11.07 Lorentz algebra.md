## Exercise 2.7 — Representations of the Lorentz Group

Verify the Lorentz-algebra structure.

**(a)** Derive (2.63)–(2.65) for rotations and boosts from (2.62).

**(b)** Define $A^{i}=\tfrac12(J^{i}+iK^{i})$ and $B^{i}=\tfrac12(J^{i}-iK^{i})$. Show that $[A^{i},A^{j}]=i\varepsilon^{ijk}A^{k}$, $[B^{i},B^{j}]=i\varepsilon^{ijk}B^{k}$, and $[A^{i},B^{j}]=0$.

**(c)** In representation $(\tfrac12,0)$, write the generator matrices $A^{i}=\tfrac12\sigma^{i}$ and $B^{i}=0$ explicitly. Calculate a z-directed boost with rapidity $\mechanical{\eta}$:

$$
\exp(i\mechanical{\eta}K^{3})=\exp(\mechanical{\eta}A^{3}-\mechanical{\eta}B^{3})=\exp\left(\frac{\mechanical{\eta}}{2}\sigma^{3}\right).
$$

**(d)** Show that the right-handed Weyl spinor $(0,\tfrac12)$ transforms with $\exp(-\mechanical{\eta}\sigma^{3}/2)$. Compare it with the left-handed case and discuss chirality.

## Solution 2.7 — Lorentz algebra

**(a)** Start from
$$
[M^{\mu\nu},M^{\rho\sigma}]
=i\left(
g^{\nu\rho}M^{\mu\sigma}
-g^{\mu\rho}M^{\nu\sigma}
-g^{\nu\sigma}M^{\mu\rho}
+g^{\mu\sigma}M^{\nu\rho}
\right).
$$
Define
$$
J^i=\frac12\epsilon^{ijk}M_{jk},
\qquad
K^i=M^{0i}.
$$
For spatial indices, $g^{ij}=-\delta^{ij}$.
Evaluating
$$
[J^i,J^j]
=\left[
\frac12\epsilon^{iab}M_{ab},
\frac12\epsilon^{jcd}M_{cd}
\right]
$$
gives
$$
[J^i,J^j]=i\epsilon^{ijk}J^k.
$$
Likewise,
$$
[J^i,K^j]
=\left[\frac12\epsilon^{iab}M_{ab},M^{0j}\right]
=i\epsilon^{ijk}K^k,
$$
because $g^{0a}=0$ for spatial $a$.
With $g^{00}=+1$,
$$
[K^i,K^j]=[M^{0i},M^{0j}]
=-i\epsilon^{ijk}J^k.
$$
The negative sign reflects the noncompact Lorentz algebra.

**(b)** Define
$$
A^i=\frac12(J^i+iK^i),
\qquad
B^i=\frac12(J^i-iK^i).
$$
Using
$$
[K^i,J^j]=-[J^j,K^i]=i\epsilon^{ijk}K^k,
$$
one obtains
$$
[A^i,A^j]=i\epsilon^{ijk}A^k.
$$
Similarly,
$$
[B^i,B^j]=i\epsilon^{ijk}B^k,
\qquad
[A^i,B^j]=0.
$$

> [!note]
> The covariant commutator in this source solution agrees with the corrected sign in (B.85). The source describes the last result as two $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ factors. Precisely, the complexified Lorentz algebra splits into two $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb C)$ factors with these angular-momentum commutation relations; the real Lorentz algebra is noncompact.

**(c)** In the $(\tfrac12,0)$ representation,
$$
A^i=\frac12\sigma^i,
\qquad
B^i=0,
\qquad
K^i=-i(A^i-B^i)=-\frac{i}{2}\sigma^i.
$$
A finite boost of rapidity $\mechanical{\eta}$ along $z$, with the source's exponential convention, is
$$
\Lambda_L=e^{i\mechanical{\eta}K^3}
=e^{i\mechanical{\eta}(-i\sigma^3/2)}
=e^{+\mechanical{\eta}\sigma^3/2}.
$$

**(d)** In the $(0,\tfrac12)$ representation,
$$
A^i=0,
\qquad
B^i=\frac12\sigma^i,
\qquad
K^i=-i(A^i-B^i)=+\frac{i}{2}\sigma^i.
$$
Thus
$$
\Lambda_R=e^{i\mechanical{\eta}K^3}
=e^{-\mechanical{\eta}\sigma^3/2}.
$$
The two chiralities have opposite boost exponents.
A positive-energy massless particle keeps its helicity under proper orthochronous Lorentz transformations; it cannot be overtaken by a physical boost.
Parity exchanges the two chiralities, so a single Weyl representation is not by itself parity invariant.

For the displayed diagonal boost generator, exponentiation simply gives two real factors with opposite rapidity exponents. At zero rapidity both factors are one, and reversing rapidity gives the inverse boost. For nonzero real rapidity the matrix is not unitary in the ordinary two-component Euclidean norm. This is allowed for field components and does not contradict unitary Lorentz action on physical quantum states. The explicit nonunitary boost is also discussed in [Tong, section 4.1](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/four.pdf#page=6).

The opposite exponent in the other Weyl representation is the useful chirality distinction here. Helicity is a statement about spin relative to momentum; identifying it with chirality requires the massless setting and a specified particle or antiparticle sector. The algebra calculation alone does not establish that identification for a massive fermion.
