---
title: "Conjugate Momentum Density and the Hamiltonian"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The field Hamiltonian is the spatial integral of a density obtained by replacing field time derivatives with conjugate momenta.

The transition from the Lagrangian to the Hamiltonian formalism uses a Legendre transformation for fields, as it does in mechanics.

The conjugate momentum of a discrete coordinate is replaced by a conjugate momentum density:

$$
\mechanical{\pi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})\equiv\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\dot\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t})}, \tag{2.15}
$$

where $\signal{\dot\phi}^{a}=\partial_{\mechanical{t}}\signal{\phi}^{a}$. The Hamiltonian density is

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}(\signal{\phi}^{a},\mechanical{\pi}^{a},\nabla\signal{\phi}^{a})=\mechanical{\pi}^{a}\signal{\dot\phi}^{a}-\energy{\mathcal L}. \tag{2.16}
$$

Express the time derivatives in terms of fields and conjugate momenta. The total Hamiltonian is $\energy{H}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{\mathcal H}$.

This construction selects time rather than space: it transforms the time derivatives but not the spatial gradients. Lorentz covariance is no longer manifest, although the physics remains relativistic. The Hamiltonian structure is useful for canonical quantization, which imposes equal-time commutation relations.

## Example 2.4 — Real Scalar Field in Hamiltonian Form

For $\energy{\mathcal L}=\tfrac12\signal{\dot\phi}^{2}-\tfrac12|\nabla\signal{\phi}|^{2}-\tfrac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}$, the momentum is $\mechanical{\pi}=\signal{\dot\phi}$. Thus

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=\mechanical{\pi}\signal{\dot\phi}-\energy{\mathcal L}
=\frac12\mechanical{\pi}^{2}+\frac12|\nabla\signal{\phi}|^{2}+\frac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}. \tag{2.17}
$$

The terms are the kinetic, gradient or elastic, and mass energy densities. All are nonnegative. The Hamiltonian is bounded below, as required for a stable system.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}^{a}$ | — | Field components | The canonical configuration variables. |
| $\mechanical{\pi}^{a}$ | — | Conjugate momentum densities | The canonical momentum variables; units depend on field type. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{x},\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Coordinates | The space and time coordinates in natural units. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L},\energy{\mathcal H}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Dynamical densities | The Lagrangian and Hamiltonian densities. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Hamiltonian | The total field energy. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Real scalar field | The canonically normalized field in Example 2.4. |
| $\signal{\dot\phi},\mechanical{\pi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{2}}$ | Scalar canonical rate and momentum | The equal field time derivative and momentum density. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The scalar-particle mass. |
