title: "Systematic Derivation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Varying the fields with fixed boundary values gives the Euler–Lagrange field equations.

Vary the fields as $\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})\to\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})+\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})$, with variations that vanish on the boundary of $\Omega$ or at spacetime infinity. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{S}&=\int_{\Omega}d^{4}\mechanical{x}\,\delta\energy{\mathcal L}\\
&=\int_{\Omega}d^{4}\mechanical{x}\left[\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\phi}^{a}}\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}\delta(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})\right].
\end{aligned} \tag{2.7}
$$

Since $\delta(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})=\partial_{\mu}(\delta\signal{\phi}^{a})$, integrate the second term by parts:

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}\partial_{\mu}(\delta\signal{\phi}^{a})
=\partial_{\mu}\left[\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}\right]
-\left[\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}\right]\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}. \tag{2.8}
$$

Stokes' theorem converts the total divergence into a boundary integral over $\partial\Omega$. It vanishes because $\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}|_{\partial\Omega}=0$. Thus

$$
\delta\energy{S}=\int_{\Omega}d^{4}\mechanical{x}\left[\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\phi}^{a}}-\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}\right]\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}. \tag{2.9}
$$

For the action variation to vanish for every allowed field variation, the coefficient must vanish at each point:

$$
\boxed{\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a})}-\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\phi}^{a}}=0,\qquad a=1,\ldots,n.} \tag{2.10}
$$

These are the Euler–Lagrange field equations. When fields depend only on time, they reduce to the ordinary equations for finitely many degrees of freedom.

## Example 2.2 — Real Scalar Field

For $\energy{\mathcal L}=\tfrac12(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})^{2}-\tfrac12\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}$,

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\phi}}=-\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi},\qquad
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})}=\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}.
$$

The field equation becomes $\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}+\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}=0$, or

$$
(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\phi}=0. \tag{2.11}
$$

Here $\square=\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}=\partial_{\mechanical{t}}^{2}-\nabla^{2}$ is the d'Alembert operator. This is the Klein–Gordon equation for a scalar field of mass $\mechanical{m}$.

## Example 2.3 — Complex Scalar Field

Treat a complex field $\signal{\phi}$ and its conjugate $\signal{\phi}^{*}$ as independent variables. For

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{*})(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})-\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{*}\signal{\phi},
$$

variation with respect to the conjugate and the field gives, respectively,

$$
(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\phi}=0,\qquad(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{\phi}^{*}=0.
$$

Both fields satisfy the Klein–Gordon equation. The complex conjugate of a propagating wave is again a propagating wave.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}^{a}$ | — | Field components | The fields varied at fixed boundary values. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime position | The integration coordinate. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The functional whose first variation vanishes. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Lagrangian density | The density varied in the action. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The canonically normalized real or complex field in the examples. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The scalar-particle mass parameter. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Time | The temporal coordinate in the d'Alembert operator. |
