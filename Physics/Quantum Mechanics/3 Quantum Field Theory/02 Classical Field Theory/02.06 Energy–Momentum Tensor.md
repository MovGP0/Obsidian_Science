---
title: "Energy–Momentum Tensor"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The canonical energy–momentum tensor can be improved to remove symmetry and gauge-dependence problems.

A conservation equation does not uniquely determine a local energy or momentum density. Adding the divergence of an antisymmetric tensor preserves the local divergence, but its effect on an integrated charge depends on the boundary. This freedom allows a useful change in local description without changing the total four-momentum of an isolated field.

First use [[02.06.01 Limits of the Canonical Tensor]] to identify the gauge and symmetry problems. Then compare the scalar and electromagnetic examples in [[02.06.02 The Belinfante–Rosenfeld Tensor]]. The scalar already has a symmetric canonical tensor; Maxwell theory makes the improvement physically useful by expressing energy and flux through field strengths.
