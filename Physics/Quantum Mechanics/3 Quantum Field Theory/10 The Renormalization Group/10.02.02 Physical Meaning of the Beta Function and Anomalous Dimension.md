---
title: "Physical Meaning of the Beta Function and Anomalous Dimension"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The beta function describes coupling evolution with scale. The anomalous dimension changes the scaling of fields and correlation functions.

The beta function $\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})$ describes how the effective coupling changes when the energy scale used to study a system changes.

- If $\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})>0$, the coupling grows as $\energy{\mu}$ increases toward the ultraviolet.
- If $\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})<0$, the coupling decreases. The source connects this behavior with asymptotic freedom.

The anomalous dimension $\signal{\gamma}(\signal{g})$ changes the field scaling dimension from its classical value.
In four dimensions, a scalar field $\signal{\phi}$ has canonical mass dimension $[\signal{\phi}]=1$.
At the quantum level, its scaling dimension is written
$$
d_\phi=1+\signal{\gamma}(\signal{g}).
$$
This changes the scaling laws of correlation functions.
The change has measurable physical consequences, especially near critical phenomena.

> [!note]
> A negative beta function indicates decreasing coupling over the scale interval in question. Asymptotic freedom specifically requires the coupling to approach zero in the ultraviolet. A definite scaling dimension of the form $d_\phi=1+\signal{\gamma}$ applies at a fixed point; away from a fixed point, the running anomalous dimension enters the scale evolution.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Effective coupling | The coupling whose evolution is described by the beta function. |
| $\signal{\beta}$ | $1$ | Beta function | The change of coupling with logarithmic energy scale. |
| $\signal{\gamma}$ | $1$ | Field anomalous dimension | The quantum contribution to field scaling. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | A scalar field with canonical mass dimension one in four dimensions. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The scale used to compare effective couplings. |
