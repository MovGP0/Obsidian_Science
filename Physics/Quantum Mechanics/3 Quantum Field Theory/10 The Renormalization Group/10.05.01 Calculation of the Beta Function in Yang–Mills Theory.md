---
title: "Calculation of the Beta Function in Yang–Mills Theory"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Gauge and ghost loops compete with fermion loops in the Yang–Mills beta function.

Consider Yang–Mills theory with gauge group $G=SU(N_c)$, coupling $\signal{g}$, and $N_f$ fermion species in the fundamental representation.
The one-loop beta function has three sources of contributions.

1. **Gluon and ghost loops.** The gauge fields and Faddeev–Popov ghosts give a negative contribution,
   $$
   \Delta_{\mathrm{gluon+ghost}}=-\frac{11}{3}C_A.
   $$
   This contribution occurs in non-Abelian theories through gluon self-interaction. The adjoint quadratic Casimir is $C_A=N_c$.

2. **Fermion loops.** The fermion species give a positive contribution,
   $$
   \Delta_{\mathrm{fermions}}=+\frac43T_FN_f,
   $$
   where $T_F=1/2$ for the fundamental representation.
3. **Scalar loops, when present.** Complex scalars in the fundamental representation contribute $+\frac13T_FN_s$.

For the gauge-plus-fermion theory, the one-loop beta function is
$$
\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})
=-\frac{\signal{g}^3}{16\pi^2}
\left(\frac{11}{3}N_c-\frac43T_FN_f\right)
+\mathcal{O}(\signal{g}^5).
$$
In terms of the coefficient $b_0$,
$$
\boxed{
\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})
=-\frac{b_0\signal{g}^3}{16\pi^2}
+\mathcal{O}(\signal{g}^5),
\qquad
b_0=\frac{11}{3}N_c-\frac23N_f.
}
$$
Here $N_f$ counts Dirac fermion species. The boxed expression uses $T_F=1/2$ and contains no scalar contribution.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Yang–Mills coupling | The dimensionless non-Abelian gauge coupling in four dimensions. |
| $\signal{\beta}$ | $1$ | Yang–Mills beta function | The logarithmic scale derivative of the gauge coupling. |
