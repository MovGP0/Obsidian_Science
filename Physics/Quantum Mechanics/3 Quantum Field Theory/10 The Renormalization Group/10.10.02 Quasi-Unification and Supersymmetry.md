---
title: "Quasi-Unification and Supersymmetry"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Standard Model couplings approach one another at high energy without meeting exactly. Supersymmetric particle content can improve this theoretical unification pattern.

Evolving the three Standard Model couplings to high energy raises the question of whether they converge to one common value.
This is the **force-unification** program.

At one loop,
$$
\signal{\alpha_i}^{-1}(\energy{\mu})
=\signal{\alpha_i}^{-1}(\mechanical{M_Z})
-\frac{b_i}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{M_Z}}.
$$
The source states that the three Standard Model couplings approach one another near
$\energy{\mu}\sim10^{15}\,\unit{GeV}$ but do not meet exactly.
Figure 10.5 illustrates this behavior.

In the **minimal supersymmetric Standard Model (MSSM)**, superpartners change the coefficients $b_i$.
The source states that the couplings then meet nearly exactly at
$$
\energy{\mu_{\mathrm{GUT}}}\approx2\times10^{16}\,\unit{GeV}.
$$
It presents this quasi-unification as evidence in favor of a theory that unifies the gauge interactions above the Standard Model.
If the forces unify at $\energy{\mu_{\mathrm{GUT}}}$, there is one gauge coupling at that scale.
RG evolution then accounts for the different low-energy coupling values measured near $\mechanical{M_Z}$.

> [!note]
> This comparison is a model-dependent extrapolation. The supersymmetric result assumes suitable particle content, superpartner thresholds, normalization, and matching conditions. The numerical meeting scale is the source's theoretical estimate.
