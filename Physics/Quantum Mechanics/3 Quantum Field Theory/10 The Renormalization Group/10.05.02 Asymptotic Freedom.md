title: "Asymptotic Freedom"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A positive one-loop coefficient b₀ makes the non-Abelian coupling decrease toward zero at high energy.

Gross, Wilczek, and Politzer discovered asymptotic freedom in 1973 and received the 2004 Nobel Prize for this work.
The coefficient $b_0$ can be positive, which makes
$\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})<0$.
For $SU(N_c)$ with fundamental fermions and no scalar contribution, this requires
$$
N_f<\frac{11}{2}N_c.
$$
For QCD with $SU(3)$ and six quark species, $N_f=6$:
$$
b_0=\frac{11}{3}\cdot3-\frac23\cdot6=11-4=7>0.
$$
Thus the QCD coupling decreases as energy increases.
This behavior is called **asymptotic freedom**:
as $\energy{\mu}\to\infty$, $\signal{g}(\energy{\mu})\to0$,
and quarks and gluons become asymptotically free.

For $SU(3)$, the condition is
$$
N_f<\frac{11}{2}\cdot3=16.5.
$$
The six observed quark species $(u,d,s,c,b,t)$ satisfy it.

The conclusion follows locally from the weak-coupling beta function: for positive b₀, increasing energy reduces a small positive coupling. Its integrated solution approaches zero only at infinite energy, not at a finite ultraviolet scale. This is consistent with a free ultraviolet fixed point whose linear slope is zero.

The six-flavor example applies above the relevant quark thresholds. At lower scales the active flavor number changes, so the same coefficient must not be used across all scales without matching. Asymptotic freedom makes high-energy expansions useful; it does not by itself prove low-energy confinement.

## Example 10.4: Historical significance

Before 1973, Bjorken scaling observed in deep-inelastic scattering at SLAC in the 1960s and 1970s showed that quarks inside protons behave approximately as free particles at short distances, or large momentum transfers.
The theoretical question was why strong interactions, which are intense at low energies, weaken at short distances.

The asymptotic-freedom result of Gross, Wilczek, and Politzer gives the answer.
QCD with its $SU(3)$ gauge group and a small number of fermion species is asymptotically free.
Its strong coupling $\signal{g_s}$, or equivalently
$\signal{\alpha_s}=\signal{g_s}^2/(4\pi)$, decreases at high energies.
At large momentum transfer, quarks therefore appear approximately free.

Bjorken scaling is an approximate law with logarithmic corrections.
These scale violations can be calculated from the evolution of
$\signal{\alpha_s}$ and are encoded in the **DGLAP equations**.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Non-Abelian gauge coupling | The coupling that tends to zero at high energy in an asymptotically free theory. |
| $\signal{\beta}$ | $1$ | Gauge beta function | The negative logarithmic derivative of the coupling in the stated regime. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The increasing energy scale in the ultraviolet limit. |
| $\signal{g_s},\signal{\alpha_s}$ | $1$ | Strong gauge coupling and strong fine-structure coupling | The equivalent QCD coupling conventions used in the historical explanation. |
