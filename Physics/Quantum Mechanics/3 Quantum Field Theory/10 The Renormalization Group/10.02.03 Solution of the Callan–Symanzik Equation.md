---
title: "Solution of the Callan–Symanzik Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The method of characteristics solves the Callan–Symanzik equation through running couplings, running masses, and a field-normalization factor.

The Callan–Symanzik equation is a first-order partial differential equation.
Solve it by the **method of characteristics**.
Introduce the running coupling $\bar{\signal{g}}(t)$, where
$t=\ln(\energy{\mu}'/\energy{\mu})$, through
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\signal{g}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
=\signal{\beta}(\bar{\signal{g}}),
\qquad\bar{\signal{g}}(0)=\signal{g}.
\tag{10.2}
$$
Similarly, define the running mass $\bar{\mechanical{m}}(t)$:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\bar{\mechanical{m}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
=-\signal{\gamma_m}(\bar{\signal{g}}),
\qquad\bar{\mechanical{m}}(0)=\mechanical{m}.
$$
The source gives the $n$-point Green-function solution as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G}^{(n)}(\mechanical{p_i};\signal{g},\mechanical{m},\energy{\mu})
={}&\exp\left[-n\int_0^t\mathrm{d}t'\,
\signal{\gamma}(\bar{\signal{g}}(t'))\right]\\
&\times\signal{G}^{(n)}
(\mechanical{p_i};\bar{\signal{g}}(t),\bar{\mechanical{m}}(t),
\energy{\mu}e^t).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10.3}
$$
One can evaluate the Green function at an arbitrary scale
$\energy{\mu}'=\energy{\mu}e^t$ if the running parameters at that scale and the corresponding field-renormalization factor are used.

> [!note]
> With the $+n\signal{\gamma}$ convention in (10.1), differentiation along the characteristics gives $\mathrm{d}\signal{G}^{(n)}(t)/\mathrm{d}t=-n\signal{\gamma}\signal{G}^{(n)}(t)$. Therefore, when the initial Green function is on the left and the evolved one on the right as in (10.3), the exponential must have a positive sign. The printed negative sign would apply to the opposite direction of the relation.

The characteristics follow a change of subtraction scale with external momenta fixed. They are not particle trajectories. Once the running parameters are known, the partial differential equation becomes an ordinary differential equation for field normalization along this path.

Check the initial condition at t = 0: the integral vanishes and both parameter values return to their reference values. A stronger check is to differentiate along the path; this tests the exponential sign and exposes the source inconsistency recorded above. If the beta and anomalous-dimension functions are truncated, the solution resums the logarithms fixed by those functions. It does not supply the unknown finite loop terms, nor does it remain controlled when the evolved coupling becomes strong. The massless example also assumes that infrared singularities do not invalidate the chosen correlation function or kinematics.

## Example 10.2: Scaling correlation functions at small masses

In the massless limit $\mechanical{m}\to0$, the source applies dimensional analysis to scaled momenta $\mechanical{p_i}\to s\mechanical{p_i}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G}^{(n)}(s\mechanical{p_i};\signal{g},0,\energy{\mu})
={}&s^{4-n}\exp\left[
-n\int_0^{\ln s}\mathrm{d}t'\,
\signal{\gamma}(\bar{\signal{g}}(t'))\right]\\
&\times\signal{G}^{(n)}
(\mechanical{p_i};\bar{\signal{g}}(\ln s),0,\energy{\mu}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The factor $s^{4-n}$ is attributed to classical dimensional analysis.
The anomalous dimension modifies the classical power law.
The running coupling in the integral is determined by the beta function.

> [!note]
> The power $4-n$ is appropriate to an amputated scalar $n$-point vertex in four dimensions, rather than the unamputated Green function introduced earlier. For example, a free two-point propagator scales as momentum to the power $-2$, while $4-n$ gives $+2$ for $n=2$. The displayed example must therefore distinguish a vertex function from an unamputated correlation function.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g},\bar{\signal{g}}$ | $1$ | Reference and running couplings | The initial coupling and its value along the characteristic flow. |
| $\mechanical{m},\bar{\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Reference and running masses | The initial mass and the mass evolved along the flow. |
| $\signal{\beta},\signal{\gamma_m},\signal{\gamma}$ | $1$ | Renormalization-group functions | The coupling beta function and mass and field anomalous dimensions. |
| $\energy{\mu},\energy{\mu}'$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Reference and evaluation scales | The scales related by the characteristic parameter t. |
| $\mechanical{p_i}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | External momenta | The fixed or rescaled momenta in the Green functions. |
| $\signal{G}^{(n)}$ | — | n-point function | The source uses this symbol for functions whose dimension depends on representation and amputation. |
