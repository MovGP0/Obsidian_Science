---
title: "The Gaussian Fixed Point and the Wilson–Fisher Point"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Gaussian fixed point is the free theory. In scalar φ⁴ theory below four dimensions, the Wilson–Fisher fixed point is interacting.

The simplest fixed point is $\signal{g^*}=0$, called the **Gaussian fixed point**, or free theory.
In four-dimensional $\signal{\phi}^4$ theory,
$$
\signal{\beta}(\signal{\lambda})
=\frac{3\signal{\lambda}^2}{16\pi^2}+\cdots>0.
$$
The positive sign makes $\signal{\lambda}(\energy{\mu})$ decrease toward the infrared and increase toward the ultraviolet.
Thus $\signal{\lambda}=0$ is infrared stable and ultraviolet unstable on the positive-coupling side.
Four-dimensional $\signal{\phi}^4$ theory is infrared free, rather than ultraviolet free.

In $d=4-\epsilon$, the **Wilson–Fisher fixed point** is
$$
\signal{\lambda^*}
=\frac{16\pi^2\epsilon}{3}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2).
$$
This interacting fixed point describes critical phenomena in the Ising universality class.
As $d\to4$, it moves to $\signal{\lambda^*}=0$ and the free theory is recovered.

> [!note]
> Here the source uses $\epsilon=4-d$. This differs by a factor of two from the earlier regularization convention $d=4-2\epsilon$. The Wilson–Fisher statement concerns the critical theory with the mass deformation tuned to criticality.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g^*}$ | $1$ | Gaussian fixed-point coupling | The zero-coupling value of the free theory. |
| $\signal{\lambda},\signal{\lambda^*}$ | $1$ | Quartic coupling and its fixed-point value | The dimensionless scalar coupling and Wilson–Fisher value. |
| $\signal{\beta}$ | $1$ | Scalar beta function | The scale derivative that determines local flow near the fixed points. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | — | Scalar field | Its canonical mass dimension depends on d; it is one in four dimensions. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The scale along the scalar coupling flow. |
