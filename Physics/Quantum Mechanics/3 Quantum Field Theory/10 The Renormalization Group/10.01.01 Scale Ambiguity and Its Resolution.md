---
title: "Scale Ambiguity and Its Resolution"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The renormalization scale selects how finite loop contributions are absorbed into renormalized parameters. Changing it generates a flow of those parameters.

Consider a theory with coupling $\signal{g}$ and mass $\mechanical{m}$.
At one loop, renormalization typically gives integrals of the form
$$
I=\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{k_E}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac1{(\mechanical{k_E}^2+\mechanical{m}^2)^2}
=\frac1{16\pi^2}\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right),
$$
where $\energy{\Lambda}$ is an ultraviolet cutoff.
In dimensional regularization, with $d=4-2\epsilon$, the source writes the same integral as
$$
I=\frac{\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}}{16\pi^2}
\left(\frac1\epsilon-\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{\energy{\mu}^2}
+\text{finite terms}\right).
$$
The mass scale $\energy{\mu}$ keeps $\signal{g}$ dimensionless.
Freedom to choose $\energy{\mu}$ corresponds to freedom to absorb different finite contributions into the renormalized parameters.

The same measured input must be used before and after this change. If the subtraction scale changes but the numerical renormalized coupling is kept fixed, the calculation generally describes a different bare theory. Running the parameters prevents that change of theory.

In four dimensions, the integration measure and the squared denominator each have mass dimension four, so the loop integral is dimensionless. This explains why the finite result can contain a logarithm of a mass ratio. A cutoff and dimensional regularization organize the divergence differently; agreement of physical predictions also requires the same renormalization conditions, including finite terms.

Changing $\energy{\mu}$ generates a flow in renormalized parameter space.
The [[10.02 The Callan–Symanzik Equation|Callan–Symanzik equation]] describes this flow.

> [!note]
> The cutoff expression uses an infinite upper limit while referring to a finite cutoff. Its logarithm represents the large-cutoff leading term, rather than the exact integral as written. In the dimensional expression, the factor $\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}$ must be combined with the mass power from the $d$-dimensional integral before expansion. After this combination, the dimensionless result contains the pole and the logarithm of $\mechanical{m}^2/\energy{\mu}^2$, without an extra dimensionful scale power left over.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Renormalized coupling | The dimensionless coupling maintained by the dimensional scale factor. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass parameter | The mass in the typical loop integral. |
| $\mechanical{k_E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Euclidean loop momentum | The integration momentum after Euclidean continuation. |
| $\energy{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Ultraviolet cutoff | The high-momentum scale in cutoff regularization. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The arbitrary mass scale in dimensional regularization. |
