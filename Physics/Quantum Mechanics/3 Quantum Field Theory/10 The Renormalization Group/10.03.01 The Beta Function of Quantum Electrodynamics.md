---
title: "The Beta Function of Quantum Electrodynamics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The QED beta function is positive. At one loop, its charge-renormalization contribution comes from fermion vacuum polarization.

The fundamental QED coupling is the electric charge $\electric{e}$, or equivalently the fine-structure constant
$\electric{\alpha}=\electric{e}^2/(4\pi)$.
At first order in $\electric{\alpha}$, compute the beta function from vacuum polarization.
Through the Ward identity, this is the divergent one-loop contribution that determines charge renormalization.

For $-\mechanical{q}^2\gg\mechanical{m}^2$, the fermion loop in the photon propagator, from (9.57), has the form
$$
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi}
\left[
\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
-\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\energy{\mu}^2}\right)
+\frac53
+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right)
\right],
$$
where $1/\bar\epsilon=1/\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln(4\pi)$.
Subtract the $1/\bar\epsilon$ pole in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme.
The charge relation between scales becomes
$$
\frac1{\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu}')}
=\frac1{\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu})}
-\frac1{3\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}'^2}{\energy{\mu}^2}
+\mathcal{O}(\electric{\alpha}).
$$
Differentiate with respect to $\ln\energy{\mu}$:
$$
\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\electric{\alpha}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
=\frac{2\electric{\alpha}^2}{3\pi}
+\mathcal{O}(\electric{\alpha}^3).
$$
Using $\electric{e}=\sqrt{4\pi\electric{\alpha}}$ gives
$$
\electric{\beta}(\electric{e})
=\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\electric{e}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
=\frac{\electric{e}^3}{12\pi^2}
+\mathcal{O}(\electric{e}^5).
\tag{10.4}
$$
The source also gives the two-loop result needed for higher-precision calculations:
$$
\electric{\beta}(\electric{e})
=\frac{\electric{e}^3}{12\pi^2}
+\frac{\electric{e}^5}{64\pi^4}
+\mathcal{O}(\electric{e}^7).
$$

> [!note]
> These coefficients describe QED with one unit-charge Dirac species. Additional charged species change the coefficients and require threshold treatment when their masses matter.

For $N_f$ fermion species with charges $\electric{Q_i}$ in units of $\electric{e}$, the one-loop beta function is
$$
\electric{\beta}(\electric{e})
=\frac{\electric{e}^3}{12\pi^2}
\sum_{i=1}^{N_f}\electric{Q_i}^2
+\mathcal{O}(\electric{e}^5).
$$
For a single electron species, the source uses $N_f=1$ and
$\electric{Q_1}=1$, recovering (10.4).
In QED, $\electric{\beta}(\electric{e})>0$, so the charge grows toward the ultraviolet.

> [!note]
> The squared charge is independent of its sign; the source uses the magnitude for the electron. Degeneracies, such as quark color, must be included in the species sum or as multiplicity factors. The stated high-energy coefficient applies when the included species can be treated as active.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{e},\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Electromagnetic charge and fine-structure constant | Equivalent dimensionless QED couplings in four dimensions. |
| $\electric{\beta}$ | $1$ | QED beta function | The change of electric charge with logarithmic scale. |
| $\electric{\Pi}$ | $1$ | Scalar vacuum-polarization function | The fermion-loop coefficient that determines charge renormalization. |
| $\mechanical{q}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon momentum | The spacelike external momentum in the high-energy limit. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fermion mass | The mass neglected relative to the external momentum. |
| $\energy{\mu},\energy{\mu}'$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scales | The two subtraction scales used to relate the coupling values. |
| $\electric{Q_i},\electric{Q_1}$ | $1$ | Relative fermion charges | Charges in units of the electromagnetic coupling; their squares enter the beta function. |
