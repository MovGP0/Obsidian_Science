---
title: "The Beta Function at Two Loops"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The two-loop gauge beta function adds a term proportional to g⁵. Its first two coefficients determine improved QCD running.

The one-loop evolution equation is an approximation.
At two loops, the gauge-theory beta function is
$$
\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})
=-\frac{b_0\signal{g}^3}{16\pi^2}
-\frac{b_1\signal{g}^5}{(16\pi^2)^2}
+\mathcal{O}(\signal{g}^7).
$$
For QCD with gauge group $SU(N_c)$ and $N_f$ quark species,
$$
\begin{aligned}
b_0&=\frac{11}{3}N_c-\frac23N_f,\\
b_1&=\frac{34}{3}N_c^2
-\left(\frac{13}{3}N_c-\frac1{N_c}\right)N_f.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $SU(3)$ with $N_f=6$, the coefficients are $b_0=7$ and $b_1=26$.
The source calls these first two coefficients scheme independent, naming MS, $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$, and MOM as examples.
Coefficients starting with $b_2$ depend on the scheme.

The second term improves the running only while it is a perturbative correction to the first. Compare their magnitudes at the coupling in use. For the six-flavor coefficients quoted here, both terms are negative at positive coupling, so the two-loop truncation has no positive nonzero fixed point. It still tends to zero toward high energy.

The integral uses the reference coupling as boundary data; when the two scales agree, both displayed differences vanish. An exact solution of the truncated equation removes integration error, not the physical uncertainty from omitted higher loops. This distinction also applies when choosing a Lambert W branch: the branch must reproduce the reference value and the intended weak-coupling trajectory.

The source gives the implicit scale-evolution relation
$$
\begin{aligned}
t
&=\int_{\signal{g}(\energy{\mu_0})}^{\signal{g}(\energy{\mu})}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\signal{g}'}{\signal{\beta}(\signal{g}')}\\
&=\frac{8\pi^2}{b_0}
\left[
\frac1{\signal{g}^2(\energy{\mu})}
-\frac1{\signal{g}^2(\energy{\mu_0})}
\right]
+\frac{b_1}{2b_0^2}
\ln\frac{\signal{g}^2(\energy{\mu})}{\signal{g}^2(\energy{\mu_0})}
+\cdots.
\end{aligned}
$$
It says that a closed-form solution can be obtained approximately with the Lambert $W$ function.

> [!note]
> Universality of the first two coefficients assumes compatible coupling normalization and an analytic, mass-independent scheme transformation in the massless setting. A mass-dependent scheme can introduce threshold and scale-ratio dependence.
>
> The displayed relation is an expansion, as indicated by the ellipsis. The beta function truncated at two loops can itself be integrated exactly and inverted with an appropriate Lambert $W$ branch. Its physical accuracy remains limited by the perturbative truncation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Gauge coupling | The dimensionless QCD coupling in four dimensions. |
| $\signal{\beta}$ | $1$ | Two-loop beta function | The logarithmic scale derivative including terms through fifth order in g. |
| $\energy{\mu},\energy{\mu_0}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Evolution and reference scales | The scales used in the implicit two-loop coupling relation. |
