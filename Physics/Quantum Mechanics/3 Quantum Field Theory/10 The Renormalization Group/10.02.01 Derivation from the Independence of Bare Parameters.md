---
title: "Derivation from the Independence of Bare Parameters"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Holding the bare theory fixed constrains how renormalized Green functions, couplings, and field factors change with scale.

The starting point is that the bare parameters in the original Lagrangian do not depend on the renormalization scale $\energy{\mu}$.
The source lists $\signal{g_0}$, $\mechanical{m_0}$, and $\signal{Z}$ as these bare quantities.
They specify the original theory in terms of the cutoff $\energy{\Lambda}$, or the dimensional regulator $\epsilon$.
The scale dependence of renormalized quantities is introduced by the renormalization procedure.

> [!note]
> The bare coupling and bare mass are held fixed under this scale variation. If $Z$ means the bare-to-renormalized field factor used below, it generally does vary with $\energy{\mu}$. It must not be confused with an independent fixed bare parameter.

Let
$\signal{G}^{(n)}(\mechanical{x_1},\ldots,\mechanical{x_n};
\signal{g},\mechanical{m},\energy{\mu})$
be the renormalized $n$-point Green function, expressed through
$\signal{g}(\energy{\mu})$, $\mechanical{m}(\energy{\mu})$, and $\energy{\mu}$.
The corresponding bare function is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G_0}^{(n)}
(\mechanical{x_1},\ldots,\mechanical{x_n};
\signal{g_0},\mechanical{m_0},\energy{\Lambda})\\
\quad=\signal{Z_\phi}^{n/2}
\signal{G}^{(n)}
(\mechanical{x_1},\ldots,\mechanical{x_n};
\signal{g},\mechanical{m},\energy{\mu}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\signal{Z_\phi}$ is the field renormalization constant:
$\signal{\phi_0}=\signal{Z_\phi}^{1/2}\signal{\phi}$.
Since the bare function does not depend on $\energy{\mu}$,
$$
\energy{\mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\energy{\mu}}\signal{G_0}^{(n)}=0.
$$
Express the total derivative through the renormalized parameters:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\energy{\mu}}
&=\left.\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\energy{\mu}}\right|_{\signal{g_0},\mechanical{m_0}}\\
&=\left.\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\energy{\mu}}\right|_{\signal{g},\mechanical{m}}
+\left(\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\signal{g}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}\right)
\frac{\partial}{\partial\signal{g}}
+\left(\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\mechanical{m}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}\right)
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{m}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Define the RG functions at fixed bare parameters and cutoff:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})
&=\left.\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\signal{g}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
\right|_{\signal{g_0},\mechanical{m_0},\energy{\Lambda}},\\
\signal{\gamma_m}(\signal{g})
&=-\left.\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{m}}
\frac{\partial\mechanical{m}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
\right|_{\signal{g_0},\mechanical{m_0},\energy{\Lambda}},\\
\signal{\gamma}(\signal{g})
&=\left.\frac{\energy{\mu}}2
\frac{\partial\ln\signal{Z_\phi}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
\right|_{\signal{g_0},\mechanical{m_0},\energy{\Lambda}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> At this step the source calls $\signal{Z_\phi}^{-n/2}\signal{G}^{(n)}$ constant. Its preceding bare-field relation instead makes $\signal{Z_\phi}^{n/2}\signal{G}^{(n)}$ constant. The positive power gives the $+n\signal{\gamma}$ term in the equation below.

The total derivative follows one fixed regulated theory, not a sequence of new bare theories. The cutoff or dimensional regulator stays fixed while the subtraction scale and renormalized parameters change together. Only after this relation has been defined does one remove the regulator where the renormalized limit exists.

To see the field term, differentiate the product of the field factor and the renormalized function. Each of the n fields contributes one half of the logarithmic derivative of the field factor. This gives the displayed positive anomalous-dimension term. The minus sign in the mass term comes from the stated definition of the mass anomalous dimension; a different definition would change that sign. Writing all RG functions as functions of the coupling alone assumes a mass-independent scheme in the stated setting; in a mass-dependent scheme, mass-to-scale ratios can also enter.

The **Callan–Symanzik equation** is
$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\Bigl[&
\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
+\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})\frac{\partial}{\partial\signal{g}}\\
&-\signal{\gamma_m}(\signal{g})\mechanical{m}
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{m}}
+n\signal{\gamma}(\signal{g})
\Bigr]
\signal{G}^{(n)}(\mechanical{p_i};\signal{g},\mechanical{m},\energy{\mu})
=0.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{10.1}
$$
Here $\signal{\beta}$ describes coupling evolution,
$\signal{\gamma}$ is the **field anomalous dimension**, and
$\signal{\gamma_m}$ describes mass evolution.
The source changes from position arguments to momentum arguments in (10.1).

## Example 10.1: φ⁴ theory at one loop

For a scalar theory in four dimensions with interaction
$\signal{\lambda}\signal{\phi}^4/4!$,
the explicit calculation of the source's “butterfly” loop diagram gives
$$
\signal{\beta}(\signal{\lambda})
=\frac{3\signal{\lambda}^2}{16\pi^2}
+\mathcal{O}(\signal{\lambda}^3).
$$
The field anomalous dimension vanishes at first order in $\signal{\lambda}$.
It starts at second order:
$$
\signal{\gamma}
=\frac{\signal{\lambda}^2}{12(16\pi^2)^2}+\cdots.
$$
These expressions follow by differentiating renormalized Green functions with respect to $\energy{\mu}$ while requiring the bare quantities to remain independent of $\energy{\mu}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g_0}$ | — | Bare coupling | The fixed original coupling, whose mass dimension depends on the theory and dimension. |
| $\mechanical{m_0}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Bare mass | The mass parameter in the fixed bare theory. |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Field renormalization factor | The factor whose scale dependence must be distinguished from fixed bare quantities. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The scale varied while the bare theory is held fixed. |
| $\energy{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Ultraviolet cutoff | The regulator scale used to define the original theory. |
| $\signal{G}^{(n)},\signal{G_0}^{(n)}$ | — | Renormalized and bare Green functions | Their dimensions depend on the field content, number of insertions, and position or momentum representation. |
| $\mechanical{x_i}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime positions | The position arguments of the n-point functions. |
| $\mechanical{p_i}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | External momenta | The momentum arguments in the final equation. |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Renormalized coupling | The scale-dependent coupling in the Green function. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalized mass | The scale-dependent mass parameter. |
| $\signal{Z_\phi}$ | $1$ | Field renormalization constant | The factor relating the bare and renormalized fields. |
| $\signal{\phi_0},\signal{\phi}$ | — | Bare and renormalized fields | The generic fields whose normalization defines Zφ; their dimension depends on the field type and spacetime dimension. |
| $\signal{\beta},\signal{\gamma_m},\signal{\gamma}$ | $1$ | Beta function and anomalous dimensions | The logarithmic scale derivatives of coupling, mass, and field normalization. |
| $\signal{\lambda}$ | $1$ | Scalar quartic coupling | The interaction coefficient in the four-dimensional example. |
