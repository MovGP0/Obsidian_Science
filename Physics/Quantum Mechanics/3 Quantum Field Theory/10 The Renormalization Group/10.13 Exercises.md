$$

$$

### Solution 10.1 — Beta function in quartic scalar theory

Consider the s-channel one-loop four-point function. The quartic vertex is $-i\signal{\lambda}$, and the two-propagator graph has symmetry factor one half:
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}_s^{(1)}
=\frac{(-i\signal{\lambda})^2}{2}
\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0}
\frac{i}{(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{p})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0}.
$$
Here $\mechanical{p}=\mechanical{p}_1+\mechanical{p}_2$ is the total s-channel momentum. The two numerator factors $i$ multiply to $-1$.

Let $I$ denote the loop integral with the propagator numerators $i$ removed. Summing the three channels gives, after removing the common dimensional vertex factor,
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}^{(1)}
=-\frac{(-i\signal{\lambda})^2}{2}
[I(\energy{s})+I(\mechanical{t})+I(\mechanical{u})]
=\frac{\signal{\lambda}^2}{2}
[I(\energy{s})+I(\mechanical{t})+I(\mechanical{u})].
$$
In $d=4-2\epsilon$, include the reference-scale factor to define the dimensionless loop integral:
$$
I(\mechanical{p}^2)
=\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0)
[(\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{p})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i0]}.
$$
Feynman parametrization and a loop-momentum shift give
$$
I(\mechanical{p}^2)
=\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}\int_0^1dx
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{(\mechanical{k}^2-\energy{\Delta}+i0)^2},
\qquad
\energy{\Delta}=\mechanical{m}^2-x(1-x)\mechanical{p}^2.
$$
The standard integral is
$$
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{(\mechanical{k}^2-\energy{\Delta}+i0)^2}
=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\Gamma(\epsilon)(\energy{\Delta}-i0)^{-\epsilon}.
$$
Thus
$$
I(\mechanical{p}^2)
=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^2}
\left[
\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi
-\int_0^1dx\ln\frac{\energy{\Delta}-i0}{\energy{\mu}^2}
\right]+\mathcal O(\epsilon).
$$
The momentum-independent pole is $I_{\mathrm{div}}=i/(16\pi^2\bar\epsilon)$. The three channels contribute
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm{div}}^{(1)}
=\frac{\signal{\lambda}^2}{2}\,3\,
\frac{i}{16\pi^2\bar\epsilon}
=\frac{3i\signal{\lambda}^2}{32\pi^2\bar\epsilon}.
$$
Use the multiplicative coupling counterterm convention
$$
\signal{\lambda}_0=\signal{Z}_\lambda\signal{\lambda}\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon},
\qquad
\signal{Z}_\lambda=1+\signal{\delta}_\lambda.
$$
The counterterm vertex $-i\signal{\lambda}\signal{\delta}_\lambda$ cancels the pole, giving
$$
\signal{\delta}_\lambda=\frac{3\signal{\lambda}}{32\pi^2\bar\epsilon}.
$$
Here $\bar\epsilon^{-1}=\epsilon^{-1}-\gamma_E+\ln4\pi$. The overall factor $\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}$ of the four-point vertex is understood in the amplitude and counterterm formulas.

The bare coupling is independent of $\energy{\mu}$. To obtain the beta function, retain the scale derivative of the complete bare expression. Set $a=3/(32\pi^2)$ and use the pole form
$$
\signal{\lambda}_0
=\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}\left(
\signal{\lambda}+\frac{a\signal{\lambda}^2}{\epsilon}
\right)+\cdots.
$$
At fixed bare coupling,
$$
0=2\epsilon\left(
\signal{\lambda}+\frac{a\signal{\lambda}^2}{\epsilon}
\right)
+\signal{\beta}(\signal{\lambda},\epsilon)
\left(1+\frac{2a\signal{\lambda}}{\epsilon}\right)+\cdots.
$$
Write $\signal{\beta}=-2\epsilon\signal{\lambda}+b\signal{\lambda}^2+\cdots$. The coefficient of $\signal{\lambda}^2$ gives $b=2a$, so
$$
\signal{\beta}(\signal{\lambda},\epsilon)
=-2\epsilon\signal{\lambda}
+\frac{3\signal{\lambda}^2}{16\pi^2}
+\mathcal O(\signal{\lambda}^3).
$$
In four dimensions,
$$
\signal{\beta}(\signal{\lambda})
=\frac{3\signal{\lambda}^2}{16\pi^2}
+\mathcal O(\signal{\lambda}^3).
$$

> [!note] Correction to the source differentiation
> The source's first differentiated equation omits the derivative of the factor $\energy{\mu}^{2\epsilon}$ multiplying the counterterm. Its later truncation also drops terms that contribute at order $\signal{\lambda}^2$ because they multiply the leading $-2\epsilon\signal{\lambda}$. Keeping the complete bare expression gives the source's correct final beta function.

### Solution 10.2 — Running QED charge

The electron-only one-loop formula (10.5) is
$$
\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu})
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}
{1-\dfrac{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}{3\pi}
\ln(\energy{\mu}^2/\mechanical{m}_e^2)}.
$$
Use the source inputs
$$
\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)^{-1}=137.036,
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_e=0.511\times10^{-3}\unit{GeV},
\qquad
\mechanical{M}_Z=91.2\unit{GeV}.
$$
Then
$$
\ln\frac{\mechanical{M}_Z^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
=2\ln\frac{91.2}{5.11\times10^{-4}}
\approx24.19,
$$
$$
\frac{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}{3\pi}
\ln\frac{\mechanical{M}_Z^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\approx\frac{24.19}{1291.5}
\approx0.01873.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
&\approx137.036(1-0.01873)\\
&\approx137.036-2.566
\approx134.47.
\end{aligned}
$$

The electron-only result is larger than the source's comparison value
$\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)\approx128.9$. Charged species contribute to the beta function above their thresholds:
$$
\electric{\beta}(\electric{e})
=\frac{\electric{e}^3}{12\pi^2}
\sum_{f:\,\mechanical{m}_f<\energy{\mu}}N_{c,f}\electric{Q}_f^2.
$$
Quarks have three colors; the up-type charges are $2/3$, down-type charges are $-1/3$, and charged leptons have charge $-1$. At the Z scale, the top quark with source mass
$\mechanical{m}_t\approx173\unit{GeV}$ is excluded. The active charge-squared sum is
$$
\sum_fN_{c,f}\electric{Q}_f^2
=3\cdot2\cdot\frac49
+3\cdot3\cdot\frac19
+3\cdot1
=\frac{20}{3}.
$$
The additional species increase the rate of running. The numerical difference from the source benchmark is
$$
134.47-128.9\approx5.6.
$$

> [!note] Threshold and matching scope
> The source attributes this difference to the remaining leptons and quarks. The charge sum at the Z scale cannot be used over the whole interval down to the electron mass: each threshold requires matching. Light-quark vacuum polarization also needs a hadronic treatment. This is a qualitative explanation, not a numerical derivation of 128.9 from the active-species sum alone.

### Solution 10.3 — Wilson–Fisher fixed point

This exercise uses $d=4-\epsilon$, rather than the $d=4-2\epsilon$ regulator convention in Solution 10.1. For the dimensionless coupling $\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}$, the fixed points satisfy
$$
\signal{\beta}(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*)=0,
\qquad
-\epsilon\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*
+\frac{3(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*)^2}{16\pi^2}=0.
$$
**(a)** The Gaussian fixed point is $\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*=0$. The nontrivial solution is
$$
\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*
=\frac{16\pi^2\epsilon}{3}+\mathcal O(\epsilon^2).
$$
**(b)** Differentiate the beta function:
$$
\signal{\beta}'(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}})
=-\epsilon+\frac{6\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}}{16\pi^2}.
$$
At the nontrivial fixed point,
$$
\signal{\beta}'(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*)
=-\epsilon+\frac6{16\pi^2}\frac{16\pi^2\epsilon}{3}
=\epsilon>0
$$
at leading order for $\epsilon>0$.

A small displacement from the fixed point scales as
$\delta\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}\propto\energy{\mu}^{\,\epsilon}$ at leading order. For $\epsilon>0$, it decreases toward low energy and grows toward high energy. The Wilson–Fisher fixed point is therefore infrared stable in this coupling direction.

> [!note]
> This stability statement concerns the quartic-coupling flow on the critical surface. A mass perturbation is relevant and must be tuned to reach the critical theory.

**(c)** As $\epsilon\to0$ and $d\to4$,
$\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*\to0$. The Wilson–Fisher fixed point merges with the Gaussian fixed point. In this perturbative approximation, four-dimensional quartic scalar theory has no nonzero interacting fixed point. For $d=3$, setting $\epsilon=1$ gives a rough route to the Ising universality class; quantitative critical exponents require higher orders or nonperturbative methods.

### Solution 10.4 — Asymptotic freedom

The one-loop evolution is
$$
\frac{d\bar{\signal{g}}}{dt}
=\signal{\beta}(\bar{\signal{g}})
=-\frac{b_0\bar{\signal{g}}^3}{16\pi^2},
\qquad b_0>0.
$$
Here $t$ is a logarithmic scale parameter that increases toward the ultraviolet. Separate variables:
$$
\frac{d\bar{\signal{g}}}{\bar{\signal{g}}^3}
=-\frac{b_0}{16\pi^2}\,dt,
\qquad
-\frac1{2\bar{\signal{g}}^2}
=-\frac{b_0t}{16\pi^2}+C.
$$
The initial condition $\bar{\signal{g}}(0)=\signal{g}_0$ gives
$C=-1/(2\signal{g}_0^2)$. Hence
$$
\frac1{\bar{\signal{g}}^2(t)}
=\frac1{\signal{g}_0^2}+\frac{b_0t}{8\pi^2},
$$
$$
\bar{\signal{g}}^2(t)
=\frac{\signal{g}_0^2}
{1+\dfrac{b_0\signal{g}_0^2}{8\pi^2}t}.
$$
As $t\to+\infty$, the denominator grows linearly and
$\bar{\signal{g}}^2(t)\to0$: the theory is asymptotically free.

If the scale is lowered, the one-loop denominator reaches zero at
$$
t^*=-\frac{8\pi^2}{b_0\signal{g}_0^2}.
$$
The source calls this an infrared Landau pole. It occurs at finite negative $t$, rather than at $t\to-\infty$, and marks failure of the perturbative one-loop running at strong coupling. The formula cannot be continued through that point as a controlled physical prediction.

For the numerical QCD estimate, use
$$
\signal{\alpha}_s(\energy{\mu})
=\frac{2\pi}{b_0\ln(\energy{\mu}/\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}})}.
$$
With the requested fixed-six-flavor inputs $b_0=7$,
$\signal{\alpha}_s(\mechanical{M}_Z)=0.118$, and $\mechanical{M}_Z=91.2\unit{GeV}$,
$$
\ln\frac{\mechanical{M}_Z}{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}}
=\frac{2\pi}{7\cdot0.118}
\approx7.606,
$$
$$
\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{QCD}}
=\mechanical{M}_Ze^{-7.606}
\approx91.2(4.96\times10^{-4})\unit{GeV}
\approx45\unit{MeV}.
$$
The source contrasts this one-loop fixed-flavor estimate with a five-flavor modified-minimal-subtraction benchmark near $210\unit{MeV}$ obtained with higher-order running and heavy-quark matching. These are different flavor and perturbative prescriptions; the fixed-six-flavor approximation is the calculation requested here.

### Solution 10.5 — Callan–Symanzik equation for the propagator

The momentum-space two-point function has canonical mass dimension $-2$. In the source's mass-anomalous-dimension convention, its Callan–Symanzik equation is
$$
\left[
\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
+\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})\frac{\partial}{\partial\signal{g}}
-\signal{\gamma}_m(\signal{g})\mechanical{m}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{m}}
+2\signal{\gamma}(\signal{g})
\right]
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p}^2;\signal{g},\mechanical{m},\energy{\mu})=0.
$$
In the massless limit, introduce the positive Euclidean momentum magnitude
$\mechanical{P}=\sqrt{-\mechanical{p}^2}$. Dimensional homogeneity gives
$$
\left(
\mechanical{P}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{P}}
+\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
\right)
\signal{G}^{(2)}=-2\signal{G}^{(2)}.
$$
Eliminating the scale derivative,
$$
\left[
-\mechanical{P}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{P}}
+\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})\frac{\partial}{\partial\signal{g}}
-2+2\signal{\gamma}(\signal{g})
\right]\signal{G}^{(2)}=0.
$$
Let $t=\ln(\mechanical{P}/\energy{\mu}_0)$ and
$d\bar{\signal{g}}/dt=\signal{\beta}(\bar{\signal{g}})$.
The characteristic solution can be written
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G}^{(2)}(-\mechanical{P}^2)
=\frac1{\mechanical{P}^2}
\exp\left[
2\int_0^{\ln(\mechanical{P}/\energy{\mu}_0)}
dt'\,\signal{\gamma}(\bar{\signal{g}}(t'))
\right]
F(\bar{\signal{g}}(\mechanical{P})).
\end{aligned}
$$
The dimensionless boundary function $F$ includes the chosen propagator normalization and is evaluated perturbatively at a reference scale of order $\mechanical{P}$. For the scalar theory, the running coupling is $\bar{\signal{\lambda}}$. Denoting the exponential by $\signal{Z}(\mechanical{P})$, the compact form is
$$
\signal{G}^{(2)}(-\mechanical{P}^2)
=\frac1{\mechanical{P}^2}F(\bar{\signal{\lambda}}(\mechanical{P}))\signal{Z}(\mechanical{P}).
$$

> [!note] Coupling label
> The source's last box changes the argument of $F$ to the strong coupling $\signal{\alpha}_s$, although the exercise concerns quartic scalar theory. The scalar form uses $\bar{\signal{\lambda}}$ as shown. The anomalous-dimension factor also remains part of the RG solution.

The factor $\signal{Z}(\mechanical{P})$ contains the anomalous dimension through
$\exp[2\int\signal{\gamma}\,dt]$.

#### Logarithmic corrections

The source next uses an asymptotically free gauge-theory example, with
$$
\signal{\gamma}(\signal{g})
=\frac{c_\gamma\signal{g}^2}{16\pi^2}+\cdots,
\qquad
\bar{\signal{g}}^2(t)\sim\frac{8\pi^2}{b_0t}
$$
at large positive $t$. For a fixed lower point $t_0$ in that regime,
$$
\int_{t_0}^{t}\signal{\gamma}(\bar{\signal{g}}(t'))\,dt'
\sim\frac{c_\gamma}{2b_0}\ln\frac{t}{t_0}.
$$
Up to a reference normalization,
$$
\signal{Z}(\mechanical{P})
\sim\left[\ln\frac{\mechanical{P}}{\energy{\mu}_0}\right]^{c_\gamma/b_0}.
$$
Thus logarithms modify the canonical propagator power $1/\mechanical{P}^2$.

> [!note] Scope of the logarithmic example
> The source changes from quartic scalar theory to an asymptotically free gauge-theory running law here. It is an illustration of the characteristic solution, not the one-loop scalar anomalous dimension. The asymptotic $1/t$ form also cannot be integrated down to $t=0$; a reference point and normalization are required.

### Solution 10.6 — Landau pole with Standard Model charged fermions

For all three families, including quark color multiplicities,
$$
\sum_fN_{c,f}\electric{Q}_f^2
=3\left[3\left(\frac23\right)^2
+3\left(\frac13\right)^2\right]+3\cdot1^2.
$$
The outer factor three counts families, and the inner factors three count quark colors. The contributions are four from the up-type quarks, one from the down-type quarks, and three from the charged leptons:
$$
\sum_fN_{c,f}\electric{Q}_f^2=4+1+3=8.
$$
The formal all-species-active QED beta function is
$$
\electric{\beta}(\electric{\alpha})
=\frac{2\electric{\alpha}^2}{3\pi}\,8
=\frac{16\electric{\alpha}^2}{3\pi}.
$$
Treating this charge sum as constant gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu})}
&=\frac1{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}
-\frac{16}{3\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{m}_e}\\
&=\frac1{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}
-\frac8{3\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This equation is the stated formal approximation. Threshold matching is required for a physical evolution from the electron mass, and above electroweak scales the complete gauge theory must be used.

The denominator of the formal eight-unit charge-squared running vanishes at
$$
\energy{\mu}_{\mathrm{Landau}}
=\mechanical{m}_e\exp\left[
\frac{3\pi}{16\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}
\right].
$$
For $\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)\approx1/137$,
$$
\frac{3\pi\cdot137}{16}\approx80.7,
$$
$$
\energy{\mu}_{\mathrm{Landau}}
\approx\mechanical{m}_ee^{80.7}
\sim5\times10^{-4}\times10^{35}\unit{GeV}
\sim10^{32}\unit{GeV}.
$$
The source notes the large reduction from the electron-only estimate near $10^{277}\unit{GeV}$. Both extrapolations are far beyond the Planck scale and outside the controlled range of the approximation.

### Solution 10.7 — Gauge-coupling unification

**(a)** A pairwise meeting condition is
$$
\signal{\alpha}_i^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
-\frac{b_i}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{M}_Z}
=
\signal{\alpha}_j^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
-\frac{b_j}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{M}_Z}.
$$
It gives
$$
\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}_{ij}}{\mechanical{M}_Z}
=\frac{2\pi[
\signal{\alpha}_i^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
-\signal{\alpha}_j^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)]}{b_i-b_j}.
$$
The source uses the rounded inputs
$$
\signal{\alpha}_1^{-1}=59,\qquad
\signal{\alpha}_2^{-1}=29,\qquad
\signal{\alpha}_3^{-1}=8.5,
$$
at $\mechanical{M}_Z$, and $b_1=4.1$, $b_2\approx-3.17$, $b_3=-7$.
The first inverse coupling decreases with scale while the second increases:
$$
\signal{\alpha}_1^{-1}(\energy{\mu})=59-\frac{4.1}{2\pi}L,
\qquad
\signal{\alpha}_2^{-1}(\energy{\mu})\approx29+\frac{3.17}{2\pi}L,
\qquad L=\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{M}_Z}.
$$
Their meeting gives
$$
L_{12}\approx\frac{30\cdot2\pi}{7.27}\approx25.93,
$$
$$
\energy{\mu}_{12}\approx\mechanical{M}_Ze^{25.93}
\approx1.66\times10^{13}\unit{GeV}.
$$
Similarly,
$$
L_{13}
=\frac{(59-8.5)2\pi}{4.1+7}
=\frac{50.5\cdot2\pi}{11.1}
\approx28.58.
$$
The source's numerical steps use the rounded $b_2$ above; the exercise gives its exact one-loop value $-19/6$.

The source's rounded SM calculation gives
$$
\energy{\mu}_{13}\approx2.38\times10^{14}\unit{GeV},
$$
$$

### Solution 10.8 — Relevant, marginal, and irrelevant operators

**(a)** In four dimensions, the scalar field has canonical mass dimension one. The kinetic operator $(\partial\signal{\phi})^2$ has dimension four, the measure $d^4\mechanical{x}$ has dimension minus four, and each Lagrangian-density term must have dimension four.

For a term $\signal{\lambda}_{2n}\signal{\phi}^{2n}/\energy{\Lambda}^{2n-4}$,
$$
[\signal{\phi}^{2n}]=2n,\qquad
\left[\frac{\signal{\lambda}_{2n}}{\energy{\Lambda}^{2n-4}}\right]=4-2n.
$$
The coefficient $\signal{\lambda}_{2n}$ is dimensionless because the explicit cutoff power carries the required mass dimension.

**(b)** For the operator $\mathcal O_n=\signal{\phi}^{2n}$, the canonical operator dimension is $2n$.

Near the Gaussian fixed point in four dimensions, the classification is:

| Operator | Canonical dimension | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}^2$ | $2<4$ | Relevant mass operator. |
| $\signal{\phi}^4$ | $4$ | Marginal by engineering dimension. |
| $\signal{\phi}^6$ | $6>4$ | Irrelevant. |
| $\signal{\phi}^{2n}$ with $n\ge3$ | $2n>4$ | Irrelevant. |

Quantum corrections determine the running of canonically marginal couplings.

**(c)** For an energy scale $\energy{E}\ll\energy{\Lambda}$, dimensional analysis gives the contribution of a dimension-$2n$ operator relative to an energy-density scale $\energy{E}^4$:
$$
\frac{\signal{\lambda}_{2n}\signal{\phi}^{2n}}{\energy{\Lambda}^{2n-4}}
\bigg|_{\signal{\phi}\sim\energy{E}}
\sim\frac{\signal{\lambda}_{2n}\energy{E}^{2n}}{\energy{\Lambda}^{2n-4}}
=\signal{\lambda}_{2n}\energy{E}^4
\left(\frac{\energy{E}}{\energy{\Lambda}}\right)^{2n-4}.
$$
The suppression is $(\energy{E}/\energy{\Lambda})^2$ for $n=3$ and
$(\energy{E}/\energy{\Lambda})^4$ for $n=4$. Thus higher-dimension operators become small in the infrared, which permits an effective theory organized by increasing powers of the energy-to-cutoff ratio. This is an expansion at a stated accuracy, with possible logarithmic running of its coefficients.
