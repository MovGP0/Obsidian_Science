---
title: "The Philosophy of Effective Field Theories"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Effective field theories describe physics at a chosen scale with all symmetry-allowed operators. High-scale effects enter through matched coefficients and suppressed corrections.

The Wilsonian approach gives the following principles:

1. At each energy scale $\energy{\mu}$, an effective action describes the relevant physics. It contains all operators compatible with the symmetries. Their couplings are fixed by matching to the more fundamental theory at a scale $\energy{\Lambda}>\energy{\mu}$.
2. Irrelevant operators contribute powers of $(\energy{\mu}/\energy{\Lambda})^n$, which become small for $\energy{\mu}\ll\energy{\Lambda}$.
3. Predictions do not require full knowledge of the physics at scale $\energy{\Lambda}$.

At scale $\energy{\mu}$, the effective theory and its couplings are sufficient for the prediction.

This is the **decoupling principle**.
At leading low-energy order, high-energy physics affects low-energy physics through a finite set of relevant and marginal coefficients.
The source describes RG structure as shielding low-energy predictions from details at scale $\energy{\Lambda}$, whether that scale involves Planck physics, string theory, or another microscopic theory.

> [!note]
> Predictions still require the effective coefficients to be fixed by matching or measurement. Higher-dimension operators encode additional ultraviolet information at the corresponding suppressed orders. The finite-parameter statement applies at a specified low-energy accuracy.

## Example 10.7: Fermi interaction as an effective theory

At energies $\energy{E}\ll\mechanical{M_W}$, the weak interaction is described by the Fermi interaction, with coefficient
$$
\frac{\signal{G_F}}{\sqrt2}
=\frac{\signal{g_2}^2}{8\mechanical{M_W}^2}.
$$
The source schematically writes the four-fermion operator as
$(\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi})^2$.
In $d=4$, it has dimension six and is irrelevant.
Its coupling satisfies
$\signal{G_F}\sim1/\mechanical{M_W}^2$,
introducing the scale $\mechanical{M_W}$.

The tree-level matching coefficient has mass dimension minus two: the gauge coupling is dimensionless and the heavy-boson mass is squared in the denominator. A four-fermion operator has dimension six, so the resulting Lagrangian term has dimension four. The dimensionless expansion parameter in a low-energy amplitude is the coefficient times energy squared.

The suppression is a comparison at fixed dimensionless couplings and appropriate external states. Numerical coefficients, symmetries, and interference can further reduce or enhance a particular observable. As energy approaches the heavy-boson mass, the local expansion fails and the propagating boson must be retained. The displayed matching relation is tree level; loop corrections require consistent parameter and operator definitions.

For $\energy{E}\ll\mechanical{M_W}$, its effects are suppressed by
$(\energy{E}/\mechanical{M_W})^2\ll1$.
This explains why the weak interaction is weak at low energy.
Integrating out the heavy $W$ and $Z$ bosons generates the effective interactions and their suppression.
This is a basic example of an EFT obtained by removing high-energy degrees of freedom.

> [!note]
> The displayed four-fermion notation is schematic. The weak Fermi interaction has a definite current and chirality structure; it is not generally a scalar bilinear squared.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Low-energy scale | The scale at which the effective theory describes the system. |
| $\energy{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | High-energy matching scale | The scale of the more fundamental physics integrated into effective coefficients. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Low process energy | The energy compared with the heavy weak-boson mass. |
| $\mechanical{M_W}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | W-boson mass | The heavy scale that suppresses the charged-current Fermi interaction. |
| $\signal{G_F}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Fermi coupling constant | The dimensionful coefficient of the low-energy four-fermion interaction. |
| $\signal{g_2}$ | $1$ | Weak gauge coupling | The dimensionless coupling in the tree-level Fermi matching relation. |
| $\signal{\psi},\bar{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Fermion fields | The fields in the schematic four-fermion operator. |
