title: "The Scale Anomaly and the Energy–Momentum Tensor"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Quantum renormalization gives massless QCD a nonzero energy–momentum trace. This trace anomaly expresses the generated QCD scale.

Classically, QCD with massless quarks has a traceless energy–momentum tensor:
$\energy{T}^\mu{}_\mu=0$.
The source relates this condition to conformal invariance.
Quantum renormalization introduces scale dependence, and the trace no longer vanishes:
$$
\energy{T}^\mu{}_\mu
=\frac{\signal{\beta}(\signal{g_s})}{2\signal{g_s}}
\signal{F}^{a\mu\nu}\signal{F}^a_{\mu\nu}.
\tag{10.7}
$$
Here $\signal{F}^a_{\mu\nu}$ is the gluon field tensor.
This is the **trace anomaly**, or **scale anomaly**, and is the mathematical expression of dimensional transmutation.

The source says that the vacuum expectation of
$\energy{T}^\mu{}_\mu$, proportional to
$\langle\signal{F}^2\rangle$, is the origin of most of the proton mass, rather than the quark masses.

> [!note]
> Equation (10.7) is the massless-quark trace relation, with the renormalized composite operator understood. The proton mass is related to matrix elements of the energy–momentum tensor in a proton state. A vacuum expectation value, such as the vacuum gluon condensate, is a different matrix element and cannot alone be identified with the proton mass.

The anomaly is an operator statement, not a classical force law. In four dimensions the field tensor has mass dimension two, so its squared contraction and the energy–momentum trace both have dimension four. The coefficient is dimensionless. This checks the structure without computing a nucleon matrix element.

With the stated weak-coupling convention, the beta function is negative. This does not imply a negative proton mass: the Lorentzian field-tensor contraction is not a positive norm, and a physical mass requires the correctly normalized proton matrix element of the full tensor. In the zero-coupling limit the anomalous coefficient tends to zero, consistent with the free massless theory. Quark mass terms must be restored if the massless assumption is relaxed.

## Example 10.6: The proton mass

The proton's light $u$ and $d$ quarks have masses of a few
$\unit{MeV}$, much smaller than the proton mass of
$938\,\unit{MeV}$.
The source attributes the difference, about 99% of the proton mass, to quark kinetic energy and gluon interaction energy within the nucleon.
It connects this with the trace anomaly (10.7).

The QCD scale
$\energy{\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}}\sim200\,\unit{MeV}$
sets the characteristic nucleon mass scale and the corresponding inverse-size scale through dimensional transmutation.

> [!note]
> The percentage is the source's approximate comparison with the small current-quark masses. A detailed separation of proton energy into quark, gluon, and anomaly contributions depends on the renormalized operator definitions and scale.
