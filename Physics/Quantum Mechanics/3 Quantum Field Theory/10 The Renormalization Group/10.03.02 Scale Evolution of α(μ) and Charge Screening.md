title: "Scale Evolution of α(μ) and Charge Screening"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The QED coupling increases logarithmically with energy because vacuum polarization screens charge at long distances.

Integrate the evolution equation (10.2) with the beta function (10.4).
At one loop,
$$
\frac1{\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu})}
=\frac1{\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu_0})}
-\frac1{3\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\energy{\mu_0}^2}.
$$
Invert the relation, using the source reference value
$\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu_0}=\mechanical{m_e})\approx1/137.036$:
$$
\boxed{
\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu})
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m_e})}
{1-\dfrac{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m_e})}{3\pi}
\ln\left(\dfrac{\energy{\mu}^2}{\mechanical{m_e}^2}\right)}.
}
\tag{10.5}
$$
At $\energy{\mu}=\mechanical{M_Z}\approx91\,\unit{GeV}$, this electron-loop-only formula gives
$\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{M_Z})\approx134.5$.
When the other Standard Model fermions—muons, tau leptons, and quarks—are included, the source quotes
$$
\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{M_Z})\approx\frac1{128.9}.
$$
It states that this agrees with direct measurements.
The variation is small but can be measured precisely, as illustrated in Fig. 10.2.
The numerical values above are reproduced from the source.

> [!note]
> The simple formula (10.5) uses the one-loop electron coefficient. The Standard Model comparison also requires the charged-species contributions, threshold treatment, and a specified coupling convention.

The scale variation has a physical interpretation as **charge screening**.
In the source's virtual-pair picture, the vacuum polarizes around a charge.
Near a negative source, virtual positrons are attracted and virtual electrons are repelled, partially screening the source.

At large distances, corresponding to low energies, a probe sees the smaller screened charge.
At short distances, corresponding to high energies, it probes further into the polarization cloud and sees a larger charge.

## Example 10.3: The Landau pole

Equation (10.5) becomes singular when its denominator vanishes:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mu_{\mathrm{Landau}}}
&=\mechanical{m_e}\exp\left(
\frac{3\pi}{2\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m_e})}\right)\\
&\approx\mechanical{m_e}e^{646}
\approx10^{277}\,\unit{GeV}.
\end{aligned}
$$

At this scale, far beyond physically accessible energies, the one-loop coupling would become infinite: the **Landau pole**.
The source interprets this as evidence that pure QED cannot remain a consistent isolated theory at every scale.
At sufficiently high energy, perturbation theory fails and a broader theory is needed.
It gives the Standard Model, where electromagnetism is combined with the weak interaction, as an example of such an extension.
The source therefore presents the Landau pole as a reason to seek a more fundamental theory beyond isolated QED.

> [!note]
> The pole is obtained by extrapolating a perturbative formula into a regime where that formula fails. It is an indication of a problem with the extrapolation, rather than a rigorous nonperturbative proof. Electroweak embedding extends QED's domain, but the Standard Model example alone does not establish an ultraviolet completion valid at arbitrarily high scales.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Running fine-structure constant | The electromagnetic coupling evaluated at the chosen scale. |
| $\energy{\mu},\energy{\mu_0}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization and reference scales | The scales in the one-loop coupling relation. |
| $\mechanical{m_e}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron mass | The reference scale in the inverted formula. |
| $\mechanical{M_Z}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Z-boson mass | The approximately 91 GeV scale used for the numerical comparison. |
| $\energy{\mu_{\mathrm{Landau}}}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Landau-pole scale | The formal singular scale of the one-loop electron-only QED coupling. |
