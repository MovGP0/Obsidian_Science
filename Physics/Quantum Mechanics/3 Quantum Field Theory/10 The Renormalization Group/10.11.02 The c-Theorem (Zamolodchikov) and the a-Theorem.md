---
title: "The c-Theorem (Zamolodchikov) and the a-Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The c-theorem in two dimensions and a-theorem in four dimensions constrain the direction of RG flow between conformal fixed points.

In $d=2$, Zamolodchikov proved in 1986 that a function
$\signal{c}(\signal{g})$ exists with the following properties under the theorem's assumptions:

- It decreases monotonically along RG flow toward the infrared:
  $\mathrm{d}\signal{c}/\mathrm{d}t\le0$.
- At a fixed point, it equals the central charge $\signal{c}$.

The source describes this function as positive definite.
The theorem gives a direction to RG flow: the effective count of degrees of freedom decreases as modes are integrated out toward the infrared.
A complicated ultraviolet theory can flow to a simpler infrared theory.

In $d=4$, the corresponding result is the **a-theorem**, attributed to Komargodski and Schwimmer (2011).
The source calls $\signal{a}$ the coefficient of the Euler anomaly “in the Weyl tensor” and states that it decreases monotonically along the flow, providing a direction in four dimensions.

> [!note]
> These statements require the appropriate assumptions, including unitarity and the relevant spacetime-symmetry conditions. The four-dimensional Euler-anomaly coefficient multiplies the Euler density in the curved-space trace anomaly; the Weyl-tensor-squared term has a different coefficient. The a-theorem compares conformal endpoints, with $\signal{a}_{\mathrm{UV}}\ge\signal{a}_{\mathrm{IR}}$. It does not by itself identify the fixed-point anomaly coefficient with a uniquely defined local function $\signal{a}(\signal{g})$ that is monotonically decreasing everywhere along every flow.

Here the monotonic parameter increases toward the infrared. It is therefore the opposite direction to the earlier logarithm of increasing subtraction energy. Reversing that parameter reverses the derivative inequality; it does not reverse the physical theorem.

In two dimensions, stress-tensor conservation and positivity of the trace two-point function give the sign of the c-function derivative. Unitarity supplies the required positivity, so this is not simply a count of fields in a Lagrangian. See [Cardy's derivation, pp. 1–2](https://www-thphys.physics.ox.ac.uk/people/JohnCardy/cft14/ctheoremnotes.pdf). In four dimensions, [Komargodski and Schwimmer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.3987) obtain the endpoint inequality using positivity in dilaton scattering. Theorems constrain allowed flows; they do not determine every intermediate coupling or replace a solution of the beta functions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Running coupling | The coordinate used to describe the RG flow. |
| $\signal{c}$ | $1$ | c-function and fixed-point central charge | The monotone function whose fixed-point value is the two-dimensional central charge. |
| $\signal{a}$ | $1$ | Euler-anomaly coefficient | The four-dimensional coefficient compared at ultraviolet and infrared conformal fixed points. |
