## Exercise 10.2: Running electric charge in QED

Starting from (10.5), the source asks for a numerical check of
$\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)\approx128.9$,
using
$\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{m}_e)=137.036$,
$\mechanical{m}_e=0.511\,\unit{MeV}$, and
$\mechanical{M}_Z=91.2\,\unit{GeV}$.
Note that (10.5) includes only the electron contribution.
Other fermions contribute at higher scales.
Discuss qualitatively the quark contribution, for which the source quotes typical masses around $1$–$5\,\unit{GeV}$, and the top-quark contribution around $173\,\unit{GeV}$.

> [!note]
> Equation (10.5) alone gives the electron-only value near 134.5 discussed in the text, rather than 128.9. Reaching the latter comparison requires the additional charged-particle contributions and matching conventions. The quoted 1–5 GeV range does not describe all quark masses; light-quark vacuum polarization also requires appropriate hadronic treatment.

## Solution 10.2 — Running QED charge

The electron-only one-loop formula (10.5) is
$$
\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu})
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}
{1-\dfrac{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}{3\pi}
\ln(\energy{\mu}^2/\mechanical{m}_e^2)}.
$$
Use the source inputs
$$
\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)^{-1}=137.036,
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_e=0.511\times10^{-3}\unit{GeV},
\qquad
\mechanical{M}_Z=91.2\unit{GeV}.
$$
Then
$$
\ln\frac{\mechanical{M}_Z^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
=2\ln\frac{91.2}{5.11\times10^{-4}}
\approx24.19,
$$
$$
\frac{\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m}_e)}{3\pi}
\ln\frac{\mechanical{M}_Z^2}{\mechanical{m}_e^2}
\approx\frac{24.19}{1291.5}
\approx0.01873.
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
&\approx137.036(1-0.01873)\\
&\approx137.036-2.566
\approx134.47.
\end{aligned}
$$

The electron-only result is larger than the source's comparison value
$\electric{\alpha}^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)\approx128.9$. Charged species contribute to the beta function above their thresholds:
$$
\electric{\beta}(\electric{e})
=\frac{\electric{e}^3}{12\pi^2}
\sum_{f:\,\mechanical{m}_f<\energy{\mu}}N_{c,f}\electric{Q}_f^2.
$$
Quarks have three colors; the up-type charges are $2/3$, down-type charges are $-1/3$, and charged leptons have charge $-1$. At the Z scale, the top quark with source mass
$\mechanical{m}_t\approx173\unit{GeV}$ is excluded. The active charge-squared sum is
$$
\sum_fN_{c,f}\electric{Q}_f^2
=3\cdot2\cdot\frac49
+3\cdot3\cdot\frac19
+3\cdot1
=\frac{20}{3}.
$$
The additional species increase the rate of running. The numerical difference from the source benchmark is
$$
134.47-128.9\approx5.6.
$$

> [!note] Threshold and matching scope
> The source attributes this difference to the remaining leptons and quarks. The charge sum at the Z scale cannot be used over the whole interval down to the electron mass: each threshold requires matching. Light-quark vacuum polarization also needs a hadronic treatment. This is a qualitative explanation, not a numerical derivation of 128.9 from the active-species sum alone.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The coupling in the charge-running numerical exercise. |
| $\mechanical{m}_e$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron mass | The 0.511 MeV reference mass. |
| $\mechanical{M}_Z$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Z-boson mass | The 91.2 GeV comparison scale. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The scale used to form the dimensionless coupling. |

| $\electric{\beta}$ | $1$ | QED beta function | The logarithmic scale derivative of the electric coupling or fine-structure constant, as specified by its argument. |
| $\mechanical{m}_f$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Charged-species masses | The masses that determine the active thresholds in the QED approximation. |
| $\mechanical{m}_t$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Top-quark mass | The source threshold estimate near 173 GeV. |
