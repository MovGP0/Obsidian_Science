## Exercise 10.3: Fixed point in the ε expansion

Consider $\signal{\phi}^4$ theory in $d=4-\epsilon$.
The dimensionless renormalized coupling is
$\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}=\signal{\lambda}\energy{\mu}^{-\epsilon}$.
Its one-loop beta function is
$$
\signal{\beta}(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}})
=-\epsilon\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}
+\frac{3\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^2}{16\pi^2}
+\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^3,
\epsilon\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^2).
$$

(a) Find the Wilson–Fisher fixed point
$\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*
=16\pi^2\epsilon/3+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$.

(b) Calculate $\signal{\beta}'(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*)$ and determine whether the fixed point is ultraviolet or infrared stable.

(c) Interpret the limit $\epsilon\to0$, or $d\to4$.
What happens to the fixed point?

## Solution 10.3 — Wilson–Fisher fixed point

This exercise uses $d=4-\epsilon$, rather than the $d=4-2\epsilon$ regulator convention in Solution 10.1. For the dimensionless coupling $\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}$, the fixed points satisfy
$$
\signal{\beta}(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*)=0,
\qquad
-\epsilon\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*
+\frac{3(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*)^2}{16\pi^2}=0.
$$
**(a)** The Gaussian fixed point is $\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*=0$. The nontrivial solution is
$$
\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*
=\frac{16\pi^2\epsilon}{3}+\mathcal O(\epsilon^2).
$$
**(b)** Differentiate the beta function:
$$
\signal{\beta}'(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}})
=-\epsilon+\frac{6\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}}{16\pi^2}.
$$
At the nontrivial fixed point,
$$
\signal{\beta}'(\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*)
=-\epsilon+\frac6{16\pi^2}\frac{16\pi^2\epsilon}{3}
=\epsilon>0
$$
at leading order for $\epsilon>0$.

A small displacement from the fixed point scales as
$\delta\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}\propto\energy{\mu}^{\,\epsilon}$ at leading order. For $\epsilon>0$, it decreases toward low energy and grows toward high energy. The Wilson–Fisher fixed point is therefore infrared stable in this coupling direction.

> [!note]
> This stability statement concerns the quartic-coupling flow on the critical surface. A mass perturbation is relevant and must be tuned to reach the critical theory.

**(c)** As $\epsilon\to0$ and $d\to4$,
$\tilde{\signal{\lambda}}^*\to0$. The Wilson–Fisher fixed point merges with the Gaussian fixed point. In this perturbative approximation, four-dimensional quartic scalar theory has no nonzero interacting fixed point. For $d=3$, setting $\epsilon=1$ gives a rough route to the Ising universality class; quantitative critical exponents require higher orders or nonperturbative methods.
