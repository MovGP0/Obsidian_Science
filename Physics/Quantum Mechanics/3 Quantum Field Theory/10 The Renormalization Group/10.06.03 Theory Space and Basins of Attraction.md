---
title: "Theory Space and Basins of Attraction"
---

> [!TLDR]
> RG flow acts in a space of theories. Theories approaching the same infrared fixed point share a universality class.

In the Wilsonian approach, all possible actions form an infinite-dimensional **theory space**.
The coordinates are the values of all possible couplings.
RG flow defines a vector field in this space, and its fixed points are stationary points of that field.

The basins of attraction of the fixed points divide theory space into regions associated with different **universality classes**.
Theories in the basin of a given infrared fixed point share its long-distance physical behavior, despite differences in microscopic details.

The comparison is between actions with the same field content, allowed symmetries, and chosen cutoff convention. Operators provide coordinates in this space; a change of coordinates does not by itself change the physics. Close to a fixed point, linearized flow separates perturbations that grow toward the infrared from those that decay.

A critical fixed point usually has relevant directions. Reaching it then requires tuning the corresponding parameters, such as the deviation from critical temperature or the mass deformation. Its attracting set is a critical surface, rather than an unrestricted neighborhood. Two theories on this surface can share critical exponents while retaining different short-distance correlations and observable normalizations. See [Tong, Statistical Field Theory, §§3.1–3.2](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/sft/sfthtml/S3.html).

Thus long-distance physics is organized into universal equivalence classes. This is the content of the renormalization-group description.
