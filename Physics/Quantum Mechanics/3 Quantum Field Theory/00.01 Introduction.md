> [!TLDR]
> Quantum field theory combines quantum mechanics and special relativity to describe particles and their fundamental interactions.

Quantum field theory is the basic language of modern particle physics and fundamental interactions. Dirac, Feynman, Schwinger, Tomonaga, Dyson, and many other physicists developed it during the twentieth century. It combines quantum mechanics with special relativity. Its direct result, the Standard Model of electroweak and strong interactions, is one of the most precisely tested physical theories.

This book presents quantum field theory in a rigorous and systematic way. It starts with the physical motivations and develops the required formalism step by step. It includes explicit calculations and a detailed discussion of the concepts. The aim is to guide the reader from nonrelativistic quantum mechanics to the modern formulation of gauge theories and then to the Standard Model.

The material follows an established sequence. The opening chapters explain the physical reasons for quantum field theory: the Klein paradox, negative probabilities, and relativistic nonlocalizability. They also introduce the covariant Lagrangian formalism and Noether's theorem. Three chapters then cover the canonical quantization of the fundamental fields: the scalar field, the Dirac field, and the electromagnetic field. These chapters discuss propagators, the Faddeev–Popov procedure, and physical polarizations.

The second part develops perturbation theory in the interaction picture, from the S-matrix and the LSZ reduction formula to Feynman diagrams. It then gives a detailed treatment of quantum electrodynamics, renormalization, and the renormalization group, through to asymptotic freedom.

The final part presents the path-integral formulation, non-Abelian gauge theories with Faddeev–Popov ghosts, quantum chromodynamics, and the Standard Model. It covers the matter content of the Standard Model, the Higgs mechanism, quark and lepton mixing, and the main experimental tests.

Each chapter contains worked examples, comments on important concepts, and exercises. A final chapter gives complete exercise solutions with explicit calculation steps. The appendices contain the conventions used for the metric, gamma matrices, and natural units. They also collect results on Clifford algebras and Lie groups, and integrals and formulas useful for perturbative calculations.

Unless stated otherwise, the text uses natural units,

$$
\energy{\hbar}=\mechanical{c}=1,
$$

and the Minkowski metric with signature $(+,-,-,-)$.

The book is intended for master's students in physics and early-career researchers who want to learn the concepts and calculation methods of quantum field theory to the level required for research.
