---
title: "Asymptotic States"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Asymptotic particle states behave as free states, while the interacting field has a different normalization and physical mass.

The earlier S-matrix treatment used free Fock states for incoming and outgoing particles. In an interacting theory, identifying the full field representation with the free one is more subtle.

Lehmann, Symanzik, and Zimmermann developed the reduction formula in 1955. It relates asymptotic particle amplitudes to interacting-field Green functions.

At $\signal{t}\to\pm\infty$, well-separated stable particles behave as free particles under the usual scattering assumptions. However, the interacting field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$ differs from a free field. Interactions change both field normalization and particle mass.

> [!note]
> The source names a “Haag–Ruelle theorem” for the obstruction to identifying free and interacting Hilbert-space representations. This terminology mixes two subjects: Haag's theorem concerns the unitary-equivalence obstruction, while Haag–Ruelle theory constructs asymptotic scattering states under suitable assumptions.

Here a stable particle means a state with a definite real mass that cannot decay into lighter allowed channels. The scalar reduction used below assumes an isolated one-particle pole with nonzero field overlap. A resonance that decays is therefore not an exact external asymptotic state in this construction, although it can appear internally in a scattering amplitude.

The separation is made with wave packets; momentum eigenstates are distributional limits. It is not a pointwise statement that the interacting field becomes a free field operator. In particular, [Collins's analysis of LSZ limits](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10923) explains that the usual asymptotic creation-operator limit is weak, in matrix elements of normalizable states. This distinction prevents the particle approximation from being mistaken for unitary equivalence of the full field theories.
