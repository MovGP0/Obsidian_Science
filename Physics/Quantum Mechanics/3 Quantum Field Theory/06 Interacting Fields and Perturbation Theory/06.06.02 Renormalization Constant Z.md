---
title: "Renormalization Constant Z"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The residue Z of the physical one-particle pole measures the interacting field's overlap with a normalized particle state.

The interacting two-point function has the Fourier form printed in the source:
$$
\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\tilde{\signal{G}}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p}),
$$
with an isolated stable-particle pole
$$
\tilde{\signal{G}}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i\signal{Z}}
{\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2+i\epsilon}
+\text{terms regular at this pole}.
$$
Here $\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}$ is the physical renormalized mass, and $\signal{Z}$ is the field- or wavefunction-renormalization constant. In the free theory, $\signal{Z}=1$ and $\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}=\mechanical{m}$.

The pole residue measures the overlap with the physical one-particle state:
$$
\langle\Omega|\signal{\phi}(0)|\mechanical{p}\rangle=\sqrt{\signal{Z}}.
$$
The state $|\Omega\rangle$ is the interacting vacuum, and $|\mechanical{p}\rangle$ is a relativistically normalized physical one-particle state.

> [!note]
> The source uses $d^4\mechanical{k}$ in the measure but $\mechanical{p}$ in the integrand. These labels must denote the same Fourier variable. Its first vacuum expectation uses $|0\rangle$; the interacting-vacuum interpretation is $|\Omega\rangle$.

Fix the state convention by $\langle\mechanical{\mathbf p}'|\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rangle=2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}'-\mechanical{\mathbf p})$, with $\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}=\sqrt{\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert^2+\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2}$. Then the stated overlap has no additional momentum normalization factor. Inserting a complete set of states into the two-point function separates this one-particle contribution from multiparticle contributions; its pole coefficient is the squared overlap.

The residue depends on the chosen field normalization. Multiplying the field by a real constant multiplies the two-point function and its pole residue by the square of that constant. LSZ removes this dependence from the particle amplitude. Thus $\signal{Z}$ is not a separately measured probability independent of the field convention. The free-limit check is that the pole and overlap reduce to those of the normalized free field.
