---
title: "Time-Evolution Operator and Dyson Series"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The interaction-picture evolution operator solves a time-dependent Schrödinger equation and generates the Dyson expansion.

Equation (6.1) is a Schrödinger equation with Hamiltonian $\energy{H}_I(\signal{t})$. Define the evolution operator by
$$
|\psi(\signal{t})\rangle_I
=U(\signal{t},\signal{t}_0)|\psi(\signal{t}_0)\rangle_I.
$$

The evolution operator satisfies
$$
i\frac{\partial}{\partial\signal{t}}U(\signal{t},\signal{t}_0)
=\energy{H}_I(\signal{t})U(\signal{t},\signal{t}_0),
\qquad U(\signal{t}_0,\signal{t}_0)=1.
$$
Formal integration gives
$$
U(\signal{t},\signal{t}_0)
=1-i\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_1\,
\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_1)U(\signal{t}_1,\signal{t}_0).
$$
Iteration gives the Dyson–Neumann series, also called the Born series:
$$
\begin{aligned}
U(\signal{t},\signal{t}_0)
={}&1+(-i)\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_1\,\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_1)\\
&+(-i)^2\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_1
\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}_1}d\signal{t}_2\,
\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_1)\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_2)\\
&+(-i)^3\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_1
\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}_1}d\signal{t}_2
\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}_2}d\signal{t}_3\,
\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_1)\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_2)\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_3)
+\cdots .
\end{aligned}
$$
The second-order region has $\signal{t}_2<\signal{t}_1<\signal{t}$, so the later operator acts after the earlier one. Symmetrizing gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_1
\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}_1}d\signal{t}_2\,
\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_1)\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_2)\\
&\qquad=\frac12\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_1
\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_2\,
\mathcal T\{\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_1)\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_2)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Time ordering

For bosonic operators,
$$
\mathcal T\{A(\signal{t}_1)B(\signal{t}_2)\}
=\begin{cases}
A(\signal{t}_1)B(\signal{t}_2),&\signal{t}_1>\signal{t}_2,\\
B(\signal{t}_2)A(\signal{t}_1),&\signal{t}_2>\signal{t}_1.
\end{cases}
$$
Later times stand to the left. For fermionic fields, each exchange contributes a minus sign; a permutation contributes its parity sign.

At order $n$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_1\cdots
\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}_{n-1}}d\signal{t}_n\,
\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_1)\cdots\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_n)\\
&\qquad=\frac1{n!}
\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_1\cdots
\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}d\signal{t}_n\,
\mathcal T\{\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_1)\cdots\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}_n)\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the compact Dyson formula is
$$
U(\signal{t},\signal{t}_0)
=\mathcal T\exp\left(-i\int_{\signal{t}_0}^{\signal{t}}
d\signal{t}'\,\energy{H}_I(\signal{t}')\right). \tag{6.2}
$$
