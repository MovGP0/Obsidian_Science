---
title: "Structure of the Diagrams"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Green-function diagrams are classified by whether their components connect external points or form vacuum bubbles.

The diagrams in an $n$-point Green function fall into three categories, illustrated in Fig. 6.1. Chapter 7 develops the diagram rules.

- **Disconnected:** The diagram splits into parts that are not joined to each other.

For a four-point example, points $\mechanical{x}_1,\mechanical{x}_2$ may form one component and $\mechanical{x}_3,\mechanical{x}_4$ another, with no connecting line.

- **Connected:** All vertices and external legs belong to one connected component. These give connected scattering contributions.
- **Vacuum bubbles:** Components without external legs, made entirely of internal lines. They multiply the external-point contributions in an unnormalized Green function.

![[QFT-06-01-Connected-and-Vacuum-Diagrams.svg|**Fig. 6.1** A disconnected contribution with a separate vacuum bubble, compared with a connected contribution. The blobs G and G_c denote the general Green function and its connected part.]]

To classify a graph, begin at an external point and follow all its lines. If this reaches every external point, the external structure is connected. A component that reaches no external point is a vacuum bubble, even when it contains vertices and loops. A closed loop attached to an external structure is not a separate vacuum bubble.

In Fig. 6.1, removing the isolated vacuum component still leaves two separate groups of external points in the disconnected example. This is why cancellation of vacuum bubbles does not suffice to select a connected amplitude. Conversely, a connected graph may contain a loop or an internal line whose removal splits it: connected does not mean tree-level or one-particle irreducible.
