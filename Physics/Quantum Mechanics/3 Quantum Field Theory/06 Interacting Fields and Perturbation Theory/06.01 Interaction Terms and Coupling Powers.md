> [!TLDR]
> Interaction terms couple free fields and lead to a perturbative expansion for scattering and decay processes.

## Chapter introduction

> “The quickest way of seeing that these equivalences hold is to look at the perturbation expansion of any S-matrix element on the assumption that the interaction is small.”
>
> — F. J. Dyson, *The Radiation Theories of Tomonaga, Schwinger, and Feynman* (1949)

The preceding chapters constructed the scalar Klein–Gordon field, Dirac field, and electromagnetic field with free Lagrangians that are quadratic in the fields. Free theories are exactly solvable. They make it possible to develop canonical quantization and study the vacuum and Fock space without interactions.

A free theory describes particles that propagate without interacting. Scattering, pair production, pion decay, and quark production instead require couplings between fields. Adding interaction terms generally removes exact solvability.

The field equations become nonlinear and generally lack closed-form solutions. For sufficiently small coupling constants, perturbation theory treats the interaction as a correction to the free theory and expands physical quantities in powers of the coupling.

This chapter develops covariant perturbation theory. The interaction picture separates free evolution from interaction effects. The S-matrix describes scattering and decay. The Dyson formula expresses its perturbative expansion. Wick's theorem reduces time-ordered products to normal-ordered terms and propagators. LSZ reduction connects Green functions with measurable S-matrix elements. The final sections discuss cross sections and decay rates.

Chapter 7 translates this structure into Feynman diagrams and derives the corresponding rules.

## Interaction terms and coupling powers

Start with the free scalar Klein–Gordon density:
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_0
=\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})(\partial^\mu\signal{\phi})
-\frac12\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\phi}^2.
$$

A simple scalar self-interaction is proportional to $\signal{\phi}^3$ or $\signal{\phi}^4$. A cubic potential on its own is unbounded below, so the source chooses
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\energy{\mathcal L}_0-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\signal{\phi}^4.
$$
This is $\signal{\phi}^4$ theory. The coupling $\lambda$ is dimensionless in four spacetime dimensions. The factor $1/4!$ is a convention that cancels combinatorial multiplicities.

## Mass dimensions and power counting

In natural units, the action is dimensionless. In four spacetime dimensions, $[d^4x]=-4$ and $[\mathcal L]=4$ denote mass dimensions. Scalar and vector fields have dimension one; a Dirac field has dimension $3/2$.

For a scalar interaction $g\signal{\phi}^n$, the coupling has mass dimension $[g]=4-n$:

| Coupling mass dimension | Power-counting class |
| --- | --- |
| $[g]>0$ | Super-renormalizable. |
| $[g]=0$ | Renormalizable by power counting. |
| $[g]<0$ | Nonrenormalizable by power counting. |

The principal examples listed by the source are:

- **Scalar quartic interaction:** $\lambda\signal{\phi}^4$, with dimensionless $\lambda$.
- **Yukawa interaction:** $g\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}\signal{\phi}$, with dimensionless Yukawa coupling $g$.
- **QED:** $\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}\electric{A}_\mu$, with dimensionless $\electric{e}$. More precisely, the expansion strength is $\electric{\alpha}=\electric{e}^{\,2}/(4\pi)\approx1/137$.

> [!note]
> The source calls the quartic interaction the only renormalizable scalar self-interaction. A cubic scalar interaction is also super-renormalizable by power counting in four dimensions; the separate issue is that a purely cubic potential is unstable.
>
> The source also says that only renormalizable theories can have a consistent perturbative definition. Nonrenormalizable interactions can be treated consistently as effective field theories order by order in a low-energy expansion. The chapter concentrates on renormalizable models.

These models recur in the following chapters. The interaction density $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}$ makes the equations nonlinear. For the quartic scalar theory,
$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{\phi}
=-\frac{\lambda}{3!}\signal{\phi}^3.
$$
Generic interacting solutions are not available in closed form. For $\lambda\ll1$, expand quantities perturbatively in powers of $\lambda$.

| $\energy{\mathcal L}_0$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Free Lagrangian density | The quadratic scalar-field density. |
