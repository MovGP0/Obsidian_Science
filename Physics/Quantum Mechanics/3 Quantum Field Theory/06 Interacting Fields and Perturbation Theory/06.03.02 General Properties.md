> [!TLDR]
> Unitarity, Lorentz invariance, and CPT symmetry constrain the S-matrix and its physical amplitudes.

## Unitarity

The S-matrix satisfies $\mathcal S^\dagger\mathcal S=\mathcal S\mathcal S^\dagger=1$, which conserves total probability. In amplitude notation, unitarity gives the optical theorem:
$$
2\operatorname{Im}\signal{\mathcal M}(i\to i)
=\sum_f\int d\signal{\Phi}_f\,
\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}(i\to f)\rvert^2.
$$
The imaginary part of the forward amplitude is related to the total cross section. The sum and phase-space integral run over all final states allowed by four-momentum conservation.

## Lorentz invariance

The S-matrix commutes with Poincaré transformations. This constrains the Lorentz-scalar or Lorentz-tensor structure of the amplitudes.

## CPT invariance

For a local, Lorentz-invariant, unitary theory under the CPT theorem's assumptions, scattering amplitudes obey the relations induced by combined charge conjugation, parity, and time reversal.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}$ | — | Invariant amplitude | The scattering amplitude; its unit depends on the external-state count. |
| $d\signal{\Phi}_f$ | — | Invariant phase-space element | The final-state measure; its mass dimension depends on the number of final particles. |
