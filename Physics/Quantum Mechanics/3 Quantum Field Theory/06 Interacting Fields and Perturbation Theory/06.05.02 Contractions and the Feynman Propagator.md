---
title: "Contractions and the Feynman Propagator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A free-field contraction is the difference between time ordering and normal ordering.

The difference between time ordering and normal ordering is the basis of Wick's theorem. Use an overbrace to denote a contraction:
$$
\overbrace{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})}
\equiv
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
-\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}. \tag{6.4}
$$

For $\mechanical{x}^0>\mechanical{y}^0$, expand the fields and move the creation part left:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
&=[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})+\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{x})]
[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{y})+\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})]\\
&=\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})
+\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{y})
+\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{y})
+\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})\\
&=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
+[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})].
\end{aligned}
$$
The commutator is a c-number. Therefore,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overbrace{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})}
&=\begin{cases}
[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})],
&\mechanical{x}^0>\mechanical{y}^0,\\
[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{y}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{x})],
&\mechanical{y}^0>\mechanical{x}^0
\end{cases}\\
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}
\left[
\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
+\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)e^{i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\right]\\
&=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Its Fourier representation is
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i\,e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}
{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
Thus the contraction equals both the scalar Feynman propagator and the vacuum two-point function:
$$
\overbrace{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})}
=\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$

## Dirac-field contractions

For the Dirac field and its adjoint,
$$
\overbrace{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
=\signal{S}_F^{\alpha\beta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}}
{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$

Reversing the Dirac fields gives
$$
\overbrace{\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\signal{\psi}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
=-\signal{S}_F^{\beta\alpha}(\mechanical{y}-\mechanical{x}).
$$
The minus sign comes from fermionic anticommutation. Contractions of two $\signal{\psi}$ fields or two $\bar{\signal{\psi}}$ fields vanish for the free Dirac theory.

## Photon contractions

The source gives
$$
\overbrace{\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{x})\electric{A}_\nu(\mechanical{y})}
=\signal{D}_{\mu\nu}^{F}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$

> [!note]
> The source calls this the propagator in “Lorenz gauge.” The displayed numerator is specifically the covariant Feynman-gauge choice $\xi=1$; a general covariant gauge also has the longitudinal term in (5.21).

| $\signal{S}_F^{\alpha\beta}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Dirac propagator | The c-number contraction of a Dirac field and its adjoint. |
| $\electric{A}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon field | The electromagnetic four-potential in Feynman gauge. |
| $\signal{D}_{\mu\nu}^{F}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Photon propagator | The position-space photon contraction. |
