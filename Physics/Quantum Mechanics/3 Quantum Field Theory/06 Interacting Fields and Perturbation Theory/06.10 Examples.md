> [!TLDR]
> The worked examples use Dyson expansion and Wick contractions to obtain loop corrections, scattering amplitudes, and decay widths.

## Example 6.8 — Second-order corrected propagator in φ⁴ theory

Consider the two-point function to second order in the quartic interaction. Its normalized expression is
$$
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})
=\frac{\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\mathcal S\}|0\rangle}
{\langle0|\mathcal S|0\rangle}.
$$

The source displays the second-order term
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})\big|_{\lambda^2}
={}&\frac12\left(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\right)^2
\int d^4\mechanical{z}_1\,d^4\mechanical{z}_2\\
&\times\langle0|\mathcal T\{
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})
\signal{\phi}^4(\mechanical{z}_1)\signal{\phi}^4(\mechanical{z}_2)\}|0\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$
Wick's theorem contracts ten scalar fields: two external fields and four at each vertex. The source identifies three topologies:

- **Disconnected contribution with a vacuum bubble:** The external points connect to one vertex, while the other vertex closes into a separate vacuum component. The normalization denominator cancels this bubble.
- **Setting-sun diagram:** The first external point connects to one vertex, the second to the other, and three internal propagators join the vertices. This gives a second-order self-energy contribution.
- **Tadpole subgraphs:** Fields at one vertex contract with each other and contribute to mass renormalization.

The momentum-space propagator is written in Dyson-resummed form:
$$
\tilde{\signal{G}}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i}{\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2
-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^{\,2})+i\epsilon}.
$$
The self-energy is calculated from loop integrals. The pole shifts to the physical mass:
$$
\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2
=\mechanical{m}^2+\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2).
$$

> [!note]
> The displayed second-order integral is the numerator contribution before the vacuum-normalization subtraction. The full normalized coefficient also includes the denominator expansion.
>
> The source calls the setting-sun term the first nontrivial propagator correction. In an un-normal-ordered quartic theory, a tadpole already contributes at first order in $\lambda$. The setting-sun diagram is a second-order, momentum-dependent correction.

## Example 6.9 — Leading two-to-two Yukawa amplitude

The source uses the Yukawa density
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not\partial-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}
+\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})^2
+\frac{\mechanical{\mu}^2}{2}\signal{\phi}^2
-g\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}\signal{\phi}.
$$

> [!note]
> The positive scalar mass term is retained as printed. With the chapter's metric and kinetic convention, a stable free scalar of mass $\mechanical{\mu}$ normally has $-\mechanical{\mu}^2\signal{\phi}^2/2$. The displayed positive sign instead gives negative scalar mass squared if $\mechanical{\mu}^2>0$.

Calculate $\mathrm f(\mechanical{p}_1)\mathrm f(\mechanical{p}_2)\to
\mathrm f(\mechanical{p}_3)\mathrm f(\mechanical{p}_4)$ to the first nonzero order, $g^2$, the Born approximation. The interaction density is $\energy{\mathcal H}_I=g\bar{\signal{\psi}}_I\signal{\psi}_I\signal{\phi}_I$.

The second-order matrix element is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\langle\mechanical{p}_3,\mechanical{p}_4;s_3,s_4|
\mathcal S^{(2)}|\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2;s_1,s_2\rangle\\
&=\frac{(-ig)^2}{2}\int d^4\mechanical{z}\,d^4\mechanical{w}\,
\langle\mechanical{p}_3,\mechanical{p}_4|
\mathcal T\{
\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{z})\,
\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{w})
\}|\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$
All three fields in each interaction product are evaluated at its displayed vertex point.

The scalar contraction between the vertices gives $\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{z}-\mechanical{w})$. The fermion fields attach to the external states. After integration,
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}
=(-ig)^2i\left[
\frac{\bar u_3u_1\,\bar u_4u_2}
{(\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_3)^2-\mechanical{\mu}^2}
-\frac{\bar u_4u_1\,\bar u_3u_2}
{(\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_4)^2-\mechanical{\mu}^2}
\right].
$$
Here $u_i=u(\mechanical{p}_i,s_i)$. The explicit $i$ comes from the scalar propagator $i/(\mechanical{q}^{\,2}-\mechanical{\mu}^2)$. The first term is $t$-channel exchange with $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_3$. The second is crossed $u$-channel exchange. Its relative minus sign follows from fermionic statistics.

> [!note]
> This amplitude uses the conventional scalar pole at $\mechanical{q}^{\,2}=\mechanical{\mu}^2$, consistent with the negative scalar mass term discussed above. The printed positive mass term in the source Lagrangian does not give that propagator. The usual Feynman boundary prescription is implicit in these exchange denominators.
