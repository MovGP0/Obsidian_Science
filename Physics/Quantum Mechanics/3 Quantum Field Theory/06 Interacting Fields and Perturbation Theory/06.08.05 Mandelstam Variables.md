title: "Mandelstam Variables"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Mandelstam invariants describe the collision energy and the two momentum-transfer channels of two-body scattering.

For two-to-two scattering with masses $\mechanical{m}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{m}_4$, define
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{s}
&=(\mechanical{p}_1+\mechanical{p}_2)^2
=(\mechanical{p}_3+\mechanical{p}_4)^2,\\
\mechanical{t}
&=(\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_3)^2
=(\mechanical{p}_2-\mechanical{p}_4)^2,\\
\mechanical{u}
&=(\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_4)^2
=(\mechanical{p}_2-\mechanical{p}_3)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Four-momentum conservation gives
$$
\energy{s}+\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{u}
=\mechanical{m}_1^2+\mechanical{m}_2^2+\mechanical{m}_3^2+\mechanical{m}_4^2.
$$
Only two of the three invariants are independent.

- **s channel:** $\energy{s}=\energy{E}_{\mathrm{CM}}^2$ is the collision energy squared. Particles 1 and 2 combine into the intermediate channel, which produces particles 3 and 4. This is the direct channel.
- **t channel:** $\mechanical{t}$ is the transferred four-momentum squared. The exchange connects lines $(1,3)$ and $(2,4)$; the exchanged line has virtuality $\mechanical{t}$.
- **u channel:** $\mechanical{u}$ describes the crossed exchange channel, with pairs $(1,4)$ and $(2,3)$.

## Center-of-mass expressions

Use $\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2=\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i$ and $\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_4=\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f$. Let $\mechanical{\theta}$ be the angle between the initial and final momenta. Then
$$
\energy{s}=(\energy{E}_1+\energy{E}_2)^2=\energy{E}_{\mathrm{CM}}^2,
$$
$$
\mechanical{t}
=-2\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert^2(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})
\qquad\text{for elastic scattering with equal masses}.
$$

![[QFT-06-02-Mandelstam-Momentum-Channels.svg|**Fig. 6.2** The three channels of two-to-two scattering: s for annihilation and production, t for direct exchange, and u for crossed exchange. The dashed line is the exchanged virtual particle.]]

For the same equal-mass elastic case,
$$
\mechanical{u}
=-2\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert^2(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
The collision invariant is positive in the physical region: $\energy{s}\ge(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)^2$. Expressing $\signal{\mathcal M}$ through $\energy{s},\mechanical{t},\mechanical{u}$ gives an economical Lorentz-invariant description of the cross section.

> [!note]
> The source states $\mechanical{t}\le0$ and $\mechanical{u}\le0$ throughout the physical scattering region. Both signs hold in the equal-mass elastic case used here, but require qualification for general masses and reactions.

## Example 6.6 — Elastic scalar scattering kinematics

Consider $\signal{\phi}\signal{\phi}\to\signal{\phi}\signal{\phi}$ with common mass $\mechanical{m}$ and CM momentum magnitude $\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert$. Then
$$
\energy{E}=\sqrt{\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert^2+\mechanical{m}^2},
\qquad
\sqrt{\energy{s}}=2\energy{E},
\qquad
\energy{s}=4(\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert^2+\mechanical{m}^2)=4\energy{E}^2.
$$
The transfer is
$$
\mechanical{t}
=(\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_3)^2
=-2\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert^2(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})
=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
Here $\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert^2=\energy{E}^2-\mechanical{m}^2=\energy{s}/4-\mechanical{m}^2$. Similarly,
$$
\mechanical{u}
=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
For the invariant-sum check,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{s}+\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{u}
=4\energy{E}^2
&-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})\\
&-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The sum reduces to
$$
\energy{s}-(\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2)
=4\mechanical{m}^2
=\mechanical{m}_1^2+\mechanical{m}_2^2+\mechanical{m}_3^2+\mechanical{m}_4^2.
$$
As $\mechanical{\theta}$ runs from $0$ to $\pi$, the transfer $\mechanical{t}$ runs from zero in the forward direction to $-(\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2)$ in the backward direction.

## Example 6.7 — Elementary Compton kinematics

For $\mathrm e^-+\gamma\to\mathrm e^-+\gamma$, use incoming electron momentum $\mechanical{p}$, incoming photon momentum $\mechanical{k}$, and outgoing momenta $\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{k}'$. The electron mass is $\mechanical{m}_e$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{s}&=(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})^2
=\mechanical{m}_e^2+2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k},\\
\mechanical{t}&=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')^2
=(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{k}')^2
=-2\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{k}',\\
\mechanical{u}&=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}')^2
=\mechanical{m}_e^2-2\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{k}'.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the laboratory frame the electron is initially at rest, $\mechanical{p}^\mu=(\mechanical{m}_e,\mathbf0)$. With initial photon energy $\energy{\omega}$, $\energy{s}=\mechanical{m}_e^2+2\mechanical{m}_e\energy{\omega}$.

For final photon energy $\energy{\omega}'$,
$$
\energy{\omega}'
=\frac{\energy{\omega}}
{1+\energy{\omega}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})/\mechanical{m}_e}.
$$
This Compton shift follows from $\mechanical{p}'^{\,2}=\mechanical{m}_e^2$ and four-momentum conservation. Energy and angular frequency coincide in the natural-unit convention.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{s}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Collision invariant | The square of the total center-of-mass energy. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{u}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Transfer invariants | The direct and crossed transferred four-momenta squared. |
| $\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2,\mechanical{p}_3,\mechanical{p}_4$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momenta | The incoming and outgoing particle momenta. |
| $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2,\mechanical{m}_3,\mechanical{m}_4$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Particle masses | The four external rest masses. |
| $\energy{E}_1,\energy{E}_2,\energy{E}_{\mathrm{CM}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Energies | The incoming energies and total center-of-mass energy. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f,\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Center-of-mass momenta | The initial, final, and equal-mass elastic spatial momenta. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $1$ | Scattering angle | The angle between initial and final momenta in the stated frame. |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}$ | $1$ | Scalar scattering amplitude | The two-to-two amplitude as a function of the Mandelstam invariants. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The field for the identical external particles. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar mass | The common external particle mass. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | CM particle energy | The energy of each particle in the elastic equal-mass example. |
| $\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p}'$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron momenta | The incoming and outgoing electron four-momenta. |
| $\mechanical{k},\mechanical{k}'$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon momenta | The incoming and outgoing photon four-momenta. |
| $\mechanical{m}_e$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron mass | The physical electron rest mass. |
| $\energy{\omega},\energy{\omega}'$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon energies | The initial and final laboratory photon energies. |
