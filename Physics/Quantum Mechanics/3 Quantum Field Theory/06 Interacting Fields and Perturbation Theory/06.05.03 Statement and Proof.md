> [!TLDR]
> Wick's theorem sums all disjoint contractions of free fields, with the uncontracted fields left inside normal ordering.

## Theorem 6.1 — Wick, 1950

For $n$ free bosonic scalar fields, the time-ordered product is the sum of all normal-ordered products with every possible set of contractions, including the empty set:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2\cdots\signal{\phi}_n\}
={}&\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2\cdots\signal{\phi}_n\}\\
&+\sum_{\text{single contractions}}
\mathcal N\{\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_i\signal{\phi}_j}\,\cdots\}\\
&+\sum_{\text{double contractions}}
\mathcal N\{\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_i\signal{\phi}_j}\,
\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_k\signal{\phi}_l}\,\cdots\}\\
&+\cdots+\text{complete contractions}.
\end{aligned} \tag{6.5}
$$
Here $\signal{\phi}_i=\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_i)$. Each set contains disjoint pairs, and the dots stand for the remaining uncontracted fields. Complete contractions are c-numbers: products of Feynman propagators.

## Proof by induction

For $n=2$, the result is definition (6.4):
$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2\}
=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2\}
+\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2}.
$$

### Induction step

Assume the theorem for $n-1$ fields. Order the times as $\mechanical{x}_1^0\ge\mechanical{x}_2^0\ge\cdots\ge\mechanical{x}_n^0$. Then
$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_1\cdots\signal{\phi}_n\}
=\signal{\phi}_1\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_2\cdots\signal{\phi}_n\}.
$$
By the induction assumption, the last time-ordered product is a sum of normal-ordered terms with every contraction among fields $2,\ldots,n$. Write one term as $\mathcal N\{F\}$, where $F$ contains its uncontracted fields.

Split $\signal{\phi}_1=\signal{\phi}_1^++\signal{\phi}_1^-$. The creation part is already at the left:
$$
\signal{\phi}_1^-\mathcal N\{F\}
=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}_1^-F\}.
$$
Move the annihilation part through the creation parts in $\mathcal N\{F\}$, using
$$
\signal{\phi}_1^+\signal{\phi}_j^-
=\signal{\phi}_j^-\signal{\phi}_1^+
+[\signal{\phi}_1^+,\signal{\phi}_j^-].
$$
Each commutator is a contraction because the first time is the latest:
$$
[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}_1),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{x}_j)]
=\overbrace{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_j)}
=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_j).
$$
Hence
$$
\signal{\phi}_1\mathcal N\{F\}
=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}_1F\}
+\sum_{j:\,\phi_j\in F}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_j)
\mathcal N\{F\text{ with }\signal{\phi}_j\text{ removed}\}.
$$
Summing all induction terms gives every allowed contraction of $n$ fields. Contractions are c-numbers and can be taken outside normal ordering. This proves the theorem.
