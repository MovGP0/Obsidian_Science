title: "Definition"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The S-matrix is the interaction-picture evolution operator from the remote past to the remote future.

In the standard short-range scattering picture, particles are well separated as $\signal{t}\to-\infty$. They meet in a bounded spacetime region and separate again as $\signal{t}\to+\infty$. The asymptotic particles are treated as free.

Represent the in and out states as free-theory Fock states. The scattering operator is
$$
\mathcal S
=\lim_{\substack{\signal{t}\to+\infty\\\signal{t}_0\to-\infty}}
U(\signal{t},\signal{t}_0)
=\mathcal T\exp\left(-i\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}
d\signal{t}\,\energy{H}_I(\signal{t})\right).
$$
Its matrix element $\langle f|\mathcal S|i\rangle$ is the transition amplitude from initial state $|i\rangle$ to final state $|f\rangle$.

Separate the no-scattering contribution:
$$
\mathcal S=1+i\mathcal T,
$$
where $\mathcal T$ is the transition operator. For momentum eigenstates,
$$
\langle f|\mathcal S|i\rangle
=\langle f|i\rangle
+i(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{P}_f-\mechanical{P}_i)
\signal{\mathcal M}(i\to f).
$$

The quantity $\signal{\mathcal M}(i\to f)$ is the invariant Feynman amplitude, from which physical observables are calculated. The delta distribution enforces conservation of total four-momentum.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{t},\signal{t}_0$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Times | The asymptotic final and initial times. |
| $\energy{H}_I$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Interaction Hamiltonian | The interaction-picture generator in the Dyson formula. |
| $\mechanical{P}_i,\mechanical{P}_f$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Total four-momenta | The incoming and outgoing total momenta. |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}$ | — | Invariant amplitude | The transition amplitude after removal of the momentum-conservation distribution; its unit depends on the external states. |
