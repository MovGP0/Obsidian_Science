## Exercise 6.1 — Scalar contraction from canonical commutators

Use the scalar canonical commutators to verify explicitly, for $\mechanical{x}^0>\mechanical{y}^0$,
$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
-\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
Calculate $[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})]$ and check that it agrees with the Feynman-propagator integral.

## Exercise 6.2 — Six-field Wick expansion

Apply Wick's theorem to
$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_2)
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_3)\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_4)
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_5)\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_6)\}.
$$
Calculate $\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2
\signal{\phi}_3\signal{\phi}_4\signal{\phi}_5\signal{\phi}_6\}|0\rangle$.
How many terms occur? The supplied answer is $5!!=15$. Write all terms explicitly for the six insertion points.

## Exercise 6.3 — Dirac-field contraction

Repeat the derivation of the Dirac-field contraction.

Show
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overbrace{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}
\Bigg[
&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)
\sum_su_\alpha^s(\mechanical{k})\bar u_\beta^s(\mechanical{k})
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&-\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)
\sum_sv_\alpha^s(\mechanical{k})\bar v_\beta^s(\mechanical{k})
e^{i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\mechanical{k}^0=\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}
=\sqrt{\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2+\mechanical{m}^2}$. Verify that the result is
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=(i\not\partial+\mechanical{m})\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$

## Exercise 6.4 — Equal-mass CM identities

For two-to-two scattering with common mass $\mechanical{m}$, total CM energy $\sqrt{\energy{s}}$, and angle $\mechanical{\theta}$, prove
$$
\mechanical{t}=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}),
\qquad
\mechanical{u}=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}),
$$
$$
\mechanical{t}\mechanical{u}
=\frac{(\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2)^2}{4}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}.
$$
Also show $\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{u}$ at $\mechanical{\theta}=\pi/2$, or 90°.

## Exercise 6.5 — Incoming flux

Show that the incoming flux $\energy{F}=4\energy{E}_1\energy{E}_2
\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf v}_1-\mechanical{\mathbf v}_2\rvert$ can be written
$$
\energy{F}=4\sqrt{(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2
-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2}.
$$
*Hint:* Start from $\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf v}_1-\mechanical{\mathbf v}_2\rvert
=\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1/\energy{E}_1-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2/\energy{E}_2\rvert$ in a frame with $\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2=0$, then verify the invariant form.

> [!note]
> The simple velocity difference assumes collinear incoming velocities, including a target-rest frame. For arbitrary velocities the Møller flux also contains the velocity cross-product term; see [[06.08.02 Differential Cross Section]].

## Exercise 6.6 — Leading scalar cross section

\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}
=\frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi\energy{s}},
\qquad \energy{s}\ge4\mechanical{m}^2.

Check the dimensions.

## Exercise 6.8 — Scalar decay through a Yukawa interaction

Calculate the leading decay width for $\signal{\phi}\to\mathrm f+\bar{\mathrm f}$. The scalar has mass $\mechanical{M}$ and the fermion has mass $\mechanical{m}$. The leading amplitude is
$$
\signal{\mathcal M}=-g\bar u(\mechanical{p}_3)v(\mechanical{p}_4).
$$
The vertex is $-ig$; an overall sign does not affect the squared amplitude.

- Calculate
  $$
  \sum_{\mathrm{spin}}\lvert\signal{\mathcal M}\rvert^2
  =g^2\operatorname{tr}
  [(\not{\mechanical{p}}_3+\mechanical{m})(\not{\mechanical{p}}_4-\mechanical{m})]
  $$
  with the gamma-matrix trace identities.
- Integrate over two-body phase space in the scalar rest frame.
- Show that
  $$
  \energy{\Gamma}
  =\frac{g^2}{8\pi}\mechanical{M}
  \left(1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{M}^2}\right)^{3/2}.
  $$
  Verify that the decay is allowed only above threshold, $\mechanical{M}>2\mechanical{m}$.

## Exercise 6.9 — Optical theorem at second order

## Exercise 6.10 — Second-order vacuum diagram

Consider a quartic-theory vacuum diagram with two interaction vertices.

At each quartic vertex, contract all four fields into two tadpoles. The disconnected graph has two separate figure-eight components, four closed loops in total.

Find its symmetry factor $S$, defined as the order of the graph automorphism group. Verify the factor $1/S$ by explicitly counting Wick contractions:

- Eight fields have $7!!=105$ complete pairings.
- Of these, $3\times3=9$ have the required two separate figure-eight components.
- Combine this count with the Dyson factor $\frac1{2!}(1/4!)^2$.

The supplied answer is $S=128$, with a contribution proportional to $\lambda^2/128$.

## Source solutions

The following solutions are supplied in Appendix D of the source.

### Solution 6.1 — Time ordering and the Feynman propagator

For $\mechanical{x}^0>\mechanical{y}^0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
&=\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\\
&=[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})+\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{x})]
[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{y})+\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})].
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\signal{\phi}^+$ contains annihilation operators and $\signal{\phi}^-$ contains creation operators. Of the four expanded terms, only $\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})$ is not normal ordered. Use
$$
\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})
=\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})
+[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})]
$$
to obtain
$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
+[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})].
$$
The commutator is
$$
\begin{aligned}
[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})]
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}\,d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}'}{(2\pi)^6}
\frac{[a_{\mathbf k},a_{\mathbf k'}^\dagger]}
{\sqrt{4\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}+i\mechanical{k}'\cdot\mechanical{y}}\\
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}
{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}
\bigg|_{\mechanical{k}^0=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The mode energy is $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}=\sqrt{|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|^2+\mechanical{m}^2}$.
For $\mechanical{y}^0>\mechanical{x}^0$, exchange the two insertion points. Combining the two time regions,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}\Bigl[
&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
+&\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigr],
\end{aligned}
$$
with $\mechanical{k}^0=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}$.

Use the step-function representation
$$
\theta(\pm\mechanical{\tau})
=\pm\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\energy{\omega}\,
\frac{e^{-i\energy{\omega}\mechanical{\tau}}}{\energy{\omega}\pm i0}.
$$
Here $\energy{\omega}$ is the Fourier energy variable, not an additional on-shell mode energy. Combining the two time regions as in Chapter 3 gives
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
Thus the difference between time ordering and normal ordering for these two free scalar fields is the Feynman propagator.

> [!note] Correction to the source step-function formula
> The source prints correlated signs in both the exponential and denominator that put the pole outside the required contour for positive time. The representation above uses a common $e^{-i\energy{\omega}\mechanical{\tau}}$ and the pole prescription appropriate to each step function.

### Solution 6.2 — Wick's theorem for six fields

Six bosonic fields have
$$
(6-1)!!=5!!=5\cdot3\cdot1=15
$$
complete pairings. Every nonempty normal-ordered field product has zero expectation value in the free vacuum. Terms with fewer than three contractions therefore leave fields that give no vacuum contribution:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2\signal{\phi}_3
\signal{\phi}_4\signal{\phi}_5\signal{\phi}_6\}|0\rangle
=\sum_{\text{complete pairings}}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_{i_1}-\mechanical{x}_{j_1})
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_{i_2}-\mechanical{x}_{j_2})
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_{i_3}-\mechanical{x}_{j_3}).
\end{aligned}
$$
Fix the partner of field 1. There are five choices; the remaining four fields have $3!!=3$ pairings, so the total is $5\times3=15$.

Set $\signal{\Delta}_{ij}=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_i-\mechanical{x}_j)$. The fifteen terms are
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\signal{\Delta}_{12}\signal{\Delta}_{34}\signal{\Delta}_{56}
+\signal{\Delta}_{12}\signal{\Delta}_{35}\signal{\Delta}_{46}
+\signal{\Delta}_{12}\signal{\Delta}_{36}\signal{\Delta}_{45}\\
+{}&\signal{\Delta}_{13}\signal{\Delta}_{24}\signal{\Delta}_{56}
+\signal{\Delta}_{13}\signal{\Delta}_{25}\signal{\Delta}_{46}
+\signal{\Delta}_{13}\signal{\Delta}_{26}\signal{\Delta}_{45}\\
+{}&\signal{\Delta}_{14}\signal{\Delta}_{23}\signal{\Delta}_{56}
+\signal{\Delta}_{14}\signal{\Delta}_{25}\signal{\Delta}_{36}
+\signal{\Delta}_{14}\signal{\Delta}_{26}\signal{\Delta}_{35}\\
+{}&\signal{\Delta}_{15}\signal{\Delta}_{23}\signal{\Delta}_{46}
+\signal{\Delta}_{15}\signal{\Delta}_{24}\signal{\Delta}_{36}
+\signal{\Delta}_{15}\signal{\Delta}_{26}\signal{\Delta}_{34}\\
+{}&\signal{\Delta}_{16}\signal{\Delta}_{23}\signal{\Delta}_{45}
+\signal{\Delta}_{16}\signal{\Delta}_{24}\signal{\Delta}_{35}
+\signal{\Delta}_{16}\signal{\Delta}_{25}\signal{\Delta}_{34}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the Gaussian moment-pairing rule, also called Isserlis' theorem, for the six field insertions.

### Solution 6.3 — Fermion propagator from Wick's theorem

The Dirac contraction is the difference between time ordering and normal ordering:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overbrace{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
={}&\mathcal T\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}\\
&-\mathcal N\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $\mechanical{x}^0>\mechanical{y}^0$, the time-ordered product has the displayed field order. Split $\signal{\psi}=\signal{\psi}^++\signal{\psi}^-$, where the positive-frequency part annihilates particles and the negative-frequency part creates antiparticles:
$$
\signal{\psi}_\alpha^+(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}}
\sum_s b_{\mathbf k}^{\,s}u_\alpha(\mechanical{k},s)e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}},
$$
$$
\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^-(\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}}
\sum_s b_{\mathbf k}^{\,s\dagger}\bar u_\beta(\mechanical{k},s)
e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{y}}.
$$
Their anticommutator is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha^+(\mechanical{x}),\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^-(\mechanical{y})\}
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}
\sum_s u_\alpha(\mechanical{k},s)\bar u_\beta(\mechanical{k},s)
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}}
{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For reversed time order, the antiparticle modes give
$$
\{\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^+(\mechanical{y}),\signal{\psi}_\alpha^-(\mechanical{x})\}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{(\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}}
{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}
e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
Combining the two regions with the fermionic minus sign,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})_{\alpha\beta}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}\Bigl[
&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
-&\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)(\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}
e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
The on-shell energy is $\mechanical{k}^0=\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}$. The step-function integral representation converts this to
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
Finally,
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=(i\not{\partial}_x+\mechanical{m})\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}),
$$
because $i\not{\partial}_x e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
=\not{\mechanical{k}}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}$.

### Solution 6.4 — Mandelstam identities for equal-mass scattering

In the center-of-mass frame for elastic scattering of equal masses,
$$
\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2=\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i,
\qquad
\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_4=\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f,
\qquad
|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f|=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|.
$$
All four energies are equal:
$$
\energy{E}_1=\energy{E}_2=\energy{E}_3=\energy{E}_4=\energy{E}
=\sqrt{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2+\mechanical{m}^2}
=\frac{\sqrt{\energy{s}}}{2}.
$$

#### t invariant

Using the mostly-minus metric,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{t}
&=(\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_3)^2\\
&=\mechanical{p}_1^2-2\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_3+\mechanical{p}_3^2\\
&=2\mechanical{m}^2-2(\energy{E}_1\energy{E}_3
-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $\energy{E}_1\energy{E}_3=\energy{s}/4$ and
$\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2\cos\mechanical{\theta}$,
$$
\mechanical{t}
=2\mechanical{m}^2-\frac{\energy{s}}2+2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2\cos\mechanical{\theta}.
$$
Insert $|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2=\energy{E}^2-\mechanical{m}^2=\energy{s}/4-\mechanical{m}^2$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{t}
&=2\mechanical{m}^2-\frac{\energy{s}}2
+\left(\frac{\energy{s}}2-2\mechanical{m}^2\right)\cos\mechanical{\theta}\\
&=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
\end{aligned}
$$

#### u invariant and product

Similarly, $\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}_4=-|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2\cos\mechanical{\theta}$, so
$$
\mechanical{u}
=2\mechanical{m}^2-\frac{\energy{s}}2-2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2\cos\mechanical{\theta}
=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
Consequently,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{t}\mechanical{u}
&=\left(\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\right)^2
(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})\\
&=\frac{(\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2)^2}{4}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}.
\end{aligned}
$$
At $\mechanical{\theta}=\pi/2$, or $90\unit{^\circ}$,
$$
\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{u}=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}.
$$
As a consistency check,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{s}+\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{u}
&=\energy{s}
-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})
-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})\\
&=4\mechanical{m}^2,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is the sum of the four external squared masses.

### Solution 6.5 — Lorentz-invariant flux factor

In the target-rest laboratory frame, particle 2 is at rest:
$$
\energy{F}_{\mathrm{lab}}
=4\energy{E}_1\mechanical{m}_2|\mechanical{\mathbf v}_1|
=4\mechanical{m}_2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1|.
$$
The momenta are
$$
\mechanical{p}_2^\mu=(\mechanical{m}_2,\mathbf0),\qquad
\mechanical{p}_1^\mu=(\energy{E}_1,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1),
\qquad
\energy{E}_1=\sqrt{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1|^2+\mechanical{m}_1^2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2=\energy{E}_1\mechanical{m}_2,
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2
&=\energy{E}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2\\
&=\mechanical{m}_2^2(\energy{E}_1^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2)
=\mechanical{m}_2^2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
4\sqrt{(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2}
=4\mechanical{m}_2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1|=\energy{F}_{\mathrm{lab}}.
$$
The squared masses and the four-momentum scalar product are Lorentz invariant. This constructs the invariant continuation of the laboratory flux:
$$
\energy{F}=4\sqrt{(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2}.
$$
In the CM frame,
$$
\mechanical{p}_1^\mu=(\energy{E}_1,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i),\qquad
\mechanical{p}_2^\mu=(\energy{E}_2,-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i),
$$
$$
\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2=\energy{E}_1\energy{E}_2+|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|^2.
$$
Using the on-shell conditions gives
$$
(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2
=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|^2\energy{s},
\qquad
\energy{F}_{\mathrm{CM}}=4|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|\sqrt{\energy{s}}.
$$

### Solution 6.6 — Leading total cross section in quartic scalar theory

The first-order amplitude is constant, $\signal{\mathcal M}=-\lambda$. Before the identical-final-state factor, the CM differential cross section is
$$
\left(\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}\right)_{\mathrm{CM}}
=\frac1{64\pi^2\energy{s}}
\frac{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f|}{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|}
|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2
=\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2\energy{s}},
$$
where elastic scattering gives $|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f|=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|$.
Integrate over the full solid angle, $\int d\mechanical{\Omega}=4\pi$, and include $1/2!$ for the two identical final particles:
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}
=\frac12\,4\pi\,\frac{\lambda^2}{64\pi^2\energy{s}}
=\frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi\energy{s}}.
$$

In four dimensions $\lambda$ is dimensionless and $\energy{s}$ has the dimension of energy squared. Thus $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ has unit $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$, the natural-unit dimension of an area.

### Solution 6.7 — LSZ for the free scalar field

The free two-point function and pole residue are
$$
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}),
\qquad \signal{Z}=1.
$$
The source writes the following formal double-amputation expression for the $1\to1$ case:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{\sqrt{\signal{Z}}}\frac1{\sqrt{\signal{Z}}}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,e^{i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
\int d^4\mechanical{y}\,e^{-i\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{y}}
(\Box_x+\mechanical{m}^2)(\Box_y+\mechanical{m}^2)
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The Fourier transform is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,d^4\mechanical{y}\,
e^{i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}-i\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{y}}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')
\tilde{\signal{\Delta}}_F(\mechanical{p}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where
$$
\tilde{\signal{\Delta}}_F(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i}{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
Each Klein–Gordon operator gives minus the corresponding inverse on-shell denominator. Their product therefore gives
$$
(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)(\mechanical{p}'^2-\mechanical{m}^2)
\tilde{\signal{\Delta}}_F(\mechanical{p})
(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}').
$$
After one pole is canceled and the regulator is taken in the usual distributional sense, the remaining formal factor is
$$
i(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)(2\pi)^4
\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}').
$$

> [!note] Correction to the source LSZ conclusion
> The source labels the double-amputation expression as a connected matrix element, then claims that an on-shell double limit of this remaining factor produces the one-particle identity normalization. It does not: the free connected scattering contribution is zero. The identity part of the $S$ matrix is a separate external-state overlap. The single-particle pole residue $\signal{Z}=1$ fixes the free field normalization; it does not turn the displayed inverse-propagator factor into that overlap.

With covariantly normalized one-particle states, the full free result is
$$
\langle\mechanical{p}'|S|\mechanical{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{free}}
=\langle\mechanical{p}'|\mechanical{p}\rangle
=(2\pi)^3\,2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf p}').
$$
Thus $S=\mathbf1$ in the free one-particle sector, while the nontrivial connected scattering amplitude vanishes.

### Solution 6.8 — Scalar decay in Yukawa theory

The interaction density is
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}
=-g\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}\signal{\phi}.
$$
At first order in $g$, the decay
$\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{p})\to\mathrm f(\mechanical{p}_3)\bar{\mathrm f}(\mechanical{p}_4)$ has amplitude
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}=-ig\bar u(\mechanical{p}_3,s_3)v(\mechanical{p}_4,s_4),
\qquad \signal{\mathcal M}=-g\bar u_3v_4.
$$

#### Spin sum

Use the completeness relations
$\sum_s u\bar u=\not{\mechanical{p}}+\mechanical{m}$ and
$\sum_s v\bar v=\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s_3,s_4}|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2
&=g^2\sum_{s_3,s_4}\bar u_3v_4\bar v_4u_3\\
&=g^2\operatorname{tr}
[(\not{\mechanical{p}}_3+\mechanical{m})(\not{\mechanical{p}}_4-\mechanical{m})].
\end{aligned}
$$
Expand the trace:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}[(\not{\mechanical{p}}_3+\mechanical{m})(\not{\mechanical{p}}_4-\mechanical{m})]
={}&\operatorname{tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}_3\not{\mechanical{p}}_4)
-\mechanical{m}^2\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf1)\\
&+\mechanical{m}\operatorname{tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}_4-\not{\mechanical{p}}_3).
\end{aligned}
$$
A single gamma matrix has zero trace, $\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf1)=4$, and
$\operatorname{tr}(\not{\mechanical{p}}_3\not{\mechanical{p}}_4)=4\mechanical{p}_3\cdot\mechanical{p}_4$. Hence
$$
\sum_{\mathrm{spin}}|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2
=4g^2(\mechanical{p}_3\cdot\mechanical{p}_4-\mechanical{m}^2).
$$

#### Rest-frame kinematics and phase space

In the scalar rest frame,
$\mechanical{p}^\mu=(\mechanical{M},\mathbf0)$ and $\mechanical{p}_3+\mechanical{p}_4=\mechanical{p}$.
Therefore
$$
\mechanical{M}^2=2\mechanical{m}^2+2\mechanical{p}_3\cdot\mechanical{p}_4,
\qquad
\mechanical{p}_3\cdot\mechanical{p}_4=\frac12(\mechanical{M}^2-2\mechanical{m}^2).
$$
The spin sum becomes
$$
\sum_{\mathrm{spin}}|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2
=2g^2(\mechanical{M}^2-4\mechanical{m}^2).
$$
The final momentum magnitude and phase space are
$$
|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3|=\frac12\sqrt{\mechanical{M}^2-4\mechanical{m}^2},
\qquad
\frac{d\signal{\Phi}_2}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
=\frac{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3|}{16\pi^2\mechanical{M}},
$$
$$
\int d\signal{\Phi}_2
=\frac{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3|}{4\pi\mechanical{M}}.
$$
From $d\energy{\Gamma}=(\sum_{\mathrm{spin}}|\signal{\mathcal M}|^2)d\signal{\Phi}_2/(2\mechanical{M})$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Gamma}
&=\frac1{2\mechanical{M}}\,2g^2(\mechanical{M}^2-4\mechanical{m}^2)
\frac{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3|}{4\pi\mechanical{M}}\\
&=\frac{g^2(\mechanical{M}^2-4\mechanical{m}^2)|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3|}
{4\pi\mechanical{M}^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The fermion and antifermion are distinct, so there is no identical-particle factor $1/2!$.

Insert
$$
|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3|
=\frac{\mechanical{M}}2\sqrt{1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{M}^2}}
$$
to obtain
$$
\energy{\Gamma}(\signal{\phi}\to\mathrm f\bar{\mathrm f})
=\frac{g^2\mechanical{M}}{8\pi}
\left(1-\frac{4\mechanical{m}^2}{\mechanical{M}^2}\right)^{3/2}.
$$
The phase-space and spin-sum factors make the width vanish at threshold $\mechanical{M}=2\mechanical{m}$. The decay is kinematically open for $\mechanical{M}>2\mechanical{m}$.

### Solution 6.9 — Optical theorem at second order in quartic theory

The optical theorem relates the forward amplitude to all allowed intermediate states:
$$
2\operatorname{Im}\signal{\mathcal M}(i\to i)
=\sum_f\int d\signal{\Phi}_f\,|\signal{\mathcal M}(i\to f)|^2,
$$
with the identical-particle counting included in the final-state sum.

#### Cross-section side

At first order, $\signal{\mathcal M}=-\lambda$. Solution 6.6 gives
$\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}=\lambda^2/(32\pi\energy{s})$.
For incoming CM momentum magnitude $|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|$,
$\energy{F}=4|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|\sqrt{\energy{s}}$. Hence
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}\energy{F}
=\frac{\lambda^2}{32\pi\energy{s}}
\,4|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|\sqrt{\energy{s}}
=\frac{\lambda^2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|}{8\pi\sqrt{\energy{s}}}.
$$

#### One-loop side

The second-order s-channel bubble is
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm{bubble}}(\energy{s})
=\frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{2}
\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
\frac{i}{(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon},
$$
where $\mechanical{p}=\mechanical{p}_1+\mechanical{p}_2$ and $\mechanical{p}^2=\energy{s}$.
For $\energy{s}>4\mechanical{m}^2$, the two internal particles can go on shell. The two-particle Cutkosky cut gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
2\operatorname{Im}\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm{bubble}}
=\frac{\lambda^2}{2}
\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}(2\pi)^2
\delta_+(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)
\delta_+((\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
The factor one half is consistent with the diagram symmetry factor and with counting the two identical cut particles only once.
Here $\delta_+(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)=\theta(\mechanical{k}^0)\delta(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)$.

> [!note] Cut convention
> The source states a replacement $-2\pi i\delta_+$ for a cut propagator. That form applies to its denominator $1/(q^2-m^2+i0)$. For a propagator including the numerator $i$, the corresponding cut factor is $2\pi\delta_+$.

The on-shell delta integrals give the two-body phase space,
$$
\int d\signal{\Phi}_2
=\frac{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f|}{4\pi\sqrt{\energy{s}}}.
$$
For elastic equal-mass scattering, $|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f|=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|$, and therefore
$$
2\operatorname{Im}\signal{\mathcal M}_{\mathrm{bubble}}
=\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\int d\signal{\Phi}_2
=\frac{\lambda^2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|}{8\pi\sqrt{\energy{s}}}.
$$

The two sides agree directly:
$$
2\operatorname{Im}\signal{\mathcal M}
=\frac{\lambda^2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|}{8\pi\sqrt{\energy{s}}}
=\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}\,4|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|\sqrt{\energy{s}}
=\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}\energy{F}.
$$
This verifies the optical theorem at order $\lambda^2$.

### Solution 6.10 — Symmetry factor of the disconnected double figure eight

Consider the second-order vacuum diagram with two quartic vertices at $\mechanical{x}$ and $\mechanical{y}$. At each vertex, all four fields contract in pairs at that same point. Each component is therefore a figure eight made from two tadpoles. The graph has two disconnected components, two vertices, four internal propagators, and four closed loops. The quartic incidence relation gives $4V=2I$; for $V=2$, $I=4$.

The second-order Dyson term is
$$
S^{(2)}
=\frac1{2!}\left(\frac{-i\lambda}{4!}\right)^2
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,d^4\mechanical{y}\,
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}^4(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^4(\mechanical{y})\}.
$$
For the specified diagram, all four fields at $\mechanical{x}$ pair only with each other, and the same is true at $\mechanical{y}$.

At $\mechanical{x}$, the four fields have
$$
\frac1{2!}\binom42=3=3!!
$$
pairings into two unordered pairs. The vertex at $\mechanical{y}$ has the same three possibilities. Since the two disconnected components are independent, their Wick counts multiply.

There are $3\times3=9$ Wick contractions for this topology. Including the Dyson and vertex factorials, the coefficient is
$$
\frac9{2!(4!)^2}=\frac9{1152}=\frac1{128}.
$$
The symmetry factor is therefore $S=128$.

Each figure-eight component has eight automorphisms: reversal of each tadpole gives $2\times2$, and exchange of its two tadpoles gives another factor two. The two components give $8\times8=64$, and their exchange adds a factor two:
$$
|\operatorname{Aut}(\text{graph})|=8\cdot8\cdot2=128.
$$
This agrees with $S=|\operatorname{Aut}(\text{graph})|$.

Denote the vacuum functional by $\signal{\mathcal Z}_{\mathrm{vac}}$ to distinguish it from the pole residue $\signal{Z}$. The graph contributes
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\mathcal Z}_{\mathrm{vac}}
&\supset\frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{128}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,d^4\mechanical{y}\,[\signal{\Delta}_F(0)]^4\\
&=-\frac{\lambda^2}{128}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,d^4\mechanical{y}\,[\signal{\Delta}_F(0)]^4.
\end{aligned}
$$
This divergent vacuum contribution is absorbed in the normalization of the vacuum functional. Since the graph is disconnected, it does not appear in $\ln\signal{\mathcal Z}_{\mathrm{vac}}$, which contains connected vacuum diagrams. It is generated by exponentiating the connected contributions: it is the product of two first-order figure-eight components, with their exchange factor.
