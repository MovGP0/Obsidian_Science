title: "Synthesis of the Perturbative Program"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Perturbative predictions follow a sequence from the interaction density through Dyson expansion, Wick contractions, LSZ reduction, and phase-space integration.

The route from an interacting Lagrangian to predictions that can be compared with experiment has these steps:

1. **Identify the interaction:** Find $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}$ and the corresponding interaction Hamiltonian.
2. **Use the interaction picture:** The fields evolve freely and expand into the known creation and annihilation operators.
3. **Construct the S-matrix:** Use the Dyson formula (6.3) and expand in powers of the coupling.
4. **Evaluate matrix elements:** Apply Wick's theorem to the time-ordered products. The result contains propagators and normal-ordered operators; the latter provide external-state factors.
5. **Apply LSZ:** Connected amputated matrix elements give the invariant amplitude $i\signal{\mathcal M}$.

6. **Calculate the observable:** Square the amplitude, perform spin and polarization sums or averages, and integrate over final-state phase space using (6.7) or (6.8).

The Dyson expansion and Wick evaluation become expensive as the number of terms grows with perturbative order. Chapter 7 packages this bookkeeping into graphical Feynman rules, so amplitudes can be assembled from diagrams.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Interaction density | The Lagrangian term that defines the perturbative coupling. |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}$ | — | Invariant amplitude | The amplitude extracted from connected amputated diagrams; its dimension depends on the process. |
