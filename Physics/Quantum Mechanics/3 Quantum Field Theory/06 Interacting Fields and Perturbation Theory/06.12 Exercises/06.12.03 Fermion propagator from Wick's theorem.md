## Exercise 6.3 — Dirac-field contraction

Repeat the derivation of the Dirac-field contraction.

Show
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overbrace{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}
\Bigg[
&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)
\sum_su_\alpha^s(\mechanical{k})\bar u_\beta^s(\mechanical{k})
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&-\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)
\sum_sv_\alpha^s(\mechanical{k})\bar v_\beta^s(\mechanical{k})
e^{i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\mechanical{k}^0=\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}
=\sqrt{\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf k}\rvert^2+\mechanical{m}^2}$. Verify that the result is
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=(i\not\partial+\mechanical{m})\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$

## Solution 6.3 — Fermion propagator from Wick's theorem

The Dirac contraction is the difference between time ordering and normal ordering:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overbrace{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})}
={}&\mathcal T\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}\\
&-\mathcal N\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha(\mechanical{x})\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta(\mechanical{y})\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $\mechanical{x}^0>\mechanical{y}^0$, the time-ordered product has the displayed field order. Split $\signal{\psi}=\signal{\psi}^++\signal{\psi}^-$, where the positive-frequency part annihilates particles and the negative-frequency part creates antiparticles:
$$
\signal{\psi}_\alpha^+(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}}
\sum_s b_{\mathbf k}^{\,s}u_\alpha(\mechanical{k},s)e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}},
$$
$$
\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^-(\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{\sqrt{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}}
\sum_s b_{\mathbf k}^{\,s\dagger}\bar u_\beta(\mechanical{k},s)
e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{y}}.
$$
Their anticommutator is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{\signal{\psi}_\alpha^+(\mechanical{x}),\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^-(\mechanical{y})\}
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}
\sum_s u_\alpha(\mechanical{k},s)\bar u_\beta(\mechanical{k},s)
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}}
{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For reversed time order, the antiparticle modes give
$$
\{\bar{\signal{\psi}}_\beta^+(\mechanical{y}),\signal{\psi}_\alpha^-(\mechanical{x})\}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{(\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}}
{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}
e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
Combining the two regions with the fermionic minus sign,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})_{\alpha\beta}
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}}\Bigl[
&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
-&\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)(\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m})_{\alpha\beta}
e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$
The on-shell energy is $\mechanical{k}^0=\energy{E}_{\mathbf k}$. The step-function integral representation converts this to
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
Finally,
$$
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=(i\not{\partial}_x+\mechanical{m})\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}),
$$
because $i\not{\partial}_x e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
=\not{\mechanical{k}}e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}$.
