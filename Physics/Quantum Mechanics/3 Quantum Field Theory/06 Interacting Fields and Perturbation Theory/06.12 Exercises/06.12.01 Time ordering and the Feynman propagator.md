## Exercise 6.1 — Scalar contraction from canonical commutators

Use the scalar canonical commutators to verify explicitly, for $\mechanical{x}^0>\mechanical{y}^0$,
$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
-\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
Calculate $[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})]$ and check that it agrees with the Feynman-propagator integral.

## Solution 6.1 — Time ordering and the Feynman propagator

For $\mechanical{x}^0>\mechanical{y}^0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
&=\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\\
&=[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})+\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{x})]
[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{y})+\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})].
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\signal{\phi}^+$ contains annihilation operators and $\signal{\phi}^-$ contains creation operators. Of the four expanded terms, only $\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})$ is not normal ordered. Use
$$
\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})
=\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x})
+[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})]
$$
to obtain
$$
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}
+[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})].
$$
The commutator is
$$
\begin{aligned}
[\signal{\phi}^+(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}^-(\mechanical{y})]
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}\,d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}'}{(2\pi)^6}
\frac{[a_{\mathbf k},a_{\mathbf k'}^\dagger]}
{\sqrt{4\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k'}}}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}+i\mechanical{k}'\cdot\mechanical{y}}\\
&=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac{e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}}
{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}
\bigg|_{\mechanical{k}^0=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The mode energy is $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}=\sqrt{|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|^2+\mechanical{m}^2}$.
For $\mechanical{y}^0>\mechanical{x}^0$, exchange the two insertion points. Combining the two time regions,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\frac1{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}\Bigl[
&\theta(\mechanical{x}^0-\mechanical{y}^0)e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}\\
+&\theta(\mechanical{y}^0-\mechanical{x}^0)e^{+i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}
\Bigr],
\end{aligned}
$$
with $\mechanical{k}^0=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}$.

Use the step-function representation
$$
\theta(\pm\mechanical{\tau})
=\pm\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\energy{\omega}\,
\frac{e^{-i\energy{\omega}\mechanical{\tau}}}{\energy{\omega}\pm i0}.
$$
Here $\energy{\omega}$ is the Fourier energy variable, not an additional on-shell mode energy. Combining the two time regions as in Chapter 3 gives
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})}.
$$
Thus the difference between time ordering and normal ordering for these two free scalar fields is the Feynman propagator.

> [!note] Correction to the source step-function formula
> The source prints correlated signs in both the exponential and denominator that put the pole outside the required contour for positive time. The representation above uses a common $e^{-i\energy{\omega}\mechanical{\tau}}$ and the pole prescription appropriate to each step function.
