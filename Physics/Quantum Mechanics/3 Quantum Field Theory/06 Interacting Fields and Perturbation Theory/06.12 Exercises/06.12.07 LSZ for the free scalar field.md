## Exercise 6.7 — LSZ for the free two-point function

Use Theorem 6.5 to reduce the free scalar two-point function.

For $\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})$, verify that LSZ returns the expected free on-shell one-particle propagation normalization for the trivial $1\to1$ process.

*Hint:* Fourier-transform and identify the pole residue. The identity contribution retains the usual external-state normalization distributions.

## Solution 6.7 — LSZ for the free scalar field

The free two-point function and pole residue are
$$
\signal{G}^{(2)}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y})=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}),
\qquad \signal{Z}=1.
$$
The source writes the following formal double-amputation expression for the $1\to1$ case:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{\sqrt{\signal{Z}}}\frac1{\sqrt{\signal{Z}}}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,e^{i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
\int d^4\mechanical{y}\,e^{-i\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{y}}
(\Box_x+\mechanical{m}^2)(\Box_y+\mechanical{m}^2)
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\end{aligned}
$$
The Fourier transform is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,d^4\mechanical{y}\,
e^{i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}-i\mechanical{p}'\cdot\mechanical{y}}
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
=(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}')
\tilde{\signal{\Delta}}_F(\mechanical{p}),
\end{aligned}
$$
where
$$
\tilde{\signal{\Delta}}_F(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i}{\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
Each Klein–Gordon operator gives minus the corresponding inverse on-shell denominator. Their product therefore gives
$$
(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)(\mechanical{p}'^2-\mechanical{m}^2)
\tilde{\signal{\Delta}}_F(\mechanical{p})
(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}').
$$
After one pole is canceled and the regulator is taken in the usual distributional sense, the remaining formal factor is
$$
i(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)(2\pi)^4
\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p}').
$$

> [!note] Correction to the source LSZ conclusion
> The source labels the double-amputation expression as a connected matrix element, then claims that an on-shell double limit of this remaining factor produces the one-particle identity normalization. It does not: the free connected scattering contribution is zero. The identity part of the $S$ matrix is a separate external-state overlap. The single-particle pole residue $\signal{Z}=1$ fixes the free field normalization; it does not turn the displayed inverse-propagator factor into that overlap.

With covariantly normalized one-particle states, the full free result is
$$
\langle\mechanical{p}'|S|\mechanical{p}\rangle_{\mathrm{free}}
=\langle\mechanical{p}'|\mechanical{p}\rangle
=(2\pi)^3\,2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}
\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\mechanical{\mathbf p}').
$$
Thus $S=\mathbf1$ in the free one-particle sector, while the nontrivial connected scattering amplitude vanishes.
