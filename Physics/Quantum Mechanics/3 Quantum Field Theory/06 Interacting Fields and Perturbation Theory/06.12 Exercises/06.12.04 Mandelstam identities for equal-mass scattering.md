## Exercise 6.4 — Equal-mass CM identities

For two-to-two scattering with common mass $\mechanical{m}$, total CM energy $\sqrt{\energy{s}}$, and angle $\mechanical{\theta}$, prove
$$
\mechanical{t}=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}),
\qquad
\mechanical{u}=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}),
$$
$$
\mechanical{t}\mechanical{u}
=\frac{(\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2)^2}{4}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}.
$$
Also show $\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{u}$ at $\mechanical{\theta}=\pi/2$, or 90°.

## Solution 6.4 — Mandelstam identities for equal-mass scattering

In the center-of-mass frame for elastic scattering of equal masses,
$$
\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2=\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i,
\qquad
\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3=-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_4=\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f,
\qquad
|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_f|=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|.
$$
All four energies are equal:
$$
\energy{E}_1=\energy{E}_2=\energy{E}_3=\energy{E}_4=\energy{E}
=\sqrt{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2+\mechanical{m}^2}
=\frac{\sqrt{\energy{s}}}{2}.
$$

### t invariant

Using the mostly-minus metric,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{t}
&=(\mechanical{p}_1-\mechanical{p}_3)^2\\
&=\mechanical{p}_1^2-2\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_3+\mechanical{p}_3^2\\
&=2\mechanical{m}^2-2(\energy{E}_1\energy{E}_3
-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $\energy{E}_1\energy{E}_3=\energy{s}/4$ and
$\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}_3=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2\cos\mechanical{\theta}$,
$$
\mechanical{t}
=2\mechanical{m}^2-\frac{\energy{s}}2+2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2\cos\mechanical{\theta}.
$$
Insert $|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2=\energy{E}^2-\mechanical{m}^2=\energy{s}/4-\mechanical{m}^2$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{t}
&=2\mechanical{m}^2-\frac{\energy{s}}2
+\left(\frac{\energy{s}}2-2\mechanical{m}^2\right)\cos\mechanical{\theta}\\
&=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The cancellation of the energy transfer is specific to the equal-mass elastic CM frame: $\energy{E}_1-\energy{E}_3=0$. Thus the transferred four-vector has zero time component, so its square is nonpositive in this metric. This explains the sign of $\mechanical{t}$ without using the final formula. It also explains why the same argument cannot establish that sign for arbitrary inelastic masses.

### u invariant and product

Similarly, $\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf p}_4=-|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2\cos\mechanical{\theta}$, so
$$
\mechanical{u}
=2\mechanical{m}^2-\frac{\energy{s}}2-2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2\cos\mechanical{\theta}
=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
Consequently,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{t}\mechanical{u}
&=\left(\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\right)^2
(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})\\
&=\frac{(\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2)^2}{4}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}.
\end{aligned}
$$
At $\mechanical{\theta}=\pi/2$, or $90\unit{^\circ}$,
$$
\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{u}=-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}.
$$
As a consistency check,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{s}+\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{u}
&=\energy{s}
-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})
-\frac{\energy{s}-4\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})\\
&=4\mechanical{m}^2,
\end{aligned}
$$
which is the sum of the four external squared masses.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Common mass | The rest mass of each elastic external particle. |
| $\energy{s}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Collision invariant | The squared CM energy. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{u}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Transfer invariants | The two squared four-momentum transfers. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $1$ | Scattering angle | The angle between the initial and final CM momenta. |
| $\energy{E},\energy{E}_{1,2,3,4}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Particle energies | The four equal on-shell CM energies. |
| $\mechanical{p}_{1,2,3,4}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momenta | The external on-shell particle momenta. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf p},\mechanical{\mathbf p}_{i,f},\mechanical{\mathbf p}_{1,2,3,4}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Spatial momenta | The incoming and outgoing CM vectors and their common magnitude. |
