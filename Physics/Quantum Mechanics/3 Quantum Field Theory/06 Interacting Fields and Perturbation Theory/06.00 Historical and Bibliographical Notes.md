> [!TLDR]
> Dyson, Wick, and LSZ established the perturbative and scattering tools that connect covariant field theory with experiment.

The modern covariant perturbation framework developed rapidly between 1946 and 1950. In 1949, Freeman Dyson showed the equivalence of the Schwinger, Tomonaga, and Feynman methods and derived the formula that now bears his name.

Gian Carlo Wick published his theorem in 1950. It became standard notation, but its introduction greatly simplified calculations that had required long algebraic manipulations.

Harry Lehmann, Kurt Symanzik, and Wolfhart Zimmermann introduced LSZ reduction in 1955.

LSZ supplied the link between field-theory Green functions and experimentally accessible S-matrix elements. The source places its axiomatic justification in Haag–Ruelle work from 1958–1962.

For further study, the source recommends the corresponding chapters in Peskin and Schroeder, Weinberg, and Itzykson and Zuber. It also recommends Srednicki for a clear presentation of LSZ.
