> [!TLDR]
> Scalar LSZ reduction applies inverse free propagators to interacting Green functions and divides by the external pole residues.

## Theorem 6.5 — Lehmann–Symanzik–Zimmermann, 1955

Consider an interacting scalar theory with field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$ and pole residue $\signal{Z}$. LSZ gives the S-matrix element for $n$ incoming momenta $\mechanical{p}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{p}_n$ and $m$ outgoing momenta $\mechanical{p}'_1,\ldots,\mechanical{p}'_m$. Here $n$ and $m$ count external particles.

The coordinate-space formula, in the source convention, is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\langle\mechanical{p}'_1,\ldots,\mechanical{p}'_m;\mathrm{out}
|\mechanical{p}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{p}_n;\mathrm{in}\rangle\\
&=\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{\signal{Z}}}\right)^{n+m}
\prod_{j=1}^{n}
\left[\int d^4\mechanical{x}_j\,
e^{i\mechanical{p}_j\cdot\mechanical{x}_j}
(\Box_{x_j}+\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2)\right]\\
&\quad\times
\prod_{k=1}^{m}
\left[\int d^4\mechanical{y}_k\,
e^{-i\mechanical{p}'_k\cdot\mechanical{y}_k}
(\Box_{y_k}+\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2)\right]
\signal{G}^{(n+m)}(\mechanical{y}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{y}_m,
\mechanical{x}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{x}_n).
\end{aligned}
$$
The full interacting Green function is
$$
\signal{G}^{(n+m)}
=\langle0|\mathcal T\{
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y}_1)\cdots\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y}_m)
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\cdots\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_n)
\}|0\rangle.
$$
The vacuum here is the interacting vacuum, denoted $|\Omega\rangle$ elsewhere.

The Klein–Gordon operator uses the physical mass. In momentum space it supplies $-(\mechanical{p}_j^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2)$. On shell, this factor vanishes unless the Green function has a corresponding one-particle pole. LSZ extracts those pole residues and removes the external propagators.

## Momentum-space form

Use the convention that all momenta enter the Green function, so $\sum_j\mechanical{p}_j=0$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}\left(\sum_j\mechanical{p}_j\right)
\tilde{\signal{G}}^{(n)}(\mechanical{p}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{p}_n)\\
&\qquad=
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left[\int d^4\mechanical{x}_j\,
e^{i\mechanical{p}_j\cdot\mechanical{x}_j}\right]
\signal{G}^{(n)}(\mechanical{x}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{x}_n).
\end{aligned}
$$
The source writes
$$
\begin{aligned}
&i\signal{\mathcal M}(\mechanical{p}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{p}_n
\to\mechanical{p}'_1,\ldots,\mechanical{p}'_m)\\
&=\left.
\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{\signal{Z}}}\right)^{n+m}
\prod_{j=1}^{n+m}
[-(\mechanical{p}_j^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2)]
\tilde{\signal{G}}^{(n+m)}(\mechanical{p}_1,\ldots)
\right|_{\mechanical{p}_j^{\,2}\to\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The momentum-space Green function is not amputated. The multiplying factors extract its external one-particle residues; the minus signs come from the Fourier transform of $\Box$.

> [!note]
> The source uses a full Green function while writing the connected scattering amplitude. For the connected amplitude, use the connected part; full Green functions also retain disconnected scattering contributions. The source also calls the remaining amplitude “irreducible.” External-leg amputation does not imply one-particle irreducibility: a connected scattering amplitude can contain internal one-particle-reducible diagrams.

For the plane wave,
$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)e^{i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
=-(\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2)e^{i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}.
$$
In perturbation theory it is convenient to calculate the connected amputated Green function directly, by removing external propagators from connected diagrams. Each external full propagator has pole factor $i\signal{Z}/(\mechanical{p}_j^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2)$.

After cancellation of these poles, the practical formula is
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}
=\left.(\sqrt{\signal{Z}})^{n+m}
\tilde{\signal{G}}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{(n+m)}
\right|_{\mechanical{p}_j^{\,2}\to\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}^2}.
$$
Here amputation uses the full external propagators in the stated convention.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The interacting field in the Green function. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime point | The field argument. |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Pole residue | The field normalization factor for external particles. |
| $\mechanical{p}_i,\mechanical{p}'_k$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | External four-momenta | The incoming and outgoing on-shell particle momenta. |
| $\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{ph}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Physical mass | The pole mass used by each LSZ reduction operator. |
| $\mechanical{x}_j,\mechanical{y}_k$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Insertion coordinates | The spacetime arguments integrated in the reduction formula. |
| $\signal{G}^{(n)},\tilde{\signal{G}}^{(n)}$ | — | Green functions | Their dimensions depend on the field count and on position or momentum representation. |
| $\mechanical{p}_j$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Green-function momenta | The all-incoming momenta in the Fourier convention. |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}$ | — | Invariant scattering amplitude | The connected amplitude after external-pole reduction; its dimension depends on the process. |
| $\tilde{\signal{G}}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{(n+m)}$ | — | Amputated Green function | The connected Green function with full external propagators removed. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The mass in the Fourier-transform identity. |
