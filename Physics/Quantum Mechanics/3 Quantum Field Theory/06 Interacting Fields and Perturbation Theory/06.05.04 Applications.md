> [!TLDR]
> A free-field vacuum expectation value is the sum of complete Wick contractions and vanishes for an odd number of scalar fields.

The practical corollary is that the vacuum expectation of a time-ordered product of $n$ free scalar fields is the sum over all complete pairwise contractions. If $n$ is odd, it is zero.

## Example 6.2 — Two-point Green function

For $n=2$, Wick's theorem gives
$$
\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})\}|0\rangle
=\overbrace{\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})}
=\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
$$
The Feynman propagator is the two-point Green function of the free theory.

## Example 6.3 — Free four-point Green function

For $\signal{\phi}_i=\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_i)$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle0|\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2\signal{\phi}_3\signal{\phi}_4\}|0\rangle
={}&\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_2}\,
\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_3\signal{\phi}_4}
+\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_3}\,
\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_2\signal{\phi}_4}
+\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_1\signal{\phi}_4}\,
\overbrace{\signal{\phi}_2\signal{\phi}_3}\\
={}&\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_2)
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_3-\mechanical{x}_4)\\
&+\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_3)
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_2-\mechanical{x}_4)\\
&+\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_4)
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}_2-\mechanical{x}_3).
\end{aligned}
$$
This is exact in the free theory. The four-point function factorizes into a sum of propagator products, as in Isserlis's theorem, also called the stochastic Wick theorem.

## Example 6.4 — First-order quartic interaction

For two-to-two scalar scattering, calculate
$$
\langle\mechanical{p}_3,\mechanical{p}_4|\mathcal S^{(1)}
|\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2\rangle
=-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,
\langle\mechanical{p}_3,\mechanical{p}_4|
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}^4(\mechanical{x})\}
|\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2\rangle.
$$
For $\signal{\phi}_x=\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$, Wick's theorem gives
$$
\mathcal T\signal{\phi}_x^4
=\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}_x^4\}
+\binom42\signal{\Delta}_F(0)\mathcal N\{\signal{\phi}_x^2\}
+3[\signal{\Delta}_F(0)]^2.
$$
The factor $\binom42=6$ counts the choice of one contracted pair. The factor $3=\binom42/2=3!!$ counts complete pairings.

> [!note]
> Coincident propagators such as $\signal{\Delta}_F(0)$ require regularization. This is a formal composite-field identity until a regulator and subtraction prescription are specified.

The coincident term is a divergent constant; Chapter 9 treats it. The initial and final states each contain two particles. The normal-ordered four-field term supplies the connected four-leg contribution because its fields attach to the four external particles.

The leading connected amplitude is
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}(\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2\to\mechanical{p}_3,\mechanical{p}_4)
=-i\lambda.
$$
It is independent of momentum. Terms with coincident contractions instead contribute to lower-point or vacuum structures and to propagator corrections and renormalization.
