> [!TLDR]
> Phase space, Liouville's theorem, ergodicity, and entropy provide the foundations for equilibrium statistical mechanics.

The main results of this chapter are:

1. **Phase space.** A system of ${\mechanical{N}}$ point particles in three dimensions is represented by one point in a $6{\mechanical{N}}$-dimensional space. Its trajectory follows Hamilton's equations.

2. **Liouville's theorem.** Phase-space density is constant along trajectories: $\mathrm d\rho/\mathrm d{\signal{t}}=0$. Hamiltonian flow is incompressible, and the measure $\mathrm d\Gamma=\prod_i\mathrm d{\mechanical{q}}_i\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{p}}_i$ is invariant.

3. **Ergodic hypothesis.** In an ergodic system, long-time averages equal ensemble averages. Recurrence times for macroscopic systems can be so large that they do not affect accessible observations.

4. **Boltzmann entropy.** The expression ${\thermal{S}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Omega$ is additive for independent systems. Equilibrium maximizes the entropy under the specified macroscopic constraints. The relation $(\partial {\thermal{S}}/\partial {\energy{E}})_{{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}}}=1/{\thermal{T}}$, and corresponding derivatives, connect counting with thermodynamics.

5. **Gibbs entropy.** The more general formula ${\thermal{S}}_G=-{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\sum_i {\mechanical{p}}_i\ln {\mechanical{p}}_i$ applies to unequal probabilities. Maximization under appropriate constraints gives the equilibrium distributions of the canonical and grand canonical ensembles.

6. **Fundamental postulate.** All accessible microstates of an isolated equilibrium system have equal probability. Ergodicity and maximum entropy give complementary justifications under their stated assumptions; neither is a proof from mechanics without additional assumptions.

The next chapter uses these concepts to construct the microcanonical ensemble and calculate thermodynamic quantities for the monatomic ideal gas.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\mechanical{q}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinate | A Cartesian coordinate; generalized coordinates can have different units. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | Momentum conjugate to a Cartesian position coordinate. |
| ${\signal{t}}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | Elapsed time; starred or subscripted forms specify prediction or recurrence times. |
| $\rho$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^{-3N}}$ | Phase-space probability density | Density relative to dΓ for N point particles in three dimensions. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Boltzmann or Gibbs entropy, as specified by the context or subscript. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion factor between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system or level energy; ΔE is an energy interval. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume occupied by the system. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}_i$ | $1$ | Microstate probability | The probability assigned to accessible microstate i. |
| $\mathrm d\Gamma$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^{3N}}$ | Phase-space volume element | The product of all coordinate and conjugate-momentum intervals in three dimensions. |
