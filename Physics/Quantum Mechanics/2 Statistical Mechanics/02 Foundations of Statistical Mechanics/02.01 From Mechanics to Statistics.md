> [!TLDR]
> Statistics describes the measurable behavior of a system when a calculation of every particle trajectory is not useful.

The physical target is a reproducible macroscopic prediction, such as the pressure of a prepared gas. A microscopic state specifies one possible realization; a statistical state specifies probabilities over realizations consistent with the preparation. These are different descriptions of the same system.

Read the scale argument first, then the predictability argument. A large number of variables makes direct calculation impractical even when the motion is regular. Chaos gives a further limit when initial data have finite precision. Neither argument alone selects a probability distribution. That later choice must state what is held fixed and what information is available.
