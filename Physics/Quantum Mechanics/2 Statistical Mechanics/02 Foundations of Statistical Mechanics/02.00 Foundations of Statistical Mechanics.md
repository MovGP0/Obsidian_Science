> [!TLDR]
> Statistical mechanics connects microscopic laws with macroscopic behavior through probability, phase space, and entropy.

Statistical mechanics starts from a simple but difficult fact. We know the equations of motion of each particle in a gas. However, we cannot solve all these equations, and we do not need all their solutions.

The aim differs from that of classical mechanics. We want to **describe macroscopic behavior**, without calculating every microscopic detail. Probability theory and statistical analysis provide the tools for this task.

This chapter gives the conceptual and mathematical foundations for the rest of the book. It starts with the need for statistics and develops two definitions of entropy: the Boltzmann definition and the Gibbs definition. Their formulas differ, but they agree under the appropriate conditions. Phase space, Liouville's theorem, the ergodic hypothesis, and the fundamental postulate connect these ideas.
