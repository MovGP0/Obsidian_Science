> [!TLDR]
> Rescaling the oscillator phase space to a sphere gives its microstate count and the classical mean energy per oscillator.

## Example 2.1 — Microstates of classical harmonic oscillators

Consider ${\mechanical{N}}$ distinguishable one-dimensional harmonic oscillators. Each has Hamiltonian:

$$
{\energy{\mathcal H}}_k=\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_k^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}}+\frac{{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2{\mechanical{q}}_k^2}{2},
\qquad {\energy{E}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{\mathcal H}}_k.
$$

We calculate the phase-space volume with energy at most ${\energy{E}}$:

$$
\Phi({\energy{E}})=\int_{{\energy{\mathcal H}}\leq {\energy{E}}}\mathrm d\Gamma
=\int_{{\energy{\mathcal H}}\leq {\energy{E}}}\prod_{k=1}^{\mechanical{N}}\mathrm d{\mechanical{q}}_k\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{p}}_k.
$$

The condition:

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\mechanical{N}}\left(\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_k^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}}+\frac{{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2{\mechanical{q}}_k^2}{2}\right)\leq {\energy{E}}
$$

defines an ellipsoid in $\mathbb R^{2{\mechanical{N}}}$.

## Rescale to a sphere

Use the integration variables:

$$
P_k=\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_k}{\sqrt{2{\mechanical{m}}}},
\qquad
Q_k={\mechanical{q}}_k\sqrt{\frac{{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2}{2}}.
$$

Then:

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{\mechanical{N}}(P_k^2+Q_k^2)\leq {\energy{E}}.
$$

This is a sphere of radius $\sqrt {\energy{E}}$ in $2{\mechanical{N}}$ dimensions. For a sphere of dimension $d$ and radius $r$:

$$
V_d(r)=\frac{\pi^{d/2}r^d}{\Gamma(d/2+1)}.
$$

Each coordinate–momentum pair contributes the Jacobian:

$$
\mathrm d{\mechanical{p}}_k\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{q}}_k=\frac2{\signal{\omega}}\,\mathrm dP_k\,\mathrm dQ_k.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\Phi({\energy{E}})
=\left(\frac2{\signal{\omega}}\right)^{\mechanical{N}}\frac{\pi^{\mechanical{N}} {\energy{E}}^{\mechanical{N}}}{\Gamma({\mechanical{N}}+1)}
=\frac{(2\pi {\energy{E}}/{\signal{\omega}})^{\mechanical{N}}}{{\mechanical{N}}!}.
$$

## Density of states and entropy

The phase-space density of states is:

$$
g({\energy{E}})=\frac{\mathrm d\Phi}{\mathrm d{\energy{E}}}
=\frac{(2\pi/{\signal{\omega}})^{\mechanical{N}} {\energy{E}}^{{\mechanical{N}}-1}}{({\mechanical{N}}-1)!}.
$$

For a sufficiently narrow energy interval:

$$
\Omega\approx\frac{g({\energy{E}})\Delta {\energy{E}}}{{\energy{h}}^{\mechanical{N}}}
=\frac1{{\energy{h}}^{\mechanical{N}}}\frac{(2\pi/{\signal{\omega}})^{\mechanical{N}} {\energy{E}}^{{\mechanical{N}}-1}}{({\mechanical{N}}-1)!}\Delta {\energy{E}}.
$$

> [!note] Two different factorials
> The factorial in $\Phi$ comes from the volume of a high-dimensional sphere. It is not a correction for indistinguishable particles. The oscillators here are distinguishable, so there is no additional division of the state count by ${\mechanical{N}}!$.

For ${\mechanical{N}}\gg1$, use $\ln {\mechanical{N}}!\approx {\mechanical{N}}\ln {\mechanical{N}}-{\mechanical{N}}$ and ${\energy{h}}=2\pi{\energy{\hbar}}$:

$$
{\thermal{S}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Omega
\approx {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[\ln\frac{{\energy{E}}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}+1\right]
+O({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln {\mechanical{N}}).
$$

The energy-window terms are subleading under the usual thermodynamic scaling of a narrow window.

> [!note] Dimensional clarification
> The source writes the remainder as $O(\ln {\mechanical{N}})$. The factor ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ is included here so that the remainder has the unit of entropy.

To leading order:

$$
\frac1{\thermal{T}}=\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}\right)_{\mechanical{N}}
\approx\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{\energy{E}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad {\energy{E}}\approx {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

Each oscillator therefore carries mean energy ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$, in agreement with equipartition.

> [!note] Finite-system qualification
> The last result uses the large-system entropy approximation. For the shell count above at a fixed energy-window width, the exact energy-dependent term is $({\mechanical{N}}-1){\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln {\energy{E}}$. Its derivative gives $({\mechanical{N}}-1){\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}/{\energy{E}}$. The difference is negligible relative to the leading term as the system becomes large.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy function of the system or individual oscillator. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system or level energy; ΔE is an energy interval. |
| ${\mechanical{q}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinate | A Cartesian coordinate; generalized coordinates can have different units. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | Momentum conjugate to a Cartesian position coordinate. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle or oscillator mass. |
| ${\signal{\omega}}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The common oscillator angular frequency. |
| ${\energy{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | The action scale in semiclassical state counting. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Planck constant divided by 2π. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Boltzmann or Gibbs entropy, as specified by the context or subscript. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion factor between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| $\Phi$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^N}$ | Integrated oscillator phase-space volume | The phase-space volume below the specified energy. |
| $g({\energy{E}})$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^N\,J^{-1}}$ | Oscillator phase-space density of states | The derivative of integrated phase-space volume with respect to energy. |
