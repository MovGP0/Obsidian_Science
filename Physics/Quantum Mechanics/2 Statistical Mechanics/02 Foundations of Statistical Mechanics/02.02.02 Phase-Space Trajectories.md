> [!TLDR]
> Hamilton's equations define a unique phase-space trajectory from each initial state.

Hamilton's equations determine the evolution of a phase-space point:

$$
\dot {\mechanical{q}}_i=\frac{\partial{\energy{\mathcal H}}}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i},
\qquad
\dot {\mechanical{p}}_i=-\frac{\partial{\energy{\mathcal H}}}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,3{\mechanical{N}}.
\tag{2.1}
$$

The solution $({\mechanical{q}}({\signal{t}}),{\mechanical{p}}({\signal{t}}))$, with initial values $({\mechanical{q}}(0),{\mechanical{p}}(0))$, gives a **trajectory** in Γ-space.

## Proposition 2.1 — Uniqueness of trajectories

For an autonomous Hamiltonian ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ of class $C^2$, two distinct phase-space trajectories cannot intersect.

**Proof.** Suppose that two trajectories pass through the same point at the same time. They then have the same initial values. The uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations, also called the Cauchy–Lipschitz theorem, makes their solutions identical wherever the solutions exist. For an autonomous system, this excludes intersecting distinct trajectories. $\square$

> [!note] Scope clarification
> The source leaves the autonomous-system condition implicit. With a time-dependent Hamiltonian, uniqueness still applies at the same time, but paths drawn without the time coordinate can pass through the same phase-space point at different times.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{q}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinate | A Cartesian coordinate; generalized coordinates can have different units. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | Momentum conjugate to a Cartesian position coordinate. |
| ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy function of the system or individual oscillator. |
| ${\signal{t}}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | Elapsed time; starred or subscripted forms specify prediction or recurrence times. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
