> [!TLDR]
> In an ergodic system, long-time averages equal ensemble averages on the accessible energy surface for almost every initial state.

## Definition 2.4 — Ergodic system

A Hamiltonian system is **ergodic** on the energy surface ${\energy{\mathcal H}}({\mechanical{q}},{\mechanical{p}})={\energy{E}}$ if, for almost every initial point, the trajectory visits measurable regions with frequencies proportional to their invariant measure. Exceptional initial points can form a set of measure zero.

## Definition 2.5 — Operational ergodic hypothesis

An equivalent formulation states that the time average of each integrable observable $A$ equals its ensemble average on the energy surface, for almost every initial condition:

$$
\overline A
\equiv\lim_{{\signal{\tau}}\to\infty}\frac1{\signal{\tau}}\int_0^{\signal{\tau}} A({\mechanical{q}}({\signal{t}}),{\mechanical{p}}({\signal{t}}))\,\mathrm d{\signal{t}}
=\langle A\rangle_{\energy{E}}
=\frac{\displaystyle\int_\Gamma A\,\delta({\energy{\mathcal H}}-{\energy{E}})\,\mathrm d\Gamma}
{\displaystyle\int_\Gamma\delta({\energy{\mathcal H}}-{\energy{E}})\,\mathrm d\Gamma}.
$$

The delta distribution restricts the integral to the energy surface. It also supplies the correct weighting inherited from phase-space volume. This need not be uniform with respect to the unweighted geometric surface area.

> [!note]
> An infinite-time statement does not specify how long a laboratory measurement must last. Agreement over a finite measurement interval also requires a suitable relaxation or averaging time.
