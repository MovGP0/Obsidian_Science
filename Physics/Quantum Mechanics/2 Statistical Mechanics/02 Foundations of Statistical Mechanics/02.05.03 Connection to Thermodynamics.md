> [!TLDR]
> Derivatives of entropy define temperature, pressure, and chemical potential.

The microscopic entropy connects with thermodynamics through:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}\right)_{{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}}}=\frac1{\thermal{T}}.
$$

This relation defines temperature in microscopic terms. The corresponding relations for pressure and chemical potential are:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{{\energy{E}},{\mechanical{N}}}=\frac {\mechanical{P}}{\thermal{T}},
\qquad
\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{{\energy{E}},{\mechanical{V}}}=-\frac{\energy{\mu}} {\thermal{T}}.
$$

They follow by solving the fundamental thermodynamic relation for the entropy differential:

$$
\mathrm d{\energy{U}}={\thermal{T}}\,\mathrm d{\thermal{S}}-{\mechanical{P}}\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{V}}+{\energy{\mu}}\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{N}},
\qquad
\mathrm d{\thermal{S}}=\frac{\mathrm d{\energy{U}}}{{\thermal{T}}}+\frac {\mechanical{P}} {\thermal{T}}\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{V}}-\frac{\energy{\mu}} {\thermal{T}}\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{N}}.
$$

For the specified system, the thermodynamic internal energy ${\energy{U}}$ is identified with ${\energy{E}}$. Chapter 3 develops the explicit microscopic calculation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Boltzmann or Gibbs entropy, as specified by the context or subscript. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system or level energy; ΔE is an energy interval. |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | The thermodynamic internal energy or ensemble mean energy. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume occupied by the system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The thermodynamic pressure. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | The energy change per added particle under the stated constraints. |
