> [!TLDR]
> A normalized phase-space density assigns probabilities to microscopic states and defines ensemble averages.

Instead of one system, consider an **ensemble**: an imagined collection of infinitely many systems prepared in slightly different ways, all consistent with the same macroscopic information. These systems form a cloud of points in Γ-space.

## Definition 2.3 — Phase-space density

The phase-space density $\rho({\mechanical{q}},{\mechanical{p}},{\signal{t}})$ is a probability density in Γ-space. The product $\rho\,\mathrm d\Gamma$ is the probability of finding the system in the volume element $\mathrm d\Gamma$ around $({\mechanical{q}},{\mechanical{p}})$ at time ${\signal{t}}$.

Normalization requires:

$$
\int_\Gamma\rho({\mechanical{q}},{\mechanical{p}},{\signal{t}})\,\mathrm d\Gamma=1.
$$

The ensemble average of an observable $A({\mechanical{q}},{\mechanical{p}})$ is:

$$
\langle A\rangle({\signal{t}})=\int_\Gamma A({\mechanical{q}},{\mechanical{p}})\rho({\mechanical{q}},{\mechanical{p}},{\signal{t}})\,\mathrm d\Gamma.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{q}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinate | A Cartesian coordinate; generalized coordinates can have different units. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | Momentum conjugate to a Cartesian position coordinate. |
| ${\signal{t}}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | Elapsed time; starred or subscripted forms specify prediction or recurrence times. |
| $\rho$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^{-3N}}$ | Phase-space probability density | Density relative to dΓ for N point particles in three dimensions. |
| $A$ | $—$ | Observable | A measurable quantity with context-dependent units; B in correlation formulas is a second observable. |
| $\mathrm d\Gamma$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^{3N}}$ | Phase-space volume element | The product of all coordinate and conjugate-momentum intervals in three dimensions. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
