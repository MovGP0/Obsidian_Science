> [!TLDR]
> Gibbs entropy equals Boltzmann entropy when all accessible microstates have equal probability.

If all $\Omega$ microstates are equally likely, then $p_i=1/\Omega$. Substitute this value into the Gibbs formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\thermal{S}}_G
&=-{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\sum_{i=1}^{\Omega}\frac1\Omega\ln\frac1\Omega\\
&=-{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\,\Omega\,\frac1\Omega(-\ln\Omega)\\
&={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Omega={\thermal{S}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The cancellation uses both ingredients of a uniform distribution: there are exactly $\Omega$ allowed terms, and each has weight $1/\Omega$. Forbidden states carry zero weight and do not add to the count. This is a useful check when a degeneracy is included: every allowed microscopic state must appear once, even if several states have the same energy.

For two allowed states, the calculation gives ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln2$. If the same two-state space is prepared with certainty in one state, its Gibbs entropy is zero, while the macrostate count remains two unless the macrostate constraint is changed. Thus the exact equality proved here is conditional on uniform weights over the same set; it is not a claim that arbitrary distributions have identical entropy.

The two definitions agree exactly in this case.
