> [!TLDR]
> In a finite measure-preserving system, almost every state returns arbitrarily close to its initial state; macroscopic recurrence times can be extremely long.

## Theorem 2.2 — Poincaré recurrence (1890)

Consider a region of finite measure that is invariant under Hamiltonian flow. Almost every initial point returns arbitrarily close to its initial point infinitely many times.

> [!note] Statement clarification
> The source says that a point returns to an invariant region. Such a point never leaves that region. The meaningful recurrence statement concerns a return to a neighborhood of its initial state, or repeated returns to a specified measurable subset.

Recurrence follows from conservation of measure and the finite accessible measure. A rough estimate of a first return time to a small target region $\mathcal V$ is:

$$
{\signal{t}}_P\sim\frac{|\Gamma_E|}{|\mathcal V|}\,{\signal{t}}_{\mathrm{micro}}.
$$

Here $|\Gamma_E|$ is the total accessible measure at the specified energy, and $|\mathcal V|$ is the target measure. The same measure convention must be used in both.

For a macroscopic gas, the source uses a ratio of order $e^{\mechanical{N}}$ to illustrate recurrence times as large as $10^{10^{23}}$ years. It compares this with an age of the universe of roughly $1.4\times10^{10}$ years.

> [!note] Scope of the estimate
> The inverse-measure estimate is a heuristic here, not a universal return-time formula for every initial point. Quantitative mean-return results require appropriate assumptions about sampling and invariant components. The recurrence theorem itself does not require ergodicity.

The finite-measure condition does physical work. A freely escaping particle in unbounded space need not return; its position can increase without bound. A spatially confined system also needs a finite accessible measure at the chosen energy before this theorem can be used.

For a finite-measure system, recurrence still does not require the trajectory to pass through every allowed region. A periodic orbit returns exactly, yet can remain inside a small invariant part of a larger energy surface. Thus a return is weaker than representative sampling. The measure-preserving formulation and its exceptional sets are treated in [Green, Ergodic Theory, §2.5](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/ergodic-2015.pdf). The time estimate above must remain a heuristic unless a sampling model is specified.

Recurrence can appear to conflict with the second law: a spontaneous return to an ordered state would reverse macroscopic entropy growth. This issue is related to the Loschmidt and Zermelo paradoxes. Extremely long recurrence times explain why a macroscopic system can appear irreversible on accessible time scales.
