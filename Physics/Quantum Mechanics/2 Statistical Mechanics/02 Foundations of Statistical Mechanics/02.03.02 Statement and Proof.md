> [!TLDR]
> Conservation of probability and Hamilton's equations give zero change of phase-space density along each trajectory.

## Theorem 2.1 — Liouville's theorem (1838)

For a Hamiltonian system, the phase-space density satisfies:

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\rho}{\mathrm d{\signal{t}}}
\equiv\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\signal{t}}}+\{\rho,{\energy{\mathcal H}}\}=0.
$$

The **Poisson bracket** is:

$$
\{f,g\}
=\sum_{i=1}^{3{\mechanical{N}}}
\left(
\frac{\partial f}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}
-\frac{\partial f}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}\frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}
\right).
$$

Thus, $\rho$ is constant along each Hamiltonian trajectory.

## Proof

Consider any fixed region $\mathcal V$ in Γ-space, with boundary $\partial\mathcal V$. Its probability content is $\int_{\mathcal V}\rho\,\mathrm d\Gamma$. It changes only because probability flows through the boundary:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial {\signal{t}}}\int_{\mathcal V}\rho\,\mathrm d\Gamma
=-\oint_{\partial\mathcal V}\rho\,\mathbf v\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\,\mathrm d\Sigma.
$$

Here $\mathbf v=(\dot {\mechanical{q}}_1,\ldots,\dot {\mechanical{q}}_{3{\mechanical{N}}},\dot {\mechanical{p}}_1,\ldots,\dot {\mechanical{p}}_{3{\mechanical{N}}})$ is the phase-space flow, and $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is the outward normal. The symbol $\mathrm d\Sigma$ denotes the boundary measure, not entropy.

Apply the divergence theorem in $6{\mechanical{N}}$ dimensions:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial {\signal{t}}}\int_{\mathcal V}\rho\,\mathrm d\Gamma
=-\int_{\mathcal V}\nabla_\Gamma\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)\,\mathrm d\Gamma.
$$

Expand the divergence:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_\Gamma\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)
&=\sum_i\left[
\frac{\partial(\rho\dot {\mechanical{q}}_i)}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}
+\frac{\partial(\rho\dot {\mechanical{p}}_i)}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}
\right]\\
&=\sum_i\left[
\dot {\mechanical{q}}_i\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}
+\dot {\mechanical{p}}_i\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}
\right]
+\rho\sum_i\left[
\frac{\partial\dot {\mechanical{q}}_i}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}
+\frac{\partial\dot {\mechanical{p}}_i}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Hamilton's equations make the last sum zero. If ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ is of class $C^2$, its mixed derivatives are equal:

$$
\frac{\partial\dot {\mechanical{q}}_i}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}
+\frac{\partial\dot {\mechanical{p}}_i}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}
=\frac{\partial^2{\energy{\mathcal H}}}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i\,\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}
-\frac{\partial^2{\energy{\mathcal H}}}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i\,\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}=0.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial {\signal{t}}}\int_{\mathcal V}\rho\,\mathrm d\Gamma
=-\int_{\mathcal V}\sum_i
\left(\dot {\mechanical{q}}_i\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}
+\dot {\mechanical{p}}_i\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}\right)\mathrm d\Gamma.
$$

Because the region is arbitrary, the integrands satisfy the local equation:

$$
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\signal{t}}}
+\sum_i\left(
\dot {\mechanical{q}}_i\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}
+\dot {\mechanical{p}}_i\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}
\right)=0.
$$

Substitute Hamilton's equations:

$$
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\signal{t}}}
+\sum_i\left(
\frac{\partial{\energy{\mathcal H}}}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}
-\frac{\partial{\energy{\mathcal H}}}{\partial {\mechanical{q}}_i}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial {\mechanical{p}}_i}
\right)=0.
$$

This is $\partial_{\signal{t}}\rho+\{\rho,{\energy{\mathcal H}}\}=0$. $\square$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{q}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinate | A Cartesian coordinate; generalized coordinates can have different units. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | Momentum conjugate to a Cartesian position coordinate. |
| ${\signal{t}}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | Elapsed time; starred or subscripted forms specify prediction or recurrence times. |
| $\rho$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^{-3N}}$ | Phase-space probability density | Density relative to dΓ for N point particles in three dimensions. |
| ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy function of the system or individual oscillator. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
| $\mathrm d\Gamma$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^{3N}}$ | Phase-space volume element | The product of all coordinate and conjugate-momentum intervals in three dimensions. |
| $\mathbf v$ | $—$ | Phase-space flow velocity | The combined coordinate and momentum rates; its components have different units. |
