> [!TLDR]
> The ergodic hypothesis connects a long-time measurement on one system with an ensemble average.

The practical issue is whether one measured system samples enough representative states during the observation time. This requires more than the statement that mechanics preserves volume. Start with the two kinds of averages, then read the infinite-time hypothesis and its finite-time qualification.

Recurrence and equilibration answer different questions. Recurrence asks whether states return near earlier states. Equilibration asks whether selected macroscopic observables settle near stable values on the time scale of interest. The critical discussion gives reasons why a many-particle Hamiltonian need not be ergodic on the whole energy surface. Use it before treating a formal infinite-time identity as a laboratory prediction.
