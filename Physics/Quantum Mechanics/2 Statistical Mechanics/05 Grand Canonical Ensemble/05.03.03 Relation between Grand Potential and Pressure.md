> [!TLDR]
> For an extensive homogeneous system, the grand potential equals minus pressure times volume.

**Proposition 5.2.** For a homogeneous extensive system:

$$
{\energy{\Phi}}=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}.\tag{5.7}
$$

**Proof.** Insert Euler's relation into ${\energy{\Phi}}={\energy{U}}-{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{S}}-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}$:

$$
{\energy{U}}={\thermal{T}}{\thermal{S}}-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}+{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
{\energy{\Phi}}={\thermal{T}}{\thermal{S}}-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}+{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}-{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{S}}-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}.
$$

It follows that:

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=-\frac{\energy{\Phi}} {\mechanical{V}}=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}\ln\Xi.\tag{5.8}
$$

This relation provides an equation of state once the grand partition function is known.

> [!note] Range of Eq. (5.7)
> The Euler step assumes extensivity and a homogeneous bulk system. Surface terms, finite-size effects, and long-range interactions can require additional terms. The derivative definition of pressure in [[05.03.02 Grand Potential]] is the more general starting point.
