> [!TLDR]
> An open system exchanges energy and particles with a reservoir at fixed temperature and chemical potential.

The microcanonical and canonical descriptions keep the particle number ${\mechanical{N}}$ fixed. This is useful for closed systems. Chemical reactions, adsorption, phase equilibrium, and particle creation or destruction require a description in which this number can change.

The **grand canonical ensemble** permits both energy and particle exchange with a reservoir. The temperature ${\thermal{T}}$, or inverse temperature $\beta=1/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})$, controls the energy distribution. The chemical potential ${\energy{\mu}}$ controls the particle distribution.

This chapter develops the chemical potential, derives the grand canonical distribution, and defines the grand partition function and grand potential. It then connects particle-number fluctuations to compressibility and compares the three ensembles. Applications include the classical ideal gas, the Langmuir adsorption model, and chemical equilibrium.
