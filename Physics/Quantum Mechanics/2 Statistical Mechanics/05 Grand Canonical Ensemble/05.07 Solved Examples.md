> [!TLDR]
> These examples apply the grand partition function to molecules, adsorption sites, and a formal fluctuating-oscillator model.

## Example 5.4 — Diatomic ideal gas

Take a diatomic gas with classical rotation. Its one-molecule partition function is:

$$
\xi_1=\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}z_{\mathrm{rot}},\qquad
z_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac {\thermal{T}}{{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}},\qquad
{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2}{2{\mechanical{I}} {\thermal{k}_{B}}}.
$$

The rotational expression assumes the rotor convention used in Chapter 4; a molecular symmetry factor can be required for a specific species. Classical indistinguishable molecules give:

$$
\Xi=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{(z\xi_1)^{\mechanical{N}}}{{\mechanical{N}}!}
=\exp\left(\frac{z{\mechanical{V}} z_{\mathrm{rot}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}\right).
$$

Thus:

$$
\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle=\frac{z{\mechanical{V}} z_{\mathrm{rot}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3},\qquad
z=\frac{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}{{\mechanical{V}} z_{\mathrm{rot}}}.
$$

The factor $\xi_1\propto {\thermal{T}}^{5/2}$ gives:

$$
\frac{\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle}{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}
=-\frac{\partial\ln\xi_1}{\partial\beta}
=\frac32 {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}+{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}=\frac52 {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}.
$$

This is equipartition for three translational and two rotational quadratic terms.

## Example 5.5 — Degenerate adsorption sites

Suppose an occupied site has $g$ states with the same energy, for example two molecular orientations. Empty sites have one state:

$$
\xi_{\mathrm{site}}=1+g e^{\beta({\energy{\mu}}+{\energy{\varepsilon}}_b)},\qquad
\theta=\frac{g e^{\beta({\energy{\mu}}+{\energy{\varepsilon}}_b)}}{1+g e^{\beta({\energy{\mu}}+{\energy{\varepsilon}}_b)}}.
$$

Using the nondegenerate reference pressure from [[05.06.02 Surface Adsorption - Langmuir Isotherm]]:

$$
\theta=\frac{g{\mechanical{P}}/{\mechanical{P}}_0}{1+g{\mechanical{P}}/{\mechanical{P}}_0}=\frac {\mechanical{P}}{{\mechanical{P}}+{\mechanical{P}}_0/g}.
$$

Degeneracy lowers the half-coverage pressure from ${\mechanical{P}}_0$ to ${\mechanical{P}}_0/g$. More occupied microstates favor adsorption.

Because ${\mechanical{N}}_{\mathrm{site}}\in\{0,1\}$, its square equals itself. Therefore:

$$
(\Delta {\mechanical{N}}_{\mathrm{site}})^2=\theta-\theta^2=\theta(1-\theta).
$$

The maximum variance is $1/4$ at half coverage; the standard deviation there is $1/2$. This binary-occupation algebra does not require the adsorbed molecules to be fermions.

## Example 5.6 — Formal grand canonical Einstein model

The source permits the number of atoms in an Einstein solid to fluctuate. Each atom contributes three independent oscillators with frequency ${\signal{\omega}}$. The partition function of one oscillator is:

$$
z_{\mathrm{osc}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}(j+1/2)}
=\frac{e^{-\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/2}}{1-e^{-\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}}.
$$

The source assigns $Z_N=z_{\mathrm{osc}}^{3{\mechanical{N}}}$ and sums over ${\mechanical{N}}$ without a Gibbs factorial. Its formal grand partition function is:

$$
\Xi=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}(z z_{\mathrm{osc}}^3)^{\mechanical{N}}
=\frac1{1-z z_{\mathrm{osc}}^3},\qquad z z_{\mathrm{osc}}^3<1.
$$

Its mean number and fugacity are:

$$
\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle=\frac{z z_{\mathrm{osc}}^3}{1-z z_{\mathrm{osc}}^3},\qquad
z=\frac{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}{(\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle+1)z_{\mathrm{osc}}^3}.
$$

The mean energy per atom is:

$$
\frac{\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle}{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}
=-3\frac{\partial\ln z_{\mathrm{osc}}}{\partial\beta}
=3{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}\left(\frac12+\frac1{e^{\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}-1}\right).
$$

At fixed mean atom number, the Einstein heat capacity is:

$$
{\thermal{C}}_{\langle N\rangle}=3\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle {\thermal{k}_{B}}
\left(\frac{\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}{e^{\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/2}-e^{-\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/2}}\right)^2.
$$

It approaches $3\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle {\thermal{k}_{B}}$ at high temperature and $3\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle {\thermal{k}_{B}}(\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}})^2e^{-\beta{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}$ at low temperature.

> [!note] Correction and model limit
> The displayed heat capacity holds the mean atom number fixed. At fixed chemical potential, the mean atom number also changes with temperature, so differentiating total energy adds a particle-exchange term. Moreover, the geometric number distribution in this model has variance $\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle(1+\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle)$: relative number fluctuations do not vanish for large mean number. It is therefore a formal variable-size oscillator model, not the usual extensive grand canonical solid. Ordinary Einstein solids keep their atom number fixed. Phonon occupation can vary, but phonon number is a different quantity from atom number and its equilibrium chemical potential is zero.

## Example 5.7 — Two internal levels on each site

There are ${\mechanical{M}}$ independent distinguishable sites. A site is empty or contains one particle in one of two internal levels, with energies zero and ${\energy{\Delta}}>0$. It exchanges particles with a reservoir at ${\thermal{T}},{\energy{\mu}}$.

$$
\xi=\underbrace{1}_{\text{empty}}
+\underbrace{e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}}}_{\text{occupied ground level}}
+\underbrace{e^{\beta({\energy{\mu}}-{\energy{\Delta}})}}_{\text{occupied excited level}}
=1+z(1+e^{-\beta{\energy{\Delta}}}).
$$

For the whole system, $\Xi=\xi^{\mechanical{M}}$ and $\ln\Xi={\mechanical{M}}\ln\xi$. The mean occupation of a site is:

$$
\langle {\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{site}}\rangle
=z\frac{\partial\ln\xi}{\partial z}
=\frac{z(1+e^{-\beta{\energy{\Delta}}})}{1+z(1+e^{-\beta{\energy{\Delta}}})}.
$$

This is a generalized Langmuir form with internal partition function $z_{\mathrm{int}}=1+e^{-\beta{\energy{\Delta}}}$. Conditional on occupation, the excited-level probability is:

$$
p(\text{excited}\mid\text{occupied})
=\frac{e^{-\beta{\energy{\Delta}}}}{1+e^{-\beta{\energy{\Delta}}}}
=\frac1{1+e^{\beta{\energy{\Delta}}}}.
$$

The fugacity cancels, so this conditional probability does not depend on chemical potential. The mean energy per site is:

$$
\langle{\energy{\varepsilon}}_{\mathrm{site}}\rangle
=\langle {\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{site}}\rangle\frac{{\energy{\Delta}}}{1+e^{\beta{\energy{\Delta}}}}.
$$

As $z\to\infty$, occupation approaches one and the energy becomes the ordinary two-level mean energy ${\energy{\Delta}}/(1+e^{\beta{\energy{\Delta}}})$.
