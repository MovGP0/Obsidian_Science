> [!TLDR]
> Reservoir state counting gives a probability proportional to the Boltzmann weight of energy minus chemical work.

The number of accessible states of the isolated composite is:

$$
\Omega_T({\energy{E}}_T,{\mechanical{N}}_T)=\sum_{E_S,N_S}\Omega_S({\energy{E}}_S,{\mechanical{N}}_S)
\Omega_R({\energy{E}}_T-{\energy{E}}_S,{\mechanical{N}}_T-{\mechanical{N}}_S).
$$

All accessible composite microstates have equal probability. For a particular system microstate $r$ with energy ${\energy{E}}_r$ and particle number ${\mechanical{N}}_r$, the probability is therefore proportional to the number of compatible reservoir states:

$$
p_r\propto\Omega_R({\energy{E}}_T-{\energy{E}}_r,{\mechanical{N}}_T-{\mechanical{N}}_r).
$$

Expand the logarithm about the reservoir state with the full composite energy and particle number:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ln\Omega_R({\energy{E}}_T-{\energy{E}}_r,{\mechanical{N}}_T-{\mechanical{N}}_r)
&=\ln\Omega_R({\energy{E}}_T,{\mechanical{N}}_T)
-{\energy{E}}_r\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_R}{\partial {\energy{E}}_R}\right)_{N_R}\\
&\quad-{\mechanical{N}}_r\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_R}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}_R}\right)_{E_R}+\cdots\\
&=\text{constant}-\frac{{\energy{E}}_r}{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}+\frac{{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}_r}{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The derivatives follow from ${\thermal{S}}_R={\thermal{k}_{B}}\ln\Omega_R$:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_R}{\partial {\energy{E}}_R}\right)_{N_R}=\frac1{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}},
\qquad
\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_R}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}_R}\right)_{E_R}=-\frac{{\energy{\mu}}}{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}.
$$

**Proposition 5.1 — Grand canonical distribution.** Neglect the higher reservoir terms and normalize:

$$
p_r=\frac1\Xi e^{-\beta({\energy{E}}_r-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}_r)},
\qquad \beta=\frac1{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}.\tag{5.4}
$$

The grand partition function $\Xi$ is defined in Eq. (5.5). The source uses calligraphic energy and particle-number symbols for a microstate; these notes use ${\energy{E}}_r,{\mechanical{N}}_r$ for the same quantities.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | System, reservoir, microstate, or site energy, as indicated. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| $\beta$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Defined as one over Boltzmann constant times temperature. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Thermodynamic or Gibbs entropy. |
