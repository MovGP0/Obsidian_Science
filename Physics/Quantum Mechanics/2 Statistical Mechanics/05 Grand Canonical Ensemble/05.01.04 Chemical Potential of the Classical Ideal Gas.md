> [!TLDR]
> The classical ideal-gas chemical potential depends logarithmically on density and pressure.

For a monatomic ideal gas:

$$
{\energy{F}}=-{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\left[\ln\left(\frac{{\mechanical{V}}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}\right)+1\right],
\qquad {\signal{\lambda}}_T=\frac{{\energy{h}}}{\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}}.
$$

Differentiate at fixed ${\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}}$. The derivative of the factor ${\mechanical{N}}$ and the derivative of $\ln {\mechanical{N}}$ cancel the constant term:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{F}}}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{T,V}
=-{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln\left(\frac{{\mechanical{V}}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}\right)
={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln\left(\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}{{\mechanical{V}}}\right).
$$

Define the number density ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{V}}$ and **quantum concentration** ${\mechanical{n}}_Q={\signal{\lambda}}_T^{-3}$. Then:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}_{\mathrm{id}}({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{n}})={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln\left(\frac{{\mechanical{n}}}{{\mechanical{n}}_Q}\right)
={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln({\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3).\tag{5.2}
$$

**Remark 5.2.** In the classical dilute regime, ${\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3\ll1$. With the translational ground-energy reference used here, ${\energy{\mu}}<0$. At fixed density, an increase in temperature makes it more negative within this regime. A decrease in density at fixed temperature also makes it more negative.

Use ${\mechanical{P}}={\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}$ to write the same result in terms of pressure:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}_{\mathrm{id}}({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}})={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln\left(\frac{{\mechanical{P}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}\right)
={\energy{\mu}}^\circ({\thermal{T}})+{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln\left(\frac{{\mechanical{P}}}{{\mechanical{P}}_0}\right).\tag{5.3}
$$

The standard chemical potential ${\energy{\mu}}^\circ({\thermal{T}})$ refers to the specified reference pressure ${\mechanical{P}}_0$. The logarithms have dimensionless arguments.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{F}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | Thermodynamic potential at fixed temperature and volume. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal de Broglie wavelength | Thermal wavelength of a particle; a scale factor of the same letter is not a wavelength. |
| ${\energy{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | Quantum phase-space normalization constant. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Particle mass | Mass of one gas particle. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Particle number per volume; a site occupation with this letter is dimensionless. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_Q$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Quantum concentration | Inverse cube of the thermal wavelength. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Bulk or partial gas pressure, as indicated. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}_0$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Reference pressure | Standard pressure, or Langmuir half-coverage pressure where stated. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}^\circ$ | $\unit{J}$ | Standard chemical potential | Chemical potential at the chosen reference pressure. |
