> [!TLDR]
> The particle-number variance is the response of the mean particle number to chemical potential.

The grand canonical particle number fluctuates about its mean. From the definition:

$$
\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle=\frac{\sum_{N,r}{\mechanical{N}} e^{-\beta({\energy{E}}_r-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}})}}\Xi
=\frac1\beta\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}.
$$

Differentiate once more at constant ${\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}}$. Each derivative of an exponential contributes a factor $\beta {\mechanical{N}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle {\mechanical{N}}^2\rangle-\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle^2
&=\frac1{\beta^2}\left[\frac1\Xi\frac{\partial^2\Xi}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}^2}
-\frac1{\Xi^2}\left(\frac{\partial\Xi}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}\right)^2\right]\\
&=\frac1{\beta^2}\frac{\partial^2\ln\Xi}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Define $\Delta {\mechanical{N}}=\sqrt{\langle {\mechanical{N}}^2\rangle-\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle^2}$. Then:

$$
(\Delta {\mechanical{N}})^2={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\left(\frac{\partial\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}\right)_{T,V}.\tag{5.9}
$$

The right side is nonnegative in a stable equilibrium. It connects a fluctuation to a measurable response.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| $\beta$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Defined as one over Boltzmann constant times temperature. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | System, reservoir, microstate, or site energy, as indicated. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
