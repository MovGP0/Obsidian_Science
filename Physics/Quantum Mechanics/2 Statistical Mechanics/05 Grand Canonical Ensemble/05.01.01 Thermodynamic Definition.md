> [!TLDR]
> The chemical potential is the change in internal energy per added particle at fixed entropy and volume.

An **open system** can exchange matter with its surroundings. For one particle species, its internal energy is a state function:

$$
{\energy{U}}={\energy{U}}({\thermal{S}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}}),\qquad d{\energy{U}}={\thermal{T}}\,d{\thermal{S}}-{\mechanical{P}}\,d{\mechanical{V}}+{\energy{\mu}}\,d{\mechanical{N}}.
$$

**Definition 5.1 — Chemical potential.**

$$
{\energy{\mu}}=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{U}}}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{S,V}.
$$

The other thermodynamic potentials give equivalent definitions:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{U}}}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{S,V}
=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{H}}}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{S,P}
=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{F}}}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{T,V}
=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{G}}}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{T,P},
$$

where ${\energy{H}}={\energy{U}}+{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}$, ${\energy{F}}={\energy{U}}-{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{S}}$, and ${\energy{G}}={\energy{U}}-{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{S}}+{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}$. Each derivative holds the natural variables of its potential constant.

For a homogeneous, single-phase system with one species, the Gibbs energy is extensive in ${\mechanical{N}}$:

$$
{\energy{G}}({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}},\lambda {\mechanical{N}})=\lambda {\energy{G}}({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}},{\mechanical{N}}).
$$

Here $\lambda$ is a dimensionless scale factor, not a wavelength. Euler's theorem gives:

$$
{\energy{G}}={\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}.\tag{5.1}
$$

Thus ${\energy{\mu}}$ is the **Gibbs energy per particle**. A molar chemical potential uses particle amount in moles and the gas constant in place of the Boltzmann constant.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Mean internal energy of the thermodynamic system. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Thermodynamic or Gibbs entropy. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Bulk or partial gas pressure, as indicated. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| ${\energy{H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Enthalpy | Internal energy plus pressure–volume term. |
| ${\energy{F}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | Thermodynamic potential at fixed temperature and volume. |
| ${\energy{G}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Gibbs energy | Thermodynamic potential at fixed temperature and pressure. |
