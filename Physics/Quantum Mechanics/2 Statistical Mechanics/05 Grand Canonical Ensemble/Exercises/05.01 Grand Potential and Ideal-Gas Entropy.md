> [!TLDR]
> The ideal-gas grand potential gives the equation of state and Sackur–Tetrode entropy.

## Question — Exercise 5.1

Calculate the grand potential ${\energy{\Phi}}({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}},{\energy{\mu}})$ of the monatomic ideal gas and verify ${\energy{\Phi}}=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}$. Derive entropy from ${\energy{\Phi}}$ and compare it with the Sackur–Tetrode formula.

## Worked solution

### Sum over particle number

The one-particle partition function is ${\mechanical{V}}/{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3$. Indistinguishability gives $Z_N=({\mechanical{V}}/{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3)^{\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{N}}!$. The factorial makes the grand sum an exponential:

$$
\Xi=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{(e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}}{\mechanical{V}}/{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3)^{\mechanical{N}}}{{\mechanical{N}}!}
=\exp\left(\frac{{\mechanical{V}} e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}\right).
$$

Thus:

$$
{\energy{\Phi}}=-\frac{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}{\mechanical{V}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}},\qquad
\overline {\mechanical{N}}\equiv\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle=\frac{{\mechanical{V}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}}.
$$

### Verify pressure

Differentiate at fixed ${\thermal{T}},{\energy{\mu}}$ so that the prefactor per volume is constant:

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=-\left(\frac{\partial{\energy{\Phi}}}{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{T,\mu}
=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}}
=\frac{\overline {\mechanical{N}} {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}.
$$

Consequently ${\energy{\Phi}}=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}$.

### Differentiate for entropy

The entropy derivative must hold chemical potential fixed. Since ${\signal{\lambda}}_T^{-3}\propto {\thermal{T}}^{3/2}$:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\overline {\mechanical{N}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{V,\mu}
=\overline {\mechanical{N}}\left(\frac{3}{2{\thermal{T}}}-\frac{{\energy{\mu}}}{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}^2}\right).
$$

Apply the product rule to ${\energy{\Phi}}=-{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\overline {\mechanical{N}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\thermal{S}}&=-\left(\frac{\partial{\energy{\Phi}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{V,\mu}\\
&={\thermal{k}_{B}}\overline {\mechanical{N}}+{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\left(\frac{\partial\overline {\mechanical{N}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{V,\mu}\\
&={\thermal{k}_{B}}\overline {\mechanical{N}}\left(\frac52-\beta{\energy{\mu}}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Eliminate ${\energy{\mu}}$ with $\beta{\energy{\mu}}=\ln(\overline {\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3/{\mechanical{V}})$ to compare ensembles at the same mean density.

## Answer

$$
{\energy{\Phi}}=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}=-\overline {\mechanical{N}} {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}},
\qquad
{\thermal{S}}=\overline {\mechanical{N}} {\thermal{k}_{B}}\left[\ln\left(\frac {\mechanical{V}}{\overline {\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}\right)+\frac52\right].
$$

This is the Sackur–Tetrode entropy at ${\mechanical{N}}=\overline {\mechanical{N}}$, to thermodynamic-limit accuracy. The classical condition is $\overline {\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3/{\mechanical{V}}\ll1$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\Phi}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Grand potential | Helmholtz free energy minus chemical work. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal de Broglie wavelength | Thermal wavelength of a particle; a scale factor of the same letter is not a wavelength. |

| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Bulk or partial gas pressure, as indicated. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Thermodynamic or Gibbs entropy. |
| $\beta$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Defined as one over Boltzmann constant times temperature. |
