> [!TLDR]
> Attractive interactions increase particle-number fluctuations before the dilute approximation fails.

## Question — Exercise 5.3

Use $(\Delta {\mechanical{N}})^2={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}(\partial\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle/\partial{\energy{\mu}})_{T,V}$ to show that a dilute van der Waals gas with ${\mechanical{b}}{\mechanical{n}}\ll1$ has:

$$
\frac{\Delta {\mechanical{N}}}{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}
=\frac1{\sqrt{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}}
\frac1{\sqrt{1-2{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{n}}^2{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T^{(0)}}},
\qquad {\mechanical{\kappa}}_T^{(0)}=\frac1{{\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}=\frac1{{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{id}}}.
$$

Here ${\mechanical{a}}$ is the attractive interaction parameter and the excluded-volume term is neglected. Discuss the behavior near the critical temperature.

## Worked solution

### Obtain compressibility from the equation of state

Write the van der Waals equation with number density, so ${\mechanical{a}}$ and ${\mechanical{b}}$ are the particle-based parameters. In the dilute approximation:

$$
{\mechanical{P}}\simeq {\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}-{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{n}}^2,\qquad
\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{P}}}{\partial {\mechanical{n}}}\right)_T={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}-2{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{n}}.
$$

Since ${\mechanical{\kappa}}_T=(1/{\mechanical{n}})(\partial {\mechanical{n}}/\partial {\mechanical{P}})_T$:

$$
{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T=\frac1{{\mechanical{n}}({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}-2{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{n}})}
=\frac{{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T^{(0)}}{1-2{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{n}}/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})}.
$$

Mechanical stability requires a positive denominator.

### Insert the fluctuation relation

Equation (5.10) gives:

$$
\left(\frac{\Delta {\mechanical{N}}}{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}\right)^2
=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T}{{\mechanical{V}}}
=\frac1{{\mechanical{n}}{\mechanical{V}}}\frac1{1-2{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{n}}/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})}.
$$

Use ${\mechanical{n}}{\mechanical{V}}=\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle$ and ${\mechanical{n}}/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})={\mechanical{n}}^2{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T^{(0)}$. Taking the positive square root gives the requested result.

### Interpret the apparent divergence

Attraction makes the fluid easier to compress, so fluctuations exceed those of the ideal gas. The dilute expression diverges when ${\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}=2{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{n}}$, where its isothermal slope vanishes.

> [!note] Critical-point limit
> The truncated equation with ${\mechanical{b}}$ removed does not predict the actual van der Waals critical point. For the full equation, ${\mechanical{n}}_c=1/(3{\mechanical{b}})$ and ${\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}_c=8{\mechanical{a}}/(27{\mechanical{b}})$; thus ${\mechanical{b}}{\mechanical{n}}_c=1/3$, outside the assumed dilute limit. The full compressibility becomes singular at the critical point, but this dilute formula cannot give a controlled critical law there. Finite-size and non-Gaussian effects also matter.

## Answer

$$
\frac{\Delta {\mechanical{N}}}{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}
=\frac1{\sqrt{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}}
\left(1-2{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{n}}^2{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T^{(0)}\right)^{-1/2}.
$$

The result applies only on the stable dilute branch, with a positive denominator and negligible excluded-volume corrections.
