> [!TLDR]
> The Schottky response at fixed chemical potential concerns heat after chemical work is removed.

## Question — Exercise 5.2

For the Langmuir model, calculate the surface heat capacity at fixed chemical potential. Show that it has an effective two-level gap ${\energy{\Delta}}=|{\energy{\varepsilon}}_0|+{\energy{\mu}}$ and a Schottky peak determined by $x=2\coth(x/2)$, where $x={\energy{\Delta}}/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})$. Find the peak temperature and discuss ${\energy{\mu}}=0$.

> [!note] Clarify the requested heat capacity
> For an open system, $(\partial {\energy{U}}/\partial {\thermal{T}})_{\energy{\mu}}$ and ${\thermal{T}}(\partial {\thermal{S}}/\partial {\thermal{T}})_{\energy{\mu}}$ differ by chemical work. The source Schottky formula describes the second quantity. A physical positive gap is $|{\energy{\Delta}}|$; the signed source gap can be negative. Volume and the number of adsorption sites stay fixed throughout.

## Worked solution

### Occupation and effective energy

Let ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_b=|{\energy{\varepsilon}}_0|>0$, ${\energy{\Delta}}={\energy{\varepsilon}}_b+{\energy{\mu}}$, and $x=\beta{\energy{\Delta}}$. The grand energy $K={\energy{E}}-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}$ of a site is zero when empty and $-{\energy{\Delta}}$ when occupied. Therefore:

$$
\xi=1+e^x,\qquad \theta=\frac{e^x}{1+e^x},
\qquad \frac{d\theta}{dx}=\theta(1-\theta).
$$

At fixed ${\energy{\mu}}$, the signed gap is constant and $dx/d{\thermal{T}}=-{\energy{\Delta}}/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}^2)$. For ${\mechanical{M}}$ independent sites:

$$
\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle={\mechanical{M}}\theta,\qquad {\energy{U}}=-{\mechanical{M}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_b\theta,
\qquad \langle K\rangle=-{\mechanical{M}}\Delta\theta.
$$

### Separate heat from chemical work

Use the first law for the surface, $d{\energy{U}}={\thermal{T}}\,d{\thermal{S}}+{\energy{\mu}}\,d\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle$. Since ${\energy{\mu}}$ is fixed, $d\langle K\rangle={\thermal{T}}\,d{\thermal{S}}$. The heat response is:

$$
{\thermal{C}}_{\energy{\mu}}\equiv {\thermal{T}}\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{\energy{\mu}}
=\left(\frac{\partial\langle K\rangle}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{\energy{\mu}}
=\frac{{\mechanical{M}}{\energy{\Delta}}^2}{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}^2}\theta(1-\theta).
$$

Thus:

$$
{\thermal{C}}_{\energy{\mu}}={\mechanical{M}}{\thermal{k}_{B}}\frac{x^2e^x}{(1+e^x)^2}
={\mechanical{M}}{\thermal{k}_{B}}\frac{x^2}{4\cosh^2(x/2)}.
$$

This is nonnegative and is unchanged by $x\mapsto-x$. For comparison, the surface-energy derivative is:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{U}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{\energy{\mu}}
=\frac{{\mechanical{M}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_b{\energy{\Delta}}}{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}^2}\theta(1-\theta),
$$

which can be negative if ${\energy{\Delta}}<0$. Particle exchange explains the difference; a negative value here is not a negative Schottky heat response.

### Locate the peak

For $y=|{\energy{\Delta}}|/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})>0$, differentiate the logarithm of ${\thermal{C}}_{\energy{\mu}}$ to avoid a long quotient derivative:

$$
\frac{d}{dy}\ln\left(\frac{y^2}{4\cosh^2(y/2)}\right)
=\frac2y-\tanh(y/2)=0.
$$

Hence $y=2\coth(y/2)$. Its positive solution is $y^*\approx2.399357$. The peak height is approximately $0.439229\,{\mechanical{M}}{\thermal{k}_{B}}$.

## Answer

$$
{\thermal{T}}^*=\frac{|{\energy{\Delta}}|}{2.399357\,{\thermal{k}_{B}}}\approx0.416778\frac{|{\energy{\Delta}}|}{{\thermal{k}_{B}}}.
$$

For ${\energy{\mu}}=0$, ${\energy{\Delta}}={\energy{\varepsilon}}_b$, so ${\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}^*\approx0.417{\energy{\varepsilon}}_b$, as stated in the source. If ${\energy{\Delta}}=0$, each site has coverage $1/2$ at every positive temperature and ${\thermal{C}}_{\energy{\mu}}=0$; there is no finite-temperature peak.
