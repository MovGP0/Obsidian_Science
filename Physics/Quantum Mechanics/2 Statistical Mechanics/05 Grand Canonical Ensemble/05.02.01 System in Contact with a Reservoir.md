> [!TLDR]
> A large reservoir fixes temperature and chemical potential while the smaller system exchanges energy and particles.

Let $\mathcal S$ be a small system and $\mathcal R$ a large reservoir. The reservoir acts as a **thermostat**, which fixes temperature, and a **particle reservoir**, which fixes chemical potential. Their composite $\mathcal T=\mathcal S+\mathcal R$ is isolated.

The system energy ${\energy{E}}_S$ and particle number ${\mechanical{N}}_S$ fluctuate. Conservation in the composite gives:

$$
{\energy{E}}_R={\energy{E}}_T-{\energy{E}}_S,\qquad {\mechanical{N}}_R={\mechanical{N}}_T-{\mechanical{N}}_S.
$$

The reservoir is much larger than the system: ${\energy{E}}_S\ll {\energy{E}}_T$ and ${\mechanical{N}}_S\ll {\mechanical{N}}_T$. Exchange therefore changes its temperature and chemical potential by negligible amounts. Weak coupling lets us neglect the interaction energy in this energy balance.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | System, reservoir, microstate, or site energy, as indicated. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
