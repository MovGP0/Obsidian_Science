> [!TLDR]
> The ideal-gas particle-number variance equals its mean, as required for a Poisson distribution.

The ideal-gas law is ${\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}=\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}$. Hence ${\mechanical{\kappa}}_T=1/{\mechanical{P}}$. Equation (5.10) becomes:

$$
(\Delta {\mechanical{N}})^2=\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle.\tag{5.11}
$$

This is consistent with a Poisson distribution. The full distribution is derived in [[05.06.01 Classical Ideal Gas in the Grand Canonical Ensemble]].

**Example 5.1 — Fluctuations in a macroscopic gas.** One mole has mean particle number $\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle=6.022\times10^{23}$. Its relative standard deviation is:

$$
\frac{\Delta {\mechanical{N}}}{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}
=\frac1{\sqrt{6.022\times10^{23}}}\approx1.3\times10^{-12}.
$$

Such a small relative fluctuation cannot be resolved by an ordinary macroscopic instrument. This supports the thermodynamic use of mean values.
