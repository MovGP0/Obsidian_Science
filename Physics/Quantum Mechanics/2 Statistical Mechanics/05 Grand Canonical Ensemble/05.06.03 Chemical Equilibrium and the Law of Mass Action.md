> [!TLDR]
> Chemical equilibrium makes the stoichiometric sum of chemical potentials vanish and gives the law of mass action.

Consider the reaction:

$$
\nu_A A+\nu_B B\rightleftharpoons\nu_C {\thermal{C}}+\nu_D D.
$$

At equilibrium:

$$
\nu_A{\energy{\mu}}_A+\nu_B{\energy{\mu}}_B=\nu_C{\energy{\mu}}_C+\nu_D{\energy{\mu}}_D.\tag{5.13}
$$

Equivalently, use signed stoichiometric coefficients: positive for products and negative for reactants. Then $\sum_i\nu_i{\energy{\mu}}_i=0$.

For ideal-gas species, Eq. (5.3) gives:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}_i={\energy{\mu}}_i^\circ({\thermal{T}})+{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln\left(\frac{{\mechanical{P}}_i}{{\mechanical{P}}_0}\right).
$$

Insert this into the equilibrium condition:

$$
\sum_i\nu_i{\energy{\mu}}_i^\circ+{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\sum_i\nu_i\ln\left(\frac{{\mechanical{P}}_i}{{\mechanical{P}}_0}\right)=0.
$$

Define the standard reaction Gibbs energy per reaction event as $\Delta {\energy{G}}^\circ=\sum_i\nu_i{\energy{\mu}}_i^\circ$. Exponentiation gives the **law of mass action**, also called the Guldberg–Waage law:

$$
K_P({\thermal{T}})=\prod_i\left(\frac{{\mechanical{P}}_i}{{\mechanical{P}}_0}\right)^{\nu_i}
=e^{-\Delta {\energy{G}}^\circ/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})}.
$$

The equilibrium constant is dimensionless. For molar standard Gibbs energies, replace ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ by the gas constant.

## Example 5.3 — Dissociation

For $A_2\rightleftharpoons2A$, the coefficients are $\nu_{A_2}=-1$ and $\nu_A=2$:

$$
K_P=\frac{({\mechanical{P}}_A/{\mechanical{P}}_0)^2}{{\mechanical{P}}_{A_2}/{\mechanical{P}}_0}
=\frac{{\mechanical{P}}_A^2}{{\mechanical{P}}_{A_2}{\mechanical{P}}_0}.
$$

Start with ${\mechanical{N}}_0$ molecules. If fraction $\alpha$ dissociates, equilibrium contains ${\mechanical{N}}_0(1-\alpha)$ molecules and $2{\mechanical{N}}_0\alpha$ atoms. Hence:

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_{A_2}=\frac{1-\alpha}{1+\alpha}{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{tot}},\qquad
{\mechanical{P}}_A=\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{tot}}.
$$

Substitution gives:

$$
K_P=\frac{4\alpha^2}{1-\alpha^2}\frac{{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{tot}}}{{\mechanical{P}}_0},
\qquad \alpha=\sqrt{\frac{K_P}{K_P+4{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{tot}}/{\mechanical{P}}_0}}.
$$

At fixed temperature, lower total pressure favors dissociation because the product side contains more particles.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
