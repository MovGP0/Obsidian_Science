> [!TLDR]
> Maximum entropy with fixed mean energy and mean particle number gives the grand canonical distribution.

An alternative derivation uses the **maximum-entropy principle** of Jaynes. The known constraints are:

$$
\sum_r p_r=1,\qquad \sum_r p_r {\energy{E}}_r=\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle,
\qquad \sum_r p_r {\mechanical{N}}_r=\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle.
$$

Maximize ${\thermal{S}}=-{\thermal{k}_{B}}\sum_r p_r\ln p_r$ with these constraints. Write the dimensionless Lagrange function as:

$$
\mathcal L=-\sum_r p_r\ln p_r
-a\left(\sum_r p_r-1\right)
-\beta\left(\sum_r p_r {\energy{E}}_r-\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle\right)
-\gamma\left(\sum_r p_r {\mechanical{N}}_r-\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle\right).
$$

Stationarity for each probability gives:

$$
-(\ln p_r+1)-a-\beta {\energy{E}}_r-\gamma {\mechanical{N}}_r=0,
\qquad p_r=e^{-\lambda_0-\beta {\energy{E}}_r-\gamma {\mechanical{N}}_r},
$$

where $\lambda_0=1+a$. Comparison with thermodynamics identifies $\beta=1/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})$ and $\gamma=-\beta{\energy{\mu}}$. Normalization gives $e^{\lambda_0}=\Xi$, which reproduces Eq. (5.4).

The entropy is concave in the probabilities. Thus the stationary distribution is the entropy maximum under these linear constraints.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Thermodynamic or Gibbs entropy. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | System, reservoir, microstate, or site energy, as indicated. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| $\beta$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Defined as one over Boltzmann constant times temperature. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
