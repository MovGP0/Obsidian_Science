> [!TLDR]
> Particles move toward lower chemical potential until chemical equilibrium is reached.

**Remark 5.1 — Meaning of the chemical potential.** The chemical potential is the energy needed to add a particle while entropy and volume stay constant. It also determines the direction of spontaneous matter transfer.

Consider two systems that can exchange particles. If ${\energy{\mu}}_A>{\energy{\mu}}_B$, particles tend to move from A to B. At chemical equilibrium:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}_A={\energy{\mu}}_B.
$$

Temperature has the corresponding role for heat transfer. Pressure has the corresponding role for volume work. When the boundary permits all three exchanges, complete equilibrium also requires ${\thermal{T}}_A={\thermal{T}}_B$ and ${\mechanical{P}}_A={\mechanical{P}}_B$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Bulk or partial gas pressure, as indicated. |
