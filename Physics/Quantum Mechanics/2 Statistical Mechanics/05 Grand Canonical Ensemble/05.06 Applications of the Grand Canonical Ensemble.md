> [!TLDR]
> Free particles, occupied sites, and reacting species use the same reservoir weight with different allowed states.

- [[05.06.01 Classical Ideal Gas in the Grand Canonical Ensemble]]
- [[05.06.02 Surface Adsorption - Langmuir Isotherm]]
- [[05.06.03 Chemical Equilibrium and the Law of Mass Action]]

Each application changes the state space while retaining the same reservoir weighting. [[05.06.01 Classical Ideal Gas in the Grand Canonical Ensemble]] sums an unbounded particle count with indistinguishable classical state counting. [[05.06.02 Surface Adsorption - Langmuir Isotherm]] instead permits only an empty or occupied state on each distinguishable site; saturation follows from that restriction.

[[05.06.03 Chemical Equilibrium and the Law of Mass Action]] permits species counts to change only in stoichiometric combinations that conserve the constituent matter. Before calculating, identify whether particles occupy free volume, fixed sites, or reaction channels. An unbounded gas sum, a product over binary sites, and a constrained reaction problem cannot use the same multiplicity factors without checking those allowed states.
