> [!TLDR]
> Phase coexistence, long-range interactions, and small systems need a separate ensemble comparison.

The simple equivalence and small-fluctuation arguments need care in the following cases.

- **First-order phase transitions.** A fixed-density canonical system can contain coexisting phases. At fixed chemical potential, the grand canonical density can jump. The distributions and accessible constraints differ. For additive systems, this does not by itself imply that the correctly transformed bulk thermodynamic potentials disagree; the coexistence construction is essential.
- **Long-range interactions.** Gravitating systems can be nonadditive. The standard extensivity argument fails, and ensembles can give different stability properties.
- **Small systems.** For clusters or nanoparticles with ${\mechanical{N}}\sim10^2$–$10^3$, finite-size and surface corrections can be relevant.

> [!note] Source qualification
> The source groups these cases under ensemble non-equivalence. At phase coexistence, distinguish different constrained distributions from a failure of thermodynamic equivalence. The usual ${\mechanical{N}}^{-1/2}$ fluctuation estimate is not a general description of a coexistence distribution.
