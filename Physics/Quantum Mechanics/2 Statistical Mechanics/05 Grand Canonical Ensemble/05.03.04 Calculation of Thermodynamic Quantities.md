> [!TLDR]
> Derivatives of the grand partition function give mean particle number, energy, pressure, and entropy.

For a homogeneous bulk system, the main working relations are:

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}\ln\Xi,
$$

$$
\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}\right)_{T,V}
=z\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial z}\right)_{T,V},
$$

$$
\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle=-\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\beta}\right)_{V,\mu}
+{\energy{\mu}}\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle,
$$

$$
{\thermal{S}}={\thermal{k}_{B}}\left[\ln\Xi-\beta\left(\frac{\partial\ln\Xi}{\partial\beta}\right)_{V,\mu}\right].
$$

Some texts call $\ln\Xi={\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})$ the grand canonical equation of state. Derivatives with respect to the natural parameters give the remaining thermodynamic quantities.

> [!note] Hold the stated variables constant
> At fixed chemical potential, differentiation of the exponent produces ${\energy{E}}_r-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}_r$. At fixed fugacity, it produces ${\energy{E}}_r$. Thus $\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle=-(\partial\ln\Xi/\partial\beta)_{V,z}$. These two derivative formulas agree because $z=e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}}$ changes with $\beta$ when ${\energy{\mu}}$ is fixed. They assume the microstate energies have no additional explicit temperature dependence.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Bulk or partial gas pressure, as indicated. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | System, reservoir, microstate, or site energy, as indicated. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Thermodynamic or Gibbs entropy. |
| $\beta$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Defined as one over Boltzmann constant times temperature. |
