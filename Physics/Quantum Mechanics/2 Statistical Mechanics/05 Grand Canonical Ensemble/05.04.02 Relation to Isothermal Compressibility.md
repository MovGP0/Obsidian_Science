> [!TLDR]
> Isothermal compressibility controls particle-number fluctuations in a homogeneous fluid.

The isothermal compressibility is:

$$
{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T=-\frac1{\mechanical{V}}\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}{\partial {\mechanical{P}}}\right)_{T,N}.
$$

Use $\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle={\mechanical{n}}{\mechanical{V}}$ at constant ${\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}}$:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}\right)_{T,V}
={\mechanical{V}}\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{n}}}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}\right)_T.
$$

At fixed temperature, Gibbs–Duhem gives $d{\energy{\mu}}=d{\mechanical{P}}/{\mechanical{n}}$. Also, differentiation of ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{V}}$ at fixed ${\mechanical{N}}$ gives $(\partial {\mechanical{n}}/\partial {\mechanical{P}})_T={\mechanical{n}}{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T$. Therefore:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{n}}}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}\right)_T
={\mechanical{n}}\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{n}}}{\partial {\mechanical{P}}}\right)_T={\mechanical{n}}^2{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T.
$$

Substitution into Eq. (5.9) gives:

$$
(\Delta {\mechanical{N}})^2={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}{\mechanical{V}}{\mechanical{n}}^2{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T
=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle^2}{{\mechanical{V}}}{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T.\tag{5.10}
$$

**Proposition 5.3 — Relative fluctuations.**

$$
\frac{\Delta {\mechanical{N}}}{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}=\sqrt{\frac{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T}{{\mechanical{V}}}}
\propto\frac1{\sqrt{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}}.
$$

The proportionality assumes fixed density and a finite compressibility as the system grows. It is a form of the law of large numbers. For a macroscopic ideal gas, relative fluctuations are of order $10^{-12}$.

**Fig. 5.3** Illustrative Gaussian particle-number distributions. Blue: relative standard deviation 5%; red dashed: 12%. The density is normalized with respect to the dimensionless horizontal variable. For an ideal gas, the two widths correspond to means 400 and about 69 particles. The exact number law is discrete and Poisson.

> [!note] Source clarification
> The density derivative holds volume fixed, not chemical potential. The source prose says “fixed temperature and chemical potential” before this derivative; the displayed derivative correctly uses fixed ${\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}}$. Near a critical point, compressibility can become large and the usual size estimate needs a separate analysis.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{\kappa}}_T$ | $\unit{Pa^{-1}}$ | Isothermal compressibility | Fractional volume response to pressure. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Bulk or partial gas pressure, as indicated. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Particle number per volume; a site occupation with this letter is dimensionless. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
