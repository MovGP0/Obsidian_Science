> [!TLDR]
> The Gibbs–Duhem relation makes temperature, pressure, and chemical potential dependent variables.

Differentiate Eq. (5.1), ${\energy{G}}={\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}$:

$$
d{\energy{G}}={\energy{\mu}}\,d{\mechanical{N}}+{\mechanical{N}}\,d{\energy{\mu}}.
$$

The differential of the Gibbs energy also gives:

$$
d{\energy{G}}=-{\thermal{S}}\,d{\thermal{T}}+{\mechanical{V}}\,d{\mechanical{P}}+{\energy{\mu}}\,d{\mechanical{N}}.
$$

Equate the two expressions and cancel the particle-exchange term. The result is the **Gibbs–Duhem relation**:

$$
{\thermal{S}}\,d{\thermal{T}}-{\mechanical{V}}\,d{\mechanical{P}}+{\mechanical{N}}\,d{\energy{\mu}}=0.
$$

For a homogeneous single-phase system with one species, only two of ${\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}},{\energy{\mu}}$ are independent. At constant temperature:

$$
d{\energy{\mu}}=\frac {\mechanical{V}}{\mechanical{N}}\,d{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{d{\mechanical{P}}}{{\mechanical{n}}},\qquad {\mechanical{n}}=\frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}.
$$

This last form is used in [[05.04.02 Relation to Isothermal Compressibility]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{G}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Gibbs energy | Thermodynamic potential at fixed temperature and pressure. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Thermodynamic or Gibbs entropy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Bulk or partial gas pressure, as indicated. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Particle number per volume; a site occupation with this letter is dimensionless. |
