> [!TLDR]
> The grand partition function sums canonical partition functions over all particle numbers with fugacity weights.

**Definition 5.2 — Grand partition function.**

$$
\Xi({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}},{\energy{\mu}})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\sum_{r(N)}e^{-\beta({\energy{E}}_r^{({\mechanical{N}})}-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}})}
=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}}Z_N({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}}).\tag{5.5}
$$

Here $Z_N=\sum_{r(N)}e^{-\beta {\energy{E}}_r^{({\mechanical{N}})}}$ is the canonical partition function for exactly ${\mechanical{N}}$ particles. The inner sum includes all microstates in that particle-number sector.

Define the **fugacity**:

$$
z=e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}}.
$$

Then:

$$
\Xi({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}},{\energy{\mu}})=\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}z^{\mechanical{N}} Z_N({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}}).\tag{5.6}
$$

Fugacity is dimensionless and positive. Zero is a limiting value. The classical dilute limit has $z\ll1$, or $\beta{\energy{\mu}}\ll-1$. For the ideal Bose gas with ground energy set to zero, condensation is approached as ${\energy{\mu}}\to0^-$ and $z\to1^-$.

**Remark 5.3.** Equation (5.6) is the generating function of the canonical functions $Z_N$. It is also a discrete Laplace transform in ${\mechanical{N}}$, with transform parameter $s=-\beta{\energy{\mu}}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| $\beta$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Defined as one over Boltzmann constant times temperature. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | System, reservoir, microstate, or site energy, as indicated. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
