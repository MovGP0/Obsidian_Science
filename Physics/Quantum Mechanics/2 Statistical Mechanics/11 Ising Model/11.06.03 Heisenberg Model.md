> [!TLDR]
> Three-component spins have continuous rotational symmetry and can show a ferromagnetic transition in three dimensions.

The **Heisenberg model** uses three-component unit spins on the sphere $S^2$:

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=-\energy{J}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}
\magnetic{\mathbf S}_i\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf S}_j,
\qquad
|\magnetic{\mathbf S}_i|=1.
$$

The model has continuous $O(3)$ symmetry. For short-range interactions, the Mermin–Wagner theorem excludes spontaneous symmetry breaking for $d\le2$ at $\thermal{T}>0$. In $d=3$, a ferromagnetic transition occurs. Its critical exponents differ from those of the Ising model.

> [!note] Remark 11.6 (Universality)
> The source groups the three-dimensional Ising model, the three-dimensional $q=2$ Potts model, and other systems with the same dimension, number of order-parameter components, and interaction range into the same universality class.

The source concludes that these systems have the same critical exponents: they belong to the same **universality class**. It says that the exponents depend on $d$ and $n$, rather than on microscopic details.

> [!note] Scope of the universality statement
> The number of components alone is not a complete classification. The order-parameter symmetry and interaction range also matter. The source statement applies when these other relevant properties agree.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\mathbf S}_i$ | $1$ | Classical spin | The normalized two-component or three-component spin. |
| $\energy{\mathcal H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy of a spin configuration. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $d,\ d_{\mathrm c}$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The lattice dimension and its upper critical value. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $n$ | $1$ | Order-parameter component count | The number of components of the order parameter. |
| $q$ | $1$ | State count | The number of allowed spin states in the Potts model. |
