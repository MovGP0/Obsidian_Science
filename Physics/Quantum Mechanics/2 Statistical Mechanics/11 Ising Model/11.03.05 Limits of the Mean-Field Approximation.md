> [!TLDR]
> Mean-field theory neglects fluctuations and becomes inaccurate near criticality below four dimensions.

Mean-field theory neglects correlations of fluctuations $\delta\magnetic{\sigma}_i\delta\magnetic{\sigma}_j$. These effects become more important as the spatial dimension decreases.

**Theorem 11.1 (Ginzburg criterion).** The source states that mean-field theory is self-consistent when fluctuations are small compared with the order parameter. This requires the spatial dimension to exceed the upper critical dimension.

The upper critical dimension is $d_{\mathrm c}=4$. More precisely, the Ginzburg condition is

$$
\frac{\langle(\delta\magnetic{m})^2\rangle_{\mathrm{fluct}}}
{\langle\magnetic{m}\rangle^2}\ll1.
$$

In the critical limit, this condition is satisfied for $d>d_{\mathrm c}=4$.

- For $d>4$, the mean-field exponents are exact. At $d=4$, logarithmic corrections apply.
- For $d=3$, mean-field exponents are approximate. The source gives $\beta\approx0.326$ and $\gamma\approx1.237$ for the three-dimensional Ising model.
- For $d=2$, mean-field theory fails near criticality. The exact magnetization exponent is $\beta=1/8$.
- For $d=1$, mean-field theory predicts $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}=2\energy{J}/\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}>0$, whereas the exact solution gives $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}=0$.

> [!note] Scope of the criterion
> The dimension statement concerns asymptotic critical behaviour. Below four dimensions, mean-field theory can still be useful outside the region where critical fluctuations are large.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\sigma}_i$ | $1$ | Ising spin | The normalized spin at site i. |
| $\magnetic{m}$ | $1$ | Magnetization per spin | The mean normalized spin; subscripts identify particular or spontaneous values. |
| $d,\ d_{\mathrm c}$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The lattice dimension and its upper critical value. |
