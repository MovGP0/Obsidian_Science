> [!TLDR]
> The self-dual point of the square-lattice model determines its critical temperature.

The **Kramers–Wannier duality** (1941) determines the critical temperature without a full solution of the model.

Here the system is the zero-field ferromagnetic square lattice with a positive reduced coupling. The useful question is whether the disordered and ordered expansions encode the same bulk partition sum in different variables. Duality relates those sums; it does not identify their ordered states or make their magnetizations equal.

## Dual lattice

The sites of the dual lattice $\Lambda^*$ lie at the centres of the squares of $\Lambda$. Each original bond is crossed at right angles by one dual bond. The dual of a square lattice is another square lattice.

At high temperature, summing over binary spins cancels terms with an odd number of selected bonds at any site. The surviving bond graphs have even degree. At low temperature, reversed domains are bounded by contours on the dual lattice. Matching the contour weights gives $e^{-2\thermal{K}^*}=\tanh\thermal{K}$. This is the origin of the coupling transformation below. See [Tong, Statistical Physics, Sections 5.3.3–5.3.4](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S5.html#S5.SS3.SSS4).

## Transformation of the free energy

The source writes the partition-function relation as

$$
\mathcal Z(\thermal{K})
=C(\thermal{K})^N\mathcal Z(\thermal{K}^*),
\qquad
\thermal{K}=\thermal{\beta}\energy{J},
\qquad
\thermal{K}^*=-\frac12\ln\tanh\thermal{K},
$$

where $C(\thermal{K})$ is a regular scale factor. The transformation maps high temperature $(\thermal{K}\to0,\ \thermal{K}^*\to\infty)$ to low temperature, and conversely.

> [!note] Boundary conditions in the duality relation
> The displayed relation states the bulk duality. For a finite lattice with periodic boundaries, an exact relation also requires the appropriate boundary sectors. The thermodynamic-limit singularity is the result used here.

If there is only one phase transition, it must lie at the fixed point of the duality transformation:

$$
\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}=\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}^*
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sinh(2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c})=1.
$$

This reproduces the Onsager critical temperature, Equation (11.8).

The single-transition assumption does essential work. A singularity away from the fixed point would have a second singularity at its dual coupling. A regular scale factor cannot remove that pair. Thus uniqueness locates the transition, but duality alone does not prove uniqueness or even the existence of a transition. As a check, the equivalent relation $\sinh(2\thermal{K})\sinh(2\thermal{K}^*)=1$ returns the original positive coupling when the transformation is applied twice.

**Proposition 11.1.** The self-duality condition implies

$$
e^{2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}}-e^{-2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}}=2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}=\frac12\ln(1+\sqrt2).
$$

Thus $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}=2\energy{J}/\ln(1+\sqrt2)$.

**Proof.** Set $x=e^{2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}}$. Then $(x-x^{-1})/2=1$, or $x^2-2x-1=0$. The positive root is $x=1+\sqrt2$. Hence $2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}=\ln(1+\sqrt2)$. $\square$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathcal Z$ | $1$ | Partition function | The sum of Boltzmann weights of the configurations. |
| $\thermal{K}$ | $1$ | Reduced coupling | The dimensionless coupling defined in this article; star and c mark dual and critical values. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
