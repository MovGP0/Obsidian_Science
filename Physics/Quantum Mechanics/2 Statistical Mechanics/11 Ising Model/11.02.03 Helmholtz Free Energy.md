> [!TLDR]
> The largest transfer-matrix eigenvalue gives an analytic free energy at every positive temperature.

In the thermodynamic limit $N\to\infty$, the largest eigenvalue determines the Helmholtz free energy per spin:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{f}
&=-\frac{1}{\thermal{\beta}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\mathcal Z\\
&=-\frac{1}{\thermal{\beta}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}
\ln\!\left[\lambda_+^N\left(1+(\lambda_-/\lambda_+)^N\right)\right]\\
&=-\frac{1}{\thermal{\beta}}\ln\lambda_+.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $|\lambda_-/\lambda_+|<1$ for $\thermal{T}>0$, the correction $(\lambda_-/\lambda_+)^N$ tends to zero exponentially.

$$
\energy{f}=-\frac{1}{\thermal{\beta}}\ln\!\left[
e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}\cosh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{h})
+\sqrt{e^{2\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}\sinh^2(\thermal{\beta}\energy{h})
+e^{-2\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}}
\right].
\tag{11.4}
$$

> [!note] Remark 11.2 (Analyticity)
> The free energy $\energy{f}(\thermal{T},\energy{h})$ is analytic for all finite parameter values with $\thermal{T}>0$. Thus the one-dimensional Ising chain has no phase transition at positive temperature. This confirms Ising's original result.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $N$ | $1$ | Spin count | The number of lattice sites or spins. |
| $\energy{f}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free energy per spin | The Helmholtz free energy per spin; subscript 0 marks a reference value. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
