> [!TLDR]
> At zero field, correlations decay exponentially along the ferromagnetic chain, while their range diverges at zero temperature.

The **connected correlation function** between sites $i$ and $j$, with $r=|i-j|$, is

$$
G(r)=\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_j\rangle
-\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_i\rangle\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_j\rangle.
$$

The transfer-matrix method gives, at $\energy{h}=0$,

$$
G(r)=\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_0\magnetic{\sigma}_r\rangle
=\left(\frac{\lambda_-}{\lambda_+}\right)^r
=\tanh^r(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}).
$$

Here $\lambda_\pm=e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}\pm e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}$, so the eigenvalue ratio is $\tanh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})$.

For $\energy{J}>0$, the correlation function decays exponentially:

$$
G(r)=e^{-r/\xi},
\qquad
\xi=-\frac{1}{\ln\tanh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})}
=\frac{1}{\ln\coth(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})}.
$$

> [!note] Range and boundary assumptions
> These expressions describe the infinite chain, or the thermodynamic limit of the periodic chain. Distance and correlation length are measured in lattice spacings. For an antiferromagnetic chain, correlations alternate in sign; the decay length uses $|\tanh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})|$.

> [!note] Remark 11.3 (Divergence of the correlation length)
> At low temperature, $\xi\sim\frac12e^{2\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}\to\infty$ as $\thermal{T}\to0$. This divergence is exponential, rather than a power law. The critical point in one dimension is $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}=0$.

## Example 11.1: Explicit transfer matrix at zero field

For $\energy{h}=0$, Equation (11.2) becomes

$$
\left.\mathbf T\right|_{h=0}=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}&e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}\\
e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}&e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The eigenvalues are

$$
\lambda_+=e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}+e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}
=2\cosh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}),
\qquad
\lambda_-=e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}-e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}
=2\sinh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}).
$$

The free energy per spin is

$$
\energy{f}=-\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}
\ln\!\left[2\cosh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})\right].
$$

Differentiation gives the internal energy per spin:

$$
\energy{u}=\energy{f}-\thermal{T}\frac{\partial\energy{f}}{\partial\thermal{T}}
=-\energy{J}\tanh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}).
$$

The heat capacity per spin is

$$
\thermal{c}=\frac{\partial\energy{u}}{\partial\thermal{T}}
=\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})^2
\operatorname{sech}^2(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}).
$$

It has a Schottky-type maximum at $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}\approx0.83\,\energy{J}$, but it does not diverge. There is no phase transition.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\sigma}_i$ | $1$ | Ising spin | The normalized spin at site i. |
| $G(r)$ | $1$ | Spin correlation | The connected correlation of normalized spins. |
| $r$ | $1$ | Lattice separation | The distance between sites, measured in lattice spacings. |
| $\xi$ | $1$ | Correlation length | The correlation range, measured in lattice spacings. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\energy{f}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free energy per spin | The Helmholtz free energy per spin; subscript 0 marks a reference value. |
| $\energy{u}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy per spin | The mean energy per spin. |
| $\thermal{c}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity per spin | The temperature derivative of internal energy per spin. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
