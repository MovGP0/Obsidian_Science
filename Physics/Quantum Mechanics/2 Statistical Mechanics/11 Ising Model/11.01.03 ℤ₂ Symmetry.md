> [!TLDR]
> At zero field, the Hamiltonian is invariant when all spins reverse together.

For $\energy{h}=0$, the Hamiltonian in Equation (11.1) is invariant under global spin inversion:

$$
\magnetic{\sigma}_i\longrightarrow-\magnetic{\sigma}_i
\qquad\forall i\in\Lambda.
$$

Each product remains unchanged:

$$
\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_j
\longrightarrow
(-\magnetic{\sigma}_i)(-\magnetic{\sigma}_j)
=\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_j.
$$

This is **$\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry**, also called spin-inversion symmetry.

For a finite system with periodic boundaries, the zero-field partition sum gives $\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_i\rangle=0$ at every temperature. Each configuration has an equally weighted configuration with all spins reversed. In the thermodynamic limit $N\to\infty$, this symmetry can break spontaneously. This is the main feature of the ferromagnetic transition; see [[11.05 Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking]].
