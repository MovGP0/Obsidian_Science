> [!TLDR]
> The mean-field approximation replaces neighbouring spins by their average and gives a self-consistency equation.

Mean-field theory is the simplest approximation for phase transitions in arbitrary dimension. It replaces interactions between spins by the interaction of one spin with an effective field produced by its neighbours.

The physical question is whether the average alignment can support itself without an applied field. A trial mean spin produces an effective field; the one-spin thermal response must reproduce that same mean. This reduces a many-spin problem to a self-consistency condition. The reduction assumes a homogeneous ferromagnetic state and discards correlations between fluctuations.

Read [[11.03.01 Derivation of the Self-Consistency Equation]] to locate the discarded term. Use [[11.03.02 Analysis of the Self-Consistency Equation]] for the onset of nonzero solutions, then [[11.03.03 Landau Free Energy and the Phase Transition]] to check which stationary solutions are stable. The exponent table in [[11.03.04 Critical Mean-Field Exponents]] describes this approximation, with its restrictions in [[11.03.05 Limits of the Mean-Field Approximation]]. The exact chain is a necessary comparison: mean field predicts a positive transition temperature there, whereas the exact solution has none. Self-consistency is therefore not a proof of physical accuracy.
