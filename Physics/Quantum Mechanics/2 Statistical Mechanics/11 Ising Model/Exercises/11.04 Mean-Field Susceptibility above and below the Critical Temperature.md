> [!TLDR]
> Differentiate the self-consistency equation to compare susceptibility amplitudes on the two sides of the transition.

**Exercise 11.4.** Verify that the mean-field susceptibility in Equation (11.7) diverges as $(\thermal{T}-\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})^{-1}$ for $\thermal{T}\to\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^+$.

Also determine the susceptibility below the critical temperature by differentiating the self-consistency equation when $\magnetic{m}\ne0$. The source asks you to show that

$$
\magnetic{\chi}=\frac{1}{2\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}(\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}-\thermal{T})}.
$$

Thus the leading divergence has half the amplitude found in the paramagnetic phase.

See [[11.03.04 Critical Mean-Field Exponents]].

> [!note] Range of the displayed result
> The stated formula below the critical temperature is the leading critical asymptote, rather than an exact expression at every lower temperature. The full mean-field result is
>
> $$
> \magnetic{\chi}=
> \frac{\thermal{\beta}(1-\magnetic{m}^2)}
> {1-\thermal{\beta}z\energy{J}(1-\magnetic{m}^2)}.
> $$

## Derive the amplitude ratio

Use ferromagnetic mean field and differentiate with respect to the field energy at fixed temperature and coupling. Below the transition, take the response within one selected phase as the field tends to zero from that side. Crossing the coexistence line switches phases and is not this derivative.

Differentiating $\magnetic{m}=\tanh[\thermal{\beta}(z\energy{J}\magnetic{m}+\energy{h})]$ gives

$$
\magnetic{\chi}
=\thermal{\beta}(1-\magnetic{m}^2)
(z\energy{J}\magnetic{\chi}+1).
$$

Solving for the susceptibility gives the full expression in the note above. Above the transition, the zero-field stable root is $\magnetic{m}=0$ and $z\energy{J}=\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$. This yields the stated Curie–Weiss law.

Below the transition, set $\thermal{\epsilon}=(\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}-\thermal{T})/\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}>0$. The critical magnetization law gives $\magnetic{m}^2=3\thermal{\epsilon}+O(\thermal{\epsilon}^2)$, so

$$
1-\thermal{\beta}z\energy{J}(1-\magnetic{m}^2)
=1-\frac{1-3\thermal{\epsilon}+O(\thermal{\epsilon}^2)}{1-\thermal{\epsilon}}
=2\thermal{\epsilon}+O(\thermal{\epsilon}^2).
$$

The numerator tends to $1/(\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})$. Division gives $\magnetic{\chi}\sim1/[2\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}(\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}-\thermal{T})]$. Both sides therefore have exponent one, but the below-transition amplitude is half the above-transition amplitude when the same temperature-distance convention is used.

The result is positive and has inverse-energy units. It concerns stable branches and small fields near the mean-field critical point. As a separate limit, the full selected-branch expression tends to zero at zero temperature when the spins saturate; the near-critical asymptote need not satisfy that far-away limit. The amplitude ratio is a prediction of mean field, not an exact result for the two-dimensional Ising model.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\chi}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Susceptibility per spin | The derivative of magnetization with respect to the field energy. |
| $\thermal{T},\ \thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature and its critical value. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\magnetic{m},\ \magnetic{m}_0,\ \magnetic{m}_{\mathrm s}$ | $1$ | Magnetization per spin | The mean normalized spin; subscripts identify spontaneous values. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The nearest-neighbour interaction energy scale. |
| $z$ | $1$ | Coordination number | The number of nearest neighbours per bulk site. |
| $\thermal{\epsilon}$ | $1$ | Reduced distance below the transition | The positive temperature difference divided by the critical temperature. |
