> [!TLDR]
> Apply duality with the honeycomb lattice to obtain the triangular-lattice critical temperature.

**Exercise 11.3.** Use Kramers–Wannier duality to determine the critical temperature of the Ising model on the triangular lattice. Each site has $z=6$ nearest neighbours.

The triangular lattice is not self-dual. Its dual is the honeycomb lattice, with $z=3$. The self-duality condition $\sinh(2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c})=1$ therefore applies only to the square lattice in the form used in this chapter.

For the triangular lattice, the source supplies the exact result

$$
e^{4\thermal{\beta}_{\mathrm c}\energy{J}}=3,
\qquad
\tanh(2\thermal{\beta}_{\mathrm c}\energy{J})=\frac12,
$$

or

$$
\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}
=\frac{4\energy{J}}{\ln3}
\approx3.641\,\energy{J}.
$$

See [[11.04.02 Kramers–Wannier Duality]].

> [!note] Additional relation needed
> Duality alone relates the triangular and honeycomb models. It does not locate their critical temperatures independently. A further relation, such as the star–triangle transformation, is needed to derive the supplied result.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $z$ | $1$ | Coordination number | The number of nearest neighbours at a lattice site. |
| $\thermal{K}$ | $1$ | Reduced coupling | The dimensionless coupling defined in this article; star and c mark dual and critical values. |
