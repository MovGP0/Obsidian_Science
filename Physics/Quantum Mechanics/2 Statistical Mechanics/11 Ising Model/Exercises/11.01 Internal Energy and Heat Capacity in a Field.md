> [!TLDR]
> Use the largest transfer-matrix eigenvalue to calculate energy and heat capacity in a nonzero field.

**Exercise 11.1.** Calculate the internal energy and heat capacity of the one-dimensional Ising model with external field $\energy{h}\ne0$. Use the eigenvalue $\lambda_+$ from Equation (11.3) in [[11.02.02 Transfer-Matrix Method]].

Verify that $\energy{h}=0$ recovers Example 11.1 in [[11.02.05 Correlation Function and Correlation Length]].

> [!note] Notation
> The related notes use $\energy{u}$ for internal energy per spin, $\thermal{c}$ for heat capacity per spin, and $\thermal{\beta}=1/(\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T})$ for inverse temperature.

## Solution route and checks

Use the canonical infinite-chain free energy, with exchange coupling and field energy held fixed. The Hamiltonian is then independent of inverse temperature, so differentiation of the partition sum gives the mean energy. Holding a ratio of field to temperature fixed instead would define a different derivative.

From $\energy{f}=-\ln\lambda_+/\thermal{\beta}$, obtain

$$
\energy{u}=-\frac{\partial\ln\lambda_+}{\partial\thermal{\beta}},
\qquad
\thermal{c}=\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{\beta}^2
\frac{\partial^2\ln\lambda_+}{\partial\thermal{\beta}^2}
=\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{\beta}^2
\left[\frac{\lambda_+''}{\lambda_+}
-\left(\frac{\lambda_+'}{\lambda_+}\right)^2\right].
$$

Primes denote derivatives with respect to inverse temperature at fixed $\energy{J}$ and $\energy{h}$. A useful way to take them is to factor the eigenvalue:

$$
\ln\lambda_+
=\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}
+\ln\!\left[
\cosh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{h})
+\sqrt{\sinh^2(\thermal{\beta}\energy{h})+e^{-4\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}}
\right].
$$

The first term contributes $-\energy{J}$ to the energy and no second derivative. Both the hyperbolic functions and the exponential inside the square root must be differentiated. The second logarithmic derivative is the energy variance per spin, so the heat capacity must be nonnegative. Its unit is energy per temperature.

At zero field, the factorization gives $\lambda_+=2\cosh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})$. The derivatives then give $\energy{u}=-\energy{J}\tanh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})$ and $\thermal{c}=\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})^2\operatorname{sech}^2(\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})$, as requested. As a separate check, at zero coupling the spins are independent and the results become $\energy{u}=-\energy{h}\tanh(\thermal{\beta}\energy{h})$ and $\thermal{c}=\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}(\thermal{\beta}\energy{h})^2\operatorname{sech}^2(\thermal{\beta}\energy{h})$. These checks probe different terms of the field-dependent derivative.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{u}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy per spin | The mean energy per spin. |
| $\thermal{c}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity per spin | The temperature derivative of internal energy per spin. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\energy{f}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free energy per spin | The infinite-chain Helmholtz free energy per spin. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
