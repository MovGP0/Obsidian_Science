> [!TLDR]
> Yang's exact magnetization vanishes at the critical temperature with exponent one eighth.

Yang (1952) calculated the spontaneous magnetization below the critical temperature, with $\energy{h}\to0^+$:

$$
\magnetic{m}_0(\thermal{T})=
\left[1-\sinh^{-4}(2\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})\right]^{1/8}.
\tag{11.9}
$$

It vanishes as $\magnetic{m}_0\sim(\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}-\thermal{T})^{1/8}$ for $\thermal{T}\to\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^-$. Thus the critical exponent is $\beta_{\mathrm c}=1/8$. Figure 11.5 compares this behaviour with the mean-field prediction.

**Fig. 11.5** Spontaneous magnetization versus reduced temperature. The mean-field critical law (blue) vanishes with exponent $1/2$. The two-dimensional Ising critical law (red, dashed) vanishes with exponent $1/8$.

> [!note] Interpretation of the source plot
> The source labels the red curve “Ising 2D exact”, but the displayed shapes are schematic normalized power laws. This reproduction uses $(1-\thermal{T}/\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})^{1/2}$ and $(1-\thermal{T}/\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})^{1/8}$ below the transition, and zero above it. They show the different exponents; they are not the full mean-field solution or Yang's formula over the whole temperature range. Use Equation (11.9) for exact numerical magnetization.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{m}$ | $1$ | Magnetization per spin | The mean normalized spin; subscripts identify particular or spontaneous values. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
