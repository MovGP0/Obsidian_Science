> [!TLDR]
> Onsager's exact square-lattice solution gives a critical temperature below the mean-field prediction.

In 1944, Lars Onsager gave the first exact solution of the square-lattice Ising model without an external field.

**Theorem 11.2 (Onsager, 1944).** For equal couplings $\energy{J}_1=\energy{J}_2=\energy{J}$ and $\energy{h}=0$, the thermodynamic-limit free energy per spin is

$$
\energy{f}=-\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}
\left[
\ln2+\frac{1}{2\pi^2}
\int_0^\pi d\theta_1\int_0^\pi d\theta_2\,
\ln\!\left(
\cosh^2(2\thermal{K})
-\sinh(2\thermal{K})(\cos\theta_1+\cos\theta_2)
\right)
\right],
$$

where $\thermal{K}=\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}$. The critical temperature satisfies

$$
\sinh(2\thermal{\beta}_{\mathrm c}\energy{J})=1,
$$

which gives

$$
\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}
=\frac{2\energy{J}}{\ln(1+\sqrt2)}
\approx2.269\,\energy{J}.
\tag{11.8}
$$

> [!note] Comparison with mean-field theory
> For the square lattice, $z=4$ and $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{MF}}=4\energy{J}$. Mean-field theory overestimates the exact critical temperature by about 76%. Fluctuations lower the transition temperature.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{f}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free energy per spin | The Helmholtz free energy per spin; subscript 0 marks a reference value. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\thermal{K}$ | $1$ | Reduced coupling | The dimensionless coupling defined in this article; star and c mark dual and critical values. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $z$ | $1$ | Coordination number | The number of nearest neighbours at a lattice site. |
