> [!TLDR]
> Exact Ising solutions show how dimension and fluctuations determine phase transitions, while mean-field theory gives a simpler approximate description.

The main results are compared below.

The exact entries refer to classical nearest-neighbour ferromagnets at zero field: an infinite chain and an isotropic square lattice. The mean-field column replaces correlated neighbours by a uniform average. Use it as an approximation to compare with the exact models, rather than as a third lattice with its own exact solution. In all critical formulas, take the thermodynamic limit and specify a selected phase when magnetization is nonzero.

| Property                                       | Ising 1D                                    | Ising 2D                                                    | Mean field                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Critical temperature $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$ | $0$                                         | $\dfrac{2\energy{J}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\ln(1+\sqrt2)}$ | $z\energy{J}/\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$                |
| Method                                         | Transfer matrix                             | Onsager solution                                            | Weiss approximation                                  |
| Magnetization exponent $\beta$                 | —                                           | $1/8$                                                       | $1/2$                                                |
| Susceptibility exponent $\gamma$               | —                                           | $7/4$                                                       | $1$                                                  |
| Correlation-length exponent $\nu$              | Exponential divergence as $\thermal{T}\to0$ | $1$                                                         | $1/2$                                                |
| Correlation function $G(r)$                    | $e^{-r/\xi}$ at zero field                  | $r^{-1/4}$ at $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$                     | $e^{-r/\xi}$ (schematic decay away from criticality) |
| Fluctuations                                   | Dominant                                    | Relevant                                                    | Neglected                                            |

> [!note] Scope of the comparison
> The source writes “$\infty$ ($\thermal{T}\to0$)” in the one-dimensional $\nu$ row. This denotes an essential, exponential divergence rather than a finite power-law exponent. Its correlation-function row compares different temperature regimes. The mean-field entry shows only exponential decay away from criticality; it is not a complete expression for the critical spatial correlation function.

## Connections

1. The **transfer matrix** is a basic example of methods that split a partition function into spatial parts. It connects to quantum transfer matrices and the Bethe ansatz.
2. The **mean-field approximation**, or Weiss approximation, leads to Landau theory of phase transitions. Ginzburg's extensions connect it to field theory in condensed matter.
3. **Duality** connects strong and weak coupling. Related ideas occur in string theory, gauge theory, and topological condensed matter.
4. **Critical exponents** and **universality** provide the language of the renormalization group, which the next chapter develops.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $z$ | $1$ | Coordination number | The number of nearest neighbours at a lattice site. |
| $G(r)$ | $1$ | Spin correlation | The connected correlation of normalized spins. |
| $r$ | $1$ | Lattice separation | The distance between sites, measured in lattice spacings. |
| $\xi$ | $1$ | Correlation length | The correlation range, measured in lattice spacings. |
