> [!TLDR]
> The Ising Hamiltonian assigns an energy to each configuration of binary spins on a lattice.

Consider a regular lattice $\Lambda$ with $N$ sites. Each site $i\in\Lambda$ has a discrete spin variable:

$$
\magnetic{\sigma}_i\in\{+1,-1\}.
$$

A configuration specifies all spins $\{\magnetic{\sigma}_i\}_{i\in\Lambda}$. The configuration space contains $2^N$ states.

**Definition 11.1 (Ising Hamiltonian).** For nearest-neighbour interactions and an external field, the Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=-\energy{J}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_j-\energy{h}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\magnetic{\sigma}_i.
\tag{11.1}
$$

The sum over $\langle i,j\rangle$ counts each nearest-neighbour pair once. The exchange coupling $\energy{J}$ has units of energy. The field parameter $\energy{h}$ also has units of energy; it already includes the magnetic-moment factor $g\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}$.

> [!note] Remark 11.1
> - For $\energy{J}>0$, the system is **ferromagnetic**. Parallel neighbouring spins give the lowest energy.
> - For $\energy{J}<0$, the system is **antiferromagnetic**. Antiparallel neighbouring spins give the lowest energy.
> - Since $\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_j\in\{+1,-1\}$, each bond contributes $-\energy{J}$ for parallel spins or $+\energy{J}$ for antiparallel spins.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $N$ | $1$ | Spin count | The number of lattice sites or spins. |
| $\magnetic{\sigma}_i$ | $1$ | Ising spin | The normalized spin at site i. |
| $\energy{\mathcal H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy of a spin configuration. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $g$ | $1$ | Landé factor | The dimensionless magnetic-moment factor. |
| $\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,T^{-1}}$ | Bohr magneton | The magnetic-moment scale included in the field parameter. |
