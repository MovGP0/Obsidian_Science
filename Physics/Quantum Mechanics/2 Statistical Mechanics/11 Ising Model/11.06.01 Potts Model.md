> [!TLDR]
> The Potts model replaces two Ising states by a specified number of discrete spin states.

The **Potts model** (Potts, 1952) allows $q$ discrete states:

$$
\magnetic{\sigma}_i\in\{1,2,\ldots,q\},
\qquad
\energy{\mathcal H}=-\energy{J}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}
\delta_{\sigma_i,\sigma_j}.
$$

Here $\delta$ is the Kronecker delta.

The integers are state labels, not components of a vector or measured magnetic moments. For positive coupling, equal neighbouring labels have bond energy $-\energy{J}$ and unequal labels have energy zero. A common permutation of all labels leaves the energy unchanged. This discrete symmetry generalizes the two-state inversion symmetry.

To check the two-state correspondence, relabel the states by $\magnetic{s}_i=\pm1$. Then $\delta_{\sigma_i,\sigma_j}=(1+\magnetic{s}_i\magnetic{s}_j)/2$. Each bond term becomes $-\energy{J}/2-(\energy{J}/2)\magnetic{s}_i\magnetic{s}_j$: the Ising exchange is one half of the Potts exchange, and the remaining constant shifts all configuration energies equally. It changes the free-energy reference but not normalized configuration probabilities.

- For $q=2$, the model is equivalent to the Ising model after a coupling rescaling and an additive energy shift.
- For $q=3,4$, the transition in two dimensions is continuous.
- For $q\ge5$, the transition in two dimensions is first order.
- For the ferromagnetic square-lattice model, the exact critical condition is

$$
e^{\thermal{\beta}_{\mathrm c}\energy{J}}=1+\sqrt q.
$$

The transition statements above concern the pure zero-field ferromagnetic model on the two-dimensional square lattice, not an arbitrary graph or an antiferromagnet. The change in transition order is established in [Baxter's critical-temperature analysis](https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3719/6/23/005). At $q=2$, the critical condition gives $\thermal{\beta}_{\mathrm c}\energy{J}=\ln(1+\sqrt2)$; with the one-half exchange conversion, this agrees with the square-lattice Ising result. This checks the factor that would otherwise give a critical-temperature error of two.

The Potts model describes structures with several states or phases, such as foams, granular structures, and graph colouring.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\sigma}_i$ | $1$ | Potts state label | The discrete state at site i. |
| $\magnetic{s}_i$ | $1$ | Binary spin | The two-state label mapped to plus or minus one. |
| $q$ | $1$ | State count | The number of allowed spin states in the Potts model. |
| $\energy{\mathcal H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy of a spin configuration. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
