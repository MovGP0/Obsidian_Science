## Example 11.2: Transfer matrix for three spins

Consider $N=3$ spins with periodic boundaries $\magnetic{\sigma}_4=\magnetic{\sigma}_1$ and $\energy{h}=0$. Write

$$
\mathbf T=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}&e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}\\
e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}&e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
a=e^{\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}},\quad b=e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}.
$$

Then

$$
\mathbf T^2=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^2+b^2&2ab\\
2ab&a^2+b^2
\end{pmatrix},
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf T^3
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
(a^2+b^2)a+2ab^2&(a^2+b^2)b+2a^2b\\
(a^2+b^2)b+2a^2b&(a^2+b^2)a+2ab^2
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
a^3+3ab^2&3a^2b+b^3\\
3a^2b+b^3&a^3+3ab^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The partition function is

$$
\mathcal Z=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf T^3)
=2(a^3+3ab^2)
=2\left(e^{3\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}+3e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}\right).
$$

For a direct check, the configurations $(+++)$ and $(---)$ each have energy $-3\energy{J}$ and weight $e^{3\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}$. Their total contribution is $2e^{3\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}$.

The other six configurations have one reversed spin or two reversed spins. For example, $(++-)$ gives bond products $+1,-1,-1$, so its energy is $-\energy{J}(1-1-1)=\energy{J}$. The six states contribute $6e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}$.

Thus

$$
\mathcal Z=2e^{3\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}}+6e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}},
$$

in agreement with the matrix result.

## Example 11.3: Mean field on the simple cubic lattice

For a simple cubic lattice in $d=3$, the coordination number is $z=6$. Mean-field theory gives

$$
\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{MF}}
=z\energy{J}=6\energy{J}.
$$

The source quotes the Monte Carlo value $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}\approx4.511\,\energy{J}$. The mean-field estimate is about 33% too high.

Above the mean-field critical temperature, the Curie–Weiss law gives

$$
\magnetic{\chi}
=\frac{1}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}(\thermal{T}-\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{MF}})}
=\frac{1}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}-6\energy{J}}.
$$

At $\thermal{T}=1.1\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{MF}}=6.6\energy{J}/\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$,

$$
\magnetic{\chi}
=\frac{1}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}(6.6\energy{J}/\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B})-6\energy{J}}
=\frac{1}{0.6\energy{J}}.
$$

At $\thermal{T}=0.9\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{MF}}$, the zero-field self-consistency equation becomes

$$
\magnetic{m}
=\tanh\!\left(\frac{6\energy{J}\magnetic{m}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}}\right)
=\tanh\!\left(\frac{\magnetic{m}}{0.9}\right)
\approx\tanh(1.111\magnetic{m}).
$$

Starting from $\magnetic{m}_0=0.5$, the source iterates

$$
\magnetic{m}_1=\tanh(0.556)\approx0.505,
\qquad
\magnetic{m}_2=\tanh(0.561)\approx0.509.
$$

The source continues with

$$
\magnetic{m}_3=\tanh(0.565)\approx0.512,
\qquad
\magnetic{m}_\infty\approx0.525.
$$

The near-critical approximation, Equation (11.6), gives

$$
\magnetic{m}\approx
\sqrt{3\left(1-\frac{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}}\right)}
=\sqrt{0.3}\approx0.548.
$$

The source describes this agreement as satisfactory when the temperature is not too close to the critical point.

> [!note] Numerical correction and approximation range
> Direct iteration of $\magnetic{m}=\tanh(\magnetic{m}/0.9)$ gives $\magnetic{m}\approx0.52543$. The first three unrounded iterates are approximately $0.50467$, $0.50853$, and $0.51170$. The square-root formula is an asymptotic approximation near the critical temperature; its accuracy improves as the temperature approaches that point from below.

## Example 11.4: Spontaneous magnetization from Yang's formula

For $\thermal{T}=0.8\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$, use Equation (11.8):

$$
\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}
=0.8\,\frac{2\energy{J}}{\ln(1+\sqrt2)},
\qquad
\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}
=\frac{\ln(1+\sqrt2)}{1.6}
\approx\frac{0.8814}{1.6}\approx0.5509.
$$

The source evaluates $\sinh(2\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})=\sinh(1.1018)\approx1.3386$, and then writes

$$
\begin{aligned}
\magnetic{m}_0
&=\left[1-(1.3386)^{-4}\right]^{1/8}\\
&\approx\left[1-\frac{1}{3.210}\right]^{1/8}
=(0.6885)^{1/8}\\
&\approx e^{-0.0467}\approx0.9544.
\end{aligned}
$$

It concludes that the spontaneous magnetization is about $0.954$, so the system is almost fully magnetized.

> [!note] Numerical correction
> With unrounded values, $\sinh(2\thermal{\beta}\energy{J})\approx1.33851$, its fourth power is approximately $3.20990$, and Equation (11.9) gives $\magnetic{m}_0\approx0.95441$. The final magnetization quoted in the source is consistent to its stated precision.

For comparison, the source uses the mean-field critical approximation at the same reduced temperature:

$$
\magnetic{m}^{\mathrm{MF}}
\approx\sqrt{3(1-0.8)}
=\sqrt{0.6}\approx0.775.
$$

It compares this value with the exact two-dimensional result $\magnetic{m}_0\approx0.954$. The smaller two-dimensional exponent $1/8$ gives a flatter critical curve than the mean-field exponent $1/2$. Critical fluctuations also lower the square-lattice critical temperature from $4\energy{J}/\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ to about $2.269\energy{J}/\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$.

> [!note] Mean-field comparison
> The value $0.775$ uses the leading near-critical formula at $\thermal{T}/\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}=0.8$. Solving the full mean-field equation instead gives approximately $0.71041$. The exponent comparison is asymptotic near the critical point.

## Example 11.5: Kramers–Wannier duality

The two-dimensional duality maps the high-temperature phase to the low-temperature phase. The source contrasts this with one dimension, where the substitution

$$
\magnetic{\mu}_i=\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_{i+1}
$$

turns the interaction into a bond-spin field with

$$
\energy{h}_{\mathrm{eff}}
=\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}\,
\operatorname{arctanh}(\tanh\thermal{K})
=\energy{J},
\qquad
\thermal{K}=\thermal{\beta}\energy{J}.
$$

There is no singularity for positive temperature, consistent with $\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}=\infty$.

> [!note] Periodic-chain constraint
> On a closed chain, the bond spins obey $\prod_i\magnetic{\mu}_i=1$ and are therefore not all independent. The high-temperature result below retains the corresponding finite-size term.

### High-temperature expansion in one dimension

At zero field,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Z
&=\sum_{\{\sigma\}}\prod_i e^{\thermal{K}\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_{i+1}}\\
&=\sum_{\{\sigma\}}\prod_i
\left[\cosh\thermal{K}
+\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_{i+1}\sinh\thermal{K}\right]\\
&=(\cosh\thermal{K})^N
\sum_{\{\sigma\}}\prod_i
\left[1+t\,\magnetic{\sigma}_i\magnetic{\sigma}_{i+1}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $t=\tanh\thermal{K}$. Expand the product and sum over all spins. Only terms in which every spin occurs to an even power survive. For a closed chain,

$$
\mathcal Z=2^N(\cosh\thermal{K})^N
\left(1+\tanh^N\thermal{K}\right).
$$

The helper $t$ in this expansion is not the reduced temperature.

### Duality on the square lattice

The high-temperature expansion sums over configurations of closed loops on the lattice. The low-temperature expansion sums over domain walls on the dual lattice. Matching the two expansions, apart from regular factors, gives

$$
\sinh(2\thermal{K})\sinh(2\thermal{K}^*)=1,
\qquad
\tanh\thermal{K}^*=e^{-2\thermal{K}}.
$$

Large $\thermal{K}$ (low temperature) maps to small $\thermal{K}^*$ (high temperature). At the fixed point,

$$
\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}=\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}^*,
\qquad
\sinh(2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c})=1,
\qquad
\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}=\frac12\ln(1+\sqrt2).
$$

This reproduces the Onsager result, Equation (11.8).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $N$ | $1$ | Spin count | The number of lattice sites or spins. |
| $\magnetic{\sigma}_i$ | $1$ | Ising spin | The normalized spin at site i. |
| $\magnetic{\mu}_i$ | $1$ | Bond spin | The product of the two spins at the ends of a bond. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\mathcal Z$ | $1$ | Partition function | The sum of Boltzmann weights of the configurations. |
| $z$ | $1$ | Coordination number | The number of nearest neighbours at a lattice site. |
| $d,\ d_{\mathrm c}$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The lattice dimension and its upper critical value. |

| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\magnetic{\chi}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Susceptibility per spin | The derivative of magnetization with respect to the field energy. |
| $\magnetic{m}$ | $1$ | Magnetization per spin | The mean normalized spin; subscripts identify particular or spontaneous values. |
| $\thermal{K}$ | $1$ | Reduced coupling | The dimensionless coupling defined in this article; star and c mark dual and critical values. |
| $\energy{h}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Effective field parameter | The mean field and applied field in energy units. |
