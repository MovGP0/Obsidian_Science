> [!TLDR]
> The magnetization changes continuously with temperature at zero field and jumps when the field crosses zero below the critical temperature.

The behaviour of the order parameter $\magnetic{m}_{\mathrm s}(\thermal{T})$ identifies the transition.

- **Second-order (continuous) transition:** $\magnetic{m}_{\mathrm s}\to0$ continuously as $\thermal{T}\to\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^-$. The free energy and its first derivatives are continuous. Second derivatives, such as heat capacity and susceptibility, have singular behaviour.
- **First-order transition:** the order parameter has a finite jump along a coexistence line.

For the ferromagnetic Ising model in $d\ge2$, changing temperature through $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$ at $\energy{h}=0$ gives a continuous transition. At $\thermal{T}<\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$, changing the field across $\energy{h}=0$ gives a first-order transition with a finite magnetization jump.

These statements describe different paths in the thermodynamic phase diagram of the short-range ferromagnet. Along the zero-field temperature path, follow one selected ordered phase: its magnetization magnitude falls continuously to zero. Along a field path at a fixed temperature below the critical value, the stable phase changes from negative to positive magnetization. The jump is $2\magnetic{m}_{\mathrm s}$ and vanishes at the critical endpoint.

The field jump is a first-derivative test. Since $\magnetic{m}=-\partial\energy{f}/\partial\energy{h}$, opposite one-sided magnetizations imply a cusp in the equilibrium free energy as a function of field. The free energy remains continuous at coexistence. For a finite system with symmetric boundaries, this cusp is rounded and the exact zero-field ensemble mean is zero. A discontinuity therefore requires both a specified path and the thermodynamic limit; an arbitrary nonzero field is not a coexistence condition.

> [!note] Source correction: coexistence field
> The source first associates a first-order transition with $\energy{h}\ne0$. Its next paragraph correctly places the coexistence line at $\energy{h}=0$ below the critical temperature. The first-order jump occurs when this zero-field line is crossed.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{m}$ | $1$ | Magnetization per spin | The mean normalized spin; subscripts identify particular or spontaneous values. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\energy{f}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free energy per spin | The equilibrium Helmholtz free energy per spin. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The dimension of the lattice. |
