> [!TLDR]
> Internal energy, enthalpy, Helmholtz free energy, and Gibbs energy describe the same thermodynamics with different natural variables. Choose the potential that matches the controlled variables.

The following relations assume a fixed amount of matter. [[01.05.04 Chemical Potential and Open Systems|Section 1.5.4]] adds particle exchange.

## Internal energy

The natural variables of $\energy{U}(\thermal{S},\mechanical{V})$ follow from:

$$
\mathrm d\energy{U}=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}
-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}.
$$

The source calls this the natural potential of an isolated system and specifies fixed entropy and volume.

> [!note] Equilibrium constraints
> Natural variables and isolation are different statements. An isolated rigid system has fixed internal energy, volume, and particle number; its equilibrium criterion is maximum entropy. The equivalent energy-minimum criterion instead holds at fixed entropy, volume, and particle number.

## Enthalpy

**Enthalpy** $\energy{H}(\thermal{S},\mechanical{P})$ is defined by:

$$
\energy{H}=\energy{U}+\mechanical{P}\mechanical{V}.
$$

Differentiate and substitute the fundamental relation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\energy{H}
&=\mathrm d\energy{U}+\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}\\
&=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}
+\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}\\
&=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Its natural variables are entropy and pressure. The derivatives give:

$$
\thermal{T}=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\thermal{S}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}},
\qquad
\mechanical{V}=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\mechanical{P}}\right)_{\thermal{S}}.
$$

Enthalpy is particularly useful for isobaric processes. At constant pressure, for a closed system with only pressure–volume work:

$$
\Delta\energy{H}=\thermal{Q}_P,
$$

where $\thermal{Q}_P$ is the heat exchanged at constant pressure.

## Helmholtz free energy

**Helmholtz free energy** $\energy{F}(\thermal{T},\mechanical{V})$ is defined by:

$$
\energy{F}=\energy{U}-\thermal{T}\thermal{S}.
$$

Its differential is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\energy{F}
&=\mathrm d\energy{U}-\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}\\
&=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}
-\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}\\
&=-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Its natural variables are temperature and volume. The derivatives give:

$$
\thermal{S}=-\left(\frac{\partial\energy{F}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}},
\qquad
\mechanical{P}=-\left(\frac{\partial\energy{F}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}.
$$

Helmholtz free energy is the natural potential of a fixed-volume system in contact with a thermostat at temperature $\thermal{T}$. At equilibrium, the system minimizes $\energy{F}$ at fixed temperature, volume, and particle number.

## Gibbs energy

**Gibbs energy** $\energy{G}(\thermal{T},\mechanical{P})$ is defined by:

$$
\energy{G}=\energy{U}-\thermal{T}\thermal{S}+\mechanical{P}\mechanical{V}
=\energy{H}-\thermal{T}\thermal{S}
=\energy{F}+\mechanical{P}\mechanical{V}.
$$

Its differential is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\energy{G}
&=\mathrm d\energy{F}+\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}\\
&=-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}
+\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}\\
&=-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Its natural variables are temperature and pressure. The derivatives give:

$$
\thermal{S}=-\left(\frac{\partial\energy{G}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}},
\qquad
\mechanical{V}=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{G}}{\partial\mechanical{P}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}.
$$

Gibbs energy is the natural potential of a system in contact with a thermostat and a barostat. It is minimal at equilibrium for fixed temperature, pressure, and particle number. These conditions are common in chemistry and many physics experiments.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Potential with entropy and volume as natural variables. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Enthalpy | Internal energy plus the pressure–volume product. |
| $\energy{F}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | Internal energy minus temperature times entropy. |
| $\energy{G}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Gibbs energy | Helmholtz free energy plus the pressure–volume product. |
| $\thermal{S}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Thermal natural variable of internal energy and enthalpy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Thermal natural variable of the free energies. |
| $\mechanical{P}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical natural variable of enthalpy and Gibbs energy. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Mechanical natural variable of internal and Helmholtz energies. |
| $\thermal{Q}_P$ | $\unit{J}$ | Isobaric heat transfer | Heat transferred into the system at constant pressure. |
