> [!TLDR]
> This chapter reviews the thermodynamic concepts and notation needed to derive macroscopic laws from microscopic physics.

Statistical mechanics aims to derive the laws of thermodynamics from a microscopic analysis of matter. To do this, we must understand the language and mathematical methods of thermodynamics. A precise knowledge of entropy, free energy, and chemical potential is needed to understand why statistical mechanics works and how it extends phenomenological thermodynamics.

This chapter assumes that the reader has already met these concepts in a general physics course. Its purpose is to organize the mathematical framework, explain details that introductory courses often treat briefly, and introduce the notation used throughout the book.

Readers who already know thermodynamic potentials and Maxwell relations can continue with Chapter 2. However, they should at least review [[01.07 Thermodynamic Stability|section 1.7 on thermodynamic stability]], which introductory courses often omit.

Start with [[01.01 Thermodynamic Systems and State Variables]]. The full chapter structure is in [[00.00 Statistical Mechanics|Contents]].
