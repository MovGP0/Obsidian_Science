> [!TLDR]
> An equation of state relates pressure, volume, and temperature. For a simple system with a fixed amount of matter, two independent variables specify the equilibrium state.

For a simple system with one component and no chemical reactions, the equilibrium-state space is two-dimensional when the amount of matter is fixed. Independent variables can be chosen as $(\thermal{T},\mechanical{V})$, $(\thermal{T},\mechanical{P})$, or $(\mechanical{P},\mechanical{V})$. All other state variables are functions of these two variables. The **equation of state** expresses this constraint:

$$
f(\mechanical{P},\mechanical{V},\thermal{T})=0.
$$

For an ideal gas:

$$
\mechanical{P}\mechanical{V}=\mechanical{n}\thermal{R}\thermal{T}
=\mechanical{N}\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}.
$$

Here, $\mechanical{N}$ is the particle number and $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ is the Boltzmann constant. A real fluid is often described by the van der Waals equation, which the source discusses in Example 1.3.

> [!note]
> Unless stated otherwise, the following treatment uses systems with one phase and one component. Multiple components are introduced explicitly when needed, for example in [[01.05 Thermodynamic Potentials|section 1.5]]. Multiple phases are treated in Chapter 10.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{P}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical state variable of the gas. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume occupied by the gas. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
| $\mechanical{n}$ | $\unit{mol}$ | Amount of substance | Amount of gas in moles. |
| $\mechanical{N}$ | $1$ | Particle number | Number of gas particles. |
| $\thermal{R}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Molar gas constant | Constant in the molar ideal-gas equation. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Constant in the particle form of the ideal-gas equation. |
