> [!TLDR]
> A Carnot engine uses two reversible isothermal processes and two reversible adiabatic processes. Its efficiency is the largest possible between the same two reservoir temperatures.

## Definition 1.7 — Carnot cycle

The **Carnot cycle** is a reversible thermodynamic cycle with four quasi-static processes between two heat reservoirs at temperatures $\thermal{T}_H>\thermal{T}_C$:

1. Reversible isothermal expansion at $\thermal{T}_H$, with heat absorption $\thermal{Q}_H>0$.
2. Reversible adiabatic expansion from $\thermal{T}_H$ to $\thermal{T}_C$.
3. Reversible isothermal compression at $\thermal{T}_C$, with heat rejection of magnitude $\thermal{Q}_C>0$ to the cold reservoir.
4. Reversible adiabatic compression from $\thermal{T}_C$ to $\thermal{T}_H$.

The arrows show heat-engine operation: $A\to B$ is the hot isotherm, $B\to C$ is an adiabat, $C\to D$ is the cold isotherm, and $D\to A$ is an adiabat. The axes use schematic scales.

The efficiency of a heat engine between these reservoirs is:

$$
\energy{\eta}=\frac{\energy{W}}{\thermal{Q}_H}
=1-\frac{\thermal{Q}_C}{\thermal{Q}_H}.
$$

## Theorem 1.4 — Carnot's theorem

Among all heat engines that operate between $\thermal{T}_H$ and $\thermal{T}_C$, a reversible engine has the greatest efficiency:

$$
\energy{\eta}_{\mathrm{rev}}=1-\frac{\thermal{T}_C}{\thermal{T}_H}. \tag{1.4}
$$

Every irreversible engine has $\energy{\eta}<\energy{\eta}_{\mathrm{rev}}$.

Carnot's theorem has a fundamental consequence: it defines the **absolute temperature scale**, or **Kelvin scale**, independently of the substance used in a thermometer.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\thermal{T}_H,\thermal{T}_C$ | $\unit{K}$ | Reservoir temperatures | Hot and cold absolute temperatures. |
| $\thermal{Q}_H,\thermal{Q}_C$ | $\unit{J}$ | Heat transfers | Positive magnitudes of heat absorbed and rejected during one cycle. |
| $\energy{W}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Work | Net work performed by the engine in one cycle. |
| $\energy{\eta},\energy{\eta}_{\mathrm{rev}}$ | $1$ | Efficiencies | General engine efficiency and reversible upper limit. |
| $\mechanical{P}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Vertical coordinate of the cycle. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Horizontal coordinate of the cycle. |
| $\thermal{\gamma}$ | $1$ | Heat-capacity ratio | Ratio of constant-pressure to constant-volume heat capacity. |
