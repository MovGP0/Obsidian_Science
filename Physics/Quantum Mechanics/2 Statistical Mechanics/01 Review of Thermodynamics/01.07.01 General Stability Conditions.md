> [!TLDR]
> A stable equilibrium resists small disturbances. For the simple system considered here, local thermal and mechanical stability require positive constant-volume heat capacity and positive isothermal compressibility.

A system is in a **stable equilibrium state** if it returns spontaneously to its original state after a small disturbance. Stability conditions follow by requiring a minimum of the appropriate thermodynamic potential, or a maximum of entropy for an isolated system.

Consider an isolated system with fixed internal energy $\energy{U}$, volume $\mechanical{V}$, and particle number $\mechanical{N}$. At equilibrium, entropy is maximal. Divide the system into two subsystems and consider a fluctuation. In the Taylor expansion of entropy about equilibrium, the first-order term must vanish. The second-order term must be negative for a strict local maximum:

$$
\delta^2\thermal{S}<0
\qquad\text{(stability)}. \tag{1.9}
$$

The equivalent energy criterion is:

$$
\delta^2\energy{U}>0
\qquad\text{at fixed }\thermal{S},\mechanical{V},\mechanical{N}.
$$

Expanding the stability conditions gives the following result for a simple homogeneous system at positive temperature and fixed composition.

## Proposition 1.2 — Thermal and mechanical stability

**Thermal stability** requires $\thermal{C}_V>0$:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial^2\energy{U}}{\partial\thermal{S}^{\,2}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{T}}{\partial\thermal{S}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}>0.
$$

**Mechanical stability** requires $\mechanical{\kappa}_T>0$:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial^2\energy{F}}{\partial\mechanical{V}^{\,2}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
-\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}>0.
$$

Thus, at fixed temperature, pressure must decrease when volume increases. For the stated thermal and volume fluctuations, these are the local stability conditions.

Violation of a condition signals instability. For a fluid with mechanical instability, the homogeneous system separates into different phases. The boundary where

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}=0
$$

is the **spinodal**. Between the spinodal and the coexistence curve, the system is **metastable**: small fluctuations do not destabilize it, but a sufficiently large fluctuation can start a new phase by **nucleation**.

> [!note] Scope of the stability test
> The source states the two conditions as necessary and sufficient. Here they apply to local thermal and mechanical stability of the simple, fixed-composition system. Mixtures require additional conditions for composition fluctuations. A local minimum alone also does not establish global stability against formation of another phase.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Fixed energy in the entropy criterion or minimized energy in the equivalent criterion. |
| $\energy{F}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | Potential whose volume curvature tests isothermal mechanical stability. |
| $\thermal{S}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | State function maximized in an isolated system. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Positive absolute temperature in the stability test. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Variable involved in mechanical fluctuations. |
| $\mechanical{P}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Must decrease with increasing volume on a stable isotherm. |
| $\mechanical{N}$ | $1$ | Particle number | Particle count held fixed. |
| $\thermal{C}_V$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Constant-volume heat capacity | Must be positive for strict thermal stability. |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}_T$ | $\unit{Pa^{-1}}$ | Isothermal compressibility | Must be positive for strict mechanical stability. |
