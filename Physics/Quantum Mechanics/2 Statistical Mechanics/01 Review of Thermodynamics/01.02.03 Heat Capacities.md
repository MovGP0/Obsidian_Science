> [!TLDR]
> Heat capacities measure the energy change with temperature under specified constraints. Their difference is related to thermal expansion and isothermal compressibility.

Heat capacities are derivatives of internal energy or enthalpy with respect to temperature under suitable constraints.

## Definition 1.6 — Heat capacities at constant volume and pressure

The **heat capacity at constant volume** is:

$$
\thermal{C}_V=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{U}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}.
$$

The **heat capacity at constant pressure** is:

$$
\thermal{C}_P=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}},
$$

where $\energy{H}=\energy{U}+\mechanical{P}\mechanical{V}$ is the enthalpy, defined in [[01.05.03 Four Thermodynamic Potentials|section 1.5.3]].

The Maxwell relations in [[01.06 Maxwell Relations|section 1.6]] give:

$$
\thermal{C}_P-\thermal{C}_V
=-\thermal{T}\frac{\left(\dfrac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}^2}{\left(\dfrac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}}
=\frac{\thermal{T}\mechanical{V}\thermal{\alpha}_P^{\,2}}{\mechanical{\kappa}_T}. \tag{1.3}
$$

The **isobaric expansion coefficient** is:

$$
\thermal{\alpha}_P=\frac{1}{\mechanical{V}}
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{V}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}}.
$$

The **isothermal compressibility** is:

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}_T=-\frac{1}{\mechanical{V}}
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{V}}{\partial\mechanical{P}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}.
$$

For an ideal gas, the result is the familiar relation:

$$
\thermal{C}_P-\thermal{C}_V=\mechanical{n}\thermal{R}.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\thermal{C}_V,\thermal{C}_P$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacities | Energy change per temperature change at fixed volume or pressure. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Energy used in the constant-volume derivative. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Enthalpy | Energy potential used in the constant-pressure derivative. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Variable with respect to which heat capacities are defined. |
| $\mechanical{P}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Constraint for the constant-pressure heat capacity. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Constraint for the constant-volume heat capacity. |
| $\thermal{\alpha}_P$ | $\unit{K^{-1}}$ | Isobaric expansion coefficient | Fractional volume change per temperature change at fixed pressure. |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}_T$ | $\unit{Pa^{-1}}$ | Isothermal compressibility | Fractional volume response to pressure at fixed temperature. |
| $\mechanical{n}$ | $\unit{mol}$ | Amount of substance | Amount of ideal gas. |
| $\thermal{R}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Molar gas constant | Constant in the ideal-gas heat-capacity difference. |
