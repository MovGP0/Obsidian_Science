> [!TLDR]
> The entropy change is at least the heat received divided by the reservoir temperature. Equality holds for a reversible process.

For an irreversible cycle:

$$
\oint\frac{\delta\thermal{Q}}{\thermal{T}}<0.
$$

The corresponding differential inequality is:

$$
\mathrm d\thermal{S}\geq\frac{\delta\thermal{Q}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

Equality holds for a reversible process. A strictly positive difference represents entropy production.

## Proposition 1.1 — Clausius inequality

For any process from state $A$ to state $B$:

$$
\thermal{S}_B-\thermal{S}_A
\geq\int_A^B\frac{\delta\thermal{Q}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

Here, $\thermal{T}$ is the temperature of the reservoir with which heat is exchanged. The equality applies to reversible processes.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\thermal{S},\thermal{S}_A,\thermal{S}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | State function and its endpoint values. |
| $\thermal{Q}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Heat | Signed heat transferred into the system. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Reservoir temperature | Absolute temperature at which heat is exchanged. |
