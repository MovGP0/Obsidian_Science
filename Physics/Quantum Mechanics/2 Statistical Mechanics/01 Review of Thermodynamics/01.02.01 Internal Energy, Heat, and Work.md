> [!TLDR]
> Internal energy is a state function. Heat and work describe energy transfer, with positive heat entering the system and positive work performed by the system.

The first law is the law of energy conservation applied to thermodynamic systems. Before stating it, we must distinguish heat from work. This distinction is a fundamental contribution of thermodynamics.

## Definition 1.3 — Internal energy

The **internal energy** $\energy{U}$ is the sum of all forms of microscopic energy. These include molecular kinetic energy, intermolecular potential energy, and molecular vibrational and rotational energy.

Internal energy is a **state function**. Its value depends only on the state of the system, independently of the path used to reach that state.

## Definition 1.4 — Heat and work

**Heat** $\thermal{Q}$ is energy transferred between the system and its surroundings through a diathermal wall because of a temperature difference. **Work** $\energy{W}$ is any other form of energy transfer, including mechanical and electrical transfer.

The distinction describes how energy crosses the system boundary. Heat and work are not state functions. They describe transfer processes. Introductory textbooks often treat this point too briefly, which can cause confusion.

## Theorem 1.1 — First law, integral form

For any process that takes a system from state $A$ to state $B$:

$$
\Delta\energy{U}=\energy{U}_B-\energy{U}_A
=\thermal{Q}-\energy{W}. \tag{1.1}
$$

The sign convention is:

- $\thermal{Q}>0$ when the system absorbs heat.
- $\energy{W}>0$ when the system performs work on its surroundings.

> [!note] Alternative sign convention
> Another convention, common in engineering texts, uses $\Delta\energy{U}=\thermal{Q}+\energy{W}$, with positive work performed on the system by the surroundings. The difference is the sign assigned to work. This book uses equation (1.1).
