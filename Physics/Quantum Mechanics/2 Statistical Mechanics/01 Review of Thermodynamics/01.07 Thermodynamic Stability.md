> [!TLDR]
> Thermodynamic stability requires the correct potential to have a local minimum, or isolated-system entropy to have a local maximum. These conditions constrain heat capacities and compressibilities.

An equilibrium condition locates a stationary state; stability asks what happens after an allowed small fluctuation. Specify the conserved totals and the exchanges permitted between subsystems before choosing the curvature test. Otherwise a derivative can be taken in a direction the system cannot explore.

First use entropy or energy curvature to obtain local thermal and mechanical conditions. Then compare response functions under different constraints. A positive local curvature does not exclude a lower-energy phase elsewhere: local stability and global equilibrium answer different questions.
