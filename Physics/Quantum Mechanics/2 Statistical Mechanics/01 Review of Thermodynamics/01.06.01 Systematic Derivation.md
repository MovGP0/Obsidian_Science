> [!TLDR]
> The four Maxwell relations follow from equality of mixed second derivatives of the thermodynamic potentials. They connect entropy with pressure, volume, and temperature derivatives.

Maxwell relations are equalities between mixed second derivatives of thermodynamic potentials. They follow from a mathematical result: if $f(x,y)$ is of class $C^2$, its mixed second derivatives are equal:

$$
\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x\,\partial y}
=\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y\,\partial x}.
$$

Apply this result, also called Schwarz's theorem, to the four thermodynamic potentials. Particle number is held fixed throughout.

## From internal energy

For $\energy{U}(\thermal{S},\mechanical{V})$:

$$
\mathrm d\energy{U}=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V},
\qquad
\thermal{T}=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{U}}{\partial\thermal{S}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}},
\qquad
\mechanical{P}=-\left(\frac{\partial\energy{U}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{S}}.
$$

Equality of the mixed derivatives gives:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{T}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{S}}
=-\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\thermal{S}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}.
$$

## From enthalpy

For $\energy{H}(\thermal{S},\mechanical{P})$, use $\mathrm d\energy{H}=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}$:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{T}}{\partial\mechanical{P}}\right)_{\thermal{S}}
=\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{V}}{\partial\thermal{S}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}}.
$$

## From Helmholtz free energy

For $\energy{F}(\thermal{T},\mechanical{V})$, use $\mathrm d\energy{F}=-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}$:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{S}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}
=\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}. \tag{1.7}
$$

## From Gibbs energy

For $\energy{G}(\thermal{T},\mechanical{P})$, use $\mathrm d\energy{G}=-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}$:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{S}}{\partial\mechanical{P}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}
=-\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{V}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}}. \tag{1.8}
$$

Relations (1.7) and (1.8) are used most often in practice. They connect readily measured volume changes with temperature or pressure to entropy, which is not directly measured.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Potential with entropy and volume as natural variables. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Enthalpy | Potential with entropy and pressure as natural variables. |
| $\energy{F}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | Potential with temperature and volume as natural variables. |
| $\energy{G}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Gibbs energy | Potential with temperature and pressure as natural variables. |
| $\thermal{S}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | State variable related to measurable derivatives. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| $\mechanical{P}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical state variable. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | System volume. |
