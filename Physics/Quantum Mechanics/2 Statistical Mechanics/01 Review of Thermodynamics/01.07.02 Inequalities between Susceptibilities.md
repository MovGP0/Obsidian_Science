> [!TLDR]
> Stability constrains response functions: constant-pressure heat capacity is no smaller than constant-volume heat capacity, and isothermal compressibility is no smaller than adiabatic compressibility.

The stability conditions imply inequalities between response functions:

$$
\thermal{C}_P\geq\thermal{C}_V\geq0,
\qquad
\mechanical{\kappa}_T\geq\mechanical{\kappa}_S\geq0.
$$

The **adiabatic compressibility**, also called isentropic compressibility for a reversible response, is:

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}_S=-\frac{1}{\mechanical{V}}
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{V}}{\partial\mechanical{P}}\right)_{\thermal{S}}.
$$

The two compressibilities are related by:

$$
\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}_T}{\mechanical{\kappa}_S}
=\frac{\thermal{C}_P}{\thermal{C}_V}
=\thermal{\gamma}\geq1,
$$

where $\thermal{\gamma}=\thermal{C}_P/\thermal{C}_V$ is the heat-capacity ratio.

> [!note]
> The ratio formula assumes finite, nonzero denominators. Strict local stability uses positive response functions; nonnegative inequalities also describe limiting cases.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\thermal{C}_P,\thermal{C}_V$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacities | Responses at constant pressure and constant volume. |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}_T$ | $\unit{Pa^{-1}}$ | Isothermal compressibility | Fractional volume response at fixed temperature. |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}_S$ | $\unit{Pa^{-1}}$ | Adiabatic compressibility | Fractional volume response at fixed entropy. |
| $\thermal{\gamma}$ | $1$ | Heat-capacity ratio | Ratio of the two heat capacities and the two compressibilities. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume used to normalize the mechanical response. |
| $\mechanical{P}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Applied mechanical variable. |
| $\thermal{S}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Constraint in the adiabatic compressibility. |
