> [!TLDR]
> Thermodynamic potentials use different natural variables to match the constraints of a system. Legendre transformations connect them, and chemical potential accounts for particle exchange.

The useful potential follows from the surroundings: a thermostat fixes temperature, a rigid wall fixes volume, and a pressure reservoir fixes pressure. Begin with internal energy, then use a Legendre transformation to put the externally controlled variables into the differential. Add chemical potential when particles can cross the boundary.

At fixed temperature and volume, compare Helmholtz energies of allowed states. At fixed temperature and pressure, compare Gibbs energies instead. The minimum principle applies under its stated constraints; knowing a potential's natural variables alone does not prove that an experimental process holds them fixed.
