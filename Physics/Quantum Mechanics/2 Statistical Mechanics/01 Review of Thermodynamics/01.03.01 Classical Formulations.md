> [!TLDR]
> Energy conservation alone does not determine which processes can occur. The equivalent Kelvin–Planck and Clausius statements express the second law through heat engines and heat transfer.

The second law is central to thermodynamics. The first law introduces a state function, internal energy. The second law restricts possible processes: some processes that satisfy energy conservation do not occur in nature.

The classical formulations come from Kelvin and Clausius. They concern heat engines, which operate in cycles to convert heat into work or work into heat.

## Theorem 1.2 — Kelvin–Planck statement

It is impossible to construct a cyclic engine whose only effect is to absorb heat from a single reservoir at a uniform temperature and convert that heat into work.

## Theorem 1.3 — Clausius statement

It is impossible for a process to have heat transfer from a colder body to a warmer body as its only result.

The two statements are equivalent. A violation of either statement would imply a violation of the other. The source describes both as expressions of the tendency of isolated systems to increase their disorder spontaneously. The precise thermodynamic statement is developed through entropy in the following sections.
