> [!TLDR]
> Maxwell relations give the volume dependence of internal energy, the difference between heat capacities, and the temperature response during Joule–Thomson throttling.

## Internal-pressure equation of state

Combine the fundamental relation $\mathrm d\energy{U}=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}$ with Maxwell relation (1.7). At fixed temperature:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac{\partial\energy{U}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}
&=\thermal{T}\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{S}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}-\mechanical{P}\\
&=\thermal{T}\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}-\mechanical{P}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the **internal-pressure equation**, also called the **Joule equation**.

For an ideal gas, $\mechanical{P}=\mechanical{N}\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}/\mechanical{V}$. Therefore:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}
=\frac{\mechanical{P}}{\thermal{T}},
\qquad
\left(\frac{\partial\energy{U}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}=0.
$$

At constant temperature, ideal-gas internal energy is independent of volume. This is a defining result for the ideal gas. Chapter 3 gives its microscopic explanation.

## Heat-capacity difference — Proof

To derive equation (1.3), write entropy as $\thermal{S}(\thermal{T},\mechanical{V})$ and apply the chain rule:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{S}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}}
=\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{S}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}
+\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{S}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{V}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}}.
$$

Multiply by temperature. Use the heat-capacity identities:

$$
\thermal{C}_V=\thermal{T}\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{S}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}},
\qquad
\thermal{C}_P=\thermal{T}\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{S}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}}.
$$

Then apply (1.7) and $\thermal{\alpha}_P=(1/\mechanical{V})(\partial\mechanical{V}/\partial\thermal{T})_{\mechanical{P}}$:

$$
\thermal{C}_P-\thermal{C}_V
=\thermal{T}\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}
\mechanical{V}\thermal{\alpha}_P.
$$

For three variables linked by an equation of state, the cyclic derivative relation is:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}
\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{T}}{\partial\mechanical{V}}\right)_{\mechanical{P}}
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{V}}{\partial\mechanical{P}}\right)_{\thermal{T}}
=-1.
$$

With $\mechanical{\kappa}_T=-(1/\mechanical{V})(\partial\mechanical{V}/\partial\mechanical{P})_{\thermal{T}}$, this gives:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{P}}{\partial\thermal{T}}\right)_{\mechanical{V}}
=\frac{\thermal{\alpha}_P}{\mechanical{\kappa}_T}.
$$

Substitute into the heat-capacity difference:

$$
\thermal{C}_P-\thermal{C}_V
=\frac{\thermal{T}\mechanical{V}\thermal{\alpha}_P^{\,2}}{\mechanical{\kappa}_T}.
$$

For a mechanically stable system, $\mechanical{\kappa}_T>0$. At positive temperature and volume, the squared expansion coefficient is nonnegative. Thus $\thermal{C}_P\geq\thermal{C}_V$, with equality when $\thermal{\alpha}_P=0$, as for water near $4\,\unit{{}^{\circ}C}$ at atmospheric pressure. [[01.07 Thermodynamic Stability|Section 1.7]] discusses stability.

## Joule–Thomson coefficient

The Joule–Thomson process describes gas expansion through a restriction at constant enthalpy. It is an **isenthalpic process**. The **Joule–Thomson coefficient** is:

$$
\thermal{\mu}_{JT}=\left(\frac{\partial\thermal{T}}{\partial\mechanical{P}}\right)_{\energy{H}}.
$$

Use $\mathrm d\energy{H}=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}$ and the chain rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\thermal{\mu}_{JT}
&=-\frac{(\partial\energy{H}/\partial\mechanical{P})_{\thermal{T}}}{(\partial\energy{H}/\partial\thermal{T})_{\mechanical{P}}}\\
&=-\frac{\mechanical{V}-\thermal{T}(\partial\mechanical{V}/\partial\thermal{T})_{\mechanical{P}}}{\thermal{C}_P}\\
&=\frac{\thermal{T}(\partial\mechanical{V}/\partial\thermal{T})_{\mechanical{P}}-\mechanical{V}}{\thermal{C}_P}
=\frac{\mechanical{V}(\thermal{T}\thermal{\alpha}_P-1)}{\thermal{C}_P}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For an ideal gas, $\thermal{\alpha}_P=1/\thermal{T}$, so $\thermal{\mu}_{JT}=0$. The gas neither heats nor cools during Joule–Thomson expansion. This is consistent with the absence of intermolecular interactions in the ideal-gas model.

For a real gas, the coefficient can be positive or negative, depending on temperature and pressure. This behavior is important for gas liquefaction.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Its volume derivative defines internal pressure. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Enthalpy | Constant energy potential during throttling. |
| $\thermal{S}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | State function used in the Maxwell identities. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute thermal state variable. |
| $\mechanical{P}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical state variable and throttling variable. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | System volume. |
| $\mechanical{N}$ | $1$ | Particle number | Number of ideal-gas particles. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Constant in the ideal-gas pressure relation. |
| $\thermal{C}_P,\thermal{C}_V$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacities | Energy response at fixed pressure or volume. |
| $\thermal{\alpha}_P$ | $\unit{K^{-1}}$ | Isobaric expansion coefficient | Fractional volume change per temperature change at fixed pressure. |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}_T$ | $\unit{Pa^{-1}}$ | Isothermal compressibility | Fractional volume response to pressure at fixed temperature. |
| $\thermal{\mu}_{JT}$ | $\unit{K\,Pa^{-1}}$ | Joule–Thomson coefficient | Temperature change per pressure change at fixed enthalpy. |
| — | $\unit{{}^{\circ}C}$ | Water expansion-zero temperature | Approximately 4 degrees Celsius near atmospheric pressure. |
