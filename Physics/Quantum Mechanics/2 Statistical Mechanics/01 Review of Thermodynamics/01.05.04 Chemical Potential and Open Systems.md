> [!TLDR]
> Chemical potential adds particle exchange to the fundamental thermodynamic relation. Extensivity gives the Euler and Gibbs–Duhem relations and identifies Gibbs energy per particle with chemical potential.

When a system has multiple components or a variable particle number, its fundamental relation needs an additional term for matter exchange.

## Definition 1.10 — Chemical potential

The **chemical potential** of species $i$ is:

$$
\energy{\mu}_i
=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{U}}{\partial\mechanical{N}_i}\right)_{\thermal{S},\mechanical{V},\mechanical{N}_{j\ne i}}
=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{F}}{\partial\mechanical{N}_i}\right)_{\thermal{T},\mechanical{V},\mechanical{N}_{j\ne i}}
=\left(\frac{\partial\energy{G}}{\partial\mechanical{N}_i}\right)_{\thermal{T},\mechanical{P},\mechanical{N}_{j\ne i}}.
$$

All other species populations are held fixed in each derivative. For $r$ components, the fundamental relation is:

$$
\mathrm d\energy{U}=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}
-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}
+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\energy{\mu}_i\,\mathrm d\mechanical{N}_i. \tag{1.5}
$$

The corresponding potential differentials are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\energy{H}&=\thermal{T}\,\mathrm d\thermal{S}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}+\sum_i\energy{\mu}_i\,\mathrm d\mechanical{N}_i,\\
\mathrm d\energy{F}&=-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}-\mechanical{P}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}+\sum_i\energy{\mu}_i\,\mathrm d\mechanical{N}_i,\\
\mathrm d\energy{G}&=-\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}+\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}+\sum_i\energy{\mu}_i\,\mathrm d\mechanical{N}_i.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Euler relation

Internal energy, entropy, volume, and particle numbers are extensive. Euler's theorem for homogeneous functions of degree one therefore gives:

$$
\energy{U}=\thermal{T}\thermal{S}-\mechanical{P}\mechanical{V}
+\sum_i\energy{\mu}_i\mechanical{N}_i. \tag{1.6}
$$

Differentiate this expression and compare it with equation (1.5). The terms containing changes in the extensive variables cancel. The result is the **Gibbs–Duhem relation**:

$$
\thermal{S}\,\mathrm d\thermal{T}-\mechanical{V}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{P}
+\sum_i\mechanical{N}_i\,\mathrm d\energy{\mu}_i=0.
$$

This relation constrains the changes in intensive variables. For a single-phase system with $r$ components and no chemical reactions, only $r+1$ intensive variables are independent. This is the single-phase form of the Gibbs phase rule.

> [!note] One-component system
> Equation (1.6) gives $\energy{G}=\energy{\mu}\mechanical{N}$. Thus, chemical potential is Gibbs energy per particle: $\energy{\mu}=\energy{G}/\mechanical{N}=\energy{g}$, where $\energy{g}$ denotes Gibbs energy per particle.
