> [!TLDR]
> Widom's hypothesis expresses the singular free energy as a homogeneous function with two temperature branches.

Before the RG was developed, Widom (1965) proposed a scaling hypothesis. It was based on the observation that equations of state near criticality have a self-similar form.

**Definition 12.3 (Widom scaling hypothesis).** The singular, nonanalytic part of the free energy has the form

$$
\energy{f}_{\mathrm s}(\thermal{t},h)
=|\thermal{t}|^{2-\alpha}
\Phi_\pm\!\left(\frac{h}{|\thermal{t}|^\Delta}\right).
\tag{12.11}
$$

The universal functions $\Phi_+$ and $\Phi_-$ describe the regions $\thermal{t}>0$ and $\thermal{t}<0$. The **gap exponent** is $\Delta=\beta\delta$.

This agrees with the RG form (12.8) when

$$
2-\alpha=\frac{d}{y_t},
\qquad
\Delta=\frac{y_h}{y_t}.
$$

Here $\Delta$ is a scaling exponent, not the superconducting gap introduced in Section 12.1.1.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{f}_{\mathrm s}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Singular free energy per site | The singular part of the free energy per lattice site. |
| $\thermal{t}$ | $1$ | Reduced temperature | The temperature difference from the critical point, divided by the critical temperature. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The number of spatial dimensions. |
| $h$ | — | Conjugate field | The field coupled to the order parameter; its unit depends on the normalization. |
