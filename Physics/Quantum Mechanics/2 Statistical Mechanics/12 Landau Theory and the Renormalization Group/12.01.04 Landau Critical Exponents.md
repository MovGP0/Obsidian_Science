> [!TLDR]
> Landau theory gives the mean-field critical exponents from the equilibrium free energy and its derivatives.

The critical exponents follow directly from the Landau expansion.

## Magnetization exponent β

Equation (12.1) gives

$$
\phi_0\sim|\thermal{t}|^{1/2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\beta=\frac12.
$$

## Susceptibility exponent γ

With an external field $h$ conjugate to $\phi$, the free energy becomes

$$
\energy{F}(\phi,\thermal{T},h)
=a\thermal{t}\phi^2+b\phi^4-h\phi.
$$

The equilibrium condition is

$$
2a\thermal{t}\phi+4b\phi^3=h.
\tag{12.2}
$$

Define the zero-field susceptibility as $\chi=\left.\partial\phi/\partial h\right|_{h=0}$. Differentiating Equation (12.2) gives

$$
(2a\thermal{t}+12b\phi^2)\chi=1.
$$

Above the transition, $\thermal{t}>0$ and $\phi_0=0$:

$$
\chi=\frac{1}{2a\thermal{t}}\propto\thermal{t}^{-1}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\gamma=1.
$$

Below the transition, $\phi_0^2=a|\thermal{t}|/(2b)$:

$$
\chi=\frac{1}{2a\thermal{t}+12b\,a|\thermal{t}|/(2b)}
=\frac{1}{-2a|\thermal{t}|+6a|\thermal{t}|}
=\frac{1}{4a|\thermal{t}|}\propto|\thermal{t}|^{-1}.
$$

Thus $\gamma=1$ on both sides. The susceptibility amplitude ratio is $\chi^+/\chi^-=2$, where the superscripts denote the two critical branches at equal $|\thermal{t}|$.

## Critical-isotherm exponent δ

At $\thermal{t}=0$, Equation (12.2) becomes

$$
4b\phi^3=h
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\phi\sim h^{1/3}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\delta=3.
$$

## Heat-capacity exponent α

Below the transition, the free energy at the minimum is

$$
\energy{F}_{\min}
=\energy{F}_0+a\thermal{t}\phi_0^2+b\phi_0^4
=\energy{F}_0-\frac{a^2\thermal{t}^2}{4b}.
$$

The entropy is $\thermal{S}=-\partial\energy{F}/\partial\thermal{T}=-(1/\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})\partial\energy{F}/\partial\thermal{t}$.
The source writes the heat-capacity contribution as

$$
\thermal{C}=-\thermal{T}\frac{\partial^2\energy{F}}{\partial\thermal{T}^2}
\propto\frac{a^2}{2b}=\text{constant}.
$$

It has a finite jump, rather than a divergence, at the critical temperature. Hence $\alpha=0$.

> [!note] Temperature factor in the heat-capacity jump
> For the displayed free energy, the contribution below the transition is $\Delta\thermal{C}=\thermal{T}a^2/(2b\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^2)$. At the transition its limiting value is $a^2/(2b\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})$. The source's proportionality states the finite critical behaviour and suppresses this temperature factor.

> [!note] Remark 12.1
> The Landau exponents are
>
> $$
> \alpha=0,\qquad\beta=\frac12,\qquad\gamma=1,\qquad\delta=3.
> $$
>
> They agree with mean-field theory and satisfy the Widom scaling relations in [[12.06 Scaling and Relations between Critical Exponents]]. They do not generally agree with measured exponents for $d<4$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\chi$ | — | Susceptibility | The derivative of the order parameter with respect to its conjugate field. |
| $\energy{F}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free energy | The effective free energy; subscript 0 marks a reference value. |
| $\phi$ | — | Order parameter | The quantity that distinguishes the phases; its unit depends on the system. |
| $\thermal{C}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity | The thermal response obtained from free energy. |
| $\thermal{S}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | The negative temperature derivative of free energy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; subscript c marks the transition temperature. |
| $a$ | — | Quadratic coefficient scale | The coefficient multiplying reduced temperature and the squared order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $b$ | — | Quartic coefficient | The coefficient of the fourth power of the order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The number of spatial dimensions. |
| $h$ | — | Conjugate field | The field coupled to the order parameter; its unit depends on the normalization. |
