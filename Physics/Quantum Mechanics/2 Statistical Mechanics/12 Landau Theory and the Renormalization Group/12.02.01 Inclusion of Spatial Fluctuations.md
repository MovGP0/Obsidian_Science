> [!TLDR]
> A position-dependent order parameter and a gradient term extend homogeneous Landau theory.

The Landau theory used so far is homogeneous: it treats the order parameter as uniform throughout the system. This approximation is too strong near the critical point, where spatial fluctuations are important.

Landau and Ginzburg replace the scalar order parameter by a spatial field $\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})$. The free energy becomes a functional:

$$
\energy{\mathcal F}[\phi]=\int d^d\mechanical{r}\,
\left[
\frac{c}{2}|\nabla\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})|^2
+a\thermal{t}\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})^2
+b\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})^4
-h(\mechanical{\mathbf r})\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})
\right].
\tag{12.4}
$$

The coefficient $c>0$ penalizes spatial variation and accounts for domain-wall energy. The number $d$ is the spatial dimension. The term $|\nabla\phi|^2$ is the simplest gradient term allowed by spatial translation and rotation symmetry. For an isotropic system, it is the lowest-order derivative term.

> [!note] Coefficient normalization
> The integrand is a free-energy density. The coefficients therefore have density-based units here. Their units also depend on the chosen order parameter and on dimension; they need not match coefficients in a total or per-spin free energy.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal F}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free-energy functional | The energy assigned to a spatial order-parameter field. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The spatial coordinate. |
| $\phi$ | — | Order parameter | The quantity that distinguishes the phases; its unit depends on the system. |
| $\thermal{t}$ | $1$ | Reduced temperature | The temperature difference from the critical point, divided by the critical temperature. |
| $a$ | — | Quadratic coefficient scale | The coefficient multiplying reduced temperature and the squared order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $b$ | — | Quartic coefficient | The coefficient of the fourth power of the order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $c$ | — | Gradient coefficient | The stiffness against spatial order-parameter changes; its unit depends on normalization and dimension. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The number of spatial dimensions. |
| $h$ | — | Conjugate field | The field coupled to the order parameter; its unit depends on the normalization. |
