> [!TLDR]
> A cubic term or a negative quartic coefficient can produce a discontinuous order-parameter jump.

Landau theory also describes **first-order transitions**, where the order parameter has a finite jump. The source gives two main cases.

## Case 1: A cubic term

If the system does not have $\phi\to-\phi$ symmetry, cubic terms are allowed:

$$
\energy{F}(\phi)=a_2\phi^2+a_3\phi^3+a_4\phi^4,
\qquad a_4>0.
$$

The cubic term can produce a second local minimum at $\phi\ne0$ even when $a_2>0$. The transition occurs when the two minima have equal depth. This happens at a temperature $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}>\thermal{T}_0$, where $a_2=0$ at $\thermal{T}_0$. The order parameter jumps from zero to a finite value.

## Case 2: A negative quartic coefficient

If $a_4<0$, a positive sixth-order coefficient $a_6>0$ is needed for stability:

$$
\energy{F}(\phi)=a_2\phi^2-|a_4|\phi^4+a_6\phi^6.
\tag{12.3}
$$

Above a transition temperature $\thermal{T}^*$, the zero-order-parameter minimum is deepest. Below it, the nonzero minima become deepest, as shown in Fig. 12.2.

**Fig. 12.2** Landau free energy with a negative quartic coefficient and a positive sixth-order coefficient. As temperature decreases, nonzero minima appear. At coexistence (green), the ordered and disordered minima have equal depth. The order parameter then jumps to a nonzero value.

> [!note] Remark 12.2 (Tricritical point)
> A first-order transition changes into a continuous transition when $a_4=0$. The corresponding point in the phase diagram is a **tricritical point**. Its mean-field exponents differ from the usual Landau values; for example, $\beta_{\mathrm{tc}}=1/4$ and $\gamma_{\mathrm{tc}}=1$.

> [!note] Temperature notation in the source
> The text uses $\thermal{T}^*$ for coexistence, while the source figure uses $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$. The reproduced legend uses $\thermal{T}^*$ consistently.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{F}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free energy | The effective free energy; subscript 0 marks a reference value. |
| $\phi$ | — | Order parameter | The quantity that distinguishes the phases; its unit depends on the system. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; subscript c marks the transition temperature. |
| $a_n$ | — | Landau coefficient | The coefficient of the nth order-parameter power; its unit depends on that power and normalization. |
