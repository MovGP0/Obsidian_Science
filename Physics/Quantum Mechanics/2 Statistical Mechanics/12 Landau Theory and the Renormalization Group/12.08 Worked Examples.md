## Example 12.3: Landau minimization with a field

Consider

$$
\energy{F}(\phi)=a\thermal{t}\phi^2+b\phi^4-h\phi,
\qquad a,b>0.
$$

Find the spontaneous order parameter and verify $\delta=3$ on the critical isotherm. The equilibrium condition is

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{F}}{\partial\phi}
=2a\thermal{t}\phi+4b\phi^3-h=0.
\tag{*}
$$

**Case 1: critical isotherm, $\thermal{t}=0$.** Equation (*) reduces to $4b\phi^3=h$, so

$$
\phi=\left(\frac{h}{4b}\right)^{1/3}\propto h^{1/\delta},
\qquad\delta=3.
$$

**Case 2: $h\to0^+$ with $\thermal{t}<0$.** In the zero-field limit the solutions are $\phi=0$ and $\phi^2=-a\thermal{t}/(2b)=a|\thermal{t}|/(2b)$. Compare their free energies:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{F}(0)&=0,\\
\energy{F}(\phi_0)
&=a\thermal{t}\phi_0^2+b\phi_0^4\\
&=-\frac{a^2\thermal{t}^2}{2b}
+\frac{a^2\thermal{t}^2}{4b}
=-\frac{a^2\thermal{t}^2}{4b}<0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The limit of a positive field selects the global minimum $\phi_0=\sqrt{a|\thermal{t}|/(2b)}$.

**Case 3: susceptibility for $\thermal{t}>0$, $h\to0$.** Differentiate Equation (*):

$$
(2a\thermal{t}+12b\phi^2)\chi=1.
$$

For $\phi\to0$, $\chi=1/(2a\thermal{t})\propto\thermal{t}^{-1}$, so $\gamma=1$.

**Case 4: heat capacity.** Above the transition, the minimum is $\energy{F}_{\min}=\energy{F}_0$. Its regular temperature dependence gives the background heat capacity. Below the transition,

$$
\energy{F}_{\min}=\energy{F}_0-\frac{a^2\thermal{t}^2}{4b}.
$$

As $\thermal{T}\to\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}^-$,

$$
\thermal{C}=\thermal{C}_{\mathrm{reg}}
+\frac{a^2}{2b\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}}.
$$

The heat-capacity jump is $\Delta\thermal{C}=a^2/(2b\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})$, with $\alpha=0$.

> [!note] Regular free energy
> The source calls $\energy{F}_0$ constant. It is constant with respect to the order parameter; it can still depend regularly on temperature.

## Example 12.4: Iterated decimation of the Ising chain

Use Equation (12.7), $K'=\frac12\ln\cosh(2K)$, with $K_0=0.5$. The source calculation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_0&=0.5000,\\
K_1&=\tfrac12\ln\cosh(1.0000)
=\tfrac12\ln(1.5431)\approx0.2169,\\
K_2&=\tfrac12\ln\cosh(0.4338)
\approx\tfrac12\ln(1.0956)\approx0.0456,\\
K_3&=\tfrac12\ln\cosh(0.0912)
\approx\tfrac12\ln(1.00416)\approx0.00208,\\
K_4&\approx4.3\times10^{-6}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The sequence rapidly approaches zero. This confirms the stable high-temperature fixed point for finite ferromagnetic coupling.

The exact correlation length is $\xi(K)=-1/\ln\tanh K$. At the initial coupling,

$$
\xi(K_0)=\frac{-1}{\ln\tanh(0.5)}
\approx\frac{-1}{\ln(0.4621)}
\approx1.295
$$

lattice spacings. After one decimation, the spacing doubles, so the numerical correlation length is halved:

$$
\xi(K_1)=\frac{-1}{\ln\tanh(0.2169)}
\approx\frac{-1}{\ln(0.2136)}
\approx0.648.
$$

This agrees with $\xi(K_0)/2$.

## Example 12.5: Two-dimensional Ising exponents from RG

For the exact two-dimensional Ising critical point, take $d=2$, $y_t=1$, and $y_h=15/8$. Equations (12.9)–(12.10) give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nu&=\frac1{y_t}=1,\\
\beta&=\frac{d-y_h}{y_t}=\frac{2-15/8}{1}=\frac18,\\
\gamma&=\frac{2y_h-d}{y_t}=\frac{30/8-2}{1}=\frac74,\\
\delta&=\frac{y_h}{d-y_h}=\frac{15/8}{1/8}=15,\\
\alpha&=2-d\nu=2-2\cdot1=0,\\
\eta&=2+d-2y_h=4-\frac{30}{8}=\frac14.
\end{aligned}
$$

Check the relations:

- Rushbrooke: $\alpha+2\beta+\gamma=0+1/4+7/4=2$.
- Widom: $\gamma=\beta(\delta-1)=(1/8)\cdot14=7/4$.
- Fisher: $\gamma=\nu(2-\eta)=1\cdot(2-1/4)=7/4$.
- Josephson: $2-\alpha=d\nu$, or $2=2\cdot1$.

The exponents agree with the exact Onsager and Yang results.

## Example 12.6: Ginzburg estimate for iron

The source uses a critical temperature $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}\approx1043\,\unit{K}$ and a lattice spacing $\mechanical{a}_0\approx2.87\,\unit{Å}$, or $2.87\times10^{-10}\,\unit{m}$.

Its three-dimensional estimate is

$$
\thermal{t}\gg Gi\equiv
\left(\frac{\mechanical{a}_0}{\mechanical{\xi}_0}\right)^6,
$$

where $\mechanical{\xi}_0$ is the microscopic amplitude in

$$
\mechanical{\xi}(\thermal{t})
=\mechanical{\xi}_0\thermal{t}^{-1/2}.
$$

For metallic ferromagnets, the source assumes $\mechanical{\xi}_0\sim2$–$3\mechanical{a}_0$. Using the lower end of this range gives

$$
\frac{\Delta\thermal{T}_{\mathrm G}}{\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}}
\sim Gi\sim\left(\frac12\right)^6\sim10^{-2},
$$

and hence an estimated $\Delta\thermal{T}_{\mathrm G}\sim10\,\unit{K}$.

The example illustrates that non-mean-field critical exponents can become visible only within a restricted region around the critical temperature.

> [!note] Estimate precision and model dependence
> The displayed Ginzburg-number formula is the source's model estimate, not a general identity fixed by two length scales alone. With the stated ratio of two, the arithmetic gives $Gi=1/64\approx0.0156$ and a width of about $16\,\unit{K}$. A ratio of three gives about $1.4\,\unit{K}$. The source's $10\,\unit{K}$ is an order-of-magnitude estimate.
