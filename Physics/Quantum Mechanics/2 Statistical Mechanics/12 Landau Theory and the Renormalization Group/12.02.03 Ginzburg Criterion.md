> [!TLDR]
> The Ginzburg criterion compares fluctuations in a correlation volume with the squared equilibrium order parameter.

Landau mean-field theory is consistent when order-parameter fluctuations are small compared with the mean order parameter. The **Ginzburg criterion** quantifies this condition.

The fluctuation in a correlation volume of order $\mechanical{\xi}^{d}$ must be small compared with $\phi_0^2$:

$$
\frac{\langle(\delta\phi)^2\rangle_{\xi}}{\phi_0^2}\ll1.
$$

Estimate the long-wavelength fluctuations as

$$
\langle(\delta\phi)^2\rangle_{\xi}
\sim
\int_0^{1/\mechanical{\xi}}
\frac{d^d\signal{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}}
{c\signal{k}^2+2a\thermal{t}}
\sim
\frac{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}}
{c\mechanical{\xi}^{d-2}}.
$$

The source writes the criterion as

$$
\frac{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}}{c\mechanical{\xi}^{d-2}}
\ll\phi_0^2\sim\frac{a\thermal{t}}{2b}.
$$

Substitution of $\mechanical{\xi}\sim(c/(2a\thermal{t}))^{1/2}$ gives

$$
\frac{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}(2a\thermal{t})^{(d-2)/2}}{c^{d/2}}
\ll\frac{a\thermal{t}}{2b}.
$$

Suppressing fixed coefficient factors, its critical scaling is

$$
\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}\,
\thermal{t}^{(d-4)/2}\ll\frac{c^{d/2}}{b}.
$$

> [!note] Reduced-temperature sign and coefficient factors
> On the ordered side, the squared order parameter is $a|\thermal{t}|/(2b)$, not $a\thermal{t}/(2b)$. Read the distance-to-transition factors above as $|\thermal{t}|$ when approaching from below. The last inequality retains the temperature exponent but suppresses powers of $a$ and numerical constants; it is a scaling statement.

**Fig. 12.3** The Ginzburg region around the critical temperature. Within the red band of width $\Delta\thermal{T}_{\mathrm G}$, fluctuations are comparable to or larger than the mean order parameter. Mean-field theory is unreliable there; outside it, mean-field theory can be a useful approximation.

For $d>4$, the left-hand side tends to zero as $|\thermal{t}|\to0$. Mean-field theory is consistent near the critical point. For $d<4$, it diverges. Mean-field theory fails in a region around the critical temperature.

**Proposition 12.1 (Ginzburg criterion).** Near the critical point, mean-field theory is asymptotically self-consistent for dimensions above the upper critical dimension, $d>d_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{sup}}=4$. For $d<4$, fluctuations are relevant and mean-field theory gives incorrect critical exponents.

The Ginzburg-region width measures how far from the critical temperature one must remain for a mean-field approximation to be accurate. The source gives these estimates:

- BCS superconductors: $\Delta\thermal{T}_{\mathrm G}/\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}\sim10^{-12}$, so mean-field theory works very well.
- Superfluids and three-dimensional magnets: $\Delta\thermal{T}_{\mathrm G}/\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}\sim10^{-2}$–$10^{-1}$, so fluctuation corrections are needed.

> [!note] Marginal dimension
> At $d=4$, logarithmic corrections require separate treatment. The criterion above concerns the asymptotic critical region, not the accuracy of mean-field theory at all temperatures.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\Delta\thermal{T}_{\mathrm G}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Ginzburg-region width | The temperature range where critical fluctuations invalidate mean-field theory. |
| $\mechanical{\xi}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Correlation length | The spatial range over which fluctuations are correlated. |
| $\phi$ | — | Order parameter | The quantity that distinguishes the phases; its unit depends on the system. |
| $\signal{\mathbf k},\ \signal{k}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavevector | The spatial Fourier wavevector and its magnitude. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; subscript c marks the transition temperature. |
| $a$ | — | Quadratic coefficient scale | The coefficient multiplying reduced temperature and the squared order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $b$ | — | Quartic coefficient | The coefficient of the fourth power of the order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $c$ | — | Gradient coefficient | The stiffness against spatial order-parameter changes; its unit depends on normalization and dimension. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The number of spatial dimensions. |
