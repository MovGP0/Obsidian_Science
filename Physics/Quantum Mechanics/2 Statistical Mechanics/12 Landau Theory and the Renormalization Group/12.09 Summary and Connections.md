> [!TLDR]
> Landau theory describes symmetry and free-energy minima, while the RG explains critical fluctuations, scaling, and universality.

This chapter develops two complementary descriptions of continuous phase transitions.

## Landau theory

Landau theory constructs the free energy as a function of the order parameter, subject to the system symmetry. It gives:

- Mean-field critical exponents: $\beta=1/2$, $\gamma=1$, $\delta=3$, and $\alpha=0$.
- A distinction between continuous and first-order transitions.
- The Landau–Ginzburg functional, which includes spatial fluctuations and defines the correlation length.

## Ginzburg criterion

The Ginzburg criterion determines when fluctuations make mean-field theory unreliable. The upper critical dimension is $d_{\mathrm c}=4$:

- For $d>4$, mean-field critical powers are exact.
- For $d<4$, critical fluctuations are essential.
- At $d=4$, logarithmic corrections require separate treatment.

## Renormalization group

The RG provides:

- A systematic procedure to sum over degrees of freedom in a range of scales, through decimation or block-spin transformations.
- Critical exponents from the eigenvalues of the RG linearized at a critical fixed point.
- An explanation of universality: distinct microscopic models can approach the same critical fixed point and share critical exponents.

## ε expansion

The Wilson–Fisher program calculates exponents in $d=4-\varepsilon$ dimensions. It connects three-dimensional critical behaviour to the Gaussian mean-field fixed point at four dimensions.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat capacity | $\thermal{C}\sim\lvert\thermal{t}\rvert^{-\alpha}$ | $\alpha=0$ |
| Magnetization | $\magnetic{m}\sim\lvert\thermal{t}\rvert^\beta$ on the ordered side | $\beta=1/2$ |
| Susceptibility | $\chi\sim\lvert\thermal{t}\rvert^{-\gamma}$ | $\gamma=1$ |
| Critical isotherm | $\magnetic{m}\sim h^{1/\delta}$ | $\delta=3$ |
| Correlation length | $\mechanical{\xi}\sim\lvert\thermal{t}\rvert^{-\nu}$ | $\nu=1/2$ |
| Anomalous dimension | $G(\mechanical{r})\sim\mechanical{r}^{-(d-2+\eta)}$ at criticality | $\eta=0$ |

| Scaling relation | Formula | Mean-field check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rushbrooke | $\alpha+2\beta+\gamma=2$ | Satisfied |

| Fisher | $\gamma=\nu(2-\eta)$ | Satisfied |

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\chi$ | — | Susceptibility | The derivative of the order parameter with respect to its conjugate field. |
| $\magnetic{m}$ | $1$ | Magnetization | The mean normalized spin. |
| $\mechanical{\xi}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Correlation length | The spatial range over which fluctuations are correlated. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The spatial distance between two field points. |
| $\thermal{C}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity | The thermal response obtained from free energy. |
| $\thermal{t}$ | $1$ | Reduced temperature | The temperature difference from the critical point, divided by the critical temperature. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The number of spatial dimensions. |
| $G(r)$ | — | Connected correlation | The covariance of the order-parameter field; its unit is the square of the field unit. |
| $h$ | — | Conjugate field | The field coupled to the order parameter; its unit depends on the normalization. |
