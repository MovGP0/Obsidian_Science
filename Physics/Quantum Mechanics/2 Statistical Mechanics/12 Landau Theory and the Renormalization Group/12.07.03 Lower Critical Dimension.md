> [!TLDR]
> The lower critical dimension marks where thermal fluctuations prevent the usual finite-temperature long-range order.

There is also a **lower critical dimension**, $d_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{inf}}$. At or below the threshold for the models considered here, thermal fluctuations prevent the usual ordered phase at positive temperature.

The **Mermin–Wagner theorem** (1966) excludes spontaneous magnetization at positive temperature in two-dimensional, short-range systems with continuous spin symmetry, including the XY and Heisenberg models. Their lower critical dimension for this long-range order is

$$
d_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{inf}}=2.
$$

The theorem does not apply to the discrete $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry of Ising. For Ising,

$$
d_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{inf}}=1.
$$

The one-dimensional model has no transition at $\thermal{T}>0$, whereas the two-dimensional model does.

> [!note] Long-range order and topological transitions
> This statement concerns the usual symmetry-breaking order. It does not exclude the two-dimensional XY model's BKT transition, which has no spontaneous magnetization at positive temperature.

**Table 12.2** Lower and upper critical dimensions for the short-range models in this chapter.

| Symmetry | Model | $d_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{inf}}$ | $d_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{sup}}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathbb Z_2$ (discrete) | Ising | $1$ | $4$ |
| $O(2)$ (continuous) | XY | $2$ | $4$ |
| $O(3)$ (continuous) | Heisenberg | $2$ | $4$ |
| $O(n)$, $n\ge2$ | n-vector | $2$ | $4$ |

> [!note] Scope of the last row
> The source labels the last row “general $O(n)$”. Its lower critical dimension of two applies to continuous symmetry, so the row is restricted to $n\ge2$. The case $n=1$ has the discrete Ising symmetry.

Continuous symmetry allows arbitrarily slow spatial rotations of the order direction. Their small gradient cost makes long-wavelength thermal fluctuations especially important in low dimension. A discrete Ising order parameter has no such smooth rotation, so the continuous-symmetry argument cannot be transferred to it. The precise finite-range, isotropic Heisenberg exclusion at nonzero temperature is stated in [Mermin and Wagner's original result](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.17.1133).

The thresholds in the table assume equilibrium short-range models in the thermodynamic limit and concern ordinary long-range order. Anisotropy, different interaction range or finite sample size can change the applicable conclusion. In particular, a large correlated region in a finite sample is not proof of spontaneous magnetization in the infinite system. To test the distinction numerically, compare the order parameter as sample size increases at fixed positive temperature; do not infer the thermodynamic phase from one small sample.
