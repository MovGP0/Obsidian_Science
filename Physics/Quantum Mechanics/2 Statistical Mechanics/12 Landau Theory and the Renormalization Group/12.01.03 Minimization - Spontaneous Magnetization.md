> [!TLDR]
> Minimizing the Landau free energy gives a zero order parameter above the transition and two symmetry-related minima below it.

The equilibrium state minimizes the free energy with respect to the order parameter:

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{F}}{\partial\phi}
=2a\thermal{t}\phi+4b\phi^3=0.
$$

The stationary solutions are:

1. $\phi=0$, which always exists.
2. $\phi^2=-a\thermal{t}/(2b)$, which is real only for $\thermal{t}<0$.

The second derivative determines stability.

**Disordered phase:** for $\thermal{t}>0$, or $\thermal{T}>\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$, the solution $\phi=0$ is the only minimum.

**Ordered phase:** for $\thermal{t}<0$, or $\thermal{T}<\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$, the origin is a local maximum. There are two equal minima:

$$
\phi_\pm=\pm\sqrt{\frac{a|\thermal{t}|}{2b}}
\propto|\thermal{t}|^{1/2}.
\tag{12.1}
$$

The transition is continuous: $\phi\to0$ as $\thermal{t}\to0^-$.

**Fig. 12.1** Landau free energy $\energy{F}-\energy{F}_0=a_2\phi^2+b\phi^4$ with $b=1$ in arbitrary units. Above the critical temperature there is one minimum at zero. Below it, two equal minima signal spontaneous breaking of $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry. At the critical temperature the quartic potential has a flat minimum.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{F}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free energy | The effective free energy; subscript 0 marks a reference value. |
| $\phi$ | — | Order parameter | The quantity that distinguishes the phases; its unit depends on the system. |
| $\thermal{t}$ | $1$ | Reduced temperature | The temperature difference from the critical point, divided by the critical temperature. |
| $a$ | — | Quadratic coefficient scale | The coefficient multiplying reduced temperature and the squared order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $b$ | — | Quartic coefficient | The coefficient of the fourth power of the order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
