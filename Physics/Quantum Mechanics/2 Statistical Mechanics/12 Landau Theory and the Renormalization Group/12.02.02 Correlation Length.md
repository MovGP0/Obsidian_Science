> [!TLDR]
> The Gaussian fluctuation spectrum defines a correlation length that diverges with exponent one half.

For $\thermal{T}>\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$, consider fluctuations about the homogeneous solution $\phi_0=0$. Write $\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})=\delta\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})$ and retain the quadratic terms:

$$
\energy{\mathcal F}[\delta\phi]\approx
\int d^d\mechanical{r}\,
\left[\frac{c}{2}|\nabla\delta\phi|^2
+a\thermal{t}(\delta\phi)^2\right].
$$

Use the Fourier convention

$$
\delta\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})
=\int\frac{d^d\signal{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\widetilde\phi(\signal{\mathbf k})e^{i\signal{\mathbf k}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf r}}.
$$

Then

$$
\energy{\mathcal F}
=\int\frac{d^d\signal{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac12(c\signal{k}^2+2a\thermal{t})
|\widetilde\phi(\signal{\mathbf k})|^2.
$$

The fluctuation–dissipation relation gives the Fourier-space correlation spectrum:

$$
\left\langle|\widetilde\phi(\signal{\mathbf k})|^2\right\rangle
=\frac{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}}
{c\signal{k}^2+2a\thermal{t}}.
$$

The denominator defines the correlation length through

$$
c\signal{k}^2+2a\thermal{t}
\propto\signal{k}^2+\mechanical{\xi}^{-2}.
$$

> [!note] Continuum Fourier normalization
> For an infinite system, the two-point Fourier covariance contains a Dirac delta. The displayed expression denotes its spectral density, or the equivalent mode variance with the appropriate volume normalization.

Thus

$$
\mechanical{\xi}=\sqrt{\frac{c}{2a\thermal{t}}}
\propto\thermal{t}^{-1/2}.
$$

For $d=3$, transforming back to real space gives the connected **Ornstein–Zernike correlation function**:

$$
G(\mechanical{r})=
\langle\phi(\mechanical{\mathbf r})\phi(0)\rangle_{\mathrm c}
\propto\frac{e^{-\mechanical{r}/\mechanical{\xi}}}{\mechanical{r}}.
$$

At criticality, where $\mechanical{\xi}\to\infty$, the general power law is

$$
\left.G(\mechanical{r})\right|_{T=T_{\mathrm c}}
\propto\frac{1}{\mechanical{r}^{\,d-2+\eta}}.
$$

The exponent $\eta$ is the anomalous dimension; see [[12.06 Scaling and Relations between Critical Exponents]]. The Gaussian Landau–Ginzburg approximation gives $\eta=0$.

> [!note] Remark 12.3
> The Landau correlation-length exponent is $\nu=1/2$, since $\mechanical{\xi}\sim\thermal{t}^{-\nu}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal F}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Free-energy functional | The energy assigned to a spatial order-parameter field. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The spatial coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{\xi}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Correlation length | The spatial range over which fluctuations are correlated. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The spatial distance between two field points. |
| $\phi$ | — | Order parameter | The quantity that distinguishes the phases; its unit depends on the system. |
| $\signal{\mathbf k},\ \signal{k}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavevector | The spatial Fourier wavevector and its magnitude. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $\thermal{t}$ | $1$ | Reduced temperature | The temperature difference from the critical point, divided by the critical temperature. |
| $\widetilde\phi(\signal{\mathbf k})$ | — | Fourier order-parameter amplitude | The spatial Fourier transform of the field; its unit depends on the transform and field normalization. |
| $a$ | — | Quadratic coefficient scale | The coefficient multiplying reduced temperature and the squared order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $b$ | — | Quartic coefficient | The coefficient of the fourth power of the order parameter; its unit depends on field normalization. |
| $c$ | — | Gradient coefficient | The stiffness against spatial order-parameter changes; its unit depends on normalization and dimension. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The number of spatial dimensions. |
| $G(r)$ | — | Connected correlation | The covariance of the order-parameter field; its unit is the square of the field unit. |
