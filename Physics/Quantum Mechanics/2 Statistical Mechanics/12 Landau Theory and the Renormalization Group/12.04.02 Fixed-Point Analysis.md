> [!TLDR]
> The one-dimensional Ising RG flow has a stable high-temperature fixed point and an unstable zero-temperature endpoint.

The RG transformation is $K\mapsto K'=R(K)$. A fixed point satisfies $K^*=R(K^*)$:

$$
K^*=\frac12\ln\cosh(2K^*).
$$

## Example 12.1: Fixed points of the one-dimensional chain

At the trivial fixed point, $K^*=0$ and $\thermal{T}=\infty$:

$$
R(0)=\frac12\ln\cosh0=\frac12\ln1=0.
$$

The other fixed point is the endpoint $K^*\to\infty$, or $\thermal{T}=0$. For large $K$,

$$
\cosh(2K)\approx\frac{e^{2K}}2,
\qquad
R(K)\approx K-\frac{\ln2}{2}<K.
$$

Formally $R(K)\to\infty$ as $K\to\infty$, so infinity is a fixed endpoint. It is **unstable**: every finite initial coupling moves away from it under iteration.

Near $K^*=0$,

$$
\cosh(2K)\approx1+2K^2,
\qquad
R(K)\approx\frac12\ln(1+2K^2)\approx K^2\ll K.
$$

Thus $K^*=0$ is **stable**. Every finite ferromagnetic coupling flows toward it.

- The high-temperature fixed point $K^*=0$ is the only stable endpoint for finite $K$. There is no transition at positive temperature.
- The unstable zero-temperature endpoint $K^*=\infty$ corresponds to the one-dimensional Ising transition at $\thermal{T}=0$.
