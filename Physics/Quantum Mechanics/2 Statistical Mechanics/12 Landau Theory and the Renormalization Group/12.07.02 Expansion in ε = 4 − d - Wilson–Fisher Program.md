> [!TLDR]
> The ε expansion studies the interacting Wilson–Fisher fixed point just below four dimensions.

Wilson and Fisher (1972) treated $\varepsilon=4-d$ as a small parameter and constructed a perturbative RG near the upper critical dimension.

1. At $d=4$, the $\phi^4$ coupling is marginal by power counting.
2. At $d=4-\varepsilon$, with small $\varepsilon>0$, it is weakly relevant. A non-Gaussian **Wilson–Fisher fixed point** appears at a coupling of order $\varepsilon$.
3. Calculate the critical exponents as series in $\varepsilon$, then estimate values in $d=3$ by setting $\varepsilon=1$.

> [!note] Source correction at four dimensions
> The source calls the Gaussian fixed point “marginally unstable” at $d=4$. For the stable short-range theory with a positive quartic coupling, that coupling is marginally irrelevant in the infrared: it tends to zero logarithmically. The critical behaviour has mean-field powers with logarithmic corrections.

For the $O(n)$-symmetric $\phi^4$ theory, which includes Ising for $n=1$, the first-order expansion begins with

$$
\eta=0+O(\varepsilon^2).
$$

The other first-order results are

$$
\nu=\frac12+\frac{n+2}{4(n+8)}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2),
\tag{12.12}
$$

$$
\gamma=1+\frac{n+2}{2(n+8)}\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2).
\tag{12.13}
$$

For Ising, set $n=1$ and $\varepsilon=1$, corresponding to $d=3$:

$$
\nu\approx\frac12+\frac{3}{36}
=\frac12+\frac1{12}
\approx0.583.
$$

The source compares this with the numerical estimate $\nu\approx0.630$. Higher-order terms improve the agreement.

> [!note] Asymptotic series
> The ε expansion is asymptotic, rather than convergent. Resummation methods, such as Padé and Borel methods, can give precise exponent estimates even at $\varepsilon=1$.
