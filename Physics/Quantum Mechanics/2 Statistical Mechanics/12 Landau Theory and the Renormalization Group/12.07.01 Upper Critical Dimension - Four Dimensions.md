> [!TLDR]
> Power counting makes the quartic coupling irrelevant above four dimensions and relevant below them.

The Ginzburg criterion identifies four as the upper critical dimension of the short-range scalar quartic theory. The RG makes this statement precise.

The interaction term $\phi^4$ has a definite **scaling dimension**. The action, or the free-energy functional divided by $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}$, must be dimensionless. At the Gaussian fixed point, where $\eta=0$, the gradient term in Equation (12.4) gives the field scaling dimension

$$
[\phi]=\frac{d-2}{2}.
$$

Here square brackets denote a scaling dimension in lattice units, rather than an SI unit.

At a non-Gaussian fixed point, the anomalous dimension changes this to $[\phi]=(d-2+\eta)/2$.

At the Gaussian fixed point, the quartic interaction $u_4\phi^4$ has coupling dimension

$$
[u_4]=d-4[\phi]=d-2(d-2)=4-d\equiv\varepsilon.
$$

- For $d<4$, $\varepsilon>0$: the quartic interaction is relevant and grows under RG. The Gaussian fixed point is unstable, and its mean-field exponents are not the correct critical values.
- For $d>4$, $\varepsilon<0$: the quartic interaction is irrelevant and decreases under RG. The Gaussian fixed point is stable in that direction, and mean-field exponents apply.

Thus $d_{\mathrm c}^{\mathrm{sup}}=4$ is the **upper critical dimension**.
