> [!TLDR]
> The exercises test first-order Landau transitions, Ising decimation, scaling relations, the ε expansion, and the three-dimensional XY model.

## Exercise 12.1: First-order Landau transition

Consider

$$
\energy{F}(\phi)=a\thermal{t}\phi^2+b\phi^4+c\phi^6,
\qquad b<0,\quad c>0.
$$

1. Find the reduced temperature $\thermal{t}$, as a function of $b$ and $c$, at which the transition occurs.
2. Calculate the jump in the order parameter.
3. Show that the transition is first order.

> [!note] Coefficient convention
> Unless $a$ has been absorbed into the definition of reduced temperature, the transition value also depends on $a$. The source request names only $b$ and $c$.

## Exercise 12.2: Iterated Ising decimation

Apply three iterations of the one-dimensional Ising decimation starting at $K_0=1.0$.

1. Calculate $K_1$, $K_2$, and $K_3$.
2. Verify that $2^n\xi(K_n)$ remains approximately constant, where $\xi(K)=-1/\ln\tanh K$.
3. Discuss the limit $K\to\infty$.

## Exercise 12.3: Ornstein–Zernike correlation function

Calculate the Ornstein–Zernike correlation function.

Work in three dimensions. Transform

$$
\widetilde G(\signal{k})
=\frac{1}{c(\signal{k}^2+\mechanical{\xi}^{-2})}
$$

back to real space, and show that

$$
G(\mechanical{r})=
\frac{e^{-\mechanical{r}/\mechanical{\xi}}}{4\pi c\mechanical{r}}.
$$

**Hint:** use spherical coordinates and integrate over the polar angle first. This gives an integral of the form

$$
\frac{1}{2\pi^2c\mechanical{r}}
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\signal{k}\sin(\signal{k}\mechanical{r})}
{\signal{k}^2+\mechanical{\xi}^{-2}}\,d\signal{k},
$$

which can be evaluated with residues.

> [!note] Kernel normalization
> The exercise uses the stated kernel without the thermal prefactor present in Section 12.2.2. Keep this normalization when performing the transform.

## Exercise 12.4: Mean-field scaling relations

Show that the mean-field exponents

$$
\alpha=0,\quad\beta=\frac12,\quad\gamma=1,\quad
\delta=3,\quad\nu=\frac12,\quad\eta=0
$$

satisfy the Rushbrooke, Widom, and Fisher relations. Check the Josephson relation $2-\alpha=d\nu$ and determine the dimension for which it holds.

## Exercise 12.5: Wilson–Fisher fixed point

For an n-component $\phi^4$ theory in $\varepsilon=4-d$, the first-order RG equation for a suitably normalized reduced coupling $u$ is

$$
\frac{du}{d\ell}
=\varepsilon u-A_nu^2+O(u^3).
$$

Here $\ell=\ln b$ is the logarithmic scale parameter and $A_n=(n+8)K_d$. The stated standard convention has $K_4=1/(8\pi^2)$ (Wilson–Fisher, 1972). A different normalization of $u$ changes $A_n$, but not the following universal result.

1. Find the Wilson–Fisher fixed point $u^*(\varepsilon,n)$, which is proportional to $\varepsilon/A_n$.
2. Calculate the RG eigenvalue about $u^*$ and verify that $y_u=-\varepsilon$, independently of $A_n$.

3. Explain why $y_u<0$ for $d<4$: the quartic deviation is an irrelevant direction at the interacting critical fixed point.

## Exercise 12.6: Three-dimensional XY model

**Open problem.** Consider a three-dimensional system with $O(2)$ symmetry.

1. Set $n=2$ in Equations (12.12)–(12.13) and estimate $\nu$ and $\gamma$ to first order in $\varepsilon=1$.
2. Compare the results with the source's numerical values $\nu\approx0.67$ and $\gamma\approx1.32$ for the three-dimensional XY model.
3. Discuss the accuracy of the first-order ε expansion.
