> [!TLDR]
> Block-spin transformations combine nearby spins and rescale the lattice to study how interactions change.

Consider a $d$-dimensional Ising lattice with lattice spacing $\mechanical{a}$. Kadanoff's block-spin procedure uses a scale factor $b$.

1. **Divide into blocks:** group $b^d$ neighbouring spins into one block.
2. **Coarse grain:** replace each block by one effective spin $s_I^{(1)}$. A possible rule uses the sign of the mean spin in that block.
3. **Rescale:** the block spacing is $\mechanical{a}'=b\mechanical{a}$. Express lengths in the new lattice units so that the effective lattice has the same form.

**Fig. 12.4** Kadanoff block spins for $b=2$ in two dimensions. Each block of four microscopic spins on the left becomes one effective spin on the right. Each effective spin follows the majority in its block. The block spacing is twice the original spacing.

At criticality, $\mechanical{\xi}=\infty$ and the system is invariant under a change of scale: there is no finite characteristic length that distinguishes scales. Away from criticality, correlations decay on the scale $\mechanical{\xi}$. Repeated blocking changes the effective parameters until the flow reaches a fixed point.

> [!note] Site count and length rescaling
> The source caption says that the rescaled system has the same number of sites. Blocking actually reduces the site count by $b^d$. Rescaling restores the lattice spacing as a unit convention; it does not restore the removed spins. The field $s_I^{(1)}$ is a normalized, dimensionless block spin.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\xi}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Correlation length | The spatial range over which fluctuations are correlated. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lattice spacing | The microscopic spacing between neighbouring sites. |
| $b$ | $1$ | Scale factor | The factor by which the block length exceeds the microscopic spacing. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The number of spatial dimensions. |
| $s_I^{(1)}$ | $1$ | Block spin | The normalized effective spin that represents one block. |
