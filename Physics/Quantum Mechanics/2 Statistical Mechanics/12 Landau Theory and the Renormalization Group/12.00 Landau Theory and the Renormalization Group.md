> [!TLDR]
> Landau theory uses symmetry to describe phase transitions, while the renormalization group explains fluctuations, critical exponents, and universality.

The previous chapters introduced phase transitions and the Ising model. Mean-field theory predicts a transition and critical exponents, but it does not reproduce the measured exponents in low dimensions. It neglects fluctuations, which become dominant near the critical point.

This chapter develops two methods that go further. **Landau theory** is a phenomenological theory that extends the mean-field description through the symmetries of the system. The **renormalization group (RG)** explains critical exponents and universality.
