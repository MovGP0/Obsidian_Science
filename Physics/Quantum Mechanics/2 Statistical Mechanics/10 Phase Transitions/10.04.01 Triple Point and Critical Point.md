> [!TLDR]
> Three phases meet at a triple point; the liquid–gas coexistence curve ends at a critical point.

**Definition 10.2 — Triple point.** The triple point is the point in the temperature–pressure plane at which three phases coexist in equilibrium.

For water, the source gives:

$${\thermal{T}}_t=273.16\,\unit{K},\qquad {\mechanical{P}}_t=611.73\,\unit{Pa}.$$

The triple-point temperature of water was used to define the kelvin until the SI revision in 2019.

**Definition 10.3 — Critical point.** The liquid–gas critical point is the endpoint of the liquid–gas coexistence curve. At this point, the density difference between the two phases vanishes.

> [!NOTE] Correction to the source
> The source states that the fluid is beyond the critical point when temperature exceeds its critical value **or** pressure exceeds its critical value. The conventional supercritical region requires **both** ${\thermal{T}}>{\thermal{T}}_c$ and ${\mechanical{P}}>{\mechanical{P}}_c$. A pressure above the critical pressure at a lower temperature does not by itself make the fluid supercritical.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
