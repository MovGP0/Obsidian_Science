> [!TLDR]
> For a positive quartic coefficient, the equilibrium order parameter approaches zero continuously at the critical temperature.

Take ${\energy{b}}>0$ and neglect the sixth-order term. The stationary points obey

$$
\frac{\partial {\energy{F}}}{\partial{\mechanical{\phi}}}=2{\energy{a}}{\mechanical{\phi}}+4{\energy{b}}{\mechanical{\phi}}^3=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
{\mechanical{\phi}}({\energy{a}}+2{\energy{b}}{\mechanical{\phi}}^2)=0.
$$

There is a stationary point at ${\mechanical{\phi}}=0$. Two additional real stationary points exist when ${\energy{a}}<0$, with ${\mechanical{\phi}}^2=-{\energy{a}}/(2{\energy{b}})$.

Let

$${\energy{a}}({\thermal{T}})={\energy{a}}_0({\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c),\qquad {\energy{a}}_0>0.$$

The second derivative, $2{\energy{a}}+12{\energy{b}}{\mechanical{\phi}}^2$, identifies the minima. Above ${\thermal{T}}_c$, the origin is stable. Below ${\thermal{T}}_c$, the two nonzero points are stable and the origin is unstable:

$$
{\mechanical{\phi}}_{\mathrm{eq}}({\thermal{T}})=
\begin{cases}
0,&{\thermal{T}}>{\thermal{T}}_c,\\
\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{\frac{{\energy{a}}_0({\thermal{T}}_c-{\thermal{T}})}{2{\energy{b}}}},&{\thermal{T}}<{\thermal{T}}_c.
\end{cases}
\tag{10.3}
$$

The magnitude scales as $({\thermal{T}}_c-{\thermal{T}})^{1/2}$, so the mean-field exponent is $\beta=1/2$. The two signs describe two equivalent ordered states.

Substitution into the free energy gives

$$
{\energy{F}}_{\mathrm{eq}}-{\energy{F}}_0=
\begin{cases}0,&{\thermal{T}}>{\thermal{T}}_c,\\-{\energy{a}}_0^2({\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c)^2/(4{\energy{b}}),&{\thermal{T}}<{\thermal{T}}_c.\end{cases}
$$

Use ${\thermal{C}}=-{\thermal{T}}\,\partial^2{\energy{F}}_{\mathrm{eq}}/\partial {\thermal{T}}^2$ to obtain the heat-capacity jump:

$$\Delta {\thermal{C}}={\thermal{C}}^- - {\thermal{C}}^+=\frac{{\thermal{T}}_c {\energy{a}}_0^2}{2{\energy{b}}}.$$

The heat capacity has a finite jump, with $\alpha=0$. It does not diverge in this approximation.

For a small conjugate field, add $-{\energy{h}}{\mechanical{\phi}}$ to ${\energy{F}}$. Linear response about an equilibrium minimum gives

$$
{\magnetic{\chi}}=\left(\left.\frac{\partial^2{\energy{F}}}{\partial{\mechanical{\phi}}^2}\right|_{\phi_{\mathrm{eq}}}\right)^{-1}
\propto |{\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c|^{-1}.
$$

Thus $\gamma=1$. See [[10.08.04 Critical Mean-Field Exponents]].
