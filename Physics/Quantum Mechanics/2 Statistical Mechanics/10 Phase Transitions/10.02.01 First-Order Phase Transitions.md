> [!TLDR]
> A first-order transition has a jump in a first derivative of the free energy and can have latent heat.

First derivatives of the Gibbs free energy give entropy and volume:

$${\thermal{S}}=-\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{G}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_P,\qquad {\mechanical{V}}=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{G}}}{\partial {\mechanical{P}}}\right)_T.$$

At a first-order transition, one or more first derivatives change discontinuously. In particular:

$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta {\thermal{S}}&={\thermal{S}}_2-{\thermal{S}}_1\ne0\quad\Rightarrow\quad {\energy{L}}={\thermal{T}}\Delta {\thermal{S}}\ne0,\\
\Delta {\mechanical{V}}&={\mechanical{V}}_2-{\mechanical{V}}_1\ne0.
\end{aligned}$$

The displayed latent heat is total heat when entropy is total entropy. Molar or mass-specific quantities require the corresponding consistent normalization.

Typical features are:

- **Phase coexistence:** the phases coexist at the transition conditions.
- **Hysteresis:** a metastable phase can persist, such as a superheated liquid or a supercooled vapor.
- **Nucleation:** the stable phase forms through small regions, or nuclei, within the old phase.

## Example 10.1 — Liquid–gas transition

For water at one atmosphere and approximately $373\,\unit{K}$, the vapor specific volume is much larger than the liquid specific volume. The ideal-gas approximation gives:

$$\Delta {\mechanical{v}}={\mechanical{v}}_{\mathrm{gas}}-{\mechanical{v}}_{\mathrm{liq}}\approx\frac{{\thermal{R}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{P}} {\mechanical{M}}_{\mathrm{H_2O}}}\approx1.70\,\unit{L\,g^{-1}}.$$

Here the mass units must be converted consistently. The specific latent heat is:

$${\energy{L}}={\thermal{T}}\Delta s\approx2257\,\unit{J\,g^{-1}}.$$

> [!NOTE] Normalization in the source
> This example uses volume, entropy, and latent heat per unit mass. These differ from the molar quantities used in the Clausius–Clapeyron derivation. The relation has the same form when all quantities use the same normalization.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Total thermodynamic entropy. |
| ${\energy{G}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Gibbs free energy | Thermodynamic potential at fixed temperature, pressure, and amount. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the sample; convert to cubic centimeters in Gaussian magnetic formulas. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Thermodynamic pressure. |
| ${\mechanical{v}}$ | $\unit{L\,g^{-1}}$ | Specific volume | Volume per gram in the water example. |
| ${\thermal{R}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Molar gas constant | Boltzmann constant times the Avogadro constant. |
| ${\mechanical{M}}$ | $\unit{kg\,mol^{-1}}$ | Molar mass | Molar mass of water. |
| ${\energy{L}}$ | $\unit{J\,g^{-1}}$ | Specific latent heat | Heat absorbed per gram in the stated phase change. |
