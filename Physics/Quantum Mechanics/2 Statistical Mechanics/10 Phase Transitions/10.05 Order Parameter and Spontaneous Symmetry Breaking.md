> [!TLDR]
> An order parameter distinguishes the ordered phase from the disordered phase.

The order parameter and the symmetry of the equilibrium state provide a common description of different phase transitions.

The task is to find a quantity that detects organization which a simple energy or temperature value does not reveal. Start by identifying the symmetry of the Hamiltonian and asking how a proposed order parameter changes under it. Then distinguish a selected ordered phase from an average over all symmetry-related phases. This distinction prevents a zero average from being misread as absence of local order.

Read the definition and examples together: a magnetization, a density difference, and a complex pairing amplitude do not have interchangeable units or transformation rules. Use the symmetry-breaking note to fix the order of the zero-field and infinite-system limits. Finally, use the temperature-dependence note to compare a continuous loss of order with a finite equilibrium jump. The framework applies to the conventional ordering examples here; it is not a proof that every transition has a local order parameter.
