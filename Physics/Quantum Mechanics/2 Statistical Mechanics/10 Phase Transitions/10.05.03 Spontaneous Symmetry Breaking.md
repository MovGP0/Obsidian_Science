> [!TLDR]
> A symmetry is spontaneously broken when the equilibrium state has less symmetry than the Hamiltonian.

## Definition 10.5 — Spontaneous symmetry breaking

A symmetry is **spontaneously broken** if the ground state, or an equilibrium phase at finite temperature, is not invariant under that symmetry although the Hamiltonian is invariant.

Below the critical temperature, the Landau free energy can have two or more degenerate minima. The system enters one minimum. An arbitrarily small field, boundary condition, or initial fluctuation can select the phase. The symmetry-breaking field is then taken to zero after the thermodynamic limit.

## Remark 10.2 — Goldstone modes

When a continuous global symmetry is spontaneously broken, the Goldstone theorem predicts low-energy collective excitations under its usual assumptions. Their frequency approaches zero at long wavelength:

$${\signal{\omega}}\longrightarrow0\qquad\text{as }{\signal{k}}\longrightarrow0.$$

Examples include magnons in an isotropic ferromagnet and acoustic phonons associated with broken continuous translations in a crystal. The number and dispersion of the modes depend on the system. Gauge fields can change this conclusion, as in a superconductor.
