> [!TLDR]
> Magnetization, density difference, and the superconducting gap are order parameters.

## Ising ferromagnet

The Ising Hamiltonian is:

$$\mathcal H=-{\energy{J}}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}s_i s_j-{\energy{H}}\sum_i s_i,\qquad s_i=\pm1.$$

The first sum is over nearest-neighbor pairs. Here ${\energy{H}}$ is an **energy coefficient**, not magnetic field strength in SI units. At ${\energy{H}}=0$, the Hamiltonian is invariant under $s_i\mapsto-s_i$. This is a $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry.

The order parameter is the mean magnetization per spin:

$${\magnetic{m}}=\langle s_i\rangle=\frac1{\mechanical{N}}\sum_i\langle s_i\rangle.$$

Below the critical temperature, a symmetry-broken equilibrium phase has ${\magnetic{m}}\ne0$ as the field approaches zero. For a finite system with exactly zero field, the symmetric ensemble can still have zero mean. Spontaneous symmetry breaking requires the appropriate thermodynamic limit.

## Liquid–gas transition

The difference between the liquid and gas densities is an order parameter. In terms of **molar** density and molar volume:

$$\phi={\mechanical{\rho}}_L-{\mechanical{\rho}}_G=\frac1{{\mechanical{v}}_L}-\frac1{{\mechanical{v}}_G}.$$

It approaches zero as ${\thermal{T}}\to {\thermal{T}}_c^-$ and is zero when the distinction between the two phases disappears.

> [!NOTE] Density units
> The equality with inverse molar volume uses molar density. For mass density, multiply each inverse molar volume by the molar mass. A normalized density difference can be used as the dimensionless Landau order parameter.

## BCS superconductor

The source gives the schematic pairing amplitude:

$${\energy{\Delta}}=|{\energy{V}}_{kk'}|\langle c_{k\uparrow}c_{-k\downarrow}\rangle.$$

The operators annihilate two electrons with opposite momenta and spins. The pairing amplitude is complex and has energy units. A complete BCS gap equation includes a sum over the paired states and the interaction matrix elements.

The phase describes superconducting coherence. Statements about a broken U(1) symmetry refer to the effective order-parameter description; a local gauge transformation is a redundancy of the description, not a distinct physical state.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{J}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange energy | Nearest-neighbor coupling in the Ising Hamiltonian. |
| ${\energy{H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Ising conjugate field | Energy coefficient multiplying a dimensionless spin; not an SI field strength. |
| ${\magnetic{m}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Reduced magnetization | Mean dimensionless Ising spin per site. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Number of molecules. |
| ${\mechanical{\rho}}$ | $\unit{mol\,m^{-3}}$ | Molar density | Amount of substance per volume; equal to the inverse molar volume. |
| ${\mechanical{v}}$ | $\unit{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$ | Molar volume | Volume per mole in the stated phase. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature of the stated phase transition. |
| ${\energy{\Delta}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Superconducting gap | Complex pairing amplitude with energy units. |
| ${\energy{V}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Pairing matrix element | Interaction matrix element in the simplified BCS expression. |
