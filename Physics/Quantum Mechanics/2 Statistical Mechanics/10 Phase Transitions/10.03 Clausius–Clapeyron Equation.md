> [!TLDR]
> The Clausius–Clapeyron relation connects the slope of a coexistence curve with latent heat and volume change.

Chemical equilibrium between phases gives a measurable relation between pressure and temperature along their coexistence curve.

The key restriction is that the pressure and temperature move together along equilibrium coexistence. This is not the pressure response of a single phase held at fixed volume. For a pure substance, equality of chemical potentials supplies the constraint. Differentiating it gives a slope determined by the entropy and volume changes of the conversion.

Start with the derivation to fix the direction of both phase differences. Use the vaporization approximation only when the gas volume dominates; use the melting calculation when two similar volumes must be subtracted. The magnetic application replaces mechanical pressure–volume work with a field and its conjugate magnetization, so its sign and magnetic units must be checked separately. A coexistence-slope measurement and independent latent-heat and volume measurements could test the identity; this note supplies the theory, not such a comparison.
