> [!TLDR]
> The connected correlation function measures fluctuations at two separated points.

A nonzero average order parameter does not tell us how fluctuations at two points are related. The connected correlation subtracts that average contribution, so it detects a collective fluctuation rather than merely a uniformly ordered background. Assume equilibrium, translation invariance, and separations large compared with microscopic distances when using the scaling forms.

## Definition 10.6 — Two-point correlation function

For a homogeneous system, the connected correlation function of the order parameter is:

$$
G(\mathbf {\mechanical{r}})=\langle{\mechanical{\phi}}(\mathbf {\mechanical{r}}){\mechanical{\phi}}(0)\rangle-\langle{\mechanical{\phi}}\rangle^2
=\langle\delta{\mechanical{\phi}}(\mathbf {\mechanical{r}})\delta{\mechanical{\phi}}(0)\rangle.
$$

The **correlation length** ${\mechanical{\xi}}$ is the characteristic distance over which the fluctuations remain correlated.

Near the critical point, the scaling form is:

$$
G({\mechanical{r}})\sim\frac1{{\mechanical{r}}^{d-2+\eta}}\,\mathcal G({\mechanical{r}}/{\mechanical{\xi}}).
$$

The scaling function decreases rapidly for large argument. Away from criticality, correlations therefore have an exponential cutoff on the scale ${\mechanical{\xi}}$. The source represents this schematically by $e^{-{\mechanical{r}}/{\mechanical{\xi}}}/{\mechanical{r}}^{d-2+\eta}$; its exact large-distance prefactor is model dependent.

At the critical point, ${\mechanical{\xi}}\to\infty$ and the decay is algebraic:

$$
G({\mechanical{r}})\sim\frac1{{\mechanical{r}}^{d-2+\eta}}.
$$

There is no finite characteristic correlation length. This is scale invariance. The approach to criticality is described by:

$$
{\mechanical{\xi}}\sim|t|^{-\nu},\qquad t=\frac{{\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\to0.
$$

The dimension $d$ and the exponents $\eta$ and $\nu$ are dimensionless. The normalization of $G$ depends on the chosen order parameter; it is not Gibbs free energy in this article.

At zero separation the connected correlation becomes the local variance and is nonnegative. At large separation in a phase with finite correlation length, the connected part tends to zero even if the mean order parameter remains nonzero. These two checks distinguish connected correlations from the un-subtracted product average.

The asymptotic proportionalities suppress dimensional amplitudes and a microscopic length scale. For a dimensionless order parameter its correlation is dimensionless, so the displayed bare inverse power of distance must carry a matching length-dependent coefficient. At criticality there is no finite bulk correlation length, but a finite sample still cuts off the accessible separations. The scaling function is a theoretical form to compare with spatial correlation or scattering data; it is not a measured correlation profile here.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $G$ | $\unit{1}$ | Connected correlation | Covariance of the dimensionless order parameter at two points. |
| ${\mechanical{\phi}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Order parameter | Dimensionless normalized order parameter used in the generic Landau model. |
| ${\mechanical{r}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | Distance between the two points of a correlation function. |
| ${\mechanical{\xi}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Correlation length | Length over which fluctuations remain correlated. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature of the stated phase transition. |
