> [!TLDR]
> The van der Waals fluid and the mean-field ferromagnet have the same local critical structure.

The density difference between the two fluid phases plays the role of the magnetization. The equation of state near the critical point gives the same leading exponents as the quartic Landau model.


The analogy compares the local form of two equilibrium models near criticality. In a fluid, compression and expansion change density; in a magnet, a conjugate field changes the order parameter. A cubic relation near the critical point produces the same mean-field exponents after the variables and reference pressure are chosen appropriately. This does not require molecules to have Ising spins.

Read the molar equation first to fix attraction and excluded-volume units. The corresponding-states note then removes material parameters within that model. Use the near-critical expansion to identify leading powers, and the Maxwell construction to replace unstable uniform states by a mixture with equal chemical potentials. The last step is essential: extracting a physical coexistence jump from an unstable loop without imposing equilibrium would not justify the analogy. Three-dimensional fluctuation exponents require a treatment beyond these mean-field equations.
