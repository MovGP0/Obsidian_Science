> [!TLDR]
> An order parameter approaches zero continuously at a continuous transition. It has a finite jump at a first-order transition.

The temperature dependence distinguishes two types of transition. At a continuous transition, the magnitude of the order parameter tends continuously to zero:

$$
|{\mechanical{\phi}}|\propto ({\thermal{T}}_c-{\thermal{T}})^\beta,\qquad {\thermal{T}}\to {\thermal{T}}_c^-.
$$

At a first-order transition, its equilibrium value has a finite jump at the transition temperature ${\thermal{T}}_0$.

The red curve shows a finite jump at a first-order transition. The vertical dashed line marks the jump; it is not a branch of equilibrium states. The two transition temperatures are distinct illustrative values. The vertical scale is arbitrary.

Only the magnitude is shown. For a scalar order parameter with the symmetry ${\mechanical{\phi}}\mapsto-{\mechanical{\phi}}$, either sign can occur in the ordered phase. See [[10.05.03 Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature of the stated phase transition. |
| ${\mechanical{\phi}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Order parameter | Dimensionless normalized order parameter used in the generic Landau model. |
