> [!TLDR]
> Fluids, magnets, superconductors, and superfluids show common features at phase transitions.

The examples below show how collective behavior changes a macroscopic state.

A useful starting question is what changes during a slow temperature sweep. At a liquid–gas transition, heat can change the phase fractions while the temperature remains fixed. At a continuous magnetic transition in zero applied field, the spontaneous magnetization instead tends continuously to zero. These observations call for different tests: latent heat and a density jump in the first case, an order parameter and response functions in the second.

The comparison assumes that each state has time to reach equilibrium. A delayed transition during rapid heating or cooling can reflect nucleation or slow relaxation; it does not by itself determine the equilibrium transition order. Read the examples to identify the measured quantity, then use the general properties to decide which features are shared and which require a continuous transition. The numerical values in the examples are source benchmarks, not measurements made in this chapter.
