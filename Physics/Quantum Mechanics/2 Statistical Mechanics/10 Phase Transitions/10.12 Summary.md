> [!TLDR]
> Phase transitions connect thermodynamic singularities with collective order, correlations, and scale invariance.

This chapter develops the following concepts:

- **Phenomenology.** Fluids, magnets, superconductors, and superfluids can show related forms of critical behavior.
- **Ehrenfest classification.** The classification examines derivatives of thermodynamic potentials. First-order transitions have discontinuous first derivatives and can have latent heat and phase coexistence. A continuous transition has no latent heat at the transition.
- **Clausius–Clapeyron equation.** The slope of a coexistence curve is

$$\frac{d{\mechanical{P}}}{d{\thermal{T}}}=\frac{{\energy{L}}}{{\thermal{T}}\,\Delta {\mechanical{v}}}.$$

- **Order parameter.** This quantity describes order in the phase. At a continuous transition it tends to zero as $({\thermal{T}}_c-{\thermal{T}})^\beta$ on the ordered side.
- **Gibbs phase rule.** The number of independent intensive variables is $\mathcal F=C-\varphi+2$, for the assumptions stated in [[10.04.02 Gibbs Phase Rule]].
- **Critical fluctuations.** The correlation length diverges at a continuous critical point. Response functions have singular behavior. A singularity can be a divergence, a jump, or a finite cusp; the heat capacity does not always diverge.
- **Critical exponents.** The exponents $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\nu,\eta$ describe the leading behavior. They obey the Rushbrooke, Widom, Fisher, and Josephson relations under the stated scaling assumptions.
- **Universality.** Critical behavior depends mainly on spatial dimension, order-parameter symmetry, and interaction range.
- **Landau theory.** Expansion in the order parameter gives a useful mean-field description. It neglects critical fluctuations. The mean-field values include $\beta=1/2$, $\gamma=1$, and $\delta=3$. Above the upper critical dimension they give the leading exponents. At the upper critical dimension, logarithmic corrections can occur.
- **Renormalization group.** Scale transformations explain universality and provide methods to calculate critical exponents.

The next chapter applies these ideas to the Ising model.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Thermodynamic pressure. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\energy{L}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}}$ | Molar latent heat | Heat absorbed per mole in the stated phase change. |
