> [!TLDR]
> A phase diagram maps stable phases and the conditions where they coexist.

The triple point, critical point, and coexistence curves organize the pressure–temperature and pressure–volume diagrams.

To use a phase diagram, first specify the controls and allow the sample to reach equilibrium. A point in the pressure–temperature plane selects a stable phase or a coexistence condition. A point inside a two-phase region in a pressure–volume diagram can instead specify a phase mixture: changing total volume can change the phase fractions while pressure and temperature remain fixed.

Read the special-point definitions before the phase rule, which counts the independent intensive controls. Then compare the two diagrams along the same subcritical isotherm. The horizontal coexistence segment in the pressure–volume plot corresponds to one coexistence point in the pressure–temperature plot, not to a two-dimensional region there. This distinction is a useful reconstruction check. The figures are equilibrium representations; a rapid sweep can follow a metastable branch and does not redraw the equilibrium phase boundary.
