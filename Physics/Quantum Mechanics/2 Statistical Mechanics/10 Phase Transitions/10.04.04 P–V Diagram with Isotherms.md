> [!TLDR]
> Below the critical temperature, the equilibrium isotherm has a constant-pressure interval for liquid–gas coexistence.

The pressure–volume diagram shows how an isotherm changes with temperature. The reduced van der Waals equation gives a useful model:

$$p=\frac{8t}{3v-1}-\frac{3}{v^2},\qquad p=\frac{{\mechanical{P}}}{{\mechanical{P}}_c},\quad v=\frac{{\mechanical{V}}}{{\mechanical{V}}_c},\quad t=\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}.$$

**Fig. 10.2** Pressure–volume isotherms calculated from the reduced van der Waals model at ${\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_c=1.15$, $1$, and $0.85$. The dashed coexistence boundary ends at the critical point. For ${\thermal{T}}<{\thermal{T}}_c$, the Maxwell plateau replaces the unstable loop. The plateau endpoints have the same pressure and chemical potential. This figure uses the model curves rather than the approximate shapes in the source sketch.

The subcritical plateau in this plot has ${\mechanical{P}}/{\mechanical{P}}_c\approx0.5045$, with liquid and gas volumes ${\mechanical{V}}_L/{\mechanical{V}}_c\approx0.5534$ and ${\mechanical{V}}_G/{\mechanical{V}}_c\approx3.128$. Varying the total volume within this interval changes the phase fractions at fixed temperature and pressure.

See [[10.09.04 Maxwell Construction]] for the equal-area condition.
