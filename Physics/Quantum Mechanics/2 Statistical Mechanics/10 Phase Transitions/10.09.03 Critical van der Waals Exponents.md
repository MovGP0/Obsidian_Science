> [!TLDR]
> Expansion of the reduced van der Waals equation gives the mean-field critical exponents.

The critical isotherm has vanishing first and second volume derivatives, so the leading pure volume deviation is cubic. This is the physical reason to expand through cubic order rather than stop at a linear ideal-gas response. Use dimensionless deviations small compared with one and retain the mixed temperature–volume term because it competes with the cubic term below criticality.

Expand near the critical point with $\widetilde {\mechanical{v}}=1+\epsilon$ and $\widetilde {\thermal{T}}=1+t$, where $|\epsilon|,|t|\ll1$. Equation (10.4) gives, to the required leading orders,

$$
\widetilde {\mechanical{P}}=1+4t-6t\epsilon-\frac32\epsilon^3+O(t\epsilon^2,\epsilon^4).
$$

Here $\epsilon$ and $t$ are dimensionless expansion variables.

- Along the critical isotherm, $t=0$, the pressure deviation is cubic in the volume deviation. Thus $\delta=3$.
- Above the critical point, the linear slope is proportional to $t$. Its inverse, the compressibility, scales as $t^{-1}$. Thus $\gamma=1$.
- Below the critical point, coexistence gives leading symmetric volume deviations proportional to $(-t)^{1/2}$. The density difference has the same leading power, so $\beta=1/2$.

These are the Landau mean-field values. The van der Waals model treats interactions through an average field and does not resolve the critical spatial fluctuations that change the exponents in three dimensions.

For coexistence below the transition, the leading odd part of the expansion has symmetric endpoints. Equal pressure at opposite nonzero deviations gives their squared magnitude equal to minus four times reduced temperature. Hence the magnitude is twice the square root of negative reduced temperature. This is a leading-order result; omitted mixed and fourth-order terms shift the asymmetry and amplitudes.

The ordering is self-consistent: if the volume deviation scales as the square root of reduced-temperature magnitude, both retained competing terms have order three halves in that magnitude, while the displayed omitted terms start at order two. Above criticality, the volume derivative at zero deviation is negative and proportional to temperature deviation, giving positive compressibility with inverse-linear growth. These algebraic checks establish model exponents, not measured fluid exponents.
