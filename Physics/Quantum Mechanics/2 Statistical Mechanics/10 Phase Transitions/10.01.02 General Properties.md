> [!TLDR]
> Singular response functions, long correlations, symmetry breaking, and universality connect many phase transitions.

Despite their different microscopic mechanisms, many phase transitions share these properties:

1. **Singular thermodynamic behavior.** Some response functions, such as heat capacity, magnetic susceptibility, and compressibility, have a jump, a cusp, or a divergence at the transition.
2. **A diverging correlation length.** Near a continuous transition, fluctuations remain correlated over larger distances:

$${\mechanical{\xi}}\longrightarrow\infty\qquad({\thermal{T}}\longrightarrow {\thermal{T}}_c).$$

   At the critical point, there is no finite characteristic correlation length. The system becomes scale invariant.
3. **Spontaneous symmetry breaking.** Below a symmetry-breaking transition, the equilibrium state has less symmetry than the Hamiltonian.
4. **Universality.** Critical exponents and qualitative behavior near the critical point depend on only a few properties: spatial dimension, order-parameter symmetry, and interaction range. Many microscopic details do not affect these results.

> [!NOTE] Range of these statements
> These are common structures, not requirements for every transition. A first-order transition need not have a diverging correlation length. A continuous transition need not have a divergent heat capacity; helium-4 has a finite cusp. Some transitions do not have a local symmetry-breaking order parameter.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{\xi}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Correlation length | Length over which fluctuations remain correlated. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature of the stated phase transition. |
