> [!TLDR]
> The magnetic coexistence slope gives the latent heat of a type-I superconducting transition.

For a type-I superconductor, the superconducting phase and the normal phase coexist along the critical-field curve. The magnetic counterpart of the Clausius–Clapeyron equation is:

$$\frac{\mathrm d{\magnetic{H}}_c}{\mathrm d{\thermal{T}}}=-\frac{\Delta {\thermal{S}}}{{\mechanical{V}}\Delta {\magnetic{M}}}.\tag{10.1}$$

Here $\Delta {\magnetic{M}}={\magnetic{M}}_{\mathrm{SC}}-{\magnetic{M}}_{\mathrm N}$ and $\Delta {\thermal{S}}={\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{SC}}-{\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm N}$. Entropy is total entropy and magnetization is per volume.

The source uses **Gaussian units** in this section. In the Meissner state, the internal magnetic induction is zero, so ${\magnetic{M}}_{\mathrm{SC}}=-{\magnetic{H}}_c/(4\pi)$. The normal magnetization is approximately zero. The positive heat absorbed in the transition from the superconducting to the normal phase is therefore:

$${\energy{L}}={\thermal{T}}({\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm N}-{\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{SC}})=-\frac{{\mechanical{V}}{\thermal{T}}}{4\pi}{\magnetic{H}}_c\frac{\mathrm d{\magnetic{H}}_c}{\mathrm d{\thermal{T}}}.$$

A common empirical approximation is:

$${\magnetic{H}}_c({\thermal{T}})\approx {\magnetic{H}}_c(0)\left[1-\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^2\right].$$

Its derivative is negative, so the latent heat is positive below the critical temperature. See [[10.03 Superconducting Transition Heat]] in the chapter Exercises folder for the full calculation.

> [!NOTE] Unit and sign convention
> The factors of $4\pi$ belong to Gaussian magnetic units. Do not combine this equation with SI field and magnetization values. The entropy difference in Eq. (10.1) is superconducting minus normal; the absorbed latent heat uses the opposite difference.
