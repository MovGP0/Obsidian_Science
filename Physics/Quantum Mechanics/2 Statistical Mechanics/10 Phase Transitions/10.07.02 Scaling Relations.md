> [!TLDR]
> Rushbrooke, Widom, Fisher, and Josephson relations connect the critical exponents.

The purpose of scaling relations is to test whether several observables can arise from one consistent critical description. They are conditional statements about the singular equilibrium part of the free energy and long-distance correlations. An inequality from stability and an equality from an added scaling hypothesis have different logical status.

The six exponents are not independent. Scaling gives relations between them.

## Theorem 10.2 — Rushbrooke inequality (1963)

Thermodynamic stability gives:

$$
\alpha+2\beta+\gamma\ge2.
$$

Under the scaling hypothesis, this becomes:

$$
\alpha+2\beta+\gamma=2.
$$

The inequality follows from stability of the free energy and nonnegative heat capacity. The equality is verified by the exactly solved two-dimensional Ising model and by mean-field theory.

## Theorem 10.3 — Widom relation (1965)

$$
\delta=1+\frac\gamma\beta=\frac{\beta+\gamma}{\beta}.
$$

## Theorem 10.4 — Fisher relation (1969)

$$
\gamma=\nu(2-\eta).
$$

**Proof.** Apart from normalization factors, the susceptibility is the zero-wave-number integral of the connected correlation function:

$$
{\magnetic{\chi}}\propto\int G({\mechanical{r}})\,\mathrm d^d {\mechanical{r}}.
$$

Use the critical scaling form with an exponential cutoff at distance ${\mechanical{\xi}}$. Set $u={\mechanical{r}}/{\mechanical{\xi}}$:

$$
{\magnetic{\chi}}\propto{\mechanical{\xi}}^{2-\eta}\int\frac{e^{-u}}{u^{d-2+\eta}}\,\mathrm d^d u
\propto{\mechanical{\xi}}^{2-\eta}\propto|t|^{-\nu(2-\eta)}.
$$

Comparison with ${\magnetic{\chi}}\sim|t|^{-\gamma}$ gives the result. The proportionality includes the temperature and order-parameter normalization needed for the physical susceptibility.

In the Fisher argument, the radial volume element contributes a distance power that cancels the explicit dimension dependence in the correlation exponent. The remaining long-distance integral grows as the correlation length to the power shown. A microscopic lower cutoff contributes regular terms; the critical singular term is controlled by large separations when that power is positive. The exponential is a convenient cutoff representation, not a universal exact correlation function.

## Theorem 10.5 — Josephson relation (hyperscaling, 1967)

$$
2-\alpha=\nu d.
$$

This relation applies below the upper critical dimension $d_c$, under the usual hyperscaling assumptions.

> [!note] Source correction — Upper critical dimension
> The source calls $d<d_c$ “above” the upper critical dimension. It means **below**. For the short-range Ising model, $d_c=4$. Above four dimensions, mean-field exponents violate hyperscaling. At four dimensions, logarithmic corrections require care.

**Remark 10.3.** Rushbrooke, Widom, and Fisher are scaling equalities. Josephson also contains the spatial dimension, which explains its different range of validity.

As an algebraic check, insert the mean-field values. Rushbrooke, Widom, and Fisher are satisfied. Josephson would require the spatial dimension to be four: above four it fails for those same powers. This counterexample shows why hyperscaling has an additional domain restriction. At four dimensions leading powers alone can hide logarithmic corrections. Agreement of fitted exponents with one equality is a consistency check, not independent evidence for the scaling hypothesis or a unique universality class.
