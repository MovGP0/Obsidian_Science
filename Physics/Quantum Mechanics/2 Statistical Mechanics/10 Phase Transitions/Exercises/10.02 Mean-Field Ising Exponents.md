> [!TLDR]
> Expansion of the Weiss self-consistency equation gives β = 1/2, γ = 1, and δ = 3.

## Question

**Exercise 10.2 — Critical exponents of the mean-field Ising model.** Calculate $\beta$, $\gamma$, and $\delta$ in the Weiss mean-field approximation.

## Calculations and explanation

The Weiss approximation replaces the fluctuating neighbor spins by their common mean. Assume a homogeneous ferromagnet with positive exchange energy and equivalent sites; correlations between a spin and its local neighbor environment are neglected. The coordination number counts neighbors. This is why a one-spin thermal average can be closed by a self-consistency equation.

Each spin feels the applied field and the mean contribution of its $z$ neighbors:

$$
h_{\mathrm{eff}}={\energy{H}}+z{\energy{J}}{\magnetic{m}}.
$$

Here ${\magnetic{m}}=\langle s\rangle$ is the mean dimensionless spin. The symbol ${\energy{H}}$ is an energy coefficient, not an SI magnetic field strength. The self-consistency equation is

$$
{\magnetic{m}}=\tanh\left(\frac{{\energy{H}}+z{\energy{J}}{\magnetic{m}}}{{\thermal{k}}_{\mathrm B}{\thermal{T}}}\right).\tag{10.5}
$$

Set ${\thermal{T}}_c=z{\energy{J}}/{\thermal{k}}_{\mathrm B}$ and $t=({\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c)/{\thermal{T}}_c$.

### Order-parameter exponent β

Set ${\energy{H}}=0$ and approach ${\thermal{T}}_c$ from below. The magnetization is small, so use $\tanh x=x-x^3/3+O(x^5)$:

$$
{\magnetic{m}}=\frac{{\thermal{T}}_c}{{\thermal{T}}}{\magnetic{m}}-\frac13\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}_c}{{\thermal{T}}}\right)^3{\magnetic{m}}^3+\cdots.
$$

Since ${\thermal{T}}_c/{\thermal{T}}=1/(1+t)\approx1-t$, retain the leading terms:

$$
0\approx-t{\magnetic{m}}-\frac{{\magnetic{m}}^3}{3}.
$$

Divide by the nonzero ordered solution ${\magnetic{m}}$. This excludes the disordered branch and gives

$$
{\magnetic{m}}^2\approx-3t,\qquad |{\magnetic{m}}|\approx\sqrt3(-t)^{1/2},\qquad \beta_{\mathrm{MF}}=\frac12.
$$

### Susceptibility exponent γ

Differentiate the self-consistency equation with respect to ${\energy{H}}$ at fixed ${\thermal{T}}$. With ${\magnetic{\chi}}=(\partial {\magnetic{m}}/\partial {\energy{H}})_T$:

$$
{\magnetic{\chi}}=\frac{1-{\magnetic{m}}^2}{{\thermal{k}}_{\mathrm B}{\thermal{T}}}(1+z{\energy{J}}{\magnetic{\chi}}).
$$

Above ${\thermal{T}}_c$, ${\magnetic{m}}=0$. Solve for ${\magnetic{\chi}}$:

$$
{\thermal{k}}_{\mathrm B}{\thermal{T}}{\magnetic{\chi}}=1+z{\energy{J}}{\magnetic{\chi}},
\qquad{\magnetic{\chi}}=\frac{1}{{\thermal{k}}_{\mathrm B}({\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c)}\propto t^{-1}.
$$

This is the Curie–Weiss law, and $\gamma_{\mathrm{MF}}=1$. Below ${\thermal{T}}_c$, inserting ${\magnetic{m}}^2\approx-3t$ gives ${\magnetic{\chi}}\approx[2{\thermal{k}}_{\mathrm B}{\thermal{T}}_c|t|]^{-1}$. The exponent is the same on both sides.

### Critical-isotherm exponent δ

At ${\thermal{T}}={\thermal{T}}_c$, put $h={\energy{H}}/({\thermal{k}}_{\mathrm B}{\thermal{T}}_c)$, a dimensionless helper variable. Then

$$
{\magnetic{m}}=\tanh({\magnetic{m}}+h)\approx {\magnetic{m}}+h-\frac{({\magnetic{m}}+h)^3}{3}.
$$

At leading order the balance is ${\magnetic{m}}^3\approx3h$. Therefore

$$
{\magnetic{m}}\propto\operatorname{sgn}({\energy{H}})|{\energy{H}}|^{1/3},\qquad\delta_{\mathrm{MF}}=3.
$$

The expansion is controlled only when its hyperbolic-tangent argument is small near criticality. Below the transition, the leading ordered solution gives magnetization of order the square root of reduced-temperature magnitude; the omitted fifth-order term is then smaller than the cubic term. Extending this expression far below the transition would eventually violate the exact bound on the mean spin, which lies between minus one and one.

At the critical isotherm, the leading balance gives the dimensionless field of order the cube of magnetization. In the expanded cubic expression, terms containing an extra field factor are therefore higher order than the pure magnetization cube. This justifies the step to the leading cube-root law. Both susceptibility branches are positive and have inverse-energy units. These checks validate the approximation's internal algebra, not its neglect of spatial critical correlations.

## Solution

$$
\boxed{\beta_{\mathrm{MF}}=\tfrac12,\quad\gamma_{\mathrm{MF}}=1,\quad\delta_{\mathrm{MF}}=3.}
$$

These are mean-field exponents. Fluctuations change them below the upper critical dimension.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $h_{\mathrm{eff}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Effective Ising field energy | Applied field energy plus the mean interaction energy. |
| ${\energy{H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Ising conjugate field | Energy coefficient multiplying a dimensionless spin; not an SI field strength. |
| ${\energy{J}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange energy | Nearest-neighbor coupling in the Ising Hamiltonian. |
| ${\magnetic{m}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Reduced magnetization | Mean dimensionless Ising spin per site. |
| ${\magnetic{\chi}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Reduced susceptibility | Response of a dimensionless order parameter to an energy-like field. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature of the stated phase transition. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy per particle. |
