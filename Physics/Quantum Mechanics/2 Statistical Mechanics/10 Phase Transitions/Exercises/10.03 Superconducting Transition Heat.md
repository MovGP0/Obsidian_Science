> [!TLDR]
> The latent heat is positive between zero temperature and Tc. It vanishes at both ends and is largest at Tc/√2.

## Question

**Exercise 10.3 — Clausius–Clapeyron relation for a superconductor.** A type-I superconductor has

$${\magnetic{H}}_c({\thermal{T}})={\magnetic{H}}_c(0)\left[1-\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^2\right].$$

Calculate the latent heat for the transition from the superconducting phase to the normal phase. Verify that it vanishes at ${\thermal{T}}=0$ and ${\thermal{T}}={\thermal{T}}_c$.

## Calculations and explanation

Use **Gaussian magnetic units**, as in the source. In these units the heat per sample volume is

$$\frac{{\energy{L}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}=-\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{4\pi}{\magnetic{H}}_c\frac{d{\magnetic{H}}_c}{d{\thermal{T}}}.$$

The minus sign gives positive absorbed heat because the critical field decreases with temperature. Differentiate first:

$$\frac{d{\magnetic{H}}_c}{d{\thermal{T}}}=-\frac{2{\magnetic{H}}_c(0){\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c^2}.$$

Substitute both expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{{\energy{L}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}
&=-\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{4\pi}{\magnetic{H}}_c(0)\left[1-\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^2\right]
\left[-\frac{2{\magnetic{H}}_c(0){\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c^2}\right]\\
&=\frac{{\magnetic{H}}_c^2(0)}{2\pi}\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^2
\left[1-\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^2\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

### End points and maximum

At ${\thermal{T}}\to0$, the factor ${\thermal{T}}^2$ makes ${\energy{L}}/{\mechanical{V}}\to0$. The entropy difference therefore vanishes in this limit, in agreement with the third law.

At ${\thermal{T}}\to {\thermal{T}}_c$, the factor $1-({\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_c)^2$ makes ${\energy{L}}/{\mechanical{V}}\to0$. This is the zero-field end of the transition line, where the transition is continuous.

To find the maximum, put $x={\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_c$. Only the factor $x^2(1-x^2)$ depends on temperature:

$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^2-x^4)=2x-4x^3=0.$$

The interior maximum is at $x=1/\sqrt2$:

$${\thermal{T}}_{\max}=\frac{{\thermal{T}}_c}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\left(\frac{{\energy{L}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{\max}=\frac{{\magnetic{H}}_c^2(0)}{8\pi}.$$

## Solution

$$\boxed{\frac{{\energy{L}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}=\frac{{\magnetic{H}}_c^2(0)}{2\pi}\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^2\left[1-\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^2\right].}$$

The result is positive for $0<{\thermal{T}}<{\thermal{T}}_c$ and zero at both end points. It describes heat absorbed during the transition to the normal phase.

> [!note] Unit convention
> These factors of $4\pi$ belong to Gaussian units. For SI field strength, the corresponding expression is ${\energy{L}}/{\mechanical{V}}=-\mu_0{\thermal{T}} {\magnetic{H}}_c\,d{\magnetic{H}}_c/d{\thermal{T}}$, with $\mu_0$ the vacuum permeability.
