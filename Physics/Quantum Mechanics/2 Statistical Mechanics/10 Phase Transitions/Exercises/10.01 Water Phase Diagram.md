> [!TLDR]
> Water has a melting curve with a negative slope. Its triple point, critical point, ice phases, and density maximum give further constraints on its phase diagram.

## Question

**Exercise 10.1 — Pressure–temperature diagram of water.** Discuss the phase diagram of water. Explain how it differs from the generic diagram.

## Calculations and explanation

### Melting curve

Ice has a lower mass density than liquid water. Use the molar mass ${\mechanical{M}}=18.015\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}$ to convert the density difference into a molar volume difference:

$$
\Delta {\mechanical{v}}={\mechanical{M}}\left(\frac{1}{{\mechanical{\rho}}_{\mathrm{liq}}}-\frac{1}{{\mechanical{\rho}}_{\mathrm{ice}}}\right)
\approx18.015\times10^{-3}\left(\frac{1}{1000}-\frac{1}{917}\right)
\approx-1.63\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

The negative sign means that melting reduces the volume. The Clausius–Clapeyron equation relates this change to the slope of the coexistence curve:

$$
\frac{d{\mechanical{P}}}{d{\thermal{T}}}=\frac{{\energy{L}}}{{\thermal{T}}\Delta {\mechanical{v}}}
\approx\frac{6012}{273.15(-1.63\times10^{-6})}
\approx-1.35\times10^7\,\mathrm{Pa\,K^{-1}}.
$$

Thus, an increase in pressure lowers the melting temperature near atmospheric pressure. The melting curve slopes to the left in a diagram with temperature on the horizontal axis.

### Characteristic points

- **Triple point:** ${\thermal{T}}_t=273.16\,\mathrm K$, ${\mechanical{P}}_t=611.73\,\mathrm{Pa}\approx6.12\,\mathrm{mbar}$. Ice, liquid water, and vapor coexist.
- **Critical point:** ${\thermal{T}}_c\approx647.1\,\mathrm K$, ${\mechanical{P}}_c\approx220.6\,\mathrm{bar}$. The liquid–vapor coexistence curve ends here.

### Further properties

Water has several crystalline ice phases, such as ice I, II, III, V, and VI. Additional phase boundaries occur at high pressure. A diagram with only one solid region cannot show them all.

At approximately $1\,\mathrm{atm}$, liquid water has its maximum density near $4\,{}^\circ\mathrm C$. This density maximum is not a phase transition. It is related to the hydrogen-bond structure of liquid water.

## Solution

The main difference from the generic diagram is the negative slope of the ice-I melting curve. Water also has several solid phases and a density maximum within its liquid phase.
