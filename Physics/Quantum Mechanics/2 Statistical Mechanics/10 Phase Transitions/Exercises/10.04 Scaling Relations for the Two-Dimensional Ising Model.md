> [!TLDR]
> The exact two-dimensional Ising exponents satisfy the Rushbrooke, Fisher, Josephson, and Widom scaling relations.

## Question

**Exercise 10.4 — Check the scaling relations.** Verify the Rushbrooke and Fisher relations for the exact two-dimensional Ising model:

$$
\alpha=0,\quad\beta=\frac18,\quad\gamma=\frac74,\quad\nu=1,\quad\eta=\frac14.
$$

## Calculations and explanation

Use the exact exponents as inputs for an equilibrium, short-range two-dimensional Ising critical point in zero field. The question asks for a consistency check among model results. It does not ask for a measurement, a fit, or a derivation of the exact Ising solution.

These exponents are dimensionless numbers. Substitute them directly into each relation. The value $\alpha=0$ denotes a logarithmic heat-capacity singularity for this model.

### Rushbrooke relation

$$
\alpha+2\beta+\gamma=0+2\left(\frac18\right)+\frac74
=\frac14+\frac74=2.
$$

Thus, the scaling equality is satisfied.

### Fisher relation

$$
\nu(2-\eta)=1\left(2-\frac14\right)=\frac74=\gamma.
$$

This checks the link between the susceptibility and the spatial correlations.

### Josephson relation — additional source check

With spatial dimension $d=2$:

$$
\nu d=1\cdot2=2=2-\alpha.
$$

The model lies below its upper critical dimension, so this hyperscaling check applies.

### Widom relation — additional check

The other exponents predict

$$
\delta=1+\frac{\gamma}{\beta}=1+\frac{7/4}{1/8}=15,
$$

which is the exact critical-isotherm exponent.

Keep the fractions until the final comparison: replacing them by short decimals can produce an artificial mismatch. The logarithmic heat-capacity singularity is compatible with zero leading power, so the Rushbrooke arithmetic does not imply finite heat capacity. Fisher connects the integrated long-distance correlation to susceptibility, while Josephson additionally uses the spatial dimension and its hyperscaling domain.

The Widom calculation predicts the critical-isotherm exponent from the temperature-path exponents. Agreement with its known exact value checks the compatibility of those limiting paths. It does not make the paths interchangeable. As a transfer check, try the mean-field exponents in Josephson at two dimensions: the equality fails, showing that internally consistent mean-field powers cannot replace the exact two-dimensional critical description.

## Solution

All checked scaling relations hold. The first two answer the question; the Josephson check is also given in the source solution.
