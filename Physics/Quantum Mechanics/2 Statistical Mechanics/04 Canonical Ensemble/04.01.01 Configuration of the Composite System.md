> [!TLDR]
> The system and bath exchange energy while their total energy remains fixed.

Consider a system $\mathcal S$ with Hamiltonian ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$. It is in contact with a heat bath $\mathcal B$, with Hamiltonian ${\energy{\mathcal H}}_{\mathcal B}$ and many more degrees of freedom. The composite system $\mathcal T=\mathcal S+\mathcal B$ is isolated:

$$
{\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}}={\energy{E}}+{\energy{E}}_{\mathcal B}=\mathrm{const}.
$$

Assume **weak coupling**: the interaction energy is negligible compared with the internal energies. Then:

$$
{\energy{\mathcal H}}_{\mathrm{tot}}={\energy{\mathcal H}}+{\energy{\mathcal H}}_{\mathcal B}.
$$

Here the additive Hamiltonian is the weak-coupling approximation, not a claim that the contact has no interaction. Energy transfer requires an interaction. It must be small enough to neglect in equilibrium state energies, yet act for long enough to establish thermal equilibrium. The outer boundary is isolated; only the inner system boundary passes heat.

If the system gains a specified energy, the bath loses the same energy. Thus the bath state count must be evaluated at the remaining bath energy, not at an independently fixed bath energy. For a finite bath, its temperature changes slightly during this transfer. The ideal thermostat approximation removes this change, not total-energy conservation.

The composite system can be treated with the microcanonical ensemble. All accessible microstates consistent with its total energy are equally probable.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The classical energy function or quantum energy operator. |
