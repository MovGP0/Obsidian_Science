> [!TLDR]
> A classical one-dimensional harmonic oscillator has mean energy equal to thermal energy.

For mass ${\mechanical{m}}$ and angular frequency ${\signal{\omega}}$:

$$
{\energy{\mathcal H}}_{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{{\mechanical{p}}^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}}+\frac12{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2{\mechanical{q}}^2.
$$

The classical partition function is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Z_1^{\mathrm{cl}}
&=\frac1{\energy{h}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm d{\mechanical{q}}
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm d{\mechanical{p}}\,
e^{-{\beta}[{\mechanical{p}}^2/(2{\mechanical{m}})+{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2{\mechanical{q}}^2/2]}\\
&=\frac1{\energy{h}}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi {\mechanical{m}}}{{\beta}}}
\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{{\beta} {\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2}}
=\frac{2\pi}{{\beta}{\signal{\omega}} {\energy{h}}}
=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The mean energy is:

$$
\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle_{\mathrm{cl}}
=-\frac{\mathrm d\ln\mathcal Z_1^{\mathrm{cl}}}{\mathrm d{\beta}}
=\frac1{\beta}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

Equipartition gives ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}/2$ for the kinetic term and the same amount for the potential term.

| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
