> [!TLDR]
> Inverse thermal energy controls how strongly higher-energy microstates are suppressed.

The parameter ${\beta}=1/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})$ is called **inverse temperature** or **coldness**. More precisely, its unit is inverse energy.

- At high positive temperature, ${\beta}\to0^+$ and the weights become less different.
- At low positive temperature, ${\beta}\to+\infty$ and probability concentrates in the lowest-energy states.

> [!note] High-temperature limit
> The source describes the limit as a uniform distribution. This is a normalized uniform limit for a finite state space. An unbounded spectrum or an infinite phase space need not have a normalizable uniform distribution.

The factor $e^{-{\beta} {\energy{E}}_i}$ is the **Boltzmann factor** or **Boltzmann weight**. The probability ratio for two microstates is:

$$
\frac{P_j}{P_i}=e^{-{\beta}({\energy{E}}_j-{\energy{E}}_i)}.
$$

A microstate higher in energy by $\Delta {\energy{E}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$ has $e^{-1}\approx0.37$ times the probability of the lower state. Thermal energy sets the relevant energy scale.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
