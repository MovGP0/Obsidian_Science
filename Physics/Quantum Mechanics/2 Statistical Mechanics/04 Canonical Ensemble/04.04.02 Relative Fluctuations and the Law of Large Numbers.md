> [!TLDR]
> For ordinary extensive systems, relative energy fluctuations decrease as the inverse square root of particle count.

Energy and heat capacity are extensive:

$$
{\energy{U}}\sim {\mechanical{N}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_0,\qquad {\thermal{C}_V}\sim {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}.
$$

Consequently:

$$
{\energy{\sigma}}_E\sim\sqrt{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}^2{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}
={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\sqrt {\mechanical{N}},
\qquad \frac{{\energy{\sigma}}_E}{\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt {\mechanical{N}}}.
$$

## Proposition 4.2 — Statistical equivalence of ensembles

As ${\mechanical{N}}\to\infty$, the canonical energy distribution concentrates around its mean. Its relative width is of order ${\mechanical{N}}^{-1/2}$ and tends to zero. Under the usual conditions for ensemble equivalence, the canonical and microcanonical descriptions give the same thermodynamics.

The energy probability density includes the density of states $g({\energy{E}})$:

$$
P({\energy{E}})=\frac{g({\energy{E}})e^{-{\beta} {\energy{E}}}}{\mathcal Z}
\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi{\energy{\sigma}}_E^2}}
\exp\left[-\frac{({\energy{E}}-\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle)^2}{2{\energy{\sigma}}_E^2}\right].
$$

The Gaussian is a local large-system approximation around a single stable maximum.

> [!note] Conditions
> Extensivity and a finite response coefficient are assumed. At phase coexistence the distribution can have two peaks; near critical points the simple Gaussian and square-root scaling need qualification.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | The canonical mean energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | 1 | Particle or subsystem count | The number of particles, localized spins, or independent oscillators. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy scale | The level spacing or characteristic energy per particle. |
| ${\thermal{C}_V}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity at constant volume | The temperature derivative of internal energy at fixed volume and particle count. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\energy{\sigma}}_E$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy standard deviation | The square root of the canonical energy variance. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
| $g(E)$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | The number of states per energy interval. |
