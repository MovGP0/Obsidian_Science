> [!TLDR]
> A two-level system has a heat-capacity maximum when thermal energy is comparable to its level spacing.

Many physical systems have a useful two-state approximation: spin-½, two-state molecules, and crystal defects.

Take energies $0$ and ${\energy{\varepsilon}}>0$:

$$
\mathcal Z=1+e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\varepsilon}}},
\qquad
\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle=\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}} e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}}{1+e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}}
=\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{e^{{\beta}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}+1}.
$$

The excited-state probability is:

$$
P_1=\frac{e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}}{1+e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}}
=\frac1{e^{{\beta}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}+1}.
$$

It has the same algebraic form as a Fermi–Dirac occupation with zero chemical potential. This form alone does not make a two-state system a gas of fermions.

The heat capacity gives the **Schottky anomaly**:

$$
{\thermal{C}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left(\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}\right)^2
\frac{e^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})}}
{[e^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})}+1]^2}. \tag{4.14}
$$

It has a maximum at ${\thermal{T}}^*\approx0.417{\energy{\varepsilon}}/{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ and tends to zero as either ${\thermal{T}}\to0^+$ or ${\thermal{T}}\to\infty$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy scale | The level spacing or characteristic energy per particle. |
| ${\thermal{C}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity | The temperature derivative of mean energy at the stated fixed external parameters. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
