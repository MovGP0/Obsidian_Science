> [!TLDR]
> The number of compatible bath states gives each system state its Boltzmann weight.

Let ${\energy{E}}_i$ be the energy of microstate $i$ of the system. Its probability is proportional to the number of bath states compatible with the remaining energy:

$$
P_i\propto\Omega_{\mathcal B}({\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}}-{\energy{E}}_i). \tag{4.1}
$$

Here $\Omega_{\mathcal B}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ counts bath states at energy ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$. For a bath much larger than the system, expand its logarithm:

$$
\ln\Omega_{\mathcal B}({\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}}-{\energy{E}}_i)
=\ln\Omega_{\mathcal B}({\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}})
-{\energy{E}}_i\left.\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_{\mathcal B}}{\partial{\energy{\varepsilon}}}\right|_{{\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}}}
+O\left(\frac{{\energy{E}}_i^2}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}^2{\thermal{C}_{\mathcal B}}}\right). \tag{4.2}
$$

The entropy derivative gives:

$$
\left.\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_{\mathcal B}}{\partial{\energy{\varepsilon}}}\right|_{{\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}}}
=\frac{1}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

Insert this result into Eq. (4.1), neglect the higher terms, and normalize:

$$
P_i=\frac{1}{\mathcal Z}e^{-{\beta} {\energy{E}}_i},
\qquad {\beta}\equiv\frac{1}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}. \tag{4.3}
$$

The normalization factor is the **canonical partition function**:

$$
\mathcal Z({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}})=\sum_i e^{-{\beta} {\energy{E}}_i}.
$$

> [!note] Remark 4.1 — Ideal heat bath
> The second derivative is $-1/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}^2{\thermal{C}_{\mathcal B}})$. Thus the leading omitted term is $-{\energy{E}}_i^2/(2{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}^2{\thermal{C}_{\mathcal B}})$. It vanishes as the bath heat capacity tends to infinity at fixed system energy. This is the formal ideal-thermostat limit.

## Definition 4.1 — Canonical ensemble

The canonical ensemble is a collection of imagined copies of a system with fixed particle count, volume, and temperature. Their microstates follow the Boltzmann distribution in Eq. (4.3).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The volume available to the particles. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | 1 | Particle or subsystem count | The number of particles, localized spins, or independent oscillators. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy scale | The level spacing or characteristic energy per particle. |
| ${\thermal{C}_{\mathcal B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Bath heat capacity | The heat capacity of the reservoir. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
| $\Omega$ | 1 | State count | The number of compatible microscopic states. |
