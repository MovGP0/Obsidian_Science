> [!TLDR]
> Each unrestricted quadratic Hamiltonian term contributes half the thermal energy in classical equilibrium.

## Theorem 4.3 — Equipartition of energy

Let a classical equilibrium Hamiltonian depend on a phase-space coordinate ${\mechanical{\xi}}_i$ only through a separate term ${a}_i{\mechanical{\xi}}_i^2$, with ${a}_i>0$. For an unrestricted integration range:

$$
\langle {a}_i{\mechanical{\xi}}_i^2\rangle=\frac12{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

The variable can be a position or a momentum.

**Proof.** Other independent factors cancel in the normalized mean:

$$
\langle {a}_i{\mechanical{\xi}}_i^2\rangle=
\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{a}_i{\mechanical{\xi}}_i^2e^{-{\beta} {a}_i{\mechanical{\xi}}_i^2}\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{\xi}}_i}
{\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-{\beta} {a}_i{\mechanical{\xi}}_i^2}\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{\xi}}_i}.
$$

Set $u=\sqrt{{\beta} {a}_i}{\mechanical{\xi}}_i$. Then $\mathrm d{\mechanical{\xi}}_i=\mathrm du/\sqrt{{\beta} {a}_i}$ and ${\mechanical{\xi}}_i^2=u^2/({\beta} {a}_i)$. The numerator becomes:

$$
\frac1{{\beta}\sqrt{{\beta} {a}_i}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}u^2e^{-u^2}\,\mathrm du
=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2{\beta}\sqrt{{\beta} {a}_i}}.
$$

The denominator is $\sqrt{\pi/({\beta} {a}_i)}$. Their ratio gives:

$$
\langle {a}_i{\mechanical{\xi}}_i^2\rangle
=\frac{\sqrt\pi/[2{\beta}\sqrt{{\beta} {a}_i}]}
{\sqrt{\pi/({\beta} {a}_i)}}=\frac1{2{\beta}}=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}{2}.
$$

This proves the result. $\square$

> [!note] Remark 4.4 — Limits of equipartition
> The theorem assumes a classical Hamiltonian with a separate quadratic term and an effectively unrestricted variable. It does not give the full energy of a nonquadratic term. Quantum excitations with level spacing comparable to or larger than thermal energy are suppressed, so their classical heat-capacity contribution fails.

For a monatomic ideal gas, the $3{\mechanical{N}}$ momentum terms give ${\energy{U}}=3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}/2$. A classical rigid diatomic gas has two additional rotational terms:

$$
{\energy{U}}=\frac52{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}},\qquad {\thermal{C}_V}=\frac52{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}.
$$

If one classical vibrational mode is active, it adds one kinetic and one potential term:

$$
{\energy{U}}=\frac72{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}},\qquad {\thermal{C}_V}=\frac72{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | The canonical mean energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | 1 | Particle or subsystem count | The number of particles, localized spins, or independent oscillators. |
| ${\thermal{C}_V}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity at constant volume | The temperature derivative of internal energy at fixed volume and particle count. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| ${\mechanical{\xi}}_i$ | — | Phase-space variable | A position or momentum coordinate in the quadratic term. |
| ${a}_i$ | — | Quadratic coefficient | Its unit makes aᵢξᵢ² an energy. |
