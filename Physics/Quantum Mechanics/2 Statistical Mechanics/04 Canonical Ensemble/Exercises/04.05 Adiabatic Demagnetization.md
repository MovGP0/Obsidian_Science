> [!TLDR]
> Reversible adiabatic demagnetization lowers temperature in proportion to field magnitude for ideal independent spins.

## Question

Consider ${\mechanical{N}}$ spin-½ moments initially in equilibrium at temperature ${\thermal{T}}_0$ in field ${\magnetic{B}}_0$. The field is reduced adiabatically and reversibly toward zero. Use ${\thermal{S}}({\thermal{T}}_f,{\magnetic{B}}_f)={\thermal{S}}({\thermal{T}}_0,{\magnetic{B}}_0)$ to find the cooling condition and explain the principle of adiabatic demagnetization.

## Worked solution

> [!note] Source clarification and added solution
> The source says that the final temperature tends to zero only when the field tends to zero slowly. In the ideal model the precise condition is an isentropic, quasistatic limit. Slow variation alone does not guarantee reversibility or thermal isolation. An exact zero-field, zero-temperature endpoint is a singular limit, not a state that this argument reaches in a finite operation.

### Express entropy through one ratio

For independent spins:

$$
{\thermal{S}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}[\ln(2\cosh x)-x\tanh x],
\qquad x=\frac{{\magnetic{\mu}_0}{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

For $x>0$:

$$
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\frac{{\thermal{S}}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}
=-x\operatorname{sech}^2x<0.
$$

The entropy depends only on $|x|$ and is strictly decreasing with this magnitude away from zero.

### Apply reversibility and thermal isolation

No heat enters a thermally isolated system. Reversibility removes entropy production, so entropy stays constant. For an initial positive-temperature state with nonzero field, the equality of entropies fixes $|x_f|=|x_0|$:

$$
\frac{|{\magnetic{B}}_f|}{{\thermal{T}}_f}=\frac{|{\magnetic{B}}_0|}{{\thermal{T}}_0},
\qquad
{\thermal{T}}_f={\thermal{T}}_0\frac{|{\magnetic{B}}_f|}{|{\magnetic{B}}_0|}.
$$

The level gap decreases with field, while the relative spin populations stay fixed. Their same population ratio can therefore be described by a lower temperature.

### Explain the limiting process

Within the ideal model, ${\thermal{T}}_f\to0^+$ as $|{\magnetic{B}}_f|\to0$ along this isentrope. If the field were set to zero while keeping a finite temperature, the entropy would instead be ${\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln2$. That endpoint is not continuous with an initial state of lower entropy along the same equilibrium family.

Real magnetic interactions, internal fields, heat leaks, and finite relaxation times limit the cooling. A sudden field change does not supply the quasistatic equilibrium argument used above.

## Answer

For reversible adiabatic demagnetization, ${\thermal{T}}_f/{\thermal{T}}_0=|{\magnetic{B}}_f/{\magnetic{B}}_0|$ in the independent-spin model. Zero temperature is approached as a limit; this calculation does not establish a finite-time route to absolute zero.
