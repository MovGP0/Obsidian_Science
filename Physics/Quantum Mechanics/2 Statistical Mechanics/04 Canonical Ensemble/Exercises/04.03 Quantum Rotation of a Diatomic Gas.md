> [!TLDR]
> Quantum rotations freeze out at low temperature and recover the classical rotational heat capacity at high temperature.

## Question

A gas contains ${\mechanical{N}}$ rigid diatomic molecules in volume ${\mechanical{V}}$ at temperature ${\thermal{T}}$. Treat rotation quantum mechanically, with levels ${\energy{E}}_\ell={\energy{\hbar}}^2\ell(\ell+1)/(2{\mechanical{I}})$ and degeneracy $2\ell+1$. Show that the classical result ${\thermal{C}_V}=5{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}/2$ is recovered at high temperature, while ${\thermal{C}_V}\to3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}/2$ at low rotational temperature.

> [!note] Source unit correction
> The source compares ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$ directly with ${\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}$. The rotational temperature has unit K, so the correct comparison is ${\thermal{T}}\gg{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}$, or ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\gg {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}$.

## Worked solution

> [!note] Added solution and regime
> The question is transcribed from Exercise 4.3. The supplied unrestricted rotor levels are used. Translation remains classical and dilute. “Low temperature” below means low compared with the rotational scale, while the translational classical approximation still applies.

### Write the rotational sum

Define:

$$
{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2}{2{\mechanical{I}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}},
\qquad a=\frac{{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}}{{\thermal{T}}},
\qquad L_\ell=\ell(\ell+1).
$$

Then:

$$
z_{\mathrm{rot}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty(2\ell+1)e^{-aL_\ell},
\qquad
\mathcal Z_N=\frac1{{\mechanical{N}}!}\left[\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3}z_{\mathrm{rot}}\right]^{\mechanical{N}}.
$$

The dimensionless helper $L_\ell$ is not angular momentum itself.

### Express rotational energy and heat capacity

With probabilities proportional to $(2\ell+1)e^{-aL_\ell}$:

$$
{\energy{U}}_{\mathrm{rot}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}\langle L\rangle,
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{rot}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}a^2(\langle L^2\rangle-\langle L\rangle^2).
$$

Translation adds $3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}/2$ to energy and $3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}/2$ to heat capacity.

### High rotational temperature

For $a\ll1$, many levels are populated, so replace the sum by an integral:

$$
z_{\mathrm{rot}}\approx
\int_0^\infty(2\ell+1)e^{-a\ell(\ell+1)}\,\mathrm d\ell.
$$

Use $u=\ell(\ell+1)$, so $\mathrm du=(2\ell+1)\mathrm d\ell$. This gives $z_{\mathrm{rot}}\approx1/a={\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}$.

Therefore:

$$
{\energy{U}}_{\mathrm{rot}}\approx {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}},\qquad
{\thermal{C}_V}\approx\frac32{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}+{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}=\frac52{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}.
$$

### Low rotational temperature

For $a\gg1$, retain the ground level $\ell=0$ and first excited level $\ell=1$:

$$
z_{\mathrm{rot}}\approx1+3e^{-2a},
\qquad
{\energy{U}}_{\mathrm{rot}}\sim6{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{\Theta}}_{\mathrm{rot}}e^{-2a}.
$$

Differentiation gives:

$$
C_{\mathrm{rot}}\sim12{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}a^2e^{-2a}\to0.
$$

## Answer

Rotation contributes ${\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ at high rotational temperature and an exponentially small amount at low rotational temperature. With classical translation, the total limits are $5{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}/2$ and $3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}/2$. Homonuclear nuclear-spin selection rules can change the detailed low-temperature rotational spectrum.
