> [!TLDR]
> The ideal-gas pressure follows from the volume derivative of the partition function in either two or three dimensions.

## Question

Calculate the pressure of a classical ideal gas from ${\mechanical{P}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}(\partial\ln\mathcal Z/\partial {\mechanical{V}})_{T,N}$ and verify ${\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$.

Then calculate the pressure of a two-dimensional ideal gas confined to area ${\mechanical{A}}$ and the analogous “surface tension” ${\mechanical{\gamma}}=-(\partial {\energy{F}}/\partial {\mechanical{A}})_T$.

## Worked solution

> [!note] Added solution
> The question is transcribed from Exercise 4.4. Keep particle count fixed in each derivative.

### Three dimensions

The partition function is:

$$
\mathcal Z_{3D}=\frac1{{\mechanical{N}}!}\left(\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3}\right)^{\mechanical{N}},
\qquad {\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}=\frac {\energy{h}}{\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}}.
$$

At fixed temperature the thermal wavelength is constant, so:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial\ln\mathcal Z_{3D}}{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{T,N}
=\frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}.
$$

Thus ${\mechanical{P}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}/{\mechanical{V}}$ and ${\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$.

### Two dimensions

There are two Gaussian momentum integrals and an area integral:

$$
\mathcal Z_{2D}=\frac1{{\mechanical{N}}!}\left(\frac {\mechanical{A}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^2}\right)^{\mechanical{N}}.
$$

The area dependence of ${\energy{F}}=-{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\ln\mathcal Z_{2D}$ gives:

$$
{\mechanical{\gamma}}=-\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{F}}}{\partial {\mechanical{A}}}\right)_{T,N}
=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{A}}}.
$$

Therefore ${\mechanical{\gamma}} {\mechanical{A}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$.

> [!note] Meaning and sign
> The source calls this quantity “surface tension.” With its specified minus sign it is the two-dimensional spreading pressure, in N/m or J/m². Conventional tensile surface tension is often defined with the opposite sign, $+(\partial {\energy{F}}/\partial {\mechanical{A}})$. The physical sign convention must therefore be stated.

## Answer

The three-dimensional equation is ${\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$. The corresponding two-dimensional equation is ${\mechanical{\gamma}} {\mechanical{A}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$ with the source's definition of ${\mechanical{\gamma}}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{F}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | The equilibrium potential at fixed temperature, volume, and particle count. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The volume available to the particles. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | 1 | Particle or subsystem count | The number of particles, localized spins, or independent oscillators. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The force per area conjugate to volume. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Particle mass | The mass of one atom or molecule. |
| ${\energy{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | The classical phase-space cell constant. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal wavelength | The thermal de Broglie wavelength. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\mechanical{A}}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Area | The available area of the two-dimensional gas. |
| ${\mechanical{\gamma}}$ | $\unit{J\,m^{-2}}$ | Two-dimensional spreading pressure | Minus the area derivative of free energy, using the source sign. |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
