> [!TLDR]
> Three equally spaced levels give a finite partition sum and a heat capacity that vanishes at both temperature limits.

## Question

A system has three nondegenerate levels with energies $0,{\energy{\varepsilon}},2{\energy{\varepsilon}}$. Calculate:

(a) $\mathcal Z({\thermal{T}})$.

(b) $\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle({\thermal{T}})$.

(c) ${\thermal{C}}({\thermal{T}})$.

(d) The low- and high-temperature limits of all these quantities.

## Worked solution

> [!note] Added solution
> The question is transcribed from Exercise 4.2. Assume ${\energy{\varepsilon}}>0$ and positive temperature.

### (a) Sum the three weights

Set $x={\beta}{\energy{\varepsilon}}$ and $q=e^{-x}$. Each nondegenerate level contributes one weight:

$$
\mathcal Z=1+q+q^2.
$$

### (b) Weight each energy

$$
\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle
=\frac{0+{\energy{\varepsilon}} q+2{\energy{\varepsilon}} q^2}{1+q+q^2}
={\energy{\varepsilon}}\frac{q+2q^2}{1+q+q^2}.
$$

### (c) Use the variance to obtain heat capacity

The second moment is:

$$
\langle {\energy{E}}^2\rangle={\energy{\varepsilon}}^2\frac{q+4q^2}{1+q+q^2}.
$$

Subtract the squared mean:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}({\energy{E}})
&={\energy{\varepsilon}}^2
\frac{(q+4q^2)(1+q+q^2)-(q+2q^2)^2}{(1+q+q^2)^2}\\
&={\energy{\varepsilon}}^2\frac{q+4q^2+q^3}{(1+q+q^2)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The canonical fluctuation relation then gives:

$$
{\thermal{C}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}x^2\frac{q+4q^2+q^3}{(1+q+q^2)^2}.
$$

### (d) Take the temperature limits

For ${\thermal{T}}\to0^+$, $q\to0$:

$$
\mathcal Z=1+e^{-x}+O(e^{-2x}),
\quad \langle {\energy{E}}\rangle\sim{\energy{\varepsilon}} e^{-x},
\quad {\thermal{C}}\sim {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}x^2e^{-x}\to0.
$$

Only the ground state remains populated. For ${\thermal{T}}\to\infty$, all three probabilities tend to $1/3$:

$$
\mathcal Z\to3,\qquad
\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle\to{\energy{\varepsilon}},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}({\energy{E}})\to\frac23{\energy{\varepsilon}}^2,
$$

$$
{\thermal{C}}\sim\frac23{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left(\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}\right)^2\to0.
$$

More precisely, $\mathcal Z=3-3x+(5/2)x^2+\cdots$ and $\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle={\energy{\varepsilon}}[1-(2/3)x+O(x^3)]$.

## Answer

The finite state space makes high-temperature probabilities uniform. The mean tends to the arithmetic mean of the levels; heat capacity tends to zero at both positive-temperature extremes.
