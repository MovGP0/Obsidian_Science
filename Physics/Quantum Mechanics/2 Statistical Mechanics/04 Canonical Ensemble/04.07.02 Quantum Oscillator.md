> [!TLDR]
> The quantum oscillator keeps zero-point energy at low temperature and approaches classical equipartition at high temperature.

The quantum oscillator has discrete levels:

$$
{\energy{E}}_n={\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}\left(n+\frac12\right),\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

Its partition function is a geometric series:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Z_1^{\mathrm{qu}}
&=\sum_{n=0}^\infty e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}(n+1/2)}
=e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty(e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}})^n\\
&=\frac{e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/2}}{1-e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}}
=\frac1{2\sinh({\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.13}
$$

The mean energy is:

$$
\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle_{\mathrm{qu}}
={\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}\left(\langle n\rangle+\frac12\right)
={\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}\left(\frac1{e^{{\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}-1}+\frac12\right).
$$

Here $\langle n\rangle=(e^{{\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}-1)^{-1}$ is the Planck mean occupation.

## Limiting cases

At high temperature, ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\gg{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}$, set $x={\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}\ll1$ and use:

$$
\frac1{e^x-1}=\frac1x-\frac12+\frac{x}{12}+\cdots.
$$

Then:

$$
\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle_{\mathrm{qu}}
\approx{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}\left(\frac1x-\frac12+\frac12\right)
=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}{x}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

The classical result is recovered. At low temperature:

$$
\langle {\energy{E}}\rangle_{\mathrm{qu}}
\approx\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}{2}+{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}} e^{-{\beta}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}.
$$

Only exponentially small thermal corrections remain above the zero-point energy.

The heat capacity is the Einstein function:

$$
{\thermal{C}_V}^{\mathrm{qu}}
={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left(\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}\right)^2
\frac{e^{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})}}
{[e^{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})}-1]^2}.
$$

This result is the basis of the Einstein model for solid heat capacities.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Planck constant divided by 2π. |
| ${\signal{\omega}}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The oscillator angular frequency. |
| ${\thermal{C}_V}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity at constant volume | The temperature derivative of internal energy at fixed volume and particle count. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
