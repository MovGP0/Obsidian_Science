> [!TLDR]
> The canonical density operator gives quantum thermal averages through a trace.

In quantum mechanics, a trace replaces the classical phase-space sum. The canonical density operator is:

$$
\hat\rho=\frac{e^{-{\beta}\hat{{\energy{\mathcal H}}}}}{\mathcal Z},
\qquad \mathcal Z=\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-{\beta}\hat{{\energy{\mathcal H}}}}).
$$

For an observable $\hat {A}$:

$$
\langle {A}\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho\hat {A})
=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(\hat {A} e^{-{\beta}\hat{{\energy{\mathcal H}}}})}
{\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-{\beta}\hat{{\energy{\mathcal H}}}})}.
$$

In an energy eigenbasis $\{|\psi_n\rangle\}$:

$$
\mathcal Z=\sum_n e^{-{\beta} {\energy{E}}_n}.
$$

This has the same formal structure as Eq. (4.4). The operator $e^{-{\beta}\hat{{\energy{\mathcal H}}}}$ is called the **Boltzmann operator** or **Euclidean propagator** because it has the imaginary-time form:

$$
e^{-{\signal{\tau}}\hat{{\energy{\mathcal H}}}/{\energy{\hbar}}},\qquad {\signal{\tau}}={\energy{\hbar}}{\beta}.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Planck constant divided by 2π. |
| ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The classical energy function or quantum energy operator. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| ${A}$ | — | Observable | A measured quantity whose unit depends on the operator. |
| ${\signal{\tau}}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Imaginary-time interval | The Euclidean time parameter ħβ. |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
