> [!TLDR]
> The Brillouin function extends the independent-spin result to arbitrary angular-momentum quantum number.

For angular-momentum quantum number $J$, the magnetic quantum number has $2J+1$ values: $m_J=-J,\ldots,J$. The one-spin partition function is:

$$
\mathcal Z_1=\sum_{m_J=-J}^J e^{{\beta} {g}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}m_J {\magnetic{B}}}
=\frac{\sinh[(2J+1)\eta/2]}{\sinh(\eta/2)},
\qquad \eta=\frac{{g}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

The total magnetic moment is:

$$
{\magnetic{M}}={\mechanical{N}}{g}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}J {\magnetic{B}}_J(\eta J).
$$

The **Brillouin function** is:

$$
{\magnetic{B}}_J(y)=\frac{2J+1}{2J}\coth\left(\frac{2J+1}{2J}y\right)
-\frac1{2J}\coth\left(\frac y{2J}\right).
$$

For $J=1/2$, ${\magnetic{B}}_{1/2}(y)=\tanh y$, consistent with Eq. (4.16). For $J\to\infty$ at fixed $y$:

$$
{\magnetic{B}}_J(y)\longrightarrow\mathcal L(y)=\coth y-\frac1y.
$$

This is the Langevin function of classical paramagnetism. At zero argument, the apparent singularities are removed by taking the continuous limit.
