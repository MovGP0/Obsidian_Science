> [!TLDR]
> The hyperbolic-tangent magnetization is linear at weak field and saturates at strong field.

The mean total magnetic moment is:

$$
{\magnetic{M}}={\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}\langle\sigma\rangle
={\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}\frac{\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\sigma e^{{\beta}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}{\magnetic{B}}\sigma}}{\mathcal Z_1}
={\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}\tanh({\beta}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}{\magnetic{B}}).
$$

The free-energy derivative gives the same result:

$$
{\magnetic{M}}=-\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{F}}}{\partial {\magnetic{B}}}\right)_{T,N}
={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\frac{\partial\ln\mathcal Z_N}{\partial {\magnetic{B}}}
={\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}\tanh({\beta}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}{\magnetic{B}}).
$$

For $x={\beta}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}{\magnetic{B}}={\magnetic{\mu}_0}{\magnetic{B}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})$:

$$
{\magnetic{M}}={\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}\tanh x,\qquad x=\frac{{\magnetic{\mu}_0}{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}. \tag{4.16}
$$

In the weak-field limit, $|x|\ll1$:

$$
{\magnetic{M}}\approx {\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}x=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}^2{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

Thus the weak-field response follows **Curie's law**:

$$
{\magnetic{\chi}}=\frac{\partial {\magnetic{M}}}{\partial {\magnetic{B}}}
\approx\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}^2}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}
=\frac{{\magnetic{C}_{\mathrm{Curie}}}}{{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

In the strong positive-field limit, $x\gg1$, ${\magnetic{M}}\to {\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_0}$: all moments align and the magnetization saturates.

> [!note] Total moment versus magnetization density
> The source calls ${\magnetic{M}}$ “total magnetization.” It is the total magnetic moment, in J/T. Accordingly, ${\magnetic{\chi}}=\partial {\magnetic{M}}/\partial {\magnetic{B}}$ has unit J/T². The usual SI volume magnetization and its dimensionless susceptibility require division by volume and the appropriate field convention. The source's Curie constant C is written ${\magnetic{C}_{\mathrm{Curie}}}$ here to distinguish it from heat capacity.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{F}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | The equilibrium potential at fixed temperature, volume, and particle count. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | 1 | Particle or subsystem count | The number of particles, localized spins, or independent oscillators. |
| ${\magnetic{B}}$ | $\unit{T}$ | Magnetic flux density | The applied magnetic field in the source convention. |
| ${\magnetic{M}}$ | $\unit{J\,T^{-1}}$ | Total magnetic moment | The sum of the moments of all spins. |
| ${\magnetic{\chi}}$ | $\unit{J\,T^{-2}}$ | Total moment response | The derivative of total magnetic moment with respect to B. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\magnetic{\mu}_0}$ | $\unit{J\,T^{-1}}$ | Effective spin moment | The moment magnitude used in this chapter, not vacuum permeability. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| ${\magnetic{C}_{\mathrm{Curie}}}$ | $\unit{J\,K\,T^{-2}}$ | Curie constant | The coefficient in the weak-field response χ=C/T. |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
