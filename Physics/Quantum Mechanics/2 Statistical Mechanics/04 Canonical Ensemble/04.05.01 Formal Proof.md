> [!TLDR]
> The leading extensive free energy is the same in the canonical and microcanonical descriptions.

## Theorem 4.2 — Equivalence of ensembles

In the thermodynamic limit, ${\mechanical{N}},{\mechanical{V}}\to\infty$ with ${\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{V}}$ fixed, canonical and microcanonical predictions for intensive thermodynamic quantities agree for ordinary additive systems in a single stable phase.

> [!note] Scope clarification
> The source states the theorem without these conditions. Short-range interactions, a suitable concave entropy, and a single dominant saddle are needed for the following proof. Long-range systems and phase coexistence require separate treatment.

**Proof.** Write the canonical partition function using the microcanonical density of states:

$$
\mathcal Z=\int_0^\infty g({\energy{E}})e^{-{\beta} {\energy{E}}}\,\mathrm d{\energy{E}}. \tag{4.10}
$$

For a macroscopic system, $g({\energy{E}})$ grows rapidly, while the Boltzmann factor decreases exponentially. Their product has a maximum at ${\energy{E}}={\energy{E}}^*$:

$$
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial {\energy{E}}}
[\ln g({\energy{E}})-{\beta} {\energy{E}}]\right|_{E^*}=0,
\qquad
\left.\frac{1}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{mc}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}\right|_{E^*}
={\beta}=\frac{1}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

This is the microcanonical temperature relation. The most probable energy ${\energy{E}}^*$ and the canonical mean $U({\thermal{T}})$ agree to leading extensive order.

Expand the exponent around ${\energy{E}}^*$:

$$
\ln g({\energy{E}})-{\beta} {\energy{E}}
\approx\ln g({\energy{E}}^*)-{\beta} {\energy{E}}^*
-\frac{({\energy{E}}-{\energy{E}}^*)^2}{2{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}^2{\thermal{C}_V}}.
$$

Integrate the resulting Gaussian:

$$
\mathcal Z\approx
g({\energy{E}}^*)e^{-{\beta} {\energy{E}}^*}\sqrt{2\pi {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}^2{\thermal{C}_V}}.
$$

To take a dimensionless logarithm, choose a fixed reference energy ${\energy{E}}_0$ and set ${\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{mc}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln[g({\energy{E}}^*){\energy{E}}_0]$. Then:

$$
\ln\mathcal Z\approx
\frac{{\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{mc}}({\energy{E}}^*)}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}-{\beta} {\energy{E}}^*
+\frac12\ln\left(\frac{2\pi {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}^2{\thermal{C}_V}}{{\energy{E}}_0^2}\right).
$$

The entropy term is $O({\mechanical{N}})$; the last term is $O(\ln {\mechanical{N}})$. Thus, at fixed temperature:

$$
{\energy{F}}={\energy{E}}^*-{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{mc}}({\energy{E}}^*)+O({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\ln {\mechanical{N}}),
$$

$$
\frac {\energy{F}}{\mechanical{N}}=\frac{{\energy{E}}^*-{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{mc}}({\energy{E}}^*)}{{\mechanical{N}}}
+O\left(\frac{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\ln {\mechanical{N}}}{{\mechanical{N}}}\right).
$$

The correction per particle tends to zero. $\square$

> [!note] Remark 4.3 — Size of the correction
> The source gives an $O(\ln {\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{N}})$ correction. This describes an intensive or per-particle thermodynamic correction, not equality of the two probability distributions. It also writes logarithms of a dimensional density of states and a dimensional Gaussian width. The reference energy above makes their combined logarithm well defined.
>
> The source labels the local Taylor remainder $O(1/{\mechanical{N}})$. On the natural fluctuation scale ${\energy{E}}-{\energy{E}}^*=O(\sqrt {\mechanical{N}})$, a generic cubic term in the exponent is instead $O({\mechanical{N}}^{-1/2})$. The quadratic approximation is sufficient for the leading result used here.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\energy{F}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | The equilibrium potential at fixed temperature, volume, and particle count. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | The thermodynamic or Gibbs entropy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The volume available to the particles. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | 1 | Particle or subsystem count | The number of particles, localized spins, or independent oscillators. |
| ${\thermal{C}_V}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity at constant volume | The temperature derivative of internal energy at fixed volume and particle count. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
| $g(E)$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | The number of states per energy interval. |
