> [!TLDR]
> Quantization changes the low-temperature oscillator energy and heat capacity.

The same confining potential can give different thermal behavior depending on whether its energy spectrum is treated as continuous or discrete. Hold the oscillator frequency fixed and couple the oscillator weakly to a reservoir. At high temperature many quantum levels contribute, so classical equipartition is a plausible limit. At low temperature, the excitation gap should suppress heat absorption.

Read the classical Gaussian integral first, then compare it with the quantum geometric sum. Distinguish a nonzero ground-state energy from heat capacity: a temperature-independent energy offset stores no additional heat when temperature changes. The comparison tests the spectrum assumption rather than a change of ensemble.
