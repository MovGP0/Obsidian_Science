> [!TLDR]
> A grand canonical quantum gas exchanges energy and particles with a reservoir at fixed temperature and chemical potential.

The natural ensemble for an ideal quantum gas is the grand canonical ensemble. A reservoir fixes ${\thermal{T}}$ and ${\energy{\mu}}$, while the system exchanges energy and particles with it.

$$
\Xi=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}(\hat H-{\energy{\mu}}\hat N)}\right],
\qquad {\thermal{\beta}}=\frac1{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}},\qquad \hat N=\sum_\alpha\hat n_\alpha.
$$

The exponential gives unnormalized statistical weights. Divide it by the trace to obtain a density operator with trace one. This normalization is what makes the mean of the identity equal to one. The exponent is dimensionless: inverse temperature multiplies an energy difference.

The reservoir fixes the chemical potential, not an exact particle number. For a prescribed mean number, solve the number equation for the chemical potential at the chosen temperature and volume. For an ideal Bose gas, this solution must also satisfy the convergence bound set by the lowest single-particle energy.

The trace is over the appropriate bosonic or fermionic Fock space. See [[05.03.01 Definition and Properties]] for the grand partition function in the preceding chapter.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
