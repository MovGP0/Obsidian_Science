> [!TLDR]
> Differentiation of the bosonic geometric series gives the Bose–Einstein occupation.

Insert Eq. (6.3) into Eq. (6.4):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle {\mechanical{n}}_\alpha\rangle_{\mathrm{BE}}
&={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}\ln\!\left[\frac1{1-e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}}\right]\\
&=-{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}\ln\!\left[1-e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}\right]\\
&=-{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\frac{-{\thermal{\beta}} e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}}{1-e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}}\\
&=\frac{e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}}{1-e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiply numerator and denominator by $e^{{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}$:

$$
\langle {\mechanical{n}}_\alpha\rangle_{\mathrm{BE}}
=\frac1{e^{{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}-1}
\equiv {\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha).
$$

Two minus signs in the derivative cancel: one comes from the negative logarithm, the other from differentiating its denominator. The occupation must be positive in the convergent domain. A negative result would indicate either a sign error or a chemical potential outside that domain.

As a direct sum check, when the geometric ratio in Eq. (6.3) is one half, the mean is one. This agrees with summing particle number times its normalized geometric probability. In the small-ratio limit, the denominator correction is negligible and the mean reduces to the Boltzmann weight.

This is the **Bose–Einstein distribution**.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
