> [!TLDR]
> Periodic boundary conditions give a regular wave-vector grid for free-particle states.

For a macroscopic gas, many closely spaced single-particle levels contribute. We can then approximate sums by integrals.

Consider free particles in a box of side length ${\mechanical{L}}$ in $d$ spatial dimensions, with spatial measure ${\mechanical{L}}^d$. Periodic boundary conditions give plane-wave states

$$
{\signal{\phi}}_{\mathbf k}({\mechanical{\mathbf r}})=\frac1{\sqrt{{\mechanical{L}}^d}}e^{i{\signal{\mathbf k}}\cdot{\mechanical{\mathbf r}}},
\qquad {\signal{k}}_i=\frac{2\pi}{{\mechanical{L}}}\ell_i,\qquad \ell_i\in\mathbb Z,\quad i=1,\ldots,d.
$$

The single-particle energy is

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}_{\mathbf k}=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2{\signal{k}}^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}}.
$$

The integer labels have been written as $\ell_i$ to avoid confusion with occupations. In three dimensions, ${\mechanical{L}}^d={\mechanical{V}}$ and the normalization is $1/\sqrt {\mechanical{V}}$. The symbol $d$ in this article is the number of dimensions, not a physical distance.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{L}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length | Length or side length of the box. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\signal{\phi}}_\alpha$ | — | Single-particle wavefunction | Normalized orbital including spin when required. |
| ${\mechanical{\mathbf r}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | Position of a particle. |
| ${\signal{k}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | Magnitude or component of a wave vector. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to $h/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | Mass of one particle. |
