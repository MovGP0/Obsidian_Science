> [!TLDR]
> A Sommerfeld expansion at fixed particle number gives a heat capacity proportional to temperature.

## Question — Exercise 6.4

For a degenerate Fermi gas at ${\thermal{T}}\ll {\thermal{T}}_F$, use the Sommerfeld expansion to show

$$
{\thermal{C}}_V\simeq\frac{\pi^2}{2}{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B\frac {\thermal{T}}{{\thermal{T}}_F}.
$$

Compare the linear temperature dependence with the classical value ${\thermal{C}}_V=3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B/2$.

## Worked calculation

This derivation is added to the supplied question. Hold ${\mechanical{N}},{\mechanical{V}}$ fixed for the three-dimensional noninteracting, nonrelativistic gas.

### 1. Use the Sommerfeld expansion

For a smooth function $F({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ and ${\energy{\mu}}\gg {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$,

$$
\int_0^\infty F({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
=\int_0^{\energy{\mu}} F({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
+\frac{\pi^2}{6}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2F'({\energy{\mu}})+O(({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^4).
$$

Only energies near ${\energy{\mu}}$ receive a thermal correction. We must first find how ${\energy{\mu}}$ changes with ${\thermal{T}}$ to preserve the particle number.

### 2. Keep the particle number fixed

For ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}$,

$$
{\mechanical{N}}=A_{3D}\left[\frac23{\energy{\mu}}^{3/2}
+\frac{\pi^2}{12}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2{\energy{\mu}}^{-1/2}+\cdots\right].
$$

At zero temperature, ${\mechanical{N}}=(2/3)A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{3/2}$. Set ${\energy{\mu}}={\energy{\varepsilon}}_F+\delta{\energy{\mu}}$ and retain terms of order ${\thermal{T}}^2$:

$$
0=A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{1/2}\delta{\energy{\mu}}
+\frac{\pi^2}{12}A_{3D}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{-1/2}.
$$

Hence

$$
{\energy{\mu}}({\thermal{T}})={\energy{\varepsilon}}_F\left[1-\frac{\pi^2}{12}\left(\frac {\thermal{T}}{{\thermal{T}}_F}\right)^2+\cdots\right],
\qquad {\thermal{T}}_F=\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}{{\thermal{k}}_B}.
$$

### 3. Calculate the energy to the same order

Apply the expansion with $F({\energy{\varepsilon}})={\energy{\varepsilon}} {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$:

$$
{\energy{U}}=A_{3D}\left[\frac25{\energy{\mu}}^{5/2}
+\frac{\pi^2}{4}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2{\energy{\mu}}^{1/2}+\cdots\right].
$$

Insert $\delta{\energy{\mu}}$ from the previous step:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\energy{U}}&={\energy{U}}_0+A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{3/2}\delta{\energy{\mu}}
+\frac{\pi^2}{4}A_{3D}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{1/2}+\cdots\\
&={\energy{U}}_0+\frac{\pi^2}{6}A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{1/2}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2+\cdots\\
&=\frac35{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F+\frac{\pi^2}{4}{\mechanical{N}}\frac{({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2}{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}+\cdots.
\end{aligned}
$$

The correction to ${\energy{\mu}}$ is essential. Holding ${\energy{\mu}}$ fixed would give a different derivative and would not calculate ${\thermal{C}}_V$ at fixed ${\mechanical{N}}$.

## Solution and interpretation

Differentiate with respect to temperature:

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{C}}_V=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{U}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{V,N}
\simeq\frac{\pi^2}{2}{\mechanical{N}}\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B^2{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}
=\frac{\pi^2}{2}{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B\frac {\thermal{T}}{{\thermal{T}}_F}.}
$$

Only a fraction of order ${\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F$ of the fermions, near the Fermi surface, can respond thermally. This suppresses heat capacity below the classical result. The expression tends to zero linearly and applies only for ${\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F\ll1$.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{C}}_V$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity | Temperature derivative of energy at fixed volume and particle number. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |

| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
