> [!TLDR]
> For the same temperature and chemical potential above μ, bosonic occupation exceeds classical occupation, which exceeds fermionic occupation.

## Question — Exercise 6.6

Let ${\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}$ and ${\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}$ be the mean occupations of the same energy ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ at the same ${\energy{\mu}},{\thermal{T}}$. Show that for every ${\energy{\varepsilon}}>{\energy{\mu}}$,

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})>{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{MB}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})>{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}).
$$

Give a physical interpretation.

## Worked calculation

This derivation is added to the supplied question. Let $y=e^{{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}$. Since ${\thermal{T}}>0$ and ${\energy{\varepsilon}}>{\energy{\mu}}$, $y>1$.

### 1. Compare positive denominators

$$
0<y-1<y<y+1.
$$

Taking reciprocals reverses the inequalities because all denominators are positive:

$$
\frac1{y-1}>\frac1y>\frac1{y+1}.
$$

### 2. Identify the distributions

The three fractions are precisely the Bose–Einstein, Maxwell–Boltzmann, and Fermi–Dirac occupations. Thus

$$
\boxed{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})>{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{MB}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})>{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}).}
$$

As $y\to\infty$, both ratios to the classical occupation tend to one:

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}}{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{MB}}}=\frac y{y-1}\to1,
\qquad
\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}}{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{MB}}}=\frac y{y+1}\to1.
$$

## Physical interpretation

At fixed ${\energy{\mu}},{\thermal{T}}$, bosonic exchange permits enhanced occupation of the same state. Fermionic exclusion reduces the occupation relative to the classical expression. At low occupation, both effects disappear.

The comparison holds at the **same chemical potential**, not necessarily at the same total density. If density is fixed instead, each gas generally needs a different chemical potential. For the Bose gas, the chemical potential must also satisfy its ground-state bound.
