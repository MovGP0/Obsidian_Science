> [!TLDR]
> Counting two photon polarizations and applying Bose–Einstein occupation gives the Planck spectrum.

## Question — Exercise 6.5

Photons are spin-1 bosons with two physical polarizations, ${\mechanical{g}}_s=2$, and chemical potential ${\energy{\mu}}=0$. Use the electromagnetic density of states and Bose–Einstein statistics to show

$$
{\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}})=\frac{8\pi {\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}^3}{{\signal{c}}^3}\frac1{e^{{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1}.
$$

## Worked calculation

This derivation is added to the supplied question. The symbol ${\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}})$ is energy per volume and per frequency interval.

### 1. Count electromagnetic modes

For periodic boundary conditions in a large volume, a shell of wave vectors contains

$$
d\mathcal N=2\frac {\mechanical{V}}{(2\pi)^3}\,4\pi {\signal{k}}^2d{\signal{k}}.
$$

The first factor two counts transverse polarizations. With ${\signal{k}}=2\pi{\signal{\nu}}/{\signal{c}}$ and $d{\signal{k}}=(2\pi/{\signal{c}})d{\signal{\nu}}$,

$$
\frac{d\mathcal N}{{\mechanical{V}}}=\frac{8\pi{\signal{\nu}}^2}{{\signal{c}}^3}\,d{\signal{\nu}}.
$$

Thus the electromagnetic mode density per volume and frequency is $8\pi{\signal{\nu}}^2/{\signal{c}}^3$. The nonrelativistic square-root density from Eq. (6.7) does not apply to photons.

### 2. Calculate the mean energy of one mode

Photon number is not conserved in thermal equilibrium with matter, so ${\energy{\mu}}=0$. For energy ${\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}$,

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}({\signal{\nu}})=\frac1{e^{{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1},
\qquad \langle{\energy{\varepsilon}}\rangle={\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}\,{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}({\signal{\nu}}).
$$

### 3. Multiply mode density by mean energy

$$
{\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}})d{\signal{\nu}}=\frac{d\mathcal N}{{\mechanical{V}}}\,{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}\,{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}({\signal{\nu}}).
$$

## Solution

$$
\boxed{{\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}})=\frac{8\pi {\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}^3}{{\signal{c}}^3}\frac1{e^{{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1}.}
$$

The units are $\unit{J\,m^{-3}\,Hz^{-1}}$. Integrating over positive frequency gives the total thermal radiation energy density. This expression excludes the zero-point vacuum term.
