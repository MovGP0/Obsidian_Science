> [!TLDR]
> Integration by parts gives PV = 2U/3 for both nonrelativistic ideal quantum gases in three dimensions.

## Question — Exercise 6.1

Show that a three-dimensional ideal quantum gas, with either Fermi–Dirac or Bose–Einstein statistics, satisfies

$$
{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}=\frac23{\energy{U}}.
$$

**Source hint:** Use Eq. (6.7), with ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\propto{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}$. Integrate by parts and compare pressure with internal energy.

## Worked calculation

This derivation is added to the supplied question. It assumes a nonrelativistic quadratic dispersion, ground energy zero, and the thermodynamic continuum limit.

### 1. Put both statistics into the same notation

Let $\chi=+1$ for fermions and $\chi=-1$ for bosons. This is a mathematical sign, not a physical quantity. Define

$$
q({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\ln\Xi_\alpha({\energy{\varepsilon}})
=\chi\ln\!\left[1+\chi e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}\right].
$$

Differentiate with respect to energy:

$$
\frac{dq}{d{\energy{\varepsilon}}}
=-\frac{{\thermal{\beta}} e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}}{1+\chi e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}}
=-{\thermal{\beta}} {\mechanical{n}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}).
$$

This derivative converts the logarithm in the pressure into the occupation that appears in the energy integral.

### 2. Write pressure and energy with the same density of states

Set ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}$. Here $g$ is the total density of states, so

$$
{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}} A_{3D}\int_0^\infty{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}q({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}},
\qquad
{\energy{U}}=A_{3D}\int_0^\infty{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}{\mechanical{n}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.
$$

### 3. Integrate the pressure by parts

Choose $u=q({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ and $dv={\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}d{\energy{\varepsilon}}$, so $v=2{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}/3$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}&={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}} A_{3D}\left[\frac23{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}q({\energy{\varepsilon}})\right]_0^\infty
-\frac23{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}} A_{3D}\int_0^\infty{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}q'({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}\\
&=\frac23{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{\beta}} A_{3D}\int_0^\infty{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}{\mechanical{n}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The upper boundary vanishes because occupations decay exponentially. At the lower boundary, the factor ${\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}$ dominates even the logarithmic Bose singularity at ${\energy{\mu}}=0$. Since ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}{\thermal{\beta}}=1$, the integral is $2{\energy{U}}/3$.

## Solution

$$
\boxed{{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}=\frac23{\energy{U}}.}
$$

The relation holds for both statistics. A zero-energy Bose condensate adds neither bulk kinetic energy nor bulk pressure, so it does not change this relation. Interactions, a different dispersion, or a shifted reference energy require a revised relation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Total energy of the gas. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | Total number of states per energy interval, including internal degeneracy. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
