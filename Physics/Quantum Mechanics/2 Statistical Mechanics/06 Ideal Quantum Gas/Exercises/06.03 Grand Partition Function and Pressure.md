> [!TLDR]
> Taking the logarithm changes the product of state partition functions into an energy integral.

## Question — Exercise 6.3

Calculate $\ln\Xi$ explicitly for an ideal Bose gas and an ideal Fermi gas as an integral over the density of states. Derive the pressure as a function of ${\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}},{\energy{\mu}}$.

## Worked calculation

This derivation is added to the supplied question. Use a three-dimensional nonrelativistic homogeneous gas, total density of states $g$, and single-particle ground energy zero.

### 1. Take the logarithm of the factorized trace

The logarithm changes a product into a sum. For fermions,

$$
\ln\Xi_{\mathrm{FD}}=\sum_\alpha\ln\!\left[1+e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}\right]
\simeq\int_0^\infty {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\ln\!\left[1+{\mechanical{z}} e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}\right]d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.
$$

For bosons,

$$
\ln\Xi_{\mathrm{BE}}=-\sum_\alpha\ln\!\left[1-e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}\right]
\simeq-\int_0^\infty {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\ln\!\left[1-{\mechanical{z}} e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}\right]d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.
$$

Here ${\mechanical{z}}=e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}}$, and $0<{\mechanical{z}}<1$ for the finite Bose system with ground energy zero.

### 2. Reduce to dimensionless integrals

Set $x={\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}$ and ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=A_{3D}\sqrt{\energy{\varepsilon}}$. Integration by parts gives

$$
\ln\Xi_{\mathrm{FD}}=\frac23 A_{3D}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^{3/2}
\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{3/2}}{{\mechanical{z}}^{-1}e^x+1}\,dx,
$$

$$
\ln\Xi_{\mathrm{BE}}=\frac23 A_{3D}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^{3/2}
\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{3/2}}{{\mechanical{z}}^{-1}e^x-1}\,dx.
$$

Boundary terms vanish as explained in [[06.01 Pressure and Energy of a Quantum Gas]]. With $\Gamma(5/2)=3\sqrt\pi/4$ and Eq. (6.7), the prefactor becomes ${\mechanical{g}}_s{\mechanical{V}}/{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3$.

### 3. Obtain the pressure

For a homogeneous bulk system, ${\mechanical{P}}={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\ln\Xi/{\mechanical{V}}$. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\ln\Xi=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s{\mechanical{V}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}
\begin{cases}f_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}),&\mathrm{FD},\\g_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}),&\mathrm{BE},\end{cases}}
$$

$$
\boxed{{\mechanical{P}}({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{V}},{\energy{\mu}})=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}
\begin{cases}f_{5/2}(e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}}),&\mathrm{FD},\\g_{5/2}(e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}}),&\mathrm{BE}.\end{cases}}
$$

At fixed ${\thermal{T}}$ and ${\energy{\mu}}$, the bulk pressure is independent of ${\mechanical{V}}$. This is consistent with $\ln\Xi$ being extensive.

## Bose ground-state qualification

At finite volume, separate the discrete ground state before the continuum approximation:

$$
\ln\Xi_{\mathrm{BE}}=-g_0\ln(1-{\mechanical{z}})+\ln\Xi_{\mathrm{ex}},
\qquad {\mechanical{N}}_0=\frac{g_0{\mechanical{z}}}{1-{\mechanical{z}}}.
$$

Here $g_0$ is the number of ground modes. In the condensed thermodynamic limit, its pressure contribution per volume vanishes. The excited-state integral determines the bulk pressure, while ${\mechanical{N}}_0$ accounts for the condensate particles.
