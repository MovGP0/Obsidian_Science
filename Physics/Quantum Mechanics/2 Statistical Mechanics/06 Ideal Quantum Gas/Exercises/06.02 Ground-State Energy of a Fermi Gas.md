> [!TLDR]
> Filling the three-dimensional density of states to the Fermi energy gives U₀ = 3NεF/5.

## Question — Exercise 6.2

Calculate the total internal energy of a Fermi gas at zero temperature:

$$
{\energy{U}}_0=\int_0^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}{\energy{\varepsilon}} {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
=\frac35{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F.
$$

**Source hint:** Insert the density of states from Eq. (6.7) and integrate analytically.

## Worked calculation

This derivation is added to the supplied question.

### 1. Count occupied states

At zero temperature, the occupation is one below ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F$ and zero above it. Write ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=A_{3D}\sqrt{\energy{\varepsilon}}$:

$$
{\mechanical{N}}=A_{3D}\int_0^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
=\frac23A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{3/2}.
$$

This relation lets us remove the coefficient $A_{3D}$ from the energy calculation.

### 2. Weight each occupied state by its energy

$$
{\energy{U}}_0=A_{3D}\int_0^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
=\frac25A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{5/2}.
$$

### 3. Divide by the particle number

$$
\frac{{\energy{U}}_0}{{\mechanical{N}}}
=\frac{(2/5)A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{5/2}}{(2/3)A_{3D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{3/2}}
=\frac35{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F.
$$

## Solution

$$
\boxed{{\energy{U}}_0=\frac35{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F.}
$$

The average is below the highest occupied energy because lower states are also occupied. Combining this result with [[06.01 Pressure and Energy of a Quantum Gas]] gives ${\mechanical{P}}_0=2{\mechanical{n}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F/5$, as in Eq. (6.10).
