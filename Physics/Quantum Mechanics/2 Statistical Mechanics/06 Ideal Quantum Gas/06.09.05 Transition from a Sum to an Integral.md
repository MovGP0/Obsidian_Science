> [!TLDR]
> Energy integrals replace sums when the density of levels is sufficiently high; total and per-volume densities must be kept distinct.

Use the continuum substitution

$$
\sum_\alpha f({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha)\longrightarrow\int_0^\infty f({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.\tag{6.8}
$$

The approximation is useful when ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$ is much larger than the relevant level spacing and the summand is sufficiently smooth. For a macroscopic gas this condition is usually well satisfied. Isolate a macroscopically occupied discrete ground state before applying it to a Bose gas near condensation.

With total density of states $g$ and $\mathcal D=g/{\mechanical{V}}$,

$$
\frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}=\frac1{\mechanical{V}}\int_0^\infty {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD},\mathrm{BE},\mathrm{MB}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
=\int_0^\infty\mathcal D({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD},\mathrm{BE},\mathrm{MB}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.
$$

The homogeneous grand potential is ${\energy{\Phi}}=-{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\ln\Xi=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}$. Therefore

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}\ln\Xi
=\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}\sum_\alpha\ln\Xi_\alpha
\longrightarrow\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}\int_0^\infty {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\ln\Xi_\alpha({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.\tag{6.9}
$$

The embedded **Exercise 6.1** from the source is collected with its derivation in [[06.01 Pressure and Energy of a Quantum Gas]].

> [!note] Source correction
> The source particle-density equation omits $1/{\mechanical{V}}$ while using the total density of states from Eq. (6.7). The equations here include the factor explicitly. Its pressure equation already has the correct factor.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | Total number of states per energy interval, including internal degeneracy. |
| $\mathcal D({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,m^{-3}}$ | Density of states per volume | Total density of states divided by the volume. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\energy{\Phi}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Grand potential | Equal to $-{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\ln\Xi$. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
