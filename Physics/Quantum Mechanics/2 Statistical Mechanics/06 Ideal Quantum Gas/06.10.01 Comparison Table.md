> [!TLDR]
> Quantum statistics differs through exchange symmetry, occupation limits, and the allowed chemical potential.

**Table 6.1 — Comparison of Fermi–Dirac, Bose–Einstein, and Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics.**

| Property | Fermi–Dirac | Bose–Einstein | Maxwell–Boltzmann |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Particle type | Fermions | Bosons | Classical dilute limit |
| Spin in the usual 3D theory | Half-integer | Integer | Either type in its classical limit |
| Wavefunction | Antisymmetric | Symmetric | Exchange effects neglected in the approximation |
| Mean occupation | $[e^{{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}+1]^{-1}$ | $[e^{{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}-1]^{-1}$ | $e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}$ |
| Mathematical occupation range | $[0,1]$ | $[0,+\infty)$ | $(0,+\infty)$ |
| Chemical-potential condition at ${\thermal{T}}>0$ | No bosonic upper bound | ${\energy{\mu}}<{\energy{\varepsilon}}_0$ | Small occupation; ${\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})\gg1$ in the relevant states |
| Pauli exclusion | Yes | No | No separate occupation cap |
| Limit ${\mechanical{\eta}}\to0$ | Maxwell–Boltzmann | Maxwell–Boltzmann | Already classical |
| Physical examples | Electrons in metals | Photons; ⁴He as a composite boson | Dilute gases |

> [!note] Scope of the comparison
> The unbounded Maxwell–Boltzmann expression is physically useful here only at small occupation. The source condition “${\energy{\mu}}\ll0$” assumes a particular energy zero; ${\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_0-{\energy{\mu}})\gg1$ is the corresponding energy-offset-independent condition. Photons have zero chemical potential and two physical polarizations; the zero-frequency limit is handled separately. Liquid ⁴He has significant interactions, so it is an example of bosonic matter rather than a quantitatively ideal gas.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/(k_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\mechanical{\eta}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Degeneracy parameter | Equal to ${\mechanical{n}}\lambda_T^3$ in the stated convention. |
