> [!TLDR]
> Counting wave-vector states gives the density of states used to replace quantum sums by energy integrals.

The free-particle energy is quadratic in the wave vector. Its density of states depends on spatial dimension.

Occupation statistics tells us how many particles occupy a state; the density of states tells us how many states are available. The latter is a property of the single-particle spectrum and the boundary conditions, so the same free-particle density can be used for bosons and fermions with the appropriate internal-state count.

Follow the geometric counting in each dimension before applying the energy integrals. A useful comparison is that increasing energy opens a spherical volume in three dimensions, a disk in two, and a pair of intervals in one. Differentiating these counts explains the different energy powers. The continuum formulas describe a large box, not its exact lowest discrete levels.
