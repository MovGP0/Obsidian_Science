> [!TLDR]
> A fermionic state has two contributions to its grand partition function: empty and occupied.

Pauli exclusion gives ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha\in\{0,1\}$ for one complete single-particle state. Thus

$$
\Xi_\alpha^{\mathrm{FD}}=\sum_{n_\alpha=0}^{1}e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}}){\mechanical{n}}_\alpha}
=1+e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}.\tag{6.2}
$$

The first term is the empty state. The second is the occupied state. The full result is $\Xi^{\mathrm{FD}}=\prod_\alpha\Xi_\alpha^{\mathrm{FD}}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/(k_BT)$. |
