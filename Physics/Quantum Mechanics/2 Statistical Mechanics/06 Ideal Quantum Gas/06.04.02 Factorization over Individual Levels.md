> [!TLDR]
> Commuting number operators make different states statistically independent in the grand canonical ideal gas.

For an ideal gas,

$$
\hat H=\sum_\alpha{\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha\hat n_\alpha,
\qquad
e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}(\hat H-{\energy{\mu}}\hat N)}
=e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}\sum_\alpha({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})\hat n_\alpha}
=\prod_\alpha e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})\hat n_\alpha}.
$$

The number operators for different states commute. The trace therefore factorizes:

$$
\Xi=\prod_\alpha\Xi_\alpha,
\qquad \Xi_\alpha=\sum_{n_\alpha}e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}}){\mechanical{n}}_\alpha}.
$$

Sum over ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha=0,1$ for fermions and ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha=0,1,2,\ldots$ for bosons.

Commuting operators permit the exponential product, but the trace product also needs independent occupation ranges. For two fermionic modes the grand trace includes all four lists $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(1,1)$; multiplying the two one-mode sums generates exactly these terms. At fixed particle number one, only the middle two lists remain, so the unrestricted product would give the wrong ensemble.

Here each level label denotes one complete state. Degenerate states contribute separate identical factors if their energies are equal.

> [!note] Remark 6.2
> This factorization is the main calculation step. It is exact for the ideal gas because the single-particle states are statistically independent in the grand canonical ensemble. Interactions generally prevent it. A fixed total particle number also couples occupations in the canonical ensemble.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/(k_BT)$. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
