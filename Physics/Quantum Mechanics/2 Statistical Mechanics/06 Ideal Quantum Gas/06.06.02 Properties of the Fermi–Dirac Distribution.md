> [!TLDR]
> The chemical potential marks half occupation, and the Fermi distribution becomes a filled Fermi sea as temperature approaches zero.

## Range and Fermi level

For ${\thermal{T}}>0$, $0<{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})<1$. The limiting values zero and one are reached in the appropriate limits. This agrees with Pauli exclusion.

The chemical potential ${\energy{\mu}}$ is also called the **Fermi level**. Its zero-temperature value defines the Fermi energy ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F={\energy{\mu}}(0)$. In general, ${\energy{\mu}}({\thermal{T}})$ differs from ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F$ at nonzero temperature.

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}({\energy{\mu}})=\frac1{e^0+1}=\frac12,\qquad {\thermal{T}}>0.
$$

Thus ${\energy{\mu}}$ is the energy of half occupation.

## Zero-temperature limit

$$
\lim_{T\to0}{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})
=\Theta({\energy{\mu}}-{\energy{\varepsilon}})=
\begin{cases}1,&{\energy{\varepsilon}}<{\energy{\mu}},\\0,&{\energy{\varepsilon}}>{\energy{\mu}}.\end{cases}
$$

States below ${\energy{\mu}}$ are full; states above it are empty. This is the ground-state filling. The formula leaves the single boundary energy unspecified; the limit at fixed ${\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{\mu}}$ is $1/2$.

## Probability interpretation and derivative

For noninteracting fermions, ${\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ is also the probability that the state is occupied.

$$
\frac{d{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}}{d{\energy{\varepsilon}}}
=-\frac{{\thermal{\beta}} e^{{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}}{(e^{{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}+1)^2}
=-{\thermal{\beta}} {\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}(1-{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD}}).
$$

The derivative is negative. Its magnitude is largest at ${\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{\mu}}$, and its energy width is of order ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$. As ${\thermal{T}}\to0$, it tends to $-\delta({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})$ in the distributional sense.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
