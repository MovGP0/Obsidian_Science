> [!TLDR]
> Differentiation of the logarithm of the state partition function gives its mean occupation.

At fixed ${\thermal{T}}$, the mean occupation of state $\alpha$ is

$$
\langle {\mechanical{n}}_\alpha\rangle=\frac1{\thermal{\beta}}\frac{\partial\ln\Xi_\alpha}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}
={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\frac{\partial\ln\Xi_\alpha}{\partial{\energy{\mu}}}.\tag{6.4}
$$

The derivative brings down ${\thermal{\beta}} {\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ from each exponential in the state sum. Division by ${\thermal{\beta}}\Xi_\alpha$ produces the probability-weighted mean.

Substitute Eq. (6.2) for [[06.06.01 Derivation|fermions]] or Eq. (6.3) for [[06.07.01 Derivation|bosons]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
