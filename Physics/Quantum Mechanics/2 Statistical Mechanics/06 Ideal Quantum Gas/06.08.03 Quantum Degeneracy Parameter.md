> [!TLDR]
> The degeneracy parameter compares the thermal quantum volume with the volume per particle.

Compare ${\signal{\lambda}}_T$ with the mean spacing $d=({\mechanical{V}}/{\mechanical{N}})^{1/3}={\mechanical{n}}^{-1/3}$, where ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{V}}$.

**Definition 6.3 — Quantum degeneracy parameter.**

$$
{\mechanical{\eta}}={\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3=\frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}\left(\frac{{\energy{h}}^2}{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}\right)^{3/2}.
$$

- ${\mechanical{\eta}}\ll1$: wave packets have little overlap; the gas is nondegenerate, and Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics is a good approximation.
- ${\mechanical{\eta}}\sim1$: quantum exchange effects become important; the gas is partly degenerate.
- ${\mechanical{\eta}}\gg1$: quantum exchange effects dominate; classical statistics is inadequate.

For one internal state, Eq. (6.6) gives ${\mechanical{z}}=e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}}={\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3={\mechanical{\eta}}\ll1$ in the classical limit. Therefore ${\energy{\mu}}={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\ln{\mechanical{\eta}}$ is negative relative to the chosen single-particle energy zero.

**Example 6.2 — Degeneracy temperature.** Set ${\mechanical{\eta}}({\thermal{T}}_D)=1$:

$$
{\thermal{T}}_D=\frac{{\energy{h}}^2}{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}}_B}\left(\frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}\right)^{2/3}.
$$

For liquid ⁴He, the source specifies ${\mechanical{m}}=4\,\unit{u}$ and ${\mechanical{n}}\simeq2\times10^{28}\,\unit{m^{-3}}$ and reports ${\thermal{T}}_D\simeq5.6\,\unit{K}$. It compares this scale with the superfluid transition near $2.17\,\unit{K}$. Liquid helium is interacting; this comparison is qualitative, rather than an ideal-gas prediction.

For conduction electrons, the source uses ${\mechanical{m}}={\mechanical{m}}_e$, ${\mechanical{n}}\simeq10^{28}\,\unit{m^{-3}}$, and reports ${\thermal{T}}_D\simeq2.6\times10^4\,\unit{K}$. Room temperature is far below this scale, so the electron gas is degenerate.

> [!note] Numerical check and spin convention
> Direct substitution of the stated densities gives approximately ${\thermal{T}}_D=5.61\,\unit{K}$ for ⁴He and $2.58\times10^4\,\unit{K}$ for electrons, in agreement with the source. With ${\mechanical{g}}_s$ internal states, the dilute criterion is more precisely ${\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3/{\mechanical{g}}_s\ll1$, and ${\mechanical{z}}\simeq {\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3/{\mechanical{g}}_s$. The definition of ${\mechanical{\eta}}$ above retains the source convention.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{\eta}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Degeneracy parameter | Equal to ${\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3$ in the stated convention. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal de Broglie wavelength | Thermal quantum length for a particle of mass m. |
| $d$ | $\unit{m}$ | Mean particle spacing | Cube root of the volume per particle in three dimensions. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | Mass of one particle. |
| ${\energy{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | Relates frequency to quantum energy. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_D$ | $\unit{K}$ | Degeneracy temperature | Temperature at which the specified degeneracy parameter is one. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\mechanical{g}}_s$ | $\unit{1}$ | Spin degeneracy | Number of internal spin states at a given orbital state. |
| ${\mechanical{z}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Fugacity | Equal to $e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}}$. |
