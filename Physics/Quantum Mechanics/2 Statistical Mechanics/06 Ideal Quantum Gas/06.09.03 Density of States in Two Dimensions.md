> [!TLDR]
> A quadratic free-particle dispersion gives a constant density of states in two dimensions.

For a system of area ${\mechanical{A}}={\mechanical{L}}^2$, count states in circular wave-vector shells:

$$
{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})d{\energy{\varepsilon}}={\mechanical{g}}_s\frac {\mechanical{A}}{(2\pi)^2}\,2\pi {\signal{k}}\,d{\signal{k}}
=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s{\mechanical{A}}}{2\pi}{\signal{k}}\,d{\signal{k}}.
$$

Since ${\signal{k}}\,d{\signal{k}}=({\mechanical{m}}/{\energy{\hbar}}^2)d{\energy{\varepsilon}}$,

$$
{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s {\mechanical{m}} {\mechanical{A}}}{2\pi{\energy{\hbar}}^2}.
$$

The total density is independent of energy for ${\energy{\varepsilon}}>0$ in the continuum approximation. The per-area density is ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})/{\mechanical{A}}$.

The shell circumference grows in proportion to wave-vector magnitude, while the energy-to-wave-vector Jacobian decreases by the same factor. Their product is constant. Thus two equal positive energy intervals contain the same number of states in this continuum model, even if one lies at a higher energy.

This statement requires quadratic in-plane dispersion and the stated internal degeneracy. A change in dispersion or the opening of another transverse subband changes the counting. At the exact lowest level of a finite sample, the discrete state must be distinguished from the constant continuum density. As a units check, mass times area divided by squared action has units of inverse energy.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | Total number of states per energy interval, including internal degeneracy. |
| ${\mechanical{g}}_s$ | $\unit{1}$ | Spin degeneracy | Number of internal spin states at a given orbital state. |
| ${\mechanical{A}}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Area | Area of a two-dimensional system. |
| ${\mechanical{L}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length | Length or side length of the box. |
| ${\signal{k}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | Magnitude or component of a wave vector. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | Mass of one particle. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to $h/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
