> [!TLDR]
> Fock space is the direct sum of all allowed particle-number sectors, including the vacuum.

For a system with variable particle number, such as a grand canonical system, introduce **Fock space**:

$$
\mathcal F=\bigoplus_{N=0}^{\infty}\mathcal H^{({\mechanical{N}})}.
$$

The space $\mathcal H^{(0)}=\mathbb C$ contains the vacuum $|0\rangle$, with no particles. The ${\mechanical{N}}$-particle sector $\mathcal H^{({\mechanical{N}})}$ is the symmetric subspace of $(\mathcal H^{(1)})^{\otimes {\mechanical{N}}}$ for bosons and the antisymmetric subspace for fermions.

The occupation-number basis vectors are

$$
|{\mechanical{n}}_1,{\mechanical{n}}_2,{\mechanical{n}}_3,\ldots\rangle.
$$

Bosonic occupations are nonnegative integers; fermionic occupations are zero or one. General Fock-space states are linear combinations of these vectors, subject to any physical superselection rules.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
