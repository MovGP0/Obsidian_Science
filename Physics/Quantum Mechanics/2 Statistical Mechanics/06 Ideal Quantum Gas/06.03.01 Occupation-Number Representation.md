> [!TLDR]
> A number-state basis lists occupations, with no upper bound for bosons and at most one fermion per complete single-particle state.

For identical particles, specify **how many particles** occupy each state. We do not specify which labelled particle occupies it.

Let $\{{\signal{\phi}}_\alpha\}_{\alpha=1,2,\ldots}$ be a complete orthonormal basis of the single-particle Hilbert space. An occupation-number basis state has occupations

$$
\{{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha\}_{\alpha=1,2,\ldots},\qquad \sum_\alpha {\mechanical{n}}_\alpha={\mechanical{N}}.
$$

For bosons, ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$. For fermions, ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha\in\{0,1\}$ by the Pauli principle. A state includes its spin quantum numbers: two fermions can occupy the same spatial orbital if their spin states differ.

> [!note] Source clarification
> An occupation list specifies a number-basis vector. A general pure state can be a superposition of such vectors, and a statistical state requires a density operator. The source statement that a state is fully specified by an occupation list applies to the basis states used in the partition sum.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\signal{\phi}}_\alpha$ | — | Single-particle wavefunction | Normalized orbital including spin when required. |
