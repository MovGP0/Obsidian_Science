> [!TLDR]
> A bosonic state gives a geometric series that converges only when the chemical potential is below its energy.

For one bosonic state, ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha=0,1,2,\ldots$. The geometric series converges if $e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}<1$. For ${\thermal{T}}>0$, this means ${\energy{\mu}}<{\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha$:

$$
\Xi_\alpha^{\mathrm{BE}}=\sum_{n_\alpha=0}^{\infty}e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}}){\mechanical{n}}_\alpha}
=\frac1{1-e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}}.\tag{6.3}
$$

A compact form for both statistics is

$$
\Xi_\alpha=\left[1\mp e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha-{\energy{\mu}})}\right]^{\mp1}.
$$

The upper signs give bosons: minus inside, exponent $-1$. The lower signs give fermions: plus inside, exponent $+1$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/(k_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
