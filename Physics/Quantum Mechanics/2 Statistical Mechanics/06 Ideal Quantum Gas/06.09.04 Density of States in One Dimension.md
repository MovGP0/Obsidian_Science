> [!TLDR]
> In one dimension, the free-particle density of states diverges as the inverse square root of energy.

For a system of length ${\mechanical{L}}$, include both directions $+{\signal{k}}$ and $-{\signal{k}}$:

$$
{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})d{\energy{\varepsilon}}={\mechanical{g}}_s\frac {\mechanical{L}}{2\pi}\,2\,d{\signal{k}}
=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s{\mechanical{L}}}\pi\,d{\signal{k}}.
$$

Using $d{\signal{k}}={\energy{\hbar}}^{-1}\sqrt{{\mechanical{m}}/(2{\energy{\varepsilon}})}\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}$ gives

$$
{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s{\mechanical{L}}}{\pi{\energy{\hbar}}}\sqrt{\frac {\mechanical{m}}{2{\energy{\varepsilon}}}}
\equiv B_{1D}{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{-1/2}.
$$

Thus ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ diverges as ${\energy{\varepsilon}}\to0^+$, although its integral over a finite low-energy interval remains finite.

> [!note] Remark 6.4 — Dimension dependence
> For the quadratic dispersion ${\energy{\varepsilon}}\propto {\signal{k}}^2$,
>
> $$
> {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\propto{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{d/2-1}
> =\begin{cases}{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{-1/2},&d=1,\\{\energy{\varepsilon}}^0,&d=2,\\{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2},&d=3.\end{cases}
> $$
>
> For a uniform Bose gas at the condensation limit, the excited-state integrand behaves as ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{BE}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\propto{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{d/2-2}$. It is integrable at zero energy only for $d>2$. This explains the special role of three dimensions. Here $d$ is a dimension count.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | Total number of states per energy interval, including internal degeneracy. |
| ${\mechanical{g}}_s$ | $\unit{1}$ | Spin degeneracy | Number of internal spin states at a given orbital state. |
| ${\mechanical{L}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length | Length or side length of the box. |
