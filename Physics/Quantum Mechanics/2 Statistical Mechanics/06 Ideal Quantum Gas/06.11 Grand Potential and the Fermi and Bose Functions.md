> [!TLDR]
> Fermi and Bose integrals express the particle density and pressure of a three-dimensional ideal quantum gas.

Define the Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein functions, for order $\ell>0$ in their convergence domains:

$$
f_\ell({\mechanical{z}})=\frac1{\Gamma(\ell)}\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{\ell-1}}{{\mechanical{z}}^{-1}e^x+1}\,dx,
\qquad
g_\ell({\mechanical{z}})=\frac1{\Gamma(\ell)}\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{\ell-1}}{{\mechanical{z}}^{-1}e^x-1}\,dx,
\qquad {\mechanical{z}}=e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}}.
$$

The order has been written as $\ell$ rather than the source ${\mechanical{n}}$ to keep it separate from particle density. The integration variable $x$ and function order are mathematical helpers.

For the three-dimensional nonrelativistic gas with ground energy zero,

$$
\frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}
\begin{cases}f_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}}),&\mathrm{FD},\\g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}}),&\mathrm{BE},\end{cases}
\qquad
\frac {\mechanical{P}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}
\begin{cases}f_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}),&\mathrm{FD},\\g_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}),&\mathrm{BE}.\end{cases}
$$

Consequently,

$$
{\energy{\Phi}}=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}=-\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s{\mechanical{V}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}
\begin{cases}f_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}),&\mathrm{FD},\\g_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}),&\mathrm{BE}.\end{cases}
$$

> [!note] Remark 6.6 — Classical limit
> As ${\mechanical{z}}\to0$, $f_\ell({\mechanical{z}})\simeq g_\ell({\mechanical{z}})\simeq {\mechanical{z}}$. Both equations then give ${\mechanical{P}}={\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$ with ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{V}}$.

> [!note] Condensed Bose gas
> The Bose integral gives the excited-state density. Below the condensation temperature, add the ground-state contribution: ${\mechanical{N}}={\mechanical{N}}_0+{\mechanical{V}}{\mechanical{g}}_sg_{3/2}(1)/{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3$ in the thermodynamic limit. The ideal zero-energy condensate does not add bulk pressure. For finite volume, retain its discrete contribution before taking the limit.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\mechanical{g}}_s$ | $\unit{1}$ | Spin degeneracy | Number of internal spin states at a given orbital state. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal de Broglie wavelength | Thermal quantum length for a particle of mass m. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\energy{\Phi}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Grand potential | Equal to $-{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\ln\Xi$. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\mechanical{z}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Fugacity | Equal to $e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}}$. |
