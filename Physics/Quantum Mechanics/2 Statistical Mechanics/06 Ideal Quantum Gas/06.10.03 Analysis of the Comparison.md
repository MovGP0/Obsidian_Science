> [!TLDR]
> Exchange increases bosonic occupation and reduces fermionic occupation relative to the classical expression.

## High energy: common Maxwell–Boltzmann limit

For ${\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}}\gg {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$, all three laws approach $e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}$. Occupations are small, and the distinction between quantum statistics is negligible.

## Low energy: different occupations

The Fermi–Dirac law approaches one for ${\energy{\varepsilon}}\ll{\energy{\mu}}$. The Bose–Einstein law grows without bound as ${\energy{\varepsilon}}\to{\energy{\mu}}^+$ within its mathematical domain. The Maxwell–Boltzmann expression also grows for ${\energy{\varepsilon}}<{\energy{\mu}}$, which shows why its use in this range is inconsistent with a dilute quantum gas.

## Quantum correction

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{FD},\mathrm{BE}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})
=\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{MB}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})}{1\pm {\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{MB}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})}.
$$

The minus sign for bosons increases occupation relative to Maxwell–Boltzmann. The plus sign for fermions reduces it. These exchange effects are sometimes called statistical attraction and statistical repulsion. They do not require a real interparticle force.

> [!note] Remark 6.5 — Physical consequences
> Bosonic enhancement is related to stimulated emission: emission into an occupied photon mode is enhanced. Laser operation also requires matter–light coupling, pumping, and losses; equilibrium Bose statistics alone does not produce a laser. Pauli exclusion prevents electrons from all occupying one state and produces degeneracy pressure. This pressure is important in white dwarfs and, together with interactions and other contributions, in neutron stars.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
