> [!TLDR]
> Quantum statistics connects particle exchange symmetry to the occupation of energy states.

Previous chapters developed statistical mechanics in the classical limit. In that limit, particle exchange is accounted for by the Gibbs factor, and independent single-particle contributions simplify the partition function. At lower temperature or higher density, this approximation fails. The quantum nature of the particles requires a different method.

This chapter develops quantum statistics from particle indistinguishability, wavefunction symmetry, and the spin–statistics theorem. From these principles, we derive the Fermi–Dirac, Bose–Einstein, and Maxwell–Boltzmann distributions and examine their conditions of use.

Start with [[06.01 Particle Indistinguishability in Quantum Mechanics]]. The chapter ends with [[06.13 Summary]] and [[06.99 Exercises]].

> [!note] Source clarification
> The source calls classical particles distinguishable. Classical trajectories permit labels, but the preceding ideal-gas calculation already uses the Gibbs factor for identical particles. The classical limit of an identical quantum gas retains this counting correction.
