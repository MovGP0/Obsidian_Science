> [!TLDR]
> Exchange of identical particles constrains the many-particle wavefunction.

Identical quantum particles cannot be identified by permanent particle labels. Their exchange must preserve the physical state.

A detector can resolve two occupied positions without giving the particles permanent identities. Thus the question is which label changes alter the physical state, not whether two detection events can be resolved. The symmetry rule applies to the complete state, including spin.

Use the following route to separate the assumptions from their consequences. Indistinguishability motivates label-invariant observables; the usual bosonic or fermionic symmetry sector is additional input. Once that sector is chosen, the algebra gives occupation restrictions.

- [[06.01.01 The Indistinguishability Problem]] defines identical particles.
- [[06.01.02 Consequences for the Wavefunction]] derives the symmetric and antisymmetric exchange rules used here.
- [[06.01.03 Symmetrization and Antisymmetrization]] constructs the corresponding states.
