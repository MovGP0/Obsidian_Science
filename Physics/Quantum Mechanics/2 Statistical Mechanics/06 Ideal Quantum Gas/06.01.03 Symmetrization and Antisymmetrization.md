> [!TLDR]
> A sum over permutations gives a bosonic state; a Slater determinant gives a fermionic state.

Start with orthonormal single-particle states ${\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_i}$. The Hartree product is

$$
{\signal{\Phi}}(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_N)={\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_1}(\xi_1){\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_2}(\xi_2)\cdots{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_N}(\xi_N).
$$

An arbitrary Hartree product does not have the required exchange symmetry. It must be symmetrized for bosons or antisymmetrized for fermions.

## Bosons: symmetric wavefunction

Add all permutations of the orbital labels:

$$
{\signal{\Psi}}_S(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_N)=\mathcal N_S
\sum_{\sigma\in S_N}\prod_{i=1}^{{\mechanical{N}}}{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_{\sigma(i)}}(\xi_i),
\qquad \mathcal N_S=\frac1{\sqrt{{\mechanical{N}}!\prod_\alpha {\mechanical{n}}_\alpha!}}.
$$

The sum contains all ${\mechanical{N}}!$ permutations, including repeated products when an orbital has occupation ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha>1$. The stated normalization includes these repeats. Then $\hat P_{ij}{\signal{\Psi}}_S=+{\signal{\Psi}}_S$. Any number of bosons can occupy the same single-particle state.

## Fermions: antisymmetric wavefunction

For distinct orthonormal orbitals, use the **Slater determinant**:

$$
{\signal{\Psi}}_A(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_N)=\frac1{\sqrt{{\mechanical{N}}!}}
\begin{vmatrix}
{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_1}(\xi_1)&{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_1}(\xi_2)&\cdots&{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_1}(\xi_N)\\
{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_2}(\xi_1)&{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_2}(\xi_2)&\cdots&{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_2}(\xi_N)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_N}(\xi_1)&{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_N}(\xi_2)&\cdots&{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_N}(\xi_N)
\end{vmatrix}.\tag{6.1}
$$

**Proposition 6.1 — Pauli exclusion principle.** If two fermions occupy the same single-particle state, the determinant has two equal rows and is zero. Such a configuration cannot be a nonzero physical state.

**Proof.** For $\alpha_i=\alpha_j$ with $i\ne j$, rows $i$ and $j$ in Eq. (6.1) are equal. Their determinant vanishes.

> [!note] Remark 6.1
> The same determinant can be written as
>
> $$
> {\signal{\Psi}}_A=\frac1{\sqrt{{\mechanical{N}}!}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_N}(-1)^{|\sigma|}\prod_{i=1}^{{\mechanical{N}}}{\signal{\phi}}_{\alpha_{\sigma(i)}}(\xi_i).
> $$
>
> Here $(-1)^{|\sigma|}$ is the permutation sign. The corresponding sum without alternating signs is the **permanent**. In general, an exact permanent is much harder to calculate than a determinant; the source identifies its counting problem as #P-complete.

> [!note] Normalization and notation
> The normalization factors above require orthonormal orbitals. For overlapping orbitals, their overlap matrix must also be included. The Hartree product ${\signal{\Phi}}$ in this article is a wavefunction, not the grand potential used later in the chapter.
