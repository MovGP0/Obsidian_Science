> [!TLDR]
> A plot of molar heat capacity divided by temperature against temperature squared separates electrons from phonons.

Low-temperature heat-capacity measurements are useful probes of the electronic properties of metals. In a simple normal metal,

$$
{\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}={\thermal{\gamma}} {\thermal{T}}+\beta_{\mathrm{ph}}{\thermal{T}}^3,
\qquad
\frac{{\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}}{{\thermal{T}}}={\thermal{\gamma}}+\beta_{\mathrm{ph}}{\thermal{T}}^2.
$$

Plotting ${\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}/{\thermal{T}}$ against ${\thermal{T}}^2$ gives a straight line. Its intercept is ${\thermal{\gamma}}$ and its slope is ${\thermal{\beta}}_{\mathrm{ph}}$. In the Debye model,

$$
{\thermal{\beta}}_{\mathrm{ph}}=\frac{12\pi^4{\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}}{5{\thermal{\Theta}}_D^3}.
$$

The two heat-capacity contributions are equal at ${\thermal{T}}^*=\sqrt{{\thermal{\gamma}}/{\thermal{\beta}}_{\mathrm{ph}}}$.

**Fig. 7.4** Molar heat capacity divided by temperature, plotted against T as in the source. The electronic term is constant and the phonon term is quadratic on this axis. The total is γ + βₚₕT². Values γ = 0.695 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻² and βₚₕ = 0.046 mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻⁴ give equal contributions near 3.9 K. Plotting the same ordinate against T² gives a straight line with intercept γ.

Typical values quoted in the source are:

| Metal | γ (mJ mol⁻¹ K⁻²) | Tꜰ (kK) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cu | 0.695 | 81 |
| Ag | 0.646 | 65 |
| Au | 0.729 | 64 |
| Al | 1.35 | 135 |
| Fe | 4.98 | 130 |

Iron has a much larger measured Sommerfeld coefficient than a simple free-electron estimate. Band structure and electronic correlations increase its low-energy density of states.

> [!note] Source notation and graph
> The source calls this a plot against T² but draws T on the horizontal axis. Fig. 7.4 retains the T axis and curved total, and states this explicitly. For the straight-line analysis, use T² instead. The source calls the lattice coefficient β; these notes use ${\thermal{\beta}}_{\mathrm{ph}}$ to distinguish it from inverse temperature. The quoted metal values are source data, not a universal relation between the two table columns.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Molar heat capacity | Heat capacity per mole of material. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{\gamma}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-2}}$ | Molar Sommerfeld coefficient | Coefficient of the linear electronic molar heat capacity. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}_{\mathrm{ph}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-4}}$ | Phonon coefficient | Coefficient of the low-temperature cubic molar heat capacity. |
| ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D$ | $\unit{K}$ | Debye temperature | Characteristic temperature of lattice vibrations. |
| ${\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Molar gas constant | Equal to the Avogadro constant times the Boltzmann constant. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_F$ | $\unit{K}$ | Fermi temperature | Fermi energy divided by the Boltzmann constant. |
