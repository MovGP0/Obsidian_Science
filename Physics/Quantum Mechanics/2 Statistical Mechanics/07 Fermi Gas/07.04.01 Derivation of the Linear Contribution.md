> [!TLDR]
> Differentiating the quadratic energy correction gives a linear electronic heat capacity.

Differentiate Equation (7.4) at fixed volume and particle number. The heat capacity per volume is

$$
{\thermal{c}}_V^{(e)}=\frac1{\mechanical{V}}\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{E}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{V,N}
=\frac{\pi^2}{2}\frac{{\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{k}}_B^2{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}
=\frac{\pi^2}{2}{\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{k}}_B\frac {\thermal{T}}{{\thermal{T}}_F}.
$$

For one mole of metal with one conduction electron per atom, the molar electronic heat capacity is

$$
{\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}^{(e)}=\frac{\pi^2}{2}\frac{{\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_F}={\thermal{\gamma}} {\thermal{T}}.
$$

The **Sommerfeld coefficient** is

$$
{\thermal{\gamma}}=\frac{\pi^2{\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}}{2{\thermal{T}}_F}
=\frac{\pi^2{\mechanical{N}}_A{\thermal{k}}_B^2}{2{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}
=\frac{\pi^2{\thermal{k}}_B^2}{3}{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F){\mechanical{V}}_{\mathrm{mol}}.
$$

Per volume, write

$$
{\thermal{\gamma}}_V=\frac{\pi^2{\thermal{k}}_B^2}{3}{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F),\qquad {\thermal{c}}_V^{(e)}={\thermal{\gamma}}_V{\thermal{T}}.
$$

A measurement of the linear heat-capacity coefficient gives the density of states at the Fermi energy. For ${\mechanical{Z}}$ conduction electrons per atom, multiply the one-electron molar expression by ${\mechanical{Z}}$.

> [!note] Notation
> The source uses γ for both a molar coefficient and a coefficient per volume in different examples. These notes use ${\thermal{\gamma}}$ for the molar coefficient and ${\thermal{\gamma}}_V$ for the coefficient per volume. The symbol ${\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}$ distinguishes the gas constant from the stellar radius used later.
