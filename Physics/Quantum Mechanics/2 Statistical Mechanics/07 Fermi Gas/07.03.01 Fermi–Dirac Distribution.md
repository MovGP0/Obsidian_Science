> [!TLDR]
> The Fermi–Dirac occupation broadens with temperature, while normalization fixes the chemical potential.

At finite temperature, the mean occupation of a complete single-particle state is

$$
{\mechanical{f}}({\energy{\varepsilon}},{\thermal{T}})=\frac1{e^{({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}+1}.
$$

For fixed number density, the chemical potential ${\energy{\mu}}={\energy{\mu}}({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{n}})$ is determined by

$$
{\mechanical{n}}=\int_0^\infty {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{f}}({\energy{\varepsilon}},{\thermal{T}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}. \tag{7.2}
$$

The curves in Fig. 7.3 show the thermal broadening. At each positive temperature, ${\mechanical{f}}({\energy{\mu}},{\thermal{T}})=1/2$.

**Fig. 7.3** Fermi–Dirac occupation at several temperatures. The zero-temperature occupation is a step. Thermal broadening increases with temperature. At fixed density, μ decreases and the point f = 1/2 moves to lower energy. The curves use the full number integral, not the low-temperature expansion at the highest displayed temperature.

> [!note] Source-figure clarification
> The source draws all finite-temperature curves through $({\energy{\varepsilon}}/{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F,{\mechanical{f}})=(1,1/2)$, but its caption says that the chemical potential moves left. The reproduced curves use the chemical potential determined at fixed density. Their half-occupation points therefore move left as temperature increases.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{f}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Mean occupation | Fermi–Dirac occupation of one complete single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,m^{-3}}$ | Density of states per volume | Number of states per energy and volume, including spin. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_F$ | $\unit{K}$ | Fermi temperature | Fermi energy divided by the Boltzmann constant. |
