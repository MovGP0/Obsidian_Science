## Question — Exercise 7.1, Sirius B

Sirius B has mass ${\mechanical{M}}\approx1.02{\mechanical{M}}_\odot$ and radius ${\mechanical{R}}\approx5570\,\unit{km}$. Assume carbon composition, so ${\mechanical{Z}}/{\mechanical{A}}=1/2$.

1. Calculate the mean mass density and electron number density.
2. Calculate the electron Fermi energy.
3. Check whether the electron gas is degenerate and whether it is ultrarelativistic.
4. Estimate its degeneracy pressure.

## Worked calculation

The supplied solution uses a uniform-density model. The steps below retain that model and explain why each relation is used. Approximate source values are compared with a consistent calculation.

### 1. Convert mass and radius, then find the volume

Use ${\mechanical{M}}_\odot\approx2.0\times10^{30}\,\unit{kg}$, as in the chapter. Then

$$
{\mechanical{M}}=2.04\times10^{30}\,\unit{kg},\qquad {\mechanical{R}}=5.57\times10^6\,\unit{m},
$$

$$
{\mechanical{V}}=\frac{4\pi}{3}{\mechanical{R}}^3\approx7.24\times10^{20}\,\unit{m^3},
\qquad
{\mechanical{\rho}}=\frac {\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{V}}\approx2.82\times10^9\,\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}.
$$

**Why:** Mass density gives the number of nuclei per volume. Carbon supplies one electron per two nucleons. Using the atomic mass constant,

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_e=\frac {\mechanical{Z}}{\mechanical{A}}\frac{{\mechanical{\rho}}}{{\mechanical{m}}_u}
\approx\frac12\frac{2.82\times10^9}{1.66054\times10^{-27}}
\approx8.49\times10^{35}\,\unit{m^{-3}}.
$$

> [!note] Source rounding
> The source rounds the mass to $2.0\times10^{30}\,\unit{kg}$ and uses the proton mass for the nucleon scale. It obtains ${\mechanical{\rho}}\approx2.77\times10^9\,\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and ${\mechanical{n}}_e\approx8.3\times10^{35}\,\unit{m^{-3}}$. These are consistent approximate values. The calculation here keeps the stated factor 1.02 and uses ${\mechanical{m}}_u$ consistently.

### 2. Find Fermi momentum before choosing the energy formula

Electrons have spin degeneracy two. State counting gives

$$
{\signal{k}}_F=(3\pi^2{\mechanical{n}}_e)^{1/3}\approx2.93\times10^{12}\,\unit{m^{-1}},
\qquad
{\mechanical{p}}_F={\energy{\hbar}} {\signal{k}}_F.
$$

**Why:** State counting fixes the momentum even when the energy–momentum relation becomes relativistic. First calculate the nonrelativistic estimate for comparison:

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{\mathrm{NR}}=\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_F^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}_e}\approx0.327\,\unit{MeV}.
$$

The associated momentum-energy scale is ${\mechanical{p}}_F{\signal{c}}\approx0.578\,\unit{MeV}$, while ${\mechanical{m}}_e{\signal{c}}^2\approx0.511\,\unit{MeV}$. Thus ${\mechanical{p}}_F/({\mechanical{m}}_e{\signal{c}})\approx1.13$: the nonrelativistic formula is not quantitatively sufficient.

Use the relativistic **kinetic** energy:

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F=\sqrt{({\mechanical{p}}_F{\signal{c}})^2+({\mechanical{m}}_e{\signal{c}}^2)^2}-{\mechanical{m}}_e{\signal{c}}^2
\approx0.260\,\unit{MeV}.
$$

The source obtains approximately 0.323 MeV nonrelativistically and 0.258 MeV relativistically with its rounded density.

### 3. Check degeneracy and relativity separately

Convert the relativistic Fermi energy to a temperature:

$$
{\thermal{T}}_F=\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}{{\thermal{k}}_B}\approx3.0\times10^9\,\unit{K}.
$$

The source compares this scale with the surface effective temperature ${\thermal{T}}_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx2.5\times10^4\,\unit{K}$. That ratio is about $8\times10^{-6}$. Interior electrons are strongly degenerate provided their local temperature is also much smaller than ${\thermal{T}}_F$, as in the cold white-dwarf model.

**Why:** Degeneracy is controlled by ${\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F$; relativity is controlled by ${\mechanical{p}}_F/({\mechanical{m}}_e{\signal{c}})$. They are different tests. Here the gas is strongly degenerate and **moderately relativistic**, rather than ultrarelativistic. The latter would require ${\mechanical{p}}_F\gg {\mechanical{m}}_e{\signal{c}}$.

The source's value ${\thermal{T}}_F\approx3.74\times10^9\,\unit{K}$ uses its nonrelativistic energy. It is a useful scale estimate, but the relativistic kinetic energy gives the consistent Fermi temperature.

### 4. Estimate pressure and assess the approximation

The nonrelativistic estimate is

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_0^{\mathrm{NR}}=\frac25{\mechanical{n}}_e{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{\mathrm{NR}}
\approx1.8\times10^{22}\,\unit{Pa}.
$$

This is the estimate requested in the source, which quotes about $1.7\times10^{22}\,\unit{Pa}$, or $1.7\times10^{17}\,\unit{atm}$.

**Why a refinement helps:** A partly relativistic electron has a speed smaller than ${\mechanical{p}}/{\mechanical{m}}_e$. The nonrelativistic pressure therefore overestimates the momentum flux. The added relativistic check uses ${\mechanical{\xi}}_F={\mechanical{p}}_F/({\mechanical{m}}_e{\signal{c}})$ and

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_0=\frac{{\mechanical{m}}_e^4{\signal{c}}^5}{3\pi^2{\energy{\hbar}}^3}
\int_0^{{\mechanical{\xi}}_F}\frac{t^4}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\,dt.
$$

An antiderivative, zero at the lower endpoint, is

$$
\int_0^{{\mechanical{\xi}}_F}\frac{t^4}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\,dt
=\frac18\left[{\mechanical{\xi}}_F(2{\mechanical{\xi}}_F^2-3)\sqrt{1+{\mechanical{\xi}}_F^2}
+3\operatorname{arsinh}{\mechanical{\xi}}_F\right].
$$

It gives ${\mechanical{P}}_0\approx1.3\times10^{22}\,\unit{Pa}$. This confirms the enormous pressure while showing the size of the relativistic correction.

## Solution

The uniform-density estimate gives a mass density near $2.8\times10^9\,\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and an electron density near $8.5\times10^{35}\,\unit{m^{-3}}$. The relativistic Fermi kinetic energy is about 0.26 MeV. The electron gas is strongly degenerate and moderately relativistic. Its degeneracy pressure is of order $10^{22}\,\unit{Pa}$.
