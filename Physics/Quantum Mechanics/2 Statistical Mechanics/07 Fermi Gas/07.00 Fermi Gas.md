> [!TLDR]
> The exclusion principle determines the ground state, thermal response, and degeneracy pressure of an ideal Fermi gas.

The ideal Fermi gas is an important model in modern physics. The Pauli exclusion principle prevents two identical fermions from occupying the same complete quantum state. This produces macroscopic results that differ strongly from classical statistics, even at very low temperature.

Conduction electrons in metals, nucleons in nuclei, electrons in white dwarfs, and neutrons in neutron stars are examples of degenerate fermionic systems. The ideal gas supplies a useful starting model; interactions are important in several of these applications.

This chapter starts at zero temperature, adds finite-temperature corrections with the Sommerfeld expansion, and then examines important applications. Start with [[07.01 Fermi Gas at Zero Temperature]]. The chapter ends with [[07.10 Summary]] and [[07.99 Exercises]].
