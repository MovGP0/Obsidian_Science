> [!TLDR]
> At fixed density, the leading finite-temperature correction to Pauli susceptibility is quadratic and negative.

In linear response, the occupation difference between the spin branches is proportional to minus the energy derivative of the Fermi occupation. The susceptibility therefore samples the density of states over a thermal window around the chemical potential. At zero temperature this window selects the Fermi energy, recovering the Pauli result.

There are two second-order effects for the square-root density of states. Its downward curvature lowers the thermal average at fixed chemical potential. The fixed-density decrease in chemical potential lowers the density of states at the center of the window as well. Both effects have the same sign, and both must be retained to obtain the coefficient below.

Applying the Sommerfeld expansion to the spin response gives, for a three-dimensional nonrelativistic ideal gas,

$$
{\magnetic{\chi}}({\thermal{T}})={\magnetic{\chi}}_{\mathrm{Pauli}}\left[1-\frac{\pi^2}{12}
\left(\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}\right)^2+\cdots\right].
$$

The chemical potential must change with temperature to keep the total density fixed. The correction is of order $({\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F)^2$ and is small for most ordinary metals at room temperature.

The correction vanishes in the zero-temperature limit and reduces the positive leading susceptibility. It does not give a Curie divergence. A different density of states can change the curvature contribution and the chemical-potential shift, so the coefficient and sign stated here belong to the three-dimensional quadratic-spectrum model. Once the thermal window is no longer narrow, the truncated expansion does not determine the response.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\magnetic{\chi}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Magnetic susceptibility | Derivative of magnetization with respect to H. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_F$ | $\unit{K}$ | Fermi temperature | Fermi energy divided by the Boltzmann constant. |
