> [!TLDR]
> At zero temperature, fermions fill the lowest available single-particle states up to the Fermi energy.

Take a fixed number of noninteracting fermions in a large box. To find the ground state, minimize the sum of their single-particle energies while allowing at most one particle in each complete state. Putting every particle into the lowest orbital would violate this constraint once its spin states are full. The remaining particles must occupy higher orbitals even at zero temperature.

Follow the occupation argument before the state count below. The sphere describes an isotropic free-particle spectrum in three dimensions; exclusion alone does not require a spherical surface for a different dispersion. As a check, increase the number of particles at fixed volume: the occupied region must grow because an already occupied complete state cannot accept another particle.
