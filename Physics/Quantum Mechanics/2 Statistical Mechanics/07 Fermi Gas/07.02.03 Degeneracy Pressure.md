> [!TLDR]
> Exclusion produces a zero-temperature pressure that increases as number density to the power five thirds.

An ideal nonrelativistic Fermi gas exerts pressure even at ${\thermal{T}}=0$. The free-particle relation ${\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}=2{\energy{E}}/3$ gives

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_0=\frac{2{\energy{E}}_0}{3{\mechanical{V}}}=\frac23\frac35{\mechanical{n}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F=\frac25{\mechanical{n}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F.
$$

The pressure relation can also be checked from the energy. At fixed particle number, the Fermi energy is proportional to volume to the power minus two thirds. The ground-state energy has the same volume dependence. Taking minus its volume derivative gives two thirds of energy per volume, with a positive sign because compression raises the energy. This route makes the constraint on the derivative explicit.

Substitute the Fermi energy:

$$
\boxed{{\mechanical{P}}_0=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2}{5{\mechanical{m}}}\left(\frac{6\pi^2}{{\mechanical{g}}_s}\right)^{2/3}{\mechanical{n}}^{5/3}.}
$$

This **degeneracy pressure** has a quantum origin and is kinetic in nature. It is not caused by an interparticle repulsive force. Electron degeneracy pressure helps support white dwarfs; see [[07.07 White Dwarfs and the Chandrasekhar Mass]].

The boxed expression has units of energy per volume, which are pressure units. Its positive density derivative means that compression increases the restoring pressure. In the dilute limit this ground-state pressure tends to zero. At sufficiently large density, however, the nonrelativistic dispersion fails; the power five thirds cannot be extended into the ultrarelativistic regime.
