> [!TLDR]
> Fermi statistics explains the ground-state energy, low-temperature response, transport scales, and white-dwarf support.

The main formulas for a three-dimensional free gas are:

| Quantity | Formula | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fermi wave vector | ${\signal{k}}_F=(6\pi^2{\mechanical{n}}/{\mechanical{g}}_s)^{1/3}$ | ${\mechanical{g}}_s=2$ for electrons |
| Fermi energy | ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F={\energy{\hbar}}^2{\signal{k}}_F^2/(2{\mechanical{m}})$ | Nonrelativistic, zero temperature |
| Mean energy | ${\energy{E}}_0/{\mechanical{N}}=3{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F/5$ | Zero temperature |
| Degeneracy pressure | ${\mechanical{P}}_0=2{\mechanical{n}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F/5$ | Nonrelativistic, zero temperature |
| Density of states per volume | ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s}{4\pi^2}(2{\mechanical{m}}/{\energy{\hbar}}^2)^{3/2}{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}$ | Three dimensions |
| Chemical potential | ${\energy{\mu}}\approx{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F[1-(\pi^2/12)({\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F)^2]$ | Fixed density, ${\thermal{T}}\ll {\thermal{T}}_F$ |
| Molar electronic heat capacity | ${\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}={\thermal{\gamma}} {\thermal{T}}$ | ${\thermal{\gamma}}=\pi^2{\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}/(2{\thermal{T}}_F)$ for one electron per atom |
| Pauli susceptibility | ${\magnetic{\chi}}={\magnetic{\mu}}_0{\magnetic{\mu}}_B^2{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F)$ | Leading low-temperature spin response |
| Lorenz number | ${\thermal{L}}_0=\pi^2{\thermal{k}}_B^2/(3{\electric{e}}^2)$ | Simple degenerate transport model |
| Chandrasekhar mass | ${\mechanical{M}}_{\mathrm{Ch}}\approx5.83{\mechanical{M}}_\odot/{\mechanical{\mu}}_e^2$ | Ideal cold, nonrotating model |

The main conclusions are:

1. Pauli exclusion produces a finite ground-state energy, pressure, and Fermi speed even at zero temperature.
2. Thermal corrections to the energy and chemical potential start at order $({\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F)^2$. They are small for ordinary metals at room temperature.
3. The Sommerfeld expansion is the main analytical tool for the low-temperature regime.
4. The linear electronic heat capacity and approximately temperature-independent Pauli susceptibility are characteristic effects of fermionic degeneracy.
5. Electron degeneracy pressure supports white dwarfs. Relativistic kinematics produces a limiting mass near 1.4 solar masses for carbon–oxygen material.
