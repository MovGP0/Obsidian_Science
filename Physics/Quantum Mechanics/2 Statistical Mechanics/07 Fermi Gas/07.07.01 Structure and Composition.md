> [!TLDR]
> A white dwarf contains nuclei embedded in a strongly degenerate electron gas.

A white dwarf is a compact stellar remnant. After a star of low or intermediate initial mass has exhausted the relevant nuclear fuel and lost its outer layers, a dense core can remain. The source gives the approximate progenitor range from one to eight solar masses; the actual evolutionary boundary depends on composition and mass loss.

The interior is typically fully ionized material, often carbon and oxygen, with nuclei embedded in a degenerate electron gas. Representative source values are:

- Mass: ${\mechanical{M}}\sim0.5$–$1.4{\mechanical{M}}_\odot$, with ${\mechanical{M}}_\odot\approx2\times10^{30}\,\unit{kg}$.
- Radius: ${\mechanical{R}}\sim5000$–$10000\,\unit{km}$, similar to the radius of Earth.
- Mean mass density: ${\mechanical{\rho}}\sim10^9\,\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$.

For a carbon–oxygen composition, ${\mechanical{Z}}/{\mechanical{A}}=1/2$ and the electron number density is

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_e=\frac {\mechanical{Z}}{\mechanical{A}}\frac{{\mechanical{\rho}}}{{\mechanical{m}}_u}\sim3\times10^{35}\,\unit{m^{-3}}.
$$

The nonrelativistic estimate gives an electron Fermi temperature of order $10^9\,\unit{K}$. It greatly exceeds typical stellar temperatures quoted in the source, $10^4$–$10^7\,\unit{K}$. Electrons are therefore strongly degenerate.

At these densities, ${\mechanical{p}}_F{\signal{c}}$ can be comparable to ${\mechanical{m}}_e{\signal{c}}^2$. The formula ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F={\energy{\hbar}}^2{\signal{k}}_F^2/(2{\mechanical{m}}_e)$ is then only an estimate. Relativistic kinematics is required; see [[07.09 Further Study - Relativistic Fermi Gas]].

> [!note] Temperature and composition
> A surface effective temperature and an interior temperature describe different regions of a star. Degeneracy is assessed with the local electron temperature. The large Fermi scale makes degeneracy robust in the cold interior model used here. The mass number per electron is ${\mechanical{\mu}}_e={\mechanical{A}}/{\mechanical{Z}}$; it is unrelated to chemical potential despite the shared letter.
