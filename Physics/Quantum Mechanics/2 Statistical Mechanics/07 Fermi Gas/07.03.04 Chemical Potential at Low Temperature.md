> [!TLDR]
> At fixed density, the chemical potential decreases quadratically with temperature.

Apply Equation (7.3) to Equation (7.2), with ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=C{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\mechanical{n}}&=\int_0^{\energy{\mu}} C{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
+\frac{\pi^2}{6}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2\frac{C}{2\sqrt{\energy{\mu}}}+O({\thermal{T}}^4)\\
&=\frac23C{\energy{\mu}}^{3/2}+\frac{\pi^2}{12}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2C{\energy{\mu}}^{-1/2}+\cdots.
\end{aligned}
$$

At zero temperature, ${\mechanical{n}}=(2/3)C{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{3/2}$. Keep the density fixed. To second order,

$$
{\energy{\mu}}^{3/2}={\energy{\varepsilon}}_F^{3/2}\left[1-\frac{\pi^2}{8}\left(\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}\right)^2+\cdots\right].
$$

Raise both sides to the power $2/3$:

$$
\boxed{{\energy{\mu}}({\thermal{T}})={\energy{\varepsilon}}_F\left[1-\frac{\pi^2}{12}\left(\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}\right)^2
+O\!\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}^4}{{\thermal{T}}_F^4}\right)\right].}
$$

Thus the first correction is negative and quadratic in ${\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,m^{-3}}$ | Density of states per volume | Number of states per energy and volume, including spin. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_F$ | $\unit{K}$ | Fermi temperature | Fermi energy divided by the Boltzmann constant. |
