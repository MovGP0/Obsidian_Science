> [!TLDR]
> Relativistic dispersion changes degeneracy pressure from density to the power five thirds to density to the power four thirds.

When ${\mechanical{p}}_F$ is comparable to ${\mechanical{m}}{\signal{c}}$, use the relativistic kinetic energy:

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}({\signal{k}})=\sqrt{({\energy{\hbar}} {\signal{c}}{\signal{k}})^2+({\mechanical{m}}{\signal{c}}^2)^2}-{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{c}}^2.
$$

The subtracted rest energy fixes the energy zero used throughout this note. At zero momentum the kinetic energy is zero. Expanding the square root for small momentum gives the nonrelativistic kinetic energy, while at very large momentum the leading term is momentum times the speed of light. State counting in momentum space is unchanged; only the conversion from momentum to energy and velocity changes.

Define the dimensionless momentum ${\mechanical{\xi}}={\mechanical{p}}/({\mechanical{m}}{\signal{c}})$. The density of states per volume is

$$
g_{\mathrm{rel}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s}{2\pi^2}\frac{{\mechanical{m}}^3{\signal{c}}^3}{{\energy{\hbar}}^3}{\mechanical{\xi}}^2\frac{d{\mechanical{\xi}}}{d{\energy{\varepsilon}}},
\qquad
\frac{d{\mechanical{\xi}}}{d{\energy{\varepsilon}}}=\frac{\sqrt{1+{\mechanical{\xi}}^2}}{{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{c}}^2{\mechanical{\xi}}}.
$$

Two limits follow:

- **Nonrelativistic**, ${\mechanical{\xi}}_F\ll1$: ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F\ll {\mechanical{m}}{\signal{c}}^2$, ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F\propto {\mechanical{n}}^{2/3}$, and ${\mechanical{P}}\propto {\mechanical{n}}^{5/3}$.
- **Ultrarelativistic**, ${\mechanical{\xi}}_F\gg1$: ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F\approx{\energy{\hbar}} {\signal{c}}{\signal{k}}_F\propto {\mechanical{n}}^{1/3}$, and ${\mechanical{P}}\propto {\mechanical{n}}^{4/3}$.

This reduction in the pressure exponent is the physical basis of the Chandrasekhar mass scale. Under stellar compression, ultrarelativistic degeneracy support and gravitational binding have the same radius scaling, so their coefficients determine a limiting mass.

For a partly relativistic gas, the exact zero-temperature pressure can be found from the isotropic momentum flux:

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_0=\frac{{\mechanical{g}}_s{\mechanical{m}}^4{\signal{c}}^5}{6\pi^2{\energy{\hbar}}^3}
\int_0^{{\mechanical{\xi}}_F}\frac{t^4}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\,dt.
$$

This last formula is an added calculation aid for the Sirius B exercise. The integration variable t is dimensionless.

The pressure integral provides its own two limit checks. For small upper momentum, the denominator approaches one and the integral is proportional to the fifth power of that momentum. For a large upper momentum, the denominator approaches the momentum variable and the integral grows as its fourth power. Since number density is proportional to the cube of the Fermi momentum, these reproduce the two pressure exponents stated above.

For pressure, a constant rest energy per particle makes no contribution to a volume derivative at fixed particle number. It must nevertheless be kept distinct from kinetic energy when defining a chemical potential or reporting an energy density. In the partly relativistic interval neither limiting pressure law supplies the full answer; use the integral instead. The integrand is positive, so the result must be positive and increase with the upper limit.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | Magnitude of particle momentum. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | Mass of one particle. |
| ${\signal{c}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | Propagation speed of light in vacuum. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to $h/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\signal{k}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | Magnitude or component of a wave vector. |
| ${\mechanical{\xi}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Relativistic momentum | Momentum divided by mc. |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,m^{-3}}$ | Density of states per volume | Number of states per energy and volume, including spin. |
| ${\mechanical{g}}_s$ | $\unit{1}$ | Spin degeneracy | Number of internal spin states at a given orbital state. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
