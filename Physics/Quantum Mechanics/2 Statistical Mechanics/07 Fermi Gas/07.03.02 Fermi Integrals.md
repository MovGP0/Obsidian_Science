> [!TLDR]
> Fermi integrals express thermodynamic averages as energy integrals with the Fermi occupation.

Many thermodynamic quantities have the form

$$
I=\int_0^\infty\phi({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{f}}({\energy{\varepsilon}},{\thermal{T}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}},
$$

where $\phi$ is a sufficiently regular weighting function. Define the unnormalized Fermi integrals by

$$
F_\nu(\eta)=\int_0^\infty\frac{x^\nu}{e^{x-\eta}+1}\,dx,
\qquad \eta=\frac{{\energy{\mu}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}},\qquad \nu>-1.
$$

Here $x$ is dimensionless energy. The source writes a dimensionless integration variable with an energy symbol; $x$ makes the units explicit. This definition has no gamma-function normalization. It differs from the normalized functions of fugacity in Chapter 6.

For ordinary metals at low temperature, ${\energy{\mu}}\approx{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F\gg {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$. The Sommerfeld expansion is then more useful than direct evaluation of each integral.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\mechanical{f}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Mean occupation | Fermi–Dirac occupation of one complete single-particle state. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
