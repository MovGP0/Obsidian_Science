> [!TLDR]
> The ground state of a free isotropic Fermi gas fills a sphere in wave-vector space.

Consider ${\mechanical{N}}$ identical, noninteracting fermions of mass ${\mechanical{m}}$ and spin ${\mechanical{s}}$ in volume ${\mechanical{V}}$. The spin degeneracy is ${\mechanical{g}}_s=2{\mechanical{s}}+1$. For electrons, ${\mechanical{s}}=1/2$ and ${\mechanical{g}}_s=2$. Each complete state, including its spin label, can contain at most one fermion.

At ${\thermal{T}}=0$, fill the energy levels from the lowest upward until all particles have been placed. Include the spin degeneracy when counting states.

**Definition 7.1 — Fermi energy.** The Fermi energy ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_F$ is the highest occupied single-particle energy in the zero-temperature ground state. In the bulk limit it equals the zero-temperature chemical potential:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}(0)={\energy{\varepsilon}}_F.
$$

For a cubic box of side ${\mechanical{L}}$, with ${\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{L}}^3$ and periodic boundaries, the allowed wave vectors are ${\signal{\mathbf k}}=(2\pi/{\mechanical{L}})(n_x,n_y,n_z)$, with integer components. The single-particle energy is

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}_k=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2 {\signal{k}}^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}},\qquad {\signal{k}}=|{\signal{\mathbf k}}|. \tag{7.1}
$$

At zero temperature, states inside $|{\signal{\mathbf k}}|\le {\signal{k}}_F$ are occupied and states outside are empty. The boundary is the **Fermi surface**. For the free isotropic gas it is a sphere, called the **Fermi sphere**.
