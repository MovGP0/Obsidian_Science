> [!TLDR]
> More massive nonrelativistic white dwarfs have smaller radii, and the ideal relativistic sequence approaches a limiting mass.

Equation (7.5), with the mass dependence of its coefficients included, gives the nonrelativistic mass–radius relation

$$
{\mechanical{R}}\propto {\mechanical{M}}^{-1/3}.
$$

Thus a more massive white dwarf is more compact. As the ideal cold sequence approaches the Chandrasekhar mass, the limiting radius decreases toward zero. Electron degeneracy can no longer supply a stable equilibrium above the ideal limiting mass.

The exclusion principle therefore has a macroscopic role in stellar support. The source calls this “quantum glue.” The support is pressure from the filled fermion states, not a separate binding force.

> [!note] Limits of the ideal sequence
> The zero-radius endpoint is a result of the simplified model. Real white dwarfs undergo other processes before that endpoint. Depending on composition and evolution, loss of support can lead to thermonuclear disruption or collapse. The mass–radius relation alone does not determine a neutron-star or black-hole outcome.
