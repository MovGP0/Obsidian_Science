> [!TLDR]
> Band structure and interactions change the density of states and the measured Sommerfeld coefficient.

Real metals differ from a free Fermi gas because electrons interact with the crystal lattice and with each other. Near an isotropic Fermi surface, a parabolic quasiparticle band with effective mass ${\mechanical{m}}^*$ has

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}({\signal{k}})-{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F\approx\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}^*}({\signal{k}}^2-{\signal{k}}_F^2)
\approx\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2{\signal{k}}_F}{{\mechanical{m}}^*}({\signal{k}}-{\signal{k}}_F).
$$

> [!note] Source correction
> The source writes ${\energy{\varepsilon}}({\signal{k}})\approx{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F+{\energy{\hbar}}^2({\signal{k}}-{\signal{k}}_F)^2/(2{\mechanical{m}}^*)$. That expression has zero slope at the Fermi surface and is not the usual effective-mass expansion of a metallic band. The corrected expression retains the Fermi velocity. General bands can require a tensor mass and a full Fermi-surface description.

For the coefficient per volume,

$$
{\thermal{\gamma}}_{V,\mathrm{exp}}=\frac{\pi^2{\thermal{k}}_B^2}{3}{\energy{g}}^*({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F)
\approx\frac{{\mechanical{m}}^*}{{\mechanical{m}}_e}{\thermal{\gamma}}_{V,\mathrm{free}}.
$$

The approximate mass ratio applies when the density and the simple isotropic band description are held fixed. It is called an **enhancement factor**. It can be much greater than one in transition metals and heavy-fermion systems. In a multiband metal, an enhanced heat-capacity coefficient does not identify a unique single effective mass.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\signal{k}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | Magnitude or component of a wave vector. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to $h/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | Mass of one particle. |
| ${\thermal{\gamma}}_V$ | $\unit{J\,m^{-3}\,K^{-2}}$ | Sommerfeld coefficient per volume | Electronic heat capacity per volume divided by temperature. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,m^{-3}}$ | Density of states per volume | Number of states per energy and volume, including spin. |
