> [!TLDR]
> The energy of a degenerate ideal Fermi gas increases quadratically with temperature at fixed density.

Use the Sommerfeld lemma with $\phi({\energy{\varepsilon}})={\energy{\varepsilon}} {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=C{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac {\energy{E}}{\mechanical{V}}&=\int_0^{\energy{\mu}} C{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{3/2}d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
+\frac{\pi^2}{6}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2C\frac32{\energy{\mu}}^{1/2}+\cdots\\
&=\frac25C{\energy{\mu}}^{5/2}+\frac{\pi^2}{4}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2C{\energy{\mu}}^{1/2}+\cdots.
\end{aligned}
$$

Insert ${\energy{\mu}}\approx{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F[1-(\pi^2/12)({\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F)^2]$ and retain terms through second order:

$$
\frac {\energy{E}}{\mechanical{V}}=\frac35{\mechanical{n}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F\left[1+\frac{5\pi^2}{12}\left(\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}\right)^2
+O\!\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}^4}{{\thermal{T}}_F^4}\right)\right]. \tag{7.4}
$$

Only particles within an energy interval of order ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$ near the Fermi surface can be thermally excited. Their fraction is of order ${\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_F$, and their typical energy change is of order ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$. This gives the quadratic correction.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | Energy of a particle or system, as indicated. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,m^{-3}}$ | Density of states per volume | Number of states per energy and volume, including spin. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_F$ | $\unit{K}$ | Fermi temperature | Fermi energy divided by the Boltzmann constant. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
