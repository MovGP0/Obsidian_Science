> [!TLDR]
> The low-temperature electronic heat capacity is linear in temperature and measures the density of states at the Fermi energy.

Heat capacity asks how much the energy changes on heating, not how much energy is already present. The filled Fermi sea has a large ground-state energy, but exclusion prevents most of its deep states from changing occupation. This is why a large electronic energy can coexist with a small electronic heat capacity.

First derive the temperature derivative at fixed particle number and volume. Then compare it with equipartition for the same particle count. Finally use the different temperature powers of the electronic and lattice terms to plan a measurement. The last step requires a normal-metal temperature interval where these are the relevant contributions; the ideal-gas derivation alone does not establish a fit range for a real sample.
