> [!TLDR]
> Only a small fraction of electrons can absorb thermal energy in a strongly degenerate gas.

For one mole of a classical monatomic gas, equipartition gives

$$
{\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}^{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac32{\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}.
$$

For the same number of fermions at ${\thermal{T}}\ll {\thermal{T}}_F$,

$$
\boxed{\frac{{\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}^{(e)}}{{\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}^{\mathrm{cl}}}
=\frac{\pi^2{\thermal{T}}}{3{\thermal{T}}_F}\ll1.}
$$

> [!note] Source correction
> The source ratio contains π rather than π². Dividing $\pi^2{\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}{\thermal{T}}/(2{\thermal{T}}_F)$ by $3{\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}/2$ gives the π² expression above.

The electronic contribution is much smaller than the classical prediction because only states near the Fermi surface can change their occupation. A metal also has a phonon contribution proportional to ${\thermal{T}}^3$ at low temperature. At sufficiently low temperature, the cubic term decreases faster than the linear electronic term, and the electrons dominate.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{C}}_{V,\mathrm{mol}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Molar heat capacity | Heat capacity per mole of material. |
| ${\thermal{R}}_{\mathrm{gas}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Molar gas constant | Equal to the Avogadro constant times the Boltzmann constant. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_F$ | $\unit{K}$ | Fermi temperature | Fermi energy divided by the Boltzmann constant. |
