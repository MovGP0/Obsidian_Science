> [!TLDR]
> In a degenerate metal, the Fermi speed sets the transport mean free path.

The Sommerfeld model retains the Drude transport structure but treats the electrons as a degenerate Fermi gas. The relevant mean free path is

$$
{\mechanical{\ell}}_F={\mechanical{v}}_F{\semiconductor{\tau}},
\qquad
{\mechanical{v}}_F=\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_F}{{\mechanical{m}}_e}=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}} {\signal{k}}_F}{{\mechanical{m}}_e}
=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}}{{\mechanical{m}}_e}(3\pi^2{\mechanical{n}})^{1/3}.
$$

Using a classical thermal speed in ${\mechanical{\ell}}={\mechanical{v}}{\semiconductor{\tau}}$ would give the wrong scale for conduction electrons. For copper, ${\mechanical{v}}_F\approx1.57\times10^6\,\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$, about 13 times the classical thermal-speed estimate at room temperature used in the source.

With ${\semiconductor{\tau}}\approx2.5\times10^{-14}\,\unit{s}$, the mean free path is approximately $40\,\unit{nm}$. Pure samples can have much longer mean free paths at low temperature.

> [!note] Temperature of the estimate
> The source calls the 40 nm value a low-temperature mean free path. Its numerical value follows from the room-temperature relaxation time quoted just before it. A low-temperature value requires the relaxation time at that temperature.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{\ell}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Mean free path | Distance travelled over one relaxation time. |
| ${\mechanical{v}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | Particle speed, including the Fermi speed. |
| ${\semiconductor{\tau}}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Relaxation time | Mean time relevant to momentum relaxation. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | Magnitude of particle momentum. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | Mass of one particle. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to $h/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\signal{k}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | Magnitude or component of a wave vector. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
