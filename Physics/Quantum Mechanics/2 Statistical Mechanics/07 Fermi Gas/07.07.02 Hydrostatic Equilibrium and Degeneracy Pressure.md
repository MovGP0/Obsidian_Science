> [!TLDR]
> Nonrelativistic electron kinetic energy rises faster under compression than the gravitational binding energy.

Hydrostatic equilibrium balances gravity, which compresses the star, and electron degeneracy pressure. In a uniform-density approximation, the gravitational energy is

$$
{\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{grav}}=-\frac{3{\mechanical{G}}{\mechanical{M}}^2}{5{\mechanical{R}}}.
$$

For nonrelativistic degenerate electrons, the kinetic energy is

$$
{\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{kin}}=\frac35{\mechanical{N}}_e{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F
=\frac35{\mechanical{N}}_e\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}_e}(3\pi^2{\mechanical{n}}_e)^{2/3}.
$$

Since ${\mechanical{n}}_e={\mechanical{N}}_e/{\mechanical{V}}=3{\mechanical{N}}_e/(4\pi {\mechanical{R}}^3)$, the kinetic energy scales as ${\mechanical{R}}^{-2}$ at fixed electron number. Write

$$
{\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}}({\mechanical{R}})=\frac A{{\mechanical{R}}^2}-\frac B {\mechanical{R}},
$$

where $A,B>0$ are mathematical coefficients fixed by mass and composition. The equilibrium condition is

$$
\frac{d{\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}}}{d{\mechanical{R}}}=-\frac{2A}{{\mechanical{R}}^3}+\frac B{{\mechanical{R}}^2}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad {\mechanical{R}}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{2A}{B}. \tag{7.5}
$$

The second derivative at this radius is $2A/{\mechanical{R}}_{\mathrm{eq}}^4>0$, so it is a minimum. Within the nonrelativistic model there is an equilibrium radius for each mass. The model ceases to apply when the electron Fermi momentum becomes relativistic.
