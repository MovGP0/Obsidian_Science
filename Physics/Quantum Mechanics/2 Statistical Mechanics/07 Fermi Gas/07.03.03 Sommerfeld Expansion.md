> [!TLDR]
> A smooth energy weight can be expanded near the chemical potential to obtain even powers of temperature.

At low temperature, $-\partial {\mechanical{f}}/\partial{\energy{\varepsilon}}$ is strongly concentrated near ${\energy{\mu}}$, with width of order ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$. Integrate by parts, with $\Phi({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\int_0^{\energy{\varepsilon}}\phi({\energy{\varepsilon}}')d{\energy{\varepsilon}}'$:

$$
I=[\Phi({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{f}}]_0^\infty+
\int_0^\infty\Phi({\energy{\varepsilon}})\left(-\frac{\partial {\mechanical{f}}}{\partial{\energy{\varepsilon}}}\right)d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.
$$

The boundary term vanishes for the weights used here. Expand about ${\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{\mu}}$:

$$
\Phi({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\Phi({\energy{\mu}})+({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})\Phi'({\energy{\mu}})
+\frac{({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})^2}{2}\Phi''({\energy{\mu}})+\cdots.
$$

The narrow derivative kernel is symmetric after the lower limit is extended to minus infinity in the scaled variable. Odd moments vanish. The **Sommerfeld lemma** is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty\phi({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{f}}({\energy{\varepsilon}},{\thermal{T}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
&=\int_0^{\energy{\mu}}\phi({\energy{\varepsilon}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}\\
&\quad+\frac{\pi^2}{6}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^2\phi'({\energy{\mu}})
+\frac{7\pi^4}{360}({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^4\phi^{(3)}({\energy{\mu}})+O({\thermal{T}}^6).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.3}
$$

> [!note] Origin of the coefficient
> Put $t=({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. The normalized kernel is $e^t/(e^t+1)^2$, and $\int_{-\infty}^\infty t^2 e^t/(e^t+1)^2\,dt=\pi^2/3$. The Taylor factor $1/2$ gives $\pi^2/6$. The source mixes an energy derivative with $dt$; the expression here uses the dimensionless kernel.

The expansion requires ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\ll{\energy{\mu}}$ and a smooth weight near ${\energy{\mu}}$. The contribution from the remote lower endpoint is exponentially small under these conditions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\mechanical{f}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Mean occupation | Fermi–Dirac occupation of one complete single-particle state. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
