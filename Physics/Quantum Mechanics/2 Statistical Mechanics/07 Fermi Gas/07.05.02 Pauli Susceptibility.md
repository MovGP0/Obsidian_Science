> [!TLDR]
> The leading Pauli susceptibility is proportional to the density of states at the Fermi energy.

For ${\magnetic{\mu}}_B{\magnetic{B}}\ll{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F$, expand to first order in the field. At zero temperature,

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_\uparrow\approx\frac {\mechanical{n}}2-g_1({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F){\magnetic{\mu}}_B{\magnetic{B}},
\qquad
{\mechanical{n}}_\downarrow\approx\frac {\mechanical{n}}2+g_1({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F){\magnetic{\mu}}_B{\magnetic{B}}.
$$

Thus

$$
{\magnetic{\mathcal M}}=2{\magnetic{\mu}}_B^2{\magnetic{B}}\,g_1({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F)={\magnetic{\mu}}_B^2{\magnetic{B}}\,{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F).
$$

In SI units, using ${\magnetic{B}}\approx{\magnetic{\mu}}_0{\magnetic{H}}$ for the weak applied field,

$$
\boxed{{\magnetic{\chi}}_{\mathrm{Pauli}}=\left.\frac{\partial{\magnetic{\mathcal M}}}{\partial {\magnetic{H}}}\right|_{H\to0}
={\magnetic{\mu}}_0{\magnetic{\mu}}_B^2{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}_F)
=\frac{3{\mechanical{n}}{\magnetic{\mu}}_0{\magnetic{\mu}}_B^2}{2{\energy{\varepsilon}}_F}
=\frac{3{\mechanical{n}}{\magnetic{\mu}}_0{\magnetic{\mu}}_B^2}{2{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}_F}.}
$$

**Remark 7.2 — Comparison with Curie response.** For localized independent quantum spins $1/2$ with moment magnitude ${\magnetic{\mu}}_B$, ${\magnetic{\chi}}_{\mathrm{Curie}}={\mechanical{n}}{\magnetic{\mu}}_0{\magnetic{\mu}}_B^2/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. Therefore

$$
\frac{{\magnetic{\chi}}_{\mathrm{Pauli}}}{{\magnetic{\chi}}_{\mathrm{Curie}}}=\frac32\frac {\thermal{T}}{{\thermal{T}}_F}\ll1.
$$

> [!note] Source correction
> The source states a Curie expression with a factor 1/3, but then gives the ratio $9{\thermal{T}}/(2{\thermal{T}}_F)$. That factor 1/3 applies to freely oriented classical moments of fixed magnitude μB, rather than quantum spins 1/2. The comparison above uses the spin-1/2 convention of the previous chapter. With the source's classical-vector convention, its ratio 9/2 follows instead. The source also omits B in one intermediate expression for magnetization; it is retained here.

The leading Pauli susceptibility is independent of temperature. Only states near the Fermi surface can redistribute between spin channels. This is the spin response of the ideal gas; real metals can also have orbital and interaction contributions.
