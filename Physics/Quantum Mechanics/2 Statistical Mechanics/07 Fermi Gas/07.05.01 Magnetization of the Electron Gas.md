> [!TLDR]
> The electron Zeeman energy shifts opposite spin populations in opposite directions.

Apply a field $\mathbf {\magnetic{B}}={\magnetic{B}}\hat{\mathbf z}$. For electron spin projections $\pm1/2$, the Zeeman energy shifts are $\pm{\magnetic{\mu}}_B{\magnetic{B}}$, where ${\magnetic{\mu}}_B={\electric{e}}{\energy{\hbar}}/(2{\mechanical{m}}_e)$ is the Bohr magneton. Each spin population has the same unshifted density of states

$$
g_1({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\frac12{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\frac C2{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}.
$$

Here the integration variable is the unshifted kinetic energy, whose lower limit remains zero. The magnetic shift is placed in the occupation argument instead. Moving that shift into the integration variable would also move the lower limit; changing one without the other would count different states. Both branches share one chemical potential in thermal and particle equilibrium.

The populations per volume are

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_\uparrow=\int_0^\infty g_1({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{f}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}+{\magnetic{\mu}}_B{\magnetic{B}},{\thermal{T}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}},
\qquad
{\mechanical{n}}_\downarrow=\int_0^\infty g_1({\energy{\varepsilon}}){\mechanical{f}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\magnetic{\mu}}_B{\magnetic{B}},{\thermal{T}})\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}.
$$

The magnetization per volume is

$$
{\magnetic{\mathcal M}}={\magnetic{\mu}}_B({\mechanical{n}}_\downarrow-{\mechanical{n}}_\uparrow).
$$

The arrows label electron spin, not magnetic moment. For an electron these directions are opposite, which explains the signs in the populations and magnetization.

For a positive applied field, the down-spin branch has lower energy and therefore larger occupation at the same kinetic energy. Its magnetic moment points along the field, so the displayed magnetization is positive. At zero field the two populations agree and the magnetization vanishes. Adding their first-order changes cancels, as required when the total density is fixed; the chemical-potential change due to a weak field starts beyond linear order.

| ${\magnetic{B}}$ | $\unit{T}$ | Magnetic flux density | Applied magnetic field in the Zeeman energy. |

| ${\electric{e}}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Elementary charge | Positive magnitude of the electron charge. |

| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\mechanical{f}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Mean occupation | Fermi–Dirac occupation of one complete single-particle state. |
