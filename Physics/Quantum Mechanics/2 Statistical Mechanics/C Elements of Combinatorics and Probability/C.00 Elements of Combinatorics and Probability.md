> [!TLDR]
> Counting and probability connect microscopic configurations with entropy, fluctuations, and diffusion.

Statistical mechanics rests on combinatorics and probability. Counting the microstates compatible with a macrostate defines Boltzmann entropy. This appendix reviews elementary counting, introduces important discrete and continuous probability distributions, connects information entropy with thermodynamic entropy, and develops the basic theory of random walks.

## How to use this appendix

Start with the sample space: what distinguishes two microscopic configurations? Use the counting notes when the configurations can be listed in principle. Then specify their probabilities. Counting alone gives probabilities only if equal weights are justified by the physical model.

For independent binary choices, follow the binomial route. Take its rare-event limit for a finite mean count, or its Gaussian central limit when both outcome populations are large. These are different limits; a small mean count does not become Gaussian merely because the number of trials is large.

Next, separate two uses of entropy: the logarithm of a macrostate's multiplicity and the uncertainty of a distribution over microstates. The entropy notes explain when they agree. Finish with random walks to see how a microscopic independence assumption produces a macroscopic diffusion law. In each application, check whether interactions, a conservation constraint, or a boundary couples the events.
