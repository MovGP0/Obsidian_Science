> [!TLDR]
> Gaussian integrals give ideal-gas state counts, molecular-speed averages, and harmonic partition functions.

The same Gaussian identity answers three different physical questions. Choose the measure and the statistical model before inserting the integral formula:

- **Count available states:** [[A.08.01 Density of States of the Ideal Gas]] uses a momentum-space ball. Differentiate its enclosed state count to obtain a count per energy interval. The smooth result approximates a discrete spectrum.
- **Average a measured speed:** [[A.08.02 Integrals over the Maxwell–Boltzmann Distribution]] uses a radial velocity measure. Normalize the source's number-density distribution before forming a single-particle mean. This is a classical equilibrium prediction.
- **Normalize a thermal weight:** [[A.08.03 Harmonic Partition Functions and Phase-Space Integrals]] integrates over both position and momentum. A quadratic Hamiltonian gives Gaussian factors; independent modes give products of partition functions.

These constructions are related but not interchangeable. A density of states counts possibilities; an equilibrium weight tells how they are occupied. A partition function combines the two. No experimental data are supplied here, so the applications are deductions from their stated models.
