> [!TLDR]
> Entropy derivatives define the intensive thermodynamic variables.

## Proposition 3.2 — Fundamental thermodynamic relations

The statistical definitions of temperature, pressure, and chemical potential are:

$$
\frac1{\thermal{T}}
=\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}\right)_{V,N}
=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{\Omega}
\left(\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial {\energy{E}}}\right)_{V,N}.
\tag{3.2}
$$

$$
\frac {\mechanical{P}}{\thermal{T}}
=\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{E,N}
=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{\Omega}
\left(\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{E,N}.
\tag{3.3}
$$

$$
\frac{\energy{\mu}} {\thermal{T}}
=-\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{E,V}
=-\frac{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{\Omega}
\left(\frac{\partial\Omega}{\partial {\mechanical{N}}}\right)_{E,V}.
$$

> [!note] Remark 3.3
> These relations connect the statistical entropy with the fundamental thermodynamic relation. If two systems A and B exchange energy while remaining isolated as a pair, maximum total entropy requires $1/{\thermal{T}}_A=1/{\thermal{T}}_B$. They therefore have the same temperature, in agreement with the zeroth law.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The bulk thermodynamic pressure. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | The thermodynamic energy change per particle under the stated constraints. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | The total thermodynamic entropy or its stated statistical approximation. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system energy; subscripts identify levels or allocated energy contributions. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume of the gas or system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
