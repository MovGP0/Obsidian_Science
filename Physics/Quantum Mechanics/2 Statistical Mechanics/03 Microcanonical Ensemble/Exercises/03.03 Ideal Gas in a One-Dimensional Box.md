> [!TLDR]
> In one dimension, momentum counting gives E = NkᴮT/2 and a wall force satisfying FL = NkᴮT.

## Question — Exercise 3.3

Consider ${\mechanical{N}}$ particles of mass ${\mechanical{m}}$ in a one-dimensional box of length ${\mechanical{L}}$.

(a) Calculate the cumulative count and entropy.  
(b) Find the “pressure” ${\mechanical{F}}={\thermal{T}}(\partial {\thermal{S}}/\partial {\mechanical{L}})_{E,N}$, whose unit is force.  
(c) Derive the analogue of the ideal-gas equation of state.

> [!note] Solution origin and convention
> The question is translated from the supplied notes. The calculation is added. Use indistinguishable particles and the corrected cumulative-volume convention, consistent with the chapter's ideal-gas derivation.

## (a) Count states and obtain entropy

The positions give ${\mechanical{L}}^{\mechanical{N}}$. The constraint $\sum_i p_i^2\leq2{\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}}$ defines an N-dimensional momentum ball. Therefore:

$$
\Phi({\energy{E}},{\mechanical{L}},{\mechanical{N}})
=\frac{{\mechanical{L}}^{\mechanical{N}}}{{\mechanical{N}}!{\energy{h}}^{\mechanical{N}}}
\frac{\pi^{{\mechanical{N}}/2}(2{\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}})^{{\mechanical{N}}/2}}{\Gamma({\mechanical{N}}/2+1)}.
$$

**Why divide by the factorial?** Exchanging labels of identical particles does not give another physical state.

The exact volume entropy is ${\thermal{S}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Phi$. Applying Stirling's approximation gives:

$$
{\thermal{S}}\approx {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[
\ln\frac {\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{N}}+\frac12\ln\!\left(\frac{4\pi {\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{h}}^2}\right)+\frac32
\right].
$$

The logarithms combine to a dimensionless argument. The temperature derivative is:

$$
\frac1{\thermal{T}}=\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}\right)_{L,N}
=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{2{\energy{E}}},
\qquad {\energy{E}}=\frac12{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

## (b) Force

Only the position factor depends on length:

$$
{\mechanical{F}}={\thermal{T}}\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\mechanical{L}}}\right)_{E,N}
=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}{\mechanical{L}}.
$$

## (c) Equation of state

$$
{\mechanical{F}}{\mechanical{L}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

This is the one-dimensional counterpart of the pressure–volume law. The force is positive because increasing the available length increases the number of position states.

> [!note] Finite-system convention
> A narrow-shell entropy has an energy power ${\mechanical{N}}/2-1$ instead of ${\mechanical{N}}/2$. It gives a finite-system correction that disappears in the thermodynamic limit. The result above uses the cumulative-volume definition requested here.
