> [!TLDR]
> The ideal-gas density of states differs from the cumulative count by an energy factor whose entropy contribution is subextensive.

## Question — Exercise 3.1

Show that an ideal gas satisfies $\omega({\energy{E}})=[3{\mechanical{N}}/(2{\energy{E}})]\Phi({\energy{E}})$. Use this result to compare $\ln\omega({\energy{E}})$ and $\ln\Phi({\energy{E}})$ and explain why their difference does not change the thermodynamic limit.

> [!note] Solution origin
> The question is translated from the supplied notes. The calculation and explanations below are added worked steps.

## Calculation

**Why differentiate a power?** At fixed volume and particle count, all energy dependence is in one power:

$$
\Phi({\energy{E}})=C {\energy{E}}^{3{\mechanical{N}}/2}.
$$

The prefactor is independent of energy. Differentiate:

$$
\omega({\energy{E}})=\frac{\mathrm d\Phi}{\mathrm d{\energy{E}}}
=C\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}{\energy{E}}^{3{\mechanical{N}}/2-1}
=\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2{\energy{E}}}\Phi({\energy{E}}).
$$

**Why introduce a reference energy?** A density of states has the unit of inverse energy. Its logarithm must have a dimensionless argument. With a fixed energy scale ${\energy{E}}_*$:

$$
\ln[{\energy{E}}_*\omega({\energy{E}})]-\ln\Phi({\energy{E}})
=\ln\!\left(\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{E}}_*}{2{\energy{E}}}\right).
$$

For the shell convention, use $\Omega\approx\omega({\energy{E}})\delta$ instead, so that the corresponding entropy difference is ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln[3{\mechanical{N}}\delta/(2{\energy{E}})]$.

## Answer

The requested derivative identity holds exactly for this cumulative count. The logarithmic correction is subextensive under the usual energy-window scaling. At fixed energy per particle, ${\energy{E}}/{\mechanical{N}}$ is constant, and a fixed reference energy gives an order-one logarithmic difference. A correction of order $\ln {\mechanical{N}}$, as stated in the source, is also negligible compared with a leading entropy of order ${\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$.

The comparison concerns leading thermodynamic behavior. It does not identify the units of $\omega$ and $\Phi$, or make finite-system temperature conventions exactly identical.
