> [!TLDR]
> At the stated metallic electron density, the thermal wavelength is too large for a dilute classical-gas approximation at either temperature.

## Question — Exercise 3.6

Calculate the thermal de Broglie wavelength of an electron with ${\mechanical{m}}_e=9.109\times10^{-31}\,\unit{kg}$ at $300\,\unit{K}$ and $10^4\,\unit{K}$. Compare it with the typical electron separation for ${\mechanical{n}}_e\sim10^{28}\,\unit{m^{-3}}$. Explain why metallic electrons require quantum statistics.

> [!note] Solution origin
> The question is translated from the supplied notes. The numerical calculations and interpretation are added.

## Calculation

Use:

$$
{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}=\frac {\energy{h}}{\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}}_e {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}},
\qquad
{\energy{h}}=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\,\unit{J\,s},
\quad {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}=1.380649\times10^{-23}\,\unit{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

At ${\thermal{T}}=300\,\unit{K}$:

$$
{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}\approx4.304\times10^{-9}\,\unit{m}
=4.304\,\unit{nm}.
$$

**Why use a temperature ratio next?** The wavelength scales as the inverse square root of temperature:

$$
{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}(10^4\,\unit{K})
={\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}(300\,\unit{K})\sqrt{\frac{300}{10^4}}
\approx0.7454\,\unit{nm}.
$$

An average volume per electron is $1/{\mechanical{n}}_e$. Its cube root estimates the separation:

$$
{\mechanical{L}}={\mechanical{n}}_e^{-1/3}\approx4.642\times10^{-10}\,\unit{m}
=0.4642\,\unit{nm}.
$$

The degeneracy parameters are approximately:

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_e{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3\approx
\begin{cases}
7.97\times10^2,&{\thermal{T}}=300\,\unit{K},\\
4.14,&{\thermal{T}}=10^4\,\unit{K}.
\end{cases}
$$

## Answer

At both temperatures, the wavelength exceeds the stated typical separation, and the degeneracy parameter is not much smaller than one. Thus the dilute Maxwell–Boltzmann criterion fails.

Electrons are fermions and obey Fermi–Dirac statistics. Including their two spin states divides the occupation parameter by two; it still does not make either case a dilute classical gas at the density specified here.
