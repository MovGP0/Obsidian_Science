> [!TLDR]
> A two-level spin system has a heat-capacity maximum when its thermal energy is comparable to the level splitting.

## Question — Exercise 3.4

For the spin system in Example 3.1:

(a) Find the equilibrium energy as a function of temperature.  
(b) Calculate its heat capacity and show that it has a maximum at a finite temperature, the Schottky maximum.  
(c) Show $0\leq {\thermal{S}}\leq {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln2$ and interpret the limits.

> [!note] Solution origin
> The question is translated from the supplied notes. The following calculation is added. Use the current chapter's convention: the “+” population has lower energy. Take fixed nonzero field and positive temperature for the heat-capacity maximum.

## (a) Equilibrium energy

Introduce the dimensionless helper $x={\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})$. The population fractions are:

$$
f_+=\frac{e^x}{2\cosh x},\qquad
f_-=\frac{e^{-x}}{2\cosh x},\qquad
f_+-f_-=\tanh x.
$$

The energy is therefore:

$$
{\energy{E}}^*({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{N}},{\magnetic{B}})=-{\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}\tanh x.
$$

The lower-energy level dominates as temperature falls.

## (b) Heat capacity and its maximum

At fixed field, $\mathrm dx/\mathrm d{\thermal{T}}=-x/{\thermal{T}}$. Differentiate the energy:

$$
{\thermal{C}}_B=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{E}}^*}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{N,B}
={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\frac{x^2}{\cosh^2x}.
$$

To locate its maximum, differentiate the dimensionless factor:

$$
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}(x^2\operatorname{sech}^2x)
=2x\operatorname{sech}^2x(1-x\tanh x).
$$

The nonzero maximum satisfies $x\tanh x=1$. Numerically:

$$
x_{\max}\approx1.19968,\qquad
{\thermal{T}}_{\max}\approx0.83356\,\frac{{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}},
\qquad
{\thermal{C}}_{\max}\approx0.43923\,{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}.
$$

At both very low and very high positive temperatures, heat capacity tends to zero. At intermediate temperature, population transfer between the two levels is most sensitive to temperature.

## (c) Entropy and limiting cases

Substitute the fractions into Eq. (3.16):

$$
{\thermal{S}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\,[\ln(2\cosh x)-x\tanh x].
$$

Binary entropy lies between zero and $\ln2$ per spin, so:

$$
0\leq {\thermal{S}}\leq {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln2.
$$

For fixed nonzero field and ${\thermal{T}}\to0^+$, every spin occupies the lower level and entropy tends to zero. For ${\thermal{T}}\to\infty$, or ${\magnetic{B}}\to0$ at fixed positive temperature, both levels have equal populations and entropy tends to its maximum.

The zero-field and zero-temperature limits need not commute because the energy levels become degenerate when the field is zero.
