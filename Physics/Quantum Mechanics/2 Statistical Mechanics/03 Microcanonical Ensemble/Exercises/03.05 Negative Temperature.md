> [!TLDR]
> Population inversion makes entropy decrease with energy, giving negative absolute temperature.

## Question — Exercise 3.5

For the spin-½ system, show that $(\partial {\thermal{S}}/\partial {\energy{E}})=1/{\thermal{T}}<0$ when ${\energy{E}}>0$, meaning more antiparallel than parallel spins. Explain why a negative temperature is hotter than any positive temperature.

> [!note] Solution origin
> The question is translated from the supplied notes. The reasoning below is added.

## Calculation

The current chapter defines:

$$
{\energy{E}}=(n_--n_+){\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}},
\qquad
\frac1{\thermal{T}}=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{2{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}}\ln\frac{n_+}{n_-}.
$$

For positive energy, $n_->n_+$. The logarithm is negative, so temperature is negative. The energy spectrum is bounded:

$$
-{\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}\leq {\energy{E}}\leq {\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}.
$$

Entropy is greatest at zero energy, where both populations are equal. Adding energy above this point reduces the number of compatible arrangements.

## Why hotter?

Let system A have negative temperature and B have positive temperature. Transfer a small positive energy $\mathrm d{\energy{E}}$ from A to B:

$$
\mathrm d{\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{tot}}
=-\frac{\mathrm d{\energy{E}}}{{\thermal{T}}_A}+\frac{\mathrm d{\energy{E}}}{{\thermal{T}}_B}
=\mathrm d{\energy{E}}\left(\frac1{{\thermal{T}}_B}-\frac1{{\thermal{T}}_A}\right)>0.
$$

The entropy-increasing direction is energy flow **from the negative-temperature system to the positive-temperature system**. This is the sense in which negative temperature is hotter.

## Answer

The inverted population has ${\thermal{T}}<0$. It is not below absolute zero on a colder extension of the positive-temperature scale. On increasing energy, inverse temperature passes through zero at maximum entropy: temperature changes from $+\infty$ to $-\infty$.

A negative-temperature description requires a bounded accessible energy spectrum and suitable isolation of the relevant degrees of freedom.
