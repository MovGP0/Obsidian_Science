> [!TLDR]
> The thermodynamic limit increases particle count and volume at fixed density.

The thermodynamic limit is:

$$
{\mechanical{N}}\to\infty,\qquad {\mechanical{V}}\to\infty,\qquad \frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{n}}=\text{constant}.
$$

For ordinary bulk systems away from critical regimes, relative fluctuations of extensive quantities typically scale as ${\mechanical{N}}^{-1/2}$. They then become negligible. Ensemble averages approach thermodynamic values.

## Proposition 3.3 — Equivalence of ensembles

In the thermodynamic limit, systems with short-range interactions, away from complications at first-order phase transitions, give the same intensive bulk thermodynamic quantities in the microcanonical, canonical, and grand canonical descriptions at matching macroscopic conditions.

This result is central to thermodynamics. Within its conditions, the ensemble can be chosen for convenience without changing the bulk physical answer.

> [!note] Scope
> The equivalence is not a claim that all finite-system distributions or fluctuation amplitudes are identical. Constraints and phase transitions can matter, and long-range interactions require separate analysis.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume of the gas or system. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | The number of particles per unit volume. |
