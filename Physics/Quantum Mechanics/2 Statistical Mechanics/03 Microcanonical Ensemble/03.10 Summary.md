> [!TLDR]
> Microcanonical counting gives ideal-gas thermodynamics, explains the Gibbs correction, and sets the limits of classical equipartition.

The main results are:

1. The microcanonical ensemble assigns equal weight to accessible states on a constant-energy surface or within a narrow energy shell.
2. The statistical entropy ${\thermal{S}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Omega$ connects microscopic mechanics with thermodynamics. Its partial derivatives give ${\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}},{\energy{\mu}}$.
3. Ideal-gas counting gives ${\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$ and ${\energy{E}}=(3/2){\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$.
4. Identical-particle permutations must not be counted as new physical states. The correction $\Phi\mapsto\Phi/{\mechanical{N}}!$ removes the extensive Gibbs-paradox term.
5. Sackur–Tetrode entropy gives quantitative results for the dilute monatomic ideal gas.
6. Each independent active classical quadratic term contributes ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}/2$. Low-temperature quantum effects limit this result.

The next chapter studies the **canonical ensemble**, which describes a system in thermal contact with a reservoir at fixed temperature. Its partition function is often easier to calculate than a microcanonical state count. Under the conditions for ensemble equivalence, it gives the same bulk thermodynamic results.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | The total thermodynamic entropy or its stated statistical approximation. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The bulk thermodynamic pressure. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | The thermodynamic energy change per particle under the stated constraints. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system energy; subscripts identify levels or allocated energy contributions. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume of the gas or system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
