> [!TLDR]
> Omitting the indistinguishability correction gives the ideal-gas laws but makes entropy nonextensive.

Use the cumulative-volume convention ${\thermal{S}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Phi$. Its difference from a suitable shell entropy is subextensive:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\thermal{S}}
&={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Phi({\energy{E}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}})\\
&={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[
{\mechanical{N}}\ln {\mechanical{V}}+\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}\ln\!\left(\frac{2\pi {\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}}}{{\energy{h}}^2}\right)
-\ln\Gamma\!\left(\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}+1\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

For large ${\mechanical{N}}$:

$$
\ln\Gamma\!\left(\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}+1\right)
\approx\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}\ln\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}-\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}.
$$

Substitution gives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\thermal{S}}&\approx {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[
{\mechanical{N}}\ln {\mechanical{V}}+\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}\ln\!\left(\frac{2\pi {\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}}}{{\energy{h}}^2}\right)
-\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}\ln\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}+\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}
\right]\\
&={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[
\ln {\mechanical{V}}+\frac32\ln\!\left(\frac{4\pi {\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}}}{3{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{h}}^2}\right)+\frac32
\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
$$

> [!note] Logarithm convention
> The separate logarithms in this source derivation must be understood together. Their combined argument is dimensionless. A separated logarithm of volume or another dimensional quantity implicitly uses fixed reference units.

> [!note] Remark 3.4 — Extensivity problem
> Double the energy, volume, and particle count. Equation (3.7) gives ${\thermal{S}}(2{\energy{E}},2{\mechanical{V}},2{\mechanical{N}})=2{\thermal{S}}({\energy{E}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}})+2{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln2$, which exceeds twice the original entropy. The entropy is therefore not extensive. This is the Gibbs paradox developed in Section 3.5.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | The total thermodynamic entropy or its stated statistical approximation. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system energy; subscripts identify levels or allocated energy contributions. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume of the gas or system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Particle mass | The mass of an atom, molecule, or electron. |
| ${\energy{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | The elementary action scale in the state-counting measure. |
