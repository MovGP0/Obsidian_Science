> [!TLDR]
> Ideal-gas state counting gives both the mean thermal energy and the pressure–volume law.

From the temperature definition:

$$
\frac1{\thermal{T}}=\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}\right)_{V,N}
={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2{\energy{E}}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad {\energy{E}}=\frac32{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
\tag{3.8}
$$

Each translational quadratic degree of freedom contributes ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}/2$. Section 3.7 proves this equipartition result.

The volume dependence of Eq. (3.7) is contained in ${\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln {\mechanical{V}}$. Therefore:

$$
\frac {\mechanical{P}}{\thermal{T}}=\left(\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{E,N}
=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{\mechanical{V}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad {\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

The ideal-gas equation of state follows from counting microscopic states within this model and the statistical definitions of the thermodynamic quantities.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | The total thermodynamic entropy or its stated statistical approximation. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system energy; subscripts identify levels or allocated energy contributions. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume of the gas or system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The bulk thermodynamic pressure. |
