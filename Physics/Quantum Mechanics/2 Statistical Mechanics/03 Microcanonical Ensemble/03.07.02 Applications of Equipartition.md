> [!TLDR]
> Equipartition predicts the classical energies of monatomic gases, diatomic gases, and harmonic solids.

## Monatomic ideal gas

Each particle has three translational terms ${\mechanical{p}}_{i\alpha}^2/(2{\mechanical{m}})$. Therefore:

$$
\langle{\energy{\varepsilon}}\rangle=3\frac12{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}=\frac32{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}},
\qquad {\energy{E}}=\frac32{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

## Diatomic ideal gas

A rigid molecule has five active quadratic contributions: three translations and two rotations. An active vibration adds two more contributions, one kinetic and one potential:

$$
{\energy{E}}=
\begin{cases}
\frac52{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}},&\text{rigid diatomic gas},\\
\frac72{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}},&\text{diatomic gas with active vibration}.
\end{cases}
$$

## Classical harmonic solid — Dulong–Petit law

Model the solid as ${\mechanical{N}}$ three-dimensional harmonic oscillators:

$$
{\energy{\mathcal H}}_i=\frac{\mathbf {\mechanical{p}}_i^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}}
+\frac{{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2}{2}\mathbf {\mechanical{q}}_i^2.
$$

Each oscillator has six quadratic contributions. Hence:

$$
{\energy{E}}={\mechanical{N}}\cdot6\cdot\frac12{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}=3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}},
\qquad {\thermal{C}}_V=3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}.
$$

This is the Dulong–Petit law. It works for many solids at temperatures high enough to activate their lattice modes. At low temperature, the classical result fails and heat capacity decreases toward zero. Quantized oscillators give the Einstein model, with a single frequency ${\signal{\omega}}_E$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system energy; subscripts identify levels or allocated energy contributions. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | The energy of a spin or the mean energy per gas particle. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy function of the system. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Translational momentum | The momentum conjugate to a Cartesian position. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Particle mass | The mass of an atom, molecule, or electron. |
| ${\signal{\omega}}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | An oscillator frequency or a vibrational cutoff frequency. |
| ${\mechanical{q}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinate | A Cartesian position; generalized angles are listed separately. |
| ${\thermal{C}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity | The total heat capacity under the stated constraint. |
