> [!TLDR]
> The microcanonical distribution assigns equal weight to accessible states in a narrow energy interval.

For an ergodic isolated system, long-time averages of integrable observables equal their averages over the constant-energy surface ${\energy{\mathcal H}}(\Gamma)={\energy{E}}$ with its invariant measure. The source describes the trajectory as densely exploring that surface.

> [!note] Ergodicity clarification
> Dense coverage alone is not sufficient for equality of time and ensemble averages. Ergodicity concerns the invariant measure and applies to almost every initial state.

## Definition 3.2 — Microcanonical ensemble: shell version

The accessible energy interval is $[{\energy{E}}-{\energy{\delta}},{\energy{E}}]$, where ${\energy{\delta}}\ll {\energy{E}}$. Introduce the dimensionless phase-space measure:

$$
\mathrm d\gamma=\frac{\mathrm d^{6{\mechanical{N}}}\Gamma}{{\energy{h}}^{3{\mechanical{N}}}}.
$$

The dimensionless shell volume is:

$$
\Omega({\energy{E}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}};{\energy{\delta}})
=\int_{E-\delta\leq\mathcal H(\Gamma)\leq E}\mathrm d\gamma.
$$

The uniform density **with respect to this measure** is:

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{mc}}(\Gamma)=
\begin{cases}
1/\Omega({\energy{E}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}};{\energy{\delta}}),&{\energy{E}}-{\energy{\delta}}\leq{\energy{\mathcal H}}(\Gamma)\leq {\energy{E}},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

Thus $\int\rho_{\mathrm{mc}}\,\mathrm d\gamma=1$. The factor ${\energy{h}}^{3{\mechanical{N}}}$ expresses phase-space volume in elementary action cells and connects the counting rule with quantum mechanics.

> [!note] Density normalization and counting convention
> The source first normalizes a density against raw phase-space volume, then writes $1/\Omega$ for a shell count that already includes ${\energy{h}}^{-3{\mechanical{N}}}$. These are different density conventions. Against raw volume, the normalized density is $\rho_{\mathrm{mc}}/{\energy{h}}^{3{\mechanical{N}}}$. This chapter initially omits the factor $1/{\mechanical{N}}!$ to expose the Gibbs paradox; [[03.05.03 Resolution - Gibbs Correction Factor]] adds it.

> [!note] Remark 3.1 — Choice of energy width
> The width is macroscopically small but large compared with microscopic level spacings. Within this regime, changing the width changes ${\thermal{S}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Omega$ only by subextensive terms. A logarithm of a dimensional width means a ratio to a fixed reference energy. The leading entropy is of order ${\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$.
