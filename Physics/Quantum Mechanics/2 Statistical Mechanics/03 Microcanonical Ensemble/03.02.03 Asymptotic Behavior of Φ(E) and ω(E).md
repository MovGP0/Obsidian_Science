> [!TLDR]
> A large power of energy makes the cumulative count and density of states rise rapidly near the upper energy boundary.

For an ideal monatomic gas:

$$
\Phi({\energy{E}})=C {\energy{E}}^\alpha,\qquad
\omega({\energy{E}})=\alpha C {\energy{E}}^{\alpha-1},\qquad
\alpha=\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}.
$$

Hence $\Phi({\energy{E}})=({\energy{E}}/\alpha)\omega({\energy{E}})$. Their logarithms differ only by the logarithm of this energy factor, after a reference energy makes the comparison dimensionless.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system energy; subscripts identify levels or allocated energy contributions. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
| $\omega({\energy{E}})$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | The energy derivative of the dimensionless cumulative count. |
