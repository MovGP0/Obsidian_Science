> [!TLDR]
> A distinguishable-particle count predicts an entropy increase when two identical gases are combined, although the macrostate does not change.

Consider a container of volume $2{\mechanical{V}}$ split into two equal parts. Each part contains ${\mechanical{N}}$ molecules of the same gas, at temperature ${\thermal{T}}$ and energy ${\energy{E}}$. Remove the partition.

1. **Thermodynamic answer.** The gas and its macroscopic conditions are identical on both sides. Removing the partition changes no bulk state variable, so the thermodynamic mixing entropy is $\Delta {\thermal{S}}=0$.

2. **Answer from Eq. (3.7).** Use ${\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tot}}=2{\energy{E}}$, ${\mechanical{V}}_{\mathrm{tot}}=2{\mechanical{V}}$, and ${\mechanical{N}}_{\mathrm{tot}}=2{\mechanical{N}}$:

   $$
   {\thermal{S}}(2{\energy{E}},2{\mechanical{V}},2{\mechanical{N}})-2{\thermal{S}}({\energy{E}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}})=2{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln2>0.
   $$

This contradiction is the **Gibbs paradox**. The uncorrected count predicts an entropy increase even for identical gases.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume of the gas or system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system energy; subscripts identify levels or allocated energy contributions. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | The total thermodynamic entropy or its stated statistical approximation. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
