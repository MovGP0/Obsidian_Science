> [!TLDR]
> The microcanonical ensemble describes an isolated system and connects microscopic state counting with thermodynamics.

The first ensemble that we study in detail is the **microcanonical ensemble**. It describes an isolated system with fixed total energy, volume, and particle count. These quantities can be specified to any required macroscopic precision.

This simple setting contains the basic structure of equilibrium statistical mechanics: a statistical definition of entropy, temperature as an entropy derivative, and the equation of state. It also exposes the Gibbs paradox. That paradox shows a limitation of counting identical particles as distinguishable and points toward the quantum description of matter.

The statistical question is which equilibrium averages follow from these constraints when the exact microscopic state is unknown. Isolation fixes the conserved quantities. It does not by itself prove that all accessible states have equal probability or that the system reaches equilibrium. Those statistical assumptions enter explicitly in the ensemble construction.

Read [[03.01 Isolated System and the Phase-Space Hypershell]] for the probability measure, then [[03.02 Density of States and Phase-Space Volume]] for the distinction between a shell count and a cumulative count. Follow [[03.03 Microcanonical Entropy]] and [[03.04 Classical Ideal Gas in the Microcanonical Ensemble]] to obtain thermodynamic derivatives. Finally, use [[03.05 Gibbs Paradox]] to check whether the counting rule gives extensive entropy before applying [[03.06 Sackur–Tetrode Formula]].

A useful test throughout this route is to ask which statements are exact for a finite system and which retain only the leading term as the particle count increases.
