> [!TLDR]
> A degree of freedom stops following classical equipartition when thermal energy is small compared with its quantum level spacing.

Classical equipartition fails when ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$ is comparable to or smaller than the relevant quantum spacing ${\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}$.

- **Molecular vibrations:** excitation is suppressed when ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\ll{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}_{\mathrm{vib}}$. Characteristic vibrational temperatures are often several thousand kelvin.
- **Molecular rotations:** excitation is suppressed when ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\ll{\energy{\hbar}}^2/(2{\mechanical{I}})$. The source quotes a rotational scale near $85\,\unit{K}$ for hydrogen and much smaller scales, sometimes below $1\,\unit{K}$, for heavy molecules.
- **Lattice vibrations:** the Dulong–Petit result fails below the Debye scale ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D={\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}_D/{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$, commonly of order $100$–$1000\,\unit{K}$.

The Einstein model (1907) and Debye model (1912) address these effects by quantizing vibrational modes.

> [!note]
> “Freezing out” means that thermal occupation of excited modes becomes small. It does not mean that all motion or quantum ground-state fluctuations stop.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The thermodynamic temperature. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Planck constant divided by 2π. |
| ${\signal{\omega}}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | An oscillator frequency or a vibrational cutoff frequency. |
| ${\mechanical{I}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia | The rotational inertia of a rigid molecule. |
| ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D$ | $\unit{K}$ | Debye temperature | The thermal scale set by the lattice-vibration cutoff. |
