> [!TLDR]
> Microcanonical constraints fix total energy, volume, and particle count, while local observables can still fluctuate.

In the exact-energy microcanonical description, $({\energy{E}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}})$ are fixed. Total energy fluctuations are therefore zero. A finite-width shell instead permits only the specified small energy tolerance.

Other quantities, such as local pressure, have nonzero fluctuations. For an ideal gas, the source gives the coarse local scaling:

$$
\langle(\delta {\mechanical{P}})^2\rangle\propto\frac1{\mechanical{v}},
\qquad {\mechanical{v}}\ll {\mechanical{V}}.
$$

Here ${\mechanical{v}}$ is a small observation volume. As the observation region grows to a macroscopic fraction of the system, relative fluctuations typically decrease as ${\mechanical{N}}^{-1/2}$ and vanish in the thermodynamic limit.

> [!note] Measurement scale
> A local pressure variance also depends on its spatial and temporal averaging prescription. The displayed expression is a scaling statement, not a complete formula for an instantaneous point pressure.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system energy; subscripts identify levels or allocated energy contributions. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume of the gas or system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle count | The number of particles, spins, or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The bulk thermodynamic pressure. |
| ${\mechanical{v}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Observation volume | The local volume over which pressure is averaged. |
