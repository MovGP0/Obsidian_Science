> [!TLDR]
> Spin populations, harmonic oscillators, argon entropy, and molecular rotations illustrate microcanonical counting.

## Example 3.1 — Spin-½ system in a magnetic field

Consider ${\mechanical{N}}$ noninteracting spin-½ degrees of freedom in an external field ${\magnetic{B}}$. Use energies ${\energy{\varepsilon}}_\pm=\mp{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}$. The population ${\mechanical{n}}_+$ is parallel to the field and has the lower energy; ${\mechanical{n}}_-={\mechanical{N}}-{\mechanical{n}}_+$ is antiparallel.

> [!note] Sign convention
> This chapter labels the lower-energy level “+”. Chapter 2's example labels the upper-energy level “+”. The physics is unchanged, but the population labels must not be carried between the two conventions without adjustment.

The total energy and populations are:

$$
{\energy{E}}={\mechanical{n}}_+(-{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}})+{\mechanical{n}}_-({\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}})
=-(2{\mechanical{n}}_+-{\mechanical{N}}){\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}.
\tag{3.14}
$$

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_+=\frac {\mechanical{N}}2-\frac {\energy{E}}{2{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}},
\qquad
{\mechanical{n}}_-=\frac {\mechanical{N}}2+\frac {\energy{E}}{2{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}}.
\tag{3.15}
$$

### Count the states and calculate entropy

$$
\Omega({\energy{E}},{\mechanical{N}})=\binom {\mechanical{N}}{{\mechanical{n}}_+}=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}!}{{\mechanical{n}}_+!{\mechanical{n}}_-!}.
$$

Stirling's approximation gives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\thermal{S}}&={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Omega\\
&\approx {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}[{\mechanical{N}}\ln {\mechanical{N}}-{\mechanical{n}}_+\ln {\mechanical{n}}_+-{\mechanical{n}}_-\ln {\mechanical{n}}_-]\\
&=-{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[
\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_+}{\mechanical{N}}\ln\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_+}{\mechanical{N}}+\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_-}{\mechanical{N}}\ln\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_-}{\mechanical{N}}
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

With $f_\pm={\mechanical{n}}_\pm/{\mechanical{N}}$:

$$
{\thermal{S}}=-{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}[f_+\ln f_++f_-\ln f_-].
\tag{3.16}
$$

### Temperature

$$
\frac1{\thermal{T}}
=\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}
=\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\mechanical{n}}_+}\frac{\partial {\mechanical{n}}_+}{\partial {\energy{E}}}
=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{2{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}}\ln\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_+}{{\mechanical{n}}_-}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_+}{{\mechanical{n}}_-}
=\exp\!\left(\frac{2{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}\right).
\tag{3.17}
$$

At positive temperature, the lower-energy level is more populated. The energy separation is $2{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}$, as required by the Boltzmann distribution.

### Magnetic response

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_+}{\mechanical{N}}=
\frac{e^{{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})}}
{e^{{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})}+e^{-{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}/({\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}})}}.
$$

The source's total magnetization is:

$$
{\magnetic{\mathcal M}}={\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}({\mechanical{n}}_+-{\mechanical{n}}_-)
={\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}\tanh\!\left(\frac{{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}\right).
$$

This ${\magnetic{\mathcal M}}$ is a **total magnetic moment**, rather than magnetic moment per unit volume. In a weak field, ${\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}\ll {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}$:

$$
{\magnetic{\mathcal M}}\approx\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}^2{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

The corresponding susceptibility follows Curie's law, ${\magnetic{\chi}}\propto1/{\thermal{T}}$; its unit depends on the magnetic-response convention.

## Example 3.2 — Distinguishable one-dimensional harmonic oscillators

Consider ${\mechanical{N}}$ classical oscillators, for example fixed at different lattice sites:

$$
{\energy{\mathcal H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\mechanical{N}}
\left(\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_i^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}}+\frac{{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2}{2}{\mechanical{q}}_i^2\right).
$$

The energy constraint defines an ellipsoid in $2{\mechanical{N}}$-dimensional phase space. Rescale:

$$
\mathsf{P}_i=\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_i}{\sqrt{2{\mechanical{m}}}},
\qquad \mathsf{Q}_i={\mechanical{q}}_i\sqrt{\frac{{\mechanical{m}}{\signal{\omega}}^2}{2}}.
$$

Then ${\energy{\mathcal H}}=\sum_i(\mathsf{P}_i^2+\mathsf{Q}_i^2)$, and the accessible region is a ball of radius $\sqrt {\energy{E}}$. The Jacobian is $(2/{\signal{\omega}})^{\mechanical{N}}$ because $\mathrm d{\mechanical{q}}_i\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{p}}_i=(2/{\signal{\omega}})\,\mathrm d\mathsf{Q}_i\,\mathrm d\mathsf{P}_i$.

The oscillators are distinguishable, so there is no extra Gibbs division by ${\mechanical{N}}!$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi({\energy{E}})
&=\frac1{{\energy{h}}^{\mechanical{N}}}\int_{\mathcal H\leq E}\mathrm d^{\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{q}}\,\mathrm d^{\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{p}}\\
&=\frac{(2/{\signal{\omega}})^{\mechanical{N}}}{{\energy{h}}^{\mechanical{N}}}
\frac{\pi^{\mechanical{N}}}{\Gamma({\mechanical{N}}+1)}{\energy{E}}^{\mechanical{N}}\\
&=\frac1{{\mechanical{N}}!}\left(\frac {\energy{E}}{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}\right)^{\mechanical{N}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The factorial here comes from the ball volume. It is not an indistinguishability correction.

Using Stirling's approximation and neglecting logarithmic terms:

$$
{\thermal{S}}={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\Phi
\approx {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[\ln\!\left(\frac {\energy{E}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}\right)+1\right].
$$

Thus:

$$
\frac1{\thermal{T}}=\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{\energy{E}},
\qquad {\energy{E}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}},
\qquad {\thermal{C}}_V={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}.
$$

Each oscillator has one kinetic and one potential quadratic term. A solid with three oscillator coordinates per atom instead gives ${\thermal{C}}_V=3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$.

## Example 3.3 — Numerical entropy of argon

Consider one mole of argon, with atomic mass $39.95\,\unit{u}$, at ${\thermal{T}}=300\,\unit{K}$ and ${\mechanical{P}}=1\,\unit{atm}\approx1.013\times10^5\,\unit{Pa}$.

The source's rounded numerical data are:

$$
{\mechanical{m}}\approx6.632\times10^{-26}\,\unit{kg},
\qquad {\mechanical{N}}=(1\,\unit{mol}){\mechanical{N}}_A\approx6.022\times10^{23},
$$

$$
{\mechanical{V}}=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}{\mechanical{P}}
\approx\frac{6.022\times10^{23}\times1.381\times10^{-23}\times300}
{1.013\times10^5}\,\unit{m^3}
\approx2.463\times10^{-2}\,\unit{m^3}.
$$

> [!note] Particle-count units
> The source writes ${\mechanical{N}}={\mechanical{N}}_A$ with the unit of inverse moles. Particle count is dimensionless. Multiplication of the Avogadro constant by the one-mole amount gives the count used here.

### Thermal wavelength

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}
&=\frac {\energy{h}}{\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}}\\
&\approx
\frac{6.626\times10^{-34}}
{\sqrt{2\pi(6.632\times10^{-26})(1.381\times10^{-23})(300)}}\,\unit{m}\\
&\approx\frac{6.626\times10^{-34}}{4.155\times10^{-23}}\,\unit{m}\\
&\approx1.595\times10^{-11}\,\unit{m}
=0.160\,\unit{\mathring A}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Degeneracy parameter and entropy

$$
{\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3
=\frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3
\approx(2.44\times10^{25})(4.06\times10^{-33})
\approx9.9\times10^{-8}\ll1.
$$

The gas is well within the classical regime.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac {\thermal{S}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}
&=\ln\!\left(\frac1{{\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3}\right)+\frac52\\
&\approx\ln(1.01\times10^7)+2.5\\
&=16.13+2.5=18.63.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, for this one-mole sample:

$$
{\thermal{S}}_m=18.63{\mechanical{N}}_A{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}=18.63{\thermal{R}}\approx154.9\,\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}},
\qquad {\thermal{S}}\approx154.9\,\unit{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

> [!note] Entropy units and rounding
> The source gives the molar unit for ${\thermal{S}}$ in the last step. Total entropy and molar entropy have the same numerical value for a one-mole sample, but different units. Its quoted experimental comparison is ${\thermal{S}}_{m,\mathrm{exp}}\approx154.8\,\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ at the stated conditions. Both values are source-rounded data.

The agreement illustrated by this calculation was important in testing statistical mechanics and relating thermodynamic measurements to the action constant. Sackur and Tetrode made such calculations in 1912, before the modern formulation of quantum mechanics.

## Example 3.4 — Rigid diatomic gas

Consider ${\mechanical{N}}$ rigid diatomic molecules of moment of inertia ${\mechanical{I}}$ in volume ${\mechanical{V}}$. Each molecule has three translational and two rotational degrees of freedom. Use polar angles $\theta\in[0,\pi]$, ${\mechanical{\phi}}\in[0,2\pi]$ with conjugate angular momenta ${\mechanical{p}}_\theta,{\mechanical{p}}_{\mechanical{\phi}}$:

$$
{\energy{h}}_i=\frac{\mathbf {\mechanical{p}}_i^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}}
+\frac1{2{\mechanical{I}}}\left({\mechanical{p}}_{\theta_i}^2+\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_{\phi_i}^2}{\sin^2\theta_i}\right).
$$

The translational state count at allocated energy ${\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ is:

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{V}}^{\mechanical{N}}}{{\energy{h}}^{3{\mechanical{N}}}}
\frac{\pi^{3{\mechanical{N}}/2}(2{\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{tr}})^{3{\mechanical{N}}/2}}{\Gamma(3{\mechanical{N}}/2+1)}.
$$

For the rotational contribution, integrate over $(\theta,{\mechanical{\phi}},{\mechanical{p}}_\theta,{\mechanical{p}}_{\mechanical{\phi}})$ subject to:

$$
{\mechanical{p}}_\theta^2+\frac{{\mechanical{p}}_{\mechanical{\phi}}^2}{\sin^2\theta}\leq2{\mechanical{I}} {\energy{E}}_{\mathrm{rot}}.
$$

Convolution over the energy shared between translation and rotation gives:

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{di}}({\energy{E}})
=\frac{{\mechanical{V}}^{\mechanical{N}}}{{\mechanical{N}}!{\energy{h}}^{5{\mechanical{N}}}}
\frac{(8\pi^2 {\mechanical{I}})^{\mechanical{N}}\pi^{3{\mechanical{N}}/2}}{\Gamma(5{\mechanical{N}}/2+1)}
(2{\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}})^{3{\mechanical{N}}/2}{\energy{E}}^{\mechanical{N}}.
$$

Using Stirling's approximation:

$$
{\thermal{S}}\approx {\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[
\ln\frac {\mechanical{V}}{\mechanical{N}}
+\frac32\ln\!\left(\frac{4\pi {\mechanical{m}}{\energy{E}}}{5{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{h}}^2}\right)
+\ln\!\left(\frac{16\pi^2 {\mechanical{I}} {\energy{E}}}{5{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{h}}^2}\right)
+\frac72
\right].
$$

The combined logarithm is dimensionless. Differentiation gives:

$$
\frac1{\thermal{T}}=\frac{\partial {\thermal{S}}}{\partial {\energy{E}}}=\frac{5{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}}{2{\energy{E}}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad {\energy{E}}=\frac52{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

> [!note] Molecular symmetry
> The source formula uses rotational symmetry number one, appropriate when the two ends of a linear molecule are distinguishable. A homonuclear molecule needs a rotational symmetry correction. This changes the entropy constant but not the classical equipartition energy.
