> [!TLDR]
> Statistical mechanics connects microscopic laws with macroscopic thermodynamic properties. This text develops the tools for equilibrium systems, quantum statistics, and phase transitions.

Statistical mechanics is one of the foundations of modern physics. It provides the conceptual and mathematical connection between the microscopic description of physical systems and their observable macroscopic properties. Classical mechanics and quantum mechanics determine the microscopic description. Thermodynamics studies the macroscopic properties.

This text aims to give a systematic, rigorous, and accessible explanation of the foundations of equilibrium statistical mechanics. Its structure follows an established approach in university teaching. It starts with a review of thermodynamics to establish the terminology and reference quantities. It then introduces the basic concepts of phase space, statistical entropy, and ensembles.

The treatment of the microcanonical, canonical, and grand canonical ensembles covers both the formal principles and their practical applications. Worked examples and exercises support each step. The text then moves to quantum statistics. Chapters on the Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein distributions explain their main physical consequences. These applications range from the free electron gas to Bose–Einstein condensation, and from blackbody radiation to the Debye model of solids.

The final part of the book studies phase transitions. It covers the van der Waals equation, the Ising model, Landau theory, and the basic principles of the renormalization group.

The appendices summarize the main mathematical tools used in the text.

The presentation keeps mathematical rigor and physical intuition together. It explains the reason for each important result and derives each formula through its main steps. Many figures help the reader visualize the concepts.
