> [!TLDR]
> The excited states have a finite capacity at fixed temperature, so excess particles form a condensate.

The key question is whether the excited states can hold the specified atom number at a given temperature. Start with the critical-temperature derivation, which tests their maximum capacity. Then use the condensate note to distribute the fixed total number between the ground state and the excited cloud. Finally read the transition note to distinguish a smooth onset of condensate population from a discontinuity in a thermodynamic derivative.

These results use a uniform, three-dimensional ideal gas in the thermodynamic limit. A finite cloud rounds the onset, and a confining potential changes the state count. Thus the power in a condensate-fraction law is a property of the spectrum and geometry; Bose statistics alone does not fix it.
