> [!TLDR]
> The ideal uniform Bose gas has a continuous heat capacity with a cusp at the condensation temperature.

## Condensed phase

Differentiate Equation (8.6) at fixed ${\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}}$. Since ${\energy{U}}\propto {\thermal{T}}^{5/2}$,

$$
{\thermal{C}}_V^{(<)}=\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{U}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{V,N}
=\frac{15}{4}{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B\frac{\zeta(5/2)}{\zeta(3/2)}\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^{3/2}.
$$

Thus ${\thermal{C}}_V\propto {\thermal{T}}^{3/2}$ at low temperature.

## Normal phase

Differentiate ${\energy{U}}=(3/2){\mechanical{V}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}g_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}})/{\signal{\lambda}}^3$ while enforcing ${\mechanical{N}}={\mechanical{V}}g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}})/{\signal{\lambda}}^3$. The number constraint gives

$$
\frac1{\mechanical{z}}\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{z}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{V,N}
=-\frac{3}{2{\thermal{T}}}\frac{g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}})}{g_{1/2}({\mechanical{z}})}.
$$

Use ${\mechanical{z}}\,dg_a/d{\mechanical{z}}=g_{a-1}$ to obtain

$$
{\thermal{C}}_V^{(>)}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B\left[\frac{15}{4}\frac{g_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}})}{g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}})}
-\frac94\frac{g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}})}{g_{1/2}({\mechanical{z}})}\right].
\tag{8.7}
$$

## Critical point

As ${\mechanical{z}}\to1^-$, $g_{1/2}({\mechanical{z}})\to\infty$, so the second term vanishes. Both one-sided limits agree:

$$
{\thermal{C}}_V({\thermal{T}}_c^+)={\thermal{C}}_V({\thermal{T}}_c^-)=\frac{15}{4}{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B\frac{\zeta(5/2)}{\zeta(3/2)}
\approx1.926{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B.
$$

The value is continuous, but the slopes differ. At high temperature, ${\thermal{C}}_V\to3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B/2$, the classical result.

**Fig. 8.2** Heat capacity at fixed volume and particle number for the ideal uniform Bose gas. Both branches meet at Cᵥ/(NkB) ≈ 1.926. The normal-phase curve uses Bose functions and the fixed-density number equation; it approaches 3/2 at high temperature.

> [!note] Heat capacity and specific heat
> These formulas give total heat capacity in joules per kelvin. Divide by particle number, mass, or amount of substance to obtain the corresponding specific or molar quantity.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{C}}_V$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity | Temperature derivative of energy at fixed volume and particle number. |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Total energy of the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature at which macroscopic ground-state occupation begins. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\mechanical{z}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Fugacity | Equal to $e^{\beta\mu}$. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal wavelength | Thermal de Broglie wavelength of one massive particle. |
