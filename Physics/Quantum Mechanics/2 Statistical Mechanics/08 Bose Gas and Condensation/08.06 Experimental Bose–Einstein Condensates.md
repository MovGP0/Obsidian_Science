> [!TLDR]
> Experiments use laser cooling and evaporative cooling to produce a coherent condensate at very low temperature.

The experimental study of Bose–Einstein condensation connects the ideal-gas model to trapped atomic gases. The following articles describe the history, the cooling methods, and the properties of the condensate.

The experimental question is more specific than making atoms cold: the gas must reach high occupation of a quantum state while remaining a dilute trapped cloud. Read the cooling methods before comparing a measured onset temperature with an ideal-gas prediction. A trap changes the density of states, while interactions change the density profile and excitation response.

Use separate observations for separate claims. A narrow component in a released cloud supports a condensate population analysis; interference probes coherence, and a flow experiment probes superfluid response. None alone proves every property of an ideal uniform gas. The source history establishes the experimental context; the theory provides models whose finite-size, trapping, and interaction assumptions must be tested for the particular cloud.
