> [!TLDR]
> The Debye temperature sets the temperature scale for the vibration heat capacity of a material.

Table 8.1 gives typical Debye temperatures for several crystal materials.

**Table 8.1** Debye temperatures ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D$ of selected crystal materials.

| Material | ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D$ ($\unit{K}$) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead (Pb) | 105 | Soft metal |
| Gold (Au) | 170 | Noble metal |
| Copper (Cu) | 343 | Common metal |
| Aluminium (Al) | 428 | Light metal |
| Iron (Fe) | 470 | Transition metal |
| Silicon (Si) | 645 | Semiconductor |
| Diamond (C) | 2230 | Strong covalent bonds |

> [!note] Remark 8.6 — Cubic-law temperature range
> The low-temperature ${\thermal{T}}^3$ approximation applies well for ${\thermal{T}}\lesssim{\thermal{\Theta}}_D/20$. For copper, ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D=343\,\unit{K}$ gives ${\thermal{T}}\lesssim17\,\unit{K}$. Experiments confirm the cubic phonon contribution in many metals and insulating crystals. In metals, distinguish this phonon contribution from the electronic heat capacity.

Use the table through the ratio of sample temperature to Debye temperature. The same laboratory temperature can be low on the diamond scale and high on the lead scale. Thus a room-temperature comparison must not assign all listed solids to the same asymptotic regime merely because their temperatures are equal.

These are typical source values, not exact universal constants or uncertainty-bounded measurements. A Debye temperature summarizes a spectrum within a model; a value inferred from low-temperature heat capacity need not represent every part of the real spectrum equally well. Keep the stated conservative cubic-law criterion separate from a specified numerical error tolerance. [[08.04 Accuracy of the Debye T³ Law]] calculates such a tolerance within the ideal model and distinguishes it from agreement with a real crystal.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D$ | $\unit{K}$ | Debye temperature | Debye cutoff energy divided by the Boltzmann constant. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
