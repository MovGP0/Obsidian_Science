> [!TLDR]
> Bose functions express the continuum integrals for the number and pressure of an ideal Bose gas.

The number of particles in excited continuum states is

$$
{\mechanical{N}}_{\mathrm{ex}}=\int_0^\infty {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})\frac{d{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{e^{{\thermal{\beta}}({\energy{\varepsilon}}-{\energy{\mu}})}-1}.
$$

For a free three-dimensional gas with one internal component, the **total** density of states is

$$
{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\frac{{\mechanical{V}}}{4\pi^2}\left(\frac{2{\mechanical{m}}}{{\energy{\hbar}}^2}\right)^{3/2}{\energy{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}
=\frac{{\mechanical{V}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}^3}\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}\frac{\sqrt{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})^{3/2}},
\qquad {\signal{\lambda}}=\frac{{\energy{h}}}{\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}}.
$$

Put $x={\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}$. Then

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{N}}_{\mathrm{ex}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}=\frac{1}{{\signal{\lambda}}^3}\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}
\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{1/2}}{{\mechanical{z}}^{-1}e^x-1}\,dx
=\frac{g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}})}{{\signal{\lambda}}^3}.
$$

**Definition 8.1 — Bose functions.** For real $0<{\mechanical{z}}<1$ and order $a>0$, define

$$
g_a({\mechanical{z}})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a)}\int_0^\infty\frac{x^{a-1}}{{\mechanical{z}}^{-1}e^x-1}\,dx
=\sum_{\ell=1}^\infty\frac{{\mechanical{z}}^\ell}{\ell^a}.
\tag{8.1}
$$

The source uses $n$ for the mathematical order; $a$ here prevents confusion with particle density. The series defines the analytic continuation for $|{\mechanical{z}}|<1$. On the boundary $|{\mechanical{z}}|=1$, convergence requires care: at ${\mechanical{z}}=1$ it converges only for $a>1$.

Useful properties are

- $g_a({\mechanical{z}})\sim {\mechanical{z}}$ as ${\mechanical{z}}\to0$: the classical limit.
- $g_a(1)=\zeta(a)$ for $a>1$.
- $g_{3/2}(1)=\zeta(3/2)\approx2.612$.
- $g_1({\mechanical{z}})=-\ln(1-{\mechanical{z}})$.
- ${\mechanical{z}}\,dg_a({\mechanical{z}})/d{\mechanical{z}}=g_{a-1}({\mechanical{z}})$ wherever the derivative is defined.

**Remark 8.2.** For $a\le1$, $g_a(1)$ diverges. This matters for compressibility and heat capacity near condensation.

> [!note] State-counting convention
> Here ${\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})$ includes volume; it counts states per energy. Chapter 7 used a density per volume. The continuum integral counts excited states. Below the critical temperature, add the separate ground-state population: ${\mechanical{N}}={\mechanical{N}}_0+{\mechanical{N}}_{\mathrm{ex}}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\energy{g(\varepsilon)}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | Total number of states per energy interval, including internal degeneracy. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | Mass of one particle. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to ${\energy{h}}/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\energy{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | Relates frequency to quantum energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
| ${\mechanical{z}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Fugacity | Equal to $e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}}$. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal wavelength | Thermal de Broglie wavelength of one massive particle. |
