> [!TLDR]
> An ideal uniform three-dimensional Bose gas condenses when its excited-state number reaches its maximum.

The function $g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}})$ increases for $0\le {\mechanical{z}}\le1$ and reaches

$$
g_{3/2}(1)=\zeta(3/2)\approx2.612.
$$

At fixed density ${\mechanical{n}}$, cooling increases ${\signal{\lambda}}\propto {\thermal{T}}^{-1/2}$ and therefore increases ${\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}^3$. The normal-phase equation cannot hold once ${\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}^3>\zeta(3/2)$.

**Theorem 8.1 — BEC critical temperature.** For an ideal uniform three-dimensional gas with one internal component, particle mass ${\mechanical{m}}$, and density ${\mechanical{n}}$, the critical temperature is

$$
{\thermal{T}}_c=\frac{2\pi{\energy{\hbar}}^2}{{\mechanical{m}}{\thermal{k}}_B}\left(\frac{{\mechanical{n}}}{\zeta(3/2)}\right)^{2/3}
\approx3.31\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2{\mechanical{n}}^{2/3}}{{\mechanical{m}}{\thermal{k}}_B}.
\tag{8.4}
$$

**Proof.** At the transition, ${\mechanical{z}}\to1$ and ${\energy{\mu}}\to0$. Substitute ${\signal{\lambda}}_c={\energy{h}}/\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}_c}$ in ${\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_c^3=\zeta(3/2)$ and solve for ${\thermal{T}}_c$.

**Remark 8.3.** The scale is ${\thermal{T}}_c\propto {\mechanical{n}}^{2/3}/{\mechanical{m}}$: a denser gas or a smaller particle mass gives a higher critical temperature. For liquid helium-4, ${\mechanical{n}}\approx2.2\times10^{28}\,\unit{m^{-3}}$ and ${\mechanical{m}}\approx4\,\unit{u}$ give about $3.1\,\unit{K}$. The observed lambda transition is near $2.17\,\unit{K}$. Liquid helium has strong interactions, so the ideal-gas estimate supplies a scale, not a quantitative model.

The continuum treatment itself remains useful below the transition for the excited states. What fails is the attempt to put all particles into that integral without a separate ground-state term.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature at which macroscopic ground-state occupation begins. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | Mass of one particle. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to ${\energy{h}}/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\energy{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | Relates frequency to quantum energy. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal wavelength | Thermal de Broglie wavelength of one massive particle. |
| ${\mechanical{z}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Fugacity | Equal to $e^{\beta{\energy{\mu}}}$. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
