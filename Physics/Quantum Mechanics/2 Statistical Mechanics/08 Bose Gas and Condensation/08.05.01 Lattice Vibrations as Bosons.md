> [!TLDR]
> Phonons are bosonic vibration quanta with zero chemical potential in thermal equilibrium.

In a crystal with ${\mechanical{N}}$ atoms, the normal vibration modes are harmonic oscillators. Their excitation quanta are called **phonons**. Phonons are bosonic quasiparticles. A three-dimensional crystal has $3{\mechanical{N}}$ normal vibration modes.

For small wave vectors, acoustic phonons have the linear dispersion relation

$$
{\signal{\omega}}={\mechanical{v}}_s|{\signal{\mathbf k}}|,
$$

where ${\mechanical{v}}_s$ is the sound speed.

As for photons in thermal equilibrium, the chemical potential of phonons is zero:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}=0.
$$

The phonon number is not conserved. Lattice anharmonicity permits processes that create or destroy phonons.

> [!note] Terminology
> A normal mode is an oscillator. A phonon is one quantum of excitation of that oscillator. The Debye model represents the crystal modes by an acoustic continuum with an upper frequency limit.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Atom number | Number of atoms in the crystal. |
| ${\signal{\omega}}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | Angular frequency of a mode or trap. |
| ${\mechanical{v}}_s$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Sound speed | Effective acoustic speed in the Debye model. |
| ${\signal{k}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | Magnitude or component of a wave vector. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Controls the mean particle number. |
