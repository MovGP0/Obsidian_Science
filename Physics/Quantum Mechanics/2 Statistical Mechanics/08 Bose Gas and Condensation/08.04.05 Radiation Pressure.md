> [!TLDR]
> Isotropic blackbody radiation exerts pressure equal to one third of its total energy density.

Photons have the ultrarelativistic dispersion relation

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{\hbar}} {\signal{c}}{\signal{k}}.
$$

For blackbody radiation, the relation between internal energy, pressure, and volume is

$$
{\energy{U}}=3{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}},
$$

instead of the nonrelativistic ideal-gas relation ${\energy{U}}=3{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}/2$. Thus, the radiation pressure is

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{rad}}=\frac{{\energy{u}}_{\mathrm{tot}}}{3}
=\frac{{\energy{a}}_R{\thermal{T}}^4}{3}.
$$

Radiation pressure is important in astrophysics, especially in very massive stars, and in cosmology.

## Remark 8.5 — Dispersion and pressure

The relation

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{{\energy{U}}}{3{\mechanical{V}}}
$$

follows directly from the linear dispersion relation ${\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{\hbar}} {\signal{c}}{\signal{k}}$. It applies to an isotropic ideal gas of ultrarelativistic particles.

For nonrelativistic particles with

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}^2{\signal{k}}^2}{2{\mechanical{m}}},
$$

the corresponding relation is

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{2{\energy{U}}}{3{\mechanical{V}}}.
$$

See [[08.04.03 Stefan–Boltzmann Law]] for the temperature dependence of the radiation energy density.

## Momentum-flux interpretation

Pressure measures momentum crossing a surface per time and area. In an isotropic field, the squared directional cosine has average one third. For a photon, energy equals momentum magnitude times speed; the same directional average therefore gives one third of energy density as pressure. This is the origin of the factor, in addition to the thermodynamic derivation.

The units are consistent because energy per volume is a pascal. Isotropy is essential: a directed beam has a different normal momentum flux, and reflection changes the momentum transferred to a wall. Thus the blackbody-cavity relation must not be used as a universal formula for force from every laser beam. The comparison with the quadratic dispersion also shows that Bose statistics does not by itself set the ratio of pressure to energy density.
