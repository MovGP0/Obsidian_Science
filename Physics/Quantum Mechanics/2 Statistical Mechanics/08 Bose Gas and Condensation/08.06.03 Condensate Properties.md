> [!TLDR]
> A condensate has a characteristic density profile and phase coherence; condensation and superfluidity describe different properties.

A Bose–Einstein condensate in a harmonic trap has the following properties.

## Density profile

For an ideal gas, the condensate has the Gaussian density profile of the harmonic-oscillator ground state. With repulsive interactions in the Thomas–Fermi approximation, the density has a parabolic profile within the condensate.

## Phase coherence

A single macroscopic field describes the condensate:

$$
{\signal{\Psi}}({\mechanical{\mathbf r}})=\sqrt{{\mechanical{n}}_0({\mechanical{\mathbf r}})}\,e^{i{\signal{\phi}}({\mechanical{\mathbf r}})}.
$$

Here ${\mechanical{n}}_0$ is the local condensate density and ${\signal{\phi}}$ is its phase. The source connects phase coherence to the interference fringes observed when two condensates overlap, and cites Ketterle's experiments in 1997.

## Superfluidity

Superfluidity is closely related to condensation, but the two terms do not have the same meaning. Condensation describes macroscopic occupation of one quantum state. Superfluidity describes a flow response with no dissipation under the appropriate conditions.

> [!note] Superfluidity: source clarification
> The source links repulsive interactions directly to superfluidity below the condensation temperature. Repulsion alone does not establish this conclusion. The excitation spectrum and the flow conditions must also satisfy a stability condition, such as the Landau criterion. Superfluidity must not be defined only as zero ordinary viscosity.

> [!note] Helium-4
> Helium-4 is a boson. The source gives its superfluid transition temperature as ${\thermal{T}}_\lambda=2.17\,\unit{K}$. Strong interactions prevent a direct quantitative comparison with the ideal Bose gas. The source states that only about 10% of its atoms are in the condensate at ${\thermal{T}}=0$, compared with 100% for the ideal gas. The condensate fraction and the superfluid fraction are different quantities.

| ${\signal{\Psi}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3/2}}$ | Condensate field | Macroscopic field whose squared magnitude is condensate number density. |
