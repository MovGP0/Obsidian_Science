> [!TLDR]
> The Einstein model gives an exponential decrease in heat capacity; the Debye acoustic spectrum gives the observed cubic low-temperature law.

Einstein proposed the first quantum model of a solid in 1907. In this model, all $3{\mechanical{N}}$ oscillators have the same angular frequency ${\signal{\omega}}_E$. Define the Einstein temperature by

$$
{\thermal{\Theta}}_E=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}_E}{{\thermal{k}}_B}.
$$

The energy is

$$
{\energy{U}}_E=3{\mechanical{N}}{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}_E
\left(\frac12+\frac{1}{e^{{\thermal{\Theta}}_E/{\thermal{T}}}-1}\right).
$$

Its constant-volume heat capacity is

$$
{\thermal{C}}_V^E=3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B\left(\frac{{\thermal{\Theta}}_E}{{\thermal{T}}}\right)^2
\frac{e^{{\thermal{\Theta}}_E/{\thermal{T}}}}{(e^{{\thermal{\Theta}}_E/{\thermal{T}}}-1)^2}.
$$

At high temperature, this expression approaches $3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B$, as does the Debye model. At low temperature,

$$
{\thermal{C}}_V^E\sim3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B\left(\frac{{\thermal{\Theta}}_E}{{\thermal{T}}}\right)^2e^{-{\thermal{\Theta}}_E/{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

The exponential term suppresses the Einstein heat capacity much faster than the Debye cubic law. A single nonzero oscillator frequency cannot reproduce the low-frequency acoustic modes that determine the experimental low-temperature heat capacity.

> [!note] Precision of the low-temperature limit
> The source describes the decrease as proportional to $e^{-{\thermal{\Theta}}_E/{\thermal{T}}}$. The full leading expression also contains the prefactor $({\thermal{\Theta}}_E/{\thermal{T}})^2$, as shown above.

**Fig. 8.4** Debye and Einstein heat capacities with ΘE = 0.83 ΘD. The full Debye integral approaches the classical value smoothly from below; it does not reach it abruptly, as the source drawing suggests. At low temperature Debye gives a T³ law, while Einstein gives exponential suppression. The dotted cubic curve is valid only near the origin.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Atom number | Number of atoms in the crystal. |
| ${\signal{\omega}}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | Angular frequency of a mode or trap. |
| ${\thermal{\Theta}}_E$ | $\unit{K}$ | Einstein temperature | Einstein oscillator energy divided by the Boltzmann constant. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to $h/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Total vibrational energy, including zero-point energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{C}}_V$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity | Temperature derivative of energy at fixed volume and particle number. |
