> [!TLDR]
> Ideal uniform Bose condensation is continuous: the condensate fraction starts at zero and there is no latent heat.

The order parameter ${\mechanical{N}}_0/{\mechanical{N}}$ grows continuously below ${\thermal{T}}_c$:

- Above ${\thermal{T}}_c$, ${\mechanical{N}}_0/{\mechanical{N}}=0$: the normal phase.
- Below ${\thermal{T}}_c$, ${\mechanical{N}}_0/{\mechanical{N}}>0$: the condensed phase.

Pressure and particle number are continuous across the transition. The heat capacity ${\thermal{C}}_V$ is continuous, but its temperature derivative has a discontinuity at ${\thermal{T}}_c$. There is no latent heat.

In the Ehrenfest classification, the uniform ideal gas has a **third-order transition**, because the third derivative of the relevant thermodynamic free energy is the first discontinuous derivative along this fixed-density path. Modern terminology calls it a **continuous phase transition**.

> [!note] A trap changes the result
> For an ideal gas in an isotropic harmonic three-dimensional trap, the density of states is different. In its continuum limit, ${\mechanical{N}}_0/{\mechanical{N}}=1-({\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_c)^3$, rather than the uniform-gas exponent $3/2$. Do not transfer the uniform-gas heat-capacity profile to a trapped gas.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature at which macroscopic ground-state occupation begins. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
| ${\thermal{C}}_V$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity | Temperature derivative of energy at fixed volume and particle number. |
