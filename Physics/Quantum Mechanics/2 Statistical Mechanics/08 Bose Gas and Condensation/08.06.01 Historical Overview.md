> [!TLDR]
> The source describes the first atomic condensates in rubidium and sodium in 1995 and the 2001 Nobel Prize.

Einstein predicted Bose–Einstein condensation in 1924–1925, from Bose's work on photon statistics. For about 70 years, the prediction remained mainly a mathematical result. In ordinary atomic gases, the gas would become a solid before it reached the conditions required for condensation.

The development of magneto-optical traps (MOTs) in the 1980s was an important step. These traps made it possible to cool atomic gases to temperatures in the microkelvin range. Additional cooling was required to reach condensation.

The source records the following sequence:

1. On 5 June 1995, the groups of Cornell and Wieman at JILA in Boulder produced the first atomic Bose–Einstein condensate in a gas of rubidium-87. Its temperature was approximately ${\thermal{T}}=170\,\unit{nK}$.
2. In September 1995, Ketterle's group at MIT produced a condensate of sodium-23.
3. Cornell, Wieman, and Ketterle shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2001.
