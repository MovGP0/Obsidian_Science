> [!TLDR]
> The grand partition function gives Bose-gas thermodynamics through the fugacity and Bose functions.

The physical task is to find equilibrium populations when energy and atoms can pass between a small subsystem and a reservoir. The grand canonical ensemble assigns a weight to each occupation configuration. Noninteracting particles make the energy additive, so the sum over configurations separates into one geometric sum per single-particle state. This factorization is the reason to use this ensemble here; it is not an extra interaction model.

Read the distribution first, then the density-of-states integral, and finally eliminate fugacity to obtain measurable pressure at specified density and temperature. In the dilute limit the result must reduce to the classical ideal-gas law. Near condensation, keep the single ground state separate from the continuum: a state with negligible weight in the density-of-states integral can still contain a macroscopic number of atoms.
