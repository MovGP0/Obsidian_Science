> [!TLDR]
> Laser cooling removes atomic motion first; evaporative cooling then removes the most energetic atoms and lowers the temperature further.

The source describes two main cooling stages.

## Laser cooling: Doppler cooling

A laser beam has a frequency slightly below the atomic transition frequency:

$$
{\signal{\omega}}_L<{\signal{\omega}}_0.
$$

An atom that moves toward this beam sees a higher frequency because of the Doppler effect. It therefore absorbs photons more readily. The momentum of an absorbed photon slows the atom. The atom emits another photon in a random direction. Over many events, this gives a force that opposes the atomic velocity:

$$
{\mechanical{\mathbf F}}=-{\mechanical{\alpha}}{\mechanical{\mathbf v}}.
$$

This damping process is called **optical molasses**. Six beams, in three pairs along perpendicular axes, produce three-dimensional optical molasses.

In the stated two-level Doppler-cooling model, the limiting temperature is

$$
{\thermal{T}}_D=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\Gamma}}}{2{\thermal{k}}_B},
$$

where ${\signal{\Gamma}}$ is the natural linewidth in the angular-frequency convention. For rubidium-87, the source gives

$$
{\thermal{T}}_D\approx146\,\unit{\mu K}.
$$

## Evaporative cooling

A trap holds the atoms while the most energetic atoms leave. The trap energy barrier is lowered, and collisions between the remaining atoms establish a new thermal distribution at a lower temperature. This process can lower the temperature from hundreds of microkelvin to the nanokelvin range.

> [!note] Trap mechanism: source clarification
> A magnetic trap confines atoms through an inhomogeneous magnetic field. An optical dipole trap confines atoms through the interaction with a spatially varying light field. The source groups these traps together under magnetic confinement; that description applies only to the magnetic trap.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\signal{\omega}}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | Angular frequency of a mode or trap. |
| ${\mechanical{\mathbf F}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | Damping force on a moving atom. |
| ${\mechanical{\alpha}}$ | $\unit{kg\,s^{-1}}$ | Damping coefficient | Force per speed in the optical-molasses model. |
