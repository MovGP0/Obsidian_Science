> [!TLDR]
> The photon mode density and the Planck occupation give the blackbody energy spectrum.

The photon density of states includes the two physical polarizations:

$$
{\signal{g}}({\signal{\omega}})\,d{\signal{\omega}}=
\frac{{\mechanical{V}}}{\pi^2{\signal{c}}^3}{\signal{\omega}}^2\,d{\signal{\omega}}.
$$

Each photon contributes the energy ${\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}$. Thus, the energy per volume and angular-frequency interval is

$$
{\energy{u}}({\signal{\omega}},{\thermal{T}})=\frac{1}{{\mechanical{V}}}\frac{d{\energy{U}}}{d{\signal{\omega}}}
=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}}{\pi^2{\signal{c}}^3}
\frac{{\signal{\omega}}^3}{e^{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1}.
$$

In terms of frequency in cycles per second,

$$
{\signal{\nu}}=\frac{{\signal{\omega}}}{2\pi},\qquad d{\signal{\omega}}=2\pi\,d{\signal{\nu}}.
$$

The corresponding spectrum is

$$
{\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})=\frac{8\pi {\energy{h}}}{{\signal{c}}^3}
\frac{{\signal{\nu}}^3}{e^{{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1}.
\tag{8.9}
$$

The angular-frequency and frequency spectra describe the same energy:

$$
{\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})\,d{\signal{\nu}}={\energy{u}}({\signal{\omega}},{\thermal{T}})\,d{\signal{\omega}}.
$$

> [!note] Spectrum units
> The spectrum per angular frequency has unit $\unit{J\,s\,m^{-3}}$. The spectrum per frequency has unit $\unit{J\,m^{-3}\,Hz^{-1}}$. The factor $2\pi$ in the change of variable is required; the two spectral functions do not have the same numerical value.

Fig. 8.3 shows the Planck spectrum at three temperatures. Each curve is divided by its own maximum to make the change in peak position clear. As temperature increases, the maximum moves to higher frequency. The Wien approximation applies at high frequencies.

**Fig. 8.3** Planck spectra at three temperatures, each divided by its own maximum. The peak moves to higher frequency as temperature rises. The gray curve is the high-frequency Wien approximation at T₁, using the same normalization as that Planck curve. Peak spectral density scales as T³; integrated energy density scales as T⁴. Peak normalization hides the amplitude change.

> [!note] Source clarification — temperature scaling
> The source caption states that the absolute intensity grows with the fourth power of temperature. This scaling applies to the integrated radiation energy density and blackbody radiant exitance. The peak height of the spectrum per frequency or angular frequency grows with the third power of temperature. Dividing each curve by its own maximum removes this change in height from Fig. 8.3.

The integral of this spectrum gives [[08.04.03 Stefan–Boltzmann Law]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\signal{g}}({\signal{\omega}})$ | $\unit{s}$ | Mode density | Number of modes per angular-frequency interval. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\signal{\omega}}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | Angular frequency of a mode or trap. |
| ${\signal{c}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed of light | Propagation speed of light in vacuum. |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Total energy of the gas. |
| ${\energy{u}}({\signal{\omega}},{\thermal{T}})$ | $\unit{J\,s\,m^{-3}}$ | Angular-frequency spectrum | Energy per volume and angular-frequency interval. |
| ${\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})$ | $\unit{J\,m^{-3}\,Hz^{-1}}$ | Spectral energy density | Energy per volume and frequency interval. |
| ${\signal{\nu}}$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Frequency | Frequency in cycles per second. |
| ${\energy{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | Relates frequency to quantum energy. |
| ${\energy{\hbar}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Equal to ${\energy{h}}/(2\pi)$. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
