> [!TLDR]
> Bose functions give the energy, pressure, heat capacity, and entropy on both sides of condensation.

Use these articles in the order energy, pressure, heat capacity, then entropy. The energy–pressure relation tests the quadratic free-particle dispersion. The pressure note then asks how adding atoms changes the condensed gas. Heat capacity requires a further choice: temperature changes at fixed atom number, so fugacity must change in the normal phase. Entropy supplies an independent check on the zero-temperature limit.

Keep the control variables fixed when comparing formulas. Below condensation, the excited population is set by temperature and volume while the ground state takes the remaining atoms. Above it, the number equation fixes fugacity. Confusing these two constraints gives an incorrect temperature derivative even when the energy formula itself is correct.
