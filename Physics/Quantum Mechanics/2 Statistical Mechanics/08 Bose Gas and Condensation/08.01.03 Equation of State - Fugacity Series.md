> [!TLDR]
> The number and pressure equations give a parametric equation of state; Bose statistics reduces pressure in the dilute limit.

Let ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{V}}$. In the normal phase, where the macroscopic ground-state term is absent, the number equation is

$$
{\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}^3=g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}}).
\tag{8.2}
$$

The pressure equation is

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{P}}{\signal{\lambda}}^3}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}=g_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}).
\tag{8.3}
$$

To derive the pressure, expand the logarithm of the grand partition function:

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}=\ln\Xi
=-\sum_k\ln(1-{\mechanical{z}}e^{-{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}_k}).
$$

Use $-\ln(1-x)=\sum_{\ell=1}^\infty x^\ell/\ell$ and replace the state sum by the continuum integral:

$$
{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\sum_{\ell=1}^\infty\frac{{\mechanical{z}}^\ell}{\ell}
\int_0^\infty {\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})e^{-\ell{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}\,d{\energy{\varepsilon}}
=\frac{{\mechanical{V}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}^3}g_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}).
$$

Eliminate ${\mechanical{z}}$ between Equations (8.2) and (8.3) to obtain ${\mechanical{P}}={\mechanical{P}}({\mechanical{n}},{\thermal{T}})$.

## Virial expansion

For ${\mechanical{z}}\ll1$,

$$
{\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}^3={\mechanical{z}}+\frac{{\mechanical{z}}^2}{2^{3/2}}+\frac{{\mechanical{z}}^3}{3^{3/2}}+\cdots,
$$

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{P}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}=\frac1{{\signal{\lambda}}^3}\left({\mechanical{z}}+\frac{{\mechanical{z}}^2}{2^{5/2}}+\frac{{\mechanical{z}}^3}{3^{5/2}}+\cdots\right).
$$

Invert the first series and substitute it into the second:

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{P}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}={\mechanical{n}}\left[1-\frac{{\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}^3}{2^{5/2}}
-\left(\frac{2}{3^{5/2}}-\frac18\right){\mechanical{n}}^2{\signal{\lambda}}^6+\cdots\right].
$$

The leading Bose correction is negative. It acts like a statistical attraction, although the ideal particles do not interact. The leading Fermi correction has the opposite sign.

> [!note] Condensed phase
> Below ${\thermal{T}}_c$, Equation (8.2) applies to ${\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{ex}}$, not the total density. Use ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{n}}_0+{\signal{\lambda}}^{-3}\zeta(3/2)$. Equation (8.3) remains valid at ${\mechanical{z}}=1$ in the thermodynamic limit.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Number of particles per volume. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Total or mean number of particles. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of a three-dimensional system. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal wavelength | Thermal de Broglie wavelength of one massive particle. |
| ${\mechanical{z}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Fugacity | Equal to $e^{{\thermal{\beta}}\mu}$. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\energy{g(\varepsilon)}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of states | Total number of states per energy interval, including internal degeneracy. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
| ${\thermal{\beta}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | Equal to $1/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature at which macroscopic ground-state occupation begins. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_0$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Condensate density | Local number density of condensed particles. |
