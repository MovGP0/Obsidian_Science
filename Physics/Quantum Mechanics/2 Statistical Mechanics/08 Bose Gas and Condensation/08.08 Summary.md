> [!TLDR]
> Bose statistics explain condensation, black-body radiation, and phonon heat capacity through the occupation of quantum modes.

This chapter develops the ideal Bose gas and two important applications. Its main results are:

1. **Bose functions.** The thermodynamic quantities are expressed through

   $$
   g_n({\mechanical{z}})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{{\mechanical{z}}^\ell}{\ell^n}.
   $$

   For a uniform three-dimensional gas, the parametric equation of state is

   $$
   {\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}^3=g_{3/2}({\mechanical{z}}),\qquad
   \frac{{\mechanical{P}}{\signal{\lambda}}^3}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}=g_{5/2}({\mechanical{z}}).
   $$

2. **Bose–Einstein condensation.** The critical temperature is

   $$
   {\thermal{T}}_c=\frac{2\pi{\energy{\hbar}}^2}{{\mechanical{m}}{\thermal{k}}_B}
   \left(\frac{{\mechanical{n}}}{\zeta(3/2)}\right)^{2/3}.
   $$

   Below this temperature, the condensate fraction is

   $$
   \frac{{\mechanical{N}}_0}{{\mechanical{N}}}=1-\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^{3/2}.
   $$

3. **Heat capacity.** The heat capacity is continuous at ${\thermal{T}}_c$, with a cusp in its temperature dependence. It satisfies ${\thermal{C}}_V\propto {\thermal{T}}^{3/2}$ below ${\thermal{T}}_c$ and approaches $3{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B/2$ at high temperature.
4. **Photons.** Photons have chemical potential ${\energy{\mu}}=0$. Their Planck distribution gives the black-body spectrum. The Stefan–Boltzmann law ${\energy{j^*}}={\energy{\sigma}} {\thermal{T}}^4$ and the frequency form of Wien's law, ${\signal{\nu}}_{\max}\propto {\thermal{T}}$, follow from this spectrum.
5. **Phonons.** The Debye model has a mode density $g_D({\signal{\omega}})\propto{\signal{\omega}}^2$ up to the cutoff frequency ${\signal{\omega}}_D$. Its low-temperature heat capacity follows the observed ${\thermal{T}}^3$ law. The Einstein model instead gives exponential suppression and does not describe this low-temperature behavior.
6. **Experiments.** The source describes the first atomic condensates produced in 1995 by Cornell, Wieman, and Ketterle in ultracold rubidium and sodium gases. These experiments confirmed Einstein's prediction.

**Table 8.2** Comparison of the bosonic systems studied in this chapter. The Bose-gas chemical potential is measured relative to its zero ground-state energy; equality is the bulk condensation limit.

| System | Particles | Chemical potential | Dispersion relation | Main result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ideal Bose gas | Atoms | ${\energy{\mu}}\leq0$ | ${\energy{\varepsilon}}\propto {\signal{k}}^2$ | Condensation at ${\thermal{T}}_c$ |
| Black-body radiation | Photons | ${\energy{\mu}}=0$ | ${\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{\hbar}} {\signal{c}}{\signal{k}}$ | Planck's law |
| Debye model | Phonons | ${\energy{\mu}}=0$ | ${\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{\hbar}} {\mechanical{v}}_s{\signal{k}}$ | ${\thermal{T}}^3$ heat-capacity law |
