## Question — Exercise 8.4

For a crystal with Debye temperature ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D=300\,\unit{K}$, estimate the temperature below which the Debye ${\thermal{T}}^3$ law is accurate to within 5%.

The supplied hint asks for an expansion of the Debye function to the next order in ${\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{\Theta}}_D$.

> [!note] Correction to the source hint
> At low temperature, the correction to the Debye ${\thermal{T}}^3$ law comes from the finite upper integration limit. It is exponentially small in ${\thermal{\Theta}}_D/{\thermal{T}}$, multiplied by a polynomial. It is not a next ordinary power of ${\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{\Theta}}_D$. We evaluate this cutoff correction instead.

## Calculation

### Define the error and the exact model

We use the relative error with respect to the exact Debye heat capacity:

$$
\frac{{\thermal{C}}_V^{({\thermal{T}}^3)}-{\thermal{C}}_V^{\mathrm{exact}}}
{{\thermal{C}}_V^{\mathrm{exact}}}\leq0.05.
$$

The approximation is greater than the exact value because it extends a positive integral to infinity. Define the dimensionless mathematical variable $y={\thermal{\Theta}}_D/{\thermal{T}}$ and the integral

$$
I(y)=\int_0^y\frac{x^4e^x}{(e^x-1)^2}\,\mathrm dx.
$$

The two heat capacities are

$$
{\thermal{C}}_V^{\mathrm{exact}}=9{\mechanical{N}} {\thermal{k}}_B\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{\Theta}}_D}\right)^3I(y),
$$

$$
{\thermal{C}}_V^{({\thermal{T}}^3)}=\frac{12\pi^4}{5}{\mechanical{N}} {\thermal{k}}_B\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{\Theta}}_D}\right)^3,
\qquad I(\infty)=\frac{4\pi^4}{15}.
$$

### Express the error as an omitted tail

Let the mathematical tail integral be

$$
R(y)=\int_y^\infty\frac{x^4e^x}{(e^x-1)^2}\,\mathrm dx.
$$

Then $I(y)=4\pi^4/15-R(y)$, and the relative error is

$$
\frac{R(y)}{4\pi^4/15-R(y)}.
$$

To calculate the small tail, use the convergent series

$$
\frac{e^x}{(e^x-1)^2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^\infty\ell e^{-\ell x}.
$$

Integrate each term. Repeated integration by parts gives

$$
R(y)=\sum_{\ell=1}^\infty e^{-\ell y}
\left(y^4+\frac{4y^3}{\ell}+\frac{12y^2}{\ell^2}
+\frac{24y}{\ell^3}+\frac{24}{\ell^4}\right).
$$

The leading term is

$$
R(y)\approx e^{-y}(y^4+4y^3+12y^2+24y+24).
$$

This form explains why a power expansion is not the correct low-temperature correction.

### Find the 5% boundary

At the error boundary,

$$
\frac{R(y)}{4\pi^4/15-R(y)}=0.05
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R(y)=\frac{0.05}{1.05}\frac{4\pi^4}{15}.
$$

Numerical solution of the convergent tail series gives

$$
y\approx9.10463,\qquad
{\thermal{T}}=\frac{300\,\unit{K}}{9.10463}\approx32.95\,\unit{K}.
$$

## Solution and scope

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{T}}\lesssim33\,\unit{K}\approx0.110{\thermal{\Theta}}_D.}
$$

This estimate compares the ${\thermal{T}}^3$ approximation with the exact ideal Debye model. Real crystals can have additional corrections from their actual phonon dispersion, electronic heat capacity, and other excitations.

The common condition ${\thermal{T}}\lesssim{\thermal{\Theta}}_D/20=15\,\unit{K}$ is more conservative. At this temperature the cutoff error is only about $1.57\times10^{-5}$, or 0.00157%, in the ideal Debye model.
