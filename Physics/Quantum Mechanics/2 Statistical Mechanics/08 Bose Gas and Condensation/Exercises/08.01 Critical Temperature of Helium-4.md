## Question — Exercise 8.1

Calculate the critical Bose–Einstein condensation temperature for a helium-4 gas with particle mass ${\mechanical{m}}=4.003\,\unit{u}$ and number density ${\mechanical{n}}=2.18\times10^{28}\,\unit{m^{-3}}$. Compare the result with the lambda-transition temperature ${\thermal{T}}_\lambda=2.17\,\unit{K}$ and discuss the role of interactions.

## Calculation

### Select the ideal-gas relation

For a homogeneous three-dimensional ideal Bose gas with one internal state, the excited states reach their maximum particle capacity when

$$
{\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_c^3=\zeta(3/2),\qquad
{\signal{\lambda}}_c=\frac{{\energy{h}}}{\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}_c}}.
$$

We use this condition because the remaining particles must then enter the ground state. Solve for the critical temperature:

$$
{\thermal{T}}_c=\frac{2\pi{\energy{\hbar}}^2}{{\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}}_B}
\left(\frac{{\mechanical{n}}}{\zeta(3/2)}\right)^{2/3}.
$$

### Convert the atomic mass to SI units

The atomic mass constant is ${\mechanical{m}}_u=1.66053907\times10^{-27}\,\unit{kg}$. Thus

$$
{\mechanical{m}}=4.003{\mechanical{m}}_u=6.64714\times10^{-27}\,\unit{kg}.
$$

With $\zeta(3/2)=2.61237535$, ${\energy{\hbar}}=1.05457182\times10^{-34}\,\unit{J\,s}$, and ${\thermal{k}}_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\,\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\thermal{T}}_c
&=\frac{2\pi(1.05457182\times10^{-34})^2}
{(6.64714\times10^{-27})(1.380649\times10^{-23})}
\left(\frac{2.18\times10^{28}}{2.61237535}\right)^{2/3}\,\unit{K}\\
&\approx3.13253\,\unit{K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Compare the temperatures

$$
{\thermal{T}}_c-{\thermal{T}}_\lambda\approx0.963\,\unit{K},\qquad
\frac{{\thermal{T}}_c-{\thermal{T}}_\lambda}{{\thermal{T}}_\lambda}\approx0.444.
$$

The ideal-gas estimate is about 44% higher than the observed transition temperature. It gives the correct temperature scale, but it does not give a precise value for liquid helium.

## Solution and physical meaning

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{T}}_c^{\mathrm{ideal}}\approx3.13\,\unit{K}.}
$$

Liquid helium-4 has strong interactions between its atoms. These interactions change the excitation spectrum and the relation between density and the transition temperature. The ideal-gas model omits these effects.

The lambda transition is a superfluid transition in an interacting liquid. Bose–Einstein condensation and superfluidity are related, but they are different properties. Therefore, the difference between 3.13 K and 2.17 K must not be treated as a small numerical error in the ideal-gas formula.
