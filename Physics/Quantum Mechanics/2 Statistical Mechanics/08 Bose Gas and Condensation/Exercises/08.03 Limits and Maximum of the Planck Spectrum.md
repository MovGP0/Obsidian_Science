## Question — Exercise 8.3

Start with the Planck spectrum ${\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})$ and derive:

1. The Wien displacement law $\lambda_{\mathrm{rad,max}}{\thermal{T}}={\signal{b}}$, where ${\signal{b}}\approx2.898\times10^{-3}\,\unit{m\,K}$.
2. The Rayleigh–Jeans law ${\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})\approx8\pi{\signal{\nu}}^2 {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}/{\signal{c}}^3$ for ${\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}\ll {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$.
3. The Wien law ${\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})\approx(8\pi {\energy{h}}/{\signal{c}}^3){\signal{\nu}}^3e^{-{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}$ for ${\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}\gg {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$.

## Starting equation

The energy per volume and frequency interval is

$$
{\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})=\frac{8\pi {\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}^3}{{\signal{c}}^3}
\frac{1}{e^{{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1}.
$$

## Calculation and solution

### Part (a) — Find the maximum of the wavelength spectrum

The question asks for a peak wavelength. We must first change from energy per frequency interval to energy per wavelength interval. Equal energy in corresponding intervals requires

$$
{\energy{u}}_\lambda({\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{rad}},{\thermal{T}})\,\mathrm d{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{rad}}
={\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})\,|\mathrm d{\signal{\nu}}|.
$$

Since ${\signal{\nu}}={\signal{c}}/{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{rad}}$, the Jacobian is $|\mathrm d{\signal{\nu}}/\mathrm d{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{rad}}|={\signal{c}}/{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{rad}}^2$. Thus

$$
{\energy{u}}_\lambda({\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{rad}},{\thermal{T}})
=\frac{8\pi {\energy{h}}{\signal{c}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{rad}}^5}
\frac{1}{e^{{\energy{h}}{\signal{c}}/(\lambda_{\mathrm{rad}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1}.
$$

At fixed temperature, introduce the dimensionless mathematical variable $x={\energy{h}}{\signal{c}}/(\lambda_{\mathrm{rad}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. Apart from factors independent of wavelength, the spectrum is $x^5/(e^x-1)$. Its stationary-point condition is

$$
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dx}\frac{x^5}{e^x-1}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
5(e^x-1)-xe^x=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
5(1-e^{-x})=x.
$$

The positive interior solution is $x_{\max}=4.96511423$. The zero solution is a boundary limit, not the spectral maximum. Therefore,

$$
\lambda_{\mathrm{rad,max}}{\thermal{T}}
=\frac{{\energy{h}}{\signal{c}}}{4.96511423{\thermal{k}}_B}
\equiv {\signal{b}}
\approx2.89777\times10^{-3}\,\unit{m\,K}.
$$

$$
\boxed{\lambda_{\mathrm{rad,max}}{\thermal{T}}\approx2.898\times10^{-3}\,\unit{m\,K}.}
$$

> [!note]
> The maximum of the frequency spectrum corresponds to ${\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})\approx2.82144$. Its inverse frequency is not the maximum of the wavelength spectrum. The Jacobian changes the shape of the spectrum.

### Part (b) — Low-frequency limit

Set $x={\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})$. When $x\ll1$, use $e^x-1=x+O(x^2)$. Keeping the leading term gives

$$
{\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})\approx\frac{8\pi {\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}^3}{{\signal{c}}^3}\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}}
=\boxed{\frac{8\pi{\signal{\nu}}^2{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\signal{c}}^3}}.
$$

The factor ${\energy{h}}$ cancels. This is the classical Rayleigh–Jeans limit. It applies only at low frequency; integration of this approximation over all frequencies would diverge.

### Part (c) — High-frequency limit

When $x\gg1$, the term 1 is negligible compared with $e^x$. Hence

$$
\frac{1}{e^x-1}\approx e^{-x},
$$

and

$$
\boxed{{\energy{u}}({\signal{\nu}},{\thermal{T}})\approx\frac{8\pi {\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}^3}{{\signal{c}}^3}
e^{-{\energy{h}}{\signal{\nu}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}.}
$$

The exponential suppresses high-energy photon modes. This suppression prevents the high-frequency divergence of the classical approximation.
