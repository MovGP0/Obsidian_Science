## Question — Exercise 8.6 (advanced)

Consider a two-dimensional gas of free bosons with area ${\mechanical{A}}$ and areal number density ${\mechanical{n}}_{2D}={\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{A}}$. Show that $g_1({\mechanical{z}})=-\ln(1-{\mechanical{z}})$ diverges as ${\mechanical{z}}\to1$, so no critical BEC temperature exists in the homogeneous two-dimensional thermodynamic limit. Compare this result with the Mermin–Wagner–Hohenberg theorem for interacting systems. What changes when a harmonic two-dimensional trap is present?

## Calculation

### Homogeneous gas — Determine the excited-state capacity

For particles with quadratic dispersion ${\energy{\varepsilon}}={\energy{\hbar}}^2{\signal{k}}^2/(2{\mechanical{m}})$, the two-dimensional density of orbital states is constant:

$$
{\energy{g}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})=\frac{{\mechanical{A}}{\mechanical{m}}}{2\pi{\energy{\hbar}}^2}.
$$

We assume one internal state. Introduce the thermal wavelength and the fugacity:

$$
{\signal{\lambda}}=\frac{{\energy{h}}}{\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}} {\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}},\qquad
{\mechanical{z}}=e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\mu}}},\qquad{\thermal{\beta}}=\frac{1}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

The excited-state number in the continuum approximation is

$$
{\mechanical{N}}_{\mathrm{ex}}=\frac{{\mechanical{A}}{\mechanical{m}}}{2\pi{\energy{\hbar}}^2}
\int_0^\infty\frac{\mathrm d{\energy{\varepsilon}}}
{{\mechanical{z}}^{-1}e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}-1}
=\frac{{\mechanical{A}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}^2}g_1({\mechanical{z}}).
$$

Expand the Bose denominator as a geometric series and integrate each term. This gives

$$
g_1({\mechanical{z}})=\sum_{\ell=1}^\infty\frac{{\mechanical{z}}^\ell}{\ell}
=-\ln(1-{\mechanical{z}}),\qquad0<{\mechanical{z}}<1.
$$

Thus

$$
\lim_{z\to1^-}g_1({\mechanical{z}})=+\infty.
$$

At every positive temperature the excited states can contain any finite areal density. They do not reach a finite saturation capacity. In fact, the number equation can be inverted explicitly:

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_{2D}{\signal{\lambda}}^2=-\ln(1-{\mechanical{z}})
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
{\mechanical{z}}=1-e^{-{\mechanical{n}}_{2D}{\signal{\lambda}}^2}<1.
$$

Consequently ${\energy{\mu}}={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\ln {\mechanical{z}}<0$ at each ${\thermal{T}}>0$. No macroscopic occupation of a single ground state is required.

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{T}}_c=0\quad\text{for the homogeneous ideal 2D gas at fixed finite areal density}.}
$$

This statement uses the thermodynamic limit ${\mechanical{N}},{\mechanical{A}}\to\infty$ at fixed ${\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{A}}$. A finite sample can show a smooth increase in ground-state occupation; it has no sharp thermodynamic singularity.

### Harmonic trap — Count the states again

The trap changes the low-energy density of states. For an isotropic two-dimensional harmonic trap with angular frequency ${\signal{\omega}}$, measure excitation energies from the ground state. They are

$$
{\energy{\varepsilon}}_{n_x,n_y}={\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}(n_x+n_y),
\qquad n_x,n_y=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

In the semiclassical limit ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\gg{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}$, the number of orbital states below energy ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ is the area of a triangle in the nonnegative quantum-number plane:

$$
\mathcal N({\energy{\varepsilon}})\approx\frac12
\left(\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}\right)^2.
$$

Differentiate to obtain

$$
{\energy{g}}_{\mathrm{trap}}({\energy{\varepsilon}})
\approx\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{({\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}})^2}.
$$

This density vanishes linearly at low energy. It removes the logarithmic divergence present in the uniform gas. The excited-state number is now

$$
{\mechanical{N}}_{\mathrm{ex}}=\int_0^\infty
\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm d{\energy{\varepsilon}}}
{({\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}})^2({\mechanical{z}}^{-1}e^{{\thermal{\beta}}{\energy{\varepsilon}}}-1)}
=\left(\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}\right)^2g_2({\mechanical{z}}).
$$

At saturation ${\mechanical{z}}\to1^-$, use $g_2(1)=\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$, which is finite. Set the maximum excited-state number equal to the total number:

$$
{\mechanical{N}}=\left(\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}_c}{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}\right)^2\zeta(2).
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{T}}_c=\frac{{\energy{\hbar}}{\signal{\omega}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B}
\sqrt{\frac{{\mechanical{N}}}{\zeta(2)}}.}
$$

Below this temperature, the ideal semiclassical model gives

$$
\boxed{\frac{{\mechanical{N}}_0}{{\mechanical{N}}}=1-\left(\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\right)^2,
\qquad {\thermal{T}}\leq {\thermal{T}}_c.}
$$

For an anisotropic trap, replace ${\signal{\omega}}^2$ with ${\signal{\omega}}_x{\signal{\omega}}_y$. A precise trap thermodynamic limit takes ${\mechanical{N}}\to\infty$ and ${\signal{\omega}}\to0$ with ${\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\omega}}^2$ fixed. The displayed transition is the result for that ideal-gas limit; a finite trapped cloud has a rounded onset.

## Physical interpretation and theorem scope

The homogeneous result and the Mermin–Wagner–Hohenberg result both show the importance of long-wavelength fluctuations in two dimensions. They do not use the same proof. The calculation above uses the constant density of states of an ideal homogeneous gas. The theorem concerns the absence of the relevant long-range order at positive temperature under its assumptions, including short-range interactions in a uniform low-dimensional system.

A harmonic trap changes the spatial geometry and the thermodynamic limit. Therefore, the trapped ideal-gas calculation does not contradict the homogeneous theorem. Interacting two-dimensional gases can also have a Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless superfluid transition, with algebraic correlations and no ordinary homogeneous condensate at positive temperature. Superfluidity must not be identified with macroscopic occupation of one orbital in every two-dimensional system.
