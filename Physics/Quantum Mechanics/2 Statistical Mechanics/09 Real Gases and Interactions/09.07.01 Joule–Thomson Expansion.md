> [!TLDR]
> An insulated throttling process conserves enthalpy when kinetic energy, potential energy, and shaft work can be neglected.

Gas flows through a valve or a porous plug from a higher pressure to a lower pressure. The surroundings supply no heat. For steady flow, and with negligible changes in kinetic and gravitational potential energies, the work needed to push gas into and out of the device gives

$$
{\energy{U}}_2+{\mechanical{P}}_2{\mechanical{V}}_2={\energy{U}}_1+{\mechanical{P}}_1{\mechanical{V}}_1,\qquad {\energy{H}}_2={\energy{H}}_1.
$$

Thus the process is **isenthalpic**:

$$
{\energy{H}}={\energy{U}}+{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}=\text{constant}.
$$

The extensive quantities in this balance refer to the same amount of gas before and after the valve.

> [!note] Correction to the source wording
> The source calls the process both isobaric and isenthalpic. The pressure decreases across the valve, so the process is not isobaric. It is an irreversible steady-flow process with conserved enthalpy under the stated assumptions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Total internal energy or configurational energy, as stated. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Total volume occupied by the gas. |
| ${\energy{H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Enthalpy | Internal energy plus pressure times volume. |
