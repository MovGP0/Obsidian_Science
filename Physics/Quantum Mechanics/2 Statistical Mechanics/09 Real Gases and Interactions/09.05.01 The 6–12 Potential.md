> [!TLDR]
> The Lennard–Jones potential has a zero at the molecular length scale and a minimum at a slightly larger separation.

The **Lennard–Jones 6–12 potential** is a common model for interactions between neutral, nonpolar molecules:

$$
{\energy{u}}_{LJ}({\mechanical{r}})=4{\energy{\varepsilon}}\left[\left(\frac{\mechanical{\sigma}} {\mechanical{r}}\right)^{12}-\left(\frac{\mechanical{\sigma}} {\mechanical{r}}\right)^6\right].
$$

- The positive parameter ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ is the well depth.
- The distance ${\mechanical{\sigma}}$ is the zero of the potential.
- The inverse twelfth-power term models short-range repulsion.
- The negative inverse sixth-power term models the dispersion attraction.

Differentiating gives the minimum:

$$
{\mechanical{r}}_{\min}=2^{1/6}{\mechanical{\sigma}}\approx1.122{\mechanical{\sigma}},\qquad {\energy{u}}_{LJ}({\mechanical{r}}_{\min})=-{\energy{\varepsilon}}.
$$

**Fig. 9.2** Lennard–Jones potential u/ε = 4[(σ/r)¹² − (σ/r)⁶]. The potential is zero at r = σ and has its minimum −ε at r = 2¹ᐟ⁶σ. The force is repulsive below the minimum and attractive above it; the distant attractive tail decays as −r⁻⁶.

> [!note] Source clarification
> A negative potential and an attractive force are different conditions. The potential is negative beyond its zero. The radial force, given by minus the potential derivative, becomes attractive only beyond the minimum. The well minimum is the lowest potential energy, not the point of greatest attractive force.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{u}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Pair potential | Interaction energy of two molecules at separation r. |
| ${\mechanical{r}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | Distance between the centers of two molecules. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Well depth | Positive energy scale of the attractive potential well. |
| ${\mechanical{\sigma}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Molecular length | Hard-sphere diameter or Lennard–Jones zero-crossing distance, as stated. |
