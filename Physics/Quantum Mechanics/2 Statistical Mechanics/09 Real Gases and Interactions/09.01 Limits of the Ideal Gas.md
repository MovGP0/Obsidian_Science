> [!TLDR]
> The ideal-gas law assumes point particles without intermolecular forces.

The equation of state of an ideal gas is

$$
{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}. \tag{9.1}
$$

With number density ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{V}}$, this becomes ${\mechanical{P}}={\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$. The relation follows from the assumptions that molecules have negligible size and do not interact.

Use this equation for a homogeneous gas in thermal equilibrium, with a fixed number of molecules in the stated volume. Here the pressure is a mechanical force per area, and the thermal energy per molecule sets the pressure scale. The right side has units of energy; so does pressure times volume. At fixed density, doubling temperature therefore doubles the predicted pressure.

Negligible interaction and negligible quantum exchange are separate conditions. This chapter studies classical interaction corrections. Low density suppresses encounters, but low temperature can make molecular attraction important; it can also invalidate the classical statistical description. Thus a deviation from Eq. (9.1) does not, by itself, identify which assumption has failed.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Total volume occupied by the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Number of molecules. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy per particle. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{n}}$ | $\unit{m^{-3}}$ | Number density | Particle number divided by volume. |
