> [!TLDR]
> The Mayer function converts the configurational Boltzmann factor into a product that can be expanded.

For a classical gas of identical molecules, the canonical partition function is

$$
\mathcal Z=\frac{Q_N}{{\mechanical{N}}!{\signal{\lambda}}_T^{3{\mechanical{N}}}},\qquad
{\signal{\lambda}}_T=\frac{{\signal{h}}}{\sqrt{2\pi {\mechanical{m}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}}.
$$

The **configurational integral** contains the position-dependent interaction energy:

$$
Q_N=\int_{V^N}\exp\left[-\frac{{\energy{U}}({\mechanical{\mathbf r}}_1,\ldots,{\mechanical{\mathbf r}}_N)}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}\right]
\,d^3{\mechanical{\mathbf r}}_1\cdots d^3{\mechanical{\mathbf r}}_N.
$$

For an ideal gas, the integrand is one and $Q_N={\mechanical{V}}^{\mechanical{N}}$. For a pair potential,

$$
{\energy{U}}=\sum_{i<j}{\energy{u}}({\mechanical{r}}_{ij}),\qquad {\mechanical{r}}_{ij}=|{\mechanical{\mathbf r}}_i-{\mechanical{\mathbf r}}_j|.
$$

The exponential of this sum becomes a product:

$$
e^{-{\energy{U}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}=\prod_{i<j}e^{-{\energy{u}}({\mechanical{r}}_{ij})/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}
=\prod_{i<j}(1+f_{ij}).
$$

The **Mayer function** is

$$
f({\mechanical{r}})=e^{-{\energy{u}}({\mechanical{r}})/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1,\qquad f_{ij}=f({\mechanical{r}}_{ij}).
$$

At large separation, the potential approaches zero and the Mayer function approaches zero. This makes an expansion in molecular clusters useful. The Mayer function is a dimensionless mathematical function, not a new interaction energy.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal wavelength | Thermal de Broglie wavelength in the classical partition function. |
| ${\signal{h}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Planck constant | Sets the elementary phase-space cell. |
| ${\mechanical{m}}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Molecular mass | Mass of one molecule. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy per particle. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | Total internal energy or configurational energy, as stated. |
| ${\mechanical{\mathbf r}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | Position vector of a molecule. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Number of molecules. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Total volume occupied by the gas. |
| ${\energy{u}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Pair potential | Interaction energy of two molecules at separation r. |
| ${\mechanical{r}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | Distance between the centers of two molecules. |
