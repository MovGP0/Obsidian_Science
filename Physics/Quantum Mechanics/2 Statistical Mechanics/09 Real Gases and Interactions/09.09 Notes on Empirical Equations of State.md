> [!TLDR]
> Empirical equations improve real-gas predictions beyond the simple van der Waals model.

The van der Waals model is useful for physical interpretation, but many engineering calculations need a more accurate equation of state. The following equations use molar volume.

## Redlich–Kwong equation (1949)

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{{\thermal{R}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{v}}_m-{\mechanical{b}}_m}-\frac{{\energy{a}}_{RK}}{\sqrt {\thermal{T}}\,{\mechanical{v}}_m({\mechanical{v}}_m+{\mechanical{b}}_m)}.
$$

The additional temperature factor changes the attractive contribution.

## Peng–Robinson equation (1976)

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{{\thermal{R}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{v}}_m-{\mechanical{b}}_m}
-\frac{{\energy{a}}_{PR}{\thermal{\alpha}}({\thermal{T}})}{{\mechanical{v}}_m({\mechanical{v}}_m+{\mechanical{b}}_m)+{\mechanical{b}}_m({\mechanical{v}}_m-{\mechanical{b}}_m)}.
$$

The empirical function depends on temperature and the molecular acentric factor. The source identifies natural-gas calculations as an application.

> [!note] Parameter convention
> These fitted parameters belong to their respective equations. In particular, the temperature factor gives the Redlich–Kwong attraction parameter different units from the van der Waals attraction parameter. Do not transfer parameter values between models without conversion and refitting.

## Virial equation through the third coefficient

$$
\frac{{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{v}}_m}{{\thermal{R}}{\thermal{T}}}=1+\frac{{\mechanical{B}}_{2,m}({\thermal{T}})}{{\mechanical{v}}_m}+\frac{{\mechanical{B}}_{3,m}({\thermal{T}})}{{\mechanical{v}}_m^2}.
$$

This form is useful at moderate densities when accurate measured or calculated coefficients are available. Check that the omitted terms are small.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
| ${\thermal{R}}$ | $\unit{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Molar gas constant | Boltzmann constant times the Avogadro constant. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{v}}_m$ | $\unit{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$ | Molar volume | Total volume divided by amount of substance. |
| ${\mechanical{b}}_m$ | $\unit{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$ | Molar excluded-volume parameter | Molar van der Waals covolume. |
| ${\energy{a}}_{RK}$ | $\unit{Pa\,m^6\,K^{1/2}\,mol^{-2}}$ | Redlich–Kwong attraction parameter | Fitted attraction parameter in the stated molar equation. |
| ${\energy{a}}_{PR}$ | $\unit{Pa\,m^6\,mol^{-2}}$ | Peng–Robinson attraction parameter | Fitted attraction parameter before its temperature correction. |
| ${\thermal{\alpha}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Attraction correction | Dimensionless temperature function in the Peng–Robinson model. |
| ${\mechanical{B}}_{2,m}$ | $\unit{m^3\,mol^{-1}}$ | Molar second virial coefficient | Coefficient used with molar density. |
| ${\mechanical{B}}_{3,m}$ | $\unit{m^6\,mol^{-2}}$ | Molar third virial coefficient | Coefficient used with squared molar density. |
