> [!TLDR]
> Expanding the product of pair factors produces graphs with one edge for each Mayer function.

Expand the product over all distinct molecular pairs:

$$
\prod_{i<j}(1+f_{ij})
=1+\sum_{i<j}f_{ij}
+\frac12\sum_{\substack{i<j,\ k<l\\ (i,j)\ne(k,l)}}f_{ij}f_{kl}+\cdots.
$$

The factor $1/2$ avoids counting the two orders of a pair of distinct edges twice. More generally, each term selects a set of different edges.

For two molecules, there is one possible pair and the product is exactly one plus one bond. For three molecules there are three possible pairs: expansion gives one empty graph, three single bonds, three distinct two-bond chains, and one triangle. No term contains the square of a bond from the same pair. This finite example checks the edge-counting factor before any position integral is taken.

The expansion is not a small-force approximation. In a hard core the bond is minus one, so it is not uniformly small. Low density instead suppresses the probability of many molecules sharing an interaction region. Systematic linked-cluster organization is most direct in the grand-canonical logarithm; conversion back to density produces the virial series. See [Tong, Classical Gases, Section 2.5.3](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/statphys/statmechhtml/S2.html#S2.SS5.SSS3).

Represent each molecule by a node and each factor by an edge. Position integrals of disconnected graphs factor into independent components. Connected graphs enter the logarithm of the partition function. A further cancellation removes reducible graphs from the density virial coefficients.
