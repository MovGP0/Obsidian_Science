> [!TLDR]
> Each exercise keeps its question, calculations, explanations, and solution in one file.

These worked solutions were added to the supplied exercise questions. Subquestions stay together. The six source exercise numbers are preserved.

Use Exercises 9.5 and 9.2 first to compare a macroscopic pressure correction with a microscopic pair integral. In 9.5 the virial coefficient comes from the same van der Waals model, so agreement between the two predictions checks truncation rather than experimental accuracy. In 9.2, cancellation at the Boyle temperature tests the sign and volume factors of the integral.

Then take 9.3 before 9.4: the coexistence slope and volume difference determine the latent-heat limit. Exercise 9.1 asks for a response near the same critical point, but its answer needs a specified path and a pure-phase derivative. Finish with 9.6 to see why the sign of a pressure correction alone cannot predict throttling. Before reading each solution, state the controlled variables and the limit that should check the answer.
