> [!TLDR]
> Real-gas models connect molecular size and attraction to pressure, phase coexistence, and gas cooling.

The ideal-gas model treats molecules as point particles without interactions. Previous chapters showed that this model gives accurate results for many systems. It becomes less accurate at high density or low temperature, when interactions between molecules matter.

This chapter builds a more detailed description in stages. It starts with the van der Waals equation, then calculates the second virial coefficient from a pair potential. The Lennard–Jones potential gives a microscopic model. The Mayer cluster expansion provides a systematic method for higher virial coefficients.

The final sections connect these results to the Joule–Thomson effect and gas liquefaction. The worked examples and dedicated exercise notes give calculations and their physical meaning.
