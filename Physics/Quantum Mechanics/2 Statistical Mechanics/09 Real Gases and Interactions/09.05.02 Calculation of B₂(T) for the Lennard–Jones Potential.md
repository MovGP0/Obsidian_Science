> [!TLDR]
> Numerical integration of the Lennard–Jones Mayer function gives the second virial coefficient and its Boyle temperature.

Insert the 6–12 potential into Eq. (9.11):

$$
{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})=-2\pi\int_0^\infty\left\{\exp\left[-\frac{4{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}
\left(\left(\frac{\mechanical{\sigma}} {\mechanical{r}}\right)^{12}-\left(\frac{\mechanical{\sigma}} {\mechanical{r}}\right)^6\right)\right]-1\right\}{\mechanical{r}}^2\,d{\mechanical{r}}.
$$

Use the mathematical integration variable $x={\mechanical{r}}/{\mechanical{\sigma}}$ and reduced temperature ${\thermal{T}}^*={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}/{\energy{\varepsilon}}$:

$$
{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})=-2\pi{\mechanical{\sigma}}^3\int_0^\infty
\left[e^{-(4/{\thermal{T}}^*)(x^{-12}-x^{-6})}-1\right]x^2\,dx.
$$

With the reference volume ${\mechanical{b}}_0=2\pi{\mechanical{\sigma}}^3/3$, define

$$
{\mechanical{B}}_2^*({\thermal{T}}^*)=\frac{{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})}{{\mechanical{b}}_0}
=-3\int_0^\infty\left[e^{-(4/{\thermal{T}}^*)(x^{-12}-x^{-6})}-1\right]x^2\,dx.
$$

This integral is convenient for numerical calculation.

## High-temperature behavior

> [!note] Correction to the source approximation
> The source writes a constant repulsive contribution minus a term proportional to inverse temperature. That is a useful hard-core model, but it is not the high-temperature asymptotic expansion of the soft Lennard–Jones core. Its effective repulsive radius decreases as temperature increases.

Rescale the repulsive part before expanding the attractive part. The first two terms are

$$
{\mechanical{B}}_2^*({\thermal{T}}^*)=\Gamma\left(\frac34\right)\left(\frac4{{\thermal{T}}^*}\right)^{1/4}
-\frac14\Gamma\left(\frac14\right)\left(\frac4{{\thermal{T}}^*}\right)^{3/4}
+O\left(({\thermal{T}}^*)^{-5/4}\right).
$$

Here $\Gamma$ is the mathematical Gamma function. The coefficient approaches zero from above at very high temperature.

## Boyle temperature

The root ${\mechanical{B}}_2^*({\thermal{T}}_B^*)=0$ is approximately ${\thermal{T}}_B^*=3.42$. The source compares this with argon: a Boyle temperature of about $411\,\unit{K}$ and a well-depth temperature of about $120\,\unit{K}$ give a ratio near 3.4.

| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Well depth | Positive energy scale of the attractive potential well. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy per particle. |

| ${\thermal{T}}^*$ | $\unit{1}$ | Reduced Lennard–Jones temperature | Thermal energy divided by well depth. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_B$ | $\unit{K}$ | Boyle temperature | Temperature at which the second virial coefficient is zero. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_B^*$ | $\unit{1}$ | Reduced Boyle temperature | Boyle temperature multiplied by Boltzmann constant and divided by well depth. |
