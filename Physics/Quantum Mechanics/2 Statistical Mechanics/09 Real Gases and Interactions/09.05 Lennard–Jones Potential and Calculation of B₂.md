> [!TLDR]
> The Lennard–Jones model combines a steep repulsive core with an attractive dispersion tail.

The following articles define the potential and calculate its leading correction to the equation of state.

This model asks whether a length scale and an energy scale can explain the leading nonideal pressure of a simple neutral gas. First locate the zero and minimum of the potential, then distinguish the sign of energy from the sign of force. The second note inserts the same potential into the classical pair integral. Thus the plotted coefficient is a prediction from the potential, rather than an additional fitted pressure law.

Use the Boyle root as a test of attraction–repulsion cancellation. Use the high-temperature limit as a separate test of the repulsive model: unlike a rigid hard sphere, the effective size of a soft core decreases with increasing thermal energy. Agreement of a reduced curve for different molecules remains conditional on the adequacy of an isotropic pair potential.
