> [!TLDR]
> Equal areas select the pressure at which liquid and vapor have the same chemical potential.

For ${\thermal{T}}<{\thermal{T}}_c$, replace the non-equilibrium loop by a constant pressure ${\mechanical{P}}_0$. Let ${\mechanical{V}}_\ell,{\mechanical{V}}_g$ be the volumes of the saturated liquid and vapor for the same particle number.

**Proposition 9.1 — Maxwell construction.** The coexistence pressure obeys

$$
\int_{V_\ell}^{{\mechanical{V}}_g}{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{vdW}}({\mechanical{V}},{\thermal{T}})\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{V}}
={\mechanical{P}}_0({\mechanical{V}}_g-{\mechanical{V}}_\ell). \tag{9.7}
$$

**Proof.** Equilibrium requires ${\energy{\mu}}_\ell({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}}_0)={\energy{\mu}}_g({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}}_0)$. Along an isotherm, Gibbs–Duhem gives $\mathrm d{\energy{\mu}}=({\mechanical{V}}/{\mechanical{N}})\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{P}}$. Hence

$$
{\energy{\mu}}_g-{\energy{\mu}}_\ell=\frac1{\mechanical{N}}\int_{V_\ell}^{{\mechanical{V}}_g}{\mechanical{V}}\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{P}}=0.
$$

Integration by parts gives

$$
[{\mechanical{V}}{\mechanical{P}}]_{V_\ell}^{{\mechanical{V}}_g}-\int_{V_\ell}^{{\mechanical{V}}_g}{\mechanical{P}}\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{V}}=0.
$$

Both endpoints have pressure ${\mechanical{P}}_0$, so this is (9.7). The excess area above the horizontal line equals the deficit below it.

The construction replaces the loop by equilibrium coexistence. The positive-slope part is unstable; parts of the negative-slope loop can be metastable before nucleation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature at the liquid–vapor critical point. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}_0$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Coexistence pressure | Pressure at which liquid and vapor coexist at the specified temperature. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Total volume occupied by the gas. |
