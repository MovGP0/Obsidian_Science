> [!TLDR]
> The leading Joule–Thomson coefficient follows by differentiating the virial volume at fixed pressure.

## Question — Exercise 9.6

For an equation of state truncated after the second virial term,

$$
{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\left(1+{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})\frac {\mechanical{N}}{\mechanical{V}}\right),
$$

verify the first-order relation

$$
{\thermal{\mu}}_{JT}=\frac1{{\thermal{c}}_P}\left({\thermal{T}}\frac{d{\mechanical{B}}_2}{d{\thermal{T}}}-{\mechanical{B}}_2\right),\qquad {\thermal{c}}_P=\frac{{\thermal{C}}_P}{{\mechanical{N}}}.
$$

Calculate it for the van der Waals gas and obtain the inversion temperature.

## Calculation and solution

### Solve for volume in the dilute limit

Let ${\mechanical{v}}={\mechanical{V}}/{\mechanical{N}}$. The equation becomes

$$
{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{v}}={\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}\left(1+\frac{{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})}{\mechanical{v}}\right).
$$

**Why:** Inside the small correction, replacing volume by its ideal value changes only higher-order terms.

Thus, to leading order in pressure,

$$
{\mechanical{v}}=\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{P}}}+{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})+O({\mechanical{P}}).
$$

Differentiate at fixed pressure:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{v}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_P
=\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B}{{\mechanical{P}}}+\frac{d{\mechanical{B}}_2}{d{\thermal{T}}}+O({\mechanical{P}}).
$$

### Insert the thermodynamic identity

From [[09.07.02 Joule–Thomson Coefficient]],

$$
{\thermal{\mu}}_{JT}=\frac1{{\thermal{c}}_P}\left[{\thermal{T}}\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{v}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_P-{\mechanical{v}}\right].
$$

The ideal-gas terms cancel. The result is

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{\mu}}_{JT}=\frac1{{\thermal{c}}_P}\left({\thermal{T}}\frac{d{\mechanical{B}}_2}{d{\thermal{T}}}-{\mechanical{B}}_2\right)+O({\mechanical{P}}).}
$$

**Unit check:** Each term in the bracket is a volume per molecule. Dividing by heat capacity per molecule gives kelvin per pascal, as required.

### Apply the van der Waals coefficient

$$
{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})={\mechanical{b}}-\frac{{\energy{a}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}},\qquad
{\thermal{T}}\frac{d{\mechanical{B}}_2}{d{\thermal{T}}}=\frac{{\energy{a}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{\mu}}_{JT}\approx\frac1{{\thermal{c}}_P}\left(\frac{2{\energy{a}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}-{\mechanical{b}}\right).}
$$

For positive heat capacity, its zero gives

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{T}}_{\mathrm{inv}}=\frac{2{\energy{a}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B{\mechanical{b}}}=2{\thermal{T}}_B=\frac{27}{4}{\thermal{T}}_c.}
$$

This is Eq. (9.15), the upper inversion temperature in the dilute limit.

### Interpret the sign

During throttling the pressure change is negative. A positive coefficient therefore gives a negative temperature change, or cooling. A negative coefficient gives heating. The derivative is local; a large pressure drop must follow a constant-enthalpy path through the changing coefficient.

The finite-pressure van der Waals inversion curve follows from the full equation of state and is given in [[09.07.04 Inversion Temperature]].

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |

| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Number of molecules. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy per particle. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{B}}_2$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Second virial coefficient | Coefficient of number density in the compressibility-factor expansion. |

| ${\thermal{c}}_P$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity per particle | Heat capacity at constant pressure divided by particle number. |
| ${\thermal{C}}_P$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity at constant pressure | Total heat capacity of the stated amount of gas. |
| ${\mechanical{v}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume per particle | Total volume divided by particle number. |
| ${\mechanical{b}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Excluded-volume parameter | Molecular van der Waals covolume per particle. |
