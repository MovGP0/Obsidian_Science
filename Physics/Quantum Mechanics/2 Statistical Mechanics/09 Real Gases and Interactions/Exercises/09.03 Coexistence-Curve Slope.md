> [!TLDR]
> The reduced coexistence pressure has slope four at the van der Waals critical point.

## Question — Exercise 9.3

Use the reduced van der Waals equation and the Maxwell construction to find the liquid–vapor coexistence slope near the critical point, approached from below. Compare it with a dimensional estimate.

$$
{\mechanical{p}}=\frac{8{\thermal{t}}}{3{\mechanical{\upsilon}}-1}-\frac3{{\mechanical{\upsilon}}^2},\qquad
{\mechanical{p}}=\frac {\mechanical{P}}{{\mechanical{P}}_c},\quad {\thermal{t}}=\frac {\thermal{T}}{{\thermal{T}}_c},\quad {\mechanical{\upsilon}}=\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\mechanical{V}}_c}.
$$

## Calculation and solution

### Expand about the critical state

**Why:** Close to the critical point, both coexistence volumes approach the critical volume. Small offsets give the leading terms directly.

Write ${\thermal{\tau}}={\thermal{t}}-1<0$ and ${\mechanical{w}}={\mechanical{\upsilon}}-1$. Expanding gives

$$
{\mechanical{p}}=1+4{\thermal{\tau}}-6{\thermal{\tau}} {\mechanical{w}}+9{\thermal{\tau}} {\mechanical{w}}^2-\frac32{\mechanical{w}}^3
+O({\thermal{\tau}} {\mechanical{w}}^3,{\mechanical{w}}^4).
$$

Near coexistence, the volume offsets scale as the square root of the temperature offset. The leading nonconstant terms in the volume offset are therefore the linear and cubic terms:

$$
{\mechanical{p}}\approx1+4{\thermal{\tau}}-6{\thermal{\tau}} {\mechanical{w}}-\frac32{\mechanical{w}}^3.
$$

### Apply equal pressure and equal area

**Why:** Equal pressure alone gives three intersections. The equal-area condition selects the equilibrium pressure.

The remaining volume-dependent polynomial is odd. Hence its signed area between symmetric outer roots is zero when

$$
{\mechanical{p}}_0=1+4{\thermal{\tau}}+O({\thermal{\tau}}^2).
$$

Its outer roots satisfy

$$
-6{\thermal{\tau}} {\mechanical{w}}-\frac32{\mechanical{w}}^3=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
{\mechanical{w}}_\ell=-2\sqrt{-{\thermal{\tau}}},\qquad {\mechanical{w}}_g=2\sqrt{-{\thermal{\tau}}}.
$$

Thus the two phases meet continuously at the critical point, and

$$
\boxed{\lim_{t\to1^-}\frac{d{\mechanical{p}}_0}{d{\thermal{t}}}=4.}
$$

### Convert to physical units

$$
\boxed{\left.\frac{d{\mechanical{P}}_0}{d{\thermal{T}}}\right|_{T_c^-}
=\frac{{\mechanical{P}}_c}{{\thermal{T}}_c}\,4=\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B}{2{\mechanical{b}}}.}
$$

The natural dimensional estimate is critical pressure divided by critical temperature. The model fixes its dimensionless multiplier as four. The slope is finite although the two coexistence volumes become equal.

See [[09.02.06 Maxwell Construction]] and [[09.04 Latent Heat near the Critical Point]].
