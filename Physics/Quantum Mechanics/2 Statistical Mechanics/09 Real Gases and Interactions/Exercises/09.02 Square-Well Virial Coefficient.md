> [!TLDR]
> A square well gives an analytic second virial coefficient and an explicit Boyle temperature.

## Question — Exercise 9.2

Calculate the second virial coefficient for the square-well potential

$$
{\energy{u}}({\mechanical{r}})=\begin{cases}
+\infty,&{\mechanical{r}}<{\mechanical{\sigma}},\\
-{\energy{\varepsilon}},&{\mechanical{\sigma}}\le {\mechanical{r}}<{\mechanical{\lambda}}{\mechanical{\sigma}},\\
0,&{\mechanical{r}}\ge{\mechanical{\lambda}}{\mechanical{\sigma}},
\end{cases}\qquad {\mechanical{\lambda}}=1.5.
$$

Find the Boyle temperature as a function of the well depth.

## Calculation and solution

### Write the Mayer function in each region

**Why:** The potential is constant in each region, so the radial integral reduces to elementary volume integrals.

$$
f({\mechanical{r}})=e^{-{\energy{u}}({\mechanical{r}})/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1
=\begin{cases}
-1,&{\mechanical{r}}<{\mechanical{\sigma}},\\
e^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1,&{\mechanical{\sigma}}\le {\mechanical{r}}<{\mechanical{\lambda}}{\mechanical{\sigma}},\\
0,&{\mechanical{r}}\ge{\mechanical{\lambda}}{\mechanical{\sigma}}.
\end{cases}
$$

### Integrate core and well separately

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})&=-2\pi\left[-\int_0^{\mechanical{\sigma}} {\mechanical{r}}^2\,d{\mechanical{r}}
+\left(e^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1\right)\int_\sigma^{{\mechanical{\lambda}}{\mechanical{\sigma}}}{\mechanical{r}}^2\,d{\mechanical{r}}\right]\\
&=\frac{2\pi{\mechanical{\sigma}}^3}{3}\left[1-({\mechanical{\lambda}}^3-1)\left(e^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The first contribution is positive and comes from the excluded core. The second is negative and comes from attraction.

### Set the coefficient to zero

**Why:** The Boyle temperature cancels the leading density correction, not all higher-density corrections.

$$
({\mechanical{\lambda}}^3-1)\left(e^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}_B)}-1\right)=1,
$$

so

$$
e^{{\energy{\varepsilon}}/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}_B)}=\frac{{\mechanical{\lambda}}^3}{{\mechanical{\lambda}}^3-1}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{{\thermal{T}}_B=\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B\ln[{\mechanical{\lambda}}^3/({\mechanical{\lambda}}^3-1)]}
\approx2.84578\frac{{\energy{\varepsilon}}}{{\thermal{k}}_B}\quad({\mechanical{\lambda}}=1.5).}
$$

## Checks

At high temperature the attractive correction vanishes, and the coefficient approaches the hard-sphere value. At lower temperature, the attractive contribution grows. If the well range approaches the core radius, the attractive region disappears and the Boyle temperature approaches zero.

See [[09.04.01 General Formula]] and [[09.04.03 Boyle Temperature]].

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
