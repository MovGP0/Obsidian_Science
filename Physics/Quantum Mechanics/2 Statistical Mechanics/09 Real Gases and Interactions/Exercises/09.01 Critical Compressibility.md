> [!TLDR]
> On the critical isochore above the critical point, the van der Waals compressibility diverges with exponent one.

## Question — Exercise 9.1

Show that the isothermal compressibility of a van der Waals gas diverges near the critical point as a power of the temperature difference. Find the critical exponent.

$$
{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T=-\frac1{\mechanical{V}}\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}{\partial {\mechanical{P}}}\right)_T
\propto|{\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c|^{-\gamma}.
$$

## Calculation and solution

### Specify the approach to the critical point

**Why:** A response function near a critical point depends on the path of approach. First use the critical isochore, ${\mechanical{v}}={\mechanical{V}}/N={\mechanical{v}}_c=3{\mechanical{b}}$, with ${\thermal{T}}>{\thermal{T}}_c$. This is a homogeneous stable gas. The exponent is a mathematical scaling exponent and has no physical unit.

For fixed particle number,

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{v}}-{\mechanical{b}}}-\frac{{\energy{a}}}{{\mechanical{v}}^2},\qquad
{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T=-\frac1{\mechanical{v}}\frac1{(\partial {\mechanical{P}}/\partial {\mechanical{v}})_T}.
$$

Differentiate before inserting the critical volume:

$$
\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{P}}}{\partial {\mechanical{v}}}\right)_T
=-\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{({\mechanical{v}}-{\mechanical{b}})^2}+\frac{2{\energy{a}}}{{\mechanical{v}}^3}.
$$

At the critical volume,

$$
\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{P}}}{\partial {\mechanical{v}}}\right)_{T,v_c}
=-\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{4{\mechanical{b}}^2}+\frac{2{\energy{a}}}{27{\mechanical{b}}^3}
=-\frac{{\thermal{k}}_B({\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c)}{4{\mechanical{b}}^2},
$$

where ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}_c=8{\energy{a}}/(27{\mechanical{b}})$ was used.

### Invert the pressure slope

**Why:** Compressibility measures the volume response to a pressure change. A vanishing pressure slope gives a large volume response.

$$
\boxed{{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T=\frac{4{\mechanical{b}}}{3{\thermal{k}}_B({\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c)},\qquad {\thermal{T}}>{\thermal{T}}_c.}
$$

It is positive in the stable region and diverges as the inverse temperature difference. Therefore

$$
\boxed{\gamma=1.}
$$

### Approach from below

The homogeneous critical isochore is unstable below the critical temperature, so its negative compressibility is not a stable-phase response. Instead, evaluate the response in either pure phase at coexistence.

Set ${\thermal{t}}={\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_c$, ${\thermal{\tau}}={\thermal{t}}-1$, and ${\mechanical{w}}={\mechanical{v}}/{\mechanical{v}}_c-1$. The equation of state near the critical point is

$$
{\mechanical{p}}=1+4{\thermal{\tau}}-6{\thermal{\tau}} {\mechanical{w}}+9{\thermal{\tau}} {\mechanical{w}}^2-\frac32{\mechanical{w}}^3+O({\thermal{\tau}} {\mechanical{w}}^3,{\mechanical{w}}^4).
$$

The coexistence volumes obey ${\mechanical{w}}_\ell\approx-2\sqrt{-{\thermal{\tau}}}$ and ${\mechanical{w}}_g\approx2\sqrt{-{\thermal{\tau}}}$. Thus

$$
\left(\frac{\partial {\mechanical{p}}}{\partial {\mechanical{w}}}\right)_t
\approx-6{\thermal{\tau}}-\frac92{\mechanical{w}}^2\approx12{\thermal{\tau}},
$$

and the compressibility within either pure phase is

$$
{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T\approx\frac1{12{\mechanical{P}}_c|{\thermal{\tau}}|}.
$$

The exponent is again one; the leading amplitude differs. This pure-phase derivative must be distinguished from changing the phase fraction in a two-phase mixture at fixed coexistence pressure.

## Result

The van der Waals model predicts the mean-field critical exponent one. Its amplitude and the meaning of the derivative require a specified path and phase.

See [[09.02.05 Critical Point]] and [[09.02.06 Maxwell Construction]].
