> [!TLDR]
> For the stated nitrogen sample, both real-gas approximations give a pressure slightly below the ideal-gas value.

## Question — Exercise 9.5

A cylinder contains ${\mechanical{N}}=10^{23}$ nitrogen molecules, or approximately ${\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{mol}}=0.166\,\unit{mol}$, at ${\thermal{T}}=300\,\unit{K}$ in ${\mechanical{V}}=1\,\unit{L}$. Use

$$
{\energy{a}}_m=0.137\,\unit{Pa\,m^6\,mol^{-2}},\qquad
{\mechanical{b}}_m=3.87\times10^{-5}\,\unit{m^3\,mol^{-1}}.
$$

Calculate the pressure with (a) the ideal-gas law, (b) the molar van der Waals equation, and (c) the virial expansion through the second coefficient. Discuss the differences.

## Calculation and solution

### Convert the input consistently

**Why:** The parameters are molar, while the particle number is given directly. Convert the particle number to moles before using these parameters.

$$
{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{mol}}=\frac {\mechanical{N}}{{\mechanical{N}}_A}\approx0.16605391\,\unit{mol},\qquad
{\mechanical{V}}=10^{-3}\,\unit{m^3}.
$$

Use ${\thermal{k}}_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\,\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$, ${\mechanical{N}}_A=6.02214076\times10^{23}\,\unit{mol^{-1}}$, and ${\thermal{R}}={\mechanical{N}}_A{\thermal{k}}_B$.

### (a) Ideal gas

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{id}}=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}
=414194.7\,\unit{Pa}\approx4.142\,\unit{bar}.
$$

### (b) van der Waals gas

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{vdW}}=\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{mol}}{\thermal{R}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}-{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{mol}}{\mechanical{b}}_m}
-\frac{{\energy{a}}_m {\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{mol}}^2}{{\mechanical{V}}^2}
\approx413096.0\,\unit{Pa}.
$$

The excluded-volume term raises the pressure. The attraction term lowers it. Under these conditions, the attraction correction is slightly larger.

### (c) Second-virial approximation

**Why:** The molar second coefficient belongs with molar density, so no additional Avogadro factor is needed.

$$
{\mechanical{B}}_{2,m}^{\mathrm{vdW}}={\mechanical{b}}_m-\frac{{\energy{a}}_m}{{\thermal{R}}{\thermal{T}}},
$$

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{vir}}=\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{mol}}{\thermal{R}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}
\left(1+{\mechanical{B}}_{2,m}^{\mathrm{vdW}}\frac{{\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{mol}}}{{\mechanical{V}}}\right)
\approx413078.8\,\unit{Pa}.
$$

## Results and interpretation

| Model | Pressure in Pa | Compressibility factor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ideal gas | 414195 | 1 |
| van der Waals | 413096 | 0.997347 |
| Second virial, vdW coefficient | 413079 | 0.997306 |

The two real-gas predictions are about 0.27 percent below the ideal value. Their difference is only about 17 pascals. The expansion parameter ${\mechanical{n}}_{\mathrm{mol}}{\mechanical{b}}_m/{\mechanical{V}}\approx0.00643$ is small, so truncating the excluded-volume expansion is consistent here.

This is a comparison of models. The second coefficient used here comes from the van der Waals model, not from an independent measurement.
