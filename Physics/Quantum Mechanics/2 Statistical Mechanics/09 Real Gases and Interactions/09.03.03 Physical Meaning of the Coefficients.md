> [!TLDR]
> Virial coefficients describe correlations between groups of molecules.

- ${\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})$ describes two-particle correlations. Positive values usually indicate a dominant repulsive contribution; negative values indicate a dominant attractive contribution.
- ${\mechanical{B}}_3({\thermal{T}})$ describes irreducible three-particle correlations. These become more important as density increases.
- More generally, ${\mechanical{B}}_k({\thermal{T}})$ describes irreducible correlations involving the corresponding number of particles.

> [!note] Source clarification
> Three-particle correlations can arise from pair potentials alone. A nonzero third virial coefficient does not require a separate three-body force.

At sufficiently low density, the second coefficient is often enough for useful calculations. The complete virial series is formally exact within its domain of convergence. Truncating it is an approximation. Convergence cannot be assumed near a phase transition or at high density.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{B}}_2$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Second virial coefficient | Coefficient of number density in the compressibility-factor expansion. |
| ${\mechanical{B}}_3$ | $\unit{m^6}$ | Third virial coefficient | Coefficient of squared number density in the compressibility-factor expansion. |
| ${\mechanical{B}}_k$ | $\unit{m^{3(k-1)}}$ | Virial coefficient | Coefficient multiplying the corresponding power of number density. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
