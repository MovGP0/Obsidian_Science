> [!TLDR]
> Reduced variables remove the gas-specific parameters from the van der Waals equation.

Define

$$
{\mechanical{p}}=\frac{{\mechanical{P}}}{{\mechanical{P}}_c},\qquad{\mechanical{\upsilon}}=\frac{{\mechanical{V}}}{{\mechanical{V}}_c},\qquad {\thermal{t}}=\frac{{\thermal{T}}}{{\thermal{T}}_c}.
$$

The source calls reduced volume $v$; these notes use ${\mechanical{\upsilon}}$ to distinguish it from the volume per particle. Substitution of (9.6) into (9.2) gives

$$
\left({\mechanical{p}}+\frac{3}{{\mechanical{\upsilon}}^2}\right)\left({\mechanical{\upsilon}}-\frac13\right)=\frac83{\thermal{t}}. \tag{9.8}
$$

**Theorem 9.1 — Law of corresponding states.** All gases that obey the van der Waals equation have the same behavior when expressed in reduced variables. States with the same reduced variables are corresponding states.

This law is exact within this model. Similarity between two-parameter molecular potentials gives it a wider approximate use away from the critical region. The source connects real-fluid critical universality to the three-dimensional Ising class. The van der Waals mean-field model itself does not give the exact Ising critical exponents.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Reduced pressure | Pressure divided by critical pressure. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Mechanical pressure of the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}_c$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Critical pressure | Pressure at the liquid–vapor critical point. |
| ${\mechanical{\upsilon}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Reduced volume | Volume divided by its critical value for fixed particle number. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Total volume occupied by the gas. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}_c$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Critical volume | Total volume at the critical point for the stated particle number. |
| ${\thermal{t}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Reduced temperature | Temperature divided by critical temperature. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature at the liquid–vapor critical point. |
