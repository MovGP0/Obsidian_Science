> [!TLDR]
> Molecular volume matters at high density, and attractive interactions matter when thermal energy is small.

Equation (9.1) becomes inaccurate in two main regimes.

1. **High density.** Molecules are close together. Their excluded volume becomes an appreciable part of the available volume. Short-range repulsion also changes the frequency and momentum transfer of collisions.
2. **Low temperature.** Mean kinetic energies become comparable to molecular interaction energies, typically from a few $\unit{meV}$ to about $0.1\,\unit{eV}$. Attractions can reduce pressure below the ideal value and can cause condensation into a liquid.

These effects require different corrections. A finite molecular size reduces the available volume. Attraction reduces the pressure at a given density and temperature.
