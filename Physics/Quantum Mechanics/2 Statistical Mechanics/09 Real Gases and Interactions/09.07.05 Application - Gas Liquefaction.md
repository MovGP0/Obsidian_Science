> [!TLDR]
> The Linde–Hampson cycle uses compression, throttling, and heat recovery to cool and partly liquefy a gas.

A gas must enter the cooling region of its inversion curve before Joule–Thomson throttling can cool it. This requirement determines the necessary precooling.

## Linde–Hampson cycle

The source describes the following cycle for air liquefaction:

1. Compress the gas while removing heat. A representative high pressure is about 200 atmospheres.
2. Pass the gas through a valve. In its cooling region, throttling decreases its temperature.
3. Use the cold outgoing stream in a counterflow heat exchanger to cool the incoming compressed stream.
4. Repeat the circulation. Part of the gas eventually becomes liquid.

Helium has an upper inversion temperature of about 45 kelvin. Liquid nitrogen at 77 kelvin cannot by itself cool helium below this value; an additional lower-temperature stage is necessary. The source gives liquid hydrogen at about 20 kelvin as an example.

Hydrogen has an upper inversion temperature of about 205 kelvin. It must be precooled below this value to obtain Joule–Thomson cooling; liquid nitrogen is one possible precooling medium.
