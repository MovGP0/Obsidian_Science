> [!TLDR]
> For an isotropic pair potential, the second virial coefficient is a radial integral.

From Eq. (9.10),

$$
{\mechanical{B}}_2({\thermal{T}})=-2\pi\int_0^\infty\left[e^{-{\energy{u}}({\mechanical{r}})/({\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}})}-1\right]{\mechanical{r}}^2\,d{\mechanical{r}}.\tag{9.11}
$$

The angular integral gives the factor $4\pi$, and pair counting gives the factor $1/2$. A repulsive core gives a negative Mayer function and a positive contribution to the coefficient. An attractive region gives the opposite sign.

The integral must converge. For example, an unscreened long-range potential can require a different treatment.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{B}}_2$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Second virial coefficient | Coefficient of number density in the compressibility-factor expansion. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the gas. |
| ${\energy{u}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Pair potential | Interaction energy of two molecules at separation r. |
| ${\mechanical{r}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | Distance between the centers of two molecules. |
| ${\thermal{k}}_B$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy per particle. |
