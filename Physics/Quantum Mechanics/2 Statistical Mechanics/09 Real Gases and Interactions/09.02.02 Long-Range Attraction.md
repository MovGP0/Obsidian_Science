> [!TLDR]
> An attractive mean field lowers the pressure by a term proportional to squared density.

Dipole interactions, induced-dipole interactions, and London dispersion can produce attraction. The dispersion tail decreases as ${\mechanical{r}}^{-6}$. In the source, “long-range” means longer than the hard repulsive core; this integrable tail is not a long-range interaction in the thermodynamic nonadditivity sense.

In a uniform gas of number density ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{N}}/{\mechanical{V}}$, the attractive energy of one molecule in the field of all others is approximated by

$$
{\energy{u}}_{\mathrm{mean}}={\mechanical{n}}\int_\sigma^\infty {\energy{u}}_{\mathrm{attr}}({\mechanical{r}})4\pi {\mechanical{r}}^2\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{r}}
=-\frac{2{\energy{a}}{\mechanical{N}}}{{\mechanical{V}}},
$$

where

$$
{\energy{a}}=-\frac12\int_\sigma^\infty {\energy{u}}_{\mathrm{attr}}({\mechanical{r}})4\pi {\mechanical{r}}^2\,\mathrm d{\mechanical{r}}>0.
$$

The factor one half prevents double counting of pairs in the total energy:

$$
{\energy{U}}_{\mathrm{attr}}=-\frac{{\energy{a}}{\mechanical{N}}^2}{{\mechanical{V}}}.
$$

Its mean-field pressure contribution is

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{attr}}=-\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{U}}_{\mathrm{attr}}}{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{T,N}
=-\frac{{\energy{a}}{\mechanical{N}}^2}{{\mechanical{V}}^2}.
$$

Attraction pulls molecules toward one another. It reduces their net outward momentum transfer to the container wall.
