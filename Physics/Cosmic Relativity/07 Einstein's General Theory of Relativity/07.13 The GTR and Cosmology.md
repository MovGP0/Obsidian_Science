## Lecture account

The GTR enabled study of the entire matter-filled Universe within a consistent theoretical formalism. This was unavailable in Newtonian theory, where uniformly distributed matter is unstable: a finite Universe collapses under its own gravity, and an infinite one is unstable to small perturbations. The GTR’s $\mechanical{g}_{\mu\nu}$ field offers mathematical flexibility to model finite or infinite, stable or unstable Universes, as described in cosmology textbooks.

The crucial assumption is a homogeneous, isotropic matter distribution. Its metric is also homogeneous and isotropic. This agrees with the observed large-scale distribution, although whether that sufficiently supports the standard model is debated. The corresponding line element is

$$
d\mechanical{s}^2=-\mechanical{c}^2dt^2+\mechanical{a}^2(t)\left\{\frac{d\mechanical{r}^2}{1-k\mechanical{r}^2}+\mechanical{r}^2(d\mechanical{\theta}^2+\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\phi}^2)\right\}. \tag{7.34}
$$

The scale factor $\mechanical{a}(t)$ is defined through the observable Hubble expansion parameter, $\mechanical{H}(t)=(d\mechanical{a}/dt)/\mechanical{a}(t)$. Spatial curvature parameter $k$ depends on matter and energy density.

The next chapter briefly reviews essential features of the evolving Universe and their theoretical treatment. Here I stress that only one Universe exists, where all physical phenomena and our activities, including theorising, occur. Thus, there is a unique metric, determined observationally. There is explicitly an absolute time, synchronised everywhere and represented by coordinate $t$ with constant metric factor $-\mechanical{c}^2$. This conflicts with relativity of simultaneity and Lorentz transformations. Yet we use one physics in the laboratory and another when studying the Universe. There is no scope for arbitrary cosmological models and parameters. Parameters are only approximately observed, allowing freedom in modelling. But *the gravity of matter in this single Universe is a permanent background to all physical processes*. It is absent from our fundamental theories, including the GTR. This is a severe inconsistency in relativity and dynamics.

> [!note]
> The Universe’s gravity should have been integral to Einstein’s gravity equations, as he nearly recognised in his 1918 discussion of Mach’s principle. Omitting this permanent gravity is the GTR’s incompleteness.

Cosmic Relativity closes this gap through a new ‘Centenary Einstein’s Equation’,

$$
\mechanical{G}_{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{\Lambda}\mechanical{g}_{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{\kappa}\energy{T}^{U}_{\mu\nu}=\mechanical{\kappa}\energy{T}_{\mu\nu}. \tag{7.35}
$$

Here $\energy{T}^U$, the factual Universe’s matter–energy tensor, is a permanent necessary addition. The $\mechanical{\Lambda}$ term is optional for theoretical generality; personally, I think observations will show it to be zero. The tensor $\energy{T}^{U}_{\mu\nu}$ sources the factual gravitational potential of all universal matter. The Newtonian cosmic potential, estimated as $\int(4\pi\mechanical{G}\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{r}^2)\,d\mechanical{r}/\mechanical{r}$, is finite in a Universe of finite age and nearly equals $\mechanical{c}^2$. Chapter 1 discussed this calculation.

Equation (7.35) is the correct gravitation theory in place of the conventional GTR equation. The proof is simple: it has a privileged absolute motion frame and absolute time, even for linear inertial motion. These agree with observations such as Galilean light propagation, CMBR dipole anisotropy, GNSS/GPS, and time-dilation measurements. It automatically gives inertial forces as real gravitational forces and a Galilean relative light velocity, as experimentally confirmed. It includes all standard GTR predictions and agrees with tested relativistic gravitation. The difference is that every phenomenon occurs in one Universe, in one unique absolute gravitational background. Thus, it conflicts directly with the STR and removes general coordinate freedom for multiple spaces and Universes.
