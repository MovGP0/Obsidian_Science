> [!TLDR]
> General relativity explains planetary and light trajectories, supports cosmology and compact-object astronomy, and supplies time corrections for satellite navigation.

## Physical question and model

Which observations test distinct parts of a gravitational model? Orbital precession, light deflection, clock redshift, waveforms, and satellite ranging are different observables. Each requires source parameters and nuisance effects in addition to the field equations.

## Lecture account

The GTR’s achievements as a gravity theory have been spectacular in several fields. Cosmology became tractable only after its formulation. Its insights are indispensable in astronomy and astrophysics, as indicated by its first two tested triumphs: explaining Mercury’s excess perihelion precession and verifying light bending by the Sun. Applications then extended to black holes, gravitational waves, and many other phenomena. Today it surrounds every branch of astronomy and astrophysics and successfully treats objects across all mass and velocity ranges. It also touches everyday life in Global Navigational Satellite Systems (GNSS), such as GPS (Chapter 11). Gravitational time dilation of precision atomic clocks, proportional to local gravitational potentials, is a crucial correction that maintains navigation and location precision.

Celestial mechanics is another area where planetary and satellite ephemerides, natural and artificial, can be charted precisely only with the GTR. This became practical around 1950 when the International Astronomical Union chose ephemeris time as the ultimate time standard and derived the standard second from it. Repeating planetary orbits, depending only on fundamental natural laws, were thought to offer the most stable time standard, despite operational difficulties and accessibility. This standard lasted less than a decade: a convenient universal atomic frequency standard became available around the same time and quickly replaced the time-measurement reference and definition of the second [23].

## Interpretation and checks

A successful fit to one effect does not prove every interpretive premise. Conversely, an alternative that reproduces one weak-field formula must also meet independent strong-field and propagation constraints. Build a small evidence ledger: prediction, measured quantity, fitted parameters, and untested regime. Treat laboratory gravity and binary dynamics as complementary tests rather than interchangeable confirmations.
