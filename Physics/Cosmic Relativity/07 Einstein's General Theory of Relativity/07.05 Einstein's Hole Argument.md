> [!TLDR]
> Einstein’s hole argument considered coordinate choices that agree outside an empty region but give different metric components within it.

## Physical question and model

Does general covariance make the gravitational prediction indeterminate? The hole argument compares field configurations related by a transformation supported inside a region. One must specify whether coordinate labels themselves identify physical events.

## Lecture account

Einstein’s ‘hole argument’ is important to the choices made about the GTR, so it is best quoted fully for precision and clarity. In the ‘Comments’ [15] on the 1914 founding paper, ‘Outline of Generalised Theory of Relativity and Theory of Gravitation’ [7], Einstein presented it as follows. Here $\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$ is matter’s energy–momentum tensor and $\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}$ is the metric (Fig. 7.3).

![[cosmic-relativity-07-03-hole-argument.svg|**Fig. 7.3** Einstein’s hole argument. The white hole contains no matter. Two coordinate choices, blue and red, agree outside it, where matter is distributed. Inside, a coordinate differs and the corresponding metrics differ, for example g₀₀ ≠ g′₀₀. Both solve the equations. The argument concludes that generally covariant equations do not uniquely determine gravitational potentials from the matter distribution, potentially giving different clock rates at the same location in the hole.]]

> When we were writing this paper we felt it a deficiency of the theory that we did not succeed in constructing equations for the gravitational field that are generally covariant, i.e., covariant with respect to arbitrary substitutions. Subsequently I found out, however, that the equations that determine $\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}$ univocally from the $\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$ and are generally covariant cannot exist at all; the proof of this is obtained as follows.
>
> Suppose our four-dimensional manifold contains a part L in which no “material process” takes place, in which, therefore, the $\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$ vanish. According to our assumption, the $\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}$ are determined everywhere, and thus also in the interior of L, by the $\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$ given outside of L. Let us now imagine that new coordinates $\mechanical{x}'_\nu$ of the following kind are introduced instead of the original coordinates $\mechanical{x}_\nu$. Outside of L, let us have everywhere $\mechanical{x}'_\nu=\mechanical{x}_\nu$, but inside L let us have $\mechanical{x}'_\nu\ne\mechanical{x}_\nu$ at least for a part of L and at least for one index $\nu$. It is clear that by means of such a substitution one can obtain $\mechanical{\gamma}'_{\mu\nu}\ne\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}$ for at least a part of L. On the other hand, one will have $\energy{\Theta}'_{\mu\nu}=\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$ everywhere, namely, outside L because for this region $\mechanical{x}'_\nu=\mechanical{x}_\nu$, but inside L because in this region $\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}=0=\energy{\Theta}'_{\mu\nu}$. From this follows

> that in the case under consideration, where all substitution are admitted as justified, more than one system of the $\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}$ are associated with the same system of the $\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$.
>
> Thus, if one sticks to the demand—as has been done in our paper—that the $\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}$ should be completely determined by the $\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$, then one is forced to restrict the choice of the reference system. In our paper we realised this restriction by postulating the validity of the conservation laws, i.e., the validity of four equations of the form of equations (19), for the material process and gravitational field taken together. This is in fact the postulate from which we derived the equations for the gravitational field (18) in Sect. 7.5.
>
> Equations are covariant only with respect to linear transformations...

This completes the hole argument and justification for limiting the 1914 GTR, and even much later, to ‘linearly covariant’ equations. The relevant equations, reversed from their order in the paper, are

$$
(19):\quad\sum_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{x}_\nu}\left\{\sqrt{-\mechanical{g}}\,\mechanical{g}_{\sigma\nu}(\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}+\energy{\theta}_{\mu\nu})\right\}=0, \tag{7.17}
$$

$$
(18):\quad\Delta_{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{\gamma})=\mechanical{\kappa}(\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}+\energy{\theta}_{\mu\nu}), \tag{7.18}
$$

$$
(17):\quad\Gamma_{\mu\nu}=\Delta_{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{\gamma})-\mechanical{\kappa}\energy{\theta}_{\mu\nu}. \tag{7.19}
$$

The hole argument was repeated in detailed expositions over the next two years. For example, Einstein and Grossmann’s 1914 paper, ‘Covariance properties of the field equations of the theory of gravitation based on the general theory of relativity’, states: “We want to now show that, completely independent of the gravitational equations we established, a complete determination of the fundamental tensor $\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}$ of a gravitational field with given $\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$ by a generally-covariant system of equations is impossible.”

In 1915 Einstein changed his view and shifted to the fully generally covariant equations that now represent the GTR. In the first paper using the new principle, transformations were general but restricted to metric determinant 1 [9]:

> Just as the special theory of relativity is based upon the postulate that all equations have to be covariant relative to linear orthogonal transformations, so the theory developed here rests upon the postulate of the covariance of all systems of equations relative to transformations with the substitution determinant 1.

At this stage, Einstein had to assume $\sum\energy{T}^{\mu}_{\mu}=0$ for matter, like the electromagnetic energy–momentum tensor. He tried unsuccessfully to justify it by saying we did not know much about matter: “One now has to remember that by our knowledge ‘matter’ is not to be perceived as something primitively given or physically plain.” Finally, on 25 November 1915, he found the correct solution: “I now quite recently found that one can get away without this hypothesis about the energy tensor of matter merely by inserting it into the field equations in a slightly different way.” He meant replacement of $\energy{T}_{\mu\nu}$ by $\energy{T}_{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{g}_{\mu\nu}\energy{T}/2$, giving the final equations.

However, no fault in the hole argument is apparent, although Einstein justified his change *verbally, without proof*, by denying physical meaning to space and coordinates that do not refer explicitly to physical events defined by material processes. I do not enter a detailed discussion of this justification’s validity. It seems possible to show that the original argument has compelling physical reasons and is logically and mathematically valid. Let us examine it carefully.

## Interpretation and checks

In the standard relational reading, diffeomorphism-related complete configurations describe the same observable situation. Match matter, metric, worldlines, and measurement events, rather than comparing one metric component at a bare coordinate label. A new coordinate assignment is not an additional clock experiment. Initial-value uniqueness is correspondingly formulated up to this gauge freedom, with appropriate constraints and boundary data.
