## Physical question and model

What would make the proposed Centenary equation a new gravity theory? State the total source, the dynamics of its cosmic part, the metric variation, and the coordinate-independent observable being predicted. Merely changing which side contains a tensor does not supply new dynamics.

## Lecture account

For macroscopic physics, the answer is simple: my modified Centenary Einstein’s equation is the correct relation between gravitational fields and matter, consistent with the observed Universe. Its empirical support and consistency with the Universe’s absolute space and time ensure this. Universal matter’s gravity cannot be eliminated through free fall or selection of another Universe. Gravity equations must therefore include this permanent background. The modification is simple because the existing mathematical structure is sound. Einstein’s GTR lacked recognition that the theory operates within this Universe’s matter and gravity. The extended equation is

$$
\mechanical{R}_{\mu\nu}-\frac12\mechanical{g}_{\mu\nu}\mechanical{R}-\mechanical{\Lambda}\mechanical{g}_{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{\kappa}\energy{T}^{U}_{\mu\nu}=\mechanical{\kappa}\energy{T}_{\mu\nu}. \tag{7.36}
$$

The universal matter–energy tensor $\energy{T}^{U}_{\mu\nu}$ and its gravity precede everything in human history. A theory of gravity, relativity, and dynamics ignoring it cannot be complete and correct. This unavoidable cosmic completion of the GTR rejects the STR and Lorentz transformations. In a freely falling frame here, the metric is the FLRW metric transformed to the moving frame, rather than Minkowski (Fig. 7.7). ## Interpretation and checks

Take a covariant divergence of the complete equation and verify compatibility with source conservation. Then recover both a local weak-field solution and a consistent cosmological background. A preferred matter congruence can be described covariantly; it need not abolish coordinate freedom. Until a distinct instrument prediction is calculated, the claimed replacement of special relativity remains an interpretation or additional assumption.
