> [!TLDR]
> The author defines physical time through comparisons between changing states of matter rather than as an independently flowing entity.

## Physical question and model

What is a clock reading physically? It is a calibrated count or phase of a reference process along a worldline. Separate the measured phase, the instrument's environmental response, and the geometric elapsed time assigned by a model.

## Lecture account

Physical time is the evolution of the physical states of matter. Both experience and measurement of time compare one local evolution with another. In the author's account, this relative comparison is physical time in every situation. Empty space without matter provides no physical basis for time.

The author argues that this is difficult to grasp because the experience of time is deeply established in living beings through biological evolution. Pure empty space without matter is outside ordinary experience. We therefore take for granted an independently flowing time with an indefinite past and future.

He instead argues that physical time requires matter and changing physical states. Its minimal meaning is comparison between states of the same piece of matter or of different pieces of matter. An observer is a complex physical structure with many evolving states. This makes intellectual separation from the experience of time difficult. A focused analysis nevertheless leads the author to conclude that time does not exist independently of physical states of matter [9].

Uniform particle motion is the simplest example of a changing physical state. Time arises when this motion is compared with another particle's motion. One particle's dynamics then serves as a clock. Since an inertial trajectory is not confined to a local region, uniform periodic motion is more convenient for a clock.

The essential point is that time cannot be separated, even conceptually, from matter and its changing physical states. A clock's rate measures the dynamics of a physical reference system. The reference may be Earth's rotation, an oscillating quartz crystal, or an atomic transition coupled to resonant light. This connection between physical time and physical clocks permits discussion of changes in time due to motion and gravity.

## Interpretation and checks

Different clocks need not share every perturbation: electric fields can shift one transition while affecting another differently. A universal ideal-clock law is therefore a substantive tested relation, not a consequence of calling all evolution time. Ask which local comparison could distinguish a common rate rescaling from a change of unit. Specify a reference process before proposing an absolute clock.
