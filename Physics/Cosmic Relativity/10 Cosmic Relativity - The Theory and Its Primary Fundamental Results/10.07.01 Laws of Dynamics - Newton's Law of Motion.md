> [!TLDR]
> The author examines inertial mass in Newton’s equation and seeks its physical origin in a particle’s gravitational interaction with universal matter.

## Physical question and model

How does a time-dependent vector potential produce a proposed inertial response? Fix its units and sign, state the source motion, and distinguish the applied force from the reaction term. Relativistic momentum has different longitudinal and transverse acceleration responses.

## Lecture account

Newton’s law was postulated more than 300 years ago and has needed only second-order relativistic modifications. It starts with the observationally abstracted law of inertia: force-free motion is straight unless the body is at rest. This predates Newton and appears in the GTR as the geodesic principle, where force-free motion follows the ‘straightest line’. Applied forces produce acceleration:

$$
\mechanical{m}_i\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}\equiv\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{\mathbf F}. \tag{10.15}
$$

Here $\mechanical{m}_i$ is inertia or inertial mass. Acceleration is proportional to applied force, which may come from any interaction, including gravity. More generally, $d\mechanical{\mathbf p}/dt=\mechanical{\mathbf F}$, with momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$. Why does dynamics have this law?

Inertia is a new quantity introduced by the law. Identifying it with a body’s amount of matter or mass is empirical. Its physical nature, apparently the same as gravity’s source mass, remains mysterious even in elementary physics.

Consider gravitational mass $\mechanical{m}$ in cosmic potential $\mechanical{\Phi}_u$. Its gravitational energy is $\energy{E}_g=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\Phi}_u$. Away from large isolated masses, this potential is uniform, with gradients below roughly one part in a billion. Even near galaxies, it varies by no more than $10^{-6}$ over tens of kiloparsecs. This holds for uniform particle motion, so local energy conservation ensures indefinite continuation of motion: Newton’s first law. It follows precisely from uniform cosmic potential, not a property of inertia.

Analyse the uniformly moving particle’s rest frame to obtain potentials it experiences. They are $\mechanical{\Phi}'_u=\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{\Phi}_u$ and $\mechanical{A}'_i=\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{v}_i\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}$, corresponding to $\mechanical{g}'_{00}(\mechanical{v})$ and $\mechanical{g}'_{0i}(\mechanical{v})$. *Any trajectory change makes the experienced potentials time-dependent.* Motion converts this to spatial variation or force, $d\mechanical{\Phi}_u/dt=(d\mechanical{\Phi}_u/d\mechanical{x})d\mechanical{x}/dt=\mechanical{v}(d\mechanical{\Phi}_u/d\mechanical{x})$. Force is proportional to acceleration and interaction energy $-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{\Phi}_u$. Thus, a trajectory changes only through external force. Changing speed changes the vector potential proportionally:

$$
\frac1{\mechanical{c}}\frac{d}{dt}(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{A}'_i)=-\mechanical{F}=-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}_i\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\left(\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{m}\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}+\mechanical{\gamma}^3\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}_i\left[\frac{\mechanical{v}_i}{\mechanical{c}^2}\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}\right]\right). \tag{10.16}
$$

Acceleration $\mechanical{a}\equiv d\mechanical{v}/dt$ splits into longitudinal and transverse components. With no acceleration along velocity,

$$
\mechanical{F}=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{m}\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\mechanical{\gamma}\frac{d\mechanical{p}}{dt}. \tag{10.17}
$$

For longitudinal acceleration, rearrangement gives

$$
\mechanical{F}=\frac{\mechanical{\gamma}^3\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\left((1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}+\frac{\mechanical{v}^2}{\mechanical{c}^2}\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}\right)=-\mechanical{\gamma}^3\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\mechanical{a}. \tag{10.18}
$$

At critical density, the source uses $\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2=1$, giving $\mechanical{F}=-\mechanical{\gamma}^3\mechanical{m}\mechanical{a}$. The applied force required is $\mechanical{F}=\mechanical{\gamma}^3\mechanical{m}\mechanical{a}$. This is relativistic Newtonian dynamics, identifying negative cosmic gravitational energy as inertia against acceleration. *Inertial mass is gravitational mass ‘dressed’ by the Universe’s gravity*:

$$
\mechanical{m}_i=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\mechanical{m}. \tag{10.19}
$$

> [!note]
> The source’s signs are retained. Equation (10.18) changes sign although its bracket simplifies to acceleration. Also, the positive relation $\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2=1$ uses a potential magnitude, while (10.10) gives $\mechanical{\Phi}_u\approx-\mechanical{c}^2$. A consistent distinction between signed potential, its magnitude, applied force, and reaction force is needed to reconcile these steps.

Newton’s law has thus been derived from Cosmic Relativity’s central premise. Gravitational charge $\mechanical{m}$ couples to cosmic potential; required force is proportional to acceleration times the potential energy’s equivalent mass, $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2$. The law becomes a physical gravitational effect rather than an a priori postulate. Its associated domino effects follow below.

For $\mechanical{v}\ll\mechanical{c}$, familiar $\mechanical{F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{a}$ results. The full acceleration law resembles electrodynamic Lenz reaction when a charge accelerates in a magnetic potential. Newton’s law is presented as a magnetic-like gravitational effect. Vehicle acceleration stresses are gravitomagnetic reactions dominated by distant universal matter.

The three spatial components $\mechanical{g}_{0i}=-\mechanical{v}_i/\mechanical{c}=\mechanical{A}_i$ resemble an electromagnetic vector potential, hence ‘gravitomagnetic’. Relative matter and energy currents source it, with $\nabla\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf A}=0$. Unlike electromagnetism, a vector gravitational potential cannot exist without scalar potential $\mechanical{\Phi}$, because mass has only one gravitational-charge sign.

## Interpretation and checks

Differentiate the Lorentz factor as well as velocity. The velocity-parallel term carries an inverse light-speed-squared factor and vanishes for acceleration exactly perpendicular to velocity. The printed potential is negative in one convention while inertial mass is positive, so the mass identification must consistently use its magnitude or the compensating minus sign. Recovering the correct relativistic momentum law requires more than matching one scalar acceleration expression.
