> [!TLDR]
> The author treats mass and spin as dynamical quantities whose responses to forces and torques require a physical interaction with cosmic matter.

## Physical question and model

How is intrinsic spin related to a macroscopic angular-momentum response? Quantum spin has a measurable angular-momentum expectation and torque response, but it is not generally a literal rigid-body mass orbit. A proposed gravitomagnetic coupling needs its own Hamiltonian.

## Lecture account

Mass and spin are fundamental quantities in the dynamics of neutral elementary particles. They determine responses to forces and torques. Spin is genuine angular momentum even for electrons and photons, as shown when the combined spin of many particles is transferred to a rotating bulk body [25].

Electric charge and magnetic moment source electric and magnetic fields. In contrast, the author states that the standard particle model associates no field with inertial mass and spin. Einstein's theory treats gravitational mass and spin as gravitational sources, but does not describe their inertial dynamics as a field–charge interaction or an interaction between currents. He argues that a conventional physical interaction is therefore missing from that account.

In Cosmic Relativity, mass is the charge of gravity, while angular momentum and spin are circulating currents of that charge. *Spin angular momentum is to gravity what magnetic moment is to electromagnetism.* The author uses this analogy to analyse spin dynamics in cosmic gravity [1, 26]. Its many consequences require a separate chapter.

Spin, as a gravitomagnetic moment, couples to matter-energy currents, just as an electromagnetic moment couples to an electric current or magnetic field. In a moving frame, the source gives the cosmic relativistic potential $\mechanical{A}_i=-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}$ and a mass-coupling expression $-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2$.

For a rotating frame in cosmic matter, it gives the gravitomagnetic field

$$
\mechanical{B}_g=\mechanical{c}\nabla\times\mechanical{g}_{0i}=-\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf v}=-2\boldsymbol{\Omega}.
$$

Here $\boldsymbol{\Omega}$ is frame angular velocity. The Coriolis force is one manifestation of this field. The field couples to mass currents. A particle with angular momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf l}$ has an energy proportional to $\mechanical{\mathbf l}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g$. The exact spin coupling is $\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g/2$, with an additional factor of two relative to magnetic-moment coupling $\magnetic{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}$. Chapter 13 develops the expressions and consequences.

> [!note]
> The source calls $-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2$ an interaction. With the potential units used here, this expression has momentum units, not energy units; a complete interaction-energy expression would need an additional velocity coupling. The gravitational field $\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g$ has angular-frequency units and is distinct from electromagnetic flux density $\magnetic{\mathbf B}$.

The author lists the following spin results of Cosmic Relativity:

- Classical and quantum dynamics of spinning particles and gyroscopes.
- Atomic fine structure and relativistic Thomas precession.
- Geometric phases of spinning particles on helical paths and in rotating frames.
- Spin-selective particle transport in helical molecules and molecular structures.
- The spin–statistics connection for fermions and bosons.

He highlights the claimed physical explanation of spin–statistics: cosmic gravity determines collective quantum behaviour, including Bose–Einstein condensation of identical bosons and Pauli exclusion of identical fermions.

## Interpretation and checks

Separate the operator, its eigenvalues, and its ensemble expectation. A classical current analogy can motivate a coupling but cannot alone determine every quantum factor or exchange rule. Check that spin times an angular-frequency-like field has energy units. When comparing field conventions, track whether the rotation is that of the lab or of the cosmic current, which have opposite signs.
