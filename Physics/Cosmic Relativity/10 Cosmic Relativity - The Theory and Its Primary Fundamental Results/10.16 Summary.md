> [!TLDR]
> This chapter develops the author's cosmic-gravity account of inertia, relativistic motion, equivalence principles, clock rates, electrodynamics, and spin.

## Physical question and model

Reconstruct the chapter's proposed mechanism through source, potential, coupling, and observable. The common cosmic-potential factor is not itself a measurement; forces, clock phases, and circuit voltages are the readouts to compare.

## Lecture account

The chapter develops primary results of the cosmic gravitational paradigm. It focuses on the general results underlying dynamics and relativity, leaving many specific applications to later chapters.

The basic gravitational elements are cosmic matter mass and its relative currents. For a frame moving at $\mechanical{v}(t)$ relative to the absolute cosmic frame, they define scalar and vector potentials $\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{\Phi}_u$ and $-\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{v}\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}$. The author argues that dynamics and relativity follow from these without additional postulates.

He derives the principle of relativity for inertially moving observers from homogeneous cosmic potentials, presenting this as a physical explanation of that principle.

For noninertial motion, he claims first-principles derivations of Newton's law, the origin of inertia, the universal inertial-to-gravitational mass ratio (WEP), universality of free fall, and local gravity–acceleration equivalence (EEP). He presents these as a unified account of principles developed from Galileo to Einstein.

The chapter identifies the physical content of Newton's law with WEP. A precision test of Newtonian dynamics in any force field is thus also a test of WEP in gravity. Centrifugal and Coriolis forces, traditionally called fictitious, are instead attributed to genuine gravitational interactions with the velocity-dependent cosmic potential. The author claims to resolve the centuries-old problem of inertial-force origins.

For free fall, the chapter distinguishes two forces that balance only locally: the reactive cosmic force from a time-dependent, spatially homogeneous potential, represented by $d\mechanical{A}_i/dt$, and local gravitational field $\mechanical{\mathbf g}=-\nabla\mechanical{\phi}$. In this account, the local field remains present and is balanced by another kind of gravitational force.

The author identifies an important consequence: EEP is claimed to fail for relative clock rates. A genuine gravitational field produces differential time dilation, while a common accelerated frame produces none between separated clocks.

The author takes the accelerated-clock prediction as evidence that GTR is incomplete and that further gravitational physics remains beyond it.

Motional time dilation is presented as gravitational dilation from the altered cosmic potential of a moving clock. It depends on each clock's velocity relative to the unique cosmic frame, rather than on velocity relative to another clock. The author claims this provides reliable calculations for all applications, especially satellite navigation clocks such as GPS.

He argues that absolute motional dilation removes twin-clock ambiguity and explains why clocks transported westward around Earth can run faster than stationary laboratory clocks. In this account, westward transport reduces the relevant absolute speed.

The chapter also revisits optical aberration, Fresnel drag, Doppler shift, and unipolar induction, stressing absolute velocity. The unipolar-induction account distinguishes two mechanisms: direct current–current interaction when the conductor moves through the magnet field, and an electric field from unequal contraction of boundary charge densities when the magnet moves past a stationary conductor.

For linear motion, these mechanisms have equal magnitudes and opposite signs, so only relative magnet–conductor motion remains relevant to the net induction. Rotational motion reveals their difference. Contraction-induced charge has cylindrical symmetry and a uniform interior potential, so it gives no interior electric field to cancel the conductor-motion force. The author claims that this explains all observed unipolar-induction cases and gives new annular-magnet predictions. He concludes that electromagnetic charge and magnet velocities refer to the absolute cosmic frame.

Finally, the chapter identifies spin with a closed current of gravitational charge, analogous to electromagnetic magnetic moment. Rotating frames have a large relative cosmic matter current. The curl of its velocity-dependent potential gives the gravitomagnetic field

$$
\mechanical{B}_g=-\frac{2\boldsymbol{\Omega}\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}.
$$

The spin interaction energy is $\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g/2$. Chapter 13 discusses the proposed effects, including the claimed explanation of the relation between spin and collective particle behaviour, separating fermions from bosons.

## Interpretation and checks

Choose one result and identify its low-speed limit, sign convention, and strongest untested assumption. Then calculate the same observable in a standard inertial or covariant description. If both predictions agree, retain that as consistency while reserving mechanism selection for a test where they differ. The later spin and Hall chapters require additional quantum assumptions and should not inherit proof status merely from this overview.
