title: "Synchronisation of Clocks in Cosmic Relativity"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The author proposes a universal cosmic clock and a light-signal synchronisation scheme based on absolute velocity and Galilean light propagation.

## Physical question and model

Does the CMB supply a synchronised clock network? A thermal-history model can associate a mean radiation temperature with cosmic age. A local measurement does not by itself compare separated events or set remote clock readings.

## Lecture account

Cosmic Relativity recognises a cosmic clock with universal time synchronised throughout the Universe. This formally matches the time coordinate in the FLRW line element. The monotonically decreasing CMBR temperature represents this time. The source gives the mean temperature as $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm{CMBR}}=2.726\,\unit{K}$ and states that it is uniform to a few microkelvin, corresponding to a cosmic-clock precision better than one part in a million, $10^{-6}$. In principle, events everywhere can refer to this one time coordinate.

The author therefore treats Poincaré–Einstein light-signal synchronisation as a convention, without a direct implication for the physical time of separated clocks. CMBR temperature changes slowly. The claimed conflict with Einstein synchronisation becomes measurable only when synchronisation distances make $\mechanical{v}\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{c}^2$ large enough relative to the resolution of temperature-derived time changes. He argues that the required distance is still much smaller than intergalactic distances.

A local clock could conceptually be synchronised with the local CMBR temperature. A practical procedure instead uses electromagnetic signals. The proposed absolute reference clock is in a frame with no CMBR dipole anisotropy. The author states that absolute synchronisation requires recognising Galilean light propagation, rather than an invariant relative light velocity. It also requires accounting for each clock's dilation from its absolute velocity relative to the cosmic frame.

In the conceptual scheme, an absolute reference clock sends light signals to another clock. Doppler-shift data combined with two-way timing synchronise the clocks to the reference clock's precision. The author also permits practical Poincaré–Einstein synchronisation as a consensus convention, provided Galilean propagation is explicitly understood. He states that inconsistencies appear in round-trip experiments, which Chapter 11 discusses.

## Interpretation and checks

The dipole must be removed, foregrounds modelled, and intrinsic anisotropies retained in a temperature inference. A quoted microkelvin variation is not automatically a global timing accuracy; converting it to time needs the derivative of temperature with cosmic time and its uncertainties. Test a nonmonotonic thermal history: the same temperature could label several epochs. Cosmic time in FLRW is a model coordinate tied to comoving observers.
