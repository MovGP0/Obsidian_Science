## Physical question and model

When does the time–time metric component determine a clock reading? Only for a worldline fixed in the spatial coordinates of that chart, with the time-coordinate normalisation stated. Rod length needs a spatial measurement and simultaneity rule, not only the time–time component.

## Lecture account

The moving-frame factor $\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$ follows from Galilean transformation $\mechanical{x}'=\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}t$ for absolute cosmic motion. This has major implications for relativistic dynamics. A clock moving relative to cosmic matter, with coordinate time $t'$, has physically dilated time:

$$
d\mechanical{\tau}=\sqrt{-\mechanical{g}'_{00}}\,dt'=(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)^{1/2}dt'. \tag{10.7}
$$

Thus, *time dilation is not relatively symmetric*: the clock changing position relative to universal matter is physically dilated. An arbitrary inertial rest-frame choice is unavailable. A clock in a frame measuring a CMBR dipole is the dilated one. The author states that existing experiments confirm this. Dilation of clocks compared in one frame depends on that frame’s absolute velocity, unlike STR dependence only on their relative motion.

The spatial metric $\mechanical{\gamma}_{ab}$ determines $d\mechanical{l}'^2=\mechanical{\gamma}_{ab}d\mechanical{x}^a d\mechanical{x}^b$. Later, homogeneity of relativistic gravitational potentials will yield the Principle of Relativity (PoR), *derived rather than postulated*. Anticipating it, we derive spatial-distance modification.

A finite distance can be measured from one reference location only by two-way transport of light or a body between its boundaries. Consider a frame moving uniformly in the x direction at $\mechanical{v}$ and a test body moving at constant relative speed $\mechanical{u}$. If round-trip duration is $\mechanical{T}'$, the PoR requires measured distance $\mechanical{d}'$ to equal the result $\mechanical{d}'_0$ at $\mechanical{v}=0$. Since $\mechanical{d}'=\mechanical{u}\mechanical{T}'/2$, time dilation affects distance measurement as $\mechanical{d}'=\sqrt{-\mechanical{g}'_{00}}\,\mechanical{u}\mechanical{T}'_0/2$. The spatial distance therefore receives the same factor:

$$
\mechanical{d}'=\sqrt{-\mechanical{g}'_{00}}\,\mechanical{d}'_0=(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)^{1/2}\mechanical{d}'_0. \tag{10.8}
$$

This is length contraction as an absolute effect.

It is remarkable that Galilean transformations in the matter-filled Universe yield essential relativistic changes of time and space. The common belief that they conflict with the PoR and relativistic effects is reversed: because the Universe is not empty, Lorentz transformations are incompatible with its factual nature.

Relativistic physics rests on these velocity-dependent length and time changes and the invariance of interaction charges, such as electric charge and gravitational mass. This reveals Cosmic Relativity’s scope.

## Interpretation and checks

The source clock factor has the expected zero-speed limit. Its separate length rule does not follow from that component alone: spatial metric terms, cross terms, endpoint events, and material dynamics must be considered. For an oblique moving rod, predict which components of its measured length change under the chosen convention. A universal multiplication of every distance by a clock factor requires a further derivation.
