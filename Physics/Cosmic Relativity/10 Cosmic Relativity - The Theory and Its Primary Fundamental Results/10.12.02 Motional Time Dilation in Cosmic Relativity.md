> [!TLDR]
> Cosmic Relativity treats motional time dilation as gravitational time dilation caused by the velocity-dependent potential of cosmic matter.

## Physical question and model

How does the author's transformed cosmic potential alter a clock phase? Specify the clock energy splitting, the coupling term, and which time parameter evolves its phase. A phase must be dimensionless, with action divided by the reduced Planck constant.

## Lecture account

In Cosmic Relativity, moving clocks change rate because of cosmic gravity. *Motional time dilation is gravitational time dilation.* The author argues that gravity is the only interaction that can change every clock's rate, regardless of its nature and mechanism.

A clock at rest in the cosmic frame experiences potential $\mechanical{\Phi}_u$ and has gravitational energy $-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\Phi}_u$. All clocks at relative rest experience the same homogeneous potential, which already determines their rates. Only comparisons between rates are measurable.

For a clock moving at absolute speed $\mechanical{v}$ relative to the average cosmic matter frame, the experienced potential changes:

$$
\mechanical{\Phi}'_u=\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{\Phi}_u=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}}.
$$

Its magnitude is greater than the rest-frame cosmic potential. The author interprets the slower moving clock as a clock in a larger-magnitude gravitational potential. Two nearby clocks can therefore experience different potentials if they move differently. Their relative rates are

$$
\frac{T'}{T_0}=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{\Phi}'_u}=\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}. \tag{10.30}
$$

The author connects this with the metric derived for motion relative to the FLRW isotropic frame. The temporal component changes to $\mechanical{g}'_{00}=-(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)$ in the frame with velocity $\mechanical{v}(t)$. An elementary clock duration becomes

$$
\Delta T'=\sqrt{-\mechanical{g}'_{00}}\,\Delta T_0.
$$

The proposed dilation depends on each clock's absolute velocity relative to the cosmic frame. The motion need not be inertial. No sequence of instantaneous inertial frames is needed. Relative time dilation can be calculated from the independent dilation of each clock using the magnitude of its absolute velocity. The author states that this predicts the asymmetry observed in the [[09.08 The Hafele-Keating Experiment|Hafele–Keating experiment]]. Chapter 11 reviews the experimental comparison.

The author then asks for the physical cause of dilation in every physical process, regardless of its interactions or mechanism. He argues that neither STR's kinematic account nor GTR answers this question, while Cosmic Relativity provides a gravitational explanation.

The proposed physical cause is gravity's modification of inertia. Gravitational interaction between a body's mass $\mechanical{m}$ and the rest of the Universe generates inertial mass

$$
\mechanical{m}_i=\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}.
$$

A change in gravitational interaction energy, $\energy{\delta E}_g=\mechanical{m}\,\delta\mechanical{\phi}$, whether from motion relative to cosmic matter or from local gravitating bodies, adds inertia:

$$
\delta\mechanical{m}_i=\frac{\mechanical{m}\,\delta\mechanical{\phi}}{\mechanical{c}^2}.
$$

The author identifies the resulting slower dynamics as the cause of gravitational time dilation in every physical process. This also connects the notions of time and physical clocks.

An atomic clock makes the connection especially clear. A quantum state of constant energy evolves through oscillator phase $\signal{\varphi}(t)=2\pi\signal{\nu}t$, with fixed frequency $\signal{\nu}=\energy{E}/\energy{h}$. Time may be identified with accumulated phase. Selecting an oscillator frequency as a common standard defines a standard time, as with a specified atomic transition.

An atomic state of energy $\energy{E}_s$ interacts gravitationally with the rest of the Universe. The author associates this with cumulative change in its quantum phase or action [18]. For constant interaction energy, he separates the phase into an energy-dependent factor and the variable $T$ called time:

$$
\signal{\varphi}=T\left(\frac{\energy{E}_s\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{c}^2}\right).
$$

Small local perturbations of the cosmic potential, such as Earth's potential, then change the rate of quantum phase evolution. The familiar GTR dilation $\delta t=\delta\mechanical{\phi}/\mechanical{c}^2$ is described as a local perturbation of a global time already controlled by cosmic gravity. *An added local potential affects a process that is already gravitational.*

> [!note]
> The source writes $\delta t=\delta\mechanical{\phi}/\mechanical{c}^2$. The right-hand side is dimensionless, so this expression must denote a fractional clock-rate change rather than a time interval in seconds.

The author presents this as a unified account of motional and local gravitational time dilation. He states that experimental evidence agrees with cosmic gravitational time dilation when velocity relative to the cosmic frame is used. He further argues that it supplies the correct basis for atomic-clock calculations in GNSS, including GPS; see [[11.08 Experimental Tests of Time Dilation - GNSS and GPS]]. He criticises current timing algorithms as using a preferred-frame calculation while claiming an STR basis for time dilation and light propagation [9].

## Interpretation and checks

The proposed square-root rate factor matches a familiar kinematic result, but equality of factors does not identify a unique cause. Check that the potential ratio, mass correction, and phase expression use the same sign and units. A genuine test needs a predicted difference for two mechanisms or paths after ordinary gravitational and motional proper-time effects are included.
