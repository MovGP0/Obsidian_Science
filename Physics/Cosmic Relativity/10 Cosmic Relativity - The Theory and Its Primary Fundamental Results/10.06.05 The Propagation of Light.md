> [!TLDR]
> Cosmic Relativity predicts Galilean relative light speed; the author derives this from the proposed clock dilation and the Principle of Relativity.

## Physical question and model

Is the claimed propagation law a coordinate speed or a local speed measurement? A Galilean coordinate chart can assign light a direction-dependent coordinate speed while the metric yields the same locally measured vacuum speed. A physical alternative must specify an actual clock-and-detector protocol.

## Lecture account

Light propagation is among physics’ most discussed subjects. Since the STR postulates invariant relative light speed, Cosmic Relativity directly contradicts it. A unique matter-filled-Universe frame gives $\mechanical{c}'=\mechanical{c}-\mechanical{v}$, its most direct prediction and a central experimental test (the source refers to Chapter 12) [2, 10]. Cosmic gravity controls light and dynamics. Light speed is $\mechanical{c}$ in the cosmic rest frame and Galilean in other frames. The author now derives consistency with clock dilation and the PoR.

Consider distance $\mechanical{L}_x$ at absolute rest. Light travels at $\mechanical{c}$ both ways, with the source writing total duration $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}$. Move the apparatus at $\mechanical{v}_y$ perpendicular to $\mechanical{L}_x$. There is dilation but no length contraction. Invariant relative light speed would keep $\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}$ fixed while the clock changes to $\mechanical{T}'=\mechanical{T}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$. This would violate the PoR by letting light detect clock-rate change. The compatible possibility is lower relative speed $\mechanical{c}'=\mechanical{c}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$, matching the dilated time:

$$
\mechanical{T}'=\frac{\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}'}\equiv\frac{\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}}. \tag{10.14}
$$

Then $\mechanical{c}'\mechanical{T}'=\mechanical{c}\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{L}$. Reduced relative light speed preserves the PoR. The author concludes that matter-filled cosmic gravity implies noninvariant relative light speed. From perpendicular propagation, parallel propagation is deduced as $\mechanical{c}\pm\mechanical{v}$ (see the source’s Fig. 3.7).

> [!note]
> The preceding text writes $\mechanical{T}'=\mechanical{T}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$, while (10.14), together with $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}$, gives the reciprocal factor. The source’s two expressions are retained.

## Interpretation and checks

The source's linear directional speed and the square-root effective speed in equation (10.14) refer to different constructions unless explicitly derived together. Check the path, direction, and clock used for each. A round-trip time cannot be replaced by one directional speed without analysis. To distinguish theories, compare their complete phase or arrival predictions after applying one common synchronisation convention.
