> [!TLDR]
> The source obtains integer and fractional filled levels from a degeneracy that depends on both applied field and actual electron density.

## Physical question and model

How does the proposed corrected state count generate a fraction sequence? The author assumes that carriers fill an integer number of effective levels after a density-dependent correction. Solving that filling condition yields familiar rational values.

## Lecture account

The proposed cosmic vector potential changes dynamical action and Landau-level degeneracy. The author claims that prominent states at filling $\nu\geq1/3$ have the same mechanism as the IQHE: weakly interacting electrons fill the available states. No new quasiparticle or changed internal magnetic field is introduced. Coulomb interactions can still determine sublevel energies below and above one.

With $\nu=\semiconductor{\rho}/(\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0)$ and $\magnetic{b}=\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0$, the degeneracy magnitude is

$$
g=\left|\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\magnetic{\phi}_0}-2k\semiconductor{\rho}\right|
=|\magnetic{b}(1-2k\nu)|. \tag{14.9}
$$

For $k=1$ and $\nu=1$, $g=|-\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0|=\magnetic{b}$. The gravitational action is twice the magnetic action and opposite in sign, so the magnitude of the state count remains unchanged.

![[cosmic-relativity-14-08-filled-landau-level.svg|**Fig. 14.8** A completely filled Landau level. The source compares magnetic state counting alone with the cosmic-corrected count. Orbit ellipses indicate electrons, arrows represent magnetic flux units, and dots indicate available states. The count magnitude is unchanged; the physical magnetic field is unchanged.]]

If carrier density exceeds one level's degeneracy, electrons enter the next Landau level in the same field. Filling that level gives the next IQHE state, such as $\nu=2$. The author regards this as recovery of all integer states without changing the theory.

![[cosmic-relativity-14-09-one-third-filling.svg|**Fig. 14.9** At ν = 1/3, five electrons occupy fifteen magnetic orbital states in the left schematic. The proposed cosmic correction leaves five available states, so the same five electrons fill the level. All fifteen magnetic-flux arrows remain.]]

At $\nu=1/3$, the standard lowest-level count is three times carrier density. The proposed correction $g_g=-2\semiconductor{\rho}$ reduces it to $g=\magnetic{B}/(3\magnetic{\phi}_0)=\semiconductor{\rho}$, so the level becomes full (Fig. 14.9).

At $\nu=1/2$, gravitational and magnetic action contributions cancel. The source calls the resulting zero state degeneracy a Fermi sea with free dynamics and no Hall plateau.

![[cosmic-relativity-14-10-half-filling.svg|**Fig. 14.10** Half filling in the source theory. (1) Seven electrons occupy fourteen magnetic states. (2) The gravitational action cancels the magnetic state count, leaving a Fermi-sea description while the applied magnetic field remains present.]]

The physical field is unchanged at $1/3$ and all other fillings. Cosmic gravity instead changes the conduction-electron action. Through Hamilton's relation $\nabla\energy{S}=\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, the author connects this to dynamics in the two-dimensional sample.

For $\nu>1$, the same argument is applied to the partially occupied higher level. At $\nu=4/3=1+1/3$, the lowest level is full and its degeneracy magnitude remains unchanged. The next level is magnetically one-third full; its cosmic-corrected count becomes fully occupied. The author also cites $5/3$ as equally accessible by this mechanism.

![[cosmic-relativity-14-11-four-thirds-filling.svg|**Fig. 14.11** The proposed ν = 4/3 state. Left: a full lowest level and a one-third-occupied next level using magnetic state counting. Right: the lowest level remains full and unchanged, while the higher level becomes full after the proposed gravitational reduction of its available states. The two levels are separated by the cyclotron energy gap.]]

For $\nu=4/3$, the author stresses that only the partially occupied level changes its count magnitude. Neither the charge nor the physical magnetic field is modified, and no added hierarchy is invoked.

For general $\nu<1$ with $k=1$, an integer number of corrected states is filled when

$$
\frac{\semiconductor{\rho}}{g}=\nu^*
=\frac{\semiconductor{\rho}}{\magnetic{b}|1-2\nu|}
=\frac{\nu}{|1-2\nu|}. \tag{14.10}
$$

For a rational filling $p/q$, the source solves

$$
\pm n=\frac{p/q}{1-2p/q}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac pq=\frac{n}{2n\pm1}. \tag{14.11}
$$

This gives the primary sequences $\nu_+=1/3,2/5,3/7,\ldots$ and $\nu_-=1,2/3,3/5,\ldots$. Adding fully filled levels gives $i+p/q$: for example $4/3,7/5,10/7,\ldots$, $7/3,12/5,\ldots$, $5/3,8/5,11/7,\ldots$, and $8/3,13/5,18/7,\ldots$. The author claims that this recovers all prominent fractions at or above $1/3$ without new quasiparticles, strong correlations, pairing, condensates, or phase transitions.

![[cosmic-relativity-14-12-two-fifths-filling.svg|**Fig. 14.12** The ν = 2/5 state in the lowest Landau level. Six electrons initially occupy fifteen magnetic states. The proposed cosmic correction leaves three states per sublevel, making effective filling ν* = 2. Two Coulomb-separated sublevels are therefore occupied.]]

At fractions such as $2/3$, $2/5$, and $3/7$, integer $\nu^*$ requires multiple sublevels inside the lowest Landau level. The author attributes their separation to ordinary Coulomb energy at the actual mean electron spacing, not a changed cyclotron frequency. He writes $\energy{E}_C\simeq\electric{e}^2/(\epsilon\mechanical{l})$. Clean samples are needed to resolve these sublevels. This differs from the CF energy scale $\energy{\hbar}\signal{\Omega}^*$ assigned to a reduced effective field.

> [!note]
> The source caption gives the mean separation as l≈√ρ. For a sheet number density, that has inverse-length units; the expected spacing scale is 1/√ρ. The energy expression also follows the source's electromagnetic unit convention and omits the SI Coulomb factor.

The author identifies the change from magnetic count b to the density-dependent count b(1−2ν) as the central physical feature behind the fractional states in a 2DEG.

## Interpretation and checks

At one-third filling with the first correction, the remaining effective density equals the carrier density. At half filling the denominator vanishes, so the integer-effective-filling argument cannot be used there. Taking an absolute value keeps a density nonnegative but loses orientation information that transport still needs. This algebra identifies candidate fractions. It does not calculate whether they are stable, their excitation gaps, or the gravitational origin of the assumed correction.
