## Physical question and model

Which objections are empirical failures and which are preferences about explanation? A criticism of naturalness or terminology differs from a numerical disagreement with data. Label each reason accordingly before judging the need for a new theory.

## Lecture account

The author accepts that existing theories have phenomenological success. His objections concern their physical basis, naturalness, and inclusion of relevant physics [13]:

1. Integer and fractional states alternate continuously in the same sample, while the theories assign them different mechanisms.
2. Small changes in field or density, sometimes less than 10%, would have to switch between nearly noninteracting and strongly correlated many-particle behaviour.
3. Several inequivalent fractional theories use different assumptions and quasiparticles.
4. Mean Hall resistance follows $\circuit{R}_H=\magnetic{B}/(\semiconductor{\rho}\electric{e})$. Longitudinal resistance is zero on plateaux but remains proportional to applied field at even-denominator fillings such as $3/2$, $3/4$, $1/2$, and $1/4$. The author contrasts this with the CF effective field, which can vanish. With $\magnetic{B}^*=\magnetic{B}(1-2\nu)$, it reverses sign above $\nu=1/2$, whereas the measured Hall-voltage sign does not reverse.
5. Graphene shows strong electron–hole symmetry. The author regards this as more natural in an independent-particle account because holes already represent collective electron transport and have a different momentum-space environment.
6. Fractions above one require nested hierarchies or condensates that the source regards as experimentally unverified.
7. The many fractions make fractional-charge theories increasingly complex.

These are the author's critiques, transcribed from the supplied source.

8. Every physical effect should be traceable to a fundamental interaction. The author restricts bulk condensed-matter explanations to the long-range electromagnetic and gravitational interactions and therefore treats a hypothetical gauge-field account as effective rather than microscopic.

He compares current phenomenological success with geocentric epicycles and calls for a change in physical viewpoint. The preceding spin-phase puzzle is presented as evidence for a velocity-dependent cosmic potential. Although QHE electrons are usually spin-polarised, the author claims that the same potential changes the orbital degeneracy and permits both integer and fractional states.

## Interpretation and checks

Choose one listed objection and turn it into a measurable comparison with controlled assumptions. State what existing theory predicts and what the cosmic proposal predicts for the same sample. Neither effective quasiparticle language nor the use of correlations is by itself a contradiction. A claim that an interaction was neglected needs a justified coupling scale and observable, not only the existence of distant matter.
