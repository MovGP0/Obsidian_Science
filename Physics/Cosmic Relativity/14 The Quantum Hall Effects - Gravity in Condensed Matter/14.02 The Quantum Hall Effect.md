> [!TLDR]
> A two-dimensional electron system shows Hall-resistance plateaux and vanishing longitudinal resistance at suitable filling factors.

## Physical question and model

What distinguishes a quantum Hall plateau from the classical Hall slope? Over a finite range of field or density, the transverse resistance remains near a quantised value while the longitudinal response is strongly suppressed. This is a joint observation, not only an isolated resistance ratio.

## Lecture account

The source describes the QHE in clean, effectively two-dimensional samples at low temperature and strong perpendicular magnetic field. Strong electric confinement in multilayer semiconductor MOSFET structures creates a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG). Monolayer graphene also permits two-dimensional transport. Fields of order $1\,\unit{T}$ or more produce cyclotron orbits much smaller than the sample.

Define the flux quantum $\magnetic{\phi}_0=\energy{h}/\electric{e}$ and the number density of flux quanta $\magnetic{b}=\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0$. This is a field normalisation, not a claim that a magnetic field consists of discrete flux lines. With sheet carrier density $\semiconductor{\rho}$, the filling factor is $\nu=\semiconductor{\rho}/\magnetic{b}$.

Klaus von Klitzing discovered the integer QHE in 1980 [3]:

$$
\circuit{R}_H=\frac{\energy{h}}{\nu\electric{e}^2}.
$$

The source records the 1985 Nobel Prize and gives the resistance standard $\circuit{R}_K=\energy{h}/\electric{e}^2=25812.80745\,\unit{\Omega}$ at $\nu=1$. This numerical value is transcribed from the source.

The resistance remains on a plateau over a finite field interval around integer filling, then changes to the next plateau. In the same regime the longitudinal current is dissipationless:

$$
\circuit{R}_{xx}=\frac{\electric{V}_x}{\electric{I}}=0.
$$

Zero longitudinal contact voltage means no longitudinal resistive loss for steady current. Explaining the plateau width and dissipationless conduction requires both fundamental dynamics and sample features such as impurities [5].

## Interpretation and checks

The ratio of Planck constant to charge squared has resistance units. Predict that increasing the positive filling factor lowers the magnitude of Hall resistance. Finite temperature, contact geometry, disorder, and breakdown current affect departures from the plateau. A formula evaluated at selected integer or fractional values does not explain why the value remains stable between them. Preserve the distinction between resistance measured at contacts and material resistivity.
