## Abstract

Restricting electron motion to a two-dimensional plane produces quantised Hall-resistance plateaux. Integer and fractional filling factors label the observed states. The source contrasts a simple integer-effect theory with effective fractional-effect theories using fractional charges or composite quasiparticles. Cosmic Relativity instead modifies quantum degeneracy through cosmic gravitomagnetic interaction, claimed to provide a microscopic unified theory with predictive scope.

## Introduction

Condensed-matter physics studies charge transport and thermal and mechanical energy in materials. Conventional accounts assign only a small role to terrestrial gravity, such as a minute density gradient in free electrons. The author argues that these accounts omit much larger cosmic gravitational effects [1, 2].

The cosmic electron energy is written

$$
|\energy{E}_g|=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\Phi}_u\simeq\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c}^2.
$$

He calls it the largest term in a conduction electron's Hamiltonian and regards the quantum Hall effects as evidence for a decisive cosmic contribution in materials.

## Interpretation and checks

Keep measured transport separate from a proposed explanation of its state count. A constant energy offset alone changes neither level spacing nor occupation probabilities at fixed particle number. The cosmic proposal therefore needs a specific dynamical coupling with observable consequences. The following sections test its assumptions against the integer limit, fractional states, and transport; reproducing one filling sequence does not establish the complete mechanism.
