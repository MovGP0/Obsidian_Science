> [!TLDR]
> Edge channels carry charge and heat; a microscopic theory must explain their conductance and upstream or downstream thermal transport together.

## Physical question and model

How do boundary modes carry charge and heat? Electrical conductance measures charge response, while thermal conductance measures energy response. They need not assign the same weight to a mode. Specify propagation direction, contacts, and equilibration length.

## Lecture account

Landau energies change near sample boundaries, allowing edge states [26] and dissipationless current [27]. The fixed chirality of cyclotron motion makes transport opposite on opposite edges. Each spin-polarised Landau level contributes one channel of conductance $\electric{e}^2/\energy{h}$.

The quantised edge conductance is

$$
\circuit{G}=\frac{n_c\electric{e}^2}{\energy{h}}=\frac{\nu\electric{e}^2}{\energy{h}}. \tag{14.18}
$$

Hierarchical and CF descriptions use several downstream and upstream edge modes, with total conductance $\circuit{G}=(\electric{e}^2/\energy{h})\sum_i\nu_i$. The source's $2/3$ example has a forward mode of charge weight $-\electric{e}$ and a backward mode $\electric{e}/3$. Its $4/7$ example has one forward mode $-\electric{e}$ and three backward modes $\electric{e}/7$.

The author states that the unmodified $2/3$ scheme would give $\circuit{G}_{2/3}=(1+1/3)\electric{e}^2/\energy{h}=4\electric{e}^2/(3\energy{h})$, whereas the observed value is $2\electric{e}^2/(3\energy{h})$. Interchannel tunnelling is then invoked to change the forward charge weight to $2\electric{e}/3$ and the backward mode to a neutral mode. The author criticises this as an added hypothesis.

These same carriers also carry energy when a temperature gradient exists. A coherent theory must therefore describe electrical and thermal transport in both 2DEGs and graphene. The source describes available experimental indications as difficult for existing schemes to reconcile.

Ballistic thermal transport is quantised in units involving $\pi^2\thermal{k}_B^2/(3\energy{h})$ [28]. Early edge-mode predictions give

$$
\circuit{G}_T=(n_+-n_-)\frac{\pi^2\thermal{k}_B^2}{3\energy{h}}\thermal{T}
\equiv n_c\thermal{\kappa}\thermal{T}. \tag{14.19}
$$

Here $n_+$ and $n_-$ count forward and backward channels. The IQHE has $n_-=0$ and $n_+=\nu$, agreeing with the measurements cited by the source.

In the gravitational account, each filled Landau or Coulomb-split sublevel can provide an electron edge channel. Transport coefficients are expectation values, so the electrical charge weight remains the actual filling $\nu$, even when $\nu^*$ is an integer. Bulk and edge electrical conductances then agree:

$$
\circuit{G}=\frac{\nu\electric{e}^2}{\energy{h}}. \tag{14.20}
$$

Only real electrons with one field-selected chirality are used: the author excludes backward and neutral modes from his proposed mechanism. Each channel contributes at most one thermal-conductance unit, depending on boundary modification. Without mode mixing or equilibration, the predicted bound is printed as

$$
\circuit{G}_T\leq\frac{\pi^2\thermal{k}_B^2}{3\energy{h}}\thermal{T}\sum_i n_i
\equiv\thermal{\kappa}\thermal{T}n_c. \tag{14.21}
$$

For $\nu=p/q$, there are at most $p$ active forward channels, so $0\leq\circuit{G}_T\leq p\thermal{\kappa}\thermal{T}$. For $\nu=n+p/q$, the integer levels give $n$ channels and the fractional part adds at most $p$:

$$
n\thermal{\kappa}\thermal{T}\leq\circuit{G}_T\leq(n+p)\thermal{\kappa}\thermal{T}.
$$

For clean samples with sharp edges, the source expects approximately the upper bound.

## Interpretation and checks

The thermal quantum has conductance units only after multiplication by temperature. In an equilibrated description the net coefficient counts signed chiral degrees of freedom, more generally central charges, rather than electric charge weights alone. Neutral modes can carry heat without net charge; net forward charge transport does not rule out upstream heat. Compare equilibration and contact assumptions before using the reported thermal values to select a model. The proposed all-forward channel bounds are hypotheses, not consequences of electrical Hall quantisation alone.
