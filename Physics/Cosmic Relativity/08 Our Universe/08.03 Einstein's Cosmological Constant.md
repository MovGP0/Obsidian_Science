> [!TLDR]
> Einstein introduced a constant term to obtain a static cosmological model despite the dynamical behaviour of a matter-filled Universe.

## Physical question and model

What role does a cosmological constant play in a homogeneous gravitational model? It is an allowed constant term in the field equations, not a directly measured mechanical repulsion inserted into a Newtonian force law. Its sign and effect depend on a stated convention.

## Lecture account

A matter-filled Universe is unstable. In 1917, when Einstein applied the GTR to cosmology, the visible Universe looked approximately static. Without an understanding of the enormous time and distance scales of actual cosmic evolution, it was natural to assume static balance over millions of years. Yet Einstein realised that his equation implied a dynamic Universe, inconsistent with this picture. A determined theoretical physicist usually has a ready remedy: since he could not change the Universe, he changed his equation. A matter-filled Universe can be balanced in a static configuration by taking a clue from Newtonian gravity. As Einstein recognised [3], modify Poisson’s equation with a term proportional to potential:

$$
\nabla^2\mechanical{\phi}-\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{\phi}=4\pi\mechanical{G}\mechanical{\rho}. \tag{8.3}
$$

If average density is spatially constant, $\mechanical{\rho}_0$, the solution is simple: both $\nabla\mechanical{\phi}$ and $\nabla^2\mechanical{\phi}$ vanish, and

$$
\mechanical{\phi}=-\frac{4\pi\mechanical{G}\mechanical{\rho}}{\mechanical{\lambda}}. \tag{8.4}
$$

This describes infinite space uniformly filled with matter and an added constant potential. Einstein used this clue to modify equation (8.1), ‘creating’ a static Universe with cosmological constant. He wrote

$$
\mechanical{R}_{ik}-\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{g}_{ik}=-\mechanical{\kappa}\left(\energy{T}_{ik}-\frac12\mechanical{g}_{ik}\energy{T}\right). \tag{8.5}
$$

For a three-dimensional space with curvature radius $\mechanical{R}$, a static Universe satisfies

$$
\frac{2}{\mechanical{R}^2}-\mechanical{\lambda}=\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{\rho}}2,\qquad\mechanical{\lambda}=\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{\rho}}2. \tag{8.6}
$$

Thus $\mechanical{\lambda}=1/\mechanical{R}^2$. This single constant determines density and size of the closed Universe. Einstein retained the static picture despite criticism of the arbitrary term’s potentially unphysical consequences. We have mentioned de Sitter’s expanding matter-free Universe. It was this example that convinced Einstein to discard Mach’s principle completely. The irony and lost opportunity become clear in Cosmic Relativity.

## Interpretation and checks

A static solution needs the expansion rate and acceleration to vanish simultaneously. Balancing a single equation is not enough, and stability is a separate question. Perturb the scale factor slightly and ask whether it returns to equilibrium. This explains why a formally balanced Einstein-static model can be unstable. The historical modified-Poisson analogy should not be substituted for the full relativistic dynamics.
