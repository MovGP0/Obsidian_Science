## Physical question and model

What does it mean to observe the present Universe? Telescopes sample a past light cone, not a simultaneous spatial slice. A homogeneous cosmological model relates those observations to a common cosmic-time description.

## Lecture account

For dynamics and relativity, the observed features of the real Universe matter. Since we cannot observe everything, we also need models consistent with observations. Only the present Universe, observed through telescopes and other means, is relevant to a cosmic gravitational paradigm. Early evolution is not relevant here: the history of seeking knowledge and making theories spans about $5000\,\unit{yr}$, during which expansion changed the Universe’s size and other features by only one part in ten million. Thus, matter distribution, average density, and cosmic gravitational potentials have remained effectively constant for the physics of our theories, such as motion laws and UFF. What are the Universe’s main characteristics?

## Interpretation and checks

Slow evolution during human history can justify holding some cosmological parameters fixed for a laboratory calculation. It does not make the early Universe irrelevant to interpreting distant radiation or density estimates. State the timescale being neglected and compare it with the expansion timescale. A lookback-time effect and a change during the duration of the experiment are different approximations.
