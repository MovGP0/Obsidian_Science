---
title: "The Concept of a ‘Critical Density’"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Critical density separates positive- and negative-curvature matter models; density fractions express each component relative to this time-dependent value.

## Physical question and model

What makes a density critical? It is the density scale set by expansion rate and gravitational coupling. The relation between that scale and spatial curvature uses the total energy budget, including any cosmological-constant contribution.

## Lecture account

In relativistic cosmological models described by Friedmann equations (8.15) and (8.16), density at zero curvature, $k=0$, is especially important. It is called critical density, printed here as $\mechanical{\rho}_c=\mechanical{H}^2/(8\pi\mechanical{G})$. For $\mechanical{\rho}>\mechanical{\rho}_c$, the model is closed with positive curvature, $k=+1$, and eventually stops expanding and collapses. For $\mechanical{\rho}<\mechanical{\rho}_c$, it is open and always expanding with negative curvature, $k=-1$. A critical Universe expands just sufficiently. Curvature preserves its sign during evolution: positive stays positive, and flat critical models stay flat. Curvature itself decreases during expansion, just as an expanding sphere’s curvature decreases as $1/\mechanical{R}^2$.

Critical density varies with time because the Hubble parameter does. The source gives the present value as $\mechanical{\rho}_c=\mechanical{H}_0^2/(8\pi\mechanical{G})\approx9\times10^{-27}\,\unit{kg/m^3}$. Contributions $\mechanical{\rho}_i$ of matter and energy types are conveniently expressed as $\mechanical{\Omega}_i=\mechanical{\rho}_i/\mechanical{\rho}_c$. Measurements consistently show visible matter’s mean density much below critical (see [[08.06 The Large Scale Distribution of Matter|Section 8.6]]).

> [!note]
> This section’s printed critical-density expressions omit a factor of 3. Equation (8.15) at $k=0$ gives $\mechanical{\rho}_c=3\mechanical{H}^2/(8\pi\mechanical{G})$, as stated in [[08.03.01 The Cosmological Principle and the Expansion]]. The printed expressions and numerical estimate are retained.

## Interpretation and checks

The conventional expression has a factor of three in the numerator; its omission in this section is inconsistent with section 8.6.5. The familiar claim that positive curvature guarantees recollapse assumes a restricted matter model, commonly pressureless matter without positive cosmological constant. Curvature alone does not decide the future of a general dark-energy cosmology. Check units: expansion rate squared divided by gravitational constant has mass-density units.
