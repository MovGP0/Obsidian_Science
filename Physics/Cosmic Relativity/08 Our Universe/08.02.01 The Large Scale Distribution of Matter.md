> [!TLDR]
> Galaxies form filaments and voids, but matter is approximately homogeneous and isotropic at large scales. Dark matter dominates the total matter density.

## Physical question and model

On what scales can a clumpy Universe be approximated as homogeneous? Galaxies, filaments, and voids show strong local structure. Statistical isotropy about an observer and statistical homogeneity across large volumes are related but distinct assumptions.

## Lecture account

Telescope images show matter in galaxies and gaseous nebulae. This distribution is highly filamentary: clusters and chains of galaxies are separated by relative voids. Yet the large-scale structure looks similar in every direction. Approximate homogeneity and isotropy appear on scales large enough, but much smaller than the visible Universe. Much dark matter was established later through several observations. This invisible entity emits no light and is not familiar proton-and-neutron matter. Its particles remain unknown, except that their nongravitational interaction with matter must be weak. Dark matter around galaxies and clusters is confirmed through gravitational effects on stellar motion and gravitational lensing through galaxy clusters.

Most normal matter consists of neutral hydrogen and helium, the simplest and lightest atoms. Its average density is below $10^{-27}\,\unit{kg/m^3}$, about one hydrogen atom per cubic metre. Dark matter is estimated to be about five times more abundant than ordinary atomic matter. Their combined average density is about $3\times10^{-27}\,\unit{kg/m^3}$.

These values provide a basis for estimating factual cosmic matter’s gravity, discussed in Chapter 10. In addition to atoms and isolated primary particles, the Universe contains substantial light and neutrinos. Light is visible; cosmic neutrinos are inferred from cosmic evolution using known particle-interaction processes rather than directly detected in a laboratory. Photon number density is nearly a billion times matter’s number density, but its equivalent mass density and gravity are negligible. The three neutrino flavours have small nonzero, currently unmeasured masses. Their mass density is probably at least ten times smaller than normal matter’s density.

## Interpretation and checks

A galaxy catalogue samples luminous tracers with a selection function; it is not a direct map of all mass. Check how inferred smoothness changes with averaging scale and survey geometry. Gravitational lensing and dynamics constrain total gravitating matter, but identifying its microscopic particles needs additional evidence. A claimed universal local effect should state how small-scale inhomogeneities enter its error estimate.
