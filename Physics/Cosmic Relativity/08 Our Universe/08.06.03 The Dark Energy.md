> [!TLDR]
> Pressure contributes to gravity through its mass-equivalent density, linking the cosmic expansion to the nature of matter, radiation, and dark energy.

## Physical question and model

Why can pressure affect cosmological acceleration? In the relativistic perfect-fluid model, energy density and pressure both enter the acceleration equation. Negative pressure is an equation-of-state property, not the negative of ordinary mass density by definition.

## Lecture account

So far we have discussed mass or energy density. Mass–energy equivalence makes all kinetic energy contribute to gravity. In particular, pressure $\mechanical{p}$ from random motion in three directions corresponds to mass density $3\mechanical{p}/\mechanical{c}^2$. This differs from Newtonian gravity, for a straightforward reason. Galactic matter, consisting of well-separated stars and dilute gas, and galaxy clusters have $\mechanical{p}\approx0$. For radiation and light of energy density $\energy{\epsilon}$, the source prints $\mechanical{p}=\energy{\epsilon}/(3\mechanical{c})$. Present radiation density is negligible compared with matter density.

> [!note]
> The printed radiation-pressure expression contains an extra $\mechanical{c}$ in the denominator. With energy density in SI units, pressure is $\mechanical{p}=\energy{\epsilon}/3$, as stated earlier in [[08.03.01 The Cosmological Principle and the Expansion]]. The printed form is retained above.

Dark matter’s constituents and equation of state, relating matter density to pressure, are unknown. Its pressure can be treated as zero because observations suggest $\mechanical{p}_{\mathrm{dm}}<10^{-3}\mechanical{\rho}_{\mathrm{dm}}$.

> [!note]
> The source compares pressure directly with mass density in this inequality. In SI units, the density term requires $\mechanical{c}^2$, or density must mean energy density.

The remaining issue is dark energy’s pressure, arguably the biggest open problem in big-bang models. It has been proposed as Einstein’s cosmological constant or real energy with a similar effect in his equation. A genuine cosmological constant is unrelated to physical matter or energy. If it is energy, it has both density and pressure. The energy–momentum tensor’s diagonal entries, $(\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{c}^2,\mechanical{p}_x,\mechanical{p}_y,\mechanical{p}_z)$, then have form $(\mechanical{\Lambda},-\mechanical{\Lambda},-\mechanical{\Lambda},-\mechanical{\Lambda})$, since the cosmological term enters as $\mechanical{g}_{ij}\mechanical{\Lambda}$. The source gives the diagonal metric entries as $(-1,-1,-1,-1)$. Thus, the equation of state is $\mechanical{p}=-\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{c}^2$, an unusual negative pressure.

Conservation equation (8.11) gives a strange consequence of $\mechanical{\rho}+\mechanical{p}/\mechanical{c}^2=0$: density stays constant while expansion changes volume. Negative-pressure changes compensate for the density change. Thus, dark energy or a cosmological constant acts as a constant density driving exponential expansion. Constant density makes $\mechanical{H}=\dot{\mechanical{a}}/\mechanical{a}=\sqrt{8\pi\mechanical{G}\mechanical{\rho}/3}\equiv\alpha\sqrt{\mechanical{\rho}}$ constant, so

$$
\mechanical{a}(t)=\mechanical{a}(0)\exp(\mechanical{H}t). \tag{8.25}
$$

In Friedmann equation (8.16), the acceleration-driving term $\mechanical{\rho}+3\mechanical{p}/\mechanical{c}^2$ becomes $\mechanical{\rho}-3\mechanical{\rho}=-2\mechanical{\rho}$, giving accelerated expansion, like de Sitter’s unusual result. More surprisingly, velocity–distance measurements using distant supernovae to redshift about 1 as standard candles are interpreted as acceleration. At a given redshift $z$, supernovae are fainter than expected from the standard relation. This is interpreted as greater distance than with unaccelerated Hubble recession. A cosmological-energy density supplying the missing 0.7 fraction of critical density agrees with these data.

Whether expansion accelerates or not, the factual mean gravitating matter–energy density is accurately critical. This is the only fact needed to calculate the total cosmic gravitational potential.

## Interpretation and checks

For radiation with energy density measured per volume, pressure is one third of that energy density. The source expression dividing by light speed has incorrect units. A positive cosmological constant can give exponential expansion when it dominates; matter-dominated evolution is different. Test the sign of acceleration by combining the density and pressure terms, not by treating every energetic component as an attractive Newtonian point mass.
