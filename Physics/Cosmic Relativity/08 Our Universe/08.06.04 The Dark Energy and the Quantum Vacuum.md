> [!TLDR]
> The author rejects zero-point field energy as an explanation for dark energy and examines whether the Casimir effect requires a real vacuum energy.

## Physical question and model

Does a vacuum-energy calculation predict the observed dark-energy density? A field-theory vacuum expectation value and a cosmological-constant parameter require a specified renormalisation and gravitational coupling. A naive sum of zero-point oscillator energies depends on its cutoff.

## Lecture account

There is currently no physical understanding of dark energy. Physicists are dissatisfied with treating it as another unknowable postulate named the cosmological constant. Attempts therefore connect $\mechanical{\Lambda}$ to a physical field, which might also provide dynamics and time evolution. A popular proposal identifies it as a field’s ‘zero-point’ or vacuum-state quantum energy.

Zero-point energy (ZPE) is quantum energy present even at zero kinetic energy. Typically, residual energy $\energy{E}_0=\energy{h}\signal{f}/2$ comes from confinement with characteristic frequency $\signal{f}$, as in an oscillator; $\energy{h}$ is Planck’s constant. Since fields are modelled as oscillators at all frequencies, they are assumed to have ZPE without real particles. The infinite number of notional oscillators makes this formally infinite. This is a difficulty of quantum field theories; see [[12.07 The Minuteness of the Quantum Zero-Point Energy|Section 12.7]] on why this unphysical energy is absent in Cosmic Relativity.

One formally removes the infinity by subtracting an infinity, arguing that energy’s reference zero has no physical consequence. This is practical in most applications, but not cosmology: every energy density contributes to cosmic expansion, equation (8.15). Even with a finite maximum-frequency cutoff, calculated field ZPE would be too large for the Universe to exist briefly. Hence, no field’s ZPE sensibly explains the cosmological constant. It would also require equation of state $\mechanical{p}\approx-\mechanical{\rho}$, attainable only by manipulating the field-state choice and not valid for electromagnetism. Despite prevalent beliefs, seeking $\mechanical{\Lambda}$ from ZPE is misguided: such electromagnetic ZPE can be proved absent [21].

> [!note]
> The source writes $\mechanical{p}\approx-\mechanical{\rho}$ here. If $\mechanical{\rho}$ is mass density, SI units require $\mechanical{p}\approx-\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{c}^2$; alternatively, the equation uses energy density or units with light speed set to one.

Experimental evidence shows only matter’s ZPE, for example expansion of a nearly zero-temperature Bose–Einstein condensate. Confined matter has ZPE. A claimed ‘proof’ for electromagnetic ZPE is the Casimir effect [22]: attraction between two nearby plane metal plates. It is attributed to radiation pressure from vacuum-state field waves without real photons. There are fewer modes, of wavelength $\signal{\lambda}$, between the plates than outside because only standing waves with $n\signal{\lambda}/2=\mechanical{d}$ fit separation $\mechanical{d}$. The pressure difference is the Casimir force, from which a real quantum-vacuum ZPE is inferred.

This ‘proof’ has a fatal fault. The Casimir effect can also be calculated directly from electromagnetic interaction between quantum fluctuations of atomic dipole moments in the plates. Vacuum-mode calculations treat plates only as boundaries, without real atoms and their quantum mechanics. Both cannot be included as independent physical effects: this would double-count and double the force. *Atoms and their ZPE are real*, directly observed in atomic experiments. Thus, wave-mode calculations are a convenient mathematical equivalent, not evidence for something physically present in empty space. *If atoms and wave modes both physically contribute, the effect doubles and disagrees with observations* [21]. Only one can be chosen as physical reality, and the choice is clear.

## Interpretation and checks

The mismatch between a simple high-energy estimate and a fitted cosmological density is a genuine theoretical problem, but it is not resolved merely by calling one contribution unphysical. Any alternative must reproduce a conserved gravitational source and observable expansion. Distinguish a measured expansion history, a fitted equation of state, and a proposed microscopic origin. A calculation with arbitrary cutoff is not an independent measurement of dark energy.
