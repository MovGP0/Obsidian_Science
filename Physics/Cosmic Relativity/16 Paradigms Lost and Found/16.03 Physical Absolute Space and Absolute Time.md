> [!TLDR]
> The author replaces abstract absolute space and hypothetical ether with the observed matter-filled Universe as a physical reference for motion and time.

## Physical question and model

What does an observed cosmic reference frame supply operationally? The microwave-background dipole can define motion relative to a cosmological matter–radiation state. That is a physical reference selected by the state of the Universe, with a specified observational procedure.

## Lecture account

Earlier debates lacked an agreed physical basis for absolute space and time. Newton's proposals, Mach's critique, and nineteenth-century ether theories offered different possibilities. The source criticises ether as an added entity with sometimes conflicting assigned properties, distinct from both empty space and matter, despite extensive experimental searches.

The author's preferred rule is that changes of physical state arise from physical interactions. He extends this to dynamical properties such as inertia, following Mach's suggestion. He advocates caution towards added hypotheses and strict empirical interpretation.

In Cosmic Relativity, observed cosmic matter supplies the absolute reference. Its gravity determines dynamics and its evolution supplies temporal ordering. The author regards this as the first consistent, observable physical basis for absolute motion and time, and expects the role of the Universe to remain part of future theories.

## Interpretation and checks

A preferred state does not automatically imply preferred local laws or a unique simultaneity convention for all spacetime. Cosmological proper time is useful in idealised homogeneous models, but its construction has assumptions and can fail as a universal coordinate in general geometries. To establish fundamental absolute time, specify the clock comparison and a prediction beyond ordinary relativity with matter. The existence of a measurable reference alone is insufficient.
