> [!TLDR]
> The source predicts moving-clock dilation from the vector sum of local clock velocity and laboratory velocity relative to the cosmic frame.

## Physical question and model

What clock difference follows from a preferred-frame speed prescription? Write both clocks' rate laws in the same cosmic chart, retain the reference-clock term with the correct subtraction sign, and specify the start and end events.

## Lecture account

[[09.06 The Predictions for Motional Time Dilation]] treated time dilation with an absolute frame. All time dilation in Cosmic Relativity is gravitational [1, 10]. Motional dilation uses the same expressions, with velocity relative to the cosmic frame as the relevant parameter. This section summarises them.

For a clock moving at local laboratory velocity $\mechanical{\mathbf v}$ while the laboratory moves through the cosmic frame at $\mechanical{\mathbf u}$,

$$
d\tau=\sqrt{1-\frac{|\mechanical{\mathbf v}+\mechanical{\mathbf u}|^2}{\mechanical{c}^2}}\,dt=\sqrt{1-\frac{\mechanical{v}^2+\mechanical{u}^2+2\mechanical{\mathbf u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf v}}{\mechanical{c}^2}}\,dt. \tag{11.12}
$$

For $\mechanical{v},\mechanical{u}\ll\mechanical{c}$, the source prints the moving-clock difference relative to a stationary clock in the same laboratory as

$$
\frac{d\tau-dt}{dt}=-\frac{\mechanical{v}^2+\mechanical{u}^2+2\mechanical{\mathbf u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf v}}{2\mechanical{c}^2}-\frac{\mechanical{u}^2}{2\mechanical{c}^2}=-\frac{\mechanical{v}^2}{2\mechanical{c}^2}-\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf v}}{\mechanical{c}^2}. \tag{11.13}
$$

> [!note]
> The printed second term has a minus sign. Subtracting the stationary clock's own dilation requires a plus sign there to cancel the common $\mechanical{u}^2$ term and obtain the final expression. The source's signs are retained.

Integrating gives total relative time dilation, allowing for changes in both velocities. The author identifies the scalar product $\mechanical{\mathbf u}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf v}$ as crucial and states that STR lacks this term. [[11.07 Experimental Tests of Time Dilation - Atomic Clocks]] and [[11.08 Experimental Tests of Time Dilation - GNSS and GPS]] discuss the claimed evidence in two contexts.

## Interpretation and checks

For a constant background velocity and a closed path, a first-order cross term proportional to net displacement can cancel. Rotation, acceleration, or a changed endpoint protocol can alter that result. Equation (11.13) does not algebraically give its final expression with the printed reference sign. Compare a corrected complete calculation with the standard proper-time prediction before treating the cross term as distinctive.
