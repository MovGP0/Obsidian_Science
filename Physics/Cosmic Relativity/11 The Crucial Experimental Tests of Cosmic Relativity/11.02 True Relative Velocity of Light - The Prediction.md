> [!TLDR]
> Cosmic Relativity predicts exactly Galilean relative light velocity and calls for a one-way travel-time test that avoids separated clocks.

## Physical question and model

What is meant by subtracting observer velocity from light velocity? It can describe a closing rate of separation in one chart or a Galilean coordinate speed. It is not automatically the speed read by the observer's local rods and clock.

## Lecture account

The theory predicts that a frame moving at absolute velocity $\mechanical{\mathbf v}$ relative to the cosmic frame measures

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf c}'=\mechanical{\mathbf c}-\mechanical{\mathbf v}.
$$

The author argues that frame length contraction and time dilation share the factor $\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$, so their ratio does not depend on frame speed. He therefore predicts no second-order correction to Galilean relative velocity. Since the prediction follows from the absolute cosmic gravitational frame, testing it can validate or refute Cosmic Relativity [9–12].

The desired test should determine propagation independently of theoretical assumptions and postulates. It must measure duration $\Delta t$ over fixed distance $\mechanical{L}$ relative to an inertial frame of speed $\mechanical{v}$. It must avoid separated clocks while keeping propagation one-way, without path reversal or retracing. Repeating the test at different speeds gives $\Delta t(\mechanical{v})$.

The author treats a first-order linear speed dependence as Galilean propagation, supporting Cosmic Relativity and contradicting STR. A constant $\Delta t=\Delta t_0$, unchanged from the value at $\mechanical{v}=0$, instead supports invariant one-way relative light speed and STR.

The apparatus must satisfy two criteria:

1. Emission point A and reception point B, separated by fixed length $\mechanical{L}$, must move inertially together at velocity $\mechanical{v}$, without relative motion.
2. The duration must be measured without two spatially separated clocks at A and B.

The source argues that one method fulfils both requirements and introduces it in the next section.

## Interpretation and checks

For standard collinear inertial velocity addition, a vacuum light ray has local speed light speed after transforming both distance and time. For a closing-rate calculation, receiver motion enters because the reception event moves. These are different operations. A proposed test must state which it measures and use the same convention in both theories. The low-speed resemblance of their intermediate formulas is not enough to distinguish them.
