> [!TLDR]
> The author compares successive relativity theories through the distinction between kinematic description and a physical dynamical explanation.

## Physical question and model

When does a simpler physical interpretation outperform another theory? A historical analogy can motivate a search, but it does not determine the present apparatus prediction. Compare mathematical consistency, scope, independent parameters, and discriminating observations.

## Lecture account

The source uses the history of planetary models as an analogy. Ptolemaic cycles and epicycles represented observed motion kinematically and geometrically, without a physical mechanism beyond occasional metaphysical or theological explanations. Copernicus proposed a common solar centre for the planets, including Earth. The author regards this as more than a coordinate choice: the simpler pattern suggested a physical explanation. Kepler and Newton later supplied gravity and dynamical laws. He also notes that established beliefs can delay such progress.

The author sees a different pattern in relativity. Galilean mechanics developed through many investigations into relativistic electrodynamics. Lorentz and Poincaré supplied a partly dynamical theory, attributing microscopic relativistic effects to electromagnetic interaction. But its hypothetical undetectable ether remained, and electromagnetism could not explain all physical phenomena. The source argues that a more universal physical interaction was needed.

Einstein removed the ether and the detailed electromagnetic explanations, achieving broad universality. The author criticises this as a return to empty space and relative velocity without a physical mechanism. He regards STR as kinematic and metaphysical rather than a dynamical advance, and attributes its alleged inconsistencies to the absence of a universal interaction.

Debate and experimental searches continued. The source describes the wartime atmosphere and distrust in Europe, then the 1919 eclipse as a turning point: reported solar light deflection brought worldwide confidence in Einstein's theories and, in the author's view, placed both GTR and STR beyond sufficient criticism.

He argues that Sagnac's and Michelson's experiments consequently received insufficient attention, and that events outside scientific method helped maintain STR as a foundation. He presents the Sagnac, Michelson, Hafele–Keating, and GNSS evidence as requiring re-examination.

The author describes Cosmic Relativity as restoring a dynamical basis a century after Einstein's theories. Gravity is universal, and he says its role was chosen because cosmic matter and its gravity are observed, rather than from theoretical preference. He argues that evidence and logic remove the need for additional postulates and give relativity a final logical and physical foundation.

## Interpretation and checks

Lorentz, Einstein, and the proposed cosmic account can share formulas in a tested regime while differing in interpretation. State exactly where the new model changes an observable. The heliocentric analogy is not a substitute for that comparison, and simplicity should not be claimed by leaving boundary or coupling assumptions unnamed. A useful exercise is to list one shared prediction and one genuinely distinct prediction for each model pair.
