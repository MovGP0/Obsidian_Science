## Physical question and model

Can coincidence between a sound pulse and repeated light paths test the relativity principle? Specify the acoustic medium, its velocity relative to the apparatus, dispersion, and all moving boundaries. Sound is not a vacuum Lorentz-invariant signal.

## Lecture account

The principle of relativity requires observable physical phenomena in an inertial frame to be independent of the velocity of a uniformly moving observer. The author regards this as a fundamental physical requirement that can reveal inconsistencies even in otherwise successful theories.

He presents the following argument as a conflict between this principle and STR's invariant relative light velocity. It combines insights from his experiments on relative light and sound speeds, whose methods and results follow in the next section. He stresses the intended significance: Einstein introduced invariant relative light speed in the belief that it was necessary to obtain conformity with the principle of relativity.

Consider simultaneous emission of a sound pulse and a light pulse at E, with $\mechanical{x}=0$ and $t=0$. Both follow a loop of total length $\mechanical{L}$ in the apparatus rest frame. During one sound circuit, light makes nearly a million circuits. Choose the situation in which both pulses meet a moving detector at R.

The two reception events have zero spatial and temporal separation: $\Delta\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{x}_l-\mechanical{x}_s=0$ and $\Delta t=t_l-t_s=0$. Both GT and LT therefore describe their coincidence as simultaneous for all observers. The same applies to emission.

In the stationary frame, the source gives the sound travel duration to the moving detector as $\mechanical{L}/[\mechanical{c}_s(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_s)]$. Light crosses the moving detector after each circuit duration $\mechanical{L}/[\mechanical{c}(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c})]$. Hence

$$
\frac{N\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c})}=\frac{\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}_s(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_s)}. \tag{11.11}
$$

This gives $N=(\mechanical{c}-\mechanical{v})/(\mechanical{c}_s-\mechanical{v})$. The author states that the principle of relativity requires this circuit count to be invariant.

For observer O′ moving at $\mechanical{v}$, sound has Galilean relative velocity $\mechanical{c}_s-\mechanical{v}$. Over duration $\delta t'$, sound travels relative distance $(\mechanical{c}_s-\mechanical{v})\delta t'$. If light instead has invariant relative speed, it travels $\mechanical{c}\delta t'$. The source then gives $N'=\mechanical{c}/(\mechanical{c}_s-\mechanical{v})$, different from $N$. The author interprets this as a conflict between the light hypothesis and the principle of relativity, and argues that Galilean light propagation alone preserves the circuit ratio.

He states that the argument was synthesised from two real experiments with sound and light. He presents their results as establishing Galilean light propagation without theoretical assumptions and therefore supporting an absolute cosmic frame for dynamics. The following section describes them.

## Interpretation and checks

A sound pulse moving in air and an optical pulse in a guided path have different environmental interactions. Their meeting event can be transformed consistently without requiring the sound speed to be invariant. A genuine contradiction requires two different predictions for that same local event within one complete model. Treating the medium as stationary in two different frames simultaneously would compare different systems, not two descriptions of one system.
