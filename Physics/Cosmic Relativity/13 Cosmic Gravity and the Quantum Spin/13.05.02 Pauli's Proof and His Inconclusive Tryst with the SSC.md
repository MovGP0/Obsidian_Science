> [!TLDR]
> Pauli's field-theory argument connects spin to exchange symmetry through operator algebra and relativistic consistency conditions.

## Physical question and model

What assumptions enter a formal spin–statistics result? Read the theorem as a conditional statement about a class of relativistic quantum fields. Physical interpretation and mathematical proof answer related but different questions.

## Lecture account

Pauli introduced his two-valued quantum number and exclusion rule in 1924–25 to explain hydrogen-like alkali spectra. The rule organised atomic matter, but he sought its reason for two decades. The inquiry shifted from exclusion's physical cause to the distinction between bosons and fermions. Integer-spin fields have scalar, vector, or tensor representations; half-integer fields are spinors. Creation and annihilation algebra encodes their different collective behaviour.

In his 1945 Nobel lecture [38], Pauli linked half-integer spin to antisymmetric states and integer spin to symmetric states, for elementary and compound systems. He argued that a theoretical explanation required relativistic wave mechanics.

For single-particle wavefunctions $\psi_1$ and $\psi_2$, the source writes the unnormalised symmetric and antisymmetric states as

$$
\Psi_s(\mechanical{x}_1,\mechanical{x}_2)=\psi_1(\mechanical{x}_1)\psi_2(\mechanical{x}_2)+\psi_2(\mechanical{x}_1)\psi_1(\mechanical{x}_2), \tag{13.20}
$$

$$
\Psi_a(\mechanical{x}_1,\mechanical{x}_2)=\psi_1(\mechanical{x}_1)\psi_2(\mechanical{x}_2)-\psi_2(\mechanical{x}_1)\psi_1(\mechanical{x}_2). \tag{13.21}
$$

The latter vanishes for identical single-particle states, expressing exclusion. Jordan and Wigner explained this through multiplication properties of de Broglie amplitudes [39]. The author finds it physically puzzling that exchange symmetry must already hold at arbitrarily large spacelike separation, before any future encounter; in this description it does not arise dynamically during approach.

Classical field amplitudes $a,a^*$ become annihilation and creation operators $a,a^\dagger$. The number operator is $N=a^\dagger a$, and a mode has energy $\energy{E}=\energy{h}\signal{\nu}(a^\dagger a+1/2)$. Bosonic commutators are

$$
\begin{aligned}
[a,a^\dagger]_-&=aa^\dagger-a^\dagger a=1,\\
[a,a]_-&=[a^\dagger,a^\dagger]_-=0.
\end{aligned}\tag{13.22}
$$

For massive Dirac fermions, separate operators describe particles and antiparticles. Exclusion is imposed through anticommutators:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[a,a^\dagger]_+&=aa^\dagger+a^\dagger a=1,\\
[b,b^\dagger]_+&=bb^\dagger+b^\dagger b=1.
\end{aligned}\tag{13.23}
$$

Other anticommutators vanish. Thus $a^\dagger a^\dagger=aa=0$, and $a^\dagger a$ and $b^\dagger b$ have eigenvalues 0 or 1. This realises exclusion without a classical field-amplitude counterpart. Pauli's own description [40] starts with relativistically invariant quantised fields and the condition that the vacuum is the lowest-energy state.

Pauli's remaining conditions are that observables commute at spacelike separation, so the measurements cannot disturb one another, and that the state-space metric is positive definite, giving positive probabilities. His 1950 account states that reversing the normal spin–statistics assignment violates vacuum-energy boundedness for half-integer fields and spacelike commutativity for integer fields, while positive state norm remains possible.

Later proofs made the mathematics more rigorous and included interactions. The author nevertheless gives five objections to the physical explanation:

1. The proof supplies no physical cause for the SSC.
2. It uses relativistic field consistency for a rule already observed in a nonrelativistic pair.
3. The wrong statistics violate different requirements for the two classes: locality for bosons and energy positivity for fermions. The author expects a common physical explanation for both.
4. The physical meaning of spin is not visible in the formal proof.
5. The SSC follows the total spin of a composite, such as a Cooper pair or helium atom, regardless of its fermionic constituent fields. The author therefore questions whether the underlying field type explains the composite rule.

He concludes that Pauli's approach does not give the universal physical account he seeks. Pauli himself ended his Nobel lecture by criticising infinite vacuum energies and charges, subtraction of infinities, and hypothetical mathematical worlds used to interpret reality. He stated that his report on exclusion and quantum mechanics had no logical conclusion.

## Interpretation and checks

Pauli explicitly uses positive energy and spacelike commutativity in his [1940 paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.58.716). The absence of a mechanical picture does not invalidate such a conditional theorem. The displayed two-particle sums also need normalisation factors that depend on overlap. Setting both fermion orbitals equal makes the antisymmetric state vanish; this is a direct check of exclusion, not an independent proof of the spin assignment.
