> [!TLDR]
> Attempts at elementary SSC proofs seek the exchange phase directly, but the author argues that a physical interaction must explain it.

## Physical question and model

What must a proposed physical proof add to an exchange-phase argument? It must connect identical-particle exchange to spin without assuming the desired symmetry through the choice of state, path, or boundary condition.

## Lecture account

The author sees a gap between the simple physical situations governed by the SSC and the assumptions of formal field-theory proofs. Researchers have therefore sought physically reasoned, simple derivations of the exchange phase without relativistic field theory. Such attempts follow Jordan and Wigner's connection between exclusion and multiplication of quantum amplitudes.

Additional assumptions can make these arguments circular. The usual SSC statement is that integer-spin particles obey Bose–Einstein statistics and half-integer-spin particles obey Fermi–Dirac statistics. In a two-particle scattering formulation, the required result is more specific:

- For integer spin, direct and exchange amplitudes add with a plus sign. Their relative phase is an integer multiple of $2\pi$.
- For half-integer spin, the amplitudes add with a minus sign. Their relative phase is an odd multiple of $\pi$.

M. V. Berry and J. M. Robbins published a geometric account [41]. Other approaches relate rotation operators to particle exchange. E. C. G. Sudarshan argued that a simple proof without assumptions specific to relativistic quantum field theory should exist [29, 42].

The source cites A. S. Wightman's critical review of Duck and Sudarshan [43] and argues that there is no agreed valid elementary proof. Even specialists disagree on the proposed accessible derivations. The author concludes that a common physical explanation remains missing.

## Interpretation and checks

Separate a physical rotation of an apparatus or spin from exchange of particle labels in configuration space. They are not automatically the same operation. Test independence from interaction strength and continuous path deformation, and state the spatial dimension. A path-dependent dynamical phase cannot serve as a universal exchange sign unless that independence is proved. This is the standard to apply to the following cosmic proposal.
