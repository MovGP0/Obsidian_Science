> [!TLDR]
> The source treats angular momentum as a closed mass current and identifies spin with a gravitomagnetic moment.

## Physical question and model

How is spin transport calculated in the proposed gravitational field? Begin with the stated interaction energy and obtain torque or operator evolution from that same Hamiltonian. Treat the field normalisation and its connection with a rotating laboratory as explicit assumptions.

## Lecture account

For classical spin, $\sum_i\mechanical{\mathbf r}_i\times\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i$, the author regards the gravitomagnetic interpretation as direct. Momentum is a current of mass, which is gravitational charge. Such a current interacts with other gravitational currents in a relativistic account.

Experiments show gravitomagnetic fields from spinning massive objects. The author assumes that quantum spin has the same role and cites the requirement that quantum ensemble averages match classical results.

The fundamental interaction energy is

$$
\energy{E}_g=\frac12\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g. \tag{13.5}
$$

In a quantum Hamiltonian, a spin operator replaces $\mechanical{\mathbf s}$. Different quantised orientations then have different interaction energies. The phase $\int\energy{E}_g\,dt/\energy{\hbar}$ depends on the orientation relative to the field and represents action in units of $\energy{\hbar}$.

For classical spin, a torque produces precession:

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\mathbf s}}{dt}=\mechanical{\mathbf T}
=\frac12\mechanical{\mathbf s}\times\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g. \tag{13.6}
$$

The precession angular frequency is $\mechanical{B}_g/2$, independent of the spin magnitude. The author compares this exact correspondence [8] with the Weak Equivalence Principle: gravitational acceleration is independent of a body's mass.

One can formally distinguish inertial spin, which resists a torque, from the gravitomagnetic moment that sources a field. The source argues that no physical distinction exists. In Cosmic Relativity, gravitational charge interacting with all cosmic matter replaces independent inertial mass, and spin dynamics inherits this property.

## Interpretation and checks

Angular momentum times the displayed gravitomagnetic field has energy units only when that field has inverse-time units. Check the factor of two against the torque and phase formulas. Spin parallel to a fixed precession axis has no transverse torque, although its state can acquire phase. Compare the precession direction with an independently fixed sign convention rather than infer the sign from the desired result.
