## Physical question and model

How does the observed large-scale Universe enter a spacetime model? The FLRW form assumes approximate homogeneity and isotropy on large scales and uses comoving coordinates. Its cosmic time is proper time along the idealised comoving matter congruence.

## Lecture account

The most important factor, and also the most neglected one, in any modern discussion of space, time, and relativity is the fact that the observable physical phenomena happen in this single Universe, in the gravitational presence of all its matter and energy. We have no physical access to any other Universe. It is also a fact that we formulate and test our theories in the same gravitational Universe. Obviously, *the space and time of all our physics, including that in the theoretical description, cannot be anything other than the space and time of this factual Universe*. Anything else would refer to an untestable theory, already irrelevant. It continues to surprise me that such an obvious fact can remain as unacceptable or incomprehensible. This is a profound fact, but the development of physics did not pay attention to this single decisive factor. The space and time of our Universe are absolute in the usual sense, defined operationally by the stationary distant stars and the CMBR. Yet, such absolute space and time are not part of the current theories, merely because they were formulated well before this crucial knowledge about the matter-filled Universe came to us through observations [19].

A discussion of the quantitative aspects of the features of the space and time of our Universe, in all necessary details, will of course be taken up later. Here I mention some salient features, as described in current cosmology [16, 20]. The space-time metric that describes the observed Universe is called the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric, representing a steadily expanding Universe, homogeneous and isotropic in space, with its ‘spatial curvature’ zero.

$$
ds^2=-\mechanical{c}^2dt^2+a^2(t)(d\mechanical{x}^2+d\mechanical{y}^2+d\mechanical{z}^2) \tag{2.6}
$$

where the time-dependent scale factor $a(t)$ is related to the Hubble expansion parameter as $H(t)=\frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{dt}$.

Most explicitly, this line element (representing the space and time of our Universe) has an absolute universal time, represented by the symbol $t$ in the metric. It is universally synchronised because it has no dependence on the spatial position in the Universe. The universal clock is isochronous with the temperature of the CMBR and the expansion of the matter distribution. It is globally synchronised, but for the small fluctuations amounting a part in a million. Any motion in such a Universe is distinguished from a state of rest because there will be a current of matter in the moving frame, reflected accurately in the Doppler anisotropy of the CMBR (see Fig. 1.2). Clearly, this is not the space and time that are used in our fundamental theories, because our current theories use the Minkowski space and the time of an empty space, without matter and gravity. But there is only one Universe in which all the physical processes happen, and that is represented by the expression Eq. (2.6) and not by the Minkowski line element of the STR. The conflict is amply clear and it stems from the historical accident of the pre-cosmology completion of our fundamental theories.

## Interpretation and checks

An approximately preferred matter frame is compatible with covariant field equations. It need not replace the local Lorentz symmetry of laboratory laws. Check that a constant scale factor can be absorbed into spatial coordinates, whereas changing scale factor affects redshift and cosmological distances. The model omits local density structures; a clock near a star needs those gravitational perturbations in addition to the homogeneous background.
