> [!TLDR]
> The chapter proposes individual action-wave dynamics and ensemble Schrödinger evolution as a common account of quantum behaviour and cosmic gravitational coupling.

## Physical question and model

Review the proposed hierarchy: individual action waves, particle dynamics, ensemble wavefunctions, and measurement probabilities. Each transition between levels needs a defined rule rather than an interpretive slogan.

## Lecture account

Cosmic Relativity is presented as a gravitational paradigm for all dynamics. Mass, spin, energy, and related quantities retain physical meaning from macroscopic to atomic systems. The chapter therefore examines how microscopic quantum dynamics couples to cosmic gravity.

The author identifies the Schrödinger equation with ensemble probability continuity rather than with one system's dynamical history. He argues that the ensemble wavefunction interpretation removes collapse, measurement, and other foundational problems.

For individual dynamics, he proposes that stationary action requires a periodic complex action entity. Interference between action waves selects stationary paths. Completing Hamilton's action mechanics then gives

$$
i\energy{\hbar}\dot{\signal{\zeta}}=\hat H\signal{\zeta},\qquad\signal{\zeta}(\energy{S})=\exp\left(\frac{i\energy{S}}{\energy{\hbar}}\right).
$$

The source presents this as a universal equation without a fundamental classical–quantum division.

In the proposed account, particles have no material wave nature. Action waves alone produce single-particle interference. The author argues that this also removes nonlocal influences that would conflict with causal physical relations; Chapter 15 develops that claim.

Earlier attempts to connect Schrödinger evolution, continuity, and Hamilton action are criticised for starting with an individual-history wavefunction. The author instead states that an ensemble equation must follow from averaging individual histories. He attributes the unresolved nonlocality of de Broglie–Bohm interpretations to reversing that relation.

Action waves are assigned no energy or momentum. The source therefore rejects field-vacuum ZPE and assigns only finite, cosmically negligible ground-state energy to real particles in the sparse Universe.

The chapter also examines the Dirac equation. The author argues that a nonrelativistic magnetic-moment and double-well tunnelling problem produces the same four-component structure, so that the matrix dimension need not originate in relativistic spacetime.

Finally, cosmic potentials alter action-wave phases through the coupling of metric components $\mechanical{g}_{00}$ and $\mechanical{g}_{0i}$ to a particle's mass and energy.

The source represents the action-phase changes through terms proportional to $\int\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}_{00}\,dt$ and $\int\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}_{0i}\,d\mechanical{x}$. It concludes that quantum dynamics, like all dynamics, is controlled by cosmic gravitational potentials.

The next two chapters apply the proposal to spin and quantum transport. The author highlights a proposed explanation of the boson–fermion division, followed by a unified account of integer and fractional quantum Hall effects. He claims to obtain Hall plateaux at the observed carrier-density-to-field ratios without quasiparticles, additional gauge fields, or arbitrary postulates.

## Interpretation and checks

As a reconstruction task, derive the two-path intensity and explain exactly which averaging removes its interference term. Then state the additional phase equation needed beyond probability continuity. Finally, compare two alternative gravitational paths and identify their relative action. These checks show what has been calculated and what remains a proposed explanation before applying the scheme to spin or entanglement.
