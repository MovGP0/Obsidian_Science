## Physical question and model

When do two theories with the same coordinate transformations become experimentally distinct? Lorentz and Einstein gave different interpretations of rods and clocks while sharing a transformation structure. An interpretation becomes testable only when it changes a complete instrument prediction.

## Lecture account

The theory of relativity in the absolute ether developed by Lorentz and Poincaré ensured the universal validity of the Principle of Relativity. Then, the ether is like an absolute reference medium that remains undetectable, somewhat like Newton's absolute space. The ether serves to remind us that all relativistic effects occur during a real motion relative to a privileged universal frame. A measuring rod that moves relative to the ether contracts and the time measured by a moving clock progresses slower. Lorentz tried to understand in microscopic detail the physical reason for the motional modifications of the spatial and temporal intervals to electromagnetic phenomena in the ether. This was justified because the atoms of matter were proposed to be held together by electromagnetic forces.

However, the scenario changed drastically when Einstein published his ideas of relativity in 1905 [25], with two fundamental postulates – the Principle of Relativity (PoR) and the invariance (constancy) of the relative velocity of light in all inertial frames. Since the PoR is already a part of Lorentz's theory, it is the second postulate—the ‘light hypothesis’—that characterises Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity. Einstein derived the Lorentz transformations from the light hypothesis. Since all inertial frames are completely equivalent in the STR, there is no privileged frame, and there is no place for the ether or an absolute space. Once the invariance of the relative velocity of light is assumed, the null result of the M-M experiment is easily explained; light takes the same duration $\signal{\Delta t}=\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}$ each way, *along both arms*, independent of the velocity of the reference frame. Therefore, the difference in the durations and the relative phase of light in the interferometer output remain constant, exactly as in the situation at rest. In other words, since any inertial frame is equivalent to a frame at rest, inertial motion has no physical effects, irrespective of how fast the frame might be moving.

If the same physical situation in a frame O is analysed from another frame O′ that is relatively moving, then the Lorentz transformations are applicable in the STR, in a manner that is superficially similar to Lorentz's relativity. But there is a crucial difference: the velocity to be used in the LT is the relative velocity between the frames O and O′, which varies with the choice of the reference frames, in stark contrast to the situation when the physical velocity is relative to a unique absolute frame.

Einstein's theory avoided the burden of looking for the physical mechanism behind the enigmatic relativistic effects of the length contraction and time dilation. With the electromagnetic ether gone, there could not be any explanation rooted in electrodynamics. The relativistic effects were more universal than the phenomena in electrodynamics.

There is an operational simplicity in the structure of the STR, in the democratic equivalence of all inertial reference frames. But, this came at the cost of a significant sacrifice of any physical interpretation of the relativistic effects. Without any universal reference frame, even notionally, the STR was symmetrical and reciprocal between any two observers. *Any observer ‘A’ can claim the state of rest and that the other observer ‘B’ is moving, with the relativistic modifications happening only to the clocks and rulers of the observer B. In turn, B can equally well claim that he is in a frame at rest, and A is moving with exactly the same modifications happening to A's clocks and rulers*.

The relativistic effects in the STR depend on the arbitrary choice of the rest frame. Between two inertial frames A and B in relative motion, either can be chosen as the frame at rest. Then the other frame is moving. There is no physical meaning to ‘real motion’ and ‘real relativistic effects’. In the chosen comoving rest frame, there are no velocity-dependent relativistic effects, because the frame is at rest according to the STR. In any other frame that is relatively moving, the spatial intervals and durations of time are altered, by the factor $\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$, where $\mechanical{v}$ is the relative velocity. The difference with the Lorentz-Poincaré theory of relativity, or any theory of relativity with an absolute frame like the Universe, should be clearly noticed and understood. In Einstein's theory, there are no relativistic effects whatsoever in the rest frame. The relative velocity of light is the same invariant constant $\mechanical{c}$ in all inertial frames and it is the maximum possible speed relative to any frame. In a theory with an absolute frame, there is only one unique rest frame and all movements are relative to that absolute rest frame. Then, the velocity-dependent relativistic effects are indeed present even if the observer is comoving with the frame, at relative rest. But they are not observable and measurable because all rulers, clocks, and measuring standards that are moving at the same absolute velocity have the same relativistic modifications. The speed of light has its maximum value $\mechanical{c}$ in the absolute frame, and in all other moving frames the relative speed is Galilean, depending linearly on the velocity of the observer's frame. So, the relative speed could exceed $\mechanical{c}$, similar to the fact that the relative speed of sound could exceed its value relative to the stationary medium.

Because of this crucial difference, the velocity-dependent relativistic effects should be traceable to a definite physical reason, like an interaction, in an absolute frame theory, while there is no rational reason whatsoever in the STR for the observable relativistic effects. The velocity-dependent effects are kinematic coordinate effects that vary with the choice of the reference frame in Einstein's theory. In other words, the PoR is physically implemented as a consequence of a fundamental interaction in the theory with an absolute reference frame for dynamics, whereas the PoR is an a priori metaphysical principle, with no reason to be sought, in the STR.

At this point, the true significance of a gravitational paradigm of relativity, introduced in the first chapter, can be sensed.

> [!note]
> Gravity is the only universal interaction that affects every phenomenon, because it acts on both mass and energy, including motional energy. Therefore, a truly physical theory of relativity and dynamics that encompasses all physical phenomena can be constructed only by relying on the gravitational interaction as its basis.

This is borne out as we shall see in detail in the formulation and the results of Cosmic Relativity.

Since the PoR is valid in both the STR and in the LPTR, and since both theories had the mathematical basis of the Lorentz transformations, both theories agreed with *almost all* known experimental results. I use the phrase ‘almost all’ to caution that there were some crucial experiments that had the demarcating ability, but were not adequately scrutinised. We will examine them in a later chapter. For now, let us proceed with the understanding that one could not favor one theory over the other based on any empirical result.

It is with this awareness of the ‘undecidability’ that we examine the circumstances that led to the birth of the Special Theory of Relativity, in the next chapter. I will mention, and also analyse, the specific empirical factors that were explicitly stated by Einstein as his motivations to develop such a theory, which rejected the notions of the absolute space and time in Galilean relativity. Anticipating the restricted empirical nature of Einstein's motivations, I now mention a class of electrodynamical experiments from the far past, performed and discussed by Faraday and Ampère.

## Interpretation and checks

List the dynamical assumptions about the rod, clock, and electromagnetic interactions before drawing a causal conclusion. If every accessible observable agrees, those experiments cannot select the preferred explanation. Conversely, a proposed preferred frame must specify a residual measurable effect rather than merely rename a coordinate. This distinction prepares the later claim that cosmic matter supplies an observable reference.
