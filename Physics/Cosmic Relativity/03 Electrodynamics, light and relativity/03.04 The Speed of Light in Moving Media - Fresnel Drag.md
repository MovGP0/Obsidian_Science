> [!TLDR]
> Fizeau's moving-water experiment tested whether a transparent medium carries the ether fully, partly, or not at all.

## Physical question and model

What does the Fizeau experiment measure about light in moving water? Treat the water as a transparent medium with a specified refractive index and velocity, and compare counter-propagating phases. The usual drag expression is first order in flow speed divided by light speed and neglects dispersion corrections.

## Lecture account

A crucial direct experiment by H. Fizeau in 1851, to determine the speed of light in moving transparent medium like water, proved to be very important in the construction of the final theory of relativity [10]. The experiment was to explicitly check which of the three possibilities for the dragging of the ether in moving matter was actually realised in nature: (a) there was no drag at all, (b) there was a complete drag, at the velocity $\mechanical{v}$ of the movement of the material, and (c) an intermediate situation with a partial drag, as proposed in Fresnel's theory of aberration.

![[cosmic-relativity-03-04-fizeau-moving-water.svg|**Fig. 3.4** Fizeau's experiment tests the dependence of light speed on the velocity of a moving medium. Flowing clear water lies in an interferometer path, and the observed fringe shift depends on the liquid velocity. The opposite optical paths must have unequal accumulated phase changes. Complete ether drag would produce a first-order effect proportional to L(n − 1)v/(λc). At about 5 m/s the expected shift is only about 1/100 of a fringe; the observed effect was still smaller, second order in the refractive index n.]]

The first-order proportionality in the source caption is $\mechanical{L}(\signal{n}-1)\mechanical{v}/(\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{c})$.

The experiment consisted of an interferometer in which light splits in amplitude at a beam splitter and travels the same path in two opposite directions, clockwise and counter-clockwise, as in Fig. 3.4. (This is the same as the Sagnac configuration and the fringes are very stable because light travels the same physical path in the two directions). A transparent tube allows water to flow continuously. In one path, light propagates along the direction of the flow of the fluid and in the other, against the direction of the flow. So, if the flowing fluid carries the ether and light with it, by some extent, then a phase difference between the two rays will be created, as some function of the velocity of the flow, and this can be detected as a shift of the interference pattern. With a relatively short pathlength and limited fluid flow velocities to avoid turbulence, the shift of the fringe is tiny, requiring high experimental skill in its measurement, which Fizeau no doubt achieved. Only about half the effect expected for a complete dragging of the ether was observed. A moving transparent medium does not ‘carry’ light along; the velocity of light in a medium with the refractive index $\signal{n}$, when the medium itself is moving at the velocity $\mechanical{v}$ is not $\mechanical{c}/\signal{n}\pm\mechanical{v}$, but it is

$$
\mechanical{c}'=\frac{\mechanical{c}}{\signal{n}}\pm\mechanical{v}(1-1/\signal{n}^2). \tag{3.3}
$$

With the refractive index of 1.33 for water, $1-1/\signal{n}^2\simeq0.44$. Thus, Fizeau verified that the dragging effect, $\mechanical{v}_d=\mechanical{v}(1-1/\signal{n}^2)$, was according to Fresnel's theory.

It was becoming clear that none of the optical experiments was able to detect the motion of the Earth or any apparatus relative to the hypothetical ether. If the ether existed, this suggested a complete dragging of the ether with the Earth. It was as if the optical phenomena in the ether were such that they were arranged to hide the presence of the ether! In other words, the result of any optical or electrodynamical experiment seemed independent of any motion relative to the stationary ether. But, it was also proved conclusively that the ether was not entirely dragged with the moving apparatus, by experiments with moving transparent media, like the one Fizeau did. Yet, the invisible ether was retained as an essential support for the wave theory of light. A lot of confusion prevailed.

## Interpretation and checks

Starting with light speed divided by refractive index in the medium rest frame, expand relativistic velocity addition to first order in the flow velocity. The coefficient of that velocity is one minus inverse index squared. It vanishes as the refractive index approaches one and does not imply a mechanical fraction of ether is entrained. For accurate data, use the frequency-dependent index and the full path geometry.
