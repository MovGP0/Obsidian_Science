> [!TLDR]
> The Trouton-Noble experiment tested whether motion through a proposed ether produces a torque on a charged parallel-plate capacitor.

## Physical question and model

Would a moving charged capacitor rotate under its own fields? Define a closed mechanical and electromagnetic system, including supports, stresses, and energy flow. A calculation using magnetic energy alone omits part of the relativistic momentum and torque balance.

## Lecture account

The results of the M-M experiment remained influential in the development of relativity for the next whole decade, while Lorentz refined his theory of the electron and the electromagnetic ether, and Poincaré completed the theory with a firm mathematical structure and a consistent physical interpretation for the Lorentz transformations. There were also other electro-mechanical experiments that were widely discussed; one experiment that attracted attention and debates was the ‘Trouton-Noble’ experiment [22]. This was originally suggested to F. T. Trouton in 1900 by Fitzgerald, who had proposed the length contraction hypothesis in 1892 to explain the null results of the M-M experiment. The second order effect that successfully resolved the M-M conundrum seemed to pose a severe problem in a modest experiment involving a simple parallel plate capacitor (Fig. 3.8).

![[cosmic-relativity-03-08-trouton-noble-capacitor.svg|**Fig. 3.8** (a) A charged parallel-plate capacitor hangs from a thin fibre. Its angular position is monitored as Earth's rotation changes its orientation relative to the velocity. (b) The source describes the electrostatic energy when the plates are perpendicular to the velocity. (c) With the plates parallel to the velocity, it predicts an extra magnetic energy and a torque towards orientation (b), contrary to the Principle of Relativity. No turning effect was observed.]]

The source caption writes the electrostatic energy in (b) as $\int\electric{E}^2d\mechanical{V}/4\pi\electric{\epsilon}_0$ and the additional magnetic energy in (c) as $\int\magnetic{\mu}_0\magnetic{B}^2d\mechanical{V}/4\pi$, with the induced field $\magnetic{\mathbf B}=\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\electric{\mathbf E}$. The diagram labels the contracted separation as $\mechanical{d}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$.

The motivation for the experiment was a puzzle arising from the relativistic transformations of electromagnetic fields, which emerged in Lorentz's work. For a capacitor made of two parallel plates of the area $\mechanical{A}$ and the separation $\mechanical{d}$, the capacitance is $\circuit{C}=\electric{\epsilon}_0\mechanical{A}/\mechanical{d}$. When the plates are charged to a potential $\electric{V}$, the electric field is $\electric{E}=\electric{V}/\mechanical{d}$ and the energy density in the field is $\electric{\epsilon}_0\electric{E}^2/2$. The total energy is $\energy{\mathcal E}=\circuit{C}\electric{V}^2/2$. When the capacitor is moving with its plates perpendicular to the velocity $\mechanical{v}$, the electric field is parallel to the velocity. The total energy remains the same because the capacitance increases by the factor $\gamma$, but the volume decreases within the capacitor by the same factor. If the capacitor is rotated by some amount, the electric field has a component that is perpendicular to the velocity, which results in a magnetic field $\magnetic{\mathbf B}=\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\electric{\mathbf E}$ (this can also be interpreted as the magnetic field created by the two parallel currents of the charges on the plates). Since the total electromagnetic energy density is $(\electric{\epsilon}_0\electric{E}^2+\magnetic{B}^2/\magnetic{\mu}_0)/2$, it turns out that the orientation with the least energy is when the plates are perpendicular to the velocity. Therefore, the capacitor will spontaneously align in that configuration, which violates the PoR. So, the detection of any such turning torque in the experiment will be a crucial experiment to test the PoR. Since the additional velocity-dependent magnetic energy is $\energy{\mathcal E}_m=\mechanical{v}^2\electric{E}^2/\mechanical{c}^2\sin\mechanical{\theta}$, the expected torque is

$$
\mechanical{T}=d\energy{\mathcal E}_m/d\mechanical{\theta}
=-2(\mechanical{v}^2\electric{E}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\theta}
=-(\mechanical{v}^2\electric{E}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)\sin2\mechanical{\theta}. \tag{3.29}
$$

The experiment done by F. T. Trouton and H. R. Noble was relatively simple in design. It involved the monitoring of the average angular position of a charged parallel plate capacitor that is suspended from a delicate fibre, as it moved with the Earth, rotating in space and in the ether. This configuration, set up as a torsional balance, is intrinsically very sensitive because it takes a very small torque to twist a thin fibre. For example, the pressure of light from a laser pointer (a few milliwatts of power) can violently deflect a small mirror, when suspended from a fibre of $20\,\unit{\mu m}$ diameter. One expects the orientation of the capacitor to change periodically, at twice the frequency of the Earth's rotation, always seeking the orientation of the lowest energy. However, no such change in the average orientation of the suspended capacitor was observed. The sensitivity was modest, just enough to detect a signal corresponding to $\mechanical{v}\approx10\,\unit{km/s}$. Some follow up attempts with better sensitivity have improved this by an order of magnitude, since the interest in this conceptually important experiment persists.

Fitzgerald died in 1901, before the experiment was completed by Trouton. In the ‘Scientific papers of Fitzgerald’, there is a note by Trouton about the idea of the experiment, as suggested by Fitzgerald. There, Trouton mentions that Fitzgerald had speculated that if a null result was seen, the likely reason would be the same second order length contraction behind the null result of the M-M experiment.

The null result of the Trouton-Noble experiment was widely debated then, and it remains as a point of discussion even today [23]. There were many elaborate analysis written and published. It is also discussed in W. Pauli's celebrated account of the theory of relativity [24]. An explanation is apparently not possible without taking into account the forces of constraint that provide rigidity to the capacitor arrangement. Therefore, a typical analysis of the experiment is complicated and prone to conceptual errors.

In any case, the null result of the important experiment by Trouton and Noble is considered resolved in a theory with an absolute frame to define motion, using the Lorentz transformations for the electrical and mechanical quantities. Then it is explained in Einstein's STR as well: when the observer is comoving inertially with the apparatus, the situation is equivalent to a state of rest, with no relativistic effects at all in the rest frame. There is no magnetic field or a length contraction in the comoving frame, irrespective of the orientation of the capacitor. When analysed from an external frame, there is a kinematical length contraction and *the explanation is identical to the one in the absolute frame theory*. This explanation is complex and elaborate in its details. *I note with emphasis that exactly the same (null) result is obtained for the Trouton-Noble experiment in both Lorentz's relativity and in Einstein's STR. Therefore, it is not suitable as a demarcating test of the two theories*.

> [!note] Source energy and field conventions
> The source mixes electromagnetic unit conventions in the caption and the body. With the SI energy density used in the body, the induced magnetic-field expression requires a factor $1/\mechanical{c}^2$. The stated magnetic energy has $\sin\mechanical{\theta}$, while the torque in Eq. (3.29) has the angular dependence of a derivative of $\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}$ and an extra minus sign. No volume or complete energy coefficient is supplied for that torque formula. The printed source expressions are retained.

## Interpretation and checks

A torque formula must have energy units because torque is the derivative of energy with respect to a dimensionless angle. The source's schematic expression omits geometry and field-volume factors, so it cannot be used as an absolute torque prediction. The null rotation result tests the complete system. As a check, explain why an observer-dependent separation into electric and magnetic energy cannot alone decide the observed angular acceleration.
