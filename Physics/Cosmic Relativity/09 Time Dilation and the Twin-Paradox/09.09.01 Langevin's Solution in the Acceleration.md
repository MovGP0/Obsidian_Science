> [!TLDR]
> The author rejects acceleration alone as a physical explanation for unequal clock ageing and criticises using only the home twin’s space-time diagram.

## Physical question and model

Does acceleration itself cause the standard twin age difference? In the ideal-clock model it changes the worldline but supplies no separate universal ageing term. The elapsed-time integral depends on the entire velocity history between common endpoints.

## Lecture account

Langevin’s usual resolution says one twin’s acceleration breaks relative-motion symmetry, making that twin age less. Calculations are typically absent, perhaps because the STR supplies neither a logical path from acceleration to ageing nor a formula for acceleration’s clock effect. Different accelerations of both twins create a further problem without a prescription.

Some texts claim a simple geometric resolution: draw the travelling twin’s trajectory in the home twin’s rest frame; it has shorter proper time, so the problem is solved. This inadvertently argues for an absolute-frame theory. Calling the accelerated twin ‘really moving’ violates the STR’s denial of absolute rest and real motion. An atomic clock has no ‘awareness’ of motion or acceleration; its reading must follow a physical mechanism and defined principles and formulae.

Treat both observers equally in relative motion, without acceleration, rather than choosing a ‘really moved’ twin, and this resolution fails. A diagram can be drawn relative to either frame, with or without acceleration. Such explanations omit the rocket-frame diagram. That diagram shows similar, approximately symmetric kinematics, apart from the short interval when the rocket observer feels inertial forces (Fig. 9.1 in [[09.04 Langevin's Twin-Paradox]]). A’s trajectory relative to B has shorter space-time distance and proper time, just as B’s relative to A does. This is the STR twin paradox. As Einstein emphasised, no acceleration-based STR resolution exists. His resolution required going beyond it, as we discuss shortly.

## Interpretation and checks

A traveller-centred chart is not globally interchangeable with an inertial chart during turnaround. Its distant simultaneity assignments change, and the metric must transform with the coordinates. Calculate a limiting case with a very short turnaround and long coasting legs: the age difference remains finite. This shows why neither an acceleration-only explanation nor a symmetric-looking sketch is sufficient.
