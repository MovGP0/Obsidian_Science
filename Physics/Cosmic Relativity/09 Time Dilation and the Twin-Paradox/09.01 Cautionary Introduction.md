## Lecture account

Chapter 5 analysed the origin and interpretation of first-order ‘local time’ in Lorentz transformations, relating to simultaneity and synchronisation of separated clocks. There I identified an inconsistency between Einstein’s light hypothesis and two moving observers’ experience of separated simultaneous events. This showed that invariant relative light speed and the STR must be abandoned. The synchronisation gap $-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{c}^2$ arises from factual Galilean light propagation, implying a real absolute frame for dynamics and relativity. Second-order time dilation must then differ quantitatively because clocks’ absolute velocities replace relative velocities. We will see that second-order effects also contradict relative-velocity-only STR. We explore experimentally accessible motional time dilation and *show that empirical data are adequately explained only by relativity with an absolute frame*. We begin with the persistent twin paradox.

P. Langevin introduced the twin-clock problem in 1911 to highlight *time’s dependence on relative motion in the STR* [1]. Repeated debates subsided after wise remarks without consensus, then returned. Serious journals now discourage such debates, understandably: vigorous criticisms in *Nature* [2] and *Physics Today* [3] contained diverse, inconclusive reasoning and established no logical or empirical inconsistency. Most physicists believe the STR resolves the paradox, perhaps through Langevin’s explanation or one of many textbook accounts. Yet this abundance is a problem: a physical paradox should have a unique solution resting on a unique physical reason. There is no consensus on the exact resolution, despite consensus that it is resolved.

Einstein’s own resolution adds confusion. He rejected STR-only resolutions as inadequate because time dilation is symmetric between relatively moving clocks. His resolution required GTR gravitational time dilation and appeared only in 1918 [4]. Einstein was relieved to find it through his new theory, yet STR textbooks remain largely unaware of it.

The situation is confused and lacks consensus. This chapter will show that the STR, treating all inertial observers equally, does not resolve the problem. We then examine experiments possible only after precision atomic clocks became available. Some results directly conflict with STR motional time dilation. There is sufficient evidence that motional time dilation depends on absolute velocity relative to a master frame rather than relative velocity between two clocks, inertial or otherwise. The source of relativistic effects leads beyond the STR and Einstein’s GTR to the vast matter-filled Universe and its enormous gravity, Cosmic Relativity’s master frame. Empirical and logical rigour should make the facts of this chapter sufficient to support this cosmic gravitational basis.
