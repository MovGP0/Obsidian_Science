## Physical question and model

Who decides what an experiment measures: the apparatus report or a later interpretation? Begin with the recorded fringe, time, frequency, or force and the calibration connecting it to a physical quantity. Historical preference for an interpretation is separate evidence from the measured result.

## Lecture account

The history of physics can sometimes be cruel to master experimenters who laid bare the crucial physical facts that eventually led to revolutionary theoretical developments. Interpretational developments of the theoretical kind sometimes wash out experimenters' own understanding of what they actually measured and verified, or falsified. In the case of ether experiments this was largely true and we comment briefly on this situation.

A. A. Michelson pursued the problem of the Earth's motion through a hypothetical ether for about 45 years, as chronicled in his masterpiece monograph ‘Studies in Optics’, written in 1927 [14]. His writings, and the publications by other experimenters who did similar experiments, claim the ‘confirmation’ of the length contraction. However, Michelson, as all other Earth dwellers, was in his rest frame while doing these experiments, and the interferometer was at rest relative to the laboratory and all the experimenters. In Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity, there is no length contraction in the rest frame, because the relative velocity is zero at all times. I repeat here what Einstein emphasised,

> Thus for a coordinate system moving with the earth the mirror system of Michelson and Morley is not shortened, but it is shortened for a coordinate system which is at rest relatively to the sun.

So, what exactly did Michelson confirm? He was of course interpreting his null data in terms of the Lorentz-Poincaré ether relativity, in which there is indeed a length contraction even in the ‘rest frame’, because the velocity factor is relative to the ‘absolute’ stationary frame of the ether. In fact, in a special conference dedicated to the Michelson-Morley experiment and its variations, at Mount Wilson in 1927 [15], Michelson stated, regarding the aberration of light, “talking in terms of the beloved old ether (which is now abandoned though I personally still cling a little to it)...”

The confusion regarding a legitimate interpretation of results is repeated when we come to other significant experiments like the Kennedy-Thorndike experiment (1935), which employed a Michelson interferometer with unequal arms [16]. We recall that the exact cancellation of the duration of propagation along the two arms of the interferometer happens only when the arms are of equal length. When one arm is much shorter compared to the other, as arranged in the Kennedy-Thorndike experiment, there is no such cancellation with only the length contraction. Then, the explanation of the null result requires both the length contraction and the time dilation in equal measure, in the ether-frame theories. The STR does not have these effects in the rest frame of the experiment—instead it just asserts that the velocity of light is always isotropic, as if the experiment is in a frame at rest. Then, no relativistic effect is required to explain the results, because the two-way phase time of the propagation of light is independent of the motion of the frame, by the light hypothesis. However, notice the title of the Kennedy-Thorndike 1932 paper: “Experimental Establishment of the Relativity of Time”. *They claimed establishing something that is impossible to establish according to Einstein's theory if the observers are in the same frame as the experimental device*. They employed an interferometer with a source of light and a detector at relative rest, and the entire apparatus was at rest in the laboratory frame, which was at rest relative to the Earth. The STR says this is equivalent to a frame at rest and no velocity dependent effect is expected. In contrast, Kennedy and Thorndike wrote, “we have shown that the frequency $\signal{\nu}'$ of an atom moving with velocity $\mechanical{v}$ bears the relation $\signal{\nu}'=\signal{\nu}(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)^{1/2}$ to that of a fixed atom”. A more modern experiment by Hils and Hall [17] is no different in this confusion—they concluded that their null result on frequency variations, an obvious result according to the STR while being at rest relative to their apparatus, had something to do with time dilation. In their paper they use the Earth's overall velocity as $377\,\unit{km/s}$ as if they were really moving in the cosmic frame. Then, of course, one cannot distinguish between the STR and the Lorentz-Poincaré theory. Other major results, like the equivalence of the energy and mass, are true in both kinds of theories. (While Lorentz made the connection between mass and electromagnetic energy, $\mechanical{m}'=\mechanical{m}+\energy{U}/\mechanical{c}^2$, it was Einstein who realised the universal nature of the relation between mass and all forms of energy.)

A wide survey of reports from experimenters who did variations of Michelson-Morley type experiments confirms a general pattern: the experimental results are by and large interpreted in terms of an absolute frame theory, by going out of the frame in which the experiment was performed, without the realisation that the only statement one can make while in the rest frame of the experiment is about the validity of the Principle of Relativity, the impossibility of detecting inertial motion employing a local experiment. If it is an experiment that uses light, one can also claim that *the null result is consistent with the isotropy of the two-way relative velocity of light* in that particular frame. None of the experimenters should have made any claim on observing or confirming time dilation or length contraction, because they are in the rest frame of the experiment and there are no relativistic modifications of length and time in a rest frame of the STR. Clearly, in all the instances, the claim of a verification was based on the Lorentz-Poincaré theory, though inadvertent. To see the relativistic effects in a moving frame, one needs experiments in which the emitter of light has non-zero relative velocity in the lab frame, like the Ives-Stilwell experiment. The physical effects like time dilation can be verified only by observing real clocks that move relatively, or by measuring the life-time of the unstable particles that move very fast relative to the reference frame of the observer. Such experiments had to wait till about 1940, much after the STR was accepted in good faith. But, importantly, identical results are predicted by the STR and the absolute frame theories in such cases.

## Interpretation and checks

Write the result as a constraint on a specified model, with its sensitivity and uncontrolled effects. An experimenter's preferred theory can be historically important without uniquely following from the data. Conversely, a later model can explain the same data without changing what was observed. As an exercise, reconstruct the Michelson–Morley null result while avoiding the words ether and spacetime until the model-comparison step.
