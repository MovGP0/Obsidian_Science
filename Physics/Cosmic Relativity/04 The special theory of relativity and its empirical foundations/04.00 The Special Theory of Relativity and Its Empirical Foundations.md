## Lecture account

## Abstract

Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity, with the postulated invariance of the relative velocity of light in all inertial reference frames, superseded the Lorentz-Poincaré theory of relativity, set in the ether medium with the Galilean relative velocity of light. Both were consistent with the Michelson-Morley experiment and all other results, but Einstein's formulation was on a much larger canvas, potentially encompassing all physics. I review carefully the empirical basis of Einstein's kinematic theory, as mentioned by him, and bring out some revealing and surprising facts for discussion.

## Introduction

In this chapter, we examine the main results of the Special Theory of Relativity (STR) and the empirical motivations that led to the STR, as mentioned in Einstein's 1905 paper and ensuing supplementary writings. Einstein was the sole originator of the theory, and entirely responsible for its peculiar features. But, it relied heavily on the previous work by Lorentz and Poincaré, on the relativity of electrodynamic phenomena, for its mathematical structure. The unique distinguishing postulate of the theory is the ‘light hypothesis’, that the relative velocity of light in any inertial frame is an invariant constant. This is the physical attribute that demarcates Einstein relativity in the featureless empty space from the Lorentz-Poincaré relativity set in the hypothetical electrodynamic medium of the ether.

In our discussion, the postulates used in building the STR will be stated precisely, with their context and justification clearly identified. We will then examine the empirical evidence usually cited in support for the fundamental and characteristic postulate of the theory, namely the constancy or the invariance of the velocity of light relative to any inertial observer. The motivation for this close scrutiny is two-fold. One is to gain clarity about the logical path from the empirical evidence to the full theory, to understand exactly the reason for the introduction of a postulate that defied the experienced wisdom. The second is to gain clarity about the real demarcating feature of the theory that gives it its unique falsifiable character, so that we can compare the STR with the Lorentz-Poincaré Theory of Relativity (LPTR) that has the same Lorentz transformations in its mathematical structure. The two theories have the same predictions for most experiments, in spite of their different physical premises and different fundamental postulates. In particular, the LPTR has the Galilean propagation of light (in the absolute ether), with the relative velocity of light dependent linearly on the velocity of the observer. It is important to clearly identify exactly what is to be experimentally examined if one wants to test the Special Theory of Relativity against other theories of relativity that may have a master (preferred) frame like the ether or the Universe.

## Interpretation and checks

Compare complete measurement procedures rather than isolated slogans about relative speed. A valid comparison holds fixed the emission and reception events, source motion, analyser geometry, and clock settings. Standard special relativity does not require the real Universe to be empty; it models flat spacetime or a local region where tidal effects are negligible. Identify which experiment would distinguish an added cosmic coupling from that approximation.
