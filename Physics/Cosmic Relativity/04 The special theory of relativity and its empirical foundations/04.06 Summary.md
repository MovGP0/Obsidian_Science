## Physical question and model

Review the chapter by separating the postulates, the transformation algebra, and the laboratory comparisons. A length, a clock rate, a Doppler shift, and a circuit emf require different event selections even when all use the same spacetime model.

## Lecture account

We have surveyed Einstein's motivations for the formulation of the Special Theory of Relativity that assigned equal status to all inertial frames, removing the notion of an absolute preferred frame in physics. The specific physical phenomena in electrodynamics and optics that motivated Einstein's unusual solitary stand, as cited by Einstein in his 1905 landmark paper, were examined in detail. These were the unipolar induction and the propagation of light. The Lorentz transformations that form the mathematical basis of Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity, and their consequences of the length contraction and time dilation, as discussed by Einstein, were presented. Then, some of the physical results derived by Einstein in the new theory of relativity were mentioned. The expression derived for the relativistic Doppler effect required an extended discussion because the formula derived by Einstein is actually ambiguous, in the context of his theory. This was related to the distinct difference between the motion of a source of radiation and the motion of the receiving observer. But, this is a distinction rejected in Einstein's STR.

Then I discussed Einstein's assertion that the unipolar induction phenomena were symmetrical in the motion of a conductor and the motion of a magnet, and pointed out that it was already contradicted by Faraday's many observations. It seems clear that Einstein was not aware of the more important electrodynamical experiments that presented the puzzles of the unipolar induction phenomena. However, the gap between the factual empirical situation and Einstein's limited treatment in his 1905 paper proves to be fatal for the STR; the results of the unipolar induction clearly show that the induction phenomena in electrodynamics involve the real motion in a preferred frame, and not just the relative velocity between a conductor and a magnet.

We entered a brief discussion about relativistic thermodynamics in the context of the STR. Here we saw that there are ambiguities that were never resolved satisfactorily, regarding the transformation of the temperature of a thermodynamic system in a moving frame.

We examined the empirical status of the central postulate of the light hypothesis in the STR. Ample clarification emerged that there is in fact no direct experimental evidence for the constancy of the relative velocity of light. In particular, I showed that the null result of the M-M experiment, with its two-way propagation of light, is the empirical support for the PoR, and not the experimental evidence for the invariance of the relative velocity of light. However, the light hypothesis is consistent with most physical situations, including of course the M-M experiment. This was already discussed in detail, in the previous chapter.

Then we analysed the common confusion in the reporting of the interpretation of experiments in which the entire experimental apparatus and the observing experimenters are all in a single frame, at relative rest. In such situations with the relative velocity zero, there are no relativistic effects like length contraction and time dilation in Einstein's theory. Therefore, experiments that employ static apparatus like an interferometer never test the second order effects in the STR, unless one imagines a hypothetical observer in a frame outside, relative to which the Earth, the laboratory, and the experimental apparatus are moving. Then, the predictions of the STR and a theory of relativity with an absolute frame are the same.

The second order relativistic effects were highlighted in the results discussed in this chapter. However, it is a first order effect in moving frames that is vital in the notion of simultaneity and the synchronisation of time between spatially separated clocks. In fact, Poincaré as well as Einstein discussed simultaneity and clock synchronisation as the central points of a theory of relativity. In his 1907 Jahrbuch article, after mentioning the conflict between Lorentz's theory (of 1895) and the Galilean transformations, Einstein wrote,

> Surprisingly, however, it turned out that a sufficiently sharpened conception of time was all that was needed to overcome the difficulty discussed. One had only to realise that an auxiliary quantity introduced by H. A. Lorentz and named by him “local time” could be defined as “time” in general. If one adheres to this definition of time, the basic equations of Lorentz's theory correspond to the principle of relativity, provided that the above transformation equations are replaced by ones that correspond to the new conception of time.

In the next chapter, we will discuss this “new conception of time”—of considering the local time defined by Lorentz as the real time marked by clocks—and its consistency with the experienced notion of simultaneity. Then we will face a crisis that places Einstein's STR in a difficult situation.

## Interpretation and checks

A reconstruction task is to derive a moving clock interval from the Lorentz transformations, then explain why the two rod endpoints require a different simultaneity condition. Next identify which parts of a one-way light-speed statement depend on synchronisation. The author's criticism should be assessed against those operational distinctions rather than taken as a consequence of the transformation algebra alone.
