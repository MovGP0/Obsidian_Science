## Reading the evidence

Use this list to trace the chapter's treatment of simultaneity, the train example, and separated-clock synchronisation. Bracketed numbers in the lecture account refer to the original numbered entries below. A reference records provenance; it does not by itself establish the conclusion for which the lecture cites it.

1. H. A. Lorentz, Electromagnetic phenomena in a system moving with any velocity smaller than that of light. *Proc. R. Netherlands Acad. Arts Sci.* **6**, 809–831 (1904).
2. A. Einstein, On the electrodynamics of moving bodies. *Ann. Phys.* **17**, 891–921 (1905). Source link labels: Crossref, zbMATH.
3. H. Poincaré, Sur la dynamique de l'électron [On the dynamics of the electron]. *Comptes Rendus* **14**, 1504–1508 (1905). Source link label: zbMATH.
4. G. Galileo, *Dialogues Concerning Two Chief World Systems* (1632), translation by S. Drake.

## Study route

Return to [[05.00 Simultaneity and the Synchronisation of Time]] after selecting one source. Identify its measured quantity, theoretical assumptions, and uncertainty or stated limitation. Track whether a cited source discusses reception time, coordinate time, or philosophical duration; do not treat those as interchangeable quantities. Keep historical descriptions separate from later experimental results, and distinguish the author's own proposals from independent tests.
