## Physical question and model

Why does the position-dependent term in Lorentz time matter? It changes how an inertial frame assigns times to separated events. Work to first order in relative speed when isolating that term, while recognising that clock-rate changes begin at second order.

## Lecture account

This chapter continues with the discussion of the central conceptual element of Einstein's STR, namely a “new conception of time”. Here, what is important is the concept of the ‘local time’ that depends on both the position and relative velocity [1]. This is the *first order* ($\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$) modification of the relative time, given by $t'=t-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{c}^2$. This term is what is crucial for the synchronisation of spatially separated clocks and for the determination of the *simultaneity* of two events in time. The notion of simultaneity is at the core of the synchronisation of spatially separated clocks and the definition of a global time. Hence, simultaneity is the central concept in building a theory of relativity, as understood clearly by Poincaré and Einstein. This is why Einstein focussed on the definition of simultaneity and on the method of the synchronisation of clocks right at the beginning of his STR paper in 1905 [2]. Einstein wrote in the first section of the paper,

> If we want to describe the motion of a material point, we give the values of its coordinates as a function of time. However, we should keep in mind that for such a mathematical description to have physical meaning, we first have to clarify what is to be understood here by “time”. We have to bear in mind that all our propositions involving time are always propositions about simultaneous events.

What is extensively discussed in this chapter is the *relativity of simultaneity*. We will meticulously analyse the consistency of Einstein's conclusions about simultaneity with the light hypothesis in the STR. This will lead to a vital realisation about the STR. Poincaré was one of the pioneers in completing the theory of relativity. Even before his commentary and completion of Lorentz's theory and the Principle of Relativity [3], Poincaré had studied the problem of the global synchronisation of time, as a member of the Bureau des Longitudes. He articulated with exceptional clarity about the interdependence of the propagation of signals, the inertial movement of reference frames, and the synchronisation of clocks, all in the context of a notion of simultaneity that is consistent with the Principle of Relativity.

The concept of simultaneity is of great importance to philosophers and sociologists as well, because of its relevance in the human context, with particular reference to the relation between the experienced time and psychological time. However, all researchers will agree that it is the physical time, marked and measured with real clocks, that forms the basis of any analysis that can be quantified. Hence, many philosophers studied the theories of relativity and commented on the developments regarding the physics of time in relativity.

The idea about the simultaneity of sensible events is ingrained in living beings, and we often take it for granted owing to our daily experience of perceiving multiple signals. However, our judgement of the simultaneity of events reaching our senses is strictly local, confined to the space of our individual physical body. We sense events as simultaneous when the sensations are inseparable within the minimum duration of time that we can discern. But, we are also aware from our acquired experiential knowledge that events at different spatial locations may have occurred at different times; yet, we may sense some events as simultaneous because different signals take different durations to reach us, from the locations of their occurrence. Articulating this general awareness on the simultaneity of events in different spatial locations, brought to us by physical signals like sound and light, in an accurate and mathematically consistent way, is the task of the physicist. It is of utmost importance in fundamental physics as well as in applications involving multiple clocks, because the establishing of a global synchronised time depends on the consistent protocol of clock synchronisation, which in turn requires clarity and precision regarding the simultaneity in time. The entire scheme of the satellite based navigational systems like the GPS relies on establishing consistency and agreement on simultaneity in time. Surprisingly, the pioneers in the theories of relativity disagreed on the physics of simultaneity. If one examines the history of physics for the evolution of the ideas on the relativity of simultaneity, some very important facts that require *immediate correction and revision* are revealed.

It is obvious that the availability of a universally synchronised clock everywhere gives physical sense to *absolute simultaneity*. Then, one can tag any physical event to the time on the local clock, at the location of the event. Since all such local clocks are synchronised, showing the same global time, the duration between any pair of events can be determined, independent of the motion of the frames or the propagation of the signals. The current theoretical framework denies any such absolute global clock, along with its denial of an absolute reference frame for dynamics. Hence, it is clear that *the factual existence of such a universal clock will at once cast a shadow of doubt on the current paradigm of relativity*. What is of great relevance is that there is indeed such a universal clock. Observational cosmology has shown us that the temperature of the CMBR is monotonically decreasing and the average temperature is the same everywhere in the Universe to very high accuracy (of a part in a million). Thus, *the temperature of the CMBR serves as a real universal time that is synchronised everywhere in the Universe*. The conceptual consequences of this fact will be discussed in [[08.05 The Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation|Chapter 8]]. This reiterates the existence of an absolute master frame for dynamics, and it should be kept in mind when we discuss the notion of simultaneity.

## Interpretation and checks

A synchronisation offset can be first order even when local clock-rate differences are smaller. Check the zero-separation limit: that position-dependent difference disappears. The term's units are time because speed times distance divided by speed squared has that unit. Do not drop the term merely because a speed is small if the experiment compares events at a large separation.
