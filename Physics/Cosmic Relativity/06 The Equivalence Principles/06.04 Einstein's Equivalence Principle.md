> [!TLDR]
> Einstein's equivalence principle identifies the local physical effects of a uniform gravitational field with those in an accelerated frame.

## Physical question and model

What disappears inside an ideal freely falling laboratory? The uniform part of the apparent gravitational acceleration can be removed along the laboratory's central trajectory. Assume a small region, a nonrotating local frame, and negligible nongravitational forces.

## Lecture account

Einstein's “happiest thought” [1] before he could embark on the program to construct the relativistic theory of gravitation was that gravity apparently disappeared in a freely falling frame. This is the essence of the UFF. A freely falling frame is accelerating freely under the action of a gravitational field, and somehow the gravitational field (gradient of the local gravitational potential) is cancelled inside such a frame leading to $\mechanical{g}_{\mathrm{in}}=0$, and hence weightlessness, $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}_{\mathrm{in}}=0$. Without asking for a reason for this happy situation, one may take this as a universal fact and consider the converse—in a frame accelerating at $d\mechanical{v}/dt=\mechanical{a}$ in a region without any gravitational field, one has the additional apparent force $\mechanical{\mathbf F}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf a}$, acting opposite to the direction of motion, and hence a ‘weight’ equal to $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{a}$. All freely floating objects in the frame will appear to move along the direction $-\mechanical{\mathbf a}$ with exactly identical acceleration, independent of any property of the bodies; *then the universality of free fall is an exact fact in such a frame*. Therefore, going into an accelerated frame is an easy method to ‘produce’ a uniform gravitational field (Fig. 6.2). Hence, Einstein equated the accelerated frame to a region of uniform gravitational field [8]. He expressed this thought as

> ...from a physical point of view, an (infinitesimally extended, homogeneous) gravitational field can be completely replaced by a state of acceleration of the reference system. This hypothesis can be expressed pictorially in the following way: An observer enclosed in a box can in no way decide whether the box is at rest in a gravitational field, or whether it is in accelerated motion, maintained by forces acting on the box, in space that is free of gravitational field (equivalence hypothesis).

![[cosmic-relativity-06-02-equivalence-principle.svg|**Fig. 6.2** Einstein’s equivalence principle: the complete local equivalence of a uniform gravitational field g in (a) and a frame without gravity in (b), with acceleration a = −g. In (a), two bodies fall with identical acceleration. In (b), the objects do not fall; the accelerated floor reaches them. The cases are kinematically equivalent, as if a fictitious gravitational field acted in the accelerating frame. The changing field directions outside the small box in (a) show why the principle applies only locally.]]

This is the statement of Einstein’s Equivalence Principle (EEP). The principle asserts that the physical laws and their consequences (results of any non-gravitational experiment) are exactly the same in a *uniform* gravitational field $\mechanical{g}$ and in a uniformly accelerated frame with $\mechanical{a}=-\mechanical{g}$. Hence, a theory of gravitational dynamics can be built by considering the dynamics in an accelerated frame (a non-inertial frame). The emphasis on the uniformity of the gravitational field is important. This restriction makes the principle applicable only locally, in a sufficiently small volume where the gravitational field can be considered uniform (constant). This was one of the most brilliant and useful insights in theoretical physics, as the subsequent developments culminating in the GTR show. Note, however, that the equivalence is assumed as a postulate, based on the empirically verified universality of free fall, without analysing or addressing the reason for the equivalence.

> [!note]
> The GTR does not have an explanation for inertia or for the universality of free fall, although it is considered a complete theory of gravitational phenomena and dynamics. The equivalence of inertia and gravitational mass is a postulate of the GTR.

The EEP is fully consistent with all experiments in non-relativistic quantum mechanics, contrary to doubts raised on various occasions (see [[06.10 Diving Deeper - Equivalent or Identical|Section 6.10]]). *The statement of the EEP is not about whether some measurable quantity depends on mass. It is about the equivalence of all physical phenomena and experimental results in a uniform gravitational field and in a frame moving with acceleration $\mechanical{a}=-\mechanical{g}$.*

## Interpretation and checks

An accelerometer measures support or other proper acceleration, so it reads zero during ideal free fall. Two separated freely falling bodies can still accelerate relative to each other because of curvature. This tidal effect limits the laboratory size and duration over which a uniform-field equivalence holds. An accelerating rocket can reproduce local weight, but it does not in general reproduce the curvature of an extended gravitational field.
