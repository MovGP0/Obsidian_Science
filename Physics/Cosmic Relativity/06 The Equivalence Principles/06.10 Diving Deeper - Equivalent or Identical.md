> The author distinguishes universal proportionality from identity of inertial and gravitational mass, and describes inertia as an effect of the cosmic gravitational environment.

## Physical question and model

Is equality of two measured roles the same as identity of two concepts? Inertia and gravitational coupling can be defined operationally in different experiments even when their numerical ratio is universal. A theory may then explain them with one parameter.

## Lecture account

An important question is whether inertial and gravitational mass are merely equivalent, with a universal ratio, or exactly the same quantity. The answer is not known in current physics, although Einstein considered the correct answer before eventually leaving it. For example, in 1913 he wrote of having “no choice” (‘The problem of gravitation’, Section 6.9: The Problem of inertia) [23]:

> Because if the inertia of a body can increase due to cumulation of masses in its vicinity, then we have no choice but to view the inertia of a point as being caused by the existence of the other masses. Thus, inertia appears to be caused by a sort of interaction between the mass point to be accelerated and all of the other mass points.

But two years later, his view had changed (see the next chapter).

The general consensus is that the ratio of these independent physical quantities, inertial and gravitational mass, is empirically universal. With a suitable choice of units, the ratio can be set to unity. On the other hand, a uniform gravitational field, already a theoretical idealisation, is equivalent to an accelerated frame only locally: the two are not identical. Therefore, in a theory that reveals the root cause of the equivalence principles, one would expect inertial and gravitational masses to be related through a universal physical mechanism rather than identical. Gravitational charge is then more fundamental, and inertia is an emergent property entirely due to the gravitational interaction with all other matter and energy. While gravitational mass is an intrinsic property of fundamental particles, like electric charge, inertia depends on their gravitational environment. This is what Cosmic Relativity demonstrates. As we will see, *inertial mass is gravitational mass ‘dressed’ by the cosmic gravitational potential: it is the product of gravitational mass and cosmic potential* (Chapter 12). Inertia acquires physical significance only with nonuniform motion. In any case, the result is the liberation of relativistic physics and dynamics from postulates of equivalence when dynamics is placed on verifiable physical reasons, without conceptual gaps.

## Interpretation and checks

Do not settle the physical question by renaming the parameter. Identify a proposed experiment in which the two roles would separate if the theory were wrong. A universal ratio can be normalised to one without determining its microscopic origin. This distinction is useful elsewhere: equality of inertial and gravitational response is unlike an accidental equality between unrelated sample properties.
