> [!TLDR]
> Bell inequalities bound combinations of local hidden-variable correlations; the source compares that bound with the cosine correlation of spin experiments.

## Physical question and model

Which assumptions give a Bell bound? Given a complete shared state, each local response depends only on its own analyser setting, and the shared-state distribution is independent of the settings. The response may be probabilistic; determinism is not required.

## Lecture account

Bell examined von Neumann's hidden-variable argument [15]. The source describes Bell's 1964 correlation near zero angle as linear, $C_T(\theta)\simeq-1+\theta$, compared with $C_e(\theta)\simeq-1+\theta^2/2$ for $-\cos\theta$ [12]. The author again disputes Bell's use of the EPR motivation and distinguishes deterministic completion from Einstein's representational question.

Let A have settings $a,a'$ and B have $b,b'$. Bracketed pairs denote their measured mean-product correlations. The printed four-setting inequality is

$$
B(C_t(\theta))\equiv(ab)+(a'b)+(a'b')-(ab')\leq2. \tag{15.7}
$$

The source sets relative angles of $45^\circ$ for $(a,b)$, $(a',b)$, and $(a',b')$, and $135^\circ$ for $(a,b')$ (Fig. 15.3).

The author substitutes the cosine correlation into the four-setting combination and writes

$$
B(C_e(\theta))\equiv(ab)+(a'b)+(a'b')-(ab')
=4\times\frac1{\sqrt2}=2\sqrt2>2. \tag{15.8}
$$

He argues that experiment tests an LHVT directly against measured correlations, without presuming quantum theory. Since he previously derived the cosine from his conservation argument, he further claims that the conflicting LHVT violates angular-momentum conservation and can be rejected on that basis. Experiments are said to confirm the inequality violation.

> [!note]
> The source prints −cos(π/4)=+1/√2, a sign error. With its anticorrelation convention and the displayed settings, the four-term sum has negative sign; its magnitude is 2√2. The printed positive result is preserved. A correlation function with a particular linear cusp is also a model example, not a unique correlation fixed by all local hidden-variable assumptions.

The author concludes that the LHVT is incompatible with his ensemble conservation argument and with experiments. He interprets Bell's bound as a restriction on the tested classical statistical models and argues that its violation does not establish a physical instantaneous influence in nature.

The source strongly criticises the broader nonlocality interpretation and the historical conflation of Einstein's representational incompleteness with von Neumann's causal incompleteness [11]. It recalls Einstein's anecdote in the Schilpp volume [2], where a physicist's belief in telepathy prompted Einstein to remark that this belonged more to physics than psychology. The author uses this to reinforce his opposition to nonlocal physical causation, invoking Popper's concern for rational physical explanation.

## Interpretation and checks

A specific linear-angle hidden-variable curve is an example, not the full class bounded by Bell inequalities. For four settings, the factorised local response averages lie between minus one and plus one; grouping the two sums of the second-side responses bounds the CHSH combination by two in magnitude. The singlet can reach twice the square root of two with appropriate settings. The general experimental comparison is discussed by [Clauser and Horne](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.10.526). Rejecting deterministic completion alone does not remove this probabilistic bound.
