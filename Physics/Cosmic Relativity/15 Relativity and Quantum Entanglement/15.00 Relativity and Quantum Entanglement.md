## Physical question and model

What does a Bell correlation test constrain? Separate finite-speed signalling, no-signalling outcome statistics, and Bell’s conditional locality assumption. The lecture’s proposed absolute cosmic frame does not make these three statements equivalent.

## Lecture account

## Abstract

Cosmic Relativity assigns space and time an absolute physical reference in cosmic matter and radiation, with gravity determining dynamics. The author derives absolute simultaneity, causality, and locality from this framework. His action mechanics remains indeterministic. He rejects nonlocal influences between separated regions and claims a fully local account of multiparticle quantum correlations consistent with experiment.

## Introduction

The source identifies the globally synchronised time with the time variable of the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) metric. Cosmic microwave-background temperature supplies an implicit time reference because a monotonic physical variable can label that time. The author treats this as a definite causal ordering of spatially separated events.

Both relativistic and nonrelativistic quantum mechanics must respect empirically established conservation laws. Momentum, angular momentum, and energy relate to spacetime symmetries. The source connects causality to locality: no instantaneous physical action across space. For separation $\mechanical{d}$, a causal influence requires at least

$$
\delta t\geq\frac{\mechanical{d}}{\mechanical{c}}.
$$

He regards locality as a non-negotiable physical principle, following Einstein [1, 2] and Popper [3]. In his view, claims that quantum theory and experiments establish nonlocality reflect a mistaken interpretation.

The chapter therefore compares cosmological time [4], entanglement, the Einstein–Podolsky–Rosen argument [5], Bell's theorem for deterministic statistical theories, and the measured correlations. The author seeks a local account of the apparent conflict between relativity and quantum correlations.

The author argues that a ban on transmitting usable energy or information is not, by itself, the full locality principle: any causal physical connection across a spacelike interval would be excluded. He presents two possibilities—real instantaneous state-changing influences, or a mistaken interpretation of correlations—and argues for the latter in this chapter.

## Interpretation and checks

Quantum correlations can violate a Bell inequality while their local outcome distributions do not enable controllable faster-than-light communication. A proposed local account must give a complete joint probability model and identify which Bell assumptions it satisfies. Neither indeterminism nor an ensemble interpretation alone avoids the bound. Read the locality claim in this chapter as a proposal to audit, rather than as an established consequence of cosmic time.
