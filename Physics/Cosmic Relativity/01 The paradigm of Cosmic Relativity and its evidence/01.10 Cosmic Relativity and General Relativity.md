## Lecture account

Cosmic Relativity treats both inertial and non-inertial motion in the same footing for calculating motional relativistic effects, in the absence of a local gravitational field. Being already a ‘gravitational’ theory, Cosmic Relativity smoothly integrates with the General Theory of Relativity, while modifying the GTR in a significant and fundamental way. Because Cosmic Relativity is based on cosmic gravitational effects, with the matter-energy in the Universe as the determining factor for dynamics and relativity, it provides a new consistent basis for General Relativity that is entirely different from Special Relativity and its empty fundamental space. It should be obvious that there is no location without gravity in our matter-filled Universe. Hence, the common statement in the GTR, that the relativistic physics becomes that of the STR in a region that is free of gravity, is inconsistent and physically meaningless. Also, what is called the ‘vacuum Einstein's equation’ of the GTR, $R_{ik}=0$, is inconsistent in a matter-filled Universe. This implies a modification of General Relativity, with the inclusion of the energy-momentum tensor of the Universe permanently in the equations relating matter and gravity. This is equivalent to reformulating the GTR with a unique privileged frame, removing its basic tenet of the general coordinate invariance.

The General Theory of Relativity is then reconstructed in a way that retains the agreement with all its impressive precision tests done during a century and yet compatible and *consistent with the prior and unique gravitational background of the Universe* with so much matter. A major prediction of the GTR is gravitational waves. Because of its anchor on the STR, the GTR predicts that gravitational waves propagate like light. However, *since light is already shown to be Galilean, from direct experiments, gravitational waves are also Galilean, as evident from the recent simultaneous detection of both gravitational waves and light* [23].

> [!note]
> Einstein's equation for gravity is modified by including the cosmic gravity and the master frame of the Universe as part of the equation itself. This necessary modification results in what I call ‘Centenary Einstein's Equation’ (CEE),
>
> $$
> R_{ik}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ik}R-\frac{8\pi\mechanical{G}}{\mechanical{c}^4}\energy{T}_{ik(U)}=\frac{8\pi\mechanical{G}}{\mechanical{c}^4}\energy{T}_{ik}. \tag{1.23}
> $$
>
> The extra piece $\energy{T}_{ik(U)}$ on the left is the energy-momentum tensor of the Universe, included as the non-removable integral part of the equation itself. Adding this term incorporates the Universe as a preferred frame for Einstein's equations, making the theory maximally ‘Machian’. Therefore, the ‘vacuum Einstein's equation’ $R_{ik}=0$ for a spatial region that is free of matter-energy is not exact.

The modified Einstein's equation has the advantage that it is fully consistent with all local precision experimental tests and also contains the origin of inertia, equivalence principles, pseudo-forces, and all relativistic effects (with absolute velocities) in inertial motion. This means that, far away from local gravitating bodies (like the Earth or the solar system), the theoretical description is not based on the Minkowski space-time, but on the absolute Robertson-Walker space-time *with a universal time and the Galilean propagation of light*, along with the second order motional modification in the rate of clocks and length of scales.

As already mentioned, the modified theory breaks the ‘general coordinate invariance’ assumed in the GTR, consistent with the lack of such invariance in the factual Universe. In fact, Einstein was in the dilemma, for about three years after his formulation of the GTR in 1915, whether to choose Mach's principle or the general coordinate invariance as the fundamental principle of his theory. Mach's principle implies an absolute frame of the matter-filled Universe, but the general coordinate invariance rejects an absolute frame, because it is an extension of the STR to frames in arbitrary motion. After several changes of opinion, Einstein finally chose the general coordinate invariance over the Machian option as the guiding principle, unfortunately. I will discuss this phase in detail in [[07.12 The GTR and Mach's Principle|Chapter 7]]. It is now clear that this modified theory of gravitation that is described by the CEE, with Cosmic Relativity as its basis, achieves the completion and consistency Einstein was looking for in a theory of gravitation, when he desired to incorporate Mach's principle in a relativistic theory of gravitation [24]. However, in 1915, the required knowledge about the matter content in the Universe was not yet available. Thus my theoretical program completes the quest for the true fundamental (classical) theory of gravity that is at once consistent with all known experimental facts and solves the open problem of the origin of inertia and pseudo-forces, left unresolved in the researches of Galileo, Newton, Mach and Einstein.

## Interpretation and checks

Moving a specified cosmic tensor from one side of an equation to the other is algebraically equivalent to including it in the total source. A distinct theory also needs its evolution law and any new constraints on the metric. Check covariant conservation of the complete source. Vacuum means vanishing local stress–energy, not necessarily zero curvature or absence of distant gravitating matter. A freely falling laboratory can use special relativity locally within tidal-error limits.
