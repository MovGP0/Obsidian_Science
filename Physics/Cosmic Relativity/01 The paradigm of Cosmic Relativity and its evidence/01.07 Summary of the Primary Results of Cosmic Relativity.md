## 3. The laws of motion (Newton's laws)

Newton's laws of motion are a set of postulates in physics. However, in Cosmic Relativity, the physical effects in relativity and dynamics are consequences of the cosmic gravity. Therefore, the laws of motion are derived from the gravitational properties of matter-energy in the Universe. (a) The first law is obviously the result of the homogeneity of the matter distribution of the gravitational potential, when the motion is not accelerated, at constant velocity. (b) When the interaction potential does not vary in space or time, the kinetic energy remains a constant. For arbitrary motion, the relevant component of the gravitomagnetic potential arising from motion along the ‘i-th axis’ is

$$
\mechanical{A}_i=-\frac{\mechanical{v}_i\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}}(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)^{-1/2}=-\gamma\frac{\mechanical{v}_i\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}}. \tag{1.10}
$$

Though $g_{i0}$ is homogeneous in a uniformly moving frame, which implies the principle of relativity, *$g_{i0}$ becomes time-dependent if there is an acceleration*, since $\mechanical{v}$ becomes time-dependent. Then, the interaction energy is time-dependent, which is another way of saying that there is a force acting. *The physical effect of a time-dependent potential is a reactive force on the accelerated system*. The force needed to maintain the acceleration is

$$
\mechanical{F}_i=-\mechanical{m}_g\frac{d\mechanical{A}_i}{\mechanical{c}\,dt}=-\mechanical{m}_g\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\left(\gamma\mechanical{a}_i+\gamma^3\mechanical{v}_i(\mechanical{\mathbf v}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf a})\right). \tag{1.11}
$$

Since $\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2=-1$ is a universal factor, we can write $-\mechanical{m}_g\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}=\mechanical{m}_i$. Then the relation between the acceleration and the force is

$$
\mechanical{F}_i=\mechanical{m}_i\left(\gamma\mechanical{a}_i+\gamma^3\mechanical{v}_i(\mechanical{\mathbf v}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf a})\right). \tag{1.12}
$$

which is the full relativistic form of Newton's law of dynamics. We have derived Newton's law of motion, without any postulate, as a consequence of cosmic gravity. All dynamics is entirely determined by cosmic gravity. Surprisingly, Newton's law of motion is deeply relativistic, because it concerns gravitomagnetism.

The conceptual implications are unprecedented. *I have unified dynamics and gravity, clarifying their physical link*. Truly fitting to the fact that both the law of motion as well as the law of gravity were discovered by the same person, Newton's dynamics is contained in Newton's gravity. Few would have imagined that Newton's law of motion was a gravitational law, and had the same feature as the Lenz's law in electromagnetic reactive induction.

In fact, Cosmic Relativity implies the geodesic principle, instead of postulating it. The geodesic principle states that freely falling particles move along geodesics of the space-time and it is the generalization of Newton's first law of motion. In Cosmic Relativity, free fall in a gravitational field itself is the result of the gravitational field $\mechanical{g}$ being exactly cancelled locally by the cosmic gravitational field induced by the accelerated motion $\mechanical{g}_a=-dg_{0i}/dt=-d\mechanical{v}/dt$ (Fig. 1.4). These very important results will be discussed in detail later.

## 4. The equivalence of inertia and gravitational mass

The physical equivalence of the ‘inertial mass’ in Newton's law of motion and the ‘gravitational mass’ that generates gravity is called the Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP). (The term ‘weak’ is not an appropriate adjective, but it just serves to distinguish the principle from a more general kind of equivalence assumed in the General Theory of Relativity.) The WEP was the basis on which Einstein postulated the local equivalence of a uniform gravitational field and a uniformly accelerated frame, now called Einstein's Equivalence Principle (EEP). Cosmic Relativity offers the deepest insight regarding the WEP (and also the EEP).

The insight was already mentioned, and it is clearly visible in the expression for Newton's law of motion.

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf F}\simeq-\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\right)\mechanical{\mathbf a}=\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\mathbf a}. \tag{1.13}
$$

*What we call inertia or the inertial mass in dynamics is the product of the gravitational mass and the gravitational potential of the Universe*: $\mechanical{m}_i=-\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2$. It is a hybrid quantity, arising solely from the interaction of a body with all the matter in the Universe. Hence the ratio $\mechanical{m}_i/\mechanical{m}_g$ is universal. We have derived the Equivalence Principle of Galileo and Newton, as a direct consequence of the gravity of the Universe. There are very important implications to this finding.

## 6. Einstein's Equivalence Principle

The EEP is Einstein's foundational postulate of the General Theory of Relativity. It asserts the local equivalence of physical effects in a uniform gravitational field $\mechanical{g}$ and in a uniformly accelerated frame with the acceleration $\mechanical{\mathbf a}=-\mechanical{\mathbf g}$. Thus, an acceleration $\mechanical{\mathbf a}$ is to be considered equivalent to a gravitational field. Since the gravitational acceleration in a gravitational field is $\mechanical{\mathbf a}=\mechanical{\mathbf g}$, the real gravitational field and the ‘equivalent field’ from the acceleration cancel each other in a frame that is freely falling in a gravitational field. Then there are no physical effects of gravity in the freely falling frame, locally. Our discussion of the accelerated frames in the context of Newton's laws shows us the cosmic gravitational origin of this important equivalence. An accelerated frame is in motion through the matter-filled Universe, experiencing the gravity of the cosmic matter. The acceleration causes the relativistic modification of the velocity-dependent potential $-\mechanical{v}(t)\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}$. The resulting inductive field is

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf g}_i=-\frac{d\mechanical{\mathbf v}}{dt}\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2=-\mechanical{\mathbf a}(t). \tag{1.14}
$$

## 7. Motional time dilation

For the first time in the long history of relativistic time dilation, we understand from Cosmic Relativity the physical reason for the slower rate of moving clocks. The theory of Cosmic Relativity establishes beyond doubt that the time dilation of a moving clock is factually the gravitational time dilation due to the velocity-dependent gravitational potential of the cosmic matter. All physical processes happen in the perennial presence of the cosmic matter and its gravity, and the rates of these processes have their ‘normal’ value (say, $\signal{f}$) at absolute rest, in the gravitational potential $\mechanical{\Phi}_u=\mechanical{c}^2$. Any motion relative to the cosmic frame alters the potential to a higher magnitude, $\mechanical{\Phi'}_u=\gamma\mechanical{\Phi}_u$. Since the gravitational time dilation of a clock is directly proportional to the gravitational potential experienced by the clock, the increased potential during the motion changes the rate of the time evolution to a lower value $\signal{f}'$. The rates of the clocks obey

$$
\frac{\signal{f}'}{\signal{f}}=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{\Phi'}_u}=\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}. \tag{1.15}
$$

The time dilation of moving clocks is

$$
\frac{\delta T}{T}=\frac{T'-T}{T}=\frac{\signal{f}'-\signal{f}}{\signal{f}}=\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}-1\simeq-\frac{\mechanical{v}^2}{2\mechanical{c}^2}. \tag{1.16}
$$

This is exactly the time dilation derived from the postulated Lorentz transformations in space devoid of matter. The crucial difference is that the time dilation of a clock depends on its ‘absolute’ velocity (speed) of motion—the velocity of true motion, relative to the master frame of the Universe. We now have a complete understanding of the time dilation of moving clocks, without any scope for paradoxes and confusion. Hitherto, physics was familiar only with the gravitational time dilation due to local sources of gravity, like the Earth. Now, Cosmic Relativity makes it clear that the time dilation of moving clocks is really the gravitational time dilation, due to the velocity-dependent gravitational potential of the entire matter in the Universe, induced only when there is real motion relative to the cosmic frame. Quantitatively, *this is the most accurate description of time dilation, applicable to any situation involving arbitrary motion*. With this key discovery of Cosmic Relativity, complete clarity and quantitative precision about the time dilation of moving clocks are gained. The understanding of the motional time dilation in Cosmic Relativity, along with the fact that the propagation of light is Galilean, will turn out to be very important when we discuss the atomic clocks in satellite-based navigational systems.

## 8. The origin of inertia and inertial forces

Our modern understanding of dynamics matured over the long duration of nearly four centuries, starting with the extensive and logically careful discussions by Galileo. However, a major puzzle that daunted one and all was the physical origin of inertia to motion, and the reasons for the inertial forces (proportional to inertia) that appear during non-uniform motion. The inertial forces are known in physics as fictitious forces or pseudo-forces. A major accomplishment of Cosmic Relativity is the complete and consistent solution to the problem of the origin of inertia that troubled Newton, Mach, and Einstein. Cosmic Relativity reveals that inertia and the inertial forces are gravitational effects because of the velocity-dependent gravitational potentials of the cosmic matter. Inertia remained as an unsolvable puzzle in physics because the current physical paradigm was formulated in an empty space without gravity. As we have already seen, the inertial mass that appears in the laws of motion is just the gravitational mass (the charge or the source of gravity) dressed by the cosmic gravitational potential, $\mechanical{m}_i=-\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2$. The familiar inertial forces are linked to the characteristic features of the two types of non-uniform motion, viz. rotation and curvilinear motion.

## 9. (a) The centrifugal force

The centrifugal force appears during any motion that deviates from a straight line. Its strength is quadratic in the velocity, and it is directed transverse to the trajectory, always opposing the deviation of the trajectory. The ‘absolute’ nature of the centrifugal force was Newton's argument for the reality of the absolute space. Ernst Mach's focussed criticism of Newton's reasoning already pointed to a possible connection with some interaction with the cosmic matter. The Machian speculation was profound, but never taken to its logical end on the basis of modern observational evidence [12–14]. Besides, Einstein's rejection of an absolute frame and his insistence on the general coordinate invariance in the GTR, buried Mach's proposal. For motion in a curved trajectory at the speed $\mechanical{v}(t)$, the direction of the gravitomagnetic potential $\mechanical{\mathbf A}=-\mechanical{\mathbf v}/\mechanical{c}$ is changing at the rate $d\mechanical{\theta}/dt=\mechanical{\Omega}$, even though its magnitude $\mechanical{A}$ could be a constant. Any change in the potential is a force acting on the body. The change in the direction of the cosmic gravitomagnetic potential is opposite to the change in the direction of the movement of the frame. Hence, *for curvilinear motion, the force is transverse and outwards, irrespective of the sense of motion*. From the time dependence of the direction of $\mechanical{A}$,

$$
\mechanical{F}_c=\mechanical{m}_g\frac{d\mechanical{\mathbf A}}{dt}=\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{A}\frac{d\hat{\mathbf A}}{dt}=-\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}=\hat{\mathbf r}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{r}. \tag{1.17}
$$

## 10. (b) The Coriolis force

In a frame that is rotating in the presence of the matter of the Universe, like our Earth, there is an enormous rotary current of the cosmic matter, which is an enormous amount of the charge of gravity rotating relative to us. The physical consequence can be easily guessed, relying on our experience of the magnetic forces in electrodynamics, due to the currents of electric charges.

In a rotating reference frame, the gravitomagnetic vector potential has the non-zero curl,

$$
-\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf v}=-2\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}\equiv\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g. \tag{1.18}
$$

This gives the cosmic gravitomagnetic field in any rotating frame. An object moving at velocity $\mechanical{\mathbf u}$ will feel a gravitomagnetic force $\mechanical{\mathbf F}_{gm}(\mechanical{\mathbf u})=\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=-\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times2\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}$. This is indeed the Coriolis force. The phenomenon of the Coriolis force was known for a long time. The description and the analysis of the force in rotating frames were done by Gustave de Coriolis in 1835. I have shown that the Coriolis force, an inertial force that remained elusive as a pseudo-force for three centuries, is the gravitomagnetic force arising because of the rotation relative to the matter distribution in the Universe.

Coming back to the experience from electromagnetism, the Coriolis force $\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g$ can be recognised as a *gravitational Lorentz force*, similar to the magnetic force $\electric{e}\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\magnetic{\mathbf B}$ that deflects a moving charged particle. In spite of the obvious similarity between the mechanical and electromagnetic phenomena, reflected in their mathematical descriptions, one did not make a firm connection earlier. Dealing with the Coriolis force directly as a cosmic gravitomagnetic force affords important applications, for example in the analysis of the physical factors in tropical cyclonic storms ([[10.09 The Twist in Tropical Cyclones|Chapter 10]]). The satisfactory understanding of the long-standing problem of the origin of inertia and the inertial forces is a great relief for physics as the basis of every science of the physical world. The alleged fictitious forces are genuine gravitational forces with the cosmic matter as their source. It is truly amazing that the distant part of the Universe is more important in determining the inertia, than the nearby Universe, because of its larger volume and matter content; the farthest 1/3 of the Universe has 4 times the amount of matter than the nearer 2/3, contributing more than half the total gravitational potential.

## 11. Cosmic gravity and spin physics

There are numerous other new results obtained in Cosmic Relativity, especially those pertaining to electrodynamics and quantum mechanics. These results and their experimental basis and tests, will be discussed in detail in [[13.03 Cosmic Gravitational Effects on Spin|Chapter 13]]. Here I briefly mention the very important fundamental results related to ‘spin’.

One area of research that opened up a physically rich and beautiful landscape in the context of Cosmic Relativity is the interaction of spinning objects, both classical and quantum, with gravity. The fresh insight is the natural extension of viewing mass as the charge of gravity, namely to *recognise spin and angular momentum as the closed (periodic) currents of the charge of gravity*. This is more than an analogy with the magnetic moment of electrodynamics because of the importance of spin in quantum physics—one may say that the magnetic moment is an auxiliary quantity that is locked to the gravitational moment. The magnetic moment is $\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}=(1/2)\int(\mechanical{\mathbf r}\times\electric{\mathbf j})\,dV$ whereas the spin angular momentum is $\mechanical{\mathbf s}=\int(\mechanical{\mathbf r}\times\mechanical{\mathbf p})\,dV$. The ratio is $\magnetic{\mu}/\mechanical{s}=\electric{e}/2\mechanical{m}$. This immediately suggests that all fundamental physical effects that are exclusively spin-dependent have a link to gravity. A reliable proof of this assertion is appropriate here.

Consider the elementary problem of an electron constrained to move in a plane, in a uniform magnetic field $\magnetic{B}$ applied perpendicular to the plane. The motion is in the familiar cyclotron orbit, with the frequency $\signal{\omega}=\electric{e}\magnetic{B}/\mechanical{m}$. The corresponding quantum mechanical situation has quantized energy levels, $(i+1/2)\energy{\hbar}\signal{\omega}$. The orbitals have to obey the Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition of quantized orbital phase $\signal{\Delta\phi}=2\pi$. For a spinless charge $\electric{e}$, the phase is

$$
\signal{\Delta\phi}=\frac{1}{\energy{\hbar}}\oint\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A}\cdot d\mechanical{\mathbf l}
=\frac{1}{\energy{\hbar}}\iint\electric{e}\nabla\times\magnetic{\mathbf B}\cdot d\mathbf s
=2\pi\frac{\magnetic{\Phi}}{\magnetic{\Phi}_0}=2\pi n. \tag{1.19}
$$

where $n$ is an integer and $\magnetic{\Phi}_0=\energy{h}/\electric{e}$, or the flux quantum; the orbitals are such that they enclose an integer number of flux quanta. However, an electron has also a spin $\energy{\hbar}/2$, and an associated magnetic moment $\magnetic{\mu}_e$. This implies an additional phase in the magnetic field, from the interaction energy $\energy{E}_s=\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}_e\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}$,

$$
\signal{\delta\phi}=\frac{1}{\energy{\hbar}}\oint\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}_e\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}
=\frac{1}{\energy{\hbar}}\oint g\magnetic{\mu}_B\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}\,dt
=\frac{1}{\energy{\hbar}}\frac{\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}}{2\mechanical{m}}(2\pi/\signal{\omega})
=\pi\frac{\magnetic{\Phi}}{\magnetic{\Phi}_0}=\pi n. \tag{1.20}
$$

For odd $n$, *the spin adds the quantum phase of $\pi$, violating the phase closure*. The fundamental quantum principle is not obeyed in the theory of a simple and ubiquitous physical situation, clearly implying an *additional phase* in the real situation, *from some other interaction with the spin*. In fact, the resolution of this serious discrepancy proves the necessity of the new gravitational paradigm of Cosmic Relativity. The interaction energy of the spin with the gravitomagnetic field $\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g=-2\signal{\boldsymbol\omega}$ in the orbital frame is $\energy{E}_g=\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g/2=-\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\omega}$ (the factor of 2 difference compared to the case of the magnetic moment is because of the factor of 2 in the definition of the spin moment and the magnetic moment). Then the gravitational phase is

$$
\signal{\delta\phi}_g=-\frac{1}{\energy{\hbar}}\oint\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\omega}\,dt=-2\pi\mechanical{s}/\energy{\hbar}=-\pi. \tag{1.21}
$$

Thus, *the missing phase required for the quantum phase closure is provided by the interaction of the spin with the cosmic gravitomagnetic field*, generated by the relative matter current in the electron frame. This is a fundamental result with far reaching implications to quantum dynamics.

What is satisfying is that Cosmic Relativity indeed confirms this in a number of physical situations. In classical physics we have both theoretical and experimental confirmations involving spinning bodies like gyroscopes. In quantum physics we have deep results ranging from geometric phases involving particles with spin and spintronics in chiral molecules to the much sought physical understanding of the spin-statistics connection ([[13.06 The Proof of the Spin-Statistics Connection|Chapter 13]]). The quantum phase $\signal{\phi}_g=\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g$ due to the interaction of the spin and the curl of the cosmic gravitomagnetic potential, is the reason behind the fundamental connection and the remarkable and robust twofold-division of particles into Bosons and Fermions [15, 16]. The same interaction energy turns out to be the deciding factor in some geometric phases, as well as in the unsolved problem of the spin-dependent transport of electrons in chiral molecules [17].

## 12. Cosmic Relativity and the quantum Hall effects

Cosmic gravitational potentials play a decisive role in the quantum dynamics of electrons in a uniform magnetic field in situations of two-dimensional confinement, like the quantum Hall systems. In particular, Cosmic Relativity has succeeded in unifying the physics of the integer and the fractional quantum Hall effects [18]. ## 13. Cosmic Relativity and electrodynamics

The modern theories of relativity were developed to explain the motional effects as well as the invariances in optical and electrodynamical phenomena. Therefore, it will be of no surprise to see that the relativistic phenomena in electrodynamics involve the absolute gravitational effects of Cosmic Relativity. Of particular importance is the fact that the ‘velocity’ that appears in Maxwell's equations, the equation of the Lorentz force etc. is the velocity of the charged particle relative to the cosmic frame, and not merely the relative velocity between two inertial frames, as currently interpreted. For example, for motion in a constant magnetic field from a magnet, the velocity in $\electric{\mathbf E}=\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\magnetic{\mathbf B}$ is that of the charged particle relative to the cosmic frame, and not the relative velocity between the frame of the magnet and the frame of the particle. This has important implications to the correct interpretations of certain crucial experiments. Also, the important electrodynamical phenomenon of unipolar induction is finally completely explained by Cosmic Relativity ([[10.14 The Unipolar Induction|Chapter 10]]).

## 14. Cosmic Relativity and cosmology

The paradigm of Cosmic Relativity is entirely based on the gravity of the matter-energy in the factual Universe. What is Cosmic Relativity's feedback into a better understanding of cosmology itself? This is a subtle issue because our understanding of the early evolution of the Universe is still incomplete. However, since Cosmic Relativity considers abstract space and time as metaphysical, giving precedence to matter and the physical phenomena involving matter, it disregards a concept like the ‘expansion of space’ as unphysical. In Cosmic Relativity, a description of the expansion of the Universe in terms of an expansion of space itself is merely a convenience, consistent with the homogeneity and isotropy of the Universe, and not a physical necessity. In Cosmic Relativity, the vitally important cosmological redshift of the radiation from distant sources is definitely a velocity-dependent relativistic Doppler effect, due to the real motion of the source galaxies.
