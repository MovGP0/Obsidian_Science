> [!TLDR]
> The source proposes replacing the squared light speed in relativistic factors with the cosmic gravitational potential.

## Physical question and model

What does a numerical relation between a cosmic potential scale and light speed establish? The estimate depends on density and the adopted cosmic distance scale. Treat its order of magnitude as a dimensional observation within that model, rather than an exact measured equality or an independent derivation of the speed of light.

## Lecture account

It has been known, to those who studied the relation between matter in the Universe and local physics, that the averaged Newtonian gravitational potential $\mechanical{\Phi}_U$ at any point in the Universe is numerically close to the square of the velocity of light, $\mechanical{\Phi}_U\approx\mechanical{c}^2$. (This assumes that the Universe has a finite age and hence, a finite causal size.)

> [!note]
> If this observation is elevated to a phenomenological assertion that all Lorentz factors appearing in relativistic physics are in fact $\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{\Phi}_U}$ and not related to light and electromagnetic phenomena (except indirectly, because cosmic gravity determines the maximal speed in the Universe), *such a new theory of relativity based on the gravitational potential of the Universe would be consistent with all known experimental results*.

The phenomenological significance of this assertion is of crucial importance for a theory of relativity. The claim that all empirical results will totally agree with the motional relativistic effects of cosmic gravitational origin already reduces the STR to a theory of merely formal value. This is because the cosmic gravitational effects necessarily imply an absolute preferred frame. Later, when we analyse several experimental results, we will also see the empirical robustness of a theory of relativity with an absolute frame. In any case, one will have to admit that every experimental result that is considered ‘special relativistic’, with the Lorentz factor $\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$, is equally well explained as a cosmic gravitational effect, with the factor $\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{\Phi}_U}$. So, if we follow the strict scientific method of sieving out the empirically unsatisfactory theory, one has to resort to new experiments that can demarcate between these theories. This situation is finally resolved by the results of certain crucial experiments and the undeniable fact that the matter and energy in the Universe have gravity. Cosmic Relativity has enough crucial predictions for the falsification and verification of the theory. The fact that both new and existing experimental evidence, without the slightest exception, supports Cosmic Relativity is its greatest strength.

## Interpretation and checks

Vary the density or the integration radius and check how the potential changes. If a parameter is chosen so that the desired equality holds, the equality is a calibration, not a prediction. To establish a physical law, specify a potential definition valid in an expanding spacetime and an observable that changes when the relation fails. A useful learning check is to list the independent measurements required before evaluating the proposed equality.
