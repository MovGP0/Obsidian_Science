> [!TLDR]
> Stationary action with fixed endpoint coordinates gives the Euler–Lagrange equation of mechanics.

<!-- Source: PDF page 135, printed page 119. -->

In the Lagrangian formulation of Newtonian mechanics, one is given a *Lagrangian* $\energy{L}(\mechanical{q},\mechanical{\dot q})$, a function of the generalized coordinate $\mechanical{q}$ and its velocity $\mechanical{\dot q}\equiv\mathrm d\mechanical{q}/\mathrm d\mechanical{t}$. One then forms an *action functional* $\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]$,

$$
\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\energy{L}(\mechanical{q},\mechanical{\dot q})\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}, \tag{4.1}
$$

by integrating the Lagrangian over a selected path $\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t})$. The path that satisfies the equations of motion is the one about which $\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]$ is stationary: Under a variation $\delta\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t})$ of this path, restricted by

$$
\delta\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t_1})=\delta\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t_2})=0 \tag{4.2}
$$

but otherwise arbitrary in the interval $\mechanical{t_1}<\mechanical{t}<\mechanical{t_2}$, the action does not change, $\delta\energy{S}=0$.

The change in the action is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{S}&=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\delta\energy{L}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\\
&=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{q}}\delta\mechanical{q}+\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{\dot q}}\delta\mechanical{\dot q}\right)\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\\
&=\left.\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{\dot q}}\delta\mechanical{q}\right|_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}
+\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{q}}-\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\mechanical{t}}\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{\dot q}}\right)\delta\mechanical{q}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t},
\end{aligned}
$$

where, in the last step, we have used $\delta\mechanical{\dot q}=\mathrm d(\delta\mechanical{q})/\mathrm d\mechanical{t}$ and integrated by parts. The boundary terms vanish by virtue of Eq. (4.2). Because the variation is arbitrary between $\mechanical{t_1}$ and $\mechanical{t_2}$,

$$
\delta\energy{S}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\mechanical{t}}\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{\dot q}}-\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{q}}=0. \tag{4.3}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 136, printed page 120. -->

This is the *Euler–Lagrange* equation for a one-dimensional mechanical system. Generalization to higher dimensions is immediate.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Lagrangian | The mechanical Lagrangian in geometrized units. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^2}$ | Action | The time integral of the Lagrangian. |
| $\mechanical{q}$ | — | Generalized coordinate | The coordinate whose physical unit depends on the mechanical system. |
| $\mechanical{\dot q}$ | — | Generalized velocity | The time derivative of the generalized coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{t_1},\mechanical{t_2}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Times | The time and the fixed endpoints. |
