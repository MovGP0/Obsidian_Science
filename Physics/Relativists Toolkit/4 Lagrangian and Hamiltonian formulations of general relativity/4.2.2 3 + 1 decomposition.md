> [!TLDR]
> A spatial foliation separates the time-flow vector into lapse and shift and expresses the spacetime metric through the spatial metric.

<!-- Source: PDF page 145, printed page 129. -->

The Hamiltonian formulation of a field theory is more involved. Here, the Hamiltonian $\energy{H}[\mechanical{p},\signal{q}]$ is a *functional* of $\signal{q}$, the field configuration, and $\mechanical{p}$, the canonical momentum, on a spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma$. To express the action in terms of the Hamiltonian it is necessary to foliate $\mathcal V$ with a family of spacelike hypersurfaces, one for each ‘instant of time.’ This is the purpose of the $3+1$ decomposition.

To effect this decomposition we introduce a scalar field $\mechanical{t}(x^\alpha)$ such that $\mechanical{t}=\mathrm{constant}$ describes a family of nonintersecting spacelike hypersurfaces $\Sigma_t$. This ‘time function’ is completely arbitrary; the only requirements are that $\mechanical{t}$ be a single-valued function of $x^\alpha$, and that $n_\alpha\propto\partial_\alpha\mechanical{t}$, the unit normal to the hypersurfaces, be a future-directed timelike vector field.

On each of the hypersurfaces $\Sigma_t$ we install coordinates $y^a$. A priori, the coordinates on one hypersurface need not be related to the coordinates on another hypersurface. It is, however, convenient to introduce a relationship, as follows (Fig. 4.2). Consider a congruence of curves $\gamma$ intersecting the hypersurfaces $\Sigma_t$. We do not assume that these curves are geodesics, nor that they intersect the hypersurfaces orthogonally. We use $\mechanical{t}$ as a parameter on the curves, and the vector $t^\alpha$ is tangent to the congruence. It is easy to check that the relation

$$
t^\alpha\partial_\alpha\mechanical{t}=1 \tag{4.31}
$$

follows from the construction. A particular curve $\gamma_P$ from the congruence defines a mapping from a point $P$ on $\Sigma_t$ to a point $P'$ on $\Sigma_{t'}$, and then to a point $P''$ on $\Sigma_{t''}$, and so on. To fix the coordinates of $P'$ and $P''$, given $y^a(P)$ on $\Sigma_t$, we simply impose $y^a(P'')=y^a(P')=y^a(P)$. Thus, $y^a$ is held constant on each member of the congruence.

This construction defines a coordinate system $(\mechanical{t},y^a)$ in $\mathcal V$. There exists a transformation between this and the system $x^\alpha$ originally in use: $x^\alpha=x^\alpha(\mechanical{t},y^a)$. We have

![[relativists-toolkit-fig-4-2-foliation.svg|**Fig. 4.2** Foliation of spacetime by spacelike hypersurfaces.]]

<!-- Source: PDF page 146, printed page 130. -->

$$
t^\alpha=\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{t}}\right)_{y^a}, \tag{4.32}
$$

and we define

$$
e^\alpha_a=\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial y^a}\right)_t \tag{4.33}
$$

to be tangent vectors on $\Sigma_t$. These relations imply that in the coordinates $(\mechanical{t},y^a)$, $t^\alpha\stackrel{*}{=}\delta^\alpha_t$ and $e^\alpha_a\stackrel{*}{=}\delta^\alpha_a$. We also have

$$
\mathcal L_t e^\alpha_a=0, \tag{4.34}
$$

which holds in any coordinate system.

We now introduce the unit normal to the hypersurfaces:

$$
n_\alpha=-N\partial_\alpha\mechanical{t},\qquad n_\alpha e^\alpha_a=0, \tag{4.35}
$$

where the scalar function $N$, called the *lapse*, ensures that $n_\alpha$ is properly normalized. Because the curves $\gamma$ do not intersect $\Sigma_t$ orthogonally, $t^\alpha$ is not parallel to $n^\alpha$. We may decompose $t^\alpha$ in the basis provided by the normal and tangent vectors (Fig. 4.3):

$$
t^\alpha=Nn^\alpha+N^ae^\alpha_a; \tag{4.36}
$$

the three-vector $N^a$ is called the *shift*. It is easy to check that Eq. (4.36) is compatible with Eq. (4.31).

We can use the coordinate transformation $x^\alpha=x^\alpha(\mechanical{t},y^a)$ to express the metric in the coordinates $(\mechanical{t},y^a)$. We start by writing

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm dx^\alpha&=t^\alpha\mathrm d\mechanical{t}+e^\alpha_a\mathrm dy^a\\
&=(N\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t})n^\alpha+(\mathrm dy^a+N^a\mathrm d\mechanical{t})e^\alpha_a,
\end{aligned}
$$

![[relativists-toolkit-fig-4-3-lapse-shift.svg|**Fig. 4.3** Decomposition of tᵅ into lapse and shift.]]

<!-- Source: PDF page 147, printed page 131. -->

which follows at once from Eqs. (4.32), (4.33), and (4.36). The line element is then given by $\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=g_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm dx^\alpha\mathrm dx^\beta$, or

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-N^2\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+h_{ab}(\mathrm dy^a+N^a\mathrm d\mechanical{t})(\mathrm dy^b+N^b\mathrm d\mechanical{t}), \tag{4.37}
$$

where $h_{ab}=g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b$ is the induced metric on $\Sigma_t$.

We may now express the metric determinant $g$ in terms of $h\equiv\det[h_{ab}]$ and the lapse function. We recall that $g^{tt}=\operatorname{cofactor}(g_{tt})/g=h/g$, as follows from Eq. (4.37). But $g^{tt}=g^{\alpha\beta}t_{,\alpha}t_{,\beta}=N^{-2}g^{\alpha\beta}n_\alpha n_\beta=-N^{-2}$, where Eq. (4.35) was used. The desired expression is therefore

$$
\sqrt{-g}=N\sqrt{h}. \tag{4.38}
$$

Equations (4.36), (4.37), and (4.38) are the fundamental results of the $3+1$ decomposition.
