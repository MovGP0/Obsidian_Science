> [!TLDR]
> A field action with fixed boundary values gives the covariant Euler–Lagrange equation, including the curved-spacetime Klein–Gordon equation.

<!-- Source: PDF page 136, printed page 120. -->

We now consider the dynamics of a field $\signal{q}(x^\alpha)$ in curved spacetime. Although this field could be of any type (scalar, vector, tensor, spinor), for simplicity we shall restrict our attention to the case of a scalar field.

In the Lagrangian formulation of a field theory, one is given an arbitrary region $\mathcal V$ of the spacetime manifold, bounded by a closed hypersurface $\partial\mathcal V$. One is also given a *Lagrangian density* $\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{q},\signal{q}_{,\alpha})$, a scalar function of the field and its first derivatives. The action functional is then

$$
\energy{S}[\signal{q}]=\int_{\mathcal V}\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{q},\signal{q}_{,\alpha})\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x. \tag{4.4}
$$

Dynamical equations for $\signal{q}$ are obtained by introducing a variation $\delta\signal{q}(x^\alpha)$ that is arbitrary within $\mathcal V$ but vanishes everywhere on $\partial\mathcal V$,

$$
\left.\delta\signal{q}\right|_{\partial\mathcal V}=0, \tag{4.5}
$$

and by demanding that $\delta\energy{S}$ vanish if the variation is about the actual path $\signal{q}(x^\alpha)$. Equation (4.5) is the field-theoretical counterpart to Eq. (4.2).

Upon such a variation (we use the notation $\mathcal L'\equiv\partial\energy{\mathcal L}/\partial\signal{q}$, $\mathcal L^\alpha\equiv\partial\energy{\mathcal L}/\partial\signal{q}_{,\alpha}$),

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{S}&=\int_{\mathcal V}(\mathcal L'\delta\signal{q}+\mathcal L^\alpha\delta\signal{q}_{,\alpha})\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x\\
&=\int_{\mathcal V}\left[\mathcal L'\delta\signal{q}+(\mathcal L^\alpha\delta\signal{q})_{;\alpha}-\mathcal L^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}\delta\signal{q}\right]\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x\\
&=\int_{\mathcal V}(\mathcal L'-\mathcal L^\alpha{}_{;\alpha})\delta\signal{q}\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x+\oint_{\partial\mathcal V}\mathcal L^\alpha\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha,
\end{aligned}
$$

where Gauss’ theorem (Section 3.3) was used in the last step. The surface integral vanishes by virtue of Eq. (4.5), and because $\delta\signal{q}$ is arbitrary within $\mathcal V$ we obtain

$$
\delta\energy{S}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\nabla_\alpha\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,\alpha}}-\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{q}}=0. \tag{4.6}
$$

This is the Euler–Lagrange equation for a single scalar field $\signal{q}$. Generalization to a collection of fields is immediate, and the procedure can be taken over to fields of arbitrary tensorial or spinorial types.

<!-- Source: PDF page 137, printed page 121. -->

As a concrete example, let us consider a Klein–Gordon field $\signal{\psi}$ with Lagrangian density

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac12\left(g^{\mu\nu}\signal{\psi}_{,\mu}\signal{\psi}_{,\nu}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}^2\right).
$$

We have $\mathcal L^\alpha=-g^{\alpha\beta}\signal{\psi}_{,\beta}$, $\mathcal L^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}=-g^{\alpha\beta}\signal{\psi}_{;\alpha\beta}$, and $\mathcal L'=-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}$. The Euler–Lagrange equation becomes

$$
g^{\alpha\beta}\signal{\psi}_{;\alpha\beta}-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}=0,
$$

which is the curved-spacetime version of the Klein–Gordon equation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{q},\signal{\psi}$ | — | Scalar fields | The general field and the Klein–Gordon field. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-2}}$ | Lagrangian density | The scalar density with the conventional geometrized action normalization. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^2}$ | Action | The spacetime integral of the Lagrangian density. |
| $g^{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Inverse metric | The metric used in the covariant field equation. |
| $x^\alpha$ | — | Coordinates | The spacetime coordinates used in the action integral. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-1}}$ | Klein–Gordon mass parameter | The inverse-length parameter in the wave equation. |
| $\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha$ | — | Directed surface element | The boundary element in Gauss' theorem. |
