## Problem 1

The Lagrangian density for the free electromagnetic field is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\electric{F^{\alpha\beta}}\electric{F_{\alpha\beta}},
$$

where $\electric{F_{\alpha\beta}}=\electric{A_\beta}{}_{;\alpha}-\electric{A_\alpha}{}_{;\beta}$ is the Faraday tensor, expressed in terms of the vector potential $\electric{A_\alpha}$.

**(a)** Derive the Maxwell field equations in vacuum, $\electric{F^{\alpha\beta}}{}_{;\beta}=0$, on the basis of this Lagrangian density.

**(b)** Show that the stress-energy tensor for the electromagnetic field is given by

$$
\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\electric{F_{\alpha\mu}}\electric{F_\beta{}^\mu}-\frac14g_{\alpha\beta}\electric{F^{\mu\nu}}\electric{F_{\mu\nu}}\right).
$$

## Problem 2

The Lagrangian density for a point particle of mass $\mechanical{m}$ moving on a world line $\mechanical{z^\alpha}(\mechanical{\lambda})$ is given by

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\mechanical{m}\int\sqrt{-g_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\dot z^\alpha}\mechanical{\dot z^\beta}}\,
\delta_4(x,z)\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda},
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 174, printed page 158. -->

where $\delta_4(x,x')$ is a four-dimensional, scalarized $\delta$-function satisfying

$$
\int_{\mathcal V}\delta_4(x,x')\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x=1
$$

if $x'$ is within the domain of integration; we also have $\mechanical{\dot z^\alpha}=\mathrm d\mechanical{z^\alpha}/\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda}$, and the parameterization of the world line is arbitrary.

**(a)** Derive an expression for the stress-energy tensor of a point particle. To simplify this expression, set $\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda}=\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau}$ (with $\mechanical{\tau}$ denoting proper time on the world line) at the end of the calculation.

**(b)** Prove that when it is applied to a point particle, the statement $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}{}_{;\beta}=0$ gives rise to the geodesic equation for $\mechanical{u^\alpha}=\mathrm d\mechanical{z^\alpha}/\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau}$.

**(c)** Explain whether the result of part (b) constitutes a valid proof of the statement that the Einstein field equations predict the motion of a massive body to be geodesic.

## Problem 3

Calculate the gravitational action $\energy{S_G}$ for a region $\mathcal V$ of Schwarzschild spacetime. Take $\mathcal V$ to be bounded by the hypersurfaces $\Sigma_{t_1}$, $\Sigma_{t_2}$, $\Sigma_R$, and $\Sigma_\rho$, where $\Sigma_{t_1}$ ($\Sigma_{t_2}$) is the spacelike hypersurface described by $\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{t_1}$ ($\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{t_2}$), and where $\Sigma_R$ ($\Sigma_\rho$) is the three-cylinder at $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{R}$ ($\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{\rho}$). Here, $2\mechanical{M}<\mechanical{\rho}<\mechanical{R}$. At the end of the calculation, take the limits $\mechanical{R}\to\infty$ and $\mechanical{\rho}\to2\mechanical{M}$.

## Problem 5

Recall that in Section 3.6.5 we introduced a mass function $\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r})$ that determines the three-metric of a spherically symmetric hypersurface. Prove that

$$
-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{S(r)}(k-k_0)\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta
=\mechanical{r}\left(1-\sqrt{1-2\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{r}}\right),
$$

where $S(\mechanical{r})$ is a two-surface of constant $\mechanical{r}$. Use this to show that $\mechanical{m}(\infty)$ is the ADM mass of this hypersurface.

## Problem 6

In this problem we explore some consequences of Eq. (4.89), which gives an expression for the ADM mass of a stationary spacetime.

**(a)** Prove that the right-hand side of Eq. (4.89) is independent of the choice of hypersurface $\Sigma$.

**(b)** Show that if $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}$ is the stress-energy tensor of a static perfect fluid, then Eq. (4.89) reduces to

$$
\mechanical{M}=\int_\Sigma(\energy{\rho}+3\mechanical{p})e^{\mechanical{\Phi}}\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y,
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 175, printed page 159. -->

where $\energy{\rho}$ is the mass density, $\mechanical{p}$ the pressure, and $e^{2\mechanical{\Phi}}\equiv-g_{\alpha\beta}\xi^\alpha_{(t)}\xi^\beta_{(t)}$. [Hints: A perfect fluid is static if its four-velocity $\mechanical{u^\alpha}$ is parallel to $\xi^\alpha_{(t)}$. You may assume that the spacetime is also static.]

**(c)** Specialize to spherical symmetry, and write the spacetime metric as

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-e^{2\mechanical{\Phi}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2
+(1-2\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{r})^{-1}\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2,
$$

in which $\mechanical{\Phi}$ and $\mechanical{m}$ are functions of $\mechanical{r}$. Refer back to the result of Problem 5 and deduce the identity

$$
\int_\Sigma\energy{\rho}(1-2\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{r})^{1/2}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}
=\int_\Sigma(\energy{\rho}+3\mechanical{p})e^{\mechanical{\Phi}}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V},
$$

where $\mathrm d\mechanical{V}=\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y$ is the invariant volume element on the hypersurface $\Sigma$.

**(d)** Specialize now to a weak-field situation, for which the metric can be expressed as

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-(1+2\mechanical{\Phi})\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2
+(1-2\mechanical{\Phi})(\mathrm d\mechanical{x}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{y}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{z}^2);
$$

the Newtonian potential $\mechanical{\Phi}$ is a function of $\mechanical{\bar r}\equiv\sqrt{\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2+\mechanical{z}^2}$. Working consistently in the weak-field approximation, show that the identity derived in part (c) reduces to

$$
\frac{1}{8\pi}\int_\Sigma|\nabla\mechanical{\Phi}|^2\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V}
=3\int_\Sigma\mechanical{p}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{V},
$$

in which $\mathrm d\mechanical{V}$ and all vectorial operations refer to the three-dimensional flat space of ordinary vector calculus. The left-hand side represents (minus) the total gravitational potential energy of the system. For a monoatomic ideal gas in thermodynamic equilibrium, the right-hand side represents twice the total kinetic energy of the system. This equation is therefore a formulation of the virial theorem of Newtonian gravitational physics. The identity of part (c) can then be interpreted as a general-relativistic version of the virial theorem.

## Problem 7

The ADM mass is usually defined by

$$
\mechanical{M}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\oint_{S\to\infty}(D^b\gamma_{ab}-D_a\gamma)r^a\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta,
$$

which is a very different expression from the one appearing in Section 4.3.1. Here, $S$ is the two-surface that encloses the spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma$. If $h_{ab}$ is the metric on $\Sigma$ in arbitrary coordinates $y^a$, then $\gamma_{ab}\equiv h_{ab}-h^0_{ab}$, where $h^0_{ab}$ is the metric of flat space in the same coordinates. We also have $\gamma\equiv\gamma^a{}_a$, and $D_a$ is the covariant derivative associated with the flat metric $h^0_{ab}$, which is used to raise and lower all indices. Finally, $r^a$ is the unit normal of the surface $S$, and $\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta$ is the surface element on $S$.

<!-- Source: PDF page 176, printed page 160. -->

The purpose of this problem is to prove that this definition is equivalent to the one given in the text,

$$
\mechanical{M}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{S\to\infty}(k-k_0)\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta,
$$

where $k$ ($k_0$) is the extrinsic curvature of $S$ embedded in $\Sigma$ (flat space). You may proceed along the following lines:

Because both expressions are invariant under a coordinate transformation, we may use, in a neighbourhood of $S$, the coordinates $(\mechanical{\ell},\theta^A)$, where $\mechanical{\ell}$ is proper distance off $S$ in the direction orthogonal to $S$, and $\theta^A$ are coordinates on $S$ which are Lie transported off $S$ along curves orthogonal to $S$. In these coordinates the metric on $\Sigma$ is given by

$$
h_{ab}\mathrm dy^a\mathrm dy^b=\mathrm d\mechanical{\ell}^2+\tilde\sigma_{AB}(\mechanical{\ell})\,\mathrm d\theta^A\mathrm d\theta^B,
$$

where $\tilde\sigma_{AB}(\mechanical{\ell})$ (which also depends on $\theta^A$) is such that $\tilde\sigma_{AB}(0)=\sigma_{AB}$, the induced metric on $S$. Similarly,

$$
h^0_{ab}\mathrm dy^a\mathrm dy^b=\mathrm d\mechanical{\ell}^2+\tilde\sigma^0_{AB}(\mechanical{\ell})\,\mathrm d\theta^A\mathrm d\theta^B.
$$

Because the induced metrics must agree on $S$, we also have $\tilde\sigma^0_{AB}(0)=\sigma_{AB}$. This implies that $\gamma_{ab}=0$ on $S$.

Using this information, show that both expressions for $\mechanical{M}$ reduce to the same form,

$$
\mechanical{M}=-\frac{1}{16\pi}\oint_{S\to\infty}h^{0ab}\gamma_{ab,\ell}\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta.
$$

This is sufficient to prove that the two expressions are indeed equivalent.

## Problem 8

In this problem we study the transport of energy and angular momentum by a scalar field $\signal{\psi}$ in flat spacetime. The metric is $\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2$ and the scalar field satisfies the wave equation $g^{\alpha\beta}\signal{\psi}_{;\alpha\beta}=-4\pi\signal{\rho}$, where $\signal{\rho}$ is a specified source. It can be shown that in the wave zone (where $\mechanical{r}$ is much larger than a typical wavelength of the radiation), the field is given by

$$
\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})
=\frac{1}{\mechanical{r}}\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}
\signal{a_{\ell m}}(\mechanical{u})Y_{\ell m}(\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})+O(\mechanical{r}^{-2}),
$$

where $Y_{\ell m}(\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ are spherical harmonics; the amplitudes $\signal{a_{\ell m}}$ are constructed from $\signal{\rho}$, and they are functions of retarded time $\mechanical{u}\equiv\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{r}$. The scalar field comes with a stress-energy tensor

<!-- Source: PDF page 177, printed page 161. -->

$$
\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}=\signal{\psi}_{,\alpha}\signal{\psi}_{,\beta}
-\frac12(\signal{\psi}^{,\mu}\signal{\psi}_{,\mu})g_{\alpha\beta},
$$

and we are interested in the transfer of energy and angular momentum across a null hypersurface $\Sigma$ defined by $\mechanical{v}=\mathrm{constant}$, where $\mechanical{v}\equiv\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{r}$ is advanced time.

**(a)** Show that $\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha=-\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{k_\alpha}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{u}\,\mathrm d\Omega$, where $\mechanical{k_\alpha}=-\frac12\partial_\alpha\mechanical{v}$ and $\mathrm d\Omega=\sin\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}$, is a surface element on $\Sigma$.

**(b)** Prove that for any test field producing a stress-energy tensor $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}$, the amount of energy crossing $\Sigma$ per unit retarded time is

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\energy{E}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{u}}
=\oint_S\mechanical{r}^2\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{k^\alpha}t^\beta\,\mathrm d\Omega,
$$

where $t^\alpha=\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{t}$ and $S$ is a two-sphere of constant $\mechanical{u}$ and $\mechanical{v}$. Prove also that the amount of angular momentum flowing across $\Sigma$ is given by

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{J}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{u}}
=-\oint_S\mechanical{r}^2\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{k^\alpha}\phi^\beta\,\mathrm d\Omega,
$$

where $\phi^\alpha=\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\phi}$.

**(c)** Show that for scalar radiation, the preceding expressions reduce to

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\energy{E}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{u}}
=\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}|\signal{\dot a_{\ell m}}(\mechanical{u})|^2
$$

and

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{J}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{u}}
=\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}im\,\signal{\dot a_{\ell m}}(\mechanical{u})\signal{a_{\ell m}^*}(\mechanical{u})
$$

in the limit $\mechanical{v}\to\infty$. Here, an overdot indicates differentiation with respect to $\mechanical{u}$ and an asterisk denotes complex conjugation.

**(d)** Suppose that the source producing the scalar radiation is in *rigid rotation* around the $\mechanical{z}$ axis, in the sense that the $\mechanical{t}$ and $\mechanical{\phi}$ dependence of $\signal{\rho}$ resides entirely in the combination $\mechanical{\phi}-\mechanical{\Omega}\mechanical{t}$, where $\mechanical{\Omega}$ is a constant angular velocity. Prove that in this situation the field satisfies

$$
\signal{\psi}_{,\alpha}\xi^\alpha=0,
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 178, printed page 162. -->

where $\xi^\alpha=t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega}\phi^\alpha$. Prove also that in the limit $\mechanical{v}\to\infty$, the transfers of energy and angular momentum are related by

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\energy{E}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{u}}=\mechanical{\Omega}\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{J}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{u}}.
$$

This relation applies to any type of radiation emitted by a source in rigid rotation. It is valid also in curved spacetimes, provided that the spacetime is stationary, axially symmetric, and asymptotically flat.
