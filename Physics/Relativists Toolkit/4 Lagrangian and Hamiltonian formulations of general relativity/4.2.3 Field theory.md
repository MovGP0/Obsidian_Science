> [!TLDR]
> Independent variations of a scalar field and its momentum density give Hamilton's field equations on a spatial foliation.

<!-- Source: PDF page 147, printed page 131. -->

We now return to the Hamiltonian formulation of a field theory. For simplicity we will assume that the field is a scalar, but the procedure can easily be applied to fields of other tensorial types. We begin by defining the ‘time derivative’ of $\signal{q}$ to be its Lie derivative along the flow vector $t^\alpha$,

$$
\signal{\dot q}\equiv\mathcal L_t\signal{q}. \tag{4.39}
$$

In the coordinates $(\mechanical{t},y^a)$, $\mathcal L_t\signal{q}\stackrel{*}{=}\partial\signal{q}/\partial\mechanical{t}$, and $\signal{\dot q}$ reduces to the ordinary time derivative. We also introduce the spatial derivatives, $\signal{q}_{,a}\equiv\signal{q}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_a$. The field’s Lagrangian density can then be expressed as $\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{q},\signal{\dot q},\signal{q}_{,a})$.

The field’s canonical momentum $\mechanical{p}$ is defined by

$$
\mechanical{p}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\signal{\dot q}}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,\energy{\mathcal L}\right). \tag{4.40}
$$

It is assumed that this relation can be inverted to give $\signal{\dot q}$ in terms of $\signal{q}$, $\signal{q}_{,a}$, and $\mechanical{p}$. The *Hamiltonian density* is then

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}(\mechanical{p},\signal{q},\signal{q}_{,a})=\mechanical{p}\signal{\dot q}-\sqrt{-g}\,\energy{\mathcal L}. \tag{4.41}
$$

The presence of $\sqrt{-g}$ in Eqs. (4.40) and (4.41) implies that the Hamiltonian density is not a scalar with respect to transformations $y^a\to y'^a$. We might introduce a scalarized version $\energy{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{scalar}}}$ defined by $\energy{\mathcal H}=\sqrt{h}\,\energy{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{scalar}}}=\sqrt{-g}\,\energy{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{scalar}}}/N$, but such an object would turn out not to be as useful as the original, nonscalar, Hamiltonian density. The *Hamiltonian functional* is defined by

![[relativists-toolkit-fig-4-4-foliated-region.svg|**Fig. 4.4** The region 𝒱, its boundary ∂𝒱, and their foliations.]]

$$
\energy{H}[\mechanical{p},\signal{q}]=\int_{\Sigma_t}\energy{\mathcal H}(\mechanical{p},\signal{q},\signal{q}_{,a})\,\mathrm d^3y. \tag{4.42}
$$

The Hamiltonian functional is an ordinary (nonscalar) function of time $\mechanical{t}$.

We consider a region $\mathcal V$ of spacetime foliated by spacelike hypersurfaces $\Sigma_t$ bounded by closed two-surfaces $S_t$ (Fig. 4.4); $\mathcal V$ itself is bounded by the hypersurfaces $\Sigma_{t_1}$, $\Sigma_{t_2}$, and $\mathcal B$, the union of all two-surfaces $S_t$. To obtain the Hamilton form of the field equations, we will vary the action with respect to $\signal{q}$ and $\mechanical{p}$, treating the variations $\delta\signal{q}$ and $\delta\mechanical{p}$ as independent. We will demand that $\delta\signal{q}$ vanish on the boundaries $\Sigma_{t_1}$, $\Sigma_{t_2}$, and $\mathcal B$.

The action functional is given by

$$
\energy{S}=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}(\mechanical{p}\signal{\dot q}-\energy{\mathcal H})\,\mathrm d^3y,
$$

and variation yields

$$
\delta\energy{S}=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}\left(
\mechanical{p}\delta\signal{\dot q}+\signal{\dot q}\delta\mechanical{p}
-\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\mechanical{p}}\delta\mechanical{p}
-\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\signal{q}}\delta\signal{q}
-\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,a}}\delta\signal{q}_{,a}\right)\mathrm d^3y.
$$

The first term may be integrated by parts:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}\mechanical{p}\delta\signal{\dot q}\,\mathrm d^3y
&=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\mechanical{t}}\int_{\Sigma_t}\mechanical{p}\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm d^3y
-\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}\mechanical{\dot p}\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm d^3y\\
&=\int_{\Sigma_{t_2}}\mechanical{p}\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm d^3y-\int_{\Sigma_{t_1}}\mechanical{p}\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm d^3y
-\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}\mechanical{\dot p}\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm d^3y\\
&=-\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}\mechanical{\dot p}\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm d^3y,
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 149, printed page 133. -->

because $\delta\signal{q}=0$ on $\Sigma_{t_1}$ and $\Sigma_{t_2}$. We treat the last term similarly:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,a}}\delta\signal{q}_{,a}\,\mathrm d^3y
&=-\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{scalar}}}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,a}}\delta\signal{q}_{,a}\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y\\
&=-\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\oint_{S_t}
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{scalar}}}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,a}}\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm dS_a\\
&\quad+\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left(\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H_{\mathrm{scalar}}}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,a}}\right)_{|a}\delta\signal{q}\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y\\
&=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left(\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,a}}\right)_{,a}\delta\signal{q}\,\mathrm d^3y.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the second line we have used the three-dimensional version of Gauss’ theorem, with $\mathrm dS_a$ denoting the surface element on $S_t$. In the third line we have used the divergence formula $A^a{}_{|a}=h^{-1/2}(h^{1/2}A^a)_{,a}$ and the fact that $\delta\signal{q}$ vanishes on $S_t$.

Gathering the results, we have

$$
\delta\energy{S}=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left\{-\left[\mechanical{\dot p}+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\signal{q}}
-\left(\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,a}}\right)_{,a}\right]\delta\signal{q}
+\left[\signal{\dot q}-\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\mechanical{p}}\right]\delta\mechanical{p}\right\}\mathrm d^3y,
$$

and

$$
\delta\energy{S}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mechanical{\dot p}=-\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\signal{q}}
+\left(\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\signal{q}_{,a}}\right)_{,a},\qquad
\signal{\dot q}=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal H}}{\partial\mechanical{p}}. \tag{4.43}
$$

These are Hamilton’s equations for a scalar field $\signal{q}$ and its canonical momentum $\mechanical{p}$.

As a concrete example we consider once again a Klein–Gordon field $\signal{\psi}$ with its Lagrangian density

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac12\left(g^{\mu\nu}\signal{\psi}_{,\mu}\signal{\psi}_{,\nu}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}^2\right).
$$

For simplicity we choose our foliation to be such that $N^a=0$. This implies $g^{tt}=1/g_{tt}$, $g^{ta}=0$, and $g^{ab}=h^{ab}$. Then $\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac12(g^{tt}\signal{\dot\psi}^2+h^{ab}\signal{\psi}_{,a}\signal{\psi}_{,b}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}^2)$, $\mechanical{p}=-\sqrt{-g}\,g^{tt}\signal{\dot\psi}$, and Eq. (4.41) gives

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=-\frac{\mechanical{p}^2}{2\sqrt{-g}\,g^{tt}}+\frac12\sqrt{-g}\left(h^{ab}\signal{\psi}_{,a}\signal{\psi}_{,b}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}^2\right).
$$

The equations of motion are

$$
\signal{\dot\psi}=-\frac{\mechanical{p}}{\sqrt{-g}\,g^{tt}},\qquad
\mechanical{\dot p}=-\sqrt{-g}\,\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}
+\left(\sqrt{-g}\,h^{ab}\signal{\psi}_{,b}\right)_{,a}.
$$

It is easy to check that from these follow the Klein–Gordon equation, $g^{\alpha\beta}\signal{\psi}_{;\alpha\beta}-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}=0$, in the selected foliation.
