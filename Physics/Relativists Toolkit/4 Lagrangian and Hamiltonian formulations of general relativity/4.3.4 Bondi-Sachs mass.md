> [!TLDR]
> Bondi–Sachs mass is defined at null infinity and decreases by the energy carried away in radiation.

<!-- Source: PDF page 167, printed page 151. -->

The ADM mass was constructed in Section 4.3.1 by selecting a closed two-surface $S_t\equiv S(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{r})$, integrating $k-k_0$ over this surface, and then taking the limit $\mechanical{r}\to\infty$. Thus,

$$
\mechanical{M_{\mathrm{ADM}}}(\mechanical{t})
=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{S(t,r\to\infty)}(k-k_0)\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta. \tag{4.91}
$$

Here, $S(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{r})$ denotes a surface of constant $\mechanical{t}$ and $\mechanical{r}$ which becomes a round two-sphere of area $4\pi\mechanical{r}^2$ as $\mechanical{r}\to\infty$. This limit, which is taken while keeping $\mechanical{t}$ fixed, is what defines ‘spatial infinity.’

<!-- Source: PDF page 168, printed page 152. -->

There exists another way of reaching infinity, and to this new limiting procedure corresponds a distinct notion of mass. This is the *Bondi–Sachs* mass, which is obtained by taking $S(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{r})$ to ‘null infinity’ instead of spatial infinity. To define this we introduce the null coordinates $\mechanical{u}=\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{r}$ (retarded time) and $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{r}$ (advanced time). In these coordinates, a two-surface of constant $\mechanical{t}$ and $\mechanical{r}$ becomes a surface of constant $\mechanical{u}$ and $\mechanical{v}$, which we denote $S(\mechanical{u},\mechanical{v})$. Null infinity corresponds to the limit $\mechanical{v}\to\infty$ keeping $\mechanical{u}$ fixed, and the Bondi–Sachs mass is defined by

$$
\mechanical{M_{\mathrm{BS}}}(\mechanical{u})
=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{S(u,v\to\infty)}(k-k_0)\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta. \tag{4.92}
$$

The physical importance of the Bondi–Sachs mass comes from the fact that when an isolated body emits radiation (in the form, say, of electromagnetic or gravitational waves), the rate of change of $\mechanical{M_{\mathrm{BS}}}(\mechanical{u})$ is directly related to the outward flux of radiated energy. If $\energy{F}$ denotes this flux, then the Bondi–Sachs mass satisfies

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{M_{\mathrm{BS}}}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{u}}
=-\oint_{S(u,v\to\infty)}\energy{F}\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta. \tag{4.93}
$$

Thus, the mass of a radiating body decreases as the radiation escapes to infinity. The proof of this statement is rather involved; it can be found in the original papers by Bondi, Sachs, and their collaborators.
