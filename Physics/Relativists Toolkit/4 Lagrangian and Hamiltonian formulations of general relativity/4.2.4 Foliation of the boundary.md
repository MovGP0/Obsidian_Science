> [!TLDR]
> Foliating the timelike boundary defines its normal, induced metric, two-surface curvature, and boundary curvature.

<!-- Source: PDF page 150, printed page 134. -->

Before tackling the case of the gravitational field, we need to provide additional details regarding the foliation of $\mathcal B$, the timelike boundary of $\mathcal V$, by the two-surfaces $S_t$. (Refer back to Fig. 4.4.)

The closed two-surface $S_t$ is the boundary of the spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma_t$, on which we have coordinates $y^a$, tangent vectors $e^\alpha_a$, and an induced metric $h_{ab}$. It is described by an equation of the form $\Phi(y^a)=0$, or by parametric relations $y^a(\theta^A)$, where $\theta^A$ are coordinates on $S_t$. We use $r_a$ to denote the unit normal to $S_t$, and we define an associated four-vector $r^\alpha$ by

$$
r^\alpha=r^ae^\alpha_a. \tag{4.44}
$$

This satisfies the relations $r^\alpha r_\alpha=1$ and $r^\alpha n_\alpha=0$, where $n^\alpha$ is the normal to $\Sigma_t$. The three-vectors $e^a_A\equiv\partial y^a/\partial\theta^A$ are tangent to $S_t$, so that $r_ae^a_A=0$. This implies $r_\alpha e^\alpha_A=0$, where

$$
e^\alpha_A\equiv e^\alpha_ae^a_A=\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\theta^A}. \tag{4.45}
$$

In this equation it is understood that $x^\alpha$ stands for the functions $x^\alpha(y^a(\theta^A))$, where $x^\alpha(y^a)$ are the parametric relations describing $\Sigma_t$.

The induced metric on $S_t$ is given by

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=\sigma_{AB}\mathrm d\theta^A\mathrm d\theta^B, \tag{4.46}
$$

where $\sigma_{AB}=h_{ab}e^a_Ae^b_B=(g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b)e^a_Ae^b_B$ or, using Eq. (4.45),

$$
\sigma_{AB}=g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B. \tag{4.47}
$$

Its inverse is denoted $\sigma^{AB}$. The three-dimensional completeness relations, $h^{ab}=r^ar^b+\sigma^{AB}e^a_Ae^b_B$, are easily established (see Section 3.1.1). It follows that the four-dimensional relations, $g^{\alpha\beta}=-n^\alpha n^\beta+h^{ab}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b$, can be expressed as

$$
g^{\alpha\beta}=-n^\alpha n^\beta+r^\alpha r^\beta+\sigma^{AB}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B. \tag{4.48}
$$

This can be verified by computing all inner products between the vectors $n^\alpha$, $r^\alpha$, and $e^\alpha_A$.

The extrinsic curvature of $S_t$ embedded in $\Sigma_t$ is defined by $k_{AB}=r_{a|b}e^a_Ae^b_B$ (see Section 3.4), or

$$
k_{AB}=r_{\alpha;\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B. \tag{4.49}
$$

We use $k$ to denote its trace: $k=\sigma^{AB}k_{AB}$.

A priori, the coordinates $\theta^A$ on a given two-surface ($S_{t'}$, say) are not related to the coordinates on another two-surface ($S_{t''}$, say). To introduce a relationship we consider a congruence of curves $\beta$ running on $\mathcal B$, intersecting the two-surfaces $S_t$ *orthogonally*, and therefore having $n^\alpha$ as their tangent vectors. We demand that if the curve $\beta_P$ intersects $S_{t'}$ at the point $P'$ labelled by $\theta^A$, then the same coordinates will designate the point $P''$ at which $\beta_P$ intersects $S_{t''}$. Because $\theta^A$ does not vary along these curves, and because $\mechanical{t}$ can be chosen as a parameter on the curves, we have

<!-- Source: PDF page 151, printed page 135. -->

$$
n^\alpha=N^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{t}}\right)_{\theta^A}, \tag{4.50}
$$

where the factor $N^{-1}$ comes from Eq. (4.35) and the normalization condition $n^\alpha n_\alpha=-1$. The construction ensures that $n^\alpha$ and $e^\alpha_A$ are everywhere orthogonal.

The hypersurface $\mathcal B$ is foliated by the two-surfaces $S_t$. We place coordinates $z^i$ on $\mathcal B$, and introduce the tangent vectors $e^\alpha_i=\partial x^\alpha/\partial z^i$. The induced metric on $\mathcal B$ is then given by

$$
\gamma_{ij}=g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ie^\beta_j. \tag{4.51}
$$

Its inverse is $\gamma^{ij}$, and the completeness relations take the form

$$
g^{\alpha\beta}=r^\alpha r^\beta+\gamma^{ij}e^\alpha_ie^\beta_j. \tag{4.52}
$$

While the coordinates $z^i$ are a priori arbitrary, the choice $z^i=(\mechanical{t},\theta^A)$ is clearly convenient. In these coordinates a displacement on $\mathcal B$ is described by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm dx^\alpha&=\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{t}}\right)_{\theta^A}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}
+\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\theta^A}\right)_t\mathrm d\theta^A\\
&=Nn^\alpha\mathrm d\mechanical{t}+e^\alpha_A\mathrm d\theta^A,
\end{aligned}
$$

where Eqs. (4.45) and (4.50) were used. The line element is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_{\mathcal B}^2&=g_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm dx^\alpha\mathrm dx^\beta\\
&=g_{\alpha\beta}(Nn^\alpha\mathrm d\mechanical{t}+e^\alpha_A\mathrm d\theta^A)(Nn^\beta\mathrm d\mechanical{t}+e^\beta_B\mathrm d\theta^B)\\
&=(g_{\alpha\beta}n^\alpha n^\beta)N^2\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+(g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B)\mathrm d\theta^A\mathrm d\theta^B,
\end{aligned}
$$

where the relation $n_\alpha e^\alpha_A=0$ was used. We have obtained

$$
\gamma_{ij}\mathrm dz^i\mathrm dz^j=-N^2\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+\sigma_{AB}\mathrm d\theta^A\mathrm d\theta^B. \tag{4.53}
$$

This implies $\sqrt{-\gamma}=N\sqrt{\sigma}$, where $\gamma$ and $\sigma$ are the determinants of $\gamma_{ij}$ and $\sigma_{AB}$, respectively.

Finally, let $\mathcal K_{ij}$ be the extrinsic curvature of $\mathcal B$ embedded in the four-dimensional spacetime. This is given by

$$
\mathcal K_{ij}=r_{\alpha;\beta}e^\alpha_ie^\beta_j, \tag{4.54}
$$
