> [!TLDR]
> Hamilton's equations evolve the spatial metric and momentum, while the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints restrict initial data.

<!-- Source: PDF page 161, printed page 145. -->

The equations of motion are obtained by varying the gravitational action, expressed as

$$
\energy{S_G}=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}
\left[\int_{\Sigma_t}\mechanical{p^{ab}}\dot h_{ab}\,\mathrm d^3y-\energy{H_G}\right],
$$

with respect to the independent variables $N$, $N^a$, $h_{ab}$, and $\mechanical{p^{ab}}$. Variation yields

$$
\delta\energy{S_G}=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}
\left[\int_{\Sigma_t}(\mechanical{p^{ab}}\delta\dot h_{ab}+\dot h_{ab}\delta\mechanical{p^{ab}})\,\mathrm d^3y-\delta\energy{H_G}\right],
$$

where $\delta\energy{H_G}$ is given by Eq. (4.73). After integrating the first term by parts we obtain

$$
\delta\energy{S_G}=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left[(\dot h_{ab}-\mathcal H_{ab})\delta\mechanical{p^{ab}}
-(\mechanical{\dot p^{ab}}+\mathcal P^{ab})\delta h_{ab}
+\mathcal C\delta N+2\mathcal C_a\delta N^a\right]\mathrm d^3y. \tag{4.74}
$$

Demanding that the action be stationary implies

$$
\dot h_{ab}=\mathcal H_{ab},\qquad
\mechanical{\dot p^{ab}}=-\mathcal P^{ab},\qquad
\mathcal C=0,\qquad \mathcal C_a=0. \tag{4.75}
$$

These are the vacuum Einstein field equations in Hamilton form. The first two govern the evolution of the conjugate variables $h_{ab}$ and $\mechanical{p^{ab}}$; it is easy to check that $\dot h_{ab}=\mathcal H_{ab}$ just reproduces the relation between $\dot h_{ab}$ and $\mechanical{p^{ab}}$ implied by Eqs. (4.57) and (4.62). The last two are the constraints equations first derived in Section 3.6; the relations $\mathcal C=0$ and $\mathcal C_a=0$ are usually referred to as the *Hamiltonian* and *momentum constraints* of general relativity, respectively.

The Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity suggests the following strategy for solving the Einstein field equations. First, select a foliation of spacetime by specifying the lapse $N$ and the shift $N^a$ as functions of $x^\alpha=(\mechanical{t},y^a)$; the choice of foliation is completely arbitrary. Defining $h_{ab}$ to be the induced metric on the spacelike hypersurfaces, the full spacetime metric is given by Eq. (4.37):

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-N^2\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+h_{ab}(\mathrm dy^a+N^a\mathrm d\mechanical{t})(\mathrm dy^b+N^b\mathrm d\mechanical{t}). \tag{4.76}
$$

Next, choose initial values for the tensor fields $h_{ab}$ and $K_{ab}$, where $K_{ab}$ is the extrinsic curvature of the spacelike hypersurfaces. This choice is not entirely arbitrary because the constraint equations must be satisfied: The initial values must be solutions to

$$
{}^3R+K^2-K^{ab}K_{ab}=0,\qquad
(K^{ab}-Kh^{ab})_{|b}=0, \tag{4.77}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 162, printed page 146. -->

where $\,{}^3R$ is the Ricci scalar associated with $h_{ab}$, and $K=h^{ab}K_{ab}$. Finally, evolve these initial values using Hamilton’s equations, $\dot h_{ab}=\mathcal H_{ab}$ and $\mechanical{\dot p^{ab}}=-\mathcal P^{ab}$, which may be written in the form (Section 4.5, Problem 4)

$$
\dot h_{ab}=2NK_{ab}+\mathcal L_N h_{ab} \tag{4.78}
$$

and

$$
\dot K_{ab}=N_{|ab}-N(R_{ab}+KK_{ab}-2K_a{}^cK_{bc})+\mathcal L_N K_{ab}. \tag{4.79}
$$

In these equations, the Lie derivatives are directed along $N^a$, the shift vector. This formulation of the vacuum field equations, usually referred to as their $3+1$ decomposition, is the usual starting point of numerical relativity.
