> [!TLDR]
> The 3 + 1 gravitational action contains a spatial curvature volume term and a two-surface boundary term with a flat reference subtraction.

<!-- Source: PDF page 152, printed page 136. -->

As a first step toward constructing the gravitational Hamiltonian, we must subject the gravitational action $\energy{S_G}$ to the $3+1$ decomposition described in Section 4.2.2. Our starting point is Eq. (4.12),

$$
(16\pi)\energy{S_G}=\int_{\mathcal V}R\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x+2\oint_{\partial\mathcal V}\varepsilon K|h|^{1/2}\,\mathrm d^3y,
$$

where the subtraction term $\energy{S_0}$ is omitted for the time being; it will be re-instated at the end of the calculation. Here, $\partial\mathcal V$ is the closed hypersurface bounding the four-dimensional region $\mathcal V$, $y^a$ are coordinates on $\partial\mathcal V$, $h_{ab}$ is the induced metric, $K$ is the trace of the extrinsic curvature, and $\varepsilon=n^\alpha n_\alpha$, where $n^\alpha$ is the outward normal to $\partial\mathcal V$.

Throughout this section the quantities $n^\alpha$, $y^a$, $h_{ab}$, and $K_{ab}$ have referred specifically to the spacelike hypersurfaces $\Sigma_t$, and we need to be more precise with our notation. We have seen that $\mathcal V$’s boundary is the union of two spacelike hypersurfaces $\Sigma_{t_1}$ and $\Sigma_{t_2}$ with a timelike hypersurface $\mathcal B$ (Fig. 4.4):

$$
\partial\mathcal V=\Sigma_{t_2}\cup(-\Sigma_{t_1})\cup\mathcal B.
$$

The minus sign in front of $\Sigma_{t_1}$ serves to remind us that while the normal to $\partial\mathcal V$ must be directed outward, the normal to $\Sigma_{t_1}$ is future-directed and therefore points inward. With the notation introduced in the preceding subsection, the gravitational action takes the form

<!-- Source: PDF page 153, printed page 137. -->

$$
\begin{aligned}
(16\pi)\energy{S_G}&=\int_{\mathcal V}R\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x-2\int_{\Sigma_{t_2}}K\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y+2\int_{\Sigma_{t_1}}K\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y\\
&\quad+2\int_{\mathcal B}\mathcal K\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\mathrm d^3z,
\end{aligned}
$$

and the integration over $\Sigma_{t_1}$ incorporates the extra minus sign just discussed.

The region $\mathcal V$ is foliated by spacelike hypersurfaces $\Sigma_t$ on which the Ricci scalar is given by (Section 3.5.3)

$$
R={}^{3}R+K^{ab}K_{ab}-K^2-2\left(n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta-n^\alpha n^\beta{}_{;\beta}\right)_{;\alpha},
$$

where $\,{}^3R$ is the Ricci scalar constructed from $h_{ab}$. Using Eq. (4.38), which we write as $\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x=N\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\,\mathrm d^3y$, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathcal V}R\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x
&=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left({}^3R+K^{ab}K_{ab}-K^2\right)N\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y\\
&\quad-2\oint_{\partial\mathcal V}\left(n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta-n^\alpha n^\beta{}_{;\beta}\right)\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$

The new boundary term can be broken down into integrals over $\Sigma_{t_1}$, $\Sigma_{t_2}$, and $\mathcal B$. On $\Sigma_{t_1}$, $\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha=n_\alpha\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y$ — this also incorporates an extra minus sign — and

$$
-2\int_{\Sigma_{t_1}}\left(n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta-n^\alpha n^\beta{}_{;\beta}\right)\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha
=-2\int_{\Sigma_{t_1}}n^\beta{}_{;\beta}\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y
=-2\int_{\Sigma_{t_1}}K\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y.
$$

We see that this term cancels out the other integral over $\Sigma_{t_1}$ coming from the original boundary term in the gravitational action. The integrals over $\Sigma_{t_2}$ cancel out also. There remains a contribution from $\mathcal B$, on which $\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha=r_\alpha\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\mathrm d^3z$, giving

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2\int_{\mathcal B}\left(n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta-n^\alpha n^\beta{}_{;\beta}\right)\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha
&=-2\int_{\mathcal B}n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta r_\alpha\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\mathrm d^3z\\
&=2\int_{\mathcal B}r_{\alpha;\beta}n^\alpha n^\beta\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\mathrm d^3z,
\end{aligned}
$$

where we have used $n^\alpha r_\alpha=0$.

Collecting the results, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
(16\pi)\energy{S_G}
&=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\int_{\Sigma_t}
\left({}^3R+K^{ab}K_{ab}-K^2\right)N\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y\\
&\quad+2\int_{\mathcal B}\left(\mathcal K+r_{\alpha;\beta}n^\alpha n^\beta\right)\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\mathrm d^3z.
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 154, printed page 138. -->

We now use the fact that $\mathcal B$ is foliated by the closed two-surfaces $S_t$. We substitute $\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\mathrm d^3z=N\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\,\mathrm d^2\theta$ and express $\mathcal K$ as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K&=\gamma^{ij}\mathcal K_{ij}\\
&=\gamma^{ij}\left(r_{\alpha;\beta}e^\alpha_ie^\beta_j\right)\\
&=r_{\alpha;\beta}\left(\gamma^{ij}e^\alpha_ie^\beta_j\right)\\
&=r_{\alpha;\beta}(g^{\alpha\beta}-r^\alpha r^\beta),
\end{aligned}
$$

so that the integrand becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K+r_{\alpha;\beta}n^\alpha n^\beta
&=r_{\alpha;\beta}(g^{\alpha\beta}-r^\alpha r^\beta+n^\alpha n^\beta)\\
&=r_{\alpha;\beta}(\sigma^{AB}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B)\\
&=\sigma^{AB}\left(r_{\alpha;\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B\right)\\
&=\sigma^{AB}k_{AB}\\
&=k.
\end{aligned}
$$

We have used Eqs. (4.48) and (4.52) in these manipulations. Substituting this into our previous expression for the gravitational action, we arrive at

$$
\energy{S_G}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}
\left\{\int_{\Sigma_t}\left({}^3R+K^{ab}K_{ab}-K^2\right)N\sqrt{h}\,\mathrm d^3y
+2\oint_{S_t}(k-k_0)N\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta\right\}. \tag{4.55}
$$

We have re-instated the subtraction term, by inserting $k_0$ into the integral over $S_t$. This is justified by the fact that the integral over $\Sigma_t$ vanishes for flat spacetime, so that the sole contribution to $\energy{S_G}$ comes from the boundary integral; the $k_0$ term prevents this integral from diverging in the limit $S_t\to\infty$, and it ensures that $\energy{S_G}$ vanishes identically for flat spacetime. Thus,

$$
k_0=\text{extrinsic curvature of }S_t\text{ embedded in flat space}.
$$

The $k_0$ term makes the gravitational action well defined for any asymptotically-flat spacetime. For compact spacetime manifolds, this term is irrelevant.

The matter action should also be subjected to the $3+1$ decomposition. Because the procedure is straightforward, and because we would do well to keep things as simple as possible, we shall omit this step here. In the remainder of this section we will consider pure gravity only, and set the matter action to zero.
