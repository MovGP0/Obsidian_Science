> [!TLDR]
> Varying the matter action defines the stress-energy tensor and combines with the gravitational variation to give Einstein's equations.

<!-- Source: PDF page 141, printed page 125. -->

Variation of $\energy{S_M}[\signal{\phi};g]$, as given by Eq. (4.11), yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{S_M}&=\int_{\mathcal V}\delta(\energy{\mathcal L}\sqrt{-g})\,\mathrm d^4x\\
&=\int_{\mathcal V}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}\sqrt{-g}+\energy{\mathcal L}\,\delta\sqrt{-g}\right)\mathrm d^4x\\
&=\int_{\mathcal V}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}-\frac12\energy{\mathcal L}g_{\alpha\beta}\right)\delta g^{\alpha\beta}\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x.
\end{aligned}
$$

If we *define* the stress-energy tensor by

$$
\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\equiv-2\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}+\energy{\mathcal L}g_{\alpha\beta}, \tag{4.21}
$$

then

$$
\delta\energy{S_M}=-\frac12\int_{\mathcal V}\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x, \tag{4.22}
$$

and this produces the correct right-hand side to the Einstein field equations.

We have obtained

$$
\delta(\energy{S_G}+\energy{S_M})=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad G_{\alpha\beta}=8\pi\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}, \tag{4.23}
$$

because the variation $\delta g^{\alpha\beta}$ is arbitrary within $\mathcal V$. The Einstein field equations therefore follow from a variational principle, and the action functional for the theory is given by Eq. (4.12).

To see that Eq. (4.21) gives a reasonable definition for the stress-energy tensor, let us consider once more a Klein–Gordon field $\signal{\psi}$ with Lagrangian density

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac12\left(g^{\mu\nu}\signal{\psi}_{,\mu}\signal{\psi}_{,\nu}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}^2\right).
$$

It is easy to check that for this, Eq. (4.21) becomes

$$
\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}=\signal{\psi}_{,\alpha}\signal{\psi}_{,\beta}-\frac12\left(\signal{\psi}^{,\mu}\signal{\psi}_{,\mu}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\psi}^2\right)g_{\alpha\beta}.
$$

This is the correct expression for the Klein–Gordon stress-energy tensor. You may look into the consistency of this result by checking that the statement of energy-momentum conservation, $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}{}_{;\beta}=0$, implies the Klein–Gordon equation.
