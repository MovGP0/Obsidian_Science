> [!TLDR]
> The gravitational action combines Hilbert, boundary, and reference terms with the matter action, while the boundary metric is held fixed.

<!-- Source: PDF page 137, printed page 121. -->

The action functional for general relativity contains a contribution $\energy{S_G}[g]$ from the gravitational field $g_{\alpha\beta}$ and a contribution $\energy{S_M}[\signal{\phi};g]$ from the matter fields, which we collectively denote $\signal{\phi}$.

The gravitational action contains a Hilbert term $\energy{S_H}[g]$, a boundary term $\energy{S_B}[g]$, and a nondynamical term $\energy{S_0}$ that affects the numerical value of the action but not the equations of motion. More explicitly,

$$
\energy{S_G}[g]=\energy{S_H}[g]+\energy{S_B}[g]-\energy{S_0}, \tag{4.7}
$$

where

$$
\energy{S_H}[g]=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\mathcal V}R\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x, \tag{4.8}
$$

$$
\energy{S_B}[g]=\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathcal V}\varepsilon K|h|^{1/2}\,\mathrm d^3y, \tag{4.9}
$$

$$
\energy{S_0}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathcal V}\varepsilon K_0|h|^{1/2}\,\mathrm d^3y. \tag{4.10}
$$

Here, $R$ is the Ricci scalar in $\mathcal V$, $K$ is the trace of the extrinsic curvature of $\partial\mathcal V$, $\varepsilon$ is equal to $+1$ where $\partial\mathcal V$ is timelike and $-1$ where $\partial\mathcal V$ is spacelike (it is assumed that $\partial\mathcal V$ is nowhere null), and $h$ is the determinant of the induced metric on $\partial\mathcal V$. Coordinates $x^\alpha$ are used in $\mathcal V$, and coordinates $y^a$ are used on $\partial\mathcal V$. The role of $\energy{S_B}[g]$ in the variational principle will be elucidated below. The presence of $\energy{S_0}$ in the action will also be explained, and this explanation will come with a precise definition for the quantity $K_0$.

The matter action is taken to be of the form

$$
\energy{S_M}[\signal{\phi};g]=\int_{\mathcal V}\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi},\signal{\phi}_{,\alpha};g_{\alpha\beta})\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x, \tag{4.11}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 138, printed page 122. -->

for some Lagrangian density $\energy{\mathcal L}$. As Eq. (4.11) indicates, it is assumed that only $g_{\alpha\beta}$, and none of its derivatives, appears in the matter action. This assumption is made for simplicity and it could easily be removed.

The complete action functional is therefore

$$
\energy{S}[g;\signal{\phi}]=\int_{\mathcal V}\left(\frac{R}{16\pi}+\energy{\mathcal L}\right)\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x+\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\partial\mathcal V}\varepsilon(K-K_0)|h|^{1/2}\,\mathrm d^3y. \tag{4.12}
$$

The Einstein field equations, $G_{\alpha\beta}=8\pi\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}$, are recovered by varying $\energy{S}[g,\signal{\phi}]$ with respect to $g_{\alpha\beta}$. The variation is subjected to the condition

$$
\left.\delta g_{\alpha\beta}\right|_{\partial\mathcal V}=0. \tag{4.13}
$$

This implies that $h_{ab}=g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b$, the induced metric on $\partial\mathcal V$, is *held fixed* during the variation.
