> [!TLDR]
> Independent variations of coordinates and canonical momenta give Hamilton's equations.

<!-- Source: PDF page 144, printed page 128. -->

The Hamiltonian formulation of Newtonian mechanics begins with the introduction of the canonical momentum $\mechanical{p}$, defined by

$$
\mechanical{p}=\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\mechanical{\dot q}}. \tag{4.28}
$$

It is assumed that this relation can be inverted to give $\mechanical{\dot q}$ as a function of $\mechanical{p}$ and $\mechanical{q}$. The *Hamiltonian* is then

$$
\energy{H}(\mechanical{p},\mechanical{q})=\mechanical{p}\mechanical{\dot q}-\energy{L}. \tag{4.29}
$$

Hamilton’s form of the equations of motion can be derived from a variational principle. Here, the action is varied with respect to $\mechanical{p}$ and $\mechanical{q}$ *independently*, with the restriction that $\delta\mechanical{q}$ must vanish at the endpoints. Thus,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{S}&=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\delta(\mechanical{p}\mechanical{\dot q}-\energy{H})\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\\
&=\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\left(\mechanical{p}\delta\mechanical{\dot q}+\mechanical{\dot q}\delta\mechanical{p}
-\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\mechanical{p}}\delta\mechanical{p}
-\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\mechanical{q}}\delta\mechanical{q}\right)\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\\
&=\left.\mechanical{p}\delta\mechanical{q}\right|_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}
+\int_{\mechanical{t_1}}^{\mechanical{t_2}}\left[-\left(\mechanical{\dot p}+\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\mechanical{q}}\right)\delta\mechanical{q}
+\left(\mechanical{\dot q}-\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\mechanical{p}}\right)\delta\mechanical{p}\right]\mathrm d\mechanical{t}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Because the variations are arbitrary between $\mechanical{t_1}$ and $\mechanical{t_2}$, but $\delta\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t_1})=\delta\mechanical{q}(\mechanical{t_2})=0$, we have

$$
\delta\energy{S}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mechanical{\dot p}=-\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\mechanical{q}},\qquad
\mechanical{\dot q}=\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial\mechanical{p}}. \tag{4.30}
$$

These are *Hamilton’s equations*. They are equivalent to the Euler–Lagrange equation (4.3).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | — | Canonical momentum | The momentum conjugate to the generalized coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{q},\mechanical{\dot q}$ | — | Coordinate and velocity | The generalized coordinate and its time derivative. |
| $\energy{L},\energy{H}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Lagrangian and Hamiltonian | The mechanical energy functions in geometrized units. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^2}$ | Action | The time integral varied to obtain Hamilton's equations. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{t_1},\mechanical{t_2}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Times | The time coordinate and fixed endpoints. |
