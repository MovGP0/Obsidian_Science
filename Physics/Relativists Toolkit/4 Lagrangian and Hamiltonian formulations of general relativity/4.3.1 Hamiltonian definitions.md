> [!TLDR]
> Asymptotic time translations define ADM mass, and asymptotic rotations define angular momentum through the gravitational boundary Hamiltonian.

<!-- Source: PDF page 162, printed page 146. -->

It is natural to expect that the gravitational mass of an asymptotically-flat spacetime — its total energy — should be related to the value of the gravitational Hamiltonian for this spacetime. We will explore this relation in this section, and motivate another between the Hamiltonian and the spacetime’s total angular momentum.

The solution-valued Hamiltonian, $\energy{H_G^{\mathrm{solution}}}$ given by Eq. (4.80), depends on the asymptotic behaviour of the spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma_t$, and on the asymptotic behaviour of the lapse and shift. While the lapse and shift are always arbitrary, the fact that the spacetime is asymptotically flat gives us a preferred behaviour for the hypersurfaces. We shall demand that $\Sigma_t$ asymptotically coincide with a surface of constant time in Minkowski spacetime: If $(\mechanical{\bar t},\mechanical{\bar x},\mechanical{\bar y},\mechanical{\bar z})$ is a Lorentzian frame at infinity, then the asymptotic portion of $\Sigma_t$ must coincide with a surface $\mechanical{\bar t}=\mathrm{constant}$.

<!-- Source: PDF page 163, printed page 147. -->

In this portion of $\Sigma_t$, the (arbitrary) coordinates $y^a$ are related to the spatial Minkowski coordinates, and we have the asymptotic relations $y^a\to y^a(\mechanical{\bar x},\mechanical{\bar y},\mechanical{\bar z})$; similarly, $x^\alpha\to x^\alpha(\mechanical{\bar t},\mechanical{\bar x},\mechanical{\bar y},\mechanical{\bar z})$. We note that $\mechanical{\bar t}$ is proper time for an observer at rest in the asymptotic region, and infer that this observer moves with a four-velocity $\mechanical{u^\alpha}=\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\bar t}$. Because this vector is normalized and orthogonal to the surfaces $\mechanical{\bar t}=\mathrm{constant}$, it must coincide with the normal vector $n^\alpha$, and we have another asymptotic relation, $n^\alpha\to\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\bar t}$. Substituting this into Eq. (4.36) gives us

$$
t^\alpha\to N\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{\bar t}}\right)_{y^a}
+N^a\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial y^a}\right)_{\bar t},
$$

an asymptotic relation for the flow vector. This shows that once the asymptotic behaviour of $\Sigma_t$ has been specified, there is a one-to-one correspondence between a choice of lapse and shift and a choice of flow vector. The solution-valued Hamiltonian can then be regarded either as a functional of $N$ and $N^a$, or as a functional of $t^\alpha$.

We shall define $\mechanical{M}$, the gravitational mass of an asymptotically-flat spacetime, to be the limit of $\energy{H_G^{\mathrm{solution}}}$ when $S_t$ is a two-sphere at spatial infinity, evaluated with the following choice of lapse and shift: $N=1$ and $N^a=0$. From Eq. (4.80),

$$
\mechanical{M}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\lim_{S_t\to\infty}\oint_{S_t}(k-k_0)\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta. \tag{4.81}
$$

Here, $\sigma_{AB}$ is the metric on $S_t$, $k=\sigma^{AB}k_{AB}$ is the extrinsic curvature of $S_t$ embedded in $\Sigma_t$, and $k_0$ is the extrinsic curvature of $S_t$ embedded in flat space. The quantity defined by Eq. (4.81) is called the *ADM mass* of the asymptotically-flat spacetime; the name refers to the seminal work by Arnowitt, Deser, and Misner.

The choice $N=1$, $N^a=0$ implies that asymptotically, $t^\alpha\to\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\bar t}$, so that the flow vector generates an *asymptotic time translation*. The ADM mass is then just the gravitational Hamiltonian for this choice of flow vector, and we have made a formal connection between total energy and time translations. This connection is both deep and compelling, and it can be adapted to give a definition of total angular momentum. Indeed, the gravitational Hamiltonian should provide a similar connection between angular momentum and *asymptotic rotations*, which are characterized by $t^\alpha\to\phi^\alpha\equiv\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\phi}$, where $\mechanical{\phi}$ is a rotation angle defined in the asymptotic region in terms of the Cartesian frame $(\mechanical{\bar x},\mechanical{\bar y},\mechanical{\bar z})$. This corresponds to the choice $N=0$, $N^a=\phi^a\equiv\partial y^a/\partial\mechanical{\phi}$ of lapse and shift.

We shall define $\mechanical{J}$, the angular momentum of an asymptotically-flat spacetime, to be (minus) the limit of $\energy{H_G^{\mathrm{solution}}}$ when $S_t$ is a two-sphere at spatial infinity, evaluated with $N=0$ and $N^a=\phi^a$. From Eq. (4.80),

<!-- Source: PDF page 164, printed page 148. -->

$$
\mechanical{J}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\lim_{S_t\to\infty}\oint_{S_t}(K_{ab}-Kh_{ab})\phi^ar^b\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta. \tag{4.82}
$$

The minus sign was inserted to recover the usual right-hand rule for the angular momentum. Notice that this definition of angular momentum refers to a specific choice of rotation axis, and $\mechanical{\phi}$ is the angle around this axis.
