## Problem 1

We consider a hypersurface $\mechanical{T}=\mathrm{constant}$ in Schwarzschild spacetime, where

$$
\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{t}+4\mechanical{M}\left[\sqrt{\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}}+\frac12\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}}-1}{\sqrt{\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}}+1}\right)\right].
$$

We use $(\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ as coordinates on the hypersurface.

**(a)** Calculate the unit normal $n_\alpha$ and find parametric equations that describe the hypersurface.

**(b)** Calculate the induced metric $h_{ab}$.

**(c)** Calculate the extrinsic curvature $K_{ab}$. Verify that your results agree with those of Section 3.6.6, and show that $K$ is equal to the expansion of the congruence considered in Section 2.3.7.

<!-- Source: PDF page 131, printed page 115. -->

**(d)** Prove that when it is expressed in terms of the coordinates $(\mechanical{T},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$, the Schwarzschild metric takes the form

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\mathrm d\mechanical{T}^2+\left(\mathrm d\mechanical{r}+\sqrt{2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{T}\right)^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2.
$$

This shows very clearly that the sections $\mechanical{T}=\mathrm{constant}$ are intrinsically flat. [This coordinate system was discovered independently by Painlevé (1921) and Gullstrand (1922). It is presented in some detail in a 2001 paper by Martel and Poisson.]

## Problem 2

A four-dimensional hypersurface is embedded in a flat, five-dimensional spacetime. We use coordinates $z^A$ in the five-dimensional world, and express the metric as

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=\eta_{AB}\mathrm dz^A\mathrm dz^B=-(\mathrm dz^0)^2+(\mathrm dz^1)^2+(\mathrm dz^2)^2+(\mathrm dz^3)^2+(\mathrm dz^4)^2;
$$

we let uppercase Latin indices run from 0 to 4. In the four-dimensional world we use coordinates $x^\alpha=(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{\chi},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$. The hypersurface is defined by parametric relations $z^A(x^\alpha)$. Explicitly,

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^0&=\mechanical{a}\sinh(\mechanical{t}/\mechanical{a}),\quad
z^1=\mechanical{a}\cosh(\mechanical{t}/\mechanical{a})\cos\mechanical{\chi},\quad
z^2=\mechanical{a}\cosh(\mechanical{t}/\mechanical{a})\sin\mechanical{\chi}\cos\mechanical{\theta},\\
z^3&=\mechanical{a}\cosh(\mechanical{t}/\mechanical{a})\sin\mechanical{\chi}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi},\quad
z^4=\mechanical{a}\cosh(\mechanical{t}/\mechanical{a})\sin\mechanical{\chi}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mechanical{a}$ is a constant.

**(a)** Compute the unit normal $n^A$ and the tangent vectors $e^A_\alpha=\partial z^A/\partial x^\alpha$ to the hypersurface.

**(b)** Compute the induced metric $g_{\alpha\beta}$. What is the physical significance of this four-dimensional metric? Does it satisfy the Einstein field equations?

**(c)** Compute the extrinsic curvature $K_{\alpha\beta}$. Use the Gauss–Codazzi equations to prove that the induced Riemann tensor can be expressed as

$$
R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\frac{1}{\mechanical{a}^2}\left(g_{\alpha\gamma}g_{\beta\delta}-g_{\alpha\delta}g_{\beta\gamma}\right).
$$

This implies that the four-dimensional hypersurface is a spacetime of constant Ricci curvature.

## Problem 3

In this problem we consider a spherically symmetric space at a moment of time symmetry. We write the three-metric as

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=\mathrm d\mechanical{\ell}^2+\mechanical{r}^2(\mechanical{\ell})\mathrm d\Omega^2,
$$

where $\mechanical{\ell}$ is proper distance from the centre.

**(a)** Show that in these coordinates, the mass function introduced in Section 3.6.5 is given by

$$
\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r})=\frac{\mechanical{r}}2\left[1-(\mathrm d\mechanical{r}/\mathrm d\mechanical{\ell})^2\right].
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 132, printed page 116. -->

**(b)** Solve the constraint equations for a uniform mass density $\energy{\rho}$ on the hypersurface. Make sure to impose the asymptotic condition $\mechanical{r}(\mechanical{\ell}\to0)\to\mechanical{\ell}$ to force the three-metric to be regular at the centre.

**(c)** Prove that $\mechanical{r}(\mechanical{\ell})$ can be no larger than $\mechanical{r_{\max}}=\sqrt{3/(8\pi\energy{\rho})}$.

**(d)** Prove that $2\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r_{\max}})=\mechanical{r_{\max}}$, and that $\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r_{\max}})$ is the maximum value of the mass function.

**(e)** What happens when $\mechanical{\ell}\to\pi\mechanical{r_{\max}}$?

## Problem 5

Prove that the surface stress-energy tensor of a thin shell satisfies the conservation equation

$$
\energy{S^{ab}}{}_{|b}=-\varepsilon[\mechanical{j^a}],
$$

where $\mechanical{j_a}\equiv\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}e^\alpha_an^\beta$. Interpret this equation physically. (Consider the case where the shell is timelike.)

## Problem 6

The metric

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{\ell}^2+\mechanical{r}^2(\mechanical{\ell})\mathrm d\Omega^2,
$$

where $\mechanical{r}(\mechanical{\ell})=\mechanical{\ell}$ when $0<\mechanical{\ell}<\mechanical{\ell_0}$ and $\mechanical{r}(\mechanical{\ell})=2\mechanical{\ell_0}-\mechanical{\ell}$ when $\mechanical{\ell_0}<\mechanical{\ell}<2\mechanical{\ell_0}$, describes a spacetime with closed spatial sections. (What is the volume of a hypersurface $\mechanical{t}=\mathrm{constant}$?) The spacetime is flat in both $\mathcal V^-$ ($\mechanical{\ell}<\mechanical{\ell_0}$) and $\mathcal V^+$ ($\mechanical{\ell}>\mechanical{\ell_0}$), but it contains a surface layer at $\mechanical{\ell}=\mechanical{\ell_0}$.

**(a)** Calculate the surface stress-energy tensor of the thin shell. Express this in terms of a velocity field $\mechanical{u^a}$, a density $\energy{\sigma}$, and a surface pressure $\mechanical{p}$.

**(b)** Consider a congruence of outgoing null geodesics in this spacetime, with its tangent vector $\mechanical{k_\alpha}=-\partial_\alpha(\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{\ell})$. Calculate $\mechanical{\theta}$, the expansion of this congruence. Show that it abruptly changes sign (from positive to negative) at $\mechanical{\ell}=\mechanical{\ell_0}$. The surface layer therefore produces a strong focusing of the null geodesics.

**(c)** Use Raychaudhuri’s equation to prove that the discontinuity in $\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}/\mathrm d\mechanical{\ell}$ is precisely accounted for by the surface stress-energy tensor.

## Problem 7

Two Schwarzschild solutions, one with mass parameter $\mechanical{m_-}$, the other with mass parameter $\mechanical{m_+}$, are joined at a radius $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{R}(\mechanical{\tau})$ by means of a spherical thin shell; $\mechanical{\tau}$ denotes proper time for an observer comoving with the shell. It is assumed that $\mechanical{m_-}$ is the interior mass ($\mechanical{m_+}$ is the exterior mass), that $\mechanical{m_+}>\mechanical{m_-}$, and that $\mechanical{R}(\mechanical{\tau})>2\mechanical{m_+}$ for all values of $\mechanical{\tau}$. The shell’s surface stress-energy tensor is given by

$$
\energy{S^{ab}}=(\energy{\sigma}+\mechanical{p})\mechanical{u^a}\mechanical{u^b}+\mechanical{p}h^{ab},
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 133, printed page 117. -->

where $\mechanical{u^a}$ is the fluid’s velocity field, $\energy{\sigma}(\mechanical{\tau})$ the surface density, $\mechanical{p}(\mechanical{\tau})$ the surface pressure, and $h_{ab}$ the induced metric.

**(a)** Derive, and interpret physically, the equation

$$
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau}}(\energy{\sigma}\mechanical{R}^2)+\mechanical{p}\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau}}(\mechanical{R}^2)=0.
$$

**(b)** Find the values of $\energy{\sigma}$ and $\mechanical{p}$ which produce a static configuration: $\mechanical{R}(\mechanical{\tau})=\mechanical{R_0}=\mathrm{constant}$. Verify that both $\energy{\sigma}$ and $\mechanical{p}$ are positive. [The stability of these static configurations was examined by Brady, Louko, and Poisson (1991).]

## Problem 9

Let spacetime be partitioned into two regions $\mathcal V^\pm$ with metrics

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_\pm^2=-f_\pm\mathrm d\mechanical{v}^2+2\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2.
$$

We assume that the coordinate system $(\mechanical{v},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ is common to both $\mathcal V^-$ and $\mathcal V^+$. (In each region we could introduce a conventional time coordinate $\mechanical{t_\pm}$ defined by $\mathrm d\mechanical{t_\pm}=\mathrm d\mechanical{v}-\mathrm d\mechanical{r}/f_\pm$, but it is much more convenient to work with the original system.) In $\mathcal V^-$ we set $f_-=1-\mechanical{r_0}/\mechanical{r}$, so that the metric is a Schwarzschild solution with mass parameter $\mechanical{M}\equiv\frac12\mechanical{r_0}$. In $\mathcal V^+$ we set $f_+=1-(\mechanical{r}/\mechanical{r_0})^2$, so that the metric is a de Sitter solution with cosmological constant $\Lambda\equiv3/\mechanical{r_0}^2$. (This metric is a solution to the modified Einstein field equations, $G_{\alpha\beta}+\Lambda g_{\alpha\beta}=0$.) The boundary $\Sigma$ between the two regions is the null surface $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_0}$, the common horizon of the Schwarzschild and de Sitter spacetimes.

Using $y^a=(\mechanical{v},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ as coordinates on $\Sigma$, calculate the surface quantities $\energy{\mu}$, $\mechanical{j^A}$, and $\mechanical{p}$ associated with the null shell. Explain whether your results are compatible with the general relation $\mechanical{p}\mechanical{\theta}=[\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{k^\alpha}\mechanical{k^\beta}]$ derived in Section 3.11.5.
