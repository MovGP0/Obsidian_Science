> [!TLDR]
> Empty flat spherical initial data admit nonzero extrinsic curvature whose trace varies as the inverse three-halves power of radius.

<!-- Source: PDF page 98, printed page 82. -->

We now solve the constraint equations for a spherically symmetric space empty of matter (so that $\energy{\rho}=0=\mechanical{j^a}$). We assume that we can endow this space with a flat metric, so that

<!-- Source: PDF page 99, printed page 83. -->

$$
h_{ab}\mathrm dy^a\mathrm dy^b=\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2.
$$

We also assume that the hypersurface does not represent a moment of time symmetry. While the flat metric and $K_{ab}=0$ make a valid solution to the constraints, this is a trivial configuration — a flat hypersurface in a flat spacetime.

Let $n_a=\partial_a\mechanical{r}$ be a unit vector that points radially outward on the hypersurface. The fact that $K_{ab}$ is a spherically symmetric tensor means that it can be decomposed as

$$
K_{ab}=K_1(\mechanical{r})n_an_b+K_2(\mechanical{r})(h_{ab}-n_an_b),
$$

with $K_1$ representing the radial component of the extrinsic curvature, and $K_2$ the angular components. In the usual spherical coordinates $(\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ we have $K^a{}_b=\operatorname{diag}(K_1,K_2,K_2)$, which is the most general expression admissible under the assumption of spherical symmetry.

Because the space is empty and flat, the first constraint equation reduces to $K^2-K^{ab}K_{ab}=0$, an algebraic equation for $K_1$ and $K_2$. This gives us the condition $(2K_1+K_2)K_2=0$. Choosing $K_2=0$ would eventually return the trivial solution $K_{ab}=0$. We choose instead $K_2=-2K_1$ and re-express the extrinsic curvature as

$$
K_{ab}=K(\mechanical{r})\left(\frac23h_{ab}-n_an_b\right),
$$

where $K=-3K_1$ is the sole remaining function to be determined.

To find $K(\mechanical{r})$ we turn to the second constraint equation, $K^b{}_{a|b}-K_{,a}=0$, which becomes

$$
\frac13K_{,a}+\left(Kn^b{}_{|b}+K_{,b}n^b\right)n_a+Kn_{a|b}n^b=0.
$$

With $K_{,a}=K'n_a$ (with a prime denoting differentiation with respect to $\mechanical{r}$), $n^b{}_{|b}=2/\mechanical{r}$, and $n_{a|b}n^b=0$ (because the radial curves are geodesics of the hypersurface), we arrive at $2\mechanical{r}K'+3K=0$. Integration yields

$$
K(\mechanical{r})=K_0(\mechanical{r_0}/\mechanical{r})^{3/2},
$$

with $K_0$ denoting the value of $K$ at the arbitrary radius $\mechanical{r_0}$.

We have found a nontrivial solution to the constraint equations for a spherical space that is both empty and flat. The physical meaning of this configuration will be revealed in Section 3.13, Problem 1.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $h_{ab}$ | — | Induced metric | The flat metric on the hypersurface. |
| $K_{ab}$ | — | Extrinsic curvature | The spherical curvature tensor of the hypersurface in spacetime. |
| $K,K_0,K_1,K_2$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-1}}$ | Curvature rates | The trace, reference trace, and radial and angular eigenvalues. |
| $\mechanical{r},\mechanical{r_0}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Radius | The areal radius and its reference value. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{rad}}$ | Angles | The spherical angular coordinates. |
| $n_a$ | — | Radial unit vector | The outward unit direction within the hypersurface. |
| $\energy{\rho}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-2}}$ | Energy density | The density, which vanishes in the empty space. |
| $\mechanical{j^a}$ | — | Momentum density | The momentum density, which also vanishes. |
| $\mechanical{s}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Interval | The line element in spacetime or on the hypersurface. |
