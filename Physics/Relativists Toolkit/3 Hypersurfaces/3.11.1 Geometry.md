> [!TLDR]
> Null-shell geometry uses generator and transverse tangent vectors, a continuous two-metric, and an arbitrary family of timelike observers.

<!-- Source: PDF page 114, printed page 98. -->

As we did in Section 3.7, we consider a hypersurface $\Sigma$ that partitions spacetime into two regions $\mathcal V^\pm$ in which the metric is $g^\pm_{\alpha\beta}$ when expressed in coordinates $x^\alpha_\pm$. Here we assume that the hypersurface is null, and our convention is such that $\mathcal V^-$ is in the past of $\Sigma$, and $\mathcal V^+$ in its future. We assume also that the hypersurface is singular, in the sense that the Riemann tensor possesses a $\delta$-function singularity at $\Sigma$. We will characterize the Ricci part of this singular curvature tensor, and relate it to the surface stress-energy tensor of the shell. (We shall have nothing to say about the interesting physical effects associated with the Weyl part of the singular curvature tensor.)

As we did in Section 3.1.2, we install coordinates

$$
y^a=(\mechanical{\lambda},\theta^A)
$$

on the hypersurface, and we assume that these coordinates are the same on both sides of $\Sigma$. We take $\mechanical{\lambda}$ to be an arbitrary parameter on the null generators of the hypersurface, and we use $\theta^A$ to label the generators. It is possible to choose $\mechanical{\lambda}$ to be an affine parameter on *one* side of the hypersurface. But as we shall see below, in general it is not possible to make $\mechanical{\lambda}$ an affine parameter on both sides of $\Sigma$.

As seen from $\mathcal V^\pm$, $\Sigma$ is described by the parametric relations $x^\alpha_\pm(y^a)$, and using these we can introduce tangent vectors $e^\alpha_{\pm a}=\partial x^\alpha_\pm/\partial y^a$ on each side of the hypersurface. These are naturally segregated into a null vector $\mechanical{k}^\alpha_\pm$ that is tangent to the generators, and two spacelike vectors $e^\alpha_{\pm A}$ that point in the directions transverse to the generators. Explicitly,

$$
\mechanical{k}^\alpha=\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{\lambda}}\right)_{\theta^A}=e^\alpha_\mechanical{\lambda},\qquad
e^\alpha_A=\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\theta^A}\right)_\mechanical{\lambda}. \tag{3.84}
$$

(Here and below, in order to keep the notation simple, we refrain from using the ‘$\pm$’ label in displayed equations; this should not create any confusion.) By construction, these vectors satisfy

$$
\mechanical{k}_\alpha \mechanical{k}^\alpha=0=\mechanical{k}_\alpha e^\alpha_A. \tag{3.85}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 115, printed page 99. -->

The remaining inner products

$$
\sigma_{AB}(\mechanical{\lambda},\theta^C)=g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B \tag{3.86}
$$

do not vanish, and we assume that they are the same on both sides of $\Sigma$:

$$
[\sigma_{AB}]=0. \tag{3.87}
$$

We recall from Section 3.1.3 that the two-tensor $\sigma_{AB}$ acts as a metric on $\Sigma$,

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_\Sigma^2=\sigma_{AB}\mathrm d\theta^A\mathrm d\theta^B,
$$

and the condition (3.87) ensures that the hypersurface possesses a well-defined intrinsic geometry.

As we did in Section 3.1.3, we complete the basis by adding an auxiliary null vector $N^\alpha_\pm$ that satisfies

$$
N_\alpha N^\alpha=0,\qquad N_\alpha \mechanical{k}^\alpha=-1,\qquad N_\alpha e^\alpha_A=0. \tag{3.88}
$$

The completed basis gives us the completeness relations

$$
g^{\alpha\beta}=-\mechanical{k}^\alpha N^\beta-N^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta+\sigma^{AB}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B \tag{3.89}
$$

for the inverse metric on either side of $\Sigma$ (in the coordinates $x^\alpha_\pm$); $\sigma^{AB}$ is the inverse of $\sigma_{AB}$, and it is the same on both sides.

To complete the geometric setup we introduce a congruence of geodesics that cross the hypersurface. In Section 3.7, in which $\Sigma$ was either timelike or spacelike, the congruence was selected by demanding that the geodesics intersect the hypersurface *orthogonally*: The vector field $\mechanical{u}^\alpha_\pm$ tangent to the congruence was set equal (on $\Sigma$) to the normal vector $n^\alpha_\pm$. When the hypersurface is null, however, this requirement does not produce a unique congruence, because a vector orthogonal to $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ can still possess an arbitrary component along $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$.

We shall have to give up on the idea of adopting a *unique* congruence. An important aspect of our description of null shells is therefore that it involves an *arbitrary* congruence of *timelike* geodesics intersecting $\Sigma$. This arbitrariness comes with the lightlike nature of the singular hypersurface, and it cannot be removed. It can, however, be physically motivated: The arbitrary vector field $\mechanical{u}^\alpha_\pm$ that enters our description of null shells can be identified with the four-velocity of a family of observers making measurements on the shell; since many different families of observers could be introduced to make such measurements, there is no reason to demand that the vector field be uniquely specified.

We therefore introduce a congruence of timelike geodesics $\gamma$ that arbitrarily intersect the hypersurface. The geodesics are parameterized by proper time $\mechanical{\tau}$, which is adjusted so that $\mechanical{\tau}=0$ when a geodesic crosses $\Sigma$; thus, $\mechanical{\tau}<0$ in $\mathcal V^-$ and $\mechanical{\tau}>0$ in $\mathcal V^+$. The vector tangent to the geodesics is $\mechanical{u}^\alpha_\pm$, and a displacement along a member of the congruence is described by

<!-- Source: PDF page 116, printed page 100. -->

$$
\mathrm dx^\alpha=\mechanical{u}^\alpha\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau}. \tag{3.90}
$$

To ensure that the congruence is smooth at the hypersurface, we demand that $\mechanical{u}^\alpha_\pm$ be ‘the same’ on both sides of $\Sigma$. This means that $\mechanical{u}_\alpha e^\alpha_a$, the tangential projections of the vector field, must be equal when evaluated on either side of the hypersurface:

$$
[-\mechanical{u}_\alpha \mechanical{k}^\alpha]=0=[\mechanical{u}_\alpha e^\alpha_A]. \tag{3.91}
$$

If, for example, $\mechanical{u}^\alpha_-$ is specified in $\mathcal V^-$, then the three conditions (3.91) are sufficient (together with the geodesic equation) to determine the three independent components of $\mechanical{u}^\alpha_+$ in $\mathcal V^+$. We note that $-\mechanical{u}_\alpha N^\alpha$, the transverse projection of the four-velocity, is allowed to be discontinuous at $\Sigma$.

The proper-time parameter on the timelike geodesics can be viewed as a scalar field $\mechanical{\tau}(x^\alpha_\pm)$ defined in a neighbourhood of $\Sigma$: Select a point $x^\alpha_\pm$ off the hypersurface and locate the unique geodesic $\gamma$ that connects this point to $\Sigma$; the value of the scalar field at $x^\alpha_\pm$ is equal to the proper-time parameter of this geodesic at that point. The hypersurface $\Sigma$ can then be described by the statement

$$
\mechanical{\tau}(x^\alpha_\pm)=0,
$$

and its normal vector $\mechanical{k}^\pm_\alpha$ will be proportional to the gradient of $\mechanical{\tau}(x^\alpha_\pm)$ evaluated at $\Sigma$. It is easy to check that the expression

$$
\mechanical{k}_\alpha=-(-\mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu)\frac{\partial\mechanical{\tau}}{\partial x^\alpha} \tag{3.92}
$$

is compatible with Eq. (3.90). We recall that the factor $-\mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu$ in Eq. (3.92) is continuous across $\Sigma$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $g^\pm_{\alpha\beta},g^{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Spacetime metrics | The metrics on both sides and their inverse. |
| $\sigma_{AB},\sigma^{AB}$ | — | Induced metric | The continuous two-metric and its inverse. |
| $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ | — | Null tangent | The generator tangent and hypersurface normal. |
| $\mechanical{\lambda}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Generator parameter | The chosen parameter in geometrized units. |
| $x^\alpha_\pm,y^a,\theta^A$ | — | Coordinates | The spacetime and intrinsic hypersurface coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{s}_\Sigma$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Surface interval | The interval within the null hypersurface. |
| $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$ | — | Observer four-velocity | The velocity of the chosen timelike observers. |
| $\mechanical{\tau}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Proper time | The signed observer time measured from the shell crossing. |
| $n^\alpha$ | — | Unit normal | The normal used for the earlier non-null construction. |
