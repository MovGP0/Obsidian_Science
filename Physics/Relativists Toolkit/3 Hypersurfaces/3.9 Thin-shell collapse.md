> [!TLDR]
> A pressureless thin shell conserves its rest mass and total gravitational energy, including its binding energy.

<!-- Source: PDF page 109, printed page 93. -->

As an application of the thin-shell formalism, we consider the gravitational collapse of a thin spherical shell. We assume that spacetime is flat inside the shell (in $\mathcal V^-$). Outside (in $\mathcal V^+$), the metric is necessarily a Schwarzschild solution (by virtue of the spherical symmetry of the matter distribution). We assume also that the shell is made of pressureless matter, in the sense that its surface stress-energy tensor is constrained to have the form

$$
\energy{S^{ab}}=\energy{\sigma}\mechanical{u^a}\mechanical{u^b}, \tag{3.68}
$$

in which $\energy{\sigma}$ is the surface density and $\mechanical{u^a}$ the shell’s velocity field. Our goal is to derive the shell’s equations of motion under the stated conditions.

Using the results derived in the preceding section, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
K^\tau_{\pm\tau}&=\dot\beta_\pm/\mechanical{\dot R},\\
K^\theta_{\pm\theta}&=K^\phi_{\pm\phi}=\beta_\pm/\mechanical{R},\\
\beta_+&=\sqrt{\mechanical{\dot R}^2+1-2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{R}},\\
\beta_-&=\sqrt{\mechanical{\dot R}^2+1},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mechanical{R}(\mechanical{\tau})$ is the shell’s radius, and $\mechanical{M}$ its gravitational mass. As we did before, we use $(\mechanical{\tau},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ as coordinates on $\Sigma$; in these coordinates $\mechanical{u^a}=\partial y^a/\partial\mechanical{\tau}$. Equation (3.56) allows us to calculate the components of the surface stress-energy tensor, and we find

$$
-\energy{\sigma}=\energy{S^\tau{}_\tau}=\frac{\beta_+-\beta_-}{4\pi\mechanical{R}},\qquad
0=\energy{S^\theta{}_\theta}=\frac{\beta_+-\beta_-}{8\pi\mechanical{R}}+\frac{\dot\beta_+-\dot\beta_-}{8\pi\mechanical{\dot R}}.
$$

The second equation can be integrated immediately, giving $(\beta_+-\beta_-)\mechanical{R}=\mathrm{constant}$. Substituting this into the first equation yields $4\pi\mechanical{R}^2\energy{\sigma}=-\mathrm{constant}$. We have obtained

$$
4\pi\mechanical{R}^2\energy{\sigma}\equiv\mechanical{m}=\mathrm{constant} \tag{3.69}
$$

and $\beta_--\beta_+=\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{R}$. The first equation states that $\mechanical{m}$, the shell’s *rest mass*, stays constant during the evolution. Squaring the second equation converts it to

$$
\mechanical{M}=\mechanical{m}\sqrt{1+\mechanical{\dot R}^2}-\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2\mechanical{R}}, \tag{3.70}
$$

which comes with a nice physical interpretation. The first term on the right-hand side is the shell’s relativistic kinetic energy, including rest mass. The second term is the shell’s binding energy, the work required to assemble the shell from its dispersed constituents. The sum of these is the total (conserved) energy, and this is equal to the shell’s gravitational mass $\mechanical{M}$. Equation (3.70) provides a vivid illustration of the general statement that *all forms* of energy contribute to the total gravitational mass of an isolated body.

<!-- Source: PDF page 110, printed page 94. -->

Equations (3.69) and (3.70) are the shell’s equations of motion. It is interesting to note that when $\mechanical{M}<\mechanical{m}$, the motion exhibits a turning point at $\mechanical{R}=\mechanical{R_{\max}}\equiv\mechanical{m}^2/[2(\mechanical{m}-\mechanical{M})]$: An expanding shell with $\mechanical{M}<\mechanical{m}$ cannot escape from its own gravitational pull.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{S^{ab}},\energy{S^\tau{}_\tau},\energy{S^\theta{}_\theta}$ | — | Surface stress-energy | The intrinsic tensor and its mixed components. |
| $\energy{\sigma}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-1}}$ | Surface mass density | The shell's rest mass per proper area. |
| $\mechanical{u^a}$ | — | Shell velocity | The velocity field within the shell's world surface. |
| $\mechanical{R},\mechanical{R_{\max}}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Shell radii | The shell radius and its maximum value for bound motion. |
| $\mechanical{\tau}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Proper time | The time measured by observers on the shell. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{rad}}$ | Angles | The shell's angular coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Masses | The gravitational mass and conserved rest mass. |
| $K^a_{\pm b}$ | — | Extrinsic curvature | The curvature on each side of the shell. |
