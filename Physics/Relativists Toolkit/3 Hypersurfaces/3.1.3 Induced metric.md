> [!TLDR]
> Restricting the spacetime line element gives an induced three-metric, or a two-metric on a null hypersurface.

<!-- PDF page 78; printed page 62 -->

The metric intrinsic to the hypersurface $\Sigma$ is obtained by restricting the line element to displacements confined to the hypersurface. Recalling the parametric equations $x^\alpha=x^\alpha(y^a)$, we have that the vectors

$$
e_a^\alpha=\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial y^a}
\tag{3.7}
$$

are tangent to curves contained in $\Sigma$. (This implies that $e_a^\alpha n_\alpha=0$ in the non-null case, and $e_a^\alpha\mechanical{k}_\alpha=0$ in the null case.) Now, for displacements within $\Sigma$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
d\mechanical{s}_\Sigma^2&=g_{\alpha\beta}\,dx^\alpha\,dx^\beta\\
&=g_{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial y^a}\,dy^a\right)
\left(\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial y^b}\,dy^b\right)\\
&=h_{ab}\,dy^a\,dy^b,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.8}
$$

where

$$
h_{ab}=g_{\alpha\beta}e_a^\alpha e_b^\beta
\tag{3.9}
$$

is the *induced metric*, or *first fundamental form*, of the hypersurface. It is a *scalar* with respect to transformations $x^\alpha\to x^{\alpha'}$ of the spacetime coordinates, but it behaves as a *tensor* under transformations $y^a\to y^{a'}$ of the hypersurface coordinates. We will refer to such objects as *three-tensors*.

These relations simplify when the hypersurface is null and we use the coordinates of Eq. (3.6). Then $e_1^\alpha=(\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\lambda})_{\theta^A}\equiv\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ and it follows that

<!-- PDF page 79; printed page 63 -->

$h_{11}=g_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta=0$ and $h_{1A}=g_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha e_A^\beta=0$, because by construction $e_A^\alpha\equiv(\partial x^\alpha/\partial\theta^A)_{\mechanical{\lambda}}$ is orthogonal to $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$. In the null case, therefore,

$$
d\mechanical{s}_\Sigma^2=\sigma_{AB}\,d\theta^A\,d\theta^B,
\tag{3.10}
$$

where

$$
\sigma_{AB}=g_{\alpha\beta}e_A^\alpha e_B^\beta,\qquad
e_A^\alpha=\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\theta^A}\right)_{\mechanical{\lambda}}.
\tag{3.11}
$$

Here the induced metric is a two-tensor.

We conclude by writing down completeness relations for the inverse metric. In the non-null case,

$$
g^{\alpha\beta}=\varepsilon n^\alpha n^\beta+h^{ab}e_a^\alpha e_b^\beta,
\tag{3.12}
$$

where $h^{ab}$ is the inverse of the induced metric. Equation (3.12) is verified by computing all inner products between $n^\alpha$ and $e_a^\alpha$ and recovering the expected results.

In the null case we must introduce, everywhere on $\Sigma$, an auxiliary null vector field $N^\alpha$ satisfying $N_\alpha\mechanical{k}^\alpha=-1$ and $N_\alpha e_A^\alpha=0$ (see Section 2.4). Then the inverse metric can be expressed as

$$
g^{\alpha\beta}
=-\mechanical{k}^\alpha N^\beta-N^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta+\sigma^{AB}e_A^\alpha e_B^\beta,
\tag{3.13}
$$

where $\sigma^{AB}$ is the inverse of $\sigma_{AB}$. Equation (3.13) is verified by computing all inner products between $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$, $N^\alpha$, and $e_A^\alpha$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x^\alpha,y^a,\theta^A$ | — | Coordinates | The arbitrary spacetime and intrinsic surface coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{s}_\Sigma$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Surface interval | The line element restricted to the hypersurface. |
| $g_{\alpha\beta},g^{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Spacetime metrics | The surrounding metric and its inverse. |
| $h_{ab},h^{ab},h_{11},h_{1A},\sigma_{AB},\sigma^{AB}$ | — | Induced metrics | The hypersurface metric, its components, and the null two-metric. |
| $n_\alpha,n^\alpha$ | — | Unit normal | The normalized normal in the non-null case. |
| $\mechanical{k}^\alpha,\mechanical{k}_\alpha$ | — | Null tangent | The normal and generator tangent in the null case. |
| $\mechanical{\lambda}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Generator parameter | The parameter along the null generators in geometrized units. |
