> [!TLDR]
> Projecting the spacetime covariant derivative gives the covariant derivative compatible with the induced metric.

<!-- PDF page 89; printed page 73 -->

We wish to examine how tangent tensor fields are *differentiated*. We want to relate the covariant derivative of $A^{\alpha\beta\cdots}$ (with respect to a connection that is compatible with the spacetime metric $g_{\alpha\beta}$) to the covariant derivative of $A^{ab\cdots}$, defined in terms of a connection that is compatible with the induced metric $h_{ab}$. For simplicity we shall restrict our attention to the case of a tangent vector field $A^\alpha$, such that

$$
A^\alpha=A^a e_a^\alpha,\qquad A^\alpha n_\alpha=0,\qquad A_a=A_\alpha e_a^\alpha.
$$

Generalization to three-tensors of higher ranks will be obvious.

<!-- PDF page 90; printed page 74 -->

We define the *intrinsic covariant derivative* of a three-vector $A_a$ to be the projection of $A_{\alpha;\beta}$ onto the hypersurface:

$$
A_{a\mid b}=A_{\alpha;\beta}e_a^\alpha e_b^\beta.
\tag{3.27}
$$

We will show that $A_{a\mid b}$, as defined here, is nothing but the covariant derivative of $A_a$ defined in the usual way in terms of a connection $\Gamma^a_{bc}$ that is compatible with $h_{ab}$.

To get started, let us express the right-hand side of Eq. (3.27) as

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{\alpha;\beta}e_a^\alpha e_b^\beta
&=(A_\alpha e_a^\alpha)_{;\beta}e_b^\beta-A_\alpha e_a^\alpha{}_{;\beta}e_b^\beta\\
&=A_{a,\beta}e_b^\beta-e_{a\gamma;\beta}e_b^\beta A^c e_c^\gamma\\
&=\frac{\partial A_a}{\partial x^\beta}\frac{\partial x^\beta}{\partial y^b}
-e_c^\gamma e_{a\gamma;\beta}e_b^\beta A^c\\
&=A_{a,b}-\Gamma_{cab}A^c,
\end{aligned}
$$

where we have defined

$$
\Gamma_{cab}=e_c^\gamma e_{a\gamma;\beta}e_b^\beta.
\tag{3.28}
$$

Equation (3.27) then reads

$$
A_{a\mid b}=A_{a,b}-\Gamma^c_{ab}A_c,
\tag{3.29}
$$

and this is the familiar expression for the covariant derivative.

The connection used here is the one defined by Eq. (3.28), and we would like to show that it is compatible with the induced metric. In other words, we would like to prove that $\Gamma_{cab}$, as defined by Eq. (3.28), can also be expressed as

$$
\Gamma_{cab}=\frac12(h_{ca,b}+h_{cb,a}-h_{ab,c}).
\tag{3.30}
$$

This could be done directly by working out the right-hand side of Eq. (3.28). It is easier, however, to show that the connection is such that $h_{ab\mid c}=h_{\alpha\beta;\gamma}e_a^\alpha e_b^\beta e_c^\gamma=0$. Indeed,

$$
\begin{aligned}
h_{\alpha\beta;\gamma}e_a^\alpha e_b^\beta e_c^\gamma
&=(g_{\alpha\beta}-\varepsilon n_\alpha n_\beta)_{;\gamma}e_a^\alpha e_b^\beta e_c^\gamma\\
&=-\varepsilon(n_{\alpha;\gamma}n_\beta+n_\alpha n_{\beta;\gamma})e_a^\alpha e_b^\beta e_c^\gamma\\
&=0,
\end{aligned}
$$

because $n_\alpha e_a^\alpha=0$. Intrinsic covariant differentiation is therefore the same operation as straightforward covariant differentiation of a three-tensor.
