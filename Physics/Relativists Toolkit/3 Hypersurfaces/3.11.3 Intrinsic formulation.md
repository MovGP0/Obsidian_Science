> [!TLDR]
> The jump in transverse curvature gives the null shell's density, current, and pressure without a continuous spacetime coordinate system.

<!-- Source: PDF page 118, printed page 102. -->

In Section 3.7, the surface stress-energy tensor of a timelike or spacelike shell was expressed in terms of *intrinsic* three-tensors — quantities that can be defined on the hypersurface only. The most important ingredients in this formulation were $h_{ab}$, the (continuous) induced metric, and $[K_{ab}]$, the discontinuity in the extrinsic curvature. We would like to achieve something similar here, and remove the need to involve a continuous coordinate system $x^\alpha$ to calculate the surface quantities $\energy{\mu}$, $\mechanical{j^A}$, and $\mechanical{p}$.

<!-- Source: PDF page 119, printed page 103. -->

We can expect that the intrinsic description of the surface stress-energy tensor of a null shell will involve $\sigma_{AB}$, the nonvanishing components of the induced metric. We might also expect that it should involve the jump in the extrinsic curvature of the null hypersurface, which would be defined by $K_{ab}=\mechanical{k}_{\alpha;\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b=\frac12(\mathcal L_k g_{\alpha\beta})e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b$. Not so. The reason is that there is nothing ‘transverse’ about this object: In the case of a timelike or spacelike hypersurface, the normal $n^\alpha$ points away from the surface, and $\mathcal L_n g_{\alpha\beta}$ truly represents the transverse derivative of the metric; when the hypersurface is null, on the other hand, $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ is tangent to the surface, and $\mathcal L_k g_{\alpha\beta}$ is a *tangential* derivative. Thus, the extrinsic curvature is necessarily continuous when the hypersurface is null, and it cannot be related to the tensor $\gamma_{\alpha\beta}$ defined by Eq. (3.94).

There is, fortunately, an easy solution to this problem: We can introduce a *transverse curvature* $C_{ab}$ that properly represents the transverse derivative of the metric. This shall be defined by $C_{ab}=\frac12(\mathcal L_N g_{\alpha\beta})e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b=\frac12(N_{\alpha;\beta}+N_{\beta;\alpha})e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b$, or

$$
C_{ab}=-N_\alpha e^\alpha_{a;\beta}e^\beta_b. \tag{3.100}
$$

To arrive at Eq. (3.100) we have used the fact that $N_\alpha e^\alpha_a$ is a constant, and the identity $e^\alpha_{a;\beta}e^\beta_b=e^\alpha_{b;\beta}e^\beta_a$, which states that each basis vector $e^\alpha_a$ is Lie transported along any other basis vector; this property ensures that $C_{ab}$, as defined by Eq. (3.100), is a symmetric three-tensor.

In the continuous coordinates $x^\alpha$, the jump in the transverse curvature is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
[C_{ab}]&=[N_{\alpha;\beta}]e^\alpha_ae^\alpha_b\\
&=-[\Gamma^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}]N_\gamma e^\alpha_ae^\alpha_b\\
&=\frac12\gamma_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\alpha_b,
\end{aligned}
$$

where we have used Eq. (3.95) and the fact that $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ is orthogonal to $e^\alpha_a$. We therefore have $[C_{\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{\lambda}}]=\frac12\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta$, $[C_{A\mechanical{\lambda}}]=\frac12\gamma_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ak^\beta\equiv\frac12\gamma_A$, and $[C_{AB}]=\frac12\gamma_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B\equiv\frac12\gamma_{AB}$, where we have invoked Eq. (3.98). Finally, we find that the surface quantities can be expressed as

$$
\energy{\mu}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\sigma^{AB}[C_{AB}],\qquad
\mechanical{j^A}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\sigma^{AB}[C_{\mechanical{\lambda} B}],\qquad
\mechanical{p}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}[C_{\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{\lambda}}]. \tag{3.101}
$$

We have established that the shell’s surface quantities can all be related to the induced metric $\sigma_{AB}$ and the jump of the transverse curvature $C_{ab}$. This completes the intrinsic formulation of our null-shell formalism.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $h_{ab},\sigma_{AB},\sigma^{AB}$ | — | Induced metrics | The non-null metric and the null two-metric and its inverse. |
| $g_{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Spacetime metric | The surrounding metric. |
| $K_{ab}$ | — | Extrinsic curvature | The tangent derivative, continuous for a null surface. |
| $C_{ab}$ | — | Transverse curvature | The curvature whose jump determines the shell matter. |
| $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ | — | Null tangent | The generator tangent and null normal. |
| $n^\alpha$ | — | Unit normal | The earlier non-null normal direction. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-1}}$ | Surface density | The density from the transverse two-curvature jump. |
| $\mechanical{j^A}$ | — | Surface current | The current from the mixed transverse-curvature jump. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-1}}$ | Surface pressure | The pressure from the generator-curvature jump. |
