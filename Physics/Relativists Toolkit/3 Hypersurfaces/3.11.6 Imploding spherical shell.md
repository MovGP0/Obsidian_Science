> [!TLDR]
> An imploding spherical null shell has density equal to its mass divided by its area and has zero surface current and pressure.

<!-- Source: PDF page 123, printed page 107. -->

For our first application of the null-shell formalism, we take another look at the gravitational collapse of a thin spherical shell, a problem that was first formulated in Section 3.9. Here we imagine that the collapse proceeds at the speed of light, and that the thin shell lies on a null hypersurface $\Sigma$. We take spacetime to be flat inside the shell (in $\mathcal V^-$), and write the metric there as

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_-^2=-\mathrm d\mechanical{t_-}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2,
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 124, printed page 108. -->

in terms of spatial coordinates $(\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ and a time coordinate $\mechanical{t_-}$. The metric outside the shell (in $\mathcal V^+$) is the Schwarzschild solution,

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_+^2=-f\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t_+}^2+f^{-1}\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2,
$$

which is expressed in the same spatial coordinates but in terms of a distinct time $\mechanical{t_+}$; here, $f=1-2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}$ and $\mechanical{M}$ denotes the gravitational mass of the collapsing shell.

As seen from $\mathcal V^-$, the null hypersurface $\Sigma$ is described by the equation $\mechanical{t_-}+\mechanical{r}\equiv\mechanical{v_-}=\mathrm{constant}$, which means that the induced metric on $\Sigma$ is given by $\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_\Sigma^2=\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2$. As seen from $\mathcal V^+$, on the other hand, the hypersurface is described by $\mechanical{t_+}+\mechanical{r^*}(\mechanical{r})\equiv\mechanical{v_+}=\mathrm{constant}$, where $\mechanical{r^*}(\mechanical{r})=\int f^{-1}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r}+2\mechanical{M}\ln(\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}-1)$, and this gives rise to the same induced metric. From these considerations we see that it was permissible to express the metrics of $\mathcal V^\pm$ in terms of the same spatial coordinates $(\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$, but that $\mechanical{t_+}$ cannot be equal to $\mechanical{t_-}$. The induced metric on the shell is

$$
\sigma_{AB}\mathrm d\theta^A\mathrm d\theta^B=\mechanical{\lambda}^2(\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2+\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}^2),
$$

where we have set $\theta^A=(\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ and identified $-\mechanical{r}$ with the parameter $\mechanical{\lambda}$ on the null generators of the hypersurface; we shall see that here, $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is an affine parameter on both sides of $\Sigma$.

As seen from $\mathcal V^-$, the parametric equations $x^\alpha=x^\alpha(\mechanical{\lambda},\theta^A)$ that describe the hypersurface have the explicit form $\mechanical{t_-}=\mechanical{v_-}+\mechanical{\lambda}$, $\mechanical{r}=-\mechanical{\lambda}$, $\mechanical{\theta}=\theta$, and $\mechanical{\phi}=\phi$. These give us the tangent vectors $\mechanical{k}^\alpha\partial_\alpha=\partial_t-\partial_r$, $e^\alpha_\theta\partial_\alpha=\partial_\theta$, and $e^\alpha_\phi\partial_\alpha=\partial_\phi$, and the basis is completed by $N_\alpha\mathrm dx^\alpha=-\frac12(\mathrm d\mechanical{t}-\mathrm d\mechanical{r})$. From all this and Eq. (3.100) we find that the nonvanishing components of the transverse curvature are

$$
C^-_{AB}=\frac{1}{2\mechanical{r}}\sigma_{AB}.
$$

The fact that $C^-_{\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{\lambda}}=0$ confirms that $\mechanical{\lambda}\equiv-\mechanical{r}$ is an affine parameter on the $\mathcal V^-$ side of $\Sigma$.

As seen from $\mathcal V^+$, the parametric equations are $\mechanical{t_+}=\mechanical{v_+}-\mechanical{r^*}(-\mechanical{\lambda})$, $\mechanical{r}=-\mechanical{\lambda}$, $\mechanical{\theta}=\theta$, and $\mechanical{\phi}=\phi$. The basis vectors are $\mechanical{k}^\alpha\partial_\alpha=f^{-1}\partial_t-\partial_r$, $e^\alpha_\theta\partial_\alpha=\partial_\theta$, $e^\alpha_\phi\partial_\alpha=\partial_\phi$, and $N_\alpha\mathrm dx^\alpha=-\frac12(f\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}-\mathrm d\mechanical{r})$. The nonvanishing components of the transverse curvature are now

$$
C^+_{AB}=\frac{f}{2\mechanical{r}}\sigma_{AB}.
$$

The fact that $C^+_{\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{\lambda}}=0$ confirms that $\mechanical{\lambda}\equiv-\mechanical{r}$ is an affine parameter on the $\mathcal V^+$ side of $\Sigma$; $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is therefore an affine parameter on both sides.

<!-- Source: PDF page 125, printed page 109. -->

The angular components of the transverse curvature are discontinuous across the shell: $[C_{AB}]=-(\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}^2)\sigma_{AB}$. According to Eq. (3.101), this means that the shell has a vanishing surface current $\mechanical{j^A}$ and a vanishing surface pressure $\mechanical{p}$, but that its surface density is

$$
\energy{\mu}=\frac{\mechanical{M}}{4\pi\mechanical{r}^2}.
$$

We have obtained the very sensible result that the surface density of a collapsing null shell is equal to its gravitational mass divided by its (ever decreasing) surface area. Notice that $\energy{\mu_{\mathrm{physical}}}=\energy{\mu}$ for observers at rest in $\mathcal V^-$. Because of the focusing action of the null shell, however, these observers do not remain at rest after crossing over to the $\mathcal V^+$ side: A simple calculation, based on Eq. (3.91), reveals that an observer at rest before crossing the shell will move according to $\mathrm d\mechanical{r}/\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau}=-(\energy{\tilde E}^2-f)^{1/2}$ after crossing the shell; the energy parameter $\energy{\tilde E}$ varies from observer to observer, and is related by $\energy{\tilde E}=1-\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r_\Sigma}$ to the radius $\mechanical{r_\Sigma}$ at which a given observer crosses the hypersurface.
