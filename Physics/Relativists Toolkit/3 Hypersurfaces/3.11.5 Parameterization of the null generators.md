> [!TLDR]
> Surface pressure controls the jump in generator nonaffinity, while reparameterization changes the bare surface quantities but preserves the full shell tensor.

<!-- Source: PDF page 120, printed page 104. -->

Our null-shell formalism is now complete, and it is ready to be involved in applications. We will consider a few in the following subsections, but we first return to a statement made earlier, that in general $\mechanical{\lambda}$ cannot be an affine parameter on both sides of the hypersurface. We shall justify this here, and also consider what happens to $\energy{\mu}$, $\mechanical{j^A}$, and $\mechanical{p}$ when the parameterization of the null generators is altered.

<!-- Source: PDF page 121, printed page 105. -->

Whether or not $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is an affine parameter can be decided by computing $\mechanical{\kappa_\pm}$, the ‘acceleration’ of the null vector $\mechanical{k}^\alpha_\pm$. This is defined on either side of the hypersurface by (Section 1.3)

$$
\mechanical{k}^\alpha{}_{;\beta}\mechanical{k}^\beta=\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{k}^\alpha,
$$

and $\mechanical{\lambda}$ will be an affine parameter on the $\mathcal V^\pm$ side of $\Sigma$ if $\mechanical{\kappa_\pm}=0$. According to Eq. (3.88), $\mechanical{\kappa}=-N_\alpha \mechanical{k}^\alpha{}_{;\beta}\mechanical{k}^\beta=-N_\alpha e^\alpha_{\mechanical{\lambda};\beta}e^\beta_\mechanical{\lambda}=C_{\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{\lambda}}$, where we have also used Eqs. (3.85) and (3.100). Equation (3.101) then relates the discontinuity in the acceleration to the surface pressure:

$$
[\mechanical{\kappa}]=-8\pi\mechanical{p}. \tag{3.102}
$$

We conclude that $\mechanical{\lambda}$ can be an affine parameter on both sides of $\Sigma$ only when the null shell has a vanishing surface pressure. When $\mechanical{p}\ne0$, $\mechanical{\lambda}$ can be chosen to be an affine parameter on *one* side of the hypersurface, but it will not be an affine parameter on the other side.

Additional insight into this matter can be gained from Raychaudhuri’s equation, which describes the transverse evolution of a congruence of null geodesics (Section 2.4). In Section 2.6, Problem 8, Raychaudhuri’s equation was written in terms of an arbitrary parameterization of the null geodesics. When the congruence is hypersurface orthogonal, it reads

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda}}+\frac12\mechanical{\theta}^2+\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
=\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{\theta}-8\pi\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta,
$$

where $\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\mechanical{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}$ are the expansion and shear of the congruence, respectively; the equation holds on either side of $\Sigma$. Because it depends only on the intrinsic geometry of the hypersurface, the left-hand side of Raychaudhuri’s equation is guaranteed to be continuous across the shell. Continuity of the right-hand side therefore implies

$$
[\mechanical{\kappa}]\mechanical{\theta}=8\pi[\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta]. \tag{3.103}
$$

This relation shows that $[\mechanical{\kappa}]\ne0$ (and therefore $\mechanical{p}\ne0$) whenever the component $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta$ of the stress-energy tensor is discontinuous at the shell. We conclude that $\mechanical{\lambda}$ cannot be an affine parameter on both sides of $\Sigma$ when $[\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta]\ne0$. (Notice that this conclusion breaks down when $\mechanical{\theta}=0$, that is, when the shell is stationary.)

Recalling (Section 2.4.8) that the expansion $\mechanical{\theta}$ is equal to the fractional rate of change of the congruence’s cross-sectional area, we find that with the help of Eq. (3.102), Eq. (3.103) can be expressed as

$$
\mechanical{p}\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda}}\mathrm d\mechanical{S}
+[\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta]\mathrm d\mechanical{S}=0, \tag{3.104}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 122, printed page 106. -->

where $\mathrm d\mechanical{S}=\sqrt{\sigma}\,\mathrm d^2\theta$ is an element of cross-sectional area on the shell (Section 3.2.2). This equation has a simple interpretation: The first term represents the work done by the shell as it expands or contracts, while the second term is the energy absorbed by the shell from its surroundings; Eq. (3.104) therefore states that all of the absorbed energy goes into work.

Having established that $\mechanical{\lambda}$ cannot, in general, be an affine parameter on both sides of the hypersurface, let us now investigate how a change of parameterization might affect the surface density $\energy{\mu}$, surface current $\mechanical{j^A}$, and surface pressure $\mechanical{p}$ of the null shell. Because each generator can be reparameterized independently of any other generator, we must consider transformations of the form

$$
\mechanical{\lambda}\to\mechanical{\bar\lambda}(\mechanical{\lambda},\theta^A). \tag{3.105}
$$

The question before us is: How do the surface quantities change under such a transformation?

To answer this we need to work out how the transformation of Eq. (3.105) affects the vectors $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$, $e^\alpha_A$, and $N^\alpha$. We first note that the differential form of Eq. (3.105) is

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{\bar\lambda}=e^\beta\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda}+c_A\mathrm d\theta^A, \tag{3.106}
$$

where

$$
e^\beta\equiv\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{\bar\lambda}}{\partial\mechanical{\lambda}}\right)_{\theta^A},\qquad
c_A\equiv\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{\bar\lambda}}{\partial\theta^A}\right)_\mechanical{\lambda}; \tag{3.107}
$$

both $e^\beta$ and $c_A$ depend on $y^a=(\mechanical{\lambda},\theta^A)$, but because they depend on the intrinsic coordinates only, we have that $[e^\beta]=0=[c_A]$. A displacement within the hypersurface can be described either by

$$
\mathrm dx^\alpha=\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda}+e^\alpha_A\mathrm d\theta^A,
$$

where $\mechanical{k}^\alpha=(\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\lambda})_{\theta^A}$ and $e^\alpha_A=(\partial x^\alpha/\partial\theta^A)_\mechanical{\lambda}$, or by

$$
\mathrm dx^\alpha=\bar \mechanical{k}^\alpha\mathrm d\mechanical{\bar\lambda}+\bar e^\alpha_A\mathrm d\theta^A,
$$

where $\bar \mechanical{k}^\alpha=(\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\bar\lambda})_{\theta^A}$ and $\bar e^\alpha_A=(\partial x^\alpha/\partial\theta^A)_{\mechanical{\bar\lambda}}$; these relations hold on either side of $\Sigma$, in the relevant coordinate system $x^\alpha_\pm$. Using Eq. (3.106), it is easy to see that the tangent vectors transform as

$$
\bar \mechanical{k}^\alpha=e^{-\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha,\qquad
\bar e^\alpha_A=e^\alpha_A-c_Ae^{-\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \tag{3.108}
$$

under the reparameterization of Eq. (3.105). It may be checked that the new basis vectors satisfy the orthogonality relations (3.85), and that the induced metric $\sigma_{AB}$ is invariant under this transformation: $\bar\sigma_{AB}\equiv g_{\alpha\beta}\bar e^\alpha_A\bar e^\beta_B=g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B=\sigma_{AB}$.

<!-- Source: PDF page 123, printed page 107. -->

To preserve the relations (3.88) we let the new auxiliary null vector be

$$
\bar N^\alpha=e^\beta N^\alpha+\frac12c^Ac_Ae^{-\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha-c^Ae^\alpha_A, \tag{3.109}
$$

where $c^A=\sigma^{AB}c_B$. This ensures that the completeness relations (3.89) take the same form in the new basis.

It is a straightforward (but slightly tedious) task to compute how the transverse curvature $C_{ab}$ changes under a reparameterization of the generators, and to then compute how the surface quantities transform. You will be asked to go through this calculation in Section 3.13, Problem 8. The answer is that under the reparameterization of Eq. (3.105), the surface quantities transform as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\bar\mu}&=e^\beta\energy{\mu}+2c_A\mechanical{j^A}+c^Ac_Ae^{-\beta}\mechanical{p},\\
\mechanical{\bar j^A}&=\mechanical{j^A}+c^Ae^{-\beta}\mechanical{p},\\
\mechanical{\bar p}&=e^{-\beta}\mechanical{p}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.110}
$$

These transformations, together with Eq. (3.108), imply that the surface stress-energy tensor becomes $\energy{\bar S^{\alpha\beta}}=e^{-\beta}\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}$. We also have $(-\bar \mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu)^{-1}=e^\beta(-\mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu)$, and these results reveal that the combination $(-\mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu)^{-1}\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}$ is *invariant* under the reparameterization. This, finally, establishes the invariance of $\energy{T_\Sigma^{\alpha\beta}}$, the full stress-energy tensor of the surface layer.

As a final remark, we note that under the reparameterization of Eq. (3.105), the physically-measured surface quantities transform as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\bar\mu_{\mathrm{physical}}}&=e^{2\beta}\energy{\mu_{\mathrm{physical}}}+2c_Ae^\beta\mechanical{j^A_{\mathrm{physical}}}+c^Ac_A\mechanical{p_{\mathrm{physical}}},\\
\mechanical{\bar j^A_{\mathrm{physical}}}&=e^\beta\mechanical{j^A_{\mathrm{physical}}}+c^A\mechanical{p_{\mathrm{physical}}},\\
\mechanical{\bar p_{\mathrm{physical}}}&=\mechanical{p_{\mathrm{physical}}};
\end{aligned} \tag{3.111}
$$

we see in particular that the physically-measured surface pressure is an invariant.
