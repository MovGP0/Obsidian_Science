> [!TLDR]
> The second junction condition requires continuous extrinsic curvature; a jump gives the thin shell's surface stress-energy.

<!-- Source: PDF page 104, printed page 88. -->

Explicitly, the surface stress-energy tensor is given by

$$
16\pi\varepsilon\energy{S_{\alpha\beta}}=\kappa_{\mu\alpha}n^\mu n_\beta+\kappa_{\mu\beta}n^\mu n_\alpha-\kappa n_\alpha n_\beta-\varepsilon\kappa_{\alpha\beta}-\left(\kappa_{\mu\nu}n^\mu n^\nu-\varepsilon\kappa\right)g_{\alpha\beta}.
$$

From this we notice that $\energy{S_{\alpha\beta}}$ is tangent to the hypersurface: $\energy{S_{\alpha\beta}}n^\beta=0$. It therefore admits the decomposition

$$
\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}=\energy{S^{ab}}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b, \tag{3.55}
$$

where $\energy{S_{ab}}=\energy{S_{\alpha\beta}}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b$ is a symmetric three-tensor. This is evaluated as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
16\pi\energy{S_{ab}}&=-\kappa_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b-\varepsilon\left(\kappa_{\mu\nu}n^\mu n^\nu-\varepsilon\kappa\right)h_{ab}\\
&=-\kappa_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b-\kappa_{\mu\nu}\left(g^{\mu\nu}-h^{mn}e^\mu_me^\nu_n\right)h_{ab}+\kappa h_{ab}\\
&=-\kappa_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b+h^{mn}\kappa_{\mu\nu}e^\mu_me^\nu_nh_{ab}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 105, printed page 89. -->

On the other hand we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
[n_{\alpha;\beta}]&=-[\Gamma^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}]n_\gamma\\
&=-\frac12\left(\kappa_{\gamma\alpha}n_\beta+\kappa_{\gamma\beta}n_\alpha-\kappa_{\alpha\beta}n_\gamma\right)n^\gamma\\
&=\frac12\left(\varepsilon\kappa_{\alpha\beta}-\kappa_{\gamma\alpha}n_\beta n^\gamma-\kappa_{\gamma\beta}n_\alpha n^\gamma\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

which allows us to write

$$
[K_{ab}]=[n_{\alpha;\beta}]e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\kappa_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b.
$$

Collecting these results we obtain

$$
\energy{S_{ab}}=-\frac{\varepsilon}{8\pi}\left([K_{ab}]-[K]h_{ab}\right), \tag{3.56}
$$

which relates the surface stress-energy tensor to the jump in extrinsic curvature from one side of $\Sigma$ to the other. The complete stress-energy tensor of the surface layer is

$$
\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}_\Sigma}=\delta(\mechanical{\ell})\energy{S^{ab}}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b. \tag{3.57}
$$

We conclude that a smooth transition across $\Sigma$ requires $[K_{ab}]=0$ — the extrinsic curvature must be the same on both sides of the hypersurface. This requirement does more than just remove the $\delta$-function term from the Einstein tensor: In Section 3.13, Problem 4 you will be asked to prove that $[K_{ab}]=0$ implies $A^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}=0$, which means that the full Riemann tensor is then nonsingular at $\Sigma$.

The condition $[K_{ab}]=0$ is our second junction condition, and it is expressed independently of the coordinates $x^\alpha$ and $x^\alpha_\pm$. If this condition is violated, then the spacetime is singular at $\Sigma$, but the singularity comes with a sound physical interpretation: a surface layer with stress-energy tensor $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}_\Sigma}$ is present at the hypersurface.
