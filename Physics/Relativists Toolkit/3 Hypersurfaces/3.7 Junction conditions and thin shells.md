> [!TLDR]
> Junction conditions determine whether two spacetime metrics join smoothly or form a thin matter shell.

<!-- Source: PDF page 100, printed page 84. -->

The following situation sometimes presents itself: A hypersurface $\Sigma$ partitions spacetime into two regions $\mathcal V^+$ and $\mathcal V^-$ (Fig. 3.5). In $\mathcal V^+$ the metric is $g^+_{\alpha\beta}$ and it is expressed in a system of coordinates $x^\alpha_+$. In $\mathcal V^-$ the metric is $g^-_{\alpha\beta}$, and it is expressed in coordinates $x^\alpha_-$. We ask: What conditions must be put on the metrics to ensure that $\mathcal V^+$ and $\mathcal V^-$ are joined *smoothly* at $\Sigma$, so that the union of $g^+_{\alpha\beta}$ and $g^-_{\alpha\beta}$ forms a valid solution to the Einstein field equations? To answer this question is not entirely straightforward because in practical situations, the coordinate systems $x^\alpha_\pm$ will often be different, and it may not be possible to compare the metrics directly. To circumvent this difficulty we will endeavour to formulate *junction conditions* that involve only three-tensors on $\Sigma$. In this section we will assume that $\Sigma$ is either timelike or spacelike; we will return to the case of a null hypersurface in Section 3.11.
