> [!TLDR]
> The singular Einstein equations give a null shell's surface density, current, and pressure from the transverse metric-derivative jump.

<!-- Source: PDF page 116, printed page 100. -->

As we did in Section 3.7, we introduce for our short-term convenience a continuous coordinate system $x^\alpha$, distinct from $x^\alpha_\pm$, in a neighbourhood of the hypersurface; the final formulation of our null-shell formalism will be independent of these coordinates. We express the metric as a distribution-valued tensor:

$$
g_{\alpha\beta}=\Theta(\mechanical{\tau})g^+_{\alpha\beta}+\Theta(-\mechanical{\tau})g^-_{\alpha\beta},
$$

where $g^\pm_{\alpha\beta}(x^\mu)$ is the metric in $\mathcal V^\pm$. We assume that in these coordinates, the metric is continuous at $\Sigma$: $[g_{\alpha\beta}]=0$; Eq. (3.87) is compatible with this requirement. We also have $[\mechanical{k}^\alpha]=[e^\alpha_A]=[N^\alpha]=[\mechanical{u}^\alpha]=0$. Differentiation of the metric proceeds as in Sections 3.7.2 and 3.7.3, except that we now write $\mechanical{\tau}$ instead of $\mechanical{\ell}$, and we use Eq. (3.92) to relate the gradient of $\mechanical{\tau}$ to the null vector $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$. We arrive at a Riemann tensor that contains a singular part given by

<!-- Source: PDF page 117, printed page 101. -->

$$
R_\Sigma{}^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}
=-(-\mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu)^{-1}\left([\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\delta}]\mechanical{k}_\gamma-[\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}]\mechanical{k}_\delta\right)\delta(\mechanical{\tau}), \tag{3.93}
$$

where $[\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}]$ is the jump in the Christoffel symbols across $\Sigma$.

In order to make Eq. (3.93) more explicit we must characterize the discontinuous behaviour of $g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}$. The condition $[g_{\alpha\beta}]=0$ guarantees that the tangential derivatives of the metric are continuous:

$$
[g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}]\mechanical{k}^\gamma=0=[g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}]e^\gamma_C.
$$

The only possible discontinuity is therefore in $g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}N^\gamma$, the transverse derivative of the metric. In view of Eq. (3.88) we conclude that there exists a tensor field $\gamma_{\alpha\beta}$ such that

$$
[g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}]=-\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}_\gamma. \tag{3.94}
$$

This tensor is given explicitly by $\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=[g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}]N^\gamma$, and it is now easy to check that

$$
[\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}]=-\frac12\left(\gamma^\alpha{}_\beta \mechanical{k}_\gamma+\gamma^\alpha{}_\gamma \mechanical{k}_\beta-\gamma_{\beta\gamma}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\right). \tag{3.95}
$$

Substituting this into Eq. (3.93) gives

$$
R_\Sigma{}^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}
=\frac12(-\mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu)^{-1}\left(\gamma^\alpha{}_\delta \mechanical{k}_\beta \mechanical{k}_\gamma-\gamma_{\beta\delta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}_\gamma-\gamma^\alpha{}_\gamma \mechanical{k}_\beta \mechanical{k}_\delta+\gamma_{\beta\gamma}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}_\delta\right)\delta(\mechanical{\tau}), \tag{3.96}
$$

and we see that $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ and $\gamma_{\alpha\beta}$ give a complete characterization of the singular part of the Riemann tensor.

From Eq. (3.96) it is easy to form the singular part of the Einstein tensor, and the Einstein field equations then give us the singular part of the stress-energy tensor:

$$
\energy{T_\Sigma^{\alpha\beta}}=(-\mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu)^{-1}\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}\delta(\mechanical{\tau}), \tag{3.97}
$$

where

$$
\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\left(\mechanical{k}^\alpha\gamma^\beta{}_\mu \mechanical{k}^\mu+\mechanical{k}^\beta\gamma^\alpha{}_\mu \mechanical{k}^\mu-\gamma^\mu{}_\mu \mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta-\gamma_{\mu\nu}\mechanical{k}^\mu \mechanical{k}^\nu g^{\alpha\beta}\right)
$$

is the surface stress-energy tensor of the null shell — up to a factor $-\mechanical{k}_\mu \mechanical{u}^\mu$ that depends on the choice of observers making measurements on the shell. Its expression can be simplified if we decompose $\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}$ in the basis $(\mechanical{k}^\alpha,e^\alpha_A,N^\alpha)$. For this purpose we introduce the projections

$$
\gamma_A\equiv\gamma_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ak^\beta,\qquad
\gamma_{AB}\equiv\gamma_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B, \tag{3.98}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 118, printed page 102. -->

and we use the completeness relations (3.89) to find that the vector $\gamma^\alpha{}_\mu \mechanical{k}^\mu$ admits the decomposition

$$
\gamma^\alpha{}_\mu \mechanical{k}^\mu=\frac12\left(\gamma^\mu{}_\mu-\sigma^{AB}\gamma_{AB}\right)\mechanical{k}^\alpha+\left(\sigma^{AB}\gamma_B\right)e^\alpha_A-\left(\gamma_{\mu\nu}\mechanical{k}^\mu \mechanical{k}^\nu\right)N^\alpha.
$$

Substituting this into our previous expression for $\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}$ and invoking once more the completeness relations, we arrive at our final expression for the surface stress-energy tensor:

$$
\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}=\energy{\mu}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta+\mechanical{j^A}\left(\mechanical{k}^\alpha e^\beta_A+e^\alpha_Ak^\beta\right)+\mechanical{p}\sigma^{AB}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B. \tag{3.99}
$$

Here,

$$
\energy{\mu}\equiv-\frac{1}{16\pi}\left(\sigma^{AB}\gamma_{AB}\right)
$$

can be interpreted as the shell’s surface density,

$$
\mechanical{j^A}\equiv\frac{1}{16\pi}\left(\sigma^{AB}\gamma_B\right)
$$

as a surface current, and

$$
\mechanical{p}\equiv-\frac{1}{16\pi}\left(\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta\right)
$$

as an isotropic surface pressure.

The surface stress-energy tensor of Eq. (3.99) is expressed in the continuous coordinates $x^\alpha$. As a matter of fact, the derivation of Eq. (3.99) relies heavily on these coordinates: The introduction of $\gamma_{\alpha\beta}$ rests on the fact that in these coordinates, $g_{\alpha\beta}$ is continuous at $\Sigma$, so that an eventual discontinuity in the metric derivative must be directed along $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$. In the next subsection we will remove the need to involve the coordinates $x^\alpha$ in practical applications of the null-shell formalism. For the time being we simply note that while Eq. (3.99) is indeed expressed in the coordinates $x^\alpha$, it is a *tensorial* equation involving vectors ($\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ and $e^\alpha_A$) and scalars ($\energy{\mu}$, $\mechanical{j^A}$, and $\mechanical{p}$). This equation can therefore be expressed in *any* coordinate system; in particular, when viewed from $\mathcal V^\pm$ the surface stress-energy tensor can be expressed in the original coordinates $x^\alpha_\pm$.
