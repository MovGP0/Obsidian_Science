> [!TLDR]
> A hypersurface can be specified by a coordinate constraint or by intrinsic parametric coordinates.

<!-- PDF page 76; printed page 60 -->

In a four-dimensional spacetime manifold, a *hypersurface* is a three-dimensional submanifold that can be either timelike, spacelike, or null. A particular hypersurface $\Sigma$ is selected either by putting a restriction on the coordinates,

$$
\Phi(x^\alpha)=0,
\tag{3.1}
$$

or by giving parametric equations of the form

$$
x^\alpha=x^\alpha(y^a),
\tag{3.2}
$$

where $y^a$ $(a=1,2,3)$ are coordinates intrinsic to the hypersurface. For example, a two-sphere in a three-dimensional flat space can be described either by $\Phi(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z})=\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2+\mechanical{z}^2-\mechanical{R}^2=0$, where $\mechanical{R}$ is the sphere's radius, or by $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{R}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi}$, $\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{R}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi}$, and $\mechanical{z}=\mechanical{R}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$, where $\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\mechanical{\phi}$ are the intrinsic coordinates. Notice that the relations $x^\alpha(y^a)$ describe curves contained entirely in $\Sigma$ (Fig. 3.1).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x^\alpha,y^a$ | — | Coordinates | The arbitrary spacetime and intrinsic hypersurface coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Cartesian coordinates | The coordinates in the flat-space sphere example. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Sphere radius | The radius of the example two-sphere. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{rad}}$ | Sphere angles | The intrinsic polar and azimuthal coordinates of the sphere. |
