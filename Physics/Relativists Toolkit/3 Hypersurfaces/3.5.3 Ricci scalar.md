> [!TLDR]
> The spacetime Ricci scalar splits into intrinsic curvature, extrinsic-curvature terms, and a divergence.

<!-- Source: PDF page 94, printed page 78. -->

We now complete the computation of the four-dimensional Ricci scalar. Our starting point is the relation

$$
R=2\varepsilon h^{ab}R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}n^\mu e^\alpha_an^\nu e^\beta_b+h^{ab}h^{mn}R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}e^\mu_me^\alpha_ae^\nu_ne^\beta_b,
$$

which was derived previously. The first term is simplified by using the completeness relations (3.12) and the fact that $R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}n^\mu n^\alpha n^\nu n^\beta=0$; it becomes $2\varepsilon R_{\alpha\beta}n^\alpha n^\beta$. Using the definition of the Riemann tensor, we rewrite this as

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\alpha\beta}n^\alpha n^\beta&=-n^\alpha{}_{;\alpha\beta}n^\beta+n^\alpha{}_{;\beta\alpha}n^\beta\\
&=-\left(n^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}n^\beta\right)_{;\beta}+n^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}n^\beta{}_{;\beta}+\left(n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta\right)_{;\alpha}-n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta{}_{;\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 95, printed page 79. -->

In the second term of this last expression we recognize $K^2$, where $K=n^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}$ is the trace of the extrinsic curvature. The fourth term, on the other hand, can be expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta{}_{;\alpha}&=g^{\beta\mu}g^{\alpha\nu}n_{\alpha;\beta}n_{\mu;\nu}\\
&=\left(\varepsilon n^\beta n^\mu+h^{\beta\mu}\right)\left(\varepsilon n^\alpha n^\nu+h^{\alpha\nu}\right)n_{\alpha;\beta}n_{\mu;\nu}\\
&=\left(\varepsilon n^\beta n^\mu+h^{\beta\mu}\right)h^{\alpha\nu}n_{\alpha;\beta}n_{\mu;\nu}\\
&=h^{\beta\mu}h^{\alpha\nu}n_{\alpha;\beta}n_{\mu;\nu}\\
&=h^{bm}h^{an}n_{\alpha;\beta}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_bn_{\mu;\nu}e^\mu_me^\nu_n\\
&=h^{bm}h^{an}K_{ab}K_{mn}\\
&=K_{ab}K^{ba}\\
&=K^{ab}K_{ab}.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the second line we have inserted the completeness relations (3.12) and recalled the notation $h^{\alpha\beta}=h^{ab}e^\alpha_ae^\beta_b$. In the third and fourth lines we have used the fact that $n^\alpha n_{\alpha;\beta}=\frac12(n^\alpha n_\alpha)_{;\beta}=0$. In the sixth line we have substituted the definition (3.31) for the extrinsic curvature. Finally, in the last line we have used the fact that $K_{ab}$ is a symmetric three-tensor.

The previous manipulations take care of the first term in our starting expression for the Ricci scalar. The second term is simplified by substituting the Gauss–Codazzi equations (3.39),

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{ab}h^{mn}R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}e^\mu_me^\alpha_ae^\nu_ne^\beta_b
&=h^{ab}h^{mn}\left[R_{manb}+\varepsilon\left(K_{mb}K_{an}-K_{mn}K_{ab}\right)\right]\\
&={}^{3}R+\varepsilon\left(K^{ab}K_{ab}-K^2\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Putting all this together, we arrive at

$$
R={}^{3}R+\varepsilon\left(K^2-K^{ab}K_{ab}\right)+2\varepsilon\left(n^\alpha{}_{;\beta}n^\beta-n^\alpha n^\beta{}_{;\beta}\right)_{;\alpha}. \tag{3.43}
$$

This is the four-dimensional Ricci scalar evaluated on the hypersurface $\Sigma$. This result will be put to good use in Chapter 4.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $R$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-2}}$ | Spacetime Ricci scalar | The scalar curvature of spacetime. |
| $\,{}^{3}R$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-2}}$ | Intrinsic Ricci scalar | The scalar curvature of the hypersurface. |
| $R_{\alpha\beta},R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta},R_{manb}$ | — | Curvature tensors | The spacetime and intrinsic curvature components. |
| $g^{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Inverse spacetime metric | The inverse four-dimensional metric. |
| $h^{ab},h^{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Induced metric | The inverse intrinsic metric and its spacetime projection. |
| $K_{ab}$ | — | Extrinsic curvature | The curvature components of the hypersurface in spacetime. |
| $K$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-1}}$ | Extrinsic-curvature trace | The trace of the extrinsic curvature. |
| $n^\alpha$ | — | Unit normal | The normal direction to the hypersurface. |
