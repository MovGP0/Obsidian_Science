> [!TLDR]
> A divergence-free current gives the same total conserved charge on different spacelike hypersurfaces.

<!-- PDF page 87; printed page 71 -->

![[relativists-toolkit-fig-3-4-spacelike-surfaces.svg|**Fig. 3.4** Two spacelike surfaces and their normal vectors.]]

Gauss' theorem has many useful applications. An example is the following conservation statement.

Suppose that a vector field $j^\alpha$ has a vanishing divergence,

$$
j^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}=0.
$$

Then $\oint_\Sigma j^\alpha\,d\Sigma_\alpha=0$ for any closed hypersurface $\Sigma$. Supposing now that $j^\alpha$ vanishes at spatial infinity, we can choose $\Sigma$ to be composed of two spacelike hypersurfaces, $\Sigma_1$ and $\Sigma_2$, extending all the way to infinity (Fig. 3.4), and of a three-cylinder at infinity, on which $j^\alpha=0$. Then

$$
\int_{\Sigma_1}j^\alpha\,d\Sigma_\alpha
+\int_{\Sigma_2}j^\alpha\,d\Sigma_\alpha=0.
$$

On each of the spacelike hypersurfaces, $d\Sigma_\alpha=-n_\alpha\sqrt h\,d^3y$, where $n_\alpha$ is the *outward* normal to the closed surface $\Sigma$ and $h$ is the determinant of the induced metric on the spacelike hypersurfaces. Letting $n_\alpha\equiv n_{2\alpha}$ on $\Sigma_2$ and $n_\alpha\equiv-n_{1\alpha}$ on $\Sigma_1$, where $n_{1\alpha}$ and $n_{2\alpha}$ are both future directed, we finally obtain

$$
j^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\int_{\Sigma_1}j^\alpha n_{1\alpha}\sqrt h\,d^3y
=\int_{\Sigma_2}j^\alpha n_{2\alpha}\sqrt h\,d^3y.
\tag{3.23}
$$

The interpretation of this result is clear: If $j^\alpha$ is a divergence-free vector, then the total ‘charge’ $\int j^\alpha n_\alpha\,d\Sigma$ is independent of the hypersurface on which it is evaluated. This is obviously a statement of ‘charge’ conservation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $j^\alpha$ | — | Conserved current | The divergence-free current, whose unit depends on the conserved quantity. |
| $d\Sigma_\alpha$ | — | Directed hypersurface element | The oriented boundary element with coordinate-dependent component units. |
| $d\Sigma,\sqrt h\,d^3y$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m^3}}$ | Proper surface volume | The invariant volume element on a spacelike hypersurface. |
| $n_\alpha,n_{1\alpha},n_{2\alpha}$ | — | Hypersurface normals | The outward or future-directed normals with coordinate-dependent component units. |
| $\int j^\alpha n_\alpha\,d\Sigma$ | — | Conserved charge | The total conserved quantity associated with the current. |
