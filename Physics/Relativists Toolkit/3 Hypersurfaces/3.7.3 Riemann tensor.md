> [!TLDR]
> A continuous induced metric gives a valid distributional Riemann tensor, with a possible singular term on the hypersurface.

<!-- Source: PDF page 103, printed page 87. -->

To find the second junction condition requires more work: we must calculate the distribution-valued Riemann tensor. Using the results obtained thus far, we have that the Christoffel symbols are

$$
\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}=\Theta(\mechanical{\ell})\Gamma^{+\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}+\Theta(-\mechanical{\ell})\Gamma^{-\alpha}_{\beta\gamma},
$$

where $\Gamma^{\pm\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}$ are the Christoffel symbols constructed from $g^\pm_{\alpha\beta}$. A straightforward calculation then reveals

$$
\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma,\delta}=\Theta(\mechanical{\ell})\Gamma^{+\alpha}_{\beta\gamma,\delta}+\Theta(-\mechanical{\ell})\Gamma^{-\alpha}_{\beta\gamma,\delta}+\varepsilon\delta(\mechanical{\ell})[\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}]n_\delta,
$$

and from this follows the Riemann tensor:

$$
R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}=\Theta(\mechanical{\ell})R^{+\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}+\Theta(-\mechanical{\ell})R^{-\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}+\delta(\mechanical{\ell})A^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}, \tag{3.50}
$$

where

$$
A^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}=\varepsilon\left([\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\delta}]n_\gamma-[\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}]n_\delta\right). \tag{3.51}
$$

We see that the Riemann tensor is properly defined as a distribution, but the $\delta$-function term represents a curvature singularity at $\Sigma$. Our second junction condition will seek to eliminate this term. Failing this, we will see that a physical interpretation can nevertheless be given to the singularity. This is our next topic.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta},R^{\pm\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}$ | — | Spacetime curvature | The combined curvature and its regular values on both sides. |
| $g^\pm_{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Spacetime metrics | The metrics that give the two connections. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Normal distance or time | The signed geodesic parameter across the surface. |
| $n_\alpha$ | — | Unit normal | The normal direction in the singular curvature term. |
