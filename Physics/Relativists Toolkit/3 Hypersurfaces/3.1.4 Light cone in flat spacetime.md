> [!TLDR]
> The flat-spacetime future light cone has null generators and an induced spherical two-metric.

<!-- PDF page 79; printed page 63 -->

An example of a null hypersurface in flat spacetime is the future light cone of an event $P$, which we place at the origin of a Cartesian coordinate system $x^\alpha$. The defining relation for this hypersurface is $\Phi\equiv\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{r}=0$, where $\mechanical{r}^2=\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2+\mechanical{z}^2$. The normal vector is $\mechanical{k}_\alpha=-\partial_\alpha(\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{r})=(-1,\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{r},\mechanical{y}/\mechanical{r},\mechanical{z}/\mechanical{r})$. A suitable set of parametric equations is $\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{\lambda}$, $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{\lambda}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi}$, $\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{\lambda}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi}$, and $\mechanical{z}=\mechanical{\lambda}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$, in which $y^a=(\mechanical{\lambda},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ are the intrinsic coordinates; $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is an affine parameter on the light cone's null generators, which move with constant values of $\theta^A=(\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$.

From the parametric equations we compute the hypersurface's tangent vectors,

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_\lambda^\alpha
&=\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{\lambda}}
=(1,\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi},\sin\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi},\cos\mechanical{\theta})
=\mechanical{k}^\alpha,\\
e_\theta^\alpha
&=\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{\theta}}
=(0,\mechanical{\lambda}\cos\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi},
\mechanical{\lambda}\cos\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi},-\mechanical{\lambda}\sin\mechanical{\theta}),\\
e_\phi^\alpha
&=\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{\phi}}
=(0,-\mechanical{\lambda}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi},
\mechanical{\lambda}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi},0).
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- PDF page 80; printed page 64 -->

You may check that these vectors are all orthogonal to $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$. Inner products between $e_\theta^\alpha$ and $e_\phi^\alpha$ define the two-metric $\sigma_{AB}$, and we find

$$
\sigma_{AB}\,d\theta^A\,d\theta^B
=\mechanical{\lambda}^2(d\mechanical{\theta}^2+\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\phi}^2).
$$

Not surprisingly, the hypersurface has a spherical geometry, and $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is the areal radius of the two-spheres.

It is easy to check that the unique null vector $N^\alpha$ that satisfies the relations $N_\alpha\mechanical{k}^\alpha=-1$ and $N_\alpha e_A^\alpha=0$ is $N^\alpha=\tfrac12(1,-\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi},-\sin\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi},-\cos\mechanical{\theta})$. You may also verify that the vectors $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$, $N^\alpha$, and $e_A^\alpha$ combine as in Eq. (3.13) to form the inverse Minkowski metric.
