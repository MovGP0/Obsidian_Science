> [!TLDR]
> A moment of time symmetry has vanishing extrinsic curvature and requires zero momentum density on the hypersurface.

<!-- Source: PDF page 97, printed page 81. -->

We notice from the previous example that $K_{ab}=0$ when $\mechanical{\dot a}=0$, that is, the extrinsic curvature vanishes when the scale factor reaches a turning point of its evolution. Because the dynamical history of the scale factor is time-symmetric about the time $\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{t_0}$ at which the turning point occurs, we may call this time a *moment of time symmetry* in the dynamical evolution of the spacetime. Thus, $K_{ab}=0$ at this moment of time symmetry.

Generalizing, we shall call *any* hypersurface $\Sigma$ on which $K_{ab}=0$ a moment of time symmetry in spacetime. Because $K_{ab}$ is essentially the ‘time derivative’ of the metric, a moment of time symmetry corresponds to a turning point of the metric’s evolution, at which its ‘time derivative’ vanishes. The dynamical history of the metric is then ‘time-symmetric’ about $\Sigma$. From Eq. (3.45) we see that a moment of time symmetry can occur only if $\mechanical{j_a}=0$ on that hypersurface.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $K_{ab}$ | — | Extrinsic curvature | The tensor that vanishes at a moment of time symmetry. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | — | Scale factor | The cosmological scale whose time derivative vanishes. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{t_0}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Time | The cosmological time and the time of the turning point. |
| $\mechanical{j_a}$ | — | Momentum density | The density that must vanish on a time-symmetric surface. |
