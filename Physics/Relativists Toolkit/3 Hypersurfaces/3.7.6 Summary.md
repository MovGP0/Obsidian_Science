> [!TLDR]
> Smooth matching requires equal induced metrics and extrinsic curvatures; otherwise the curvature jump describes a thin shell.

<!-- Source: PDF page 105, printed page 89. -->

The junction conditions for a smooth joining of two metrics at a hypersurface $\Sigma$ (assumed not to be null) are

$$
[h_{ab}]=[K_{ab}]=0.
$$

If the extrinsic curvature is not the same on both sides of $\Sigma$, then a thin shell with surface stress-energy tensor

$$
\energy{S_{ab}}=-\frac{\varepsilon}{8\pi}\left([K_{ab}]-[K]h_{ab}\right)
$$

is present at $\Sigma$. The complete stress-energy tensor of the surface layer is given by Eq. (3.57) in the continuous coordinates $x^\alpha$. In the coordinate system $x^\alpha_\pm$ used originally in $\mathcal V^\pm$, it is

<!-- Source: PDF page 106, printed page 90. -->

$$
\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}_\Sigma}=\energy{S^{ab}}\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha_\pm}{\partial y^a}\right)\left(\frac{\partial x^\beta_\pm}{\partial y^b}\right)\delta(\mechanical{\ell}).
$$

This follows from Eq. (3.57) by a simple coordinate transformation from $x^\alpha$ to $x^\alpha_\pm$; such a transformation leaves both $\mechanical{\ell}$ and $\energy{S^{ab}}$ unchanged.

This formulation of the junction conditions is due to Darmois (1927) and Israel (1966). The thin-shell formalism is due to Lanczos (1922 and 1924) and Israel (1966). An extension to null hypersurfaces will be presented in Section 3.11.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $h_{ab}$ | — | Induced metric | The intrinsic metric shared by the two regions. |
| $K_{ab}$ | — | Extrinsic curvature | The tensor that must be continuous for smooth matching. |
| $K$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-1}}$ | Extrinsic-curvature trace | The trace used in the surface tensor. |
| $\energy{S_{ab}}$ | — | Surface stress-energy | The intrinsic matter tensor on the shell. |
| $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}_\Sigma}$ | — | Shell stress-energy | The shell tensor in the original spacetime coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Normal distance or time | The parameter that locates the surface layer. |
