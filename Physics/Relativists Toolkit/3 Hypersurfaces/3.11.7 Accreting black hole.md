> [!TLDR]
> An accreting rotating null shell carries a surface matter current and approaches corotation with its generators at the black-hole horizon.

<!-- Source: PDF page 125, printed page 109. -->

Our second application of the null-shell formalism features a nonrotating black hole of mass $(\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{m})$ which suddenly acquires additional material of mass $\mechanical{m}$ and angular momentum $\mechanical{J}\equiv\mechanical{a}\mechanical{M}$. We suppose that the accretion process is virtually instantaneous, that the material falls in with the speed of light, and that $\mechanical{J}\ll\mechanical{M}^2$. We idealize the accreting material as a singular matter distribution supported on a null hypersurface $\Sigma$.

The spacetime in the future of $\Sigma$ (in $\mathcal V^+$) is that of a slowly rotating black hole of mass $\mechanical{M}$ and (small) angular momentum $\mechanical{a}\mechanical{M}$. We write the metric in $\mathcal V^+$ as in Eq. (3.71),

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_+^2=-f\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+f^{-1}\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2-\frac{4\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{r}}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi},
$$

where $f=1-2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}$; this is the slow-rotation limit of the Kerr metric, and throughout this subsection we will work consistently to first order in the small parameter $\mechanical{a}$.

As seen from $\mathcal V^+$, the null hypersurface $\Sigma$ is described by $\mechanical{v}\equiv\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{r^*}=0$, where $\mechanical{r^*}=\int f^{-1}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r}+2\mechanical{M}\ln(\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}-1)$; you may check that in the slow-rotation limit, every surface $\mechanical{v}=\mathrm{constant}$ is null. It follows that the vector $\mechanical{k}^\alpha=g^{\alpha\beta}(-\partial_\beta\mechanical{v})$ is normal to $\Sigma$ and tangent to its null generators. We have

$$
\mechanical{k}^\alpha\partial_\alpha=\frac1f\partial_t-\partial_r+\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{r}^3f}\partial_\phi,
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 126, printed page 110. -->

and from this expression we deduce four important properties of the generators. First, the generators are affinely parameterized by $\mechanical{\lambda}\equiv-\mechanical{r}$. Second, as measured by inertial observers at infinity, the generators move with an (ever increasing) angular velocity

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{t}}\equiv\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{generators}}}=\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{r}^3}.
$$

Third, $\mechanical{\theta}$ is constant on each generator. And fourth, integration of $\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}/(-\mathrm d\mechanical{r})=2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}/(\mechanical{r}^3f)$ reveals that

$$
\mechanical{\psi}\equiv\mechanical{\phi}+\frac{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{r}}\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{r}}{2\mechanical{M}}\ln f\right)
$$

also is constant on the generators.

We shall use $y^a=(\mechanical{\lambda}\equiv-\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\psi})$ as coordinates on $\Sigma$; as we have just seen, these coordinates are well adapted to the generators, and this property is required by the null-shell formalism. Remembering that $\mathrm d\mechanical{t}=-\mathrm d\mechanical{r}/f$ and $\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}=\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}-(2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}/\mechanical{r}^3f)\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}$ on $\Sigma$, we find that the induced metric is

$$
\sigma_{AB}\mathrm d\theta^A\mathrm d\theta^B=\mechanical{r}^2(\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2+\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}^2),
$$

and that the hypersurface is intrinsically spherical.

The parametric description of $\Sigma$, as seen from $\mathcal V^+$, is $x^\alpha(-\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\psi})$, and from this we form the tangent vectors $e^\alpha_\mechanical{\lambda}=\mechanical{k}^\alpha$, $e^\alpha_\theta=\delta^\alpha_\theta$, and $e^\alpha_\psi=\delta^\alpha_\phi$. The basis is completed by $N_\alpha\mathrm dx^\alpha=\frac12(-f\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}+\mathrm d\mechanical{r})$. From Eq. (3.100) we obtain

$$
C^+_{\mechanical{\lambda}\psi}=\frac{3\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{r}^2}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta},\qquad
C^+_{AB}=\frac{f}{2\mechanical{r}}\sigma_{AB}
$$

for the nonvanishing components of the transverse curvature.

The spacetime in the past of $\Sigma$ (in $\mathcal V^-$) is that of a nonrotating black hole of mass $(\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{m})$. Here we write the metric as

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_-^2=-F\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\bar t}^2+F^{-1}\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2(\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2+\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}^2),
$$

in terms of a distinct time coordinate $\mechanical{\bar t}$ and the angles $\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\mechanical{\psi}$; we also have $F=1-2(\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{m})/\mechanical{r}$. This choice of angular coordinates implies that inertial observers within $\mathcal V^-$ corotate with the shell’s null generators; this is another manifestation of the dragging of inertial frames, a phenomenon already encountered in Section 3.10. As we shall see presently, this choice of coordinates is dictated by continuity of the induced metric at $\Sigma$.

The mathematical description of the hypersurface, as seen from $\mathcal V^-$, is identical to its external description provided that we make the substitutions $\mechanical{t}\to\mechanical{\bar t}$, $\mechanical{\phi}\to\mechanical{\psi}$, $\mechanical{M}\to\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{m}$, and $\mechanical{a}\to0$. According to this, the induced metric on $\Sigma$ is still given by $\mathrm d\mechanical{s}_\Sigma^2=\mechanical{r}^2(\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2+\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}^2)$, as required. The basis vectors are now $\mechanical{k}^\alpha\partial_\alpha=F^{-1}\partial_{\bar t}-\partial_r$, $e^\alpha_\theta\partial_\alpha=\partial_\theta$, $e^\alpha_\psi\partial_\alpha=\partial_\psi$, and $N_\alpha\mathrm dx^\alpha=\frac12(-F\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\bar t}+\mathrm d\mechanical{r})$. This gives us

<!-- Source: PDF page 127, printed page 111. -->

$$
C^-_{AB}=\frac{F}{2\mechanical{r}}\sigma_{AB}
$$

for the nonvanishing components of the transverse curvature.

The transverse curvature is discontinuous at $\Sigma$, and Eqs. (3.101) allow us to compute the shell’s surface quantities. Because the generators are affinely parameterized by $-\mechanical{r}$ on both sides of the shell, we have that $\mechanical{p}=0$ — the shell has a vanishing surface pressure. On the other hand, its surface density is given by

$$
\energy{\mu}=\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi\mechanical{r}^2},
$$

the ratio of the shell’s gravitational mass $\mechanical{m}$ to its (ever decreasing) surface area $4\pi\mechanical{r}^2$. Thus far our results are virtually identical to those obtained in the preceding subsection. What is new in this context is the presence of a surface current $\mechanical{j^A}$, whose sole component is

$$
\mechanical{j^\psi}=\frac{3\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}}{8\pi\mechanical{r}^4}.
$$

This comes from the shell’s rotation, and the fact that the situation is not entirely spherically symmetric.

To better understand the physical significance of the surface current, we express the shell’s surface stress-energy tensor,

$$
\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}=\energy{\mu}\mechanical{k}^\alpha \mechanical{k}^\beta+\mechanical{j^\psi}(\mechanical{k}^\alpha e^\beta_\psi+e^\alpha_\psi \mechanical{k}^\beta),
$$

in terms of the vector $\mechanical{\ell^\alpha}\equiv \mechanical{k}^\alpha+(\mechanical{j^\psi}/\energy{\mu})e^\alpha_\psi$. This vector is null (when we appropriately discard terms of order $\mechanical{a}^2$ in the calculation of $g_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\ell^\alpha}\mechanical{\ell^\beta}$), and it has the components

$$
\mechanical{\ell^\alpha}\partial_\alpha=\frac1f\partial_t-\partial_r+\frac1f\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{fluid}}}\partial_\phi
$$

in the coordinates $x^\alpha=(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ used in $\mathcal V^+$; we have set

$$
\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{fluid}}}\equiv\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{r}^3}+\frac{3\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2}f.
$$

The shell’s surface stress-energy tensor is now given by the simple expression

$$
\energy{S^{\alpha\beta}}=\energy{\mu}\mechanical{\ell^\alpha}\mechanical{\ell^\beta},
$$

which corresponds to a pressureless fluid of density $\energy{\mu}$ moving with a four-velocity $\mechanical{\ell^\alpha}$. We see that the fluid is moving along null curves (not geodesics!) that do not coincide with the shell’s null generators. The motion across generators is created by a mismatch between $\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{fluid}}}$, the fluid’s angular velocity, and $\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{generators}}}$, the angular velocity of the generators. The mismatch is directly related to $\mechanical{j^A}$:

<!-- Source: PDF page 128, printed page 112. -->

$$
\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{relative}}}
=\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{fluid}}}-\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{generators}}}
=\frac{\mechanical{j^\psi}}{\energy{\mu}}
=\frac{3\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2}f.
$$

Notice that the fluid rotates faster than the generators, which share their angular velocity with inertial observers within $\mathcal V^-$; such a phenomenon was encountered before, in the context of the stationary rotating shell of Section 3.10. But notice also that $\mechanical{\Omega_{\mathrm{relative}}}$ decreases to zero as $\mechanical{r}$ approaches $2\mechanical{M}$: The fluid ends up corotating with the generators when the shell crosses the black-hole horizon.
