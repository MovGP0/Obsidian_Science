> [!TLDR]
> The singular part of Einstein's equations represents a thin layer of matter with a surface stress-energy tensor.

<!-- Source: PDF page 103, printed page 87. -->

Although they are constructed from Christoffel symbols, the quantities $A^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}$ form a tensor because the *difference* between two sets of Christoffel symbols is a tensorial quantity (see Section 1.2). We would like to find an explicit expression for this tensor.

The fact that the metric is continuous across $\Sigma$ in the coordinates $x^\alpha$ implies that its tangential derivatives also must be continuous. This means that if $g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}$ is to be discontinuous, the discontinuity must be directed along the normal vector $n^\alpha$. There must therefore exist a tensor field $\kappa_{\alpha\beta}$ such that

$$
[g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}]=\kappa_{\alpha\beta}n_\gamma; \tag{3.52}
$$

this tensor is given explicitly by

$$
\kappa_{\alpha\beta}=\varepsilon[g_{\alpha\beta,\gamma}]n^\gamma. \tag{3.53}
$$

Equation (3.52) implies

$$
[\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}]=\frac12\left(\kappa^\alpha{}_\beta n_\gamma+\kappa^\alpha{}_\gamma n_\beta-\kappa_{\beta\gamma}n^\alpha\right),
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 104, printed page 88. -->

and we obtain

$$
A^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(\kappa^\alpha{}_\delta n_\beta n_\gamma-\kappa^\alpha{}_\gamma n_\beta n_\delta-\kappa_{\beta\delta}n^\alpha n_\gamma+\kappa_{\beta\gamma}n^\alpha n_\delta\right).
$$

This is the $\delta$-function part of the Riemann tensor.

Contracting over the first and third indices gives the $\delta$-function part of the Ricci tensor:

$$
A_{\alpha\beta}\equiv A^\mu{}_{\alpha\mu\beta}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\left(\kappa_{\mu\alpha}n^\mu n_\beta+\kappa_{\mu\beta}n^\mu n_\alpha-\kappa n_\alpha n_\beta-\varepsilon\kappa_{\alpha\beta}\right),
$$

where $\kappa\equiv\kappa^\alpha{}_\alpha$. After an additional contraction we obtain the $\delta$-function part of the Ricci scalar,

$$
A\equiv A^\alpha{}_\alpha=\varepsilon\left(\kappa_{\mu\nu}n^\mu n^\nu-\varepsilon\kappa\right).
$$

With this we form the $\delta$-function part of the Einstein tensor, and after using the Einstein field equations we obtain an expression for the stress-energy tensor:

$$
\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}=\Theta(\mechanical{\ell})\energy{T^+_{\alpha\beta}}+\Theta(-\mechanical{\ell})\energy{T^-_{\alpha\beta}}+\delta(\mechanical{\ell})\energy{S_{\alpha\beta}}, \tag{3.54}
$$

where $8\pi\energy{S_{\alpha\beta}}\equiv A_{\alpha\beta}-\frac12Ag_{\alpha\beta}$. On the right-hand side of Eq. (3.54) the first and second terms represent the stress-energy tensors of regions $\mathcal V^+$ and $\mathcal V^-$, respectively. The $\delta$-function term, on the other hand, comes with a clear interpretation: It is associated with the presence of a thin distribution of matter — a *surface layer*, or a *thin shell* — at $\Sigma$; this thin shell has a surface stress-energy tensor equal to $\energy{S_{\alpha\beta}}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $g_{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Spacetime metric | The continuous metric across the surface. |
| $n^\alpha$ | — | Unit normal | The direction of the jump in the metric derivative. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Normal distance or time | The parameter that locates the matter layer. |
| $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}},\energy{T^\pm_{\alpha\beta}}$ | — | Stress-energy tensors | The combined and regular matter tensors. |
| $\energy{S_{\alpha\beta}}$ | — | Surface stress-energy tensor | The matter tensor concentrated on the thin shell. |
