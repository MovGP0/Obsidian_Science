> [!TLDR]
> For spherical initial data at a moment of time symmetry, the constraint determines the mass function from the density.

<!-- Source: PDF page 98, printed page 82. -->

As a second example, we solve the constraint equations for a spherically symmetric spacetime at a moment of time symmetry. The three-metric can be expressed as

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=\left[1-2\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r})/\mechanical{r}\right]^{-1}\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2,
$$

for some function $\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r})$; to enforce regularity of the metric at $\mechanical{r}=0$ we must impose $\mechanical{m}(0)=\mechanical{m'}(0)=0$, with a prime denoting differentiation with respect to $\mechanical{r}$. For this metric the Ricci scalar is given by $\,{}^3R=4\mechanical{m'}/\mechanical{r}^2$. Because $K_{ab}=0$ at a moment of time symmetry, Eq. (3.44) implies $16\pi\energy{\rho}={}^{3}R$. Solving for $\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r})$ returns

$$
\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r})=\int_0^{\mechanical{r}}4\pi\mechanical{r'}^2\energy{\rho}(\mechanical{r'})\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r'}.
$$

This states, loosely speaking, that $\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{r})$ is the mass-energy contained inside a sphere of radius $\mechanical{r}$, at the selected moment of time symmetry.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Radius | The areal radius of the sphere. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Mass function | The enclosed mass in geometrized units. |
| $\energy{\rho}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-2}}$ | Mass-energy density | The density on the initial spatial surface. |
| $\,{}^3R$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-2}}$ | Intrinsic Ricci scalar | The spatial scalar curvature. |
| $K_{ab}$ | — | Extrinsic curvature | The tensor that vanishes at time symmetry. |
| $\mechanical{s}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Interval | The line element in spacetime or on the hypersurface. |
