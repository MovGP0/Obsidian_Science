> [!TLDR]
> For a spatially flat homogeneous and isotropic cosmology, the initial constraint gives a Friedmann equation.

<!-- Source: PDF page 96, printed page 80. -->

As an example, let us solve the constraint equations for a spatially flat, isotropic, and homogeneous cosmology. To satisfy these requirements the three-metric must take the form

<!-- Source: PDF page 97, printed page 81. -->

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=\mechanical{a}^2(\mathrm dx^2+\mathrm dy^2+\mathrm dz^2),
$$

where $\mechanical{a}$ is the scale factor, which is a constant on the hypersurface. Isotropy and homogeneity also imply $\energy{\rho}=\mathrm{constant}$, $\mechanical{j_a}=0$, and

$$
K_{ab}=\frac13 Kh_{ab},
$$

where $K$ is a constant. The second constraint equation is therefore trivially satisfied. The first one implies

$$
16\pi\energy{\rho}=K^2-K^{ab}K_{ab}=\frac23K^2,
$$

and this provides the complete solution to the initial-value problem.

To recognize the physical meaning of this last equation, we use the fact that in the complete spacetime, $K=n^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}$, where $n^\alpha$ is the unit normal to surfaces of constant $\mechanical{t}$. The full metric is given by the Friedmann–Robertson–Walker form

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+\mechanical{a}^2(\mechanical{t})(\mathrm dx^2+\mathrm dy^2+\mathrm dz^2),
$$

so that $n_\alpha=-\partial_\alpha\mechanical{t}$ and $K=3\mechanical{\dot a}/\mechanical{a}$, where an overdot indicates differentiation with respect to $\mechanical{t}$. The first constraint equation is therefore equivalent to

$$
3(\mechanical{\dot a}/\mechanical{a})^2=8\pi\energy{\rho},
$$

which is one of the Friedmann equations governing the evolution of the scale factor.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | — | Scale factor | The spatial scale; its unit depends on the spatial-coordinate convention. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Time | The cosmological time in geometrized units. |
| $\energy{\rho}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-2}}$ | Energy density | The uniform density on each spatial surface. |
| $\mechanical{j_a}$ | — | Momentum density | The momentum density, which vanishes in this example. |
| $K_{ab}$ | — | Extrinsic curvature | The isotropic extrinsic-curvature tensor. |
| $K$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}^{-1}}$ | Extrinsic-curvature trace | Three times the fractional rate of expansion. |
| $h_{ab}$ | — | Induced metric | The spatial metric. |
| $n^\alpha$ | — | Unit normal | The normal to surfaces of constant cosmological time. |
| $\mechanical{s}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Interval | The line element in spacetime or on the hypersurface. |
