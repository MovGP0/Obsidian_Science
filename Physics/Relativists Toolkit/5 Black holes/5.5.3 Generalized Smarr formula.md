> [!TLDR]
> The generalized Smarr formula separates horizon mass and angular momentum from the contribution of exterior matter.

<!-- Source: PDF page 225, printed page 209. -->

Before moving on to the first law, we generalize Smarr’s formula (Section 5.3.12) that relates the black-hole mass $\mechanical{M}$ to its angular momentum $\mechanical{J}$, angular velocity $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$, surface gravity $\mechanical{\kappa}$, and surface area $\mechanical{A}$. In the present context the black hole is stationary and axially symmetric, but it is not assumed to be a Kerr black hole.

Our starting point is the Komar expressions for total mass and angular momentum (Section 4.3.3):

$$
\mechanical{M}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_S\nabla^\alpha t^\beta\,\mathrm dS_{\alpha\beta},\qquad
\mechanical{J}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\oint_S\nabla^\alpha\phi^\beta\,\mathrm dS_{\alpha\beta},
$$

where the integrations are over a closed two-surface at infinity. We consider a spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma$ extending from the event horizon to spatial infinity (Fig. 5.17). Its inner boundary is $\mathcal H$, a two-dimensional cross section of the event horizon, and its outer boundary is $S$. Using Gauss’ theorem, as was done in Section 4.3.3 (but without the inner boundary), we find that $\mechanical{M}$ and $\mechanical{J}$ can be expressed as

$$
\mechanical{M}=\mechanical{M_H}+2\int_\Sigma\left(\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}-\frac12\energy{T}g_{\alpha\beta}\right)n^\alpha t^\beta\sqrt h\,\mathrm d^3y \tag{5.102}
$$

and

$$
\mechanical{J}=\mechanical{J_H}-\int_\Sigma\left(\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}-\frac12\energy{T}g_{\alpha\beta}\right)n^\alpha\phi^\beta\sqrt h\,\mathrm d^3y, \tag{5.103}
$$

where $\mechanical{M_H}$ and $\mechanical{J_H}$ are the black-hole mass and angular momentum, respectively. They are given by surface integrals over $\mathcal H$:

$$
\mechanical{M_H}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\mathcal H}\nabla^\alpha t^\beta\,\mathrm dS_{\alpha\beta} \tag{5.104}
$$

and

$$
\mechanical{J_H}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\oint_{\mathcal H}\nabla^\alpha\phi^\beta\,\mathrm dS_{\alpha\beta}, \tag{5.105}
$$

where $\mathrm dS_{\alpha\beta}$ is the surface element of Eq. (5.96). The interpretation of Eqs. (5.102) and (5.103) is clear: The total mass $\mechanical{M}$ (angular momentum $\mechanical{J}$) is given by a contribution $\mechanical{M_H}$ ($\mechanical{J_H}$) from the black hole, plus a contribution from the matter distribution outside the hole. If the black hole is in vacuum, then $\mechanical{M}=\mechanical{M_H}$ and $\mechanical{J}=\mechanical{J_H}$.

Smarr’s formula emerges after a few simple steps. Using Eqs. (5.96), (5.104), and (5.105) we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{M_H}-2\mechanical{\Omega_H}\mechanical{J_H}
&=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\mathcal H}\nabla^\alpha(t^\beta+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\phi^\beta)\,\mathrm dS_{\alpha\beta}\\
&=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint_{\mathcal H}\nabla^\alpha\xi^\beta\,\mathrm dS_{\alpha\beta}\\
&=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal H}\xi^{\beta;\alpha}\xi_\alpha N_\beta\,\mathrm d\mechanical{S}\\
&=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal H}\mechanical{\kappa}\xi^\beta N_\beta\,\mathrm d\mechanical{S}\\
&=\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}}{4\pi}\oint_{\mathcal H}\mathrm d\mechanical{S},
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 227, printed page 211. -->

where we have used the relation $\xi^\alpha N_\alpha=-1$ and the fact that $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is constant over $\mathcal H$. The last integration gives the horizon’s surface area and we arrive at

$$
\mechanical{M_H}=2\mechanical{\Omega_H}\mechanical{J_H}+\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{A}}{4\pi}, \tag{5.106}
$$

the generalized Smarr formula.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{M_H}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Total and horizon mass | The total mass and black-hole contribution in geometrized units. |
| $\mechanical{J},\mechanical{J_H}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Total and horizon angular momentum | The total angular momentum and black-hole contribution. |
| $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Horizon angular velocity | The rotation rate in the mass relation. |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Surface gravity | The uniform coefficient of the horizon area. |
| $\mechanical{A},\mathrm d\mechanical{S}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Horizon area and area element | The integrated and differential areas of the horizon cross section. |
| $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}$ | — | Stress-energy tensor | Matter stress components in the coordinate basis. |
| $\energy{T}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-2}}$ | Stress-energy trace | The scalar trace of the matter stress tensor. |
