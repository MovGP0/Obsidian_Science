> [!TLDR]
> The Kerr metric describes a rotating black hole and separates coordinate singularities from true curvature singularities.

<!-- Source: PDF page 203, printed page 187. -->

A solution to the Einstein field equations describing a rotating black hole was discovered by Roy Kerr in 1963. (There is also a solution to the Einstein–Maxwell equations that describes a charged, rotating black hole. It is known as the Kerr–Newman solution, and it is described in Section 5.7, Problem 8.) As we shall see, the Kerr metric can be written in a number of different ways. In the standard Boyer–Lindquist coordinates it is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2
&=-\left(1-\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}}{\rho^2}\right)\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2
-\frac{4\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}\mechanical{r}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}}{\rho^2}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}
+\frac{\Sigma}{\rho^2}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}^2
+\frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\rho^2\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2\\
&=-\frac{\rho^2\Delta}{\Sigma}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2
+\frac{\Sigma}{\rho^2}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}(\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}-\mechanical{\omega}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t})^2
+\frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\rho^2\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2,
\end{aligned} \tag{5.44}
$$

where

$$
\begin{gathered}
\rho^2=\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta},\qquad
\Delta=\mechanical{r}^2-2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}+\mechanical{a}^2,\\
\Sigma=(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)^2-\mechanical{a}^2\Delta\sin^2\mechanical{\theta},\qquad
\mechanical{\omega}\equiv-\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\phi\phi}}=\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}\mechanical{r}}{\Sigma}.
\end{gathered} \tag{5.45}
$$

The Kerr metric is stationary and axially symmetric; it therefore admits the Killing vectors $t^\alpha=\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{t}$ and $\phi^\alpha=\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{\phi}$. It is also asymptotically flat. The Komar formulae (Section 4.3) confirm that $\mechanical{M}$ is the spacetime’s ADM mass, and show that $\mechanical{J}=\mechanical{a}\mechanical{M}$ is the angular momentum (so that $\mechanical{a}$ is the ratio of angular momentum to mass).

The components of the inverse metric are

$$
\begin{gathered}
g^{tt}=-\frac{\Sigma}{\rho^2\Delta},\qquad
g^{t\phi}=-\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}\mechanical{r}}{\rho^2\Delta},\qquad
g^{\phi\phi}=\frac{\Delta-\mechanical{a}^2\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}}{\rho^2\Delta\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}},\\
g^{rr}=\frac{\Delta}{\rho^2},\qquad
g^{\theta\theta}=\frac{1}{\rho^2}.
\end{gathered} \tag{5.46}
$$

The metric and its inverse have singularities at $\Delta=0$ and $\rho^2=0$. To distinguish between coordinate and curvature singularities, we examine the squared Riemann

<!-- Source: PDF page 204, printed page 188. -->

tensor of the Kerr spacetime:

$$
R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
=\frac{48\mechanical{M}^2(\mechanical{r}^2-\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})(\rho^4-16\mechanical{a}^2\mechanical{r}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})}{\rho^{12}}. \tag{5.47}
$$

This reveals that the singularity of the metric at $\Delta=0$ is just a coordinate singularity, but that the Kerr spacetime is truly singular at $\rho^2=0$. The nature of the curvature singularity will be clarified in Section 5.3.8.

Various properties of the Kerr spacetime will be examined in the following subsections. To facilitate this discussion we will introduce three families of observers: zero-angular-momentum observers (ZAMOs), static observers, and stationary observers.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | ADM mass | The total black-hole mass in geometrized units. |
| $\mechanical{J}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Angular momentum | The angular momentum of the black hole. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Specific angular momentum | The ratio of angular momentum to mass. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Frame-dragging angular velocity | The angular velocity defined by the metric components. |
| $\mechanical{s},\mechanical{t},\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Interval, time, and radius | The interval and Boyer–Lindquist length coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{rad}}$ | Angular coordinates | The polar and azimuthal angles. |
| $R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-4}}$ | Curvature invariant | The squared Riemann tensor used to test singularities. |
