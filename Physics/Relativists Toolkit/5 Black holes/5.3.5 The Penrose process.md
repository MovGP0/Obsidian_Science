> [!TLDR]
> The Penrose process extracts rotational energy when the hole absorbs a negative-energy particle from its ergosphere.

<!-- Source: PDF page 207, printed page 191. -->

The fact that $t^\alpha$ is spacelike in the ergosphere – the region $\mechanical{r_+}<\mechanical{r}<\mechanical{r_{\mathrm{sl}}}(\mechanical{\theta})$ of the Kerr spacetime – implies that the (conserved) energy $\energy{E}=-\mechanical{p_\alpha}t^\alpha$ of a particle with four-momentum $\mechanical{p^\alpha}$ can be of either sign. Particles with negative energy can therefore exist in the ergosphere, but they would never be able to escape from this region. (Note that $\energy{E}<0$ refers to the energy that *would* be measured at infinity *if* the particle could be brought there. Any local measurement of the particle’s energy inside the static limit would return a positive value.)

It is easy to elaborate a scenario in which negative-energy particles created in the ergosphere are used to extract positive energy from a Kerr black hole. Imagine that a particle of energy $\energy{E_1}>0$ comes from infinity and enters the ergosphere. There, it decays into two new particles, one with energy $-\energy{E_2}<0$, the other with energy $\energy{E_3}=\energy{E_1}+\energy{E_2}>\energy{E_1}$. While the negative-energy particle remains inside the static limit, the positive-energy particle escapes to infinity where its energy is extracted. Because $\energy{E_3}$ is larger than the energy of the initial particle, the black hole must have given off some of its own energy. This is the Penrose process, by which some of the energy of a rotating black hole can be extracted.

The Penrose process is self-limiting: Only a fraction of the hole’s total energy can be tapped. Suppose that in order to exploit the Penrose process, a rotating

black hole is made to absorb a particle of energy $\energy{E}=-\mechanical{p_\alpha}t^\alpha<0$ and angular momentum $\mechanical{L}=\mechanical{p_\alpha}\phi^\alpha$. Because the Killing vector $\xi^\alpha=t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\phi^\alpha$ is timelike just outside the event horizon, the combination $\energy{E}-\mechanical{\Omega_H}\mechanical{L}=-\mechanical{p_\alpha}\xi^\alpha$ must be positive; otherwise the particle would not be able to penetrate the horizon. Thus $\mechanical{L}<\energy{E}/\mechanical{\Omega_H}$ and $\mechanical{L}$ must be negative if $\energy{E}<0$. The black hole will therefore lose angular momentum during the Penrose process. Eventually the hole’s angular momentum will go to zero, the ergosphere will disappear, and the Penrose process will stop. We might say that only the hole’s *rotational energy* can be extracted by the Penrose process.

Note that in a process by which a black hole absorbs a particle of energy $\energy{E}$ (of either sign) and angular momentum $\mechanical{L}$, its parameters change by amounts $\delta\mechanical{M}=\energy{E}$ and $\delta\mechanical{J}=\mechanical{L}$. Since $\energy{E}-\mechanical{\Omega_H}\mechanical{L}$ must be positive, we have

$$
\delta\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{\Omega_H}\delta\mechanical{J}>0.
$$

As we shall see, this inequality is a direct consequence of the first and second laws of black-hole mechanics.
