> [!TLDR]
> A stationary horizon is a Killing horizon with no matter flux, expansion, or shear along its null generators.

<!-- Source: PDF page 220, printed page 204. -->

It was established by Hawking in 1972 that if a black hole is stationary, then it must be either *static* or *axially symmetric*. This means that the stationary spacetime of a rotating hole is necessarily axially symmetric and that it admits two Killing vectors, $t^\alpha$ and $\phi^\alpha$. Hawking was also able to show that a linear combination of these vectors,

$$
\xi^\alpha=t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\phi^\alpha, \tag{5.88}
$$

is null at the event horizon. Here, $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$ is the hole’s angular velocity, which vanishes if the spacetime is nonrotating (and therefore static). Thus, the event horizon is a Killing horizon and $\xi^\alpha$ is tangent to the horizon’s null generators. These are parameterized by advanced time $\mechanical{v}$, so that a displacement along a generator is described by $\mathrm dx^\alpha=\xi^\alpha\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}$. The hole’s surface gravity $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is then defined by the relation

$$
\xi^\alpha{}_{;\beta}\xi^\beta=\mechanical{\kappa}\xi^\alpha, \tag{5.89}
$$

which holds on the horizon. We will prove in Section 5.5.2 that $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is constant along the horizon’s null generators. (Indeed, it is uniform over the entire horizon.) This means that we can replace $\mechanical{v}$ by an affine parameter $\mechanical{\lambda}=V/\mechanical{\kappa}$, where $V=e^{\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{v}}$ (Section 5.3.10). Then

$$
\mechanical{k^\alpha}=V^{-1}\xi^\alpha \tag{5.90}
$$

satisfies the geodesic equation in affine-parameter form. It follows that if $\mechanical{\kappa}\ne0$ and the horizon is geodesically complete (in the sense that its generators never leave the horizon when followed into the past), then there exists a two-surface, called the *bifurcation two-sphere*, on which $\xi^\alpha=0$.

<!-- Source: PDF page 221, printed page 205. -->

The properties of stationary black holes listed here were all encountered before, during our presentation of the Kerr solution. It should be appreciated, however, that Eqs. (5.88)–(5.90) hold by virtue of the sole fact that the black hole is stationary; these results do not depend on the specific details of a particular metric.

The observation that a stationary black hole must be axially symmetric if it is rotating might seem puzzling. After all, it should be possible to place a nonsymmetrical distribution of matter outside the hole, and let it tidally distort the event horizon in a nonsymmetrical manner. This, presumably, would produce a black hole that is still stationary and rotating, but not axially symmetric. Hawking and Hartle (1972) have shown that this, in fact, is false! The reason is that such a distribution of matter would impart a torque on the black hole, which would force it to spin down to a nonrotating (and static) configuration. Thus, such a situation would not leave the black hole stationary.

Additional properties of stationary black holes can be inferred from Raychaudhuri’s equation (Section 2.4.4),

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda}}
=-\frac12\mechanical{\theta}^2-\mechanical{\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}
-R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k^\alpha}\mechanical{k^\beta}, \tag{5.91}
$$

in which we have put $\mechanical{\omega_{\alpha\beta}}\equiv0$ to reflect the fact that the congruence of null generators is necessarily hypersurface orthogonal. The event horizon will be stationary if $\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}/\mathrm d\mechanical{\lambda}$ are both zero. Using the Einstein field equations and the null energy condition, Eq. (5.91) implies that the stress-energy tensor must satisfy

$$
\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\xi^\alpha\xi^\beta=0 \tag{5.92}
$$

on the horizon. This means that matter cannot be flowing across the event horizon; if it were, the generators would get focused and the black hole would not be stationary. Raychaudhuri’s equation also implies

$$
\mechanical{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}=0; \tag{5.93}
$$

the null generators of the event horizon have a vanishing shear tensor.
