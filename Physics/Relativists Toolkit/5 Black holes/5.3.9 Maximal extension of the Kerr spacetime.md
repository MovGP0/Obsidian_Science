> [!TLDR]
> The exact Kerr solution extends through both horizons and through the regular rotation-axis section at r = 0.

<!-- Source: PDF page 212, printed page 196. -->

We have already constructed coordinate systems that allow the continuation of the Kerr metric across the event horizon. We now complete the discussion and show how the spacetime can also be extended beyond the inner horizon. For simplicity we shall work with the two-dimensional section of the Kerr spacetime obtained by setting $\mechanical{\theta}=0$. This is the rotation axis, and because the Kerr metric is not spherically symmetric, this does represent a loss of generality.

Going back to Eq. (5.44) and the original Boyer–Lindquist coordinates, we find that when $\mechanical{\theta}=0$, the Kerr metric reduces to

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2&=-\left(1-\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}\right)\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2
+\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2\\
&=-\frac{\Delta}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}
\left(\mathrm d\mechanical{t}-\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}\right)
\left(\mathrm d\mechanical{t}+\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

or

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-f\,\mathrm d\mechanical{u}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}, \tag{5.72}
$$

where $\mechanical{u}=\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{r^*}$ and $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{r^*}$ are the coordinates of Section 5.3.6. Here,

$$
f=\frac{\Delta}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}
=\frac{(\mechanical{r}-\mechanical{r_+})(\mechanical{r}-\mechanical{r_-})}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}, \tag{5.73}
$$

and $\mechanical{r_\pm}=\mechanical{M}\pm\sqrt{\mechanical{M}^2-\mechanical{a}^2}$ denote the positions of the outer and inner horizons, respectively. The metric of Eq. (5.72) is extremely simple, and the construction of Kruskal coordinates for the $\mechanical{\theta}=0$ section of the Kerr spacetime proceeds just as for the Reissner–Nordström (RN) black hole (Section 5.2.2).

**Fig. 5.13** Kruskal patches for the Kerr spacetime.

We first consider the continuation of the metric across the event horizon. Near $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_+}$ Eq. (5.73) can be approximated by

$$
f\simeq2\mechanical{\kappa_+}(\mechanical{r}-\mechanical{r_+}),
$$

where $\mechanical{\kappa_+}=\frac12f'(\mechanical{r_+})$. It follows that

$$
\mechanical{r^*}=\int\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{r}}{f}\simeq\frac{1}{2\mechanical{\kappa_+}}\ln\lvert\mechanical{\kappa_+}(\mechanical{r}-\mechanical{r_+})\rvert
$$

and $f\simeq\pm2e^{2\mechanical{\kappa_+}\mechanical{r^*}}=\pm2e^{\mechanical{\kappa_+}(\mechanical{v}-\mechanical{u})}$; the upper sign refers to $\mechanical{r}>\mechanical{r_+}$ and the lower sign to $\mechanical{r}<\mechanical{r_+}$. Introducing the new coordinates

$$
U_+=\mp e^{-\mechanical{\kappa_+}\mechanical{u}},\qquad V_+=e^{\mechanical{\kappa_+}\mechanical{v}}, \tag{5.74}
$$

we find that near $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_+}$ the Kerr metric admits the manifestly regular form $\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2\simeq-2\mechanical{\kappa_+}^{-2}\,\mathrm dU_+\,\mathrm dV_+$.

Just as for the RN spacetime, the coordinates $U_+$ and $V_+$ are singular at the inner horizon, and another coordinate patch is required to extend the Kerr metric beyond this horizon (Fig. 5.13). The procedure is now familiar. Near $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_-}$ we approximate Eq. (5.73) by $f\simeq-2\mechanical{\kappa_-}(\mechanical{r}-\mechanical{r_-})$, where $\mechanical{\kappa_-}=\frac12\lvert f'(\mechanical{r_-})\rvert$, so that $f\simeq\mp2e^{-2\mechanical{\kappa_-}\mechanical{r^*}}=\mp2e^{\mechanical{\kappa_-}(\mechanical{u}-\mechanical{v})}$. The appropriate coordinate transformation is now

$$
U_-=\mp e^{\mechanical{\kappa_-}\mechanical{u}},\qquad V_+=-e^{-\mechanical{\kappa_-}\mechanical{v}}, \tag{5.75}
$$

and the metric becomes $\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2\simeq-2\mechanical{\kappa_-}^{-2}\,\mathrm dU_-\,\mathrm dV_-$.

Just as for the RN spacetime, another copy of the outer horizon presents itself in the future of the inner horizon, and another Kruskal patch is required to extend the spacetime beyond this new horizon. There is more, however: It is easy to check that in a spacetime diagram based on the $(U_-,V_-)$ coordinates, the surface $\mechanical{r}=0$ is represented by $U_-V_-=-1$. This is a timelike surface, and on the rotation axis this surface is *nonsingular*. The Kerr spacetime can therefore be extended beyond $\mechanical{r}=0$, into a region in which $\mechanical{r}$ adopts negative values. This new region has no analogue in the RN spacetime; it contains no horizons and it becomes flat in the limit $\mechanical{r}\to-\infty$. Observers in this region interpret the Kerr metric as describing the gravitational field of a (naked) ring singularity. You should be able to convince yourself that this singularity has a *negative* mass.

The maximally extended Kerr spacetime can be represented by a Penrose–Carter diagram (Fig. 5.14). The resulting causal structure is extremely complex. It should be kept in mind, however, that the interior of a Kerr black hole is subject to the same instability as that of a RN black hole (see Section 5.2.3 and Section 5.7, Problem 3). The tunnels to other universes, and the regions of negative $\mechanical{r}$, are not present inside physically realistic black holes.
