> [!TLDR]
> Compactified Kruskal coordinates produce Penrose–Carter diagrams that show the causal structure and the different boundaries at infinity.

<!-- Source: PDF page 184, printed page 168. -->

The double-null Kruskal coordinates make the causal structure of the Schwarzschild spacetime very clear, and this is their main advantage. Another useful set of double-null coordinates is obtained by applying the transformation

$$
\mechanical{\tilde U}=\arctan\mechanical{U},\qquad
\mechanical{\tilde V}=\arctan\mechanical{V}. \tag{5.13}
$$

**Fig. 5.4** Compactified coordinates for the Schwarzschild spacetime.

This rescaling of the null coordinates does not affect the appearance of radial light rays, which still propagate at 45 degrees in a spacetime diagram based on the new coordinates (Fig. 5.4). However, while the range of the initial coordinates was infinite (for example, $-\infty<\mechanical{U}<\infty$), it is finite for the new coordinates (for example, $-\pi/2<\mechanical{\tilde U}<\pi/2$). The entire spacetime is therefore mapped onto a finite domain of the $\mechanical{\tilde U}$–$\mechanical{\tilde V}$ plane. This compactification of the manifold introduces bad coordinate singularities at the boundaries of the new coordinate system, but these are of no concern when the purpose is simply to construct a compact map of the entire spacetime.

In the new coordinates the surfaces $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$ are located at $\mechanical{\tilde U}=0$ and $\mechanical{\tilde V}=0$, and the singularities at $\mechanical{r}=0$, or $\mechanical{U}\mechanical{V}=1$, are now at $\mechanical{\tilde U}+\mechanical{\tilde V}=\pm\pi/2$. The spacetime is also bounded by the surfaces $\mechanical{\tilde U}=\pm\pi/2$ and $\mechanical{\tilde V}=\pm\pi/2$. The four points $(\mechanical{\tilde U},\mechanical{\tilde V})=(\pm\pi/2,\pm\pi/2)$ are singularities of the coordinate transformation: In the actual spacetime the surfaces $\mechanical{U}=0$, $\mechanical{U}=\infty$, and $\mechanical{U}\mechanical{V}=1$ never meet.

It is useful to assign names to the various boundaries of the compactified spacetime (Fig. 5.5). The surfaces $\mechanical{\tilde U}=\pi/2$ and $\mechanical{\tilde V}=\pi/2$ are called *future null infinity* and are labelled $\mathscr I^+$ (pronounced ‘scri plus’). The diagram makes it clear that $\mathscr I^+$ contains the future endpoints of all outgoing null geodesics (those along which $\mechanical{r}$ increases). Similarly, the surfaces $\mechanical{\tilde U}=-\pi/2$ and $\mechanical{\tilde V}=-\pi/2$ are called *past null infinity* and are labelled $\mathscr I^-$. These contain the past endpoints of all ingoing null geodesics (those along which $\mechanical{r}$ decreases). The points at which $\mathscr I^+$ and $\mathscr I^-$ meet are called *spacelike infinity* and are labelled $i^0$. These contain the endpoints of all spacelike geodesics. The points $(\mechanical{\tilde U},\mechanical{\tilde V})=(0,\pi/2)$ and $(\mechanical{\tilde U},\mechanical{\tilde V})=(\pi/2,0)$ are called *future timelike infinity* and are labelled $i^+$. These contain the future endpoints of all timelike geodesics that do not terminate at $\mechanical{r}=0$. Finally, the points $(\mechanical{\tilde U},\mechanical{\tilde V})=(0,-\pi/2)$ and $(\mechanical{\tilde U},\mechanical{\tilde V})=(-\pi/2,0)$ are called *past timelike infinity* and are labelled $i^-$. These contain the past endpoints of all timelike geodesics that do not originate at $\mechanical{r}=0$. Table 5.1 provides a summary of these definitions.

**Table 5.1** *Boundaries of the compactified Schwarzschild spacetime.*

| Label | Name | Definition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathscr I^+$ | Future null infinity | $\mechanical{v}=\infty$, $\mechanical{u}$ finite |
| $\mathscr I^-$ | Past null infinity | $\mechanical{u}=-\infty$, $\mechanical{v}$ finite |
| $i^0$ | Spatial infinity | $\mechanical{r}=\infty$, $\mechanical{t}$ finite |
| $i^+$ | Future timelike infinity | $\mechanical{t}=\infty$, $\mechanical{r}$ finite |
| $i^-$ | Past timelike infinity | $\mechanical{t}=-\infty$, $\mechanical{r}$ finite |

**Fig. 5.5** Penrose–Carter diagram of the Schwarzschild spacetime.

Compactified maps such as the one displayed in Fig. 5.5 are called *Penrose–Carter diagrams*. They display, at a glance, the complete causal structure of the spacetime under consideration. They make a very useful tool in general relativity.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{U},\mechanical{V},\mechanical{\tilde U},\mechanical{\tilde V}$ | $1$ | Null coordinates | The original and compactified Kruskal coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Mass | The Schwarzschild black-hole mass. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Radius | The spherical areal radius used to locate horizons and singularities. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{u},\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Time coordinates | The Schwarzschild, retarded, and advanced times in the boundary definitions. |
