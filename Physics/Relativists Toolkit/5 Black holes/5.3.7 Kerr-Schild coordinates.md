> [!TLDR]
> Kerr–Schild coordinates express the Kerr metric as a flat metric plus a term built from an ingoing null vector.

<!-- Source: PDF page 210, printed page 194. -->

Another useful set of coordinates for the Kerr metric is $(\mechanical{t'},\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z})$, the pseudo-Lorentzian *Kerr–Schild* coordinates in terms of which the metric takes a particularly interesting form. These are constructed as follows.

We start with Eq. (5.65) and separate out the terms that are proportional to $\mechanical{M}$. After some algebra we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2={}&-\mathrm d\mechanical{v}^2+2\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}
-2\mechanical{a}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}
+(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}^2
+\rho^2\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2\\
&+\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}}{\rho^2}(\mathrm d\mechanical{v}-\mechanical{a}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi})^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The terms that do not involve $\mechanical{M}$ have a simple interpretation: They give the metric of flat spacetime in an unfamiliar coordinate system. The rest of the line element can be written neatly in terms of $\mechanical{l_\alpha}$: Recalling that $\mechanical{l^r}=-1$ is the only nonvanishing component of $\mechanical{l^\alpha}$, we find that

$$
-\mechanical{l_\alpha}\mathrm dx^\alpha=\mathrm d\mechanical{v}-\mechanical{a}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 211, printed page 195. -->

and the line element becomes

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=(\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2)_{\mathrm{flat}}+\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}}{\rho^2}(\mechanical{l_\alpha}\mathrm dx^\alpha)^2. \tag{5.66}
$$

The Kerr metric can therefore be expressed as

$$
g_{\alpha\beta}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}}{\rho^2}\mechanical{l_\alpha}\mechanical{l_\beta}, \tag{5.67}
$$

where $\eta_{\alpha\beta}$ is the metric of flat spacetime in the coordinates $(\mechanical{v},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\psi})$.

Equation (5.67) gives the Kerr metric in a rather attractive form. Any metric that can be written as $g_{\alpha\beta}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}+H\mechanical{l_\alpha}\mechanical{l_\beta}$, where $H$ is a scalar function and $\mechanical{l_\alpha}$ a null vector field, is known as a *Kerr–Schild* metric. It is by adopting such an expression that Kerr discovered his solution in 1963. (Some general aspects of the Kerr–Schild decomposition are worked out in Section 5.7, Problem 5.)

The next order of business is to find the coordinate transformation that brings $\eta_{\alpha\beta}$ to the standard Minkowski form. The answer is

$$
\mechanical{x}+i\mechanical{y}=(\mechanical{r}+i\mechanical{a})\sin\mechanical{\theta}\,e^{i\mechanical{\psi}},\qquad
\mechanical{z}=\mechanical{r}\cos\mechanical{\theta},\qquad
\mechanical{t'}=\mechanical{v}-\mechanical{r}. \tag{5.68}
$$

Going through the necessary algebra does indeed reveal that in these coordinates,

$$
(\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2)_{\mathrm{flat}}=-\mathrm d\mechanical{t'}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{x}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{y}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{z}^2. \tag{5.69}
$$

It is easy to work out the components of $\mechanical{l_\alpha}$ in this coordinate system. Because the null geodesics move with constant values of $\mechanical{v}$, $\mechanical{\theta}$, and $\mechanical{\psi}$, we have that $\mechanical{\dot x}+i\mechanical{\dot y}=-\sin\mechanical{\theta}\,e^{i\mechanical{\psi}}$, $\mechanical{\dot z}=-\cos\mechanical{\theta}$, and $\mechanical{\dot t'}=1$, where we have used $\mechanical{\dot r}=-1$. Lowering the indices is a trivial matter (see Section 5.7, Problem 5), and expressing the right-hand sides in terms of the new coordinates gives

$$
-\mechanical{l_\alpha}\mathrm dx^\alpha=\mathrm d\mechanical{t'}
+\frac{\mechanical{r}\mechanical{x}+\mechanical{a}\mechanical{y}}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{x}
+\frac{\mechanical{r}\mechanical{y}-\mechanical{a}\mechanical{x}}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{y}
+\frac{\mechanical{z}}{\mechanical{r}}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{z}. \tag{5.70}
$$

The quantity $\mechanical{r}$ must now be expressed in terms of $\mechanical{x}$, $\mechanical{y}$, and $\mechanical{z}$. Starting with $\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2=(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}$, it is easy to show that

$$
\mechanical{r}^4-(\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2+\mechanical{z}^2-\mechanical{a}^2)\mechanical{r}^2-\mechanical{a}^2\mechanical{z}^2=0, \tag{5.71}
$$

which may be solved for $\mechanical{r}(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z})$. Equations (5.66), (5.69)–(5.71) give the explicit form of the Kerr metric in the Kerr–Schild coordinates.
