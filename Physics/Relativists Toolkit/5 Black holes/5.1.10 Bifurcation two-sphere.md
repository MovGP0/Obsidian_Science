> [!TLDR]
> At the bifurcation two-sphere of the maximally extended Schwarzschild spacetime, the stationary Killing vector vanishes.

<!-- Source: PDF page 192, printed page 176. -->

The point $(\mechanical{U},\mechanical{V})=(0,0)$ in a Kruskal diagram, at which the past and future horizons intersect, represents the *bifurcation two-sphere* of the Schwarzschild spacetime. This two-surface is characterized by the fact that the Killing vector $t^\alpha=\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{t}$ vanishes there. To recognize this we need to work out the components of this vector in Kruskal coordinates. From Eqs. (5.5) and (5.7) we get the relation $e^{\mechanical{t}/2\mechanical{M}}=-\mechanical{V}/\mechanical{U}$, and after using Eq. (5.8) we obtain

$$
\mechanical{U}^2=e^{(\mechanical{r}-\mechanical{t})/2\mechanical{M}}\left(\frac{\mechanical{r}}{2\mechanical{M}}-1\right),\qquad
\mechanical{V}^2=e^{(\mechanical{r}+\mechanical{t})/2\mechanical{M}}\left(\frac{\mechanical{r}}{2\mechanical{M}}-1\right).
$$

Taking partial derivatives with respect to $\mechanical{t}$, we arrive at

$$
t^U=-\frac{\mechanical{U}}{4\mechanical{M}},\qquad
t^V=\frac{\mechanical{V}}{4\mechanical{M}}. \tag{5.19}
$$

It follows immediately that $t^\alpha=0$ at the bifurcation two-sphere. It should be noted that the bifurcation two-sphere exists only in the maximally extended Schwarzschild spacetime. If the black hole is the result of gravitational collapse, then the bifurcation two-sphere is not part of the actual spacetime.

According to our previous calculation, $t^V$ is the only nonvanishing component of the Killing vector on the future horizon. This implies that $t_\alpha\propto-\partial_\alpha\mechanical{U}$ at $\mechanical{U}=0$, and we have the important result that $t^\alpha$ is tangent to the null generators of the event horizon. This was to be expected from the fact that the event horizon of the Schwarzschild spacetime is also a Killing horizon.
