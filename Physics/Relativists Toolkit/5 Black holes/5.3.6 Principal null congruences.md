> [!TLDR]
> Principal null geodesics define ingoing and outgoing Kerr coordinates that remain regular on their respective horizons.

<!-- Source: PDF page 208, printed page 192. -->

The Boyer–Lindquist coordinates, like the Schwarzschild coordinates, are singular at the event horizon: While a trip down to the event horizon requires a finite proper time, the interval of coordinate time $\mechanical{t}$ is infinite. Moreover, because the angular velocity $\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}/\mathrm d\mechanical{t}$ tends to a finite limit at the horizon, $\mechanical{\phi}$ also increases by an infinite amount. We therefore need another coordinate system to extend the Kerr metric beyond the event horizon. It is advantageous to tailor these new coordinates to the behaviour of null geodesics. The two congruences considered here (which are known as the *principal null congruences* of the Kerr spacetime) are especially simple to deal with; we will use them to construct new coordinates for the Kerr metric.

It is a remarkable feature of the Kerr metric that the equations for geodesic motion can be expressed in a decoupled, first-order form. These equations involve three constants of the motion: the energy parameter $\energy{\tilde E}$, the angular-momentum parameter $\mechanical{\tilde L}$, and the ‘Carter constant’ $\mechanical{\mathcal Q}$. (This last constant appears because of the existence of a Killing tensor. This is explained in Section 5.7, Problem 4, which also provides a derivation of the geodesic equations.) For null geodesics the equations are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho^2\mechanical{\dot t}&=-\mechanical{a}(\mechanical{a}\energy{\tilde E}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}-\mechanical{\tilde L})+(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)P/\Delta,\\
\rho^2\mechanical{\dot r}&=\pm\sqrt R,\\
\rho^2\mechanical{\dot\theta}&=\pm\sqrt\Theta,\\
\rho^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}&=-(\mechanical{a}\energy{\tilde E}-\mechanical{\tilde L}/\sin^2\mechanical{\theta})+\mechanical{a}P/\Delta,
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 209, printed page 193. -->

in which an overdot indicates differentiation with respect to an affine parameter $\mechanical{\lambda}$, and

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&=\energy{\tilde E}(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)-\mechanical{a}\mechanical{\tilde L},\\
R&=P^2-\Delta[(\mechanical{\tilde L}-\mechanical{a}\energy{\tilde E})^2+\mechanical{\mathcal Q}],\\
\Theta&=\mechanical{\mathcal Q}+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}(\mechanical{a}^2\energy{\tilde E}^2-\mechanical{\tilde L}^2/\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}).
\end{aligned}
$$

We simplify these equations by making the following choices:

$$
\mechanical{\tilde L}=\mechanical{a}\energy{\tilde E}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta},\qquad
\mechanical{\mathcal Q}=-(\mechanical{\tilde L}-\mechanical{a}\energy{\tilde E})^2=-(\mechanical{a}\energy{\tilde E}\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})^2.
$$

It is easy to check that these imply $\Theta=0$, so that our geodesics move with a constant value of $\mechanical{\theta}$. We also have $P=\energy{\tilde E}\rho^2$ and $R=(\energy{\tilde E}\rho^2)^2$, which give

$$
\mechanical{\dot t}=\energy{\tilde E}(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)/\Delta,\qquad
\mechanical{\dot r}=\pm\energy{\tilde E},\qquad
\mechanical{\dot\theta}=0,\qquad
\mechanical{\dot\phi}=\mechanical{a}\energy{\tilde E}/\Delta.
$$

The constant $\energy{\tilde E}$ can be absorbed into the affine parameter $\mechanical{\lambda}$. We obtain an *ingoing* congruence by choosing the negative sign for $\mechanical{\dot r}$, and we shall use $\mechanical{l^\alpha}$ to denote its tangent vector field:

$$
\mechanical{l^\alpha}\partial_\alpha=\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\Delta}\partial_t-\partial_r+\frac{\mechanical{a}}{\Delta}\partial_\phi. \tag{5.59}
$$

Choosing instead the positive sign gives an *outgoing* congruence, with

$$
\mechanical{k^\alpha}\partial_\alpha=\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\Delta}\partial_t+\partial_r+\frac{\mechanical{a}}{\Delta}\partial_\phi \tag{5.60}
$$

as its tangent vector field.

To give the simplest description of the *ingoing* congruence, we introduce new coordinates $\mechanical{v}$ and $\mechanical{\psi}$ defined by

$$
\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{r^*},\qquad
\mechanical{\psi}=\mechanical{\phi}+r^\sharp, \tag{5.61}
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{r^*}&\equiv\int\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}\\
&=\mechanical{r}+\frac{\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r_+}}{\sqrt{\mechanical{M}^2-\mechanical{a}^2}}\ln\left\lvert\frac{\mechanical{r}}{\mechanical{r_+}}-1\right\rvert
-\frac{\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r_-}}{\sqrt{\mechanical{M}^2-\mechanical{a}^2}}\ln\left\lvert\frac{\mechanical{r}}{\mechanical{r_-}}-1\right\rvert
\end{aligned} \tag{5.62}
$$

and

$$
r^\sharp\equiv\int\frac{\mechanical{a}}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}
=\frac{\mechanical{a}}{2\sqrt{\mechanical{M}^2-\mechanical{a}^2}}\ln\left\lvert\frac{\mechanical{r}-\mechanical{r_+}}{\mechanical{r}-\mechanical{r_-}}\right\rvert. \tag{5.63}
$$

It is easy to check that in these coordinates, $\mechanical{l^r}=-1$ is the only nonvanishing component of the tangent vector. This means that $\mechanical{v}$ and $\mechanical{\psi}$ (as well as $\mechanical{\theta}$) are constant on each of the ingoing null geodesics, and that $-\mechanical{r}$ is the affine parameter.

The simplest description of the *outgoing* congruence is provided by the coordinates $(\mechanical{u},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\chi})$, where

$$
\mechanical{u}=\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{r^*},\qquad
\mechanical{\chi}=\mechanical{\phi}-r^\sharp. \tag{5.64}
$$

In these coordinates $\mechanical{k^r}=+1$ is the only nonvanishing component of the tangent vector. This shows that $\mechanical{u}$ and $\mechanical{\chi}$ (as well as $\mechanical{\theta}$) are constant along the outgoing null geodesics, and that $\mechanical{r}$ is the affine parameter.

The Kerr metric can be expressed in either one of these new coordinate systems. While the coordinates $(\mechanical{v},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\psi})$ are well behaved on the future horizon but singular on the past horizon, the coordinates $(\mechanical{u},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\chi})$ are well behaved on the past horizon but singular on the future horizon. For example, a straightforward computation reveals that after a transformation to the ingoing coordinates, the Kerr metric becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2={}&-\left(1-\frac{2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}}{\rho^2}\right)\mathrm d\mechanical{v}^2
+2\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}
-2\mechanical{a}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}\\
&-\frac{4\mechanical{M}\mechanical{a}\mechanical{r}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}}{\rho^2}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}
+\frac{\Sigma}{\rho^2}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}^2
+\rho^2\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2.
\end{aligned} \tag{5.65}
$$

These coordinates produce an extension of the Kerr metric across the future horizon. Several coordinate patches, both ingoing and outgoing, are required to cover the entire Kerr spacetime, whose causal structure is very similar to that of the Reissner–Nordström spacetime. We shall return to this topic in Section 5.3.9.

| $\mechanical{\mathcal Q}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Carter constant | The conserved quantity from the Kerr Killing tensor. |
| $\mechanical{\dot t},\mechanical{\dot r}$ | $1$ | Time and radial rates | The length-coordinate derivatives with respect to the affine parameter. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\theta},\mechanical{\dot\phi}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Angular rates | The angular derivatives with respect to the affine parameter. |
| $\mechanical{l^\alpha},\mechanical{k^\alpha}$ | — | Principal null tangents | The ingoing and outgoing tangent components in the coordinate basis. |
