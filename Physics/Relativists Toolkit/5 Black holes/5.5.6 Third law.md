> [!TLDR]
> Under the stated energy conditions, a black hole cannot reach zero surface gravity in finite advanced time.

<!-- Source: PDF page 229, printed page 213. -->

The third law of black-hole mechanics states that if the stress-energy tensor is bounded and satisfies the weak energy condition, then *the surface gravity of a black hole cannot be reduced to zero within a finite advanced time*. A precise formulation of this law was given by Werner Israel in 1986.

We have seen that a black hole of zero surface gravity is an extreme black hole. (Recall that a Kerr black hole is extremal if $\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{M}$; for a Reissner–Nordström black hole the condition is $\lvert\electric{Q}\rvert=\mechanical{M}$.) An equivalent statement of the third law is therefore that under the stated conditions on the stress-energy tensor, it is impossible for a black hole to become extremal within a finite advanced time.

The proof of the third law is rather involved and we will not attempt to go through it here. Instead of presenting a proof, we will illustrate the fact that the third law is essentially a consequence of the weak energy condition.

For the purpose of this discussion we need a black-hole spacetime which is sufficiently dynamical that it has the potential of becoming extremal at a finite advanced time $\mechanical{v}$. A simple choice is the charged generalization of the ingoing Vaidya spacetime, whose metric is given by

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-f\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}^2+2\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2, \tag{5.110}
$$

with

$$
f=1-\frac{2\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{v})}{\mechanical{r}}+\frac{\electric{q}^2(\mechanical{v})}{\mechanical{r}^2}. \tag{5.111}
$$

This metric describes a black hole whose mass $\mechanical{m}$ and charge $\electric{q}$ change with time because of irradiation by charged null dust, a fictitious form of matter. This interpretation is confirmed by inspection of the stress-energy tensor,

$$
\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}=\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathrm{dust}}}+\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathrm{em}}}, \tag{5.112}
$$

where

$$
\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathrm{dust}}}=\energy{\rho}\mechanical{l^\alpha}\mechanical{l^\beta},\qquad
\energy{\rho}=\frac{1}{4\pi\mechanical{r}^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{v}}
\left(\mechanical{m}-\frac{\electric{q}^2}{2\mechanical{r}}\right) \tag{5.113}
$$

is the contribution from the null dust ($\mechanical{l_\alpha}=-\partial_\alpha\mechanical{v}$ is a null vector), and

$$
\energy{T^\alpha_{\mathrm{em}\,\beta}}=\energy{P}\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1,1),\qquad
\energy{P}=\frac{\electric{q}^2}{8\pi\mechanical{r}^4} \tag{5.114}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 230, printed page 214. -->

is the contribution from the electromagnetic field. The spacetime of Eqs. (5.110) and (5.111) will produce a violation of the third law if $\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{v_0})=\electric{q}(\mechanical{v_0})$ for some advanced time $\mechanical{v_0}<\infty$.

An essential aspect of this discussion is the weak energy condition (Section 2.1), which states that the energy density measured by an observer with four-velocity $\mechanical{u^\alpha}$ will always be positive:

$$
\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{u^\alpha}\mechanical{u^\beta}>0.
$$

Here, $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}$ is the stress-energy tensor of Eq. (5.112). If our observer is restricted to move in the radial direction only, then $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{u^\alpha}\mechanical{u^\beta}=\energy{\rho}(\mathrm d\mechanical{v}/\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau})^2+\energy{P}$. Because $\mathrm d\mechanical{v}/\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau}$ can be arbitrarily large, the weak energy condition requires $\energy{\rho}>0$. In particular $\energy{\rho}$ must be positive at the apparent horizon, $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_+}(\mechanical{v})$, where $\mechanical{r_+}=\mechanical{m}+(\mechanical{m}^2-\electric{q}^2)^{1/2}$. This gives us the following condition:

$$
4\pi\mechanical{r_+}^3\energy{\rho}(\mechanical{r_+})
=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot m}-\electric{q}\electric{\dot q}
+(\mechanical{m}^2-\electric{q}^2)^{1/2}\mechanical{\dot m}>0, \tag{5.115}
$$

where an overdot indicates differentiation with respect to $\mechanical{v}$.

Let us imagine a situation in which the black hole becomes extremal at a finite advanced time $\mechanical{v_0}$. This means that $\Delta(\mechanical{v_0})=0$, where $\Delta(\mechanical{v})\equiv\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{v})-\electric{q}(\mechanical{v})$. Because the black hole was not extremal before $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{v_0}$, we have that $\Delta(\mechanical{v})>0$ for $\mechanical{v}<\mechanical{v_0}$ and $\Delta(\mechanical{v})$ must be *decreasing* as $\mechanical{v}$ approaches $\mechanical{v_0}$. However, Eq. (5.115) implies

$$
\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{v_0})\dot\Delta(\mechanical{v_0})>0,
$$

according to which $\Delta(\mechanical{v})$ must be *increasing*. We have a contradiction and we conclude that the weak energy condition prevents the black hole from ever becoming extremal at a finite advanced time.
