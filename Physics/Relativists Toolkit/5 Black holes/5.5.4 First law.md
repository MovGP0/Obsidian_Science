> [!TLDR]
> The first law follows from matter flux through the horizon and the linearized Raychaudhuri equation.

<!-- Source: PDF page 227, printed page 211. -->

We consider a quasi-static process during which a stationary black hole of mass $\mechanical{M}$, angular momentum $\mechanical{J}$, and surface area $\mechanical{A}$ is taken to a new stationary black hole with parameters $\mechanical{M}+\delta\mechanical{M}$, $\mechanical{J}+\delta\mechanical{J}$, and $\mechanical{A}+\delta\mechanical{A}$. The first law of black-hole mechanics states that *the changes in mass, angular momentum, and surface area are related by*

$$
\delta\mechanical{M}=\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}}{8\pi}\delta\mechanical{A}+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\delta\mechanical{J}. \tag{5.107}
$$

If the initial and final black holes are in vacuum, then they are Kerr black holes by virtue of the uniqueness theorems, and a derivation of Eq. (5.107) was already presented in Section 5.3.13. That derivation, however, relied heavily on the details of the Kerr metric. We will now present a derivation that is largely independent of those details. In particular we will not assume that the black hole is in vacuum.

We suppose that a black hole, initially in a stationary state, is perturbed by a small quantity of matter described by the (infinitesimal) stress-energy tensor $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}$. As a result the mass and angular momentum of the black hole increase by amounts (Section 4.3.4)

$$
\delta\mechanical{M}=-\int_H\energy{T^\alpha_\beta}t^\beta\,\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha \tag{5.108}
$$

and

$$
\delta\mechanical{J}=\int_H\energy{T^\alpha_\beta}\phi^\beta\,\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha, \tag{5.109}
$$

where the integrations are over the entire event horizon. We will be working to first order in the perturbation $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}$, keeping $t^\alpha$, $\phi^\alpha$, and $\mathrm d\Sigma_\alpha$ at their unperturbed values. We assume that at the end of the process, the black hole is returned to another stationary state.

Substituting the surface element of Eq. (5.95) into Eqs. (5.108) and (5.109) we find

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{\Omega_H}\delta\mechanical{J}
&=\int_H\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}(t^\beta+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\phi^\beta)\xi^\alpha\,\mathrm d\mechanical{S}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\\
&=\int\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\oint_{\mathcal H(\mechanical{v})}\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\xi^\alpha\xi^\beta\,\mathrm d\mechanical{S}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 228, printed page 212. -->

To work out the integral we turn to Eq. (5.97). Because $\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\mechanical{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}$ are quantities of the first order in $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}$, it is appropriate to neglect the quadratic terms and Raychaudhuri’s equation simplifies to

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{v}}
=\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{\theta}-8\pi\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\xi^\alpha\xi^\beta.
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{\Omega_H}\delta\mechanical{J}
&=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\oint_{\mathcal H(\mechanical{v})}
\left(\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{v}}-\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{\theta}\right)\mathrm d\mechanical{S}\\
&=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\left.\oint_{\mathcal H(\mechanical{v})}\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{S}\right|_{-\infty}^{\infty}
+\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}}{8\pi}\int\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\oint_{\mathcal H(\mechanical{v})}\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{S}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Because the black hole is stationary both before and after the perturbation, $\mechanical{\theta}(\mechanical{v}=\pm\infty)=0$ and the boundary terms vanish. Using the fact that $\mechanical{\theta}$ is the fractional rate of change of the congruence’s cross-sectional area, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{\Omega_H}\delta\mechanical{J}
&=\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}}{8\pi}\int\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\oint_{\mathcal H(\mechanical{v})}
\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm d\mechanical{S}}\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\mechanical{v}}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{S}\right)\mathrm d\mechanical{S}\\
&=\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}}{8\pi}\left.\oint_{\mathcal H(\mechanical{v})}\mathrm d\mechanical{S}\right|_{-\infty}^{\infty}\\
&=\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}}{8\pi}\delta\mechanical{A},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\delta\mechanical{A}$ is the change in the black hole’s surface area. This is Eq. (5.107), the statement of the first law of black-hole mechanics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Black-hole mass | The mass changed by the matter perturbation. |
| $\mechanical{J}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Angular momentum | The angular momentum changed by matter flux. |
| $\mechanical{A},\mathrm d\mechanical{S}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Area and area element | The horizon area and its local element. |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Surface gravity | The unperturbed coefficient of the area change. |
| $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Horizon angular velocity | The unperturbed coefficient of the angular-momentum change. |
| $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}},\energy{T^\alpha_\beta}$ | — | Stress-energy tensor | The first-order matter perturbation in the coordinate basis. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Advanced time | The parameter integrated over the horizon. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Expansion | The first-order fractional area rate. |
| $\mechanical{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}$ | — | Shear tensor | The first-order shear whose square is neglected. |
