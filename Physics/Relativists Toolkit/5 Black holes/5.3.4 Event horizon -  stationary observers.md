> [!TLDR]
> Allowed stationary angular velocities merge at the Kerr horizon, where observers corotate with its null Killing generators.

<!-- Source: PDF page 205, printed page 189. -->

We now consider observers moving in the $\mechanical{\phi}$ direction with an arbitrary, but uniform, angular velocity $\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}/\mathrm d\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{\Omega}$. Because such observers do not perceive any time variation in the black hole’s gravitational field, they are called *stationary* observers. They move with a four-velocity

$$
\mechanical{u^\alpha}=\mechanical{\gamma}(t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega}\phi^\alpha), \tag{5.51}
$$

where $t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega}\phi^\alpha$ is a Killing vector for the Kerr spacetime, and $\mechanical{\gamma}$ a new normalization factor given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\gamma}^{-2}
&=-g_{\alpha\beta}(t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega}\phi^\alpha)(t^\beta+\mechanical{\Omega}\phi^\beta)\\
&=-g_{tt}-2\mechanical{\Omega}g_{t\phi}-\mechanical{\Omega}^2g_{\phi\phi}\\
&=-g_{\phi\phi}(\mechanical{\Omega}^2-2\mechanical{\omega}\mechanical{\Omega}+g_{tt}/g_{\phi\phi}),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mechanical{\omega}=-g_{t\phi}/g_{tt}$.

<!-- Source: PDF page 206, printed page 190. -->

Stationary observers cannot exist everywhere in the Kerr spacetime: The vector $t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega}\phi^\alpha$ must be timelike, and this fails to be true when $\mechanical{\gamma}^{-2}$ is nonpositive. It is easy to check that the condition $\mechanical{\gamma}^{-2}>0$ gives rise to the following requirement on the angular velocity:

$$
\mechanical{\Omega_-}<\mechanical{\Omega}<\mechanical{\Omega_+}, \tag{5.52}
$$

where $\mechanical{\Omega_\pm}=\mechanical{\omega}\pm\sqrt{\mechanical{\omega}^2-g_{tt}/g_{\phi\phi}}$. After some algebra, using Eqs. (5.44) and (5.45), this reduces to

$$
\mechanical{\Omega_\pm}=\mechanical{\omega}\pm\frac{\Delta^{1/2}\rho^2}{\Sigma\sin\mechanical{\theta}}. \tag{5.53}
$$

A stationary observer with $\mechanical{\Omega}=0$ is a static observer, and we know that static observers exist only outside the static limit. It must therefore be true that $\mechanical{\Omega_-}$ changes sign at $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_{\mathrm{sl}}}(\mechanical{\theta})$. This is confirmed by a few lines of algebra, using Eqs. (5.50) and (5.53). As $\mechanical{r}$ decreases further from $\mechanical{r_{\mathrm{sl}}}(\mechanical{\theta})$, $\mechanical{\Omega_-}$ increases while $\mechanical{\Omega_+}$ decreases. Eventually we arrive at the situation $\mechanical{\Omega_-}=\mechanical{\Omega_+}$, which implies $\mechanical{\Omega}=\mechanical{\omega}$; at this point the stationary observer is forced to move around the black hole with an angular velocity equal to $\mechanical{\omega}$. This occurs when $\Delta=0$, or $\mechanical{r}^2-2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}+\mechanical{a}^2=0$. The largest solution is $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_+}$, where

$$
\mechanical{r_+}=\mechanical{M}+\sqrt{\mechanical{M}^2-\mechanical{a}^2}. \tag{5.54}
$$

Notice that the roots of $\Delta=0$ are real if and only if $\mechanical{a}\le\mechanical{M}$, or $\mechanical{J}\le\mechanical{M}^2$. There is an upper limit on the angular momentum of a black hole. Kerr black holes with $\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{M}$ are said to be *extremal*. For $\mechanical{a}>\mechanical{M}$ the Kerr metric describes a naked singularity.

The vector $t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega}\phi^\alpha$ becomes null at $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_+}$ and stationary observers cannot exist inside this surface, which we identify with the black hole’s *event horizon* (Fig. 5.12). The quantity

$$
\mechanical{\Omega_H}=\mechanical{\omega}(\mechanical{r_+})=\frac{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2} \tag{5.55}
$$

is then interpreted as the angular velocity of the black hole. Stationary observers just outside the horizon have an angular velocity equal to $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$ – they are in a state of *corotation* with the black hole.

To confirm that $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_+}$ is truly the event horizon, we use the property that in a stationary spacetime, the event horizon is also an apparent horizon – a surface of zero expansion for a congruence of outgoing null geodesics orthogonal to the surface. The event horizon must therefore be a null, stationary surface. Now, the normal to any stationary surface must be proportional to $\partial_\alpha\mechanical{r}$, and such a surface will

<!-- Source: PDF page 207, printed page 191. -->

be null if $g^{\alpha\beta}(\partial_\alpha\mechanical{r})(\partial_\beta\mechanical{r})=g^{rr}=0$. Using Eq. (5.46) gives

$$
\Delta\equiv\mechanical{r}^2-2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}+\mechanical{a}^2=0. \tag{5.56}
$$

The largest solution, $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_+}$, designates the event horizon. The other root,

$$
\mechanical{r_-}=\mechanical{M}-\sqrt{\mechanical{M}^2-\mechanical{a}^2}, \tag{5.57}
$$

describes the black hole’s inner apparent horizon, which is analogous to the inner horizon of the Reissner–Nordström black hole.

We have found that the vector

$$
\xi^\alpha=t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\phi^\alpha \tag{5.58}
$$

is null at the event horizon. It is tangent to the horizon’s null generators, which wrap around the horizon with an angular velocity $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$. Because it is a linear combination of two Killing vectors, $\xi^\alpha$ is also a Killing vector, and the event horizon of the Kerr spacetime is a Killing horizon. Notice an important difference between stationary and static black holes: For a static black hole, $t^\alpha$ becomes null at the event horizon; for a stationary black hole, $t^\alpha$ is null at the static limit and $\xi^\alpha$ becomes null at the event horizon.
