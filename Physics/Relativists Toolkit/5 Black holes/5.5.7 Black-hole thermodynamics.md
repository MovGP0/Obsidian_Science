> [!TLDR]
> Hawking temperature and horizon entropy give the four mechanical laws a thermodynamic meaning.

<!-- Source: PDF page 230, printed page 214. -->

The four laws of black-hole mechanics bear a striking resemblance to the laws of thermodynamics, with $\mechanical{\kappa}$ playing the role of temperature, $\mechanical{A}$ that of entropy, and $\mechanical{M}$ that of internal energy. Hawking’s discovery that quantum processes give rise to a thermal flux of particles from black holes implies they do indeed behave as thermodynamic systems. Black holes have a well-defined temperature, which as a matter of fact is proportional to the hole’s surface gravity:

$$
\thermal{T}=\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{2\pi}\mechanical{\kappa}. \tag{5.116}
$$

The zeroth law is therefore a special case of the corresponding law of thermodynamics, which states that a system in thermal equilibrium has a uniform

<!-- Source: PDF page 231, printed page 215. -->

temperature. The first law, when recognized as a special case of the corresponding law of thermodynamics, implies that the black-hole entropy must be given by

$$
\thermal{S}=\frac{1}{4\energy{\hbar}}\mechanical{A}. \tag{5.117}
$$

The second law is therefore also a special case of the corresponding law of thermodynamics, which states that the entropy of an isolated system can never decrease. In this regard it should be noted that Hawking radiation actually causes the black-hole area to *decrease*, in violation of the area theorem. (The radiation’s stress-energy tensor does not satisfy the null energy condition.) However, the process of black-hole evaporation does *not* violate the *generalized* second law, which states that the *total* entropy, the sum of radiation and black-hole entropies, does not decrease.

The fact that black holes behave as thermodynamic systems reveals a deep connection between such disparate fields as gravitation, quantum mechanics, and thermodynamics. This connection is still poorly understood today.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Surface gravity | The geometric parameter that fixes Hawking temperature. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Horizon area | The area that fixes the black-hole entropy. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Black-hole mass | The mass that acts as internal energy in geometrized units. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Hawking temperature | Temperature in energy units with the Boltzmann constant set to one. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The quantum of action with G = c = 1. |
| $\thermal{S}$ | $1$ | Black-hole entropy | The entropy in units of the Boltzmann constant. |
