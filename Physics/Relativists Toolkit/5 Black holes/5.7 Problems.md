## Problem 1

The metric of an extreme $(\electric{Q}=\pm\mechanical{M})$ Reissner–Nordström black hole is given by

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\left(1-\frac{\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}}\right)^2\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2
+\left(1-\frac{\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}}\right)^{-2}\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2.
$$

**(a)** Find an appropriate set of Kruskal coordinates for this spacetime.

**(b)** Show that the region $\mechanical{r}\le\mechanical{M}$ does not contain trapped surfaces.

**(c)** Sketch a Penrose–Carter diagram for this spacetime.

**(d)** Find a coordinate transformation that brings the metric to the form

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{\bar r}}\right)^{-2}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2
+\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{\bar r}}\right)^2
(\mathrm d\mechanical{x}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{y}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{z}^2),
$$

where $\mechanical{\bar r}^2=\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2+\mechanical{z}^2$. Show that in these coordinates, the electromagnetic field tensor can be generated from the vector potential $\electric{A_\alpha}\mathrm dx^\alpha=\mp(1+\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{\bar r})^{-1}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}$, in which the upper (lower) sign gives rise to a positive (negative) electric charge.

<!-- Source: PDF page 233, printed page 217. -->

**(e)** Show that the metric

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\Phi^{-2}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2+\Phi^2(\mathrm d\mechanical{x}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{y}^2+\mathrm d\mechanical{z}^2)
$$

and the vector potential $\electric{A_\alpha}\mathrm dx^\alpha=\mp\Phi^{-1}\mathrm d\mechanical{t}$ produce an exact solution to the Einstein–Maxwell equations provided that $\Phi(\mechanical{\boldsymbol x})$ satisfies Laplace’s equation $\nabla^2\Phi=0$. Here, $\nabla^2$ is the usual Laplacian operator of three-dimensional flat space and $\mechanical{\boldsymbol x}=(\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z})$. This metric is known as the *Majumdar–Papapetrou solution*. Prove that if the spacetime is asymptotically flat, then the total charge $\electric{Q}$ and the ADM mass $\mechanical{M}$ are related by $\electric{Q}=\pm\mechanical{M}$. Finally, find an expression for $\Phi(\mechanical{\boldsymbol x})$ that corresponds to a collection of $N$ black holes situated at arbitrary positions $\mechanical{\boldsymbol x_n}$ $(n=1,2,\ldots,N)$.

## Problem 2

A black hole is formed by the gravitational collapse of null dust. During the collapse the metric is given by an ingoing Vaidya solution with mass function $\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{v})=\mechanical{v}/16$. Spacetime is assumed to be flat before the collapse $(\mechanical{v}<0)$, and after the collapse $(\mechanical{v}>\mechanical{v_0})$ the metric is given by a Schwarzschild solution with mass $\mechanical{m_0}=\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{v_0})=\mechanical{v_0}/16$. We want to study various properties of this spacetime.

**(a)** Show that in the interval $0<\mechanical{v}<\mechanical{v_0}$, outgoing light rays are described by the parametric equations

$$
\mechanical{r}(\lambda)=\mechanical{c}\lambda e^{-\lambda},\qquad
\mechanical{v}(\lambda)=4\mechanical{c}(1+\lambda)e^{-\lambda},
$$

where $\mechanical{c}$ is a constant. Show that $\mechanical{v}=4\mechanical{r}$ also describes an outgoing light ray. Plot a few of these curves in the $(\mechanical{v},\mechanical{r})$ plane, using both positive and negative values of $\mechanical{c}$. Plot also the position of the apparent horizon.

**(b)** Find the parametric equations that describe the event horizon.

**(c)** Prove that the curvature singularity at $\mechanical{r}=0$ is *naked*, in the sense that it is visible to observers at large distances. Prove also that at the moment it is visible, the singularity is *massless*. [It is generally true that the central singularity of a spherical collapse must be massless if it is naked. This was established by Lake (1992).]

## Problem 3

In this problem we have a closer look at the instability of black-hole tunnels, a topic that was mentioned briefly in Sections 5.2.3 and 5.3.9. We will see that the instability is caused by the pathological behaviour of the ingoing branch $(\mechanical{v}=\infty)$ of the inner horizon $(\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r_-})$. For reasons that will become clear, we shall call this the *Cauchy horizon* of the black-hole spacetime. For simplicity we shall restrict our attention to the Reissner–Nordström (RN) spacetime. [The physics of the Cauchy-horizon instability was this author’s Ph.D. topic; see

<!-- Source: PDF page 234, printed page 218. -->

Poisson and Israel (1990). The book by Burko and Ori (1997) presents a rather complete review of this fascinating part of black-hole physics.]

**(a)** Consider an event $P$ located anywhere in the future of the Cauchy horizon. Argue that the conditions at $P$ are not uniquely determined by initial data placed on a spacelike hypersurface $\Sigma$ located outside the black hole. Then argue that the Cauchy horizon is the boundary of the region of spacetime for which the evolution of this data is unique. (This region is called the *domain of dependence* of $\Sigma$, and we say that the *Cauchy problem* of general relativity is well posed in this region. The Cauchy horizon is the place at which the evolution ceases to be uniquely determined by the initial data; the Cauchy problem breaks down. In effect, the predictive power of the theory is lost at the Cauchy horizon.)

**(b)** Consider a test null fluid with stress-energy tensor $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}=\energy{\rho}\mechanical{l^\alpha}\mechanical{l^\beta}$, where $\energy{\rho}$ is the energy density and $\mechanical{l_\alpha}=-\partial_\alpha\mechanical{v}$ the four-velocity. The fluid moves parallel to the Cauchy horizon, along ingoing null geodesics. Prove that $\energy{\rho}$ must be of the form

$$
\energy{\rho}=\frac{\energy{L}(\mechanical{v})}{4\pi\mechanical{r}^2},
$$

where $\energy{L}(\mechanical{v})$ is an arbitrary function of advanced time $\mechanical{v}$. Show that if a finite quantity of energy is to enter the black hole, then $\energy{L}\to0$ as $\mechanical{v}\to\infty$. (How fast must $\energy{L}$ vanish?) Typically, radiative fields outside black holes decay in time according to an inverse power law (Price 1972). We shall therefore take $\energy{L}(\mechanical{v})\sim\mechanical{v}^{-p}$ as $\mechanical{v}\to\infty$, with $p$ larger than, say, 2.

**(c)** Consider now a free-falling observer inside the black hole. This observer moves in the outward radial direction, encounters the null dust, and measures its energy density to be $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{u^\alpha}\mechanical{u^\beta}$, where $\mechanical{u^\alpha}$ is the observer’s four-velocity. Show that as the observer crosses the Cauchy horizon,

$$
\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{u^\alpha}\mechanical{u^\beta}
=\frac{\energy{\tilde E}^2}{4\pi\mechanical{r_-}^2}\energy{L}(\mechanical{v})e^{2\mechanical{\kappa_-}\mechanical{v}},
$$

where $\energy{\tilde E}=-\mechanical{u_\alpha}t^\alpha$ and $\mechanical{\kappa_-}$ was defined in Section 5.2.2. Conclude that the measured energy density diverges at the Cauchy horizon, even though the total amount of energy entering the hole is finite. This is the pathology of the Cauchy horizon, which ultimately is responsible for the instability of black-hole tunnels.

## Problem 4

The equations governing geodesic motion in the Kerr spacetime were given without justification in Section 5.3.6. Here we provide a derivation, which is

<!-- Source: PDF page 235, printed page 219. -->

valid both for timelike and null geodesics. [The general form of the geodesic equations can be found in Section 33.5 of Misner, Thorne, and Wheeler (1973).]

**(a)** By definition, a *Killing tensor* field $\xi_{\alpha\beta}$ is one which satisfies the equation $\xi_{(\alpha\beta;\gamma)}=0$. Show that if $\xi_{\alpha\beta}$ is a Killing tensor and $\mechanical{u^\alpha}$ satisfies the geodesic equation $(\mechanical{u^\alpha}{}_{;\beta}\mechanical{u^\beta}=0)$, then $\xi_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u^\alpha}\mechanical{u^\beta}$ is a constant of the motion.

**(b)** Verify that

$$
\xi_{\alpha\beta}=\Delta\mechanical{k_{(\alpha}}\mechanical{l_{\beta)}}+\mechanical{r}^2g_{\alpha\beta}
$$

is a Killing tensor of the Kerr spacetime. Here, $\mechanical{k^\alpha}$ and $\mechanical{l^\alpha}$ are the null vectors defined in Section 5.3.6.

**(c)** Write the relations $\energy{\tilde E}=-t^\alpha\mechanical{u_\alpha}$ and $\mechanical{\tilde L}=\phi^\alpha\mechanical{u_\alpha}$ explicitly in terms of $\mechanical{u^\alpha}=(\mechanical{\dot t},\mechanical{\dot r},\mechanical{\dot\theta},\mechanical{\dot\phi})$. Then invert these relations to obtain the equations for $\mechanical{\dot t}$ and $\mechanical{\dot\phi}$. [Hint: Make sure to involve the inverse metric.]

**(d)** The Carter constant $\mechanical{\mathcal Q}$ is defined by

$$
\xi_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u^\alpha}\mechanical{u^\beta}
=\mechanical{\mathcal Q}+(\mechanical{\tilde L}-\mechanical{a}\energy{\tilde E})^2.
$$

By working out the left-hand side, derive the equation for $\mechanical{\dot r}$. [Hint: Express $\mechanical{k_\alpha}$ and $\mechanical{l_\alpha}$ in terms of the Killing vectors, and then $\xi_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u^\alpha}\mechanical{u^\beta}$ in terms of $\energy{\tilde E}$, $\mechanical{\tilde L}$, and $\mechanical{\dot r}^2$.]

**(e)** Finally, use the normalization condition $g_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u^\alpha}\mechanical{u^\beta}=-\zeta$ (where $\zeta=1$ for timelike geodesics and $\zeta=0$ for null geodesics) to obtain the equation for $\mechanical{\dot\theta}$.

## Problem 5

Let $\mechanical{l^\alpha}$ be a null, geodesic vector field in flat spacetime. With this vector and an arbitrary scalar function $H$ we construct a new metric tensor $g_{\alpha\beta}$:

$$
g_{\alpha\beta}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}+H\mechanical{l_\alpha}\mechanical{l_\beta},
$$

where $\eta_{\alpha\beta}$ is the Minkowski metric and $\mechanical{l_\alpha}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{l^\beta}$. Such a metric is called a Kerr–Schild metric.

**(a)** Show that $\mechanical{l^\alpha}$ is null with respect to both metrics.

**(b)** Show that $g^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}-H\mechanical{l^\alpha}\mechanical{l^\beta}$ is the inverse metric.

**(c)** Prove that $\mechanical{l_\alpha}=g_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{l^\beta}$ and $\mechanical{l^\alpha}=g^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{l_\beta}$. Thus, indices on the null vector can be lowered and raised with either metric.

**(d)** Calculate the Christoffel symbols for $g_{\alpha\beta}$. Show that they satisfy the relations

$$
\mechanical{l_\mu}\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}=-\frac12\dot H\mechanical{l_\alpha}\mechanical{l_\beta},\qquad
\mechanical{l^\mu}\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\beta}=\frac12\dot H\mechanical{l^\alpha}\mechanical{l_\beta},
$$

where $\dot H\equiv H_{,\mu}\mechanical{l^\mu}$.

**(e)** Prove that $\mechanical{l^\alpha}{}_{;\beta}\mechanical{l^\beta}=0$. Thus, $\mechanical{l^\alpha}$ is a geodesic vector field in both metrics.

**(f)** Prove that the component $R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{l^\alpha}\mechanical{l^\beta}$ of the Ricci tensor vanishes for any choice of function $H$.

## Problem 7

In this problem we provide an alternative derivation of the zeroth law of black-hole mechanics. This derivation is based on the original presentation by Bardeen, Carter, and Hawking (1973); it uses the Einstein field equations and the dominant energy condition.

**(a)** We have seen that the vector $\xi^\alpha$ is tangent to the null generators of the event horizon. It possesses the following properties: (i) $\xi^\alpha$ is null on the horizon; (ii) $\xi^\alpha{}_{;\beta}\xi^\beta=\mechanical{\kappa}\xi^\alpha$ on the horizon; (iii) $\xi^\alpha$ is a Killing vector; and (vi) the congruence of null generators has zero expansion, shear, and rotation. Use these facts to infer

$$
\xi_{\alpha;\beta}=(\mechanical{\kappa}N_\alpha+c^Ae_{A\alpha})\xi_\beta
-\xi_\alpha(\mechanical{\kappa}N_\beta+c^Be_{B\beta}),
$$

where $c^A\equiv\sigma^{AB}\xi_{\alpha;\beta}N^\alpha e^\beta_B$. This relation holds on the horizon only.

**(b)** Prove that the gradient of the surface gravity (in the directions tangent to the horizon) is given by

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}=-R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\xi^\beta N^\gamma\xi^\delta-(\sigma_{AB}c^Ac^B)\xi_\alpha.
$$

This immediately implies that $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is constant on each generator: $\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}\xi^\alpha=0$.

**(c)** Show that the result of part (b) also implies

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A
=-R_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_A\xi^\beta-\sigma^{BC}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_Be^\gamma_C\xi^\delta.
$$

**(d)** The quantities $\mechanical{B_{AB}}\equiv\xi_{\alpha;\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B$ and their tangential derivatives must all vanish on the horizon. Use this observation to derive

$$
R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_Be^\gamma_C\xi^\delta=0.
$$

This relations holds on the horizon only.

**(e)** Collecting the results of parts (c) and (d), use the Einstein field equations to write

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A=8\pi\mechanical{j_\alpha}e^\alpha_A,
$$

where $\mechanical{j^\alpha}=-\energy{T^\alpha_\beta}\xi^\beta$ represents a flux of momentum across the horizon.

**(f)** The dominant energy condition states that $\mechanical{j^\alpha}$ should be either timelike or null, and future directed. Use this, together with the stationary

<!-- Source: PDF page 237, printed page 221. -->

condition $\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\xi^\alpha\xi^\beta=0$, to prove that $\mechanical{j^\alpha}$ must be parallel to $\xi^\alpha$. Under these conditions, therefore,

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A=0,
$$

and the zeroth law is established.

## Problem 8

The unique solution to the Einstein–Maxwell equations describing an isolated black hole of mass $\mechanical{M}$, angular momentum $\mechanical{J}\equiv\mechanical{a}\mechanical{M}$, and electric charge $\electric{Q}$ is known as the *Kerr–Newman solution*; it was discovered by Newman *et al.* in 1965. The Kerr–Newman metric can be expressed as

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\frac{\rho^2\Delta}{\Sigma}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t}^2
+\frac{\Sigma}{\rho^2}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}(\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}-\mechanical{\omega}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{t})^2
+\frac{\rho^2}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\rho^2\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2,
$$

where $\rho^2=\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}$, $\Delta=\mechanical{r}^2-2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r}+\mechanical{a}^2+\electric{Q}^2$, $\Sigma=(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)^2-\mechanical{a}^2\Delta\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}$, and $\mechanical{\omega}=\mechanical{a}(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2-\Delta)/\Sigma$. The metric comes with a vector potential

$$
\electric{A_\alpha}\mathrm dx^\alpha=-\frac{\electric{Q}\mechanical{r}}{\rho^2}(\mathrm d\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{a}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\phi}).
$$

When $\electric{Q}=0$, $\electric{A_\alpha}=0$ and this reduces to the Kerr solution.

**(a)** Find expressions for $\mechanical{r_+}$, the radius of the event horizon, and $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$, the angular velocity of the black hole.

**(b)** Prove that the vector field

$$
\mechanical{l^\alpha}\partial_\alpha=\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\Delta}\partial_t-\partial_r+\frac{\mechanical{a}}{\Delta}\partial_\phi
$$

is tangent to a congruence of ingoing null geodesics. Prove also that

$$
\mechanical{v}\equiv\mechanical{t}+\int\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}
$$

and

$$
\mechanical{\psi}\equiv\mechanical{\phi}+\int\frac{\mechanical{a}}{\Delta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}
$$

are constant on each member of the congruence.

**(c)** Show that in the coordinates $(\mechanical{v},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\psi})$, the Kerr–Newman metric takes the form

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2={}&-\frac{\Delta-\mechanical{a}^2\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}}{\rho^2}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}^2
+2\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}
-2\mechanical{a}\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2-\Delta}{\rho^2}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{v}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}\\
&-2\mechanical{a}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}
+\frac{\Sigma}{\rho^2}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{\psi}^2+\rho^2\mathrm d\mechanical{\theta}^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Find an expression for $\electric{A_\alpha}$ is this coordinate system.

<!-- Source: PDF page 238, printed page 222. -->

**(d)** Show that the vectors

$$
\xi^\alpha\partial_\alpha=\partial_v+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\partial_\psi,\qquad
e^\alpha_\theta\partial_\alpha=\partial_\theta,\qquad
e^\alpha_\psi\partial_\alpha=\partial_\psi,
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
N^\alpha\partial_\alpha={}&-\frac{\mechanical{a}^2\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}}{2(\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})}\partial_v
-\frac{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}\partial_r\\
&-\frac{\mechanical{a}}{2(\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)}
\frac{2\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})}{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}\partial_\psi
\end{aligned}
$$

form a good basis on the event horizon. In particular, prove that they give rise to the completeness relations $g^{\alpha\beta}=-\xi^\alpha N^\beta-N^\alpha\xi^\beta+\sigma^{AB}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B$, where $\sigma^{AB}$ is the inverse of $\sigma_{AB}=g_{\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_Ae^\beta_B$.

**(e)** Prove that the surface gravity of a Kerr–Newman black hole is given by

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}=\frac{\mechanical{r_+}-\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}.
$$

Prove also that the hole’s surface area is

$$
\mechanical{A}=4\pi(\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2).
$$

**(f)** Compute the black-hole mass $\mechanical{M_H}$ and the black-hole angular momentum $\mechanical{J_H}$ of a Kerr–Newman black hole. (These quantities are defined in Section 5.5.3.) Make sure that your results are compatible with the following expressions:

$$
\mechanical{M_H}=\frac{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{2\mechanical{r_+}}
\left[1-\frac{\electric{Q}^2}{\mechanical{a}\mechanical{r_+}}\arctan(\mechanical{a}/\mechanical{r_+})\right]
$$

and

$$
\mechanical{J_H}=\mechanical{a}\frac{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{2\mechanical{r_+}}
\left\{1+\frac{\electric{Q}^2}{2\mechanical{a}^2}
\left[1-\frac{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}{\mechanical{a}\mechanical{r_+}}\arctan(\mechanical{a}/\mechanical{r_+})\right]\right\}.
$$

Verify that these expressions satisfy the generalized Smarr formula.

**(g)** Derive the following alternative version of Smarr’s formula:

$$
\mechanical{M}=2\mechanical{\Omega_H}\mechanical{J}+\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{A}}{4\pi}+\electric{\Phi_H}\electric{Q},
$$

where

$$
\electric{\Phi_H}\equiv-\left.\electric{A_\alpha}\xi^\alpha\right|_{r=r_+}
=\frac{\mechanical{r_+}\electric{Q}}{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}
$$

is the electrostatic potential at the horizon.

**(h)** Consider a quasi-static process during which a stationary black hole of mass $\mechanical{M}$, angular momentum $\mechanical{J}$, and electric charge $\electric{Q}$ is taken to a new stationary black hole with parameters $\mechanical{M}+\delta\mechanical{M}$, $\mechanical{J}+\delta\mechanical{J}$, and $\electric{Q}+\delta\electric{Q}$. Prove that during such a transformation, the hole’s surface area $\mechanical{A}$ will change by an amount $\delta\mechanical{A}$ given by

$$
\delta\mechanical{M}=\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}}{8\pi}\delta\mechanical{A}+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\delta\mechanical{J}+\electric{\Phi_H}\delta\electric{Q}.
$$

This is the first law of black-hole mechanics for charged, rotating black holes.

## Problem 9

Consider a quasi-static process during which the surface area of a black hole changes. (By quasi-static we mean that $\mathrm d\mechanical{A}/\mathrm d\mechanical{v}$ is very small.) Derive the *Hawking–Hartle formula*,

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\mechanical{A}}{\mathrm d\mechanical{v}}
=\frac{8\pi}{\mechanical{\kappa}}\oint_{\mathcal H(\mechanical{v})}
\left(\frac{1}{8\pi}\mechanical{\sigma^{\alpha\beta}}\mechanical{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}
+\energy{T_{\alpha\beta}}\xi^\alpha\xi^\beta\right)\mathrm d\mechanical{S},
$$

in which $\xi^\alpha$ is tangent to the null generators of the event horizon and $\mechanical{\sigma_{\alpha\beta}}$ is their shear tensor. The second term within the integral represents the effect of accreting matter on the surface area. The first term represents the effect of gravitational radiation flowing across the horizon.
