> [!TLDR]
> The Schwarzschild future event horizon prevents signals from the interior from reaching the exterior, while the past horizon bounds a white-hole region.

<!-- Source: PDF page 186, printed page 170. -->

On a Kruskal diagram (Fig. 5.2), all radial light rays move along curves $\mechanical{U}=\mathrm{constant}$ or $\mechanical{V}=\mathrm{constant}$. The light cones are therefore oriented at 45 degrees, and timelike world lines, which lie *within* the light cones, move with a slope larger than unity. The one-way character of the surface $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$ separating regions **I** and **II** of the Schwarzschild spacetime is then clear: An observer crossing this surface can never retrace her steps, and cannot elude an encounter with the curvature singularity at $\mechanical{r}=0$. It is also clear that after crossing $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$, the observer can no longer send signals to the outside world, although she may continue to receive them. The surface $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$ therefore prevents any external observer from detecting what goes on inside. In this context it is called the black-hole’s *event horizon*. The region within the event horizon (region **II**) is called the *black-hole region* of the Schwarzschild spacetime.

<!-- Source: PDF page 187, printed page 171. -->

The surface $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$ that separates regions **I** and **II** must be distinguished from the surface $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$ that separates regions **IV** and **I**. It is clear that the latter is an event horizon to all observers living inside region **IV** (who cannot perceive what goes on in region **I**). It is also a one-way surface, because observers from the outside cannot cross it. To distinguish between the two surfaces $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$, it is usual to refer to the first as a *future horizon* and to the second as a *past horizon*. The region within the past horizon (region **IV**) is called the *white-hole region* of the Schwarzschild spacetime.
