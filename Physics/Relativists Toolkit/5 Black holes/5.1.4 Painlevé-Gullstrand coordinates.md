> [!TLDR]
> Painlevé–Gullstrand time is the proper time of radial observers falling from rest at infinity, and its constant-time hypersurfaces are intrinsically flat.

<!-- Source: PDF page 184, printed page 168. -->

Another useful set of coordinates for the Schwarzschild spacetime are the *Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates* first considered in Section 3.13, Problem 1. Here, as with the Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates, the spatial coordinates $(\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ are the same as in the original form of the metric, Eq. (5.1), but the time coordinate is different: $\mechanical{T}$ is proper time as measured by a free-falling observer starting from rest at infinity and moving radially inward.

The four-velocity of such an observer is given by $\mechanical{u^\alpha}\partial_\alpha=f^{-1}\partial_t-\sqrt{1-f}\,\partial_r$, where $f=1-2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}$. From this we deduce that $\mechanical{u_\alpha}=-\partial_\alpha\mechanical{T}$, where the time function $\mechanical{T}$ is obtained by integrating $\mathrm d\mechanical{T}=\mathrm d\mechanical{t}+f^{-1}\sqrt{1-f}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{r}\equiv\mathrm d\mechanical{\tau}$, where $\mechanical{\tau}$ is proper time. (Integration is elementary, and the result appears in Section 3.13, Problem 1.) After inserting this expression for $\mathrm d\mechanical{t}$ into Eq. (5.1), we obtain the Painlevé–Gullstrand form of the Schwarzschild metric:

$$
\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=-\mathrm d\mechanical{T}^2
+\left(\mathrm d\mechanical{r}+\sqrt{2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}\,\mathrm d\mechanical{T}\right)^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2. \tag{5.12}
$$

The coordinates $(\mechanical{T},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ give rise to a metric that is regular at $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$, in correspondence with the fact that our free-falling observer does not consider this surface to be in any way special. Because this observer originates in region **I** of the spacetime (at $\mechanical{r}=\infty$) and ends up in region **II** (at $\mechanical{r}=0$), the new coordinates cover only these two regions of the Kruskal diagram. By reversing the motion — letting $\mathrm d\mechanical{r}$ become $-\mathrm d\mechanical{r}$ in Eq. (5.12) — an alternative coordinate system is produced that covers regions **VI** and **I** instead.

From Eq. (5.12) we infer a rather striking property of the Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates: The hypersurfaces $\mechanical{T}=\mathrm{constant}$ are all intrinsically flat. This can be seen directly from the fact that the induced metric on any such hypersurface is given by $\mathrm d\mechanical{s}^2=\mathrm d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2\mathrm d\Omega^2$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Mass | The Schwarzschild black-hole mass. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Radius | The spherical areal radius. |
| $\mechanical{t},\mechanical{T},\mechanical{\tau}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Times | The Schwarzschild time, Painlevé–Gullstrand time, and observer proper time. |
