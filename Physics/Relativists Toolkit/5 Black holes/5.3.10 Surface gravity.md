> [!TLDR]
> Kerr surface gravity follows from the horizon Killing vector and has the same value at every point on the horizon.

<!-- Source: PDF page 214, printed page 198. -->

As was pointed out in Section 5.3.4, the vector

$$
\xi^\alpha=t^\alpha+\mechanical{\Omega_H}\phi^\alpha, \tag{5.76}
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 215, printed page 199. -->

where $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$ is given by Eq. (5.55), is null at the event horizon and is in fact tangent to the horizon’s null generators. From the same arguments as those presented in Section 5.2.4, the black hole’s surface gravity $\mechanical{\kappa}$ can be defined by

$$
(-\xi^\beta\xi_\beta)_{;\alpha}=2\mechanical{\kappa}\xi_\alpha, \tag{5.77}
$$

or by

$$
\xi^\alpha{}_{;\beta}\xi^\beta=\mechanical{\kappa}\xi^\alpha, \tag{5.78}
$$

or finally, by

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}^2=-\frac12\xi^{\alpha;\beta}\xi_{\alpha;\beta}. \tag{5.79}
$$

These definitions are all equivalent.

Let us use Eq. (5.77) to calculate the surface gravity. The norm of $\xi^\alpha$ is given by

$$
\xi^\beta\xi_\beta=\frac{\Sigma\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}}{\rho^2}(\mechanical{\Omega_H}-\mechanical{\omega})^2-\frac{\rho^2\Delta}{\Sigma},
$$

and differentiation yields

$$
(-\xi^\beta\xi_\beta)_{;\alpha}=\frac{\rho^2}{\Sigma}\Delta_{,\alpha}
$$

on the horizon, at which $\mechanical{\omega}=\mechanical{\Omega_H}$ and $\Delta=0$. We have that $\Delta_{,\alpha}=2(\mechanical{r_+}-\mechanical{M})\partial_\alpha\mechanical{r}$ and $\xi_\alpha=(1-\mechanical{a}\mechanical{\Omega_H}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta})\partial_\alpha\mechanical{r}$ on the horizon, and a few lines of algebra reveal that the surface gravity is

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}=\frac{\mechanical{r_+}-\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}
=\frac{\sqrt{\mechanical{M}^2-\mechanical{a}^2}}{\mechanical{r_+}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}. \tag{5.80}
$$

Notice that this is the same quantity that was denoted $\mechanical{\kappa_+}$ in Section 5.3.9. Notice also that $\mechanical{\kappa}=0$ for an extreme Kerr black hole. And finally, notice that in the general case $\mechanical{\kappa}$ does not depend on $\mechanical{\theta}$ – the surface gravity is uniform on the event horizon. We shall return to this remarkable property in Section 5.5.1.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\kappa},\mechanical{\kappa_+}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Surface gravity | The uniform horizon acceleration parameter. |
| $\mechanical{\Omega_H},\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Angular velocities | The horizon rate and local frame-dragging rate. |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Mass and specific angular momentum | The Kerr parameters in geometrized units. |
| $\mechanical{r},\mechanical{r_+}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Radius and outer horizon radius | The radial coordinate and event-horizon location. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{rad}}$ | Polar angle | Surface gravity has no dependence on this angle. |
