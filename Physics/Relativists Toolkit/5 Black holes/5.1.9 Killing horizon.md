> [!TLDR]
> The Schwarzschild event horizon is a Killing horizon, where the stationary Killing vector becomes null.

<!-- Source: PDF page 191, printed page 175. -->

The vector $t^\alpha=\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{t}$ is a Killing vector of the Schwarzschild spacetime. While this vector is timelike outside the black hole, it is *null* on the event horizon, and it is *spacelike* inside:

$$
g_{\alpha\beta}t^\alpha t^\beta=1-\frac{2\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}}.
$$

<!-- Source: PDF page 192, printed page 176. -->

The surface $\mechanical{r}=2\mechanical{M}$ can therefore be called a *Killing horizon*, a hypersurface on which the norm of a Killing vector goes to zero. In static black-hole spacetimes, the event, apparent, and Killing horizons all coincide.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Mass | The Schwarzschild black-hole mass. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Radius | The areal radius where the Killing vector changes causal character. |
| $\mechanical{t}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Time | The stationary time coordinate. |
| $t^\alpha$ | — | Killing vector | The vector that generates stationary time translations. |
| $g_{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Spacetime metric | The metric used to compute the Killing-vector norm. |
| $x^\alpha$ | — | Coordinates | The spacetime coordinates in the Killing-vector definition. |
