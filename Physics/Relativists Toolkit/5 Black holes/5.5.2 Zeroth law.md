> [!TLDR]
> The zeroth law states that surface gravity is constant along and across every stationary event horizon.

<!-- Source: PDF page 224, printed page 208. -->

The zeroth law of black-hole mechanics states that *the surface gravity of a stationary black hole is uniform over the entire event horizon*. We saw in Section 5.3.10 that this statement is indeed true for the specific case of a Kerr black hole, but the scope of the zeroth law is much wider: The black hole need not be isolated and its metric need not be the Kerr metric.

To prove that $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is uniform on the event horizon, we need to establish that (i) $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is constant along the horizon’s null generators, and (ii) $\mechanical{\kappa}$ does not vary from generator to generator. We will prove both statements in turn, starting with

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}^2=-\frac12\xi^{\alpha;\beta}\xi_{\alpha;\beta} \tag{5.98}
$$

as our definition for the surface gravity. (We saw in Section 5.2.4 that this relation is equivalent to $\xi^\alpha{}_{;\beta}\xi^\beta=\mechanical{\kappa}\xi^\alpha$.) We shall need the identity

$$
\xi_{\alpha;\mu\nu}=R_{\alpha\mu\nu\beta}\xi^\beta, \tag{5.99}
$$

which is satisfied by any Killing vector $\xi^\alpha$. (This was derived in Section 1.13, Problem 9.)

We differentiate Eq. (5.98) in the directions tangent to the horizon. (Because $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is defined only on the event horizon, its normal derivative does not exist.) Using Eq. (5.99) we obtain

$$
2\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}=-\xi^{\mu;\nu}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\xi^\beta. \tag{5.100}
$$

The fact that $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is constant on each generator follows immediately from this:

$$
\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}\xi^\alpha=0. \tag{5.101}
$$

We must now examine how $\mechanical{\kappa}$ changes in the transverse directions. Equation (5.100) implies

$$
2\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A=-\xi^{\mu;\nu}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_A\xi^\beta,
$$

and we would like to show that the right-hand side is zero. Let us first assume that the event horizon is geodesically complete, so that it contains a bifurcation two-sphere, at which $\xi^\alpha=0$. Then the last equation implies that $\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A=0$ at the bifurcation two-sphere. Because $\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A$ is constant on the null generators (Section 5.7, Problem 6), we have that $\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A=0$ on all cross sections $\mechanical{v}=\text{constant}$ of the event horizon. This shows that the value of $\mechanical{\kappa}$ does not change from generator to generator, and we conclude that $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is uniform over the entire event horizon.

It is easy to see that the property $\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A=0$ must be independent of the existence of a bifurcation two-sphere. Consider two stationary black holes, identical

<!-- Source: PDF page 225, printed page 209. -->

in every respect in the future of $\mechanical{v}=0$ (say), but different in the past, so that only one of them possesses a bifurcation two-sphere. (We imagine that the first black hole has existed forever, and that the second black hole was formed prior to $\mechanical{v}=0$ by gravitational collapse; the second black hole is stationary only for $\mechanical{v}>0$.) Our proof that $\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A=0$ on all cross sections $\mechanical{v}=\text{constant}$ of the event horizon applies to the first black hole. But since the spacetimes are identical for $\mechanical{v}>0$, the property $\mechanical{\kappa}_{,\alpha}e^\alpha_A=0$ must apply also to the second black hole. Thus, the zeroth law is established for all stationary black holes, whether or not they are geodesically complete.

It is clear that the relation $\xi^{\mu;\nu}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}e^\alpha_A\xi^\beta=0$ must hold everywhere on a stationary event horizon, but it is surprisingly difficult to prove this. In their original discussion Bardeen, Carter, and Hawking establish this identity by using the Einstein field equations and the dominant energy condition (Section 5.7, Problem 7). This restriction was lifted in a 1996 paper by Rácz and Wald.
