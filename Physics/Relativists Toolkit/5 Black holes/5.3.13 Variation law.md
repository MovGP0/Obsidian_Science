> [!TLDR]
> For a Kerr black hole, mass and angular-momentum changes determine the horizon-area change through the variation law.

<!-- Source: PDF page 217, printed page 201. -->

It is clear that the surface area of a black hole is a function of its mass and angular momentum: $\mechanical{A}=\mechanical{A}(\mechanical{M},\mechanical{J})$. Suppose that a black hole of mass $\mechanical{M}$ and angular momentum $\mechanical{J}$ is perturbed so that its parameters become $\mechanical{M}+\delta\mechanical{M}$ and $\mechanical{J}+\delta\mechanical{J}$. (For example, the black hole might absorb a particle, as was considered in Section 5.3.5.) How does the area change? There exists a simple formula relating $\delta\mechanical{A}$ to the changes in mass and angular momentum. It is

$$
\frac{\mechanical{\kappa}}{8\pi}\delta\mechanical{A}=\delta\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{\Omega_H}\delta\mechanical{J}. \tag{5.85}
$$

To derive this we start with Eq. (5.83), which immediately implies

$$
\frac{\delta\mechanical{A}}{8\pi}=\mechanical{r_+}\delta\mechanical{r_+}+\mechanical{a}\delta\mechanical{a}.
$$

But the horizon radius $\mechanical{r_+}$ depends on $\mechanical{M}$ and $\mechanical{a}$; the defining relation is $\mechanical{r_+}^2-2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{r_+}+\mechanical{a}^2=0$ and this gives us

$$
(\mechanical{r_+}-\mechanical{M})\delta\mechanical{r_+}=\mechanical{r_+}\delta\mechanical{M}-\mechanical{a}\delta\mechanical{a}.
$$

This result can be substituted into the preceding expression for $\delta\mechanical{A}$. The final step is to relate $\mechanical{a}$ to the hole’s angular momentum $\mechanical{J}$; we have that $\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{J}/\mechanical{M}$ and this implies $\mechanical{M}\delta\mechanical{a}=\delta\mechanical{J}-\mechanical{a}\delta\mechanical{M}$. Collecting these results, we arrive at Eq. (5.85) after invoking Eqs. (5.55) and (5.80).

In Section 5.3.5 we found that the right-hand side of Eq. (5.85) must be positive. What we have, therefore, is the statement that the surface area of a Kerr black hole *always increases* during a process by which it absorbs a particle. This is a restricted version of the second law of black-hole mechanics, to which we shall return in Section 5.5.4.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Mass and specific angular momentum | The Kerr parameters that change during the perturbation. |
| $\mechanical{J}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Angular momentum | The angular momentum before and after the perturbation. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^2}$ | Horizon area | The area whose change follows the variation law. |
| $\mechanical{\Omega_H}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Horizon angular velocity | The angular rate in the variation law. |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m^{-1}}$ | Surface gravity | The coefficient of the area change. |
| $\mechanical{r_+}$ | $\unit{\mathrm m}$ | Event-horizon radius | The root varied in the derivation. |
