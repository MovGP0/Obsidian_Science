> [!TLDR]
> Comoving world lines in an expanding universe have expansion equal to three times the fractional scale-factor rate.

<!-- PDF page 57; printed page 41 -->

As an illustrative example, let us consider the congruence of comoving world lines in an expanding universe with metric

$$
d\mechanical{s}^2=-d\mechanical{t}^2
+\mechanical{a}^2(\mechanical{t})(d\mechanical{x}^2+d\mechanical{y}^2+d\mechanical{z}^2),
$$

where $\mechanical{a}(\mechanical{t})$ is the scale factor. The tangent vector field is $\mechanical{u}_\alpha=-\partial_\alpha\mechanical{t}$, and a quick calculation reveals that

$$
\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}=\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\beta}
=\frac{\dot{\mechanical{a}}}{\mechanical{a}}h_{\alpha\beta},
$$

where an overdot indicates differentiation with respect to $\mechanical{t}$. This shows that the shear and rotation tensors are both zero for this congruence. The expansion, on the

<!-- PDF page 58; printed page 42 -->

other hand, is given by

$$
\mechanical{\theta}=3\frac{\dot{\mechanical{a}}}{\mechanical{a}}
=\frac{1}{\mechanical{a}^3}\frac{d}{d\mechanical{t}}\mechanical{a}^3.
$$

This illustrates rather well the general statement (made in Section 2.3.8 below) that the expansion is the fractional rate of change of the congruence's cross-sectional volume (which is here proportional to $\mechanical{a}^3$).
