> [!TLDR]
> Raychaudhuri's equation determines the expansion rate through expansion, shear, rotation, and Ricci curvature.

<!-- PDF page 56; printed page 40 -->

We now want to derive an evolution equation for $\mechanical{\theta}$, the expansion scalar. We begin by developing an equation for $\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}$ itself:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta;\mu}\mechanical{u}^\mu
&=\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\beta\mu}\mechanical{u}^\mu\\
&=(\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\mu\beta}-R_{\alpha\nu\beta\mu}\mechanical{u}^\nu)\mechanical{u}^\mu\\
&=(\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\mu}\mechanical{u}^\mu)_{;\beta}
-\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\mu}\mechanical{u}^\mu{}_{;\beta}
-R_{\alpha\nu\beta\mu}\mechanical{u}^\nu\mechanical{u}^\mu\\
&=-\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\mu}\mechanical{B}^\mu{}_\beta
-R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\mechanical{u}^\mu\mechanical{u}^\nu.
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation for $\mechanical{\theta}$ is obtained by taking the trace:

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\theta}}{d\mechanical{\tau}}
=-\mechanical{B}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{B}_{\beta\alpha}
-R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u}^\alpha\mechanical{u}^\beta.
$$

It is then easy to check that $\mechanical{B}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{B}_{\beta\alpha}=\tfrac13\mechanical{\theta}^2+\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}-\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}$. Making the substitution, we arrive at

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\theta}}{d\mechanical{\tau}}
=-\frac13\mechanical{\theta}^2
-\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
+\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}
-R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u}^\alpha\mechanical{u}^\beta.
\tag{2.20}
$$

This is *Raychaudhuri's equation* for a congruence of timelike geodesics. We note that since the shear and rotation tensors are purely spatial, $\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}\ge0$ and $\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}\ge0$, with the equality sign holding if and only if the tensor is identically zero.
