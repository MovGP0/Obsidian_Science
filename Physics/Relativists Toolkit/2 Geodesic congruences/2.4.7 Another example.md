> [!TLDR]
> Radial Schwarzschild null congruences have opposite expansion signs and the same negative expansion derivative.

<!-- PDF page 68; printed page 52 -->

As a second example we consider the radial null geodesics of Schwarzschild spacetime. For $d\mechanical{\theta}=d\mechanical{\phi}=0$ the Schwarzschild line element reduces to

$$
d\mechanical{s}^2=-f\,d\mechanical{t}^2+f^{-1}d\mechanical{r}^2
=-f(d\mechanical{t}-f^{-1}d\mechanical{r})(d\mechanical{t}+f^{-1}d\mechanical{r}),
$$

where $f=1-2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}$. The displacements will be null if $d\mechanical{s}^2=0$. If we define

$$
\mechanical{u}=\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{r}^*,\qquad
\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{r}^*,
$$

where $\mechanical{r}^*=\int f^{-1}\,d\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r}+2\mechanical{M}\ln(\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}-1)$, we find that $\mechanical{u}=\text{constant}$ on outgoing null geodesics, while $\mechanical{v}=\text{constant}$ on ingoing null geodesics. The vector fields

$$
\mechanical{k}^{\mathrm{out}}_\alpha=-\partial_\alpha\mechanical{u},\qquad
\mechanical{k}^{\mathrm{in}}_\alpha=-\partial_\alpha\mechanical{v}
$$

are null and they both satisfy the geodesic equation, with $+\mechanical{r}$ as an affine parameter for $\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm{out}}^\alpha$ and $-\mechanical{r}$ as an affine parameter for $\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm{in}}^\alpha$. (Check this.) As their labels indicate, $\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm{out}}^\alpha$ is tangent to the outgoing geodesics, while $\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm{in}}^\alpha$ is tangent to the ingoing geodesics. The congruences are clearly hypersurface orthogonal. Their expansions are easily calculated:

$$
\mechanical{\theta}=\pm\frac{2}{\mechanical{r}},
$$

where the positive (negative) sign refers to the outgoing (ingoing) congruence. We also have

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\theta}}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}=-\frac{2}{\mechanical{r}^2},
$$

which is properly negative.
