> [!TLDR]
> Quantum fields can violate local energy conditions; averaged conditions constrain integrals along null geodesics.

<!-- PDF page 48; printed page 32 -->

While the energy conditions typically hold for classical matter, they can be violated by quantized matter fields. A well-known example is the Casimir vacuum energy between two conducting plates separated by a distance $\mechanical{d}$:

$$
\energy{\rho}=-\frac{\pi^2}{720}\frac{\energy{\hbar}}{\mechanical{d}^4}.
$$

Although quantum effects allow for a localized violation of the energy conditions, recent work suggests that there is a limit to the extent by which the energy conditions can be violated *globally*. In this context it is useful to formulate averaged versions of the energy conditions. For example, the *averaged null energy condition* states that the integral of $\energy{T}_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta$ along a null geodesic $\gamma$ must be nonnegative:

$$
\int_\gamma\energy{T}_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta\,d\mechanical{\lambda}\ge0.
$$

Such averaged energy conditions play a central role in the theory of traversable wormholes (see Section 2.6, Problem 1). The averaged null energy condition is known to always hold in flat spacetime, for noninteracting scalar and electromagnetic fields in arbitrary quantum states; this is true in spite of the fact that $\energy{T}_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta$ can be negative somewhere along the geodesic. Its status in curved spacetimes

is not yet fully settled. A complete discussion, as of 1994, can be found in Matt Visser's book.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{T}_{\alpha\beta},\energy{T}^\alpha{}_\beta$ | — | Stress-energy tensor | The matter tensor with coordinate-dependent component units. |
| $\energy{\rho}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m^{-2}}}$ | Energy density | The density in the matter's orthonormal frame, in geometrized units. |
| $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ | — | Null vector | The future-directed null vector with arbitrary normalization. |
| $\mechanical{d}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Plate separation | The distance between the conducting plates. |
| $\energy{\hbar}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m^2}}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The quantum constant in geometrized units. |
| $\mechanical{\lambda}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m}}$ | Affine parameter | A length parameter along the null geodesic. |
