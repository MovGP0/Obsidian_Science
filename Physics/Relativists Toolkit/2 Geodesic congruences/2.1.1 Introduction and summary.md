> [!TLDR]
> An orthonormal decomposition expresses the stress-energy tensor through energy density and principal pressures.

<!-- PDF page 45; printed page 29 -->

In the context of classical general relativity, it is reasonable to expect that the stress-energy tensor will satisfy certain conditions, such as positivity of the energy density and dominance of the energy density over the pressure. Such requirements are embodied in the *energy conditions*, which are summarized in Table 2.1.

To put the energy conditions in concrete form it is useful to assume that the stress-energy tensor admits the decomposition

$$
\energy{T}^{\alpha\beta}
=\energy{\rho}\hat e_0^\alpha\hat e_0^\beta
+\mechanical{p}_1\hat e_1^\alpha\hat e_1^\beta
+\mechanical{p}_2\hat e_2^\alpha\hat e_2^\beta
+\mechanical{p}_3\hat e_3^\alpha\hat e_3^\beta,
\tag{2.1}
$$

in which the vectors $\hat e_\mu^\alpha$ form an orthonormal basis; they satisfy the relations

$$
g_{\alpha\beta}\hat e_\mu^\alpha\hat e_\nu^\beta=\eta_{\mu\nu},
\tag{2.2}
$$

where $\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ is the Minkowski metric. (It goes without saying that the basis vectors are functions of the coordinates.) Equations (2.1) and (2.2) imply that the quantities $\energy{\rho}$ (energy density) and $\mechanical{p}_i$ (principal pressures) are eigenvalues of the stress-energy tensor, and $\hat e_\mu^\alpha$ are the normalized eigenvectors. The inverse metric can neatly be expressed in terms of the basis vectors. It is easy to check that the relation

$$
g^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\mu\nu}\hat e_\mu^\alpha\hat e_\nu^\beta,
\tag{2.3}
$$

where $\eta^{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ is the inverse of $\eta_{\mu\nu}$, is compatible with Eq. (2.2). Equations such as (2.3) are called *completeness relations*.

If the stress-energy tensor is that of a perfect fluid, then $\mechanical{p}_1=\mechanical{p}_2=\mechanical{p}_3\equiv\mechanical{p}$. Substituting this into Eq. (2.1) and using Eq. (2.3) yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{T}^{\alpha\beta}
&=\energy{\rho}\hat e_0^\alpha\hat e_0^\beta
+\mechanical{p}(\hat e_1^\alpha\hat e_1^\beta+\hat e_2^\alpha\hat e_2^\beta+\hat e_3^\alpha\hat e_3^\beta)\\
&=\energy{\rho}\hat e_0^\alpha\hat e_0^\beta+\mechanical{p}(g^{\alpha\beta}+\hat e_0^\alpha\hat e_0^\beta)\\
&=(\energy{\rho}+\mechanical{p})\hat e_0^\alpha\hat e_0^\beta+\mechanical{p}g^{\alpha\beta}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The vector $\hat e_0^\alpha$ is identified with the four-velocity of the perfect fluid.

Some of the energy conditions are formulated in terms of a normalized, future-directed, but otherwise arbitrary timelike vector $\mechanical{v}^\alpha$; this represents the four-velocity of an arbitrary observer in spacetime. Such a vector can be decomposed as

$$
\mechanical{v}^\alpha=\mechanical{\gamma}(\hat e_0^\alpha+a\hat e_1^\alpha+b\hat e_2^\alpha+c\hat e_3^\alpha),\qquad
\mechanical{\gamma}=(1-a^2-b^2-c^2)^{-1/2},
\tag{2.4}
$$

where $a,b$, and $c$ are arbitrary functions of the coordinates, restricted by $a^2+b^2+c^2<1$. We will also need an arbitrary, future-directed null vector $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$. This we shall express as

$$
\mechanical{k}^\alpha=\hat e_0^\alpha+a'\hat e_1^\alpha+b'\hat e_2^\alpha+c'\hat e_3^\alpha,
\tag{2.5}
$$

where $a',b'$, and $c'$ are arbitrary functions of the coordinates, restricted by $a'^2+b'^2+c'^2=1$. Recall that the normalization of a null vector is always arbitrary.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{T}^{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Stress-energy tensor | The matter tensor with coordinate-dependent component units. |
| $\energy{\rho}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m^{-2}}}$ | Energy density | The energy-density eigenvalue in geometrized units. |
| $\mechanical{p}_i,\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m^{-2}}}$ | Pressure | The principal pressures or the common perfect-fluid pressure. |
| $g_{\alpha\beta},g^{\alpha\beta},\eta_{\mu\nu},\eta^{\mu\nu}$ | — | Metrics | The spacetime and orthonormal-frame metrics and their inverses. |
| $\hat e_0^\alpha,\mechanical{v}^\alpha$ | — | Four-velocities | The fluid or observer velocity with coordinate-dependent component units. |
| $\mechanical{\gamma}$ | $1$ | Velocity normalization | The factor that normalizes the observer's timelike four-velocity. |
| $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ | — | Null vector | The null direction with arbitrary normalization. |
