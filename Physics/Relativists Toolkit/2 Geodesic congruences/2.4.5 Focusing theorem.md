> [!TLDR]
> A hypersurface-orthogonal null congruence focuses when the null energy condition holds and can form a caustic in finite affine distance.

<!-- PDF page 66; printed page 50 -->

The null version of the focusing theorem goes as follows: Let a congruence of null geodesics be *hypersurface orthogonal*, so that $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$, and let the *null energy condition* hold, so that (by virtue of the Einstein field equations) $R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta\ge0$. Then the Raychaudhuri equation implies

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\theta}}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}
=-\frac12\mechanical{\theta}^2
-\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
-R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta\le0,
$$

which means that the geodesics are focused during the evolution of the congruence. Integrating $d\mechanical{\theta}/d\mechanical{\lambda}\le-\tfrac12\mechanical{\theta}^2$ yields

$$
\mechanical{\theta}^{-1}(\mechanical{\lambda})
\ge\mechanical{\theta}_0^{-1}+\frac{\mechanical{\lambda}}{2},
$$

<!-- PDF page 67; printed page 51 -->

where $\mechanical{\theta}_0\equiv\mechanical{\theta}(0)$. This shows that if the congruence is initially converging $(\mechanical{\theta}_0<0)$, then $\mechanical{\theta}(\mechanical{\lambda})\to-\infty$ within an affine parameter $\mechanical{\lambda}\le2/|\mechanical{\theta}_0|$. As in the case of a timelike congruence, this generally signals the occurrence of a caustic.
