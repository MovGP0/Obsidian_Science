> [!TLDR]
> A flat-spacetime null cone has zero shear and rotation, with expansion equal to twice the inverse radius.

<!-- PDF page 67; printed page 51 -->

As an illustrative example, let us consider the congruence formed by the generators of a null cone in flat spacetime. The geodesics emanate from a single point $P$ (which we place at the origin of the coordinate system) and they radiate in all directions; note that $P$ is a caustic of the congruence. In spherical coordinates, the geodesics are described by the relations $\mechanical{t}=\mechanical{\lambda}$, $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{\lambda}$, $\mechanical{\theta}=\text{constant}$, and $\mechanical{\phi}=\text{constant}$, in which $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is the affine parameter. The tangent vector field is

$$
\mechanical{k}_\alpha=-\partial_\alpha(\mechanical{t}-\mechanical{r}).
$$

We must find an auxiliary null vector field $N^\alpha$ that satisfies $\mechanical{k}_\alpha N^\alpha=-1$. If we choose $N^\alpha$ to lie in the $(\mechanical{t},\mechanical{r})$ plane, the unique solution is $N_\alpha=-\tfrac12\partial_\alpha(\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{r})$. With this choice we find that the transverse metric is given by $h_{\alpha\beta}=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,\mechanical{r}^2,\mechanical{r}^2\sin^2\mechanical{\theta})$. A straightforward calculation gives $\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}=\mechanical{k}_{\alpha;\beta}=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,\mechanical{r},\mechanical{r}\sin^2\mechanical{\theta})$, and we see that $\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}$ is already transverse for this choice of $N^\alpha$. We have found

$$
\mechanical{\tilde B}_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{\mechanical{r}}h_{\alpha\beta},
$$

and this shows that the shear and rotation tensors are both zero for this congruence. The expansion, on the other hand, is given by

$$
\mechanical{\theta}=\frac{2}{\mechanical{r}}
=\frac{1}{4\pi\mechanical{r}^2}\frac{d}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}(4\pi\mechanical{r}^2).
$$

This verifies the general statement (made in Section 2.4.8 below) that the expansion is the fractional rate of change of the congruence's cross-sectional area.

We might worry that making a different choice for $N^\alpha$ would affect our results. It is easy to check that the vector $N_\alpha\,dx^\alpha=-d\mechanical{t}+\mechanical{r}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\phi}$ satisfies both $N_\alpha N^\alpha=0$ and $N_\alpha\mechanical{k}^\alpha=-1$. It is therefore an acceptable choice of auxiliary null vector field. This choice leads to a complicated expression for the transverse metric, which now has components along $\mechanical{t}$ and $\mechanical{r}$. And while the expression for $\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}$ does not change, we find that $\mechanical{\tilde B}_{\alpha\beta}$ is no longer equal to $\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}$, and is much more complicated than the expression given previously. You may check, however, that the relation $\mechanical{\tilde B}_{\alpha\beta}=h_{\alpha\beta}/\mechanical{r}$ is not affected by the change of auxiliary null vector. Our results for $\mechanical{\theta}$, $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}$, and $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}$ are therefore preserved.
