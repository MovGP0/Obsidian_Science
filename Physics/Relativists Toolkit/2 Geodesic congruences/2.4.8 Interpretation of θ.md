> [!TLDR]
> The null expansion scalar equals the fractional rate of change of a congruence's transverse cross-sectional area.

<!-- PDF page 68; printed page 52 -->

We shall now give a formal proof of the statement that $\mechanical{\theta}$ is the fractional rate of change of the congruence's cross-sectional area:

$$
\mechanical{\theta}=\frac{1}{\delta\mechanical{A}}\frac{d}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}\delta\mechanical{A},
\tag{2.40}
$$

where $\delta\mechanical{A}$ is measured in the purely transverse directions. The proof is very similar to what was presented in Section 2.3.8; the only crucial difference concerns the dimensionality of the transverse space.

We pick a particular geodesic $\gamma$ from the congruence, and on this geodesic we select a point $P$ at which $\mechanical{\lambda}=\mechanical{\lambda}_P$. We then consider the null curves to which $N^\alpha$ is tangent, and we let $\mu$ be the parameter on these *auxiliary curves*; we adjust the

<!-- PDF page 69; printed page 53 -->

parameterization so that $\mu$ is constant on the null geodesics. The auxiliary curve that passes through $P$ is called $\beta$, and we have that $\mu=\mu_\gamma$ at $P$. The *cross section* $\delta S(\mechanical{\lambda}_P)$ is defined to be a small set of points $P'$ in a neighbourhood of $P$ such that (i) through each of these points there passes another geodesic from the congruence *and* another auxiliary curve, and (ii) at each point $P'$, $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is also equal to $\mechanical{\lambda}_P$ and $\mu$ is equal to $\mu_\gamma$. This set forms a two-dimensional region, the intersection of small segments of the hypersurfaces $\mechanical{\lambda}=\mechanical{\lambda}_P$ and $\mu=\mu_\gamma$. We assume that the parameterization has been adjusted so that both $\gamma$ and $\beta$ intersect $\delta S(\mechanical{\lambda}_P)$ orthogonally. (There is no requirement that other curves do.)

We introduce coordinates in $\delta S(\mechanical{\lambda}_P)$ by assigning a label $\theta^A$ $(A=2,3)$ to each point in the set. Recalling that through each of these points there passes a geodesic from the congruence, we see that we may use $\theta^A$ to label the geodesics themselves. By demanding that each geodesic keep its label as it moves away from $\delta S(\mechanical{\lambda}_P)$, we simultaneously obtain a coordinate system $\theta^A$ in any other cross-section $\delta S(\mechanical{\lambda})$. This construction therefore produces a coordinate system $(\mechanical{\lambda},\mu,\theta^A)$ in a neighbourhood of the geodesic $\gamma$, and there exists a transformation between this system and the one originally in use: $x^\alpha=x^\alpha(\mechanical{\lambda},\mu,\theta^A)$. Because $\mu$ and $\theta^A$ are constant along the geodesics, we have

$$
\mechanical{k}^\alpha=\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\mechanical{\lambda}}\right)_{\mu,\theta^A}.
$$

On the other hand, the vectors

$$
e_A^\alpha=\left(\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\theta^A}\right)_{\mechanical{\lambda},\mu}
$$

are tangent to the cross sections. These relations imply $\mathcal L_k e_A^\alpha=0$ and we also have that on $\gamma$ (and only $\gamma$), $\mechanical{k}_\alpha e_A^\alpha=N_\alpha e_A^\alpha=0$.

The remaining steps are very similar to those carried out in Section 2.3.8, and it will suffice to present a brief outline. The two-tensor

$$
\sigma_{AB}=g_{\alpha\beta}e_A^\alpha e_B^\beta
$$

acts as a metric on $\delta S(\mechanical{\lambda})$. The *cross-sectional area* is therefore defined by $\delta\mechanical{A}=\sqrt\sigma\,d^2\theta$, where $\sigma=\det[\sigma_{AB}]$. The inverse $\sigma^{AB}$ of the two-metric is such that $h^{\alpha\beta}=\sigma^{AB}e_A^\alpha e_B^\beta$ on $\gamma$, where $h_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}+\mechanical{k}_\alpha N_\beta+N_\alpha\mechanical{k}_\beta$ is the transverse metric. The relation

$$
\frac{d\sigma_{AB}}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}
=(\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}+\mechanical{B}_{\beta\alpha})e_A^\alpha e_B^\beta
$$

<!-- PDF page 70; printed page 54 -->

follows, and taking its trace yields

$$
\mechanical{\theta}=\frac{1}{\sqrt\sigma}\frac{d}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}\sqrt\sigma.
$$

This statement is equivalent to Eq. (2.40).
