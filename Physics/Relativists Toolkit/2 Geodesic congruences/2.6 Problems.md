> [!TLDR]
> These problems test energy conditions, timelike and null congruences, transverse projections, and nonaffine geodesic evolution.

<!-- PDF page 70; printed page 54 -->

> [!warning]
> The results derived in Problem 8 are used in later portions of this book.

### 1

Consider a curved spacetime with metric

$$
d\mechanical{s}^2=-d\mechanical{t}^2+d\mechanical{\ell}^2
+\mechanical{r}^2(\mechanical{\ell})\,d\mechanical{\Omega}^2,
$$

where the function $\mechanical{r}(\mechanical{\ell})$ is such that (i) it is minimum at $\mechanical{\ell}=0$, with a value $\mechanical{r}_0$, and (ii) it asymptotically becomes equal to $|\mechanical{\ell}|$ as $\mechanical{\ell}\to\pm\infty$.

(a) Argue that this spacetime contains a traversable wormhole between two asymptotically-flat regions, with a throat of radius $\mechanical{r}_0$.

(b) Find which energy conditions are violated at $\mechanical{\ell}=0$.

### 2

We examine the congruence of comoving world lines of a Friedmann–Robertson–Walker spacetime. The metric is

$$
d\mechanical{s}^2=-d\mechanical{t}^2
+\mechanical{a}^2(\mechanical{t})
\left(\frac{d\mechanical{r}^2}{1-k\mechanical{r}^2}+\mechanical{r}^2d\mechanical{\Omega}^2\right),
$$

<!-- PDF page 71; printed page 55 -->

where $\mechanical{a}(\mechanical{t})$ is the scale factor and $k$ a constant normalized to either $\pm1$ or zero. The vector tangent to the congruence is $\mechanical{u}^\alpha=\partial x^\alpha/\partial\mechanical{t}$.

(a) Show that the congruence is geodesic.

(b) Calculate the expansion, shear, and rotation of this congruence.

(c) Use the Raychaudhuri equation to deduce

$$
\frac{\ddot{\mechanical{a}}}{\mechanical{a}}
=-\frac{4\pi}{3}(\energy{\rho}+3\mechanical{p}),
$$

where $\energy{\rho}$ is the energy density of a perfect fluid with four-velocity $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$, and $\mechanical{p}$ is the pressure.

### 3

In this problem we consider the vector field

$$
\mechanical{u}^\alpha\partial_\alpha
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-3\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}}
\left(\partial_t+\sqrt{\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}^3}\,\partial_\theta\right)
$$

in Schwarzschild spacetime; the vector is expressed in terms of the usual Schwarzschild coordinates, and $\mechanical{M}$ is the mass of the black hole.

(a) Show that the vector field is timelike and geodesic. Describe the geodesics to which $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$ is tangent.

(b) Calculate the expansion of the congruence. Explain why the expansion is positive in the northern hemisphere and negative in the southern hemisphere. Explain also why the expansion is singular at the north and south poles.

(c) Compute the rotation tensor for this congruence. Check that its square is given by

$$
\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}
=\frac{\mechanical{M}}{8\mechanical{r}^3}
\left(\frac{1-6\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}{1-3\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}\right)^2.
$$

(d) Calculate $d\mechanical{\theta}/d\mechanical{\tau}$ and check that Raychaudhuri's equation is satisfied.

### 4

Derive the following evolution equations for the shear and rotation tensors of a congruence of timelike geodesics:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta;\mu}\mechanical{u}^\mu
={}&-\frac23\mechanical{\theta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
-\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\mu}\mechanical{\sigma}^\mu{}_\beta
-\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\mu}\mechanical{\omega}^\mu{}_\beta\\
&+\frac13(\mechanical{\sigma}^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mu\nu}
-\mechanical{\omega}^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{\omega}_{\mu\nu})h_{\alpha\beta}
-C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\mechanical{u}^\mu\mechanical{u}^\nu
+\frac12R_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathrm{TT}},\\
\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta;\mu}\mechanical{u}^\mu
={}&-\frac23\mechanical{\theta}\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}
-\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\mu}\mechanical{\omega}^\mu{}_\beta
-\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\mu}\mechanical{\sigma}^\mu{}_\beta.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, $C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}$ is the Weyl tensor (Section 1.13, Problem 8), and $R_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathrm{TT}}\equiv R_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathrm T}-\tfrac13(h^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm T})h_{\alpha\beta}$ is the ‘transverse-tracefree’ part of the Ricci tensor; its transverse part is $R_{\alpha\beta}^{\mathrm T}=h_\alpha{}^\mu h_\beta{}^\nu R_{\mu\nu}$.

### 5

In this problem we consider a spacetime with metric

$$
\begin{aligned}
d\mechanical{s}^2={}&-d\mechanical{t}^2
+\frac{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}\,d\mechanical{r}^2
+(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})\,d\mechanical{\theta}^2\\
&+(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2)\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\phi}^2,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mechanical{a}$ is a constant, together with a congruence of null geodesics with tangent vector field

$$
\mechanical{k}^\alpha\partial_\alpha
=\partial_t+\partial_r
+\frac{\mechanical{a}}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}\,\partial_\phi.
$$

(a) Check that $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ is null, that it satisfies the geodesic equation, and that $\mechanical{r}$ is an affine parameter.

(b) Find a suitable auxiliary null vector $N^\alpha$ and calculate the congruence's expansion, shear, and rotation. In particular, verify the following results:

$$
\mechanical{\theta}=\frac{2\mechanical{r}}{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}},\quad
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}=0,\quad
\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}
=\frac{2\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}}
{(\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\theta})^2}.
$$

These reveal that the congruence is diverging, shear-free, and not hypersurface orthogonal.

(c) Show that the coordinate transformation

$$
\mechanical{x}=\sqrt{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\phi},\quad
\mechanical{y}=\sqrt{\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{a}^2}\sin\mechanical{\theta}\sin\mechanical{\phi},\quad
\mechanical{z}=\mechanical{r}\cos\mechanical{\theta}
$$

brings the metric to the standard Minkowski form for flat spacetime. Express $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ in this coordinate system.

### 6

The auxiliary null vector $N^\alpha$ introduced in Section 2.4 is not unique, and in this problem we examine various consequences of this fact. For the purpose of this discussion we introduce vectors $\hat e_A^\alpha$ $(A=2,3)$ that point in the two directions orthogonal to both $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ and $N^\alpha$, and we choose them to be orthonormal, so that they satisfy $g_{\alpha\beta}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta=\delta_{AB}$. We also introduce the $2\times2$ matrix

$$
\mechanical{B}_{AB}=\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta,
$$

the projection of the tensor $\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}=\mechanical{k}_{\alpha;\beta}$ in the transverse space spanned by the vectors $\hat e_A^\alpha$. In the following we shall use $\delta_{AB}$ and $\delta^{AB}$ to lower and raise uppercase Latin indices; for example, $\mechanical{B}^{AB}=\delta^{AM}\delta^{BN}\mechanical{B}_{MN}$.

(a) Derive the following relations:

$$
\begin{gathered}
h^{\alpha\beta}=\delta^{AB}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta,\qquad
\mechanical{\tilde B}^{\alpha\beta}=\mechanical{B}^{AB}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta,\\
\mechanical{\theta}=\delta_{AB}\mechanical{B}^{AB},\qquad
\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}=\mechanical{\sigma}^{AB}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta,\qquad
\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}=\mechanical{\omega}^{AB}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta,
\end{gathered}
$$

<!-- PDF page 73; printed page 57 -->

where $\mechanical{\sigma}^{AB}=\tfrac12(\mechanical{B}^{AB}+\mechanical{B}^{BA}-\mechanical{\theta}\delta^{AB})$ and $\mechanical{\omega}^{AB}=\tfrac12(\mechanical{B}^{AB}-\mechanical{B}^{BA})$. These confirm that the tensors $h_{\alpha\beta}$, $\mechanical{\tilde B}_{\alpha\beta}$, $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}$, and $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}$ are all orthogonal to both $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ and $N^\alpha$. We now must determine how a change of auxiliary null vector affects these results.

(b) The vector $N^\alpha$ must satisfy the relations $N^\alpha N_\alpha=0$ and $\mechanical{k}^\alpha N_\alpha=-1$. Prove that the transformation

$$
N^\alpha\to N'^\alpha=N^\alpha+c\mechanical{k}^\alpha+c^A\hat e_A^\alpha,
$$

where $c=\tfrac12c_Ac^A$, is the only one that preserves the defining relations for the auxiliary null vector. (The coefficients $c^A$ are arbitrary.)

(c) Calculate how $h^{\alpha\beta}$ changes under this transformation.

(d) Calculate how $\mechanical{\tilde B}^{\alpha\beta}$ changes.

(e) Show that $\mechanical{\theta}$ is invariant under the transformation.

(f) Prove that $\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}$ changes according to

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\sigma}'^{\alpha\beta}
={}&(c^Ac^B\mechanical{\sigma}_{AB})\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta
+(c^A\mechanical{\sigma}_A{}^B)\mechanical{k}^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta\\
&+(c^B\mechanical{\sigma}_B{}^A)\hat e_A^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta
+\mechanical{\sigma}^{AB}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta.
\end{aligned}
$$

This shows that if $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}=0$ for one choice of $N^\alpha$, then $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}=0$ for *any other* choice. Prove that $\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}$ is invariant under the transformation.

(g) Prove that $\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}$ changes according to

$$
\mechanical{\omega}'^{\alpha\beta}
=(c^A\mechanical{\omega}_A{}^B)\mechanical{k}^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta
-(c^B\mechanical{\omega}_B{}^A)\hat e_A^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta
+\mechanical{\omega}^{AB}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta.
$$

This shows that if $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$ for one choice of $N^\alpha$, then $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$ for *any other* choice. Prove that $\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}$ is invariant under the transformation.

The results imply that the Raychaudhuri equation is invariant under a change of auxiliary null vector field. They also show that $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$ implies hypersurface orthogonality for any choice of $N^\alpha$.

### 7

We want to derive evolution equations for the shear and rotation tensors of a congruence of null geodesics. For this purpose it is useful to refer back to the basis $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$, $N^\alpha$, $\hat e_A^\alpha$, and the $2\times2$ matrix $\mechanical{B}_{AB}=\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}\hat e_A^\alpha\hat e_B^\beta$, introduced in Problem 6. We shall also need

$$
R_{AB}=R_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\hat e_A^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\mu\hat e_B^\beta\mechanical{k}^\nu,\qquad
\Gamma_{AB}=\hat e_{B\mu}\hat e_A^\mu{}_{;\nu}\mechanical{k}^\nu.
$$

Notice that $R_{AB}$ is a symmetric matrix, while $\Gamma_{AB}$ is antisymmetric. Notice also that it is possible to set $\Gamma_{AB}=0$ by choosing $\hat e_A^\alpha$ to be parallel transported along the congruence.

(a) First, derive the main evolution equation,

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{B}_{AB}}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}
=-\mechanical{B}_{AC}\mechanical{B}^C{}_B-R_{AB}
+\Gamma_A{}^C\mechanical{B}_{CB}+\Gamma_B{}^C\mechanical{B}_{AC}.
$$

<!-- PDF page 74; printed page 58 -->

(b) Second, decompose the various matrices into their irreducible parts, as

$$
\mechanical{B}_{AB}
=\frac12\mechanical{\theta}\delta_{AB}+\mechanical{\sigma}_{AB}+\mechanical{\omega}_{AB},\qquad
R_{AB}=\frac12\mathcal R\,\delta_{AB}+C_{AB},
$$

where $\mechanical{\sigma}_{AB}$ and $C_{AB}$ are both symmetric and tracefree, while $\mechanical{\omega}_{AB}$ is antisymmetric. Prove that $\mathcal R=R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta$ and $C_{AB}=C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}\hat e_A^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\mu\hat e_B^\beta\mechanical{k}^\nu$, where $C_{\alpha\mu\beta\nu}$ is the Weyl tensor (Section 1.13, Problem 8). Then introduce the parameterization

$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}\mechanical{\sigma}_+&\mechanical{\sigma}_\times\\\mechanical{\sigma}_\times&-\mechanical{\sigma}_+\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}C_+&C_\times\\C_\times&-C_+\end{pmatrix}
$$

for the symmetric-tracefree matrices, and

$$
\mechanical{\omega}_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mechanical{\omega}\\-\mechanical{\omega}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Gamma_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\Gamma\\-\Gamma&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

for the antisymmetric matrices.

(c) Third, and finally, derive the following explicit forms for the evolution equations,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\mechanical{\theta}}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}
&=-\frac12\mechanical{\theta}^2
-2(\mechanical{\sigma}_+^2+\mechanical{\sigma}_\times^2)
+2\mechanical{\omega}^2-\mathcal R,\\
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}_+}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}
&=-\mechanical{\theta}\mechanical{\sigma}_+-C_++2\Gamma\mechanical{\sigma}_\times,\\
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}_\times}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}
&=-\mechanical{\theta}\mechanical{\sigma}_\times-C_\times-2\Gamma\mechanical{\sigma}_+,\\
\frac{d\mechanical{\omega}}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}
&=-\mechanical{\theta}\mechanical{\omega}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Check that the equation for $\mechanical{\theta}$ agrees with the form of Raychaudhuri's equation given in the text. Recall that we can always set $\Gamma=0$ by taking $\hat e_A^\alpha$ to be parallel transported along the congruence; this eliminates the coupling between the shear parameters.

### 8

Retrace the steps of Section 2.4, but without the assumption that the null geodesics are affinely parameterized. Show that:

(a) equation (2.35) stays unchanged;

(b) the expansion is now given by $\mechanical{\theta}=\mechanical{k}^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}-\mechanical{\kappa}$, where $\mechanical{\kappa}$ is defined by the relation $\mechanical{k}^\alpha{}_{;\beta}\mechanical{k}^\beta=\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{k}^\alpha$.

(c) Raychaudhuri's equation now takes the form

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\theta}}{d\mechanical{\lambda}}
=\mechanical{\kappa}\mechanical{\theta}
-\frac12\mechanical{\theta}^2
-\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
+\mechanical{\omega}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}
-R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}^\alpha\mechanical{k}^\beta.
$$
