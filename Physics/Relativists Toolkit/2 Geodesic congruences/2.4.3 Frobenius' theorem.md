> [!TLDR]
> A hypersurface-orthogonal null congruence has zero rotation and generates the null hypersurfaces to which it is orthogonal.

<!-- PDF page 64; printed page 48 -->

We now show that if the vector field $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ is such that $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$, then the congruence is hypersurface orthogonal, in the sense that $\mechanical{k}_\alpha$ must be proportional to the normal $\Phi_{,\alpha}$ of a family of hypersurfaces described by $\Phi(x^\alpha)=c$. These hypersurfaces must clearly be null: $g^{\alpha\beta}\Phi_{,\alpha}\Phi_{,\beta}\propto g^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{k}_\alpha\mechanical{k}_\beta=0$. Furthermore, because

<!-- PDF page 65; printed page 49 -->

![[relativists-toolkit-fig-2-7-null-hypersurfaces.svg|**Fig. 2.7** Family of hypersurfaces orthogonal to a congruence of null geodesics.]]

$\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ is at once parallel and orthogonal to $\Phi_{,\alpha}$ $(\mechanical{k}^\alpha\Phi_{,\alpha}=0)$, the vector $\mechanical{k}^\alpha$ is also *tangent* to the hypersurfaces. The null geodesics therefore lie *within* the hypersurfaces (Fig. 2.7); they are called the *null generators* of the hypersurfaces $\Phi(x^\alpha)=c$.

We begin with the general statement of Frobenius' theorem derived in Section 2.3.3: The congruence is hypersurface orthogonal if and only if $\mechanical{k}_{[\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{k}_{\gamma]}=0$. This condition implies $\mechanical{B}_{[\alpha\beta]}\mechanical{k}_\gamma+\mechanical{B}_{[\gamma\alpha]}\mechanical{k}_\beta+\mechanical{B}_{[\beta\gamma]}\mechanical{k}_\alpha=0$, and transvecting with $N^\gamma$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{B}_{[\alpha\beta]}
&=\mechanical{B}_{[\gamma\alpha]}\mechanical{k}_\beta N^\gamma
+\mechanical{B}_{[\beta\gamma]}\mechanical{k}_\alpha N^\gamma\\
&=\frac12(\mechanical{B}_{\gamma\alpha}\mechanical{k}_\beta
-\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\gamma}\mechanical{k}_\beta
+\mechanical{B}_{\beta\gamma}\mechanical{k}_\alpha
-\mechanical{B}_{\gamma\beta}\mechanical{k}_\alpha)N^\gamma\\
&=\mechanical{B}_{\gamma[\alpha}\mechanical{k}_{\beta]}N^\gamma
+\mechanical{k}_{[\alpha}\mechanical{B}_{\beta]\gamma}N^\gamma.
\end{aligned}
$$

But from Eq. (2.35) we also have

$$
\mechanical{\tilde B}_{[\alpha\beta]}
=\mechanical{B}_{[\alpha\beta]}
-\mechanical{B}_{\mu[\alpha}\mechanical{k}_{\beta]}N^\mu
-\mechanical{k}_{[\alpha}\mechanical{B}_{\beta]\mu}N^\mu,
$$

and it follows immediately that $\mechanical{\tilde B}_{[\alpha\beta]}=0$. We therefore can say

$$
\text{hypersurface orthogonal}\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0,
\tag{2.38}
$$

and this concludes the proof. (In Section 2.6, Problem 6 you will show that if $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$ for a specific choice of auxiliary null vector $N^\alpha$, then $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$ for all possible choices.)

The congruence is hypersurface orthogonal if there exists a scalar field $\Phi(x^\alpha)$ which is constant on the hypersurfaces and $\mechanical{k}_\alpha=-\mu\Phi_{,\alpha}$ for some scalar $\mu$. A

<!-- PDF page 66; printed page 50 -->

vector of this form automatically satisfies the geodesic equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{k}_{\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{k}^\beta
&=-(\mu\Phi_{;\alpha\beta}+\Phi_{,\alpha}\mu_{,\beta})\mechanical{k}^\beta\\
&=-(\mu_{,\beta}\Phi^{,\beta})\mechanical{k}_\alpha,
\end{aligned}
$$

where we have used $\Phi_{;\alpha\beta}\Phi^{,\beta}=\Phi_{;\beta\alpha}\Phi^{,\beta}=\tfrac12(\Phi_{,\beta}\Phi^{,\beta})_{,\alpha}=0$. This is the general form of the geodesic equation, corresponding to a parameterization that is not affine. Affine parameterization is recovered when $\mu_{,\alpha}\mechanical{k}^\alpha=0$, that is, when $\mu$ does not vary along the geodesics.
