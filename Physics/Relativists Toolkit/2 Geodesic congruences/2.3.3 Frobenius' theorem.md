> [!TLDR]
> Frobenius' theorem relates hypersurface orthogonality to an antisymmetric derivative; timelike geodesic flow then has zero rotation.

<!-- PDF page 54; printed page 38 -->

![[relativists-toolkit-fig-2-4-orthogonal-hypersurfaces.svg|**Fig. 2.4** Family of hypersurfaces orthogonal to a congruence of timelike geodesics.]]

Some congruences have a vanishing rotation tensor, $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$. These are said to be *hypersurface orthogonal*, meaning that the congruence is everywhere orthogonal to a family of spacelike hypersurfaces foliating $\mathcal O$ (Fig. 2.4). We now provide a partial proof of this statement.

The congruence will be hypersurface orthogonal if $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$ is everywhere proportional to $n^\alpha$, the normal to the hypersurfaces. Supposing that these are described by equations of the form $\Phi(x^\alpha)=c$, where $c$ is a constant specific to each hypersurface, then $n_\alpha\propto\Phi_{,\alpha}$ and

$$
\mechanical{u}_\alpha=-\mu\Phi_{,\alpha},
$$

for some proportionality factor $\mu$. (We suppose that $\Phi$ increases toward the future, and the positive quantity $\mu$ can be determined from the normalization condition $\mechanical{u}^\alpha\mechanical{u}_\alpha=-1$.) Differentiating this equation gives $\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\beta}=-\mu\Phi_{;\alpha\beta}-\Phi_{,\alpha}\mu_{,\beta}$. Consider now the completely antisymmetric tensor

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{u}_{\gamma]}
\equiv\frac{1}{3!}\big(&
\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{u}_\gamma
+\mechanical{u}_{\gamma;\alpha}\mechanical{u}_\beta
+\mechanical{u}_{\beta;\gamma}\mechanical{u}_\alpha\\
&-\mechanical{u}_{\beta;\alpha}\mechanical{u}_\gamma
-\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\gamma}\mechanical{u}_\beta
-\mechanical{u}_{\gamma;\beta}\mechanical{u}_\alpha\big).
\end{aligned}
$$

Direct evaluation of the right-hand side, using $\Phi_{;\beta\alpha}=\Phi_{;\alpha\beta}$, returns zero. We therefore have

$$
\text{hypersurface orthogonal}\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{u}_{\gamma]}=0.
\tag{2.18}
$$

The converse of this statement, that $\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{u}_{\gamma]}=0$ implies the existence of a scalar field $\Phi$ such that $\mechanical{u}_\alpha\propto\Phi_{,\alpha}$, is also true (but harder to prove).

<!-- PDF page 55; printed page 39 -->

Equation (2.18) is a useful result, because whether or not $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$ is hypersurface orthogonal can be decided on the basis of the vector field alone, without having to find $\Phi$ explicitly. We note that the geodesic equation $\mechanical{u}^\alpha{}_{;\beta}\mechanical{u}^\beta=0$ was never used in the derivation of Eq. (2.18). We also never used the fact that $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$ was normalized. Equation (2.18) is therefore quite general: A congruence of curves (timelike, spacelike, or null) is hypersurface orthogonal if and only if $\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{u}_{\gamma]}=0$, where $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$ is tangent to the curves. This statement is known as *Frobenius' theorem*.

We return to our geodesic congruence, and use Eqs. (2.15) and (2.17) to calculate

$$
\begin{aligned}
3!\,\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{u}_{\gamma]}
&=2(\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta]}\mechanical{u}_\gamma
+\mechanical{u}_{[\gamma;\alpha]}\mechanical{u}_\beta
+\mechanical{u}_{[\beta;\gamma]}\mechanical{u}_\alpha)\\
&=2(\mechanical{B}_{[\alpha\beta]}\mechanical{u}_\gamma
+\mechanical{B}_{[\gamma\alpha]}\mechanical{u}_\beta
+\mechanical{B}_{[\beta\gamma]}\mechanical{u}_\alpha)\\
&=2(\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u}_\gamma
+\mechanical{\omega}_{\gamma\alpha}\mechanical{u}_\beta
+\mechanical{\omega}_{\beta\gamma}\mechanical{u}_\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$

If we put the left-hand side to zero and multiply the right-hand side by $\mechanical{u}^\gamma$, we obtain $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$, because $\mechanical{\omega}_{\gamma\alpha}\mechanical{u}^\gamma=0=\mechanical{\omega}_{\beta\gamma}\mechanical{u}^\gamma$. (Recall the purely transverse property of $\mechanical{B}_{\alpha\beta}$.) Therefore,

$$
\text{hypersurface orthogonal}\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0.
\tag{2.19}
$$

This concludes the proof of our initial statement.

Notice that Eq. (2.19) holds for timelike geodesics only, whereas Eq. (2.18) is general. In fact, Eq. (2.19) could have been derived much more directly, but in doing so we would have bypassed the more general formulation of Frobenius' theorem. The direct proof goes as follows.

If $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$ is hypersurface orthogonal, then $\mechanical{u}_\alpha=-\mu\Phi_{,\alpha}$ for some scalars $\mu$ and $\Phi$. It follows from $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta]}$ and the symmetry of $\Phi_{;\alpha\beta}$ that

$$
\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=-\Phi_{[\alpha}\mu_{,\beta]}
=\frac{1}{\mu}\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha}\mu_{,\beta]}.
$$

But we know that $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}$ must be orthogonal to $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$, and the relation $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u}^\beta=0$ implies $\mu_{,\alpha}=-(\mu_{,\beta}\mechanical{u}^\beta)\mechanical{u}_\alpha$. This, in turn, establishes that the rotation tensor vanishes identically: $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$.

We have learned that $\mu$ must be constant on each hypersurface, because it varies only in the direction normal to the hypersurfaces. Thus, $\mu$ can be expressed as a function of $\Phi$, and defining a new scalar $\Psi=\int\mu(\Phi)\,d\Phi$ we find that $\mechanical{u}_\alpha$ is not only proportional to a gradient, it is *equal* to one: $\mechanical{u}_\alpha=-\Psi_{,\alpha}$. Notice that if $\mechanical{u}_\alpha$ can be expressed in this form, then it automatically satisfies the geodesic equation:

$$
\mechanical{u}_{\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{u}^\beta
=\Psi_{;\alpha\beta}\Psi^{,\beta}
=\Psi_{;\beta\alpha}\Psi^{,\beta}
=\frac12(\Psi^{,\beta}\Psi_{,\beta})_{;\alpha}
=\frac12(\mechanical{u}^\beta\mechanical{u}_\beta)_{;\alpha}=0.
$$

<!-- PDF page 56; printed page 40 -->

In summary:

A vector field $\mechanical{u}^\alpha$ (timelike, spacelike, or null, and not necessarily geodesic) is hypersurface orthogonal if there exists a scalar field $\Phi$ such that $\mechanical{u}_\alpha\propto\Phi_{,\alpha}$, which implies $\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta}\mechanical{u}_{\gamma]}=0$. If the vector field is timelike and geodesic, then it is hypersurface orthogonal if there exists a scalar field $\Psi$ such that $\mechanical{u}_\alpha=-\Psi_{,\alpha}$, which implies $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=\mechanical{u}_{[\alpha;\beta]}=0$.
