> [!TLDR]
> Radial marginally bound Schwarzschild geodesics have decreasing expansion for both ingoing and outgoing flow.

<!-- PDF page 58; printed page 42 -->

As a second example we consider a congruence of radial, marginally bound, timelike geodesics of the Schwarzschild spacetime. The metric is

$$
d\mechanical{s}^2=-f\,d\mechanical{t}^2+f^{-1}d\mechanical{r}^2+\mechanical{r}^2d\mechanical{\Omega}^2,
$$

where $f=1-2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}$ and $d\mechanical{\Omega}^2=d\mechanical{\theta}^2+\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,d\mechanical{\phi}^2$. For radial geodesics, $\mechanical{u}^{\theta}=\mechanical{u}^{\phi}=0$, and the geodesics are marginally bound if $1=\energy{\widetilde E}\equiv-\mechanical{u}_\alpha\xi^\alpha_{(t)}=-\mechanical{u}_t$. This means that the conserved energy is precisely equal to the rest-mass energy, and this gives the equation $\mechanical{u}^t=1/f$. From the normalization condition $g_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u}^\alpha\mechanical{u}^\beta=-1$ we also get $\mechanical{u}^r=\pm\sqrt{2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}$; the upper sign applies to outgoing geodesics, and the lower sign applies to ingoing geodesics.

The four-velocity is therefore given by

$$
\mechanical{u}^\alpha\partial_\alpha=f^{-1}\partial_t
\pm\sqrt{2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}\,\partial_r,\qquad
\mechanical{u}_\alpha\,dx^\alpha=-d\mechanical{t}
\pm f^{-1}\sqrt{2\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}\,d\mechanical{r}.
$$

It follows that $\mechanical{u}_\alpha$ is equal to a gradient: $\mechanical{u}_\alpha=-\Phi_{,\alpha}$, where

$$
\Phi=\mechanical{t}\mp4\mechanical{M}
\left[\sqrt{\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}}
+\frac12\ln\left(\frac{\sqrt{\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}}-1}{\sqrt{\mechanical{r}/2\mechanical{M}}+1}\right)\right].
$$

This means that the congruence is everywhere orthogonal to the spacelike hypersurfaces $\Phi=\text{constant}$.

The expansion is calculated as

$$
\mechanical{\theta}=\mechanical{u}^\alpha{}_{;\alpha}
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}(\sqrt{-g}\,\mechanical{u}^\alpha)_{,\alpha}
=\frac{1}{\mechanical{r}^2}(\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{u}^r)',
$$

where a prime indicates differentiation with respect to $\mechanical{r}$. Completing the calculation gives

$$
\mechanical{\theta}=\pm\frac32\sqrt{\frac{2\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}^3}}.
$$

Not surprisingly, the congruence is diverging $(\mechanical{\theta}>0)$ if the geodesics are outgoing, and converging $(\mechanical{\theta}<0)$ if the geodesics are ingoing. The rate of change of the

<!-- PDF page 59; printed page 43 -->

expansion is calculated as $d\mechanical{\theta}/d\mechanical{\tau}=(d\mechanical{\theta}/d\mechanical{r})(d\mechanical{r}/d\mechanical{\tau})=\mechanical{\theta}'\mechanical{u}^r$, and the result is

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\theta}}{d\mechanical{\tau}}=-\frac{9\mechanical{M}}{2\mechanical{r}^3}.
$$

As dictated by the focusing theorem, $d\mechanical{\theta}/d\mechanical{\tau}$ is negative in both cases.
