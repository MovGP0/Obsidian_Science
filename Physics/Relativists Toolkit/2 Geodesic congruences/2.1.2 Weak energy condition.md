> [!TLDR]
> The weak energy condition requires every timelike observer to measure nonnegative matter energy density.

<!-- PDF page 46; printed page 30 -->

The weak energy condition states that the energy density of any matter distribution, as measured by any observer in spacetime, must be nonnegative. Because an observer with four-velocity $\mechanical{v}^\alpha$ measures the energy density to be $\energy{T}_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{v}^\alpha\mechanical{v}^\beta$, we must have

$$
\energy{T}_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{v}^\alpha\mechanical{v}^\beta\ge0
\tag{2.6}
$$

for any future-directed timelike vector $\mechanical{v}^\alpha$. To put this in concrete form we substitute Eqs. (2.1) and (2.4), which gives

$$
\energy{\rho}+a^2\mechanical{p}_1+b^2\mechanical{p}_2+c^2\mechanical{p}_3\ge0.
$$

Because $a,b,c$ are arbitrary, we may choose $a=b=c=0$, and this gives $\energy{\rho}\ge0$. Alternatively, we may choose $b=c=0$, which gives $\energy{\rho}+a^2\mechanical{p}_1\ge0$. Recalling that $a^2$ must be smaller than unity, we obtain $0\le\energy{\rho}+a^2\mechanical{p}_1<\energy{\rho}+\mechanical{p}_1$. So $\energy{\rho}+\mechanical{p}_1>0$, and similar expressions hold for $\mechanical{p}_2$ and $\mechanical{p}_3$. The weak energy condition

<!-- PDF page 47; printed page 31 -->

therefore implies

$$
\energy{\rho}\ge0,\qquad\energy{\rho}+\mechanical{p}_i>0.
\tag{2.7}
$$

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |

| $\mechanical{p}_i$ | $\unit{\mathrm{m^{-2}}}$ | Principal pressures | The pressure eigenvalues in geometrized units. |
| $\mechanical{v}^\alpha$ | — | Observer four-velocity | The observer's future-directed timelike velocity. |
