> [!TLDR]
> A hypersurface-orthogonal timelike congruence focuses when the strong energy condition holds and can form a caustic in finite proper time.

<!-- PDF page 56; printed page 40 -->

The importance of Eq. (2.20) for general relativity is revealed by the following theorem: Let a congruence of timelike geodesics be *hypersurface orthogonal*, so that $\mechanical{\omega}_{\alpha\beta}=0$, and let the *strong energy condition* hold, so that (by virtue of the Einstein field equations) $R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u}^\alpha\mechanical{u}^\beta\ge0$. Then the Raychaudhuri equation implies

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\theta}}{d\mechanical{\tau}}
=-\frac13\mechanical{\theta}^2
-\mechanical{\sigma}^{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{\sigma}_{\alpha\beta}
-R_{\alpha\beta}\mechanical{u}^\alpha\mechanical{u}^\beta\le0.
$$

<!-- PDF page 57; printed page 41 -->

![[relativists-toolkit-fig-2-5-caustic.svg|**Fig. 2.5** Geodesics converge into a caustic of the congruence.]]

The expansion must therefore *decrease* during the congruence's evolution. Thus, an initially diverging $(\mechanical{\theta}>0)$ congruence will diverge less rapidly in the future, while an initially converging $(\mechanical{\theta}<0)$ congruence will converge more rapidly in the future. This is the statement of the focusing theorem. Its physical interpretation is that gravitation is an attractive force when the strong energy condition holds, and the geodesics get focused as a result of this attraction.

It also follows from Raychaudhuri's equation that under the conditions of the focusing theorem, $d\mechanical{\theta}/d\mechanical{\tau}\le-\tfrac13\mechanical{\theta}^2$. This can be integrated at once, giving

$$
\mechanical{\theta}^{-1}(\mechanical{\tau})\ge\mechanical{\theta}_0^{-1}+\frac{\mechanical{\tau}}{3},
$$

where $\mechanical{\theta}_0\equiv\mechanical{\theta}(0)$. This shows that if the congruence is initially converging $(\mechanical{\theta}_0<0)$, then $\mechanical{\theta}(\mechanical{\tau})\to-\infty$ within a proper time $\mechanical{\tau}\le3/|\mechanical{\theta}_0|$. The interpretation of this result is that the congruence will develop a *caustic*, a point at which some of the geodesics come together (Fig. 2.5). Obviously, a caustic is a singularity of the congruence, and equations such as (2.20) lose their meaning at such points.
