> [!TLDR]
> The Fermi normal metric equals the Minkowski metric everywhere on the reference geodesic.

<!-- PDF page 38; printed page 22 -->

The components of the metric in the Fermi normal coordinates are related to the old components by the general relation

$$
g_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\partial x^{\alpha'}}{\partial x^\alpha}\frac{\partial x^{\beta'}}{\partial x^\beta}g_{\alpha'\beta'}.
$$

Evaluating this on $\gamma$ yields $g_{\alpha\beta}|_\gamma=\hat e^{\alpha'}_\alpha\hat e^{\beta'}_\beta g_{\alpha'\beta'}$, after using Eqs. (1.54) and (1.55). Substituting Eq. (1.49) we arrive at

$$
g_{\alpha\beta}|_\gamma=\eta_{\alpha\beta}. \tag{1.58}
$$

This states that in the Fermi normal coordinates, the metric is Minkowski everywhere on the geodesic $\gamma$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $g_{\alpha\beta}$ | $1$ | Fermi metric tensor | The metric in Fermi normal coordinates. |
| $g_{\alpha'\beta'}$ | — | Original metric tensor | The metric components in the original coordinates. |
| $\eta_{\alpha\beta}$ | $1$ | Minkowski metric | The metric on the reference geodesic. |
