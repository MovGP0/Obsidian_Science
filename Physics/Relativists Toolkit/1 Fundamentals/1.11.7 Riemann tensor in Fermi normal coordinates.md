> [!TLDR]
> Projection onto the transported orthonormal frame gives the Riemann tensor components needed for the Fermi normal metric.

<!-- PDF page 40; printed page 24 -->

To express a given metric as an expansion in Fermi normal coordinates, it is necessary to evaluate the Riemann tensor on the reference geodesic, and write it as a function of time in this coordinate system. This is not as hard as it may seem. Because the Riemann tensor is evaluated only on $\gamma$, we just need to know the coordinate transformation only at $\gamma$; as was noted above, this is given by $\partial x^{\alpha'}/\partial x^\mu=\hat e^{\alpha'}_\mu$. We therefore have, for example,

$$
R_{tabc}(t)=R_{\mu'\alpha'\beta'\gamma'}\hat e^{\mu'}_t\hat e^{\alpha'}_a\hat e^{\beta'}_b\hat e^{\gamma'}_c.
$$

The difficult part of the calculation is therefore the determination of the orthonormal basis (which is parallel transported on the reference geodesic). Once this is known, the Fermi components of the Riemann tensor are obtained by projection, and these will naturally be expressed in terms of proper time $t$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $R_{tabc}$ | $\unit{m^{-2}}$ | Fermi curvature components | The components obtained by projection onto the orthonormal frame. |
| $R_{\mu'\alpha'\beta'\gamma'}$ | — | Original curvature components | The Riemann tensor components in the original coordinates. |
| $t$ | $\unit{m}$ | Proper time | The proper time along the reference geodesic. |
