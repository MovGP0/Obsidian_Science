> [!TLDR]
> The Riemann tensor describes curvature; its contractions lead to the Einstein equations and energy-momentum conservation.

<!-- PDF page 31; printed page 15 -->

The *Riemann tensor* $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}$ may be defined by the relation

$$
A^\mu{}_{;\alpha\beta}-A^\mu{}_{;\beta\alpha}=-R^\mu{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}A^\nu, \tag{1.31}
$$

which holds for any vector field $A^\alpha$. Evaluating the left-hand side explicitly yields

$$
R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}=\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\delta,\gamma}-\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma,\delta}
+\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\gamma}\Gamma^\mu_{\beta\delta}-\Gamma^\alpha_{\mu\delta}\Gamma^\mu_{\beta\gamma}. \tag{1.32}
$$

The Riemann tensor is obviously antisymmetric in the last two indices. Its other symmetry properties can be established by evaluating $R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}$ in a local Lorentz frame at some point $P$. A straightforward computation gives

$$
R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\overset{*}{=}\frac12
(g_{\alpha\delta,\beta\gamma}-g_{\alpha\gamma,\beta\delta}-g_{\beta\delta,\alpha\gamma}+g_{\beta\gamma,\alpha\delta}),
$$

and this implies the tensorial relations

$$
R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=-R_{\beta\alpha\gamma\delta}=-R_{\alpha\beta\delta\gamma}=R_{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta} \tag{1.33}
$$

and

$$
R_{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}+R_{\mu\gamma\alpha\beta}+R_{\mu\beta\gamma\alpha}=0, \tag{1.34}
$$

which are valid in any coordinate system. A little more work along the same lines reveals that the Riemann tensor satisfies the *Bianchi identities*,

$$
R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta;\gamma}+R_{\mu\nu\gamma\alpha;\beta}+R_{\mu\nu\beta\gamma;\alpha}=0. \tag{1.35}
$$

In addition to Eq. (1.31), the Riemann tensor satisfies the relations

$$
p_{\mu;\alpha\beta}-p_{\mu;\beta\alpha}=R^\nu{}_{\mu\alpha\beta}p_\nu \tag{1.36}
$$

and

$$
T^\mu{}_{\nu;\alpha\beta}-T^\mu{}_{\nu;\beta\alpha}=
-R^\mu{}_{\lambda\alpha\beta}T^\lambda{}_\nu+R^\lambda{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}T^\mu{}_\lambda, \tag{1.37}
$$

<!-- PDF page 32; printed page 16; text before section 1.10 -->

which hold for arbitrary tensors $p_\alpha$ and $T^\alpha{}_\beta$. Generalization to tensors of higher ranks is obvious: the number of Riemann-tensor terms on the right-hand side is equal to the number of tensorial indices.

Contractions of the Riemann tensor produce the *Ricci tensor* $R_{\alpha\beta}$ and the *Ricci scalar* $R$. These are defined by

$$
R_{\alpha\beta}=R^\mu{}_{\alpha\mu\beta},\qquad R=R^\alpha{}_\alpha. \tag{1.38}
$$

It is easy to show that $R_{\alpha\beta}$ is a symmetric tensor. The *Einstein tensor* is defined by

$$
G_{\alpha\beta}=R_{\alpha\beta}-\frac12 Rg_{\alpha\beta}; \tag{1.39}
$$

this is also a symmetric tensor. By virtue of Eq. (1.35), the Einstein tensor satisfies

$$
G^{\alpha\beta}{}_{;\beta}=0, \tag{1.40}
$$

the contracted Bianchi identities.

The *Einstein field equations*,

$$
G^{\alpha\beta}=8\pi\,\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}, \tag{1.41}
$$

relate the spacetime curvature (as represented by the Einstein tensor) to the distribution of matter (as represented by $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}$, the stress–energy tensor). Equation (1.40) implies that the stress–energy tensor must have a zero divergence: $\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}{}_{;\beta}=0$. This is the tensorial expression for energy–momentum conservation. Equation (1.40) implies also that of the ten equations (1.41), only six are independent. The metric can therefore be determined up to four arbitrary functions, and this reflects our complete freedom in choosing the coordinate system. We note that the field equations can also be written in the form

$$
R^{\alpha\beta}=8\pi\left(\energy{T^{\alpha\beta}}-\frac12\energy{T}g^{\alpha\beta}\right), \tag{1.42}
$$

where $\energy{T}\equiv\energy{T^\alpha{}_\alpha}$ is the trace of the stress–energy tensor.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |

| $g_{\alpha\beta},g^{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Metric and inverse metric | The gravitational field; component units depend on coordinates. |

| $R_{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Ricci tensor | A contraction of the curvature tensor. |
| $R$ | $\unit{m^{-2}}$ | Ricci scalar | The scalar contraction of the Ricci tensor. |
| $G_{\alpha\beta},G^{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Einstein tensor | The curvature combination in Einstein’s equations. |
