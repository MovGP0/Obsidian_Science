> [!TLDR]
> An orthonormal frame transported along the reference geodesic gives spatial coordinates through orthogonal spacelike geodesics.

<!-- PDF page 35; printed page 19 -->

We will use $x^\alpha=(t,x^a)$ to denote the Fermi normal coordinates, and $x^{\alpha'}$ will refer to an arbitrary coordinate system. We imagine that we are given a spacetime with a metric $g_{\alpha'\beta'}$ expressed in these coordinates.

We consider a timelike geodesic $\gamma$ in this spacetime. Its tangent vector is $u^{\alpha'}$, and we let $t$ be proper time along $\gamma$. On this geodesic we select a point $O$ at which we set $t=0$. At this point we erect an orthonormal basis $\hat e^{\alpha'}_\mu$ (the subscript $\mu$ serves to label the four basis vectors), and we identify $\hat e^{\alpha'}_t$ with the tangent vector $u^{\alpha'}$ at $O$. From this we construct a basis everywhere on $\gamma$ by parallel transporting $\hat e^{\alpha'}_\mu$ away from $O$. Our basis vectors therefore satisfy

$$
\hat e^{\alpha'}_{\mu;\beta'}u^{\beta'}=0,\qquad\hat e^{\alpha'}_t=u^{\alpha'}, \tag{1.48}
$$

as well as

$$
g_{\alpha'\beta'}\hat e^{\alpha'}_\mu\hat e^{\beta'}_\nu=\eta_{\mu\nu}, \tag{1.49}
$$

everywhere on $\gamma$. Here, $\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)$ is the Minkowski metric.

Consider now a spacelike geodesic $\beta$, originating at a point $P$ on $\gamma$, at which $t=t_P$. This geodesic has a tangent vector $v^{\alpha'}$, and we let $s$ denote proper distance along $\beta$; we set $s=0$ at $P$. We assume that at $P$, $v^{\alpha'}$ is orthogonal to $u^{\alpha'}$, so that it admits the decomposition

$$
v^{\alpha'}|_\gamma=\Omega^a\hat e^{\alpha'}_a. \tag{1.50}
$$

To ensure that $v^{\alpha'}$ is properly normalized, the expansion coefficients must satisfy $\delta_{ab}\Omega^a\Omega^b=1$. By choosing different coefficients $\Omega^a$ we can construct new geodesics $\beta$ that are also orthogonal to $\gamma$ at $P$. We shall denote this entire family of spacelike geodesics by $\beta(t_P,\Omega^a)$.

The Fermi normal coordinates of a point $Q$ located away from the geodesic $\gamma$ are constructed as follows (Fig. 1.4). First we find the unique geodesic that passes through $Q$ and intersects $\gamma$ orthogonally. Let the intersection point be $P$, and

we call this geodesic $\beta(t_P,\Omega^a_Q)$, with $t_P$ denoting proper time at the intersection point and $\Omega^a_Q$ the expansion coefficients of $v^{\alpha'}$ at that point. We then assign to $Q$ the new coordinates

$$
x^0=t_P,\qquad x^a=\Omega^a_Q s_Q, \tag{1.51}
$$

where $s_Q$ is proper distance from $P$ to $Q$. These are the Fermi normal coordinates of the point $Q$. Generically, therefore, $x^\alpha=(t,\Omega^a s)$, and we must now figure out how these coordinates are related to $x^{\alpha'}$, the original system.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t,t_P$ | $\unit{m}$ | Proper time | Proper time along γ in geometrized units. |
| $s,s_Q$ | $\unit{m}$ | Proper distance | Distance along an orthogonal spacelike geodesic. |
| $x^0,x^a$ | $\unit{m}$ | Fermi coordinates | The assigned time and spatial coordinates. |
| $\Omega^a,\Omega^a_Q$ | $1$ | Spatial direction components | The normalized components that select the spacelike geodesic. |
| $u^{\alpha'},v^{\alpha'}$ | — | Geodesic tangents | The tangent components depend on the original coordinates. |
| $g_{\alpha'\beta'}$ | — | Metric tensor | The metric in the original coordinate system. |
