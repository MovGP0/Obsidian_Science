> [!TLDR]
> Killing vectors describe metric symmetries and give conserved quantities along geodesics.

<!-- PDF page 26; printed page 10 -->

If, in a given coordinate system, the components of the metric do not depend on $x^0$, then by the preceding theorem $\mathcal{L}_\xi g_{\alpha\beta}=0$, where $\xi^\alpha\overset{*}{=}\delta^\alpha_0$. The vector $\xi^\alpha$ is then called a *Killing vector*. The condition for $\xi^\alpha$ to be a Killing vector is that

$$
0=\mathcal{L}_\xi g_{\alpha\beta}=\xi_{\alpha;\beta}+\xi_{\beta;\alpha}. \tag{1.21}
$$

Thus, the tensor $\xi_{\alpha;\beta}$ is antisymmetric if $\xi^\alpha$ is a Killing vector.

Killing vectors can be used to find constants associated with the motion along a geodesic. Suppose that $u^\alpha$ is tangent to a geodesic affinely parameterized by $\lambda$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{d\lambda}(u^\alpha\xi_\alpha)&=(u^\alpha\xi_\alpha)_{;\beta}u^\beta\\
&=u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta\xi_\alpha+\xi_{\alpha;\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta\\
&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

In the second line, the first term vanishes by virtue of the geodesic equation, and the second term vanishes because $\xi_{\alpha;\beta}$ is an antisymmetric tensor while $u^\alpha u^\beta$ is symmetric. Thus, $u^\alpha\xi_\alpha$ is constant along the geodesic.

As an example, consider a static, spherically symmetric spacetime with metric

$$
ds^2=-A(r)\,dt^2+B(r)\,dr^2+r^2\,d\Omega^2,
$$

where $d\Omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2$. Because the metric does not depend on $t$ nor $\phi$, the vectors

$$
\xi^\alpha_{(t)}=\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial t},\qquad
\xi^\alpha_{(\phi)}=\frac{\partial x^\alpha}{\partial\phi}
$$

are Killing vectors. The quantities

$$
\energy{\widetilde E}=-u_\alpha\xi^\alpha_{(t)},\qquad
\mechanical{\widetilde L}=u_\alpha\xi^\alpha_{(\phi)}
$$

are then constant along a geodesic to which $u^\alpha$ is tangent. If the geodesic is timelike and $u^\alpha$ is the four-velocity of a particle moving on that geodesic, then $\energy{\widetilde E}$ and $\mechanical{\widetilde L}$ can be interpreted as energy and angular momentum per unit mass, respectively. It should also be noted that spherical symmetry implies the existence of two

<!-- PDF page 27; printed page 11; text before section 1.6 -->

additional Killing vectors,

$$
\xi^\alpha_{(1)}\partial_\alpha=\sin\phi\,\partial_\theta+\cot\theta\cos\phi\,\partial_\phi,\qquad
\xi^\alpha_{(2)}\partial_\alpha=-\cos\phi\,\partial_\theta+\cot\theta\sin\phi\,\partial_\phi.
$$

It is straightforward to show that these do indeed satisfy Killing’s equation (1.21). (To prove this is the purpose of Section 1.13, Problem 5.)
