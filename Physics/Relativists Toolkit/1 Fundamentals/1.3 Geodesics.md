> [!TLDR]
> Geodesics extremize distance; an affine parameter removes the tangent term from their equation.

<!-- PDF page 22; printed page 6 -->

A curve is a *geodesic* if it extremizes the distance between two fixed points. Let a curve $\gamma$ be described by the relations $x^\alpha(\lambda)$, where $\lambda$ is an arbitrary parameter, and let $P$ and $Q$ be two points on this curve. The distance between $P$ and $Q$ along $\gamma$ is given by

$$
\ell=\int_P^Q\sqrt{\pm g_{\alpha\beta}\dot x^\alpha\dot x^\beta}\,d\lambda, \tag{1.12}
$$

where $\dot x^\alpha\equiv dx^\alpha/d\lambda$. In the square root, the positive (negative) sign is chosen if the curve is spacelike (timelike); it is assumed that $\gamma$ is nowhere null. It should be clear that $\ell$ is invariant under a reparameterization of the curve, $\lambda\to\lambda'(\lambda)$.

The curve for which $\ell$ is an extremum is determined by substituting the ‘Lagrangian’ $L(\dot x^\mu,x^\mu)=(\pm g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu)^{1/2}$ into the Euler–Lagrange equations,

$$
\frac{d}{d\lambda}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\alpha}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x^\alpha}=0.
$$

<!-- PDF page 23; printed page 7 -->

A straightforward calculation shows that $x^\alpha(\lambda)$ must satisfy the differential equation

$$
\ddot x^\alpha+\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}\dot x^\beta\dot x^\gamma=\kappa(\lambda)\dot x^\alpha
\qquad\text{(arbitrary parameter)}, \tag{1.13}
$$

where $\kappa\equiv d\ln L/d\lambda$. The *geodesic equation* can also be written as $u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta=\kappa u^\alpha$, in which $u^\alpha=\dot x^\alpha$ is tangent to the geodesic.

A particularly useful choice of parameter is proper time $\tau$ when the geodesic is timelike, or proper distance $s$ when the geodesic is spacelike. (It is important that this choice be made *after* extremization, and not before.) Because $d\tau^2=-g_{\alpha\beta}dx^\alpha dx^\beta$ for timelike geodesics and $ds^2=g_{\alpha\beta}dx^\alpha dx^\beta$ for spacelike geodesics, we have that $L=1$ in either case, and this implies $\kappa=0$. The geodesic equation becomes

$$
\ddot x^\alpha+\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}\dot x^\beta\dot x^\gamma=0
\qquad\text{(affine parameter)}, \tag{1.14}
$$

or $u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta=0$, which states that the tangent vector is parallel transported along the geodesic. These equations are invariant under reparameterizations of the form $\lambda\to\lambda'=a\lambda+b$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants. Parameters related to $s$ and $\tau$ by such transformations are called *affine parameters*. It is useful to note that Eq. (1.14) can be recovered by substituting $L'=\frac12 g_{\alpha\beta}\dot x^\alpha\dot x^\beta$ into the Euler–Lagrange equations; this gives rise to a practical method of computing the Christoffel symbols.

By continuity, the general form $u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta=\kappa u^\alpha$ for the geodesic equation must be valid also for null geodesics. For this to be true, the parameter $\lambda$ cannot be affine, because $ds=d\tau=0$ along a null geodesic, and the limit is then singular. However, affine parameters can nevertheless be found for null geodesics. Starting from Eq. (1.13) it is always possible to introduce a new parameter $\lambda^*$ such that the geodesic equation will take the form of Eq. (1.14). It is easy to check that the appropriate transformation is

$$
\frac{d\lambda^*}{d\lambda}=\exp\left[\int^\lambda\kappa(\lambda')\,d\lambda'\right]. \tag{1.15}
$$

(You will be asked to provide a proof of this statement in Section 1.13, Problem 2.) It should be noted that while the null version of Eq. (1.13) was obtained by a limiting procedure, the null version of Eq. (1.14) cannot be considered to be a limit of the same equation for timelike or spacelike geodesics: the parameterization is highly discontinuous.

We conclude this section with the following remark: Along an affinely parameterized geodesic (timelike, spacelike, or null), the scalar quantity $\varepsilon=u^\alpha u_\alpha$ is a constant. The proof requires a single line:

$$
\frac{d\varepsilon}{d\lambda}=(u^\alpha u_\alpha)_{;\beta}u^\beta=
(u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta)u_\alpha+u^\alpha(u_{\alpha;\beta}u^\beta)=0.
$$

If proper time or proper distance is chosen for $\lambda$, then $\varepsilon=\mp1$, respectively. For a null geodesic, $\varepsilon=0$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\ell$ | $\unit{m}$ | Curve length | The invariant distance between P and Q along the curve. |
| $\tau$ | $\unit{m}$ | Proper time | Proper time in the source’s geometrized units, c = 1. |
| $s$ | $\unit{m}$ | Proper distance | The proper distance along a spacelike geodesic. |
| $g_{\alpha\beta}$ | — | Metric tensor | The gravitational field; component units depend on the coordinates. |
| $u^\alpha$ | — | Tangent vector | The tangent; its units depend on the coordinates and curve parameter. |
| $\varepsilon$ | $1$ | Tangent norm | The normalized squared tangent norm for the stated proper parameters. |
