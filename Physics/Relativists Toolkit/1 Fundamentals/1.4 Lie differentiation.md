> [!TLDR]
> The Lie derivative compares a tensor along a flow without a connection and expresses invariance under a change of position.

<!-- PDF page 24; printed page 8 -->

In Section 1.2, covariant differentiation was defined by introducing a rule to transport a tensor from a point $Q$ to a neighbouring point $P$, at which the derivative was to be evaluated. This rule involved the introduction of a new structure on the manifold, the connection. In this section we define another type of derivative—the Lie derivative—without introducing any additional structure.

Consider a curve $\gamma$, its tangent vector $u^\alpha=dx^\alpha/d\lambda$, and a vector field $A^\alpha$ defined in a neighbourhood of $\gamma$ (Fig. 1.2). As before, the point $P$ shall have the coordinates $x^\alpha$, while the point $Q$ shall be at $x^\alpha+dx^\alpha$. The equation

$$
x^{\prime\alpha}\equiv x^\alpha+dx^\alpha=x^\alpha+u^\alpha d\lambda
$$

can be interpreted as an infinitesimal coordinate transformation from the system $x$ to the system $x'$. Under this transformation, the vector $A^\alpha$ becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{\prime\alpha}(x')&=\frac{\partial x^{\prime\alpha}}{\partial x^\beta}A^\beta(x)\\
&=(\delta^\alpha_\beta+u^\alpha{}_{,\beta}\,d\lambda)A^\beta(x)\\
&=A^\alpha(x)+u^\alpha{}_{,\beta}A^\beta(x)\,d\lambda.
\end{aligned}
$$

In other words,

$$
A^{\prime\alpha}(Q)=A^\alpha(P)+u^\alpha{}_{,\beta}A^\beta(P)\,d\lambda.
$$

On the other hand, $A^\alpha(Q)$, the value of the original vector field at the point $Q$, can be expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^\alpha(Q)&=A^\alpha(x+dx)\\
&=A^\alpha(x)+A^\alpha{}_{,\beta}(x)\,dx^\beta\\
&=A^\alpha(P)+u^\beta A^\alpha{}_{,\beta}(P)\,d\lambda.
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, $A^{\prime\alpha}(Q)$ and $A^\alpha(Q)$ will not be equal. Their difference defines the *Lie derivative* of the vector $A^\alpha$ along the curve $\gamma$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_u A^\alpha(P)\equiv\frac{A^\alpha(Q)-A^{\prime\alpha}(Q)}{d\lambda}.
$$

Combining the previous three equations yields

$$
\mathcal{L}_u A^\alpha=A^\alpha{}_{,\beta}u^\beta-u^\alpha{}_{,\beta}A^\beta. \tag{1.16}
$$

<!-- PDF page 25; printed page 9 -->

Despite an appearance to the contrary, $\mathcal{L}_u A^\alpha$ is a tensor: It is easy to check that Eq. (1.16) is equivalent to

$$
\mathcal{L}_u A^\alpha=A^\alpha{}_{;\beta}u^\beta-u^\alpha{}_{;\beta}A^\beta, \tag{1.17}
$$

whose tensorial nature is evident. The fact that $\mathcal{L}_u A^\alpha$ can be defined without a connection means that Lie differentiation is a more primitive operation than covariant differentiation.

The definition of the Lie derivative extends to all types of tensors. For scalars, $\mathcal{L}_u f\equiv df/d\lambda=f_{,\alpha}u^\alpha$. For dual vectors, the same steps reveal that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_u p_\alpha&=p_{\alpha,\beta}u^\beta+u^\beta{}_{,\alpha}p_\beta\\
&=p_{\alpha;\beta}u^\beta+u^\beta{}_{;\alpha}p_\beta.
\end{aligned} \tag{1.18}
$$

As another example, the Lie derivative of a type-$(1,1)$ tensor is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_u T^\alpha{}_\beta&=T^\alpha{}_{\beta,\mu}u^\mu-u^\alpha{}_{,\mu}T^\mu{}_\beta+u^\mu{}_{,\beta}T^\alpha{}_\mu\\
&=T^\alpha{}_{\beta;\mu}u^\mu-u^\alpha{}_{;\mu}T^\mu{}_\beta+u^\mu{}_{;\beta}T^\alpha{}_\mu.
\end{aligned} \tag{1.19}
$$

Further generalizations are obvious. It may be verified that the Lie derivative obeys the product rule of differential calculus. For example, the relation

$$
\mathcal{L}_u(A^\alpha p_\alpha)=(\mathcal{L}_u A^\alpha)p_\alpha+A^\alpha(\mathcal{L}_u p_\alpha) \tag{1.20}
$$

is easily established.

A tensor field $T^{\alpha\cdots}{}_{\beta\cdots}$ is said to be *Lie transported* along a curve $\gamma$ if its Lie derivative along the curve vanishes: $\mathcal{L}_u T^{\alpha\cdots}{}_{\beta\cdots}=0$, where $u^\alpha$ is the curve’s tangent vector. Suppose that the coordinates are chosen so that $x^1$, $x^2$, and $x^3$ are all constant on $\gamma$, while $x^0\equiv\lambda$ varies on $\gamma$. In such a coordinate system,

$$
u^\alpha=\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\lambda}\overset{*}{=}\delta^\alpha_0,
$$

where the symbol ‘$\overset{*}{=}$’ means ‘equals in the specified coordinate system’. It follows that $u^\alpha{}_{,\beta}\overset{*}{=}0$, so that

$$
\mathcal{L}_u T^{\alpha\cdots}{}_{\beta\cdots}\overset{*}{=}
T^{\alpha\cdots}{}_{\beta\cdots,\mu}u^\mu\overset{*}{=}
\frac{\partial}{\partial x^0}T^{\alpha\cdots}{}_{\beta\cdots}.
$$

If the tensor is Lie transported along $\gamma$, then the tensor’s components are all independent of $x^0$ in the specified coordinate system.

We have formulated the following theorem:

If $\mathcal{L}_u T^{\alpha\cdots}{}_{\beta\cdots}=0$, that is, if a tensor is Lie transported along a curve $\gamma$ with tangent vector $u^\alpha$, then a coordinate system can be constructed such that $u^\alpha\overset{*}{=}\delta^\alpha_0$ and $T^{\alpha\cdots}{}_{\beta\cdots,0}\overset{*}{=}0$. Conversely, if in a given coordinate system the components of

a tensor do not depend on a particular coordinate $x^0$, then the Lie derivative of the tensor in the direction of $u^\alpha$ vanishes.

The Lie derivative is therefore the natural construct to express, covariantly, the invariance of a tensor under a change of position.
