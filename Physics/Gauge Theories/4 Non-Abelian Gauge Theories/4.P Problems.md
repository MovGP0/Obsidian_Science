### 4.1

Derive the Yang–Mills Lagrangian for a scalar field theory in which the three real scalar fields correspond to the triplet representation of SU(2). The free-particle Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=\frac12[(\partial_\mu\boldsymbol\phi)^2-\mechanical{m}^2\boldsymbol\phi^2],
$$

<!-- PDF page 81; printed page 67 -->

with

$$
\boldsymbol\phi=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_1\\\phi_2\\\phi_3\end{pmatrix}.
$$

### 4.2

(a) By making the minimal substitution $\partial_\mu\rightarrow D_\mu$ in the free-particle Lagrangian, construct a theory of the electrodynamics of a massive spin-1 boson $V^\pm$ and deduce the Feynman rules for the theory.

(b) Now, compute the differential cross section $d\sigma/d\Omega$ and the total cross section $\sigma\equiv\int d\Omega(d\sigma/d\Omega)$ for the reaction $e^+e^-\rightarrow V^+V^-$ for various helicities of the produced particles. Work in the c.m. frame and in the high-energy limit (where all the masses may be neglected). Assume the colliding beams are unpolarized. Compare the results with the cross sections for $e^+e^-\rightarrow\sigma^+\sigma^-$ and $e^+e^-\rightarrow\mu^+\mu^-$ derived in problems 1.4 and 1.5. [For Feynman rules, see J. D. Bjorken and S. D. Drell, *Relativistic Quantum Mechanics*, McGraw-Hill, New York, 1964, appendix B.]

### 4.3

Show that the transformations

$$
B'_\mu=GB_\mu G^{-1}+\frac{i}{g}(\partial_\mu G)G^{-1}
=G\left[B_\mu+\frac{i}{g}G^{-1}(\partial_\mu G)\right]G^{-1} \tag{4.2.10}
$$

of the gauge fields form a group—that is, under successive transformations on the matter field given by $\psi\rightarrow\psi'=G_1\psi\rightarrow\psi''=G_2\psi'$, the transformation of the gauge field is characterized by $G=G_2G_1$.

### 4.4

Apply the analysis of nucleon–nucleon forces of section 4.3 to the nucleon–antinucleon case. Compare the Yang–Mills “predictions” with the spectrum of nonstrange mesons.

### 4.5

The flavor symmetry $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{isospin}}$ and the rotational symmetry $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{spin}}$ may be combined systematically in the group $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$. In nuclear physics, this symmetry group provides the basis for classification into “Wigner supermultiplets” [E. P. Wigner, *Phys. Rev.* **51**, 106 (1937)]. For the quark model, the fundamental representation of $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$ is

$$
\mathbf4\equiv\begin{pmatrix}u_\uparrow\\u_\downarrow\\d_\uparrow\\d_\downarrow\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Using the notation $(2I+1,2s+1)$ for the isospin $\times$ spin decomposition of $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$ representations, we may write $\mathbf4=(2,2)$, which recalls that the $\mathbf4$ transforms as a doublet under isospin rotations and under spin rotations.

(a) Work out the $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$ content of the product $\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4^*$. Characterize each representation by its Young tableau, symmetry properties, and dimension.

(b) Give the $(2I+1,2s+1)$ content of each of the $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$ representations in your decomposition of $\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4^*$.

<!-- PDF page 82; printed page 68 -->

(c) Consider the implied $q\bar q$ bound states. Show that a bound state with orbital angular momentum $\mechanical{L}$, isospin $I$, and total spin $S$ must have quantum numbers

$$
C=(-1)^{\mechanical{L}+S};\quad P=(-1)^{\mechanical{L}+1};\quad G=(-1)^{\mechanical{L}+S+I}.
$$

Confirm that $\rho,\omega,\pi$ may be assigned to the $s$-wave $\mathbf{15}$, whereas $\eta$ corresponds to $\mathbf1$. (The physical $\eta$ contains an $s\bar s$ admixture.)

### 4.6

Now consider some implications of $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$ for baryons.

(a) Work out the $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$ content of the product $\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4$. Characterize each representation by its Young tableau, symmetry properties, and dimension.

(b) Give the $(2I+1,2s+1)$ content of each of the $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$ representations in your decomposition of $\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4\otimes\mathbf4$.

(c) Show that the nucleon and $\Delta(1232)$ resonances are plausibly members of the same flavor-spin supermultiplet, the $\mathbf{20}$:

$$
\begin{array}{|c|c|c|}\hline\phantom{a}&\phantom{a}&\phantom{a}\\\hline\end{array}.
$$

Discuss the significance of the flavor $\times$ spin symmetry of the three-quark wave function.

### 4.7

To test the idea that the lowest-lying nucleon states make up a $\mathbf{20}$ of $\mathrm{SU}(4)_{Is}$, let us examine in more detail the consequences for the proton and neutron.

(a) Construct *symmetric* flavor-spin wavefunctions for the nucleons. Note that there are two distinct paths to isodoublet three-quark states. The third quark (underlined) may be combined with an isoscalar pair to give

$$
\varphi_A^p=|\tfrac12,\tfrac12\rangle_0=(ud-du)\underline u/\sqrt2,
$$

which is antisymmetric under interchange of quarks 1 and 2; a similar situation holds for the neutron. Alternatively, the final quark may be added to an isovector pair, symmetric under interchange of the first two; show that this leads to

$$
\varphi_S^p=|\tfrac12,\tfrac12\rangle_1=-[(ud+du)\underline u-2uu\underline d]/\sqrt6.
$$

The corresponding spin parts of the wave functions $(\chi_{A,S}^\uparrow)$ can be read from their flavor counterparts using the transcription

$$
\text{flavor}\quad\left.\begin{matrix}u\\d\end{matrix}\right\}\rightarrow\left\{\begin{matrix}\uparrow\\\downarrow\end{matrix}\right.\quad\text{spin}.
$$

Compute the fully symmetrized, normalized wave function for a proton with spin up, $|p\uparrow\rangle=(\varphi_A^p\chi_A^\uparrow+\varphi_S^p\chi_S^\uparrow)/\sqrt2$, and show that

$$
\begin{aligned}
|p\uparrow\rangle=\big(&2u_\uparrow d_\downarrow u_\uparrow-u_\downarrow d_\uparrow u_\uparrow-u_\uparrow d_\uparrow u_\downarrow\\
&-d_\uparrow u_\downarrow u_\uparrow+2d_\downarrow u_\uparrow u_\uparrow-d_\uparrow u_\uparrow u_\downarrow\\
&-u_\uparrow u_\downarrow d_\uparrow-u_\downarrow u_\uparrow d_\uparrow+2u_\uparrow u_\uparrow d_\downarrow\big)/\sqrt{18}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The neutron counterpart, $|n\uparrow\rangle$, is obtained by interchanging $u\leftrightarrow d$.

(b) Take the quarks to be Dirac particles, with magnetic moments $\magnetic{\mu_q}=\electric{e_q}\hbar/2\mechanical{m_q}c$, and assume that the constituent masses of up and down quarks are

<!-- PDF page 83; printed page 69; text before For Further Reading -->

equal: $\mechanical{m_u}=\mechanical{m_d}=\mechanical{m}$, so that $\magnetic{\mu_u}=\frac23\magnetic{\mu}$, $\magnetic{\mu_d}=-\frac13\magnetic{\mu}$, with $\magnetic{\mu}=e\hbar/2\mechanical{m}c$. In terms of quark constituents, the magnetic moment of a nucleon $h=p,n$ is given by

$$
\magnetic{\mu_h}=\sum_i\langle h\uparrow|\magnetic{\mu_i}\sigma_z^{(i)}|h\uparrow\rangle.
$$

Show that $\magnetic{\mu_p}=\magnetic{\mu}$, and $\magnetic{\mu_n}=-\frac23\magnetic{\mu}$, so that $\magnetic{\mu_n}/\magnetic{\mu_p}=-\frac23$. Compare with data, and observe whether your prediction supports the $\mathbf{20}$ assignment with symmetric flavor $\times$ spin wave functions.
