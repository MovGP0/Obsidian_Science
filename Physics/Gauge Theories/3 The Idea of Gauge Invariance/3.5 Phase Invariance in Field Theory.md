> [!TLDR]
> Global phase invariance gives a conserved current; local phase invariance gives scalar and spinor electromagnetic interactions.

<!-- PDF page 60; printed page 46 -->

In the preceding sections we have seen something of the connection between electromagnetic gauge invariance and the conservation of charge and have found that by generalizing the global phase invariance of quantum mechanics to a local phase symmetry, we may be led from a theory describing free particles to one in which the particles experience electromagnetic interactions. With this as background, it is now time to bring the logical structure into clear focus by making a more systematic investigation of phase invariance in the framework of Lagrangian field theory. To avoid becoming lost in formalism and because we shall take this opportunity to derive some specific results that will be of use later, we proceed by example.

<!-- PDF page 61; printed page 47 -->

Consider the Lagrangian for a free complex scalar field,

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=|\partial^\mu\phi|^2-\mechanical{m}^2|\phi|^2, \tag{3.5.1}
$$

from which the Euler–Lagrange equations lead to the Klein–Gordon equations

$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\phi(x)=0,\quad(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\phi^*(x), \tag{3.5.2}
$$

as shown in section section 2.2. A global transformation on these fields,

$$
\phi(x)\rightarrow e^{i\electric{q}\signal{\alpha}}\phi(x),\quad\phi^*(x)\rightarrow e^{-i\electric{q}\signal{\alpha}}\phi^*(x), \tag{3.5.3}
$$

leads to the infinitesimal variations

$$
\delta\phi=i\electric{q}(\delta\signal{\alpha})\phi,\quad\delta\phi^*=-i\electric{q}(\delta\signal{\alpha})\phi^*; \tag{3.5.4}
$$

$$
\delta(\partial_\mu\phi)=i\electric{q}(\delta\signal{\alpha})\partial_\mu\phi,\quad
\delta(\partial_\mu\phi^*)=-i\electric{q}(\delta\signal{\alpha})\partial_\mu\phi^*. \tag{3.5.5}
$$

The statement of global phase invariance is that such transformations leave the Lagrangian unchanged:

$$
\delta\energy{\mathcal L}=0. \tag{3.5.6}
$$

Explicit computation yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\energy{\mathcal L}&=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\phi}\delta\phi
+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta(\partial_\mu\phi)
+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\phi^*}\delta\phi^*
+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)}\delta(\partial_\mu\phi^*)\\
&=\left[\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right]i\electric{q}(\delta\signal{\alpha})\phi
+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}i\electric{q}(\delta\signal{\alpha})\partial_\mu\phi-(\phi\rightarrow\phi^*)\\
&=i\electric{q}(\delta\signal{\alpha})\partial_\mu\left[
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\phi-
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)}\phi^*\right]\equiv0,
\end{aligned} \tag{3.5.7}
$$

where the equations of motion have been used in passing to the second line. Evidently we may identify a conserved Noether current,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\electric{j^\mu}&=-i\electric{q}\left[
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\phi-
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)}\phi^*\right]\\
&=i\electric{q}[\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-(\partial^\mu\phi)\phi^*]\equiv i\electric{q}\phi^*\overleftrightarrow{\partial^\mu}\phi,
\end{aligned} \tag{3.5.8}
$$

which satisfies

$$
\partial_\mu\electric{j^\mu}=0. \tag{3.5.9}
$$

With the identification of $\electric{q}$ as the electric charge, (3.5.8) is recognizable at once as the electromagnetic current of the charged scalar field. The connection between

<!-- PDF page 62; printed page 48 -->

global phase invariance and current conservation is thus made explicit. Noether’s theorem guarantees that this relation is in fact a general one.

What are the consequences of imposing invariance under *local* phase rotations, which transform the fields as

$$
\phi(x)\rightarrow e^{i\electric{q}\signal{\alpha}(x)}\phi(x)? \tag{3.5.10}
$$

Terms in the Lagrangian that depend only on the fields are left invariant, just as before, so there are no additional consequences beyond those of global gauge invariance. However, as we have seen in the quantum-mechanical discussion of section 3.3, gradient terms transform as

$$
\partial_\mu\phi(x)\rightarrow e^{i\electric{q}\signal{\alpha}(x)}[\partial_\mu\phi(x)+i\electric{q}(\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}(x))\phi(x)], \tag{3.5.11}
$$

which necessitate the introduction of the gauge-covariant derivative

$$
D_\mu\equiv\partial_\mu+i\electric{q}\magnetic{A_\mu}(x). \tag{3.5.12}
$$

Objects such as $D_\mu\phi$ will then undergo the same phase rotation as the fields, namely,

$$
D_\mu\phi\rightarrow e^{i\electric{q}\signal{\alpha}(x)}D_\mu\phi, \tag{3.5.13}
$$

provided that the vector field $\magnetic{A_\mu}$ transforms as

$$
\magnetic{A_\mu}(x)\rightarrow\magnetic{A_\mu}(x)-\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}(x). \tag{3.5.14}
$$

We remarked in section 3.3 that the replacement $\partial_\mu\rightarrow D_\mu$ prescribes the form of the interaction between the gauge field $\magnetic{A_\mu}$ and matter. To see how this comes about in Lagrangian field theory, let us look explicitly at the Dirac equation. The free-particle Lagrangian

$$
\energy{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{free}}}=\bar\psi(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-\mechanical{m})\psi \tag{3.5.15}
$$

is replaced by the locally gauge-invariant expression

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal L}&=\bar\psi(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu-\mechanical{m})\psi\\
&=\bar\psi(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-\mechanical{m})\psi-\electric{q}\magnetic{A_\mu}\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\\
&=\energy{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{free}}}-\electric{J^\mu}\magnetic{A_\mu},
\end{aligned} \tag{3.5.16}
$$

where the (conserved) electromagnetic current has the familiar form

$$
\electric{J^\mu}=\electric{q}\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi. \tag{3.5.17}
$$

Precisely this form of the current follows from the requirement of global phase invariance. It is easy to verify that the Lagrangian (3.5.16) is indeed invariant under the combined transformations (3.5.10) and (3.5.14).

To arrive at the complete Lagrangian for quantum electrodynamics, it remains only to add a kinetic energy term for the vector field to describe the propagation of

<!-- PDF page 63; printed page 49 -->

free photons. The Lagrangian (2.2.8) leads to Maxwell’s equations and is manifestly invariant under local gauge transformations (3.5.14). Assembling all the pieces, we therefore have

$$
\energy{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{QED}}}=\energy{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{free}}}-\electric{J^\mu}\magnetic{A_\mu}-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}, \tag{3.5.18}
$$

which is indeed the usual QED Lagrangian [10].

A photon mass term would have the form

$$
\energy{\mathcal L_\gamma}=\frac12\mechanical{m}^2\magnetic{A^\mu}\magnetic{A_\mu}, \tag{3.5.19}
$$

which obviously violates local gauge invariance because

$$
\magnetic{A^\mu}\magnetic{A_\mu}\rightarrow(\magnetic{A^\mu}-\partial^\mu\signal{\alpha})(\magnetic{A_\mu}-\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha})\ne\magnetic{A^\mu}\magnetic{A_\mu}. \tag{3.5.20}
$$

Thus, we find that local gauge invariance has led us to the existence of a massless photon. (Although this conclusion is not inescapable, it can be avoided only at a price, as will be explained in chapter 5.)

A restrictive direct limit on the photon mass has been deduced from a magnetohydrodynamic analysis of the solar wind data at Pluto’s orbitcollected by the Voyager 1 and 2 missions [11]. The upper limit is

$$
\mechanical{M_\gamma}<1\times10^{-18}\,\unit{eV}, \tag{3.5.21}
$$

corresponding to a modified Coulomb potential of the form

$$
\electric{V}\sim\frac{\exp(-\mechanical{r}/\mechanical{r_0})}{\mechanical{r}}, \tag{3.5.22}
$$

with $\mechanical{r_0}>2\times10^8\,\unit{km}$ $(=2\times10^{26}\,\unit{fm})$.

We have now seen how global phase invariance leads to the existence of a conserved charge. The more constraining requirement of local phase invariance necessitates the introduction of a massless gauge field and restricts the possible interactions of radiation with matter. For the Dirac equation, the interaction term was simply of the form $-\electric{J^\mu}\magnetic{A_\mu}$, with $\electric{J^\mu}$ the conserved current of the free-fermion Lagrangian. In general, some care is required in making this identification because the structure of the current may be altered by the interactions. Such a modification occurs, for example, in the other case we have examined, that of the complex scalar field.

Replacing the gradient $\partial_\mu$ by the gauge-covariant derivative $D_\mu$ in the Lagrangian (3.5.1) yields the locally gauge-invariant form

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal L}&=|D^\mu\phi|^2-\mechanical{m}^2|\phi|^2\\
&=|\partial^\mu\phi|^2-\mechanical{m}^2|\phi|^2-i\electric{q}\magnetic{A_\mu}\phi^*\overleftrightarrow{\partial^\mu}\phi+\electric{q}^2\magnetic{A}^2\phi^*\phi\\
&=|\partial^\mu\phi|^2-\mechanical{m}^2|\phi|^2-\electric{j^\mu}\magnetic{A_\mu}+\electric{q}^2\magnetic{A}^2\phi^*\phi,
\end{aligned} \tag{3.5.23}
$$

where $\electric{j^\mu}$ is the conserved current (3.5.8) for the free-particle Lagrangian. The final term in (3.5.23), which corresponds to a “contact interaction,” can be identified as

<!-- PDF page 64; printed page 50; text before section 3.6 -->

a $\electric{J}\cdot\magnetic{A}$ contribution when Noether’s theorem is used to define a conserved current for the full Lagrangian, including interactions.
