## 8.1

If the quark colors are designated as red ($R$), green ($G$), and blue ($B$), the gluons may be represented conveniently as $\bar{R}G$, $\bar{R}B$, $\bar{G}R$, $\bar{G}B$, $\bar{B}R$, $\bar{B}G$, $(\bar{R}R-\bar{G}G)/\sqrt{2}$, and $(\bar{R}R+\bar{G}G-2\bar{B}B)/\sqrt{6}$. The last two are color-preserving forms that are orthogonal to the color-singlet combination $(\bar{R}R+\bar{G}G+\bar{B}B)/\sqrt{3}$. The elementary quark–gluon interactions will be of the form

$$
\text{red quark}+\bar{R}G\text{ gluon}\to\text{green quark},
$$

and so on. Repeat in this language the maximally attractive channel analysis of section 8.1, and show that the color-singlet $q\bar{q}$ and $qqq$ configurations are energetically favored. [References: R. P. Feynman, in *Weak and Electromagnetic Interactions at High Energy*, 1976 Les Houches Lectures, ed. R. Balian and C. H. Llewellyn Smith, North-Holland, Amsterdam, 1977, p. 120; C. Quigg, in *Techniques and Concepts of High-Energy Physics*, ed. T. Ferbel, Plenum, New York, 1981, p. 143, §6.A, \[<http://j.mp/eV81cR>\]] .

## 8.4

To investigate the main features of the hadron spectrum, recast the “interaction energies” for $(qq)$ and $(q\bar{q})$ systems introduced in section 8.1.1 as Coulomb potentials of the form

$$
\energy{V}(\mechanical{r})=
\frac{\alpha_s}{\mechanical{r}}\langle\mathbf{T}^{(1)}\cdot\mathbf{T}^{(2)}\rangle,
$$

with expectation values given in table 8.3, so that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{V_{(q\bar{q})_1}}(\mechanical{r})&=-\frac{4}{3}\alpha_s\mechanical{r},
&\energy{V_{(q\bar{q})_8}}(\mechanical{r})&=+\frac{1}{6}\alpha_s\mechanical{r},\\
\energy{V_{(qq)_{3^*}}}(\mechanical{r})&=-\frac{2}{3}\alpha_s\mechanical{r},
&\energy{V_{(qq)_6}}(\mechanical{r})&=+\frac{1}{3}\alpha_s\mechanical{r}.
\end{aligned}
$$

<!-- PDF page 373; printed page 359 -->

These interactions underlie a rough description of $L=0$ meson and baryon masses as the sum of “constituent-quark” masses plus the sum of two-body interactions. This picture distinguishes hadrons with different flavor content but does not account for mass differences between states that contain the same quarks, but in different spin configurations.

To improve the description of hadron masses, introduce a color hyperfine interaction in analogy to the form familiar from the Coulomb problem of atomic physics:

$$
\energy{\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{hf}}}=
\nabla^2\energy{V}\,
\frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(2)}}{6\mechanical{m_1}\mechanical{m_2}},
$$

where $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ is a Pauli spin matrix.

(a) Show that in the one-gluon-exchange picture of hadron structure, the corresponding energy shift between two quarks in a relative $s$-wave becomes

$$
\energy{\Delta E^{\mathrm{hf}}}=
-\frac{2\pi\alpha_s|\psi(0)|^2}{3\mechanical{m_1}\mechanical{m_2}}
\langle\mathbf{T}^{(1)}\cdot\mathbf{T}^{(2)}\rangle
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(2)},
$$

where $|\psi(0)|^2$ represents the square of the two-body wave function at zero separation. Verify that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Delta E^{\mathrm{hf}}_{(q\bar{q})_1}}&=
\frac{8\pi\alpha_s}{9\mechanical{m_1}\mechanical{m_2}}|\psi(0)|^2
\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(2)}\rangle
&&\text{(mesons)},\\
\energy{\Delta E^{\mathrm{hf}}_{(qq)_{3^*}}}&=
\frac{4\pi\alpha_s}{9\mechanical{m_1}\mechanical{m_2}}|\psi(0)|^2
\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(2)}\rangle
&&\text{(baryons)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

(b) Using the techniques employed in section 8.1.1 to evaluate expectation values of scalar products of color matrices, compute the strength of the hyperfine interactions for spin-singlet and spin-triplet $L=0$ mesons. Show that the hyperfine interaction is attractive for $J^P=0^-$ mesons and repulsive for $J^P=1^-$ mesons and that the $K^*$-$K$ splitting is smaller than the $\rho$-$\pi$ splitting.

(c) Now apply similar reasoning to the $L=0$ baryon states, for which the relevant expectation value will be

$$
\sum_{i<j}\frac{\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(i)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(j)}\rangle}
{\mechanical{m_i}\mechanical{m_j}}.
$$

Compute the hyperfine contribution to the masses of the $J^P=\frac{1}{2}^+$ and $\frac{3}{2}^+$ baryons, and show that the $\frac{3}{2}^+$ decimet states lie above their $\frac{1}{2}^+$ octet counterparts.

(d) Consider the ground-state baryons $\Lambda$ and $\Sigma$. From the requirement that fermion wavefunctions be antisymmetric under the exchange of space $\times$ spin $\times$ isospin $\times$ color, deduce that the nonstrange quarks must couple to spin 0 in the $\Lambda$ and to spin 1 in the $\Sigma$. Then compute the hyperfine contributions to the $\Lambda$ and $\Sigma^0$ masses, taking into account all the pairwise hyperfine interactions and idealizing $|\psi(0)|^2$ to be universal. Show that the $\Lambda$-$\Sigma^0$ splitting is understood as a consequence of the color hyperfine

## 8.5

The one-gluon-exchange arguments of section 8.1.1 give no indication that tetraquark ($qq\bar{q}\bar{q}$) mesons, pentaquark ($4q\bar{q}$) baryons, or ($6q$) baryons should be appreciably bound by the color force. Let us investigate whether a strong hyperfine interaction might cause some specific configurations to be bound. It is convenient to compute the parameter of interest in a multiquark state, $\mathcal{C}\equiv\langle\sum_{i<j}\mathbf{T}^{(i)}\cdot\mathbf{T}^{(j)}\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(i)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{(j)}\rangle$, using a colorspin technique. An $\mathrm{SU}(6)$ color $\otimes$ spin algebra can be constructed out of the 35 operators

$$
\mathbf{G}^{(6)}\equiv
\left\{\begin{aligned}
T_a&\otimes\frac{\sigma_b}{\sqrt{2}},\\
T_a&\otimes\frac{\mathbf{1}_{2\times2}}{\sqrt{2}},\\
\mathbf{1}_{3\times3}&\otimes\frac{\sigma_b}{2\sqrt{3}},
\end{aligned}\right.,
$$

which are normalized so that $\operatorname{tr}(G_i^{(6)}G_j^{(6)})=\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}$. In similar notation, the normalized generators of $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ are $\mathbf{G}^{(2)}=\boldsymbol{\sigma}/2$ and $\mathbf{G}^{(3)}=\mathbf{T}$ [cf. (8.1.16)]. ## 8.27

The model of M.-Y. Han and Y. Nambu [*Phys. Rev.* **149B**, 1006 (1965)] was proposed as an integer-charge alternative to the fractional-charge quark model, with charges assigned as

| Color | Flavor $u$ | Flavor $d$ | Flavor $s$ |

| $G$ | $1$ | $0$ | $0$ |

$$

(c) Now consider the reaction $\gamma\gamma\to\text{hadrons}$, viewed as $\gamma\gamma\to q\bar{q}$. Show that with fractionally charged quarks

## 8.28

Consider the Schrödinger equation for $s$-wave bound states of a $1/\mechanical{r}$ potential in $N$ space dimensions,

$$
\left[\nabla^2+2\mechanical{\mu}
\left(\energy{E}+\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{r}}\right)\right]\psi(\mechanical{r})=0.
$$

(a) Show that the radial equation is

$$
\left[\frac{d^2}{d\mechanical{r}^2}
+\frac{(N-1)}{\mechanical{r}}\frac{d}{d\mechanical{r}}
+2\mechanical{\mu}\left(\energy{E}+\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{r}}\right)\right]
\psi(\mechanical{r})=0.
$$

(b) Now take the limit of large $N$, so that $(N-1)\to N$. Introduce a reduced radial wave function $u=\mechanical{r}^{N/2}\psi$ and a scaled radial coordinate $\mechanical{R}=\mechanical{r}/N^2$. Show that the radial equation becomes

$$
\frac{1}{N^2}\frac{d^2u}{d\mechanical{R}^2}
-\frac{u}{4\mechanical{R}^2}
+2\mechanical{\mu}\left(N^2\energy{E}+\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{R}}\right)u=0.
$$

(c) Apart from the factor $N^2$ that sets the scale of $\energy{E}$, this equation describes a particle with effective mass $\mechanical{\mu}N^2$ moving in an effective potential

$$
\energy{V_{\mathrm{eff}}}=
\frac{1}{8\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{R}^2}-\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{R}}.
$$

Find the energy of the ground state in the limit as $N\to\infty$, for which the kinetic energy vanishes. Show that it is given by the absolute minimum of $\energy{V_{\mathrm{eff}}}$,

<!-- PDF page 383; printed page 369 -->

so that

$$
\energy{E_{N\to\infty}}=-\frac{2\mechanical{\mu}\alpha^2}{N^2}.
$$

Corrections to this result may be obtained by expanding $\energy{V_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ about the minimum and treating the additional terms as perturbations.

(d) The solution to the exact eigenvalue equation of part (a) is easily verified to be

$$
\energy{E_{\mathrm{exact}}}=-\frac{2\mechanical{\mu}\alpha^2}{(N-1)^2}.
$$

Show that it can be recast in the form of an expansion in inverse powers of $N$ as

$$
\energy{E_{\mathrm{exact}}}=
-\frac{2\mechanical{\mu}\alpha^2}{N^2}\sum_{j=1}^\infty jN^{1-j}
=\energy{E_{N\to\infty}}\left(1+\sum_{j=2}^\infty jN^{1-j}\right),
$$

so that the $N\to\infty$ result may form the basis for a systematic approximation scheme. How many terms must be retained to obtain a 1% approximation for $N=3$? [L. D. Mlodinow and N. Papanicolaou, *Ann. Phys. (NY)* **128**, 314 (1980).]
