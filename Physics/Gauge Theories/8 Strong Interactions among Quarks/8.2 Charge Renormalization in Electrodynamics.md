In quantum field theories, observables such as scattering amplitudes may be sensitive to higher-order corrections, in addition to Born diagrams. The modifications to lowest-order contributions are, in general, dependent upon kinematic variables. A convenient way of expressing an important class of these modifications is to introduce a so-called running coupling constant, which is to say an effective coupling strength that depends upon the kinematical circumstances. A phenomenon of this sort is, in fact, familiar in the classical electrodynamics of a polarizable substance. In the absence of a test charge, molecules within the medium will orient themselves to ensure that the substance is locally neutral, as shown in figure 8.10(a). A test charge placed in the medium will polarize the substance, attracting opposite charges and repelling like charges, as shown in figure 8.10(b). At any radius $\mechanical{r}$ (larger than the molecular scale) from the test charge, the total charge enclosed within a sphere centered on the test charge will be smaller than the test charge itself. By Gauss’s law, the effective charge will be smaller in magnitude than the test charge,

$$
\electric{Q}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\electric{Q}_{\mathrm{test}}}{\electric{\epsilon}}, \tag{8.2.1}
$$

where $\electric{\epsilon}\ge1$ is the dielectric constant of the medium. By this we mean that the electric field due to the test charge in the medium is related to what the field would have been in vacuo by

$$
\electric{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathrm{medium}}
=\frac{\electric{\mathbf{E}}_{\mathrm{vacuo}}}{\electric{\epsilon}}. \tag{8.2.2}
$$

At very short distances, closer to the test charge than the molecular size, screening cannot occur, and the effective charge is equal to the full magnitude of the test

charge. Thus we may say that the effective electric charge increases at short distances. In quantum electrodynamics, the vacuum itself behaves as a polarizable medium in which virtual electron–positron pairs are polarized by the test charge. Consequently, the effective charge shows a similar dependence on the distance scale, which we shall now derive.

## 8.2.1 Charge Renormalization in Quantum Electrodynamics

The lowest-order manifestation of the vacuum polarization phenomenon occurs in the one-loop corrections to Coulomb scattering. For definiteness, we consider the scattering of an electron from an infinitely massive “proton,” for which the Born diagram is shown in figure 8.11(a). The invariant amplitude is (see the Feynman rules in appendix B)

$$
\mathcal{M}=-i\electric{e}^2
\bar u(\mechanical{p'})\gamma_\mu u(\mechanical{p})
\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{q}^2}
\bar U(\mechanical{P'})\gamma_\nu U(\mechanical{P}), \tag{8.2.3}
$$

for which the momentum transfer is

$$
\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{p'} \tag{8.2.4}
$$

and $U$ denotes the proton spinors. In the limit of very large proton mass, these become

$$
\left.
\begin{aligned}
U_\lambda(\mechanical{P})&=\sqrt{2\mechanical{M}}
\begin{pmatrix}\chi_\lambda\\0\end{pmatrix}\\
\bar U_{\lambda'}(\mechanical{P'})&=\sqrt{2\mechanical{M}}
(\bar\chi_{\lambda'},0)
\end{aligned}
\right\}, \tag{8.2.5}
$$

<!-- PDF page 297; printed page 283 -->

![[gauge-theories-fig-8-11-coulomb-born-and-vacuum-polarization.svg|**Fig. 8.11** Feynman diagrams for Coulomb scattering. (a) Born term; (b) one-loop correction to the photon propagator.]]

so that

$$
\bar U_{\lambda'}(\mechanical{P'})\gamma^\nu U_\lambda(\mechanical{P})
=2\mechanical{M}\delta_{\lambda\lambda'}\delta^{\nu0}. \tag{8.2.6}
$$

The last result follows because the $\gamma$-matrices mix large and small components. In this limit, the invariant amplitude takes the form

$$
\mathcal{M}
=-\frac{i\electric{e}^2}{\mechanical{q}^2}
\bar u(\mechanical{p'})\gamma_0u(\mechanical{p})\cdot2\mechanical{M}, \tag{8.2.7}
$$

which is precisely the form of a Coulomb interaction, namely, the interaction of the lepton current with a purely timelike potential.

Next we examine the contribution of a one-loop vacuum-polarization graph shown in figure 8.11(b). The invariant amplitude is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{M}'=-\operatorname{tr}\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\Bigg[&(+i\electric{e})\bar u(\mechanical{p'})\gamma_\mu u(\mechanical{p})
\frac{-ig^{\mu\rho}}{\mechanical{q}^2}
(i\electric{e})\gamma_\rho
\frac{i(\mechanical{\not\!k}+\mechanical{m})}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}\\
&\times(i\electric{e})\gamma_\sigma
\frac{i(\mechanical{\not\!k}-\mechanical{\not\!q}+\mechanical{m})}
{(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
\frac{-ig^{\nu\sigma}}{\mechanical{q}^2}
(-i\electric{e})\bar U(\mechanical{P'})\gamma_\nu U(\mechanical{P})\Bigg],
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.8}
$$

corresponding to the replacement in (8.2.3) of $-ig^{\mu\nu}/\mechanical{q}^2$ by

$$
\left(\frac{-i}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right)I^{\mu\nu}
\left(\frac{-i}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right), \tag{8.2.9}
$$

<!-- PDF page 298; printed page 284 -->

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\mu\nu}
&=-\operatorname{tr}\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
(i\electric{e})^2\gamma^\mu
\frac{i(\mechanical{\not\!k}+\mechanical{m})}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
\gamma^\nu\frac{i(\mechanical{\not\!k}-\mechanical{\not\!q}+\mechanical{m})}
{(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}\\
&=\electric{e}^2\operatorname{tr}\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\gamma^\mu\frac{i(\mechanical{\not\!k}+\mechanical{m})}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
\gamma^\nu\frac{i(\mechanical{\not\!k}-\mechanical{\not\!q}+\mechanical{m})}
{(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.10}
$$

The closed-loop integral diverges for $\mechanical{k}\to\infty$, and its evaluation thus will require some cutoff procedure. To proceed further, it is convenient to bring the integrand to an exponential form, in order that gaussian methods may eventually be used. With the aid of the identity

$$
\frac{i(\mechanical{\not\!k}+\mechanical{m})}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}
=(\mechanical{\not\!k}+\mechanical{m})\int_0^\infty
dz\,e^{iz(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)}, \tag{8.2.11}
$$

we may write

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\mu\nu}
=\electric{e}^2\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2
\big(&\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu(\mechanical{\not\!k}+\mechanical{m})
\gamma^\nu(\mechanical{\not\!k}-\mechanical{\not\!q}+\mechanical{m})]\\
&\times\exp\{iz_1(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)
+iz_2[(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon]\}\big).
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.12}
$$

A convenient, and gauge-invariant, cutoff procedure is the method of regulators due to Pauli and Villars [16], which consists in adding to the Lagrangian unphysical fields with adjustable masses that are taken to infinity at the end of the calculation. The procedure succeeds if observables are finite and independent of the cutoff masses. In cases such as the one before us, this amounts to the replacement

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
&\to\bar I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
+\sum_{i=1}^Na_iI^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{M_i}^2)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^Na_iI^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{M_i}^2),
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.13}
$$

where the number and coefficients of the regulator terms are chosen to render the loop integral convergent and, evidently, $a_0=1$, $\mechanical{M_0}^2=\mechanical{m}^2$. Evaluation of integrals of the general form (8.2.12) will be facilitated by completing the square in the exponential. Because the final expression will be convergent, we are free to introduce the new integration variable

$$
\mechanical{l}
=\frac{\mechanical{k}z_1+(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})z_2}{z_1+z_2}, \tag{8.2.14}
$$

in terms of which

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{k}&=\mechanical{l}+\mechanical{q}z_2/(z_1+z_2),\\
\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q}&=\mechanical{l}-\mechanical{q}z_1/(z_1+z_2).
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.15}
$$

<!-- PDF page 299; printed page 285 -->

Carrying out the trace, completing the square in the exponential, and inverting the order of integration, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
={}&4\electric{e}^2\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2
\exp\left\{i\left[\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-(\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)(z_1+z_2)\right]\right\}\\
&\times\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{l}}{(2\pi)^4}
e^{i\mechanical{l}^2(z_1+z_2)}
\Bigg(2\mechanical{l}^\mu\mechanical{l}^\nu
-(\mechanical{l}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu+\mechanical{l}^\nu\mechanical{q}^\mu)
\frac{z_1-z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-2\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu\frac{z_1z_2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}\\
&\qquad+g^{\mu\nu}\left\{\mechanical{m}^2
-\left[\mechanical{l}^2-\mechanical{l}\cdot\mechanical{q}
\frac{z_1-z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-\mechanical{q}^2\frac{z_1z_2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}\right]\right\}\Bigg).
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.16}
$$

The momentum integrals are conveniently organized according to powers of $\mechanical{l}$ as

$$
\mathcal{I}_2^{\mu\nu}
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{l}}{(2\pi)^4}
e^{i\mechanical{l}^2(z_1+z_2)}
(2\mechanical{l}^\mu\mechanical{l}^\nu-g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{l}^2), \tag{8.2.17}
$$

$$
\mathcal{I}_1^{\mu\nu}
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{l}}{(2\pi)^4}
e^{i\mechanical{l}^2(z_1+z_2)}
(g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{l}\cdot\mechanical{q}
-\mechanical{l}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{l}^\nu\mechanical{q}^\mu)
\frac{z_1-z_2}{z_1+z_2}, \tag{8.2.18}
$$

$$
\mathcal{I}_0^{\mu\nu}
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{l}}{(2\pi)^4}
e^{i\mechanical{l}^2(z_1+z_2)}
\left[(g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2-2\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\frac{z_1z_2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}+g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{m}^2\right]. \tag{8.2.19}
$$

By symmetric integration [cf. (B.3.11a), (B.3.17)], $\mathcal{I}_1^{\mu\nu}$ vanishes and the other pieces become

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{I}_2^{\mu\nu}
&=\frac1{16\pi^2i(z_1+z_2)^2}
\frac{ig^{\mu\nu}}{2(z_1+z_2)}(2-4)\\
&=\frac{-g^{\mu\nu}}{16\pi^2(z_1+z_2)^3}
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.20}
$$

and

$$
\mathcal{I}_0^{\mu\nu}
=\frac1{16\pi^2i(z_1+z_2)^2}
\left\{2(g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\frac{z_1z_2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}
+g^{\mu\nu}\left[\mechanical{m}^2
-\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}\right]\right\}. \tag{8.2.21}
$$

We therefore obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
={}&\frac{-i\alpha}{\pi}\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2
\frac1{(z_1+z_2)^2}
\exp\left\{i\left[\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-(\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)(z_1+z_2)\right]\right\}\\
&\times\left\{2(g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\frac{z_1z_2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}
+g^{\mu\nu}\left[\mechanical{m}^2
-\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}
-\frac{i}{z_1+z_2}\right]\right\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.22}
$$

The first term in braces has been constructed to satisfy the current-conservation requirement

$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu I^{\mu\nu}\equiv0=\mechanical{q}_\nu I^{\mu\nu}. \tag{8.2.23}
$$

The term proportional to $g^{\mu\nu}$ does not obey this condition but can be shown to vanish identically, if we note that its contribution to $I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2)$ may be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^{\mu\nu}
={}&-\frac{i\alpha}{\pi}g^{\mu\nu}
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2
\frac1{(z_1+z_2)^3}i\sigma\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}\frac1\sigma\\
&\times\left.\sum_{i=0}^Na_i
\exp\left\{i\sigma\left[\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-(\mechanical{M_i}^2-i\epsilon)(z_1+z_2)\right]\right\}\right|_{\sigma=1}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.24}
$$

Because the integrals are convergent, we may interchange the order of the integrations and the differentiation $\partial/\partial\sigma$, so that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^{\mu\nu}
={}&\frac{\alpha}{\pi}g^{\mu\nu}\sigma\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}
\Bigg(\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2
\frac1{(z_1+z_2)^3\sigma}\\
&\left.\times\sum_{i=0}^Na_i
\exp\left\{i\sigma\left[\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-(\mechanical{M_i}^2-i\epsilon)(z_1+z_2)\right]\right\}\Bigg)\right|_{\sigma=1}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.25}
$$

If we now rescale the integration variables as

$$
z_i\to\frac{z_i}{\sigma}, \tag{8.2.26}
$$

the expression in parentheses is seen to be independent of $\sigma$, so the contribution $\Delta^{\mu\nu}$ vanishes.

We are left with the task of evaluating

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2)
={}&-\frac{2i\alpha}{\pi}
(g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu)
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2\frac{z_1z_2}{(z_1+z_2)^4}\\
&\times\sum_{i=0}^Na_i
\exp\left\{i\sigma\left[\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-(\mechanical{M_i}^2-i\epsilon)(z_1+z_2)\right]\right\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.27}
$$

This is conveniently accomplished by rescaling the integration variables. Introduce the factor

$$
1=\int_0^\infty d\sigma\,\delta(\sigma-x_1-x_2) \tag{8.2.28}
$$

under the integral signs and let $z_i\to\sigma z_i$, so that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2)
={}&\frac{2i\alpha}{\pi}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2)
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2\,z_1z_2\delta(1-z_1-z_2)\\
&\times\int_0^\infty\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma}
\sum_{i=0}^Na_i\exp[i\sigma(\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2-\mechanical{M_i}^2+i\epsilon)],
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.29}
$$

which appears logarithmically divergent at the point $\sigma=0$. This mild divergence can be removed with a single regulator, by the choice

$$
a_1=-1,\qquad\mechanical{M_1}^2\equiv\energy{\Lambda}^2
\gg\mechanical{m}^2,\mechanical{q}^2. \tag{8.2.30}
$$

Now, for $\mechanical{q}^2<4\mechanical{m}^2$, we find

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2)
&=I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
-I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\energy{\Lambda}^2)\\
&=\frac{2i\alpha}{\pi}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2)
\int_0^1 dz\,z(1-z)
\ln\left[\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2}
{\mechanical{m}^2-\mechanical{q}^2z(1-z)}\right]\\
&=\frac{i\alpha}{3\pi}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2)
\left\{\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)
-6\int_0^1 dz\,z(1-z)
\ln\left[1-\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z(1-z)}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right]\right\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.31}
$$

Returning to the problem of original interest, the scattering of an electron in a Coulomb field, we note that the $\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu$ factor does not contribute to the scattering amplitude by virtue of current conservation at the electron vertex (or, more operationally, because of the Dirac equation). Hence, the modified photon propagator is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{q}^2}
+\left(\frac{-i}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right)\bar I^{\mu\nu}
\left(\frac{-i}{\mechanical{q}^2}\right)
=\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{q}^2}
\Bigg\{&1-\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}
\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)\\
&+\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\int_0^1 dz\,z(1-z)
\ln\left[1-\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z(1-z)}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right]\Bigg\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.32}
$$

A number of limiting cases are worth comment. For $\mechanical{q}^2\to0$, the long-wavelength limit, the propagator is modified by the factor

$$
1-\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)
\equiv Z_3, \tag{8.2.33}
$$

so that the Coulomb scattering amplitude is changed by the same factor. In other words, the electric charge measured in Coulomb scattering is not the “bare” charge $\electric{e}$ that appears in the Lagrangian, but a renormalized quantity $\electric{e}_{\mathrm{R}}$ that differs by a factor $\sqrt{Z_3}$. The term $(-\alpha/3\pi)\ln(\energy{\Lambda}^2/\mechanical{m}^2)$ is but the first term in the renormalization of the electric charge, which can be carried out systematically to all orders in perturbation theory. We accommodate this by replacing the bare charge $\electric{e}$ by the renormalized charge $\electric{e}_{\mathrm{R}}$ in all our expressions for observables, although it will usually be convenient to omit the subscript. It is thus the renormalized charge that is measured in experiments as $\electric{e}_{\mathrm{R}}^2\approx4\pi/137$.

Of greater interest for us in what follows is the modification to the Coulomb scattering amplitude for large spacelike values of $\mechanical{q}^2$, such that $-\mechanical{q}^2\gg\mechanical{m}^2$. In this circumstance, the remaining integral in (8.2.32) may be approximated by

$$
\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\int_0^1 dz\,z(1-z)
\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)
=\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right). \tag{8.2.34}
$$

As a result, the photon propagator is given by

$$
\frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{q}^2}
\left[1-\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\Lambda}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)
+\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)\right], \tag{8.2.35}
$$

which amounts to the replacement of $\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}$ in the scattering amplitude by the “running coupling constant”

$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)
\left[1+\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)}{3\pi}
\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)
+O(\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}^2)\right]. \tag{8.2.36}
$$

We see that to this approximation the effective electric charges increases logarithmically with $-\mechanical{q}^2$, which is to say with improving resolution. This is the quantum-mechanical analogue of the macroscopic charge screening discussed at the beginning of this section.

For small values of $-\mechanical{q}^2\ll\mechanical{m}^2$, it is straightforward (see problem 8.9) to derive the modification to Coulomb’s law in position space that results from vacuum polarization. The additional term in the force law, first calculated by Uehling [17] in 1935, induces a shift in the $s$-wave energy levels of atoms that is especially significant for high-$Z$ muonic atoms.

It is possible to continue the study of modifications to Coulomb’s law in higher orders of perturbation theory. For this purpose, the most important class of Feynman diagrams is the “sum of bubbles” indicated in figure 8.12, which corresponds to the most divergent set of logarithms. It does not require detailed calculation to see that for $-\mechanical{q}^2\gg\mechanical{m}^2$, the running coupling constant will be given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{q}^2)
&=\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)
\left\{1+\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)}{3\pi}
\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)
+\left[\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)}{3\pi}
\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)\right]^2+\cdots\right\}\\
&\to\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)}
{1-\dfrac{\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)}{3\pi}
\ln\left(\dfrac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)},
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.37}
$$

Pure QED, the theory of photons and electrons, describes nature only over a finite (but highly important) range of energies. For momentum transfers greater than a few hundred $\unit{MeV}$, the evolution of $1/\alpha$ will be influenced by other virtual particles, beginning with the muon. The solid line in the right panel of figure 8.13 shows the result of such a calculation [19]. ## 8.2.2 Charge Renormalization in Scalar Electrodynamics

Although the principal interest in this chapter is QCD and, among other things, the comportment of the running coupling constant in that theory, it will be useful to spend a little more time on electrodynamics. We consider the evolution of the running coupling constant in scalar electrodynamics, both because the result is interesting and instructive and also in order to introduce a second regularization technique that is better suited to non-Abelian gauge theories than the method of Pauli and Villars.

Applying the Feynman rules for scalar electrodynamics given in appendix B.5, we find that the lowest-order corrections to the photon propagator are the two diagrams shown in figure 8.14. In the same notation employed previously in this section, we wish to evaluate

$$
I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
=\electric{e}^2\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\left\{\frac{(2\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^\mu(2\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^\nu}
{(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)
[(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon]}
-\frac{2g^{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}\right\}, \tag{8.2.40}
$$

where the first term corresponds to the bubble of figure 8.14(a) and the second, to the contact term of figure 8.14(b). We combine the terms over a common denominator as

$$
I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
=\electric{e}^2\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{(2\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^\mu(2\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^\nu
-2g^{\mu\nu}[(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon]}
{(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)
[(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon]} \tag{8.2.41}
$$

and promote the denominators to an exponent by means of the identity

$$
i(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)^{-1}
=\int_0^\infty dz\,e^{iz(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)}. \tag{8.2.42}
$$

<!-- PDF page 305; printed page 291 -->

The result is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
={}&-\electric{e}^2\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2
\left\{(2\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^\mu(2\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^\nu
-2g^{\mu\nu}[(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon]\right\}\\
&\times\exp\{iz_1(\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)
+iz_2[(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon]\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.43}
$$

We complete the square in the exponential on introducing the new variable

$$
\mechanical{l}=\frac{\mechanical{k}z_1+(\mechanical{k}-\mechanical{q})z_2}{z_1+z_2} \tag{8.2.14}
$$

and regularize the integral by changing the dimensionality of spacetime to $n$. We are left with

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{q}^2,\mechanical{m}^2)
={}&-\electric{e}^2\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2
\exp\left\{i\left[\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-(\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)(z_1+z_2)\right]\right\}\\
&\times\int\frac{d^n\mechanical{l}}{(2\pi)^n}
e^{i\mechanical{l}^2(z_1+z_2)}
\left\{4\mechanical{l}^\mu\mechanical{l}^\nu
+\frac{(z_1-z_2)^2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu
-2g^{\mu\nu}\left[\mechanical{l}^2-\mechanical{m}^2
+\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1^2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}\right]\right\},
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.44}
$$

where odd powers of $\mechanical{l}$ have been omitted in anticipation of symmetric integration, and the order of integrations has been interchanged. As we did for spinor QED, we may rearrange the momentum integral as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{d^n\mechanical{l}}{(2\pi)^n}e^{i\mechanical{l}^2(z_1+z_2)}
\Bigg(&\frac{(z_1-z_2)^2}{(z_1+z_2)^2}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}^2)\\
&+4\mechanical{l}^\mu\mechanical{l}^\nu
-2g^{\mu\nu}\left\{\mechanical{l}^2-\mechanical{m}^2
+\mechanical{q}^2\frac{[z_1^2-(z_1-z_2)^2/2]}{(z_1+z_2)^2}\right\}\Bigg).
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.45}
$$

The first term in large parentheses satisfies the requirements of current conservation, and we shall evaluate its contribution presently. First, we show by the ’t Hooft–Veltman method of dimensional regularization [20] that the remaining terms do not, in fact, contribute. Using the expressions for $n$-dimensional gaussian integrals given in appendix B.4, we may write

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\int\frac{d^n\mechanical{l}}{(2\pi)^n}e^{i\mechanical{l}^2(z_1+z_2)}
\left(4\mechanical{l}^\mu\mechanical{l}^\nu
-2\mechanical{l}^2g^{\mu\nu}
+g^{\mu\nu}\left\{2\mechanical{m}^2
-2\frac{[z_1^2-(z_1-z_2)^2/2]}{(z_1+z_2)^2}\mechanical{q}^2\right\}\right)\\
&\quad=\frac{ie^{-i\pi n/4}g^{\mu\nu}}{[4\pi(z_1+z_2)]^{n/2}}
\left\{\frac{in(4/n-2)}{2(z_1+z_2)}
+2\mechanical{m}^2
-\frac{2[z_1^2-(z_1-z_2)^2/2]}{(z_1+z_2)^2}\mechanical{q}^2\right\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.46}
$$

<!-- PDF page 306; printed page 292 -->

The contribution to $I^{\mu\nu}$ is, therefore.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^{\mu\nu}
={}&\frac{i\electric{e}^2e^{-i\pi n/4}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}g^{\mu\nu}
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2
\exp\left\{i\left[\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-(\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)(z_1+z_2)\right]\right\}\\
&\times\frac2{(z_1+z_2)^{1+n/2}}
\left\{i\left(1-\frac n2\right)+\mechanical{m}^2(z_1+z_2)
-\frac{[z_1^2-(z_1-z_2)^2/2]\mechanical{q}^2}{z_1+z_2}\right\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.47}
$$

Introducing a scaling parameter $\sigma$ as before, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta^{\mu\nu}
={}&\frac{-2\electric{e}^2e^{-i\pi n/4}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}g^{\mu\nu}
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2\,\delta(1-z_1-z_2)
\int_0^\infty\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma^{n/2-1}}\\
&\times\exp[i\sigma(\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)]
\times\left[\frac{(1-n/2)}{\sigma}
+i(z_1z_2\mechanical{q}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)\right],
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.48}
$$

in which, in anticipation of the restrictions on the $z_1$ and $z_2$ integrations, we have replaced $z_1+z_2$ by 1 and dropped terms that are odd in $(1-2z_i)$. The integral over $\sigma$ may be expressed as

$$
\int_0^\infty d\sigma\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}
\{\sigma^{1-n/2}\exp[i\sigma(\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)]\}, \tag{8.2.49}
$$

which is convergent and equal to zero for sufficiently small values of $n$, namely, $n<2$. Thus, by dimensional regularization, we obtain the anticipated gauge-invariant result,

$$
\Delta^{\mu\nu}\equiv0. \tag{8.2.50}
$$

Let us return to the gauge-invariant contribution. We have

$$
\begin{aligned}
I^{\mu\nu}
={}&\frac{i\electric{e}^2e^{-i\pi n/4}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu})
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2\\
&\times\exp\left\{i\left[\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2}{z_1+z_2}
-(\mechanical{m}^2-i\epsilon)(z_1+z_2)\right]\right\}
\frac{(z_1-z_2)^2}{(z_1+z_2)^{2+n/2}}\\
={}&\frac{i\electric{e}^2e^{-i\pi n/4}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu})
\int_0^\infty dz_1\int_0^\infty dz_2\,\delta(1-z_1-z_2)
\int_0^\infty\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma^{n/2-1}}\\
&\times\exp[i\sigma(\mechanical{q}^2z_1z_2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon)](z_1-z_2)^2\\
={}&\frac{i\electric{e}^2e^{-i\pi n/4}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu})
\int_0^1 dz\,(1-2z)^2\\
&\times\int_0^\infty\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma^{n/2-1}}
\exp\{i\sigma[\mechanical{q}^2z(1-z)-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon]\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.51}
$$

The $\sigma$-integration produces a gamma function, so that, finally, we have

$$
I^{\mu\nu}
=\frac{i\electric{e}^2e^{-i\pi n/4}}{(4\pi)^{n/2}}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu})
\Gamma\left(2-\frac n2\right)
\int_0^1\frac{dz\,(1-2z)^2}
{[\mechanical{q}^2z(1-z)-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon]^{2-n/2}}, \tag{8.2.52}
$$

which has simple poles at $n=4,6,8,\ldots$. To regularize the integral, we subtract from (8.2.52) the pole at $n=4$ with its residue, given by

$$
I_{\mathrm{pole}}^{\mu\nu}
=\frac{-i\electric{e}^2}{16\pi^2}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu})
\frac2{4-n}\int_0^1 dz\,(1-2z)^2. \tag{8.2.53}
$$

Subtracting the pole term and taking the limit $n\to4$ in the regularized expression, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar I^{\mu\nu}
&=\lim_{n\to4}(I^{\mu\nu}-I_{\mathrm{pole}}^{\mu\nu})
=\frac{-i\electric{e}^2}{16\pi^2}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu})\\
&\quad\times\left\{\int_0^1 dz\,(1-2z)^2
\ln\left[1-\frac{\mechanical{q}^2z(1-z)}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right]+K\right\},
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.54}
$$

where the constant $K$ arises from the $n$-dependence other than in the denominator of the integrand in (8.2.52). It is arbitrary in the same way as the Pauli–Villars cutoff is arbitrary and contributes to the overall charge renormalization.

Let us compare the $\mechanical{q}^2$-evolution of the coupling constant with the result we found in spinor electrodynamics. For $-\mechanical{q}^2\gg\mechanical{m}^2$, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar I^{\mu\nu}
&=\frac{-i\alpha}{4\pi}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu})
\left[K+\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)
\int_0^1 dz\,(1-2z)^2\right]\\
&=\frac{-i\alpha}{4\pi}
(\mechanical{q}^\mu\mechanical{q}^\nu-\mechanical{q}^2g^{\mu\nu})
\left[K+\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)\left(\frac13\right)\right].
\end{aligned} \tag{8.2.55}
$$

Forming the photon propagator as in (8.2.32) and using current conservation at the scalar vertex, we find, for the running renormalized coupling constant in scalar electrodynamics,

$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)
\left[1+\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}(\mechanical{m}^2)}{12\pi}
\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\mechanical{m}^2}\right)+\cdots\right], \tag{8.2.56}
$$

which is to be compared with the expression (8.2.36) obtained in spinor electrodynamics. The rate of change of the coupling constant in scalar electrodynamics is one-fourth of that in spinor electrodynamics. This is precisely the ratio of the cross section for the reactions $e^+e^-\to\sigma^+\sigma^-$ and $e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$ evaluated in problems 1.4 and 1.5, which are directly related to the same polarization tensor.

It is of some importance that, whereas the variation in the effective coupling constant is logarithmically divergent, the quantity $\partial\alpha(\mechanical{q}^2)/\partial(\ln\mechanical{q}^2)$ is finite. This tame
