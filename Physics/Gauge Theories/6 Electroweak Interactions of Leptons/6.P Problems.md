## 6.13

Because the most serious high-energy divergences of a spontaneously broken gauge theory are associated with the longitudinal degrees of freedom of the gauge bosons, which arise from auxiliary scalars, it is instructive to study the Higgs sector in isolation. Consider, therefore, the Lagrangian for the Higgs sector of the Weinberg–Salam model before the gauge couplings are turned on,

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{scalar}}=(\partial^\mu\phi)^\dagger(\partial_\mu\phi)-\mechanical{\mu}^2(\phi^\dagger\phi)-|\lambda|(\phi^\dagger\phi)^2.
$$

(a) Choosing $\mechanical{\mu}^2<0$, investigate the effect of spontaneous symmetry breaking. Show that the theory describes three massless scalars $(w^+,w^-,z^0)$ and one massive neutral scalar $(h)$, which interact according to

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}=-|\lambda|vh(2w^+w^-+z^2+h^2)-(|\lambda|/4)(2w^+w^-+h^2)^2,
$$

where $v^2=-\mechanical{\mu}^2/|\lambda|$. In the language of the full electroweak theory, $1/v^2=G_F\sqrt2$ and $\lambda=G_F\mechanical{M_H}^2/\sqrt2$. Note the resemblance to the $\sigma$-model Lagrangian of (5.4.24). The correspondence is $\pi^\pm\leftrightarrow w^\pm$, $\pi^0\leftrightarrow z^0$, $h\leftrightarrow\sigma$. In the $\sigma$ model, $v=f_\pi$ and $|\lambda|=\mechanical{m_\sigma}^2/2f_\pi^2$.

(b) Deduce the Feynman rules for interactions and compute the lowest-order (tree diagram) amplitude for the reaction $hz\rightarrow hz$.

(c) Compute the $s$-wave partial-wave amplitude in the high-energy limit and show that it respect partial-wave unitarity only if $\mechanical{M_H}^2<8\pi\sqrt2/G_F$. [B. W. Lee, C. Quigg, and H. H. Thacker, *Phys. Rev. D* **16**, 1519 (1977).]

## 6.22

Beginning with the observation that neutrino flavor eigenstates $|\nu_\alpha\rangle$ and mass eigenstates $|\nu_i\rangle$ are related through $|\nu_\alpha\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^3\mathcal V_{\alpha i}^*|\nu_i\rangle$, show that the $\nu_\alpha\rightarrow\nu_\beta$ oscillation probability for ultrarelativistic neutrinos of energy $\energy{E}$ over a baseline of length $\mechanical{L}$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\nu_\alpha\rightarrow\nu_\beta)&=\left|\sum_i\mathcal V_{\alpha i}^*\mathcal V_{\beta i}e^{-i\mechanical{m_i}^2\mechanical{L}/2\energy{E}}\right|^2\\
&=\delta_{\alpha\beta}-4\sum_{i>j}\operatorname{Re}(\mathcal V_{\alpha i}^*\mathcal V_{\alpha j}\mathcal V_{\beta i}\mathcal V_{\beta j}^*)\sin^2(\Delta\mechanical{m_{ij}^2}\mechanical{L}/4\energy{E})\\
&\quad+2\sum_{i>j}\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal V_{\alpha i}^*\mathcal V_{\alpha j}\mathcal V_{\beta i}\mathcal V_{\beta j}^*)\sin(\Delta\mechanical{m_{ij}^2}\mechanical{L}/2\energy{E}),
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\Delta\mechanical{m_{ij}^2}=\mechanical{m_i}^2-\mechanical{m_j}^2$ and the neutrino mixing matrix $\mathcal V$ is defined in (6.7.13). [For example, see C. Giunti and M. Laveder, “Neutrino mixing,” arXiv:hep-ph/0310238.]

## 6.23

At low energies, inelastic scattering can be neglected, so only forward elastic scattering is relevant. (a) Demonstrate that in ordinary matter, $\Delta\energy{V}$ arises from the charged-current contribution to $\nu_e e$ elastic scattering, so that $\Delta\energy{V}=G_F\sqrt2n_e$, where $n_e$ is the number density of electrons in the medium.

(b) Show that the Hamiltonian governing the evolution of the $(\nu_e,\nu_\mu)$ system may be written as

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=
\begin{bmatrix}
-\dfrac{\Delta\mechanical{m^2}}{4\energy{E}}\cos2\theta+G_F\sqrt2n_e&\dfrac{\Delta\mechanical{m^2}}{4\energy{E}}\sin2\theta\\[0.6em]
\dfrac{\Delta\mechanical{m^2}}{4\energy{E}}\sin2\theta&\dfrac{\Delta\mechanical{m^2}}{4\energy{E}}\cos2\theta
\end{bmatrix},
$$

where $\Delta\mechanical{m^2}\equiv\mechanical{m_2}^2-\mechanical{m_1}^2$ and $\theta$ is the $(\nu_e,\nu_\mu)$ mixing angle in the vacuum.

(c) How does the Hamiltonian change in the case of antineutrino propagation?
