## 9.1

Consider a unified theory based on the gauge group $\mathrm{SO}(10)$.

(a) By referring to the $\mathrm{SU}(5)\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)$ decomposition of the representations of $\mathrm{SO}(10)$, show that each fermion generation can be accommodated in an irreducible $16$-dimensional representation, which also has a place for a left-handed antineutrino.

(b) Show that the adjoint $45$ representation contains the gauge bosons of the $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ theory. To do so, give the transformation properties of the $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ gauge bosons under $\mathrm{SU}(3)\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)$, and identify the $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ gauge bosons among them.

(c) Now examine the branching of $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ into $\mathrm{SU}(4)\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)$ and the subsequent branching of $\mathrm{SU}(4)$ into $\mathrm{SU}(3)\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)$. Use the $\mathrm{SU}(3)\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)$ decomposition of the fermion representation to show that $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ contains the left–right symmetric electroweak group as a subgroup.

[H. Georgi, “The State of the Art—Gauge Theories,” *AIP Conf. Proc.* **23**, 575 (1975); H. Fritzsch and P. Minkowski, *Ann. Phys. (NY)* **93**, 193 (1975).]

## 9.3

In the usual extension of $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ to $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ discussed in problem 9.1, the standard-model gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ is embedded directly in the Georgi–Glashow $\mathrm{SU}(5)$. If, instead, we consider $\mathrm{SO}(10)$ as a unifying group on its own, two distinct paths $\mathrm{SO}(10)\to\mathrm{SU}(5)\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ lead to the standard-model gauge group above the electroweak scale. Consider the symmetry breaking pattern $\mathrm{SO}(10)\to\mathrm{SU}(5)_Z\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_X$, with $\mathrm{SU}(5)_Z\to\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_Z$. The weak hypercharge must be a linear combination of the additive quantum numbers associated with $\mathrm{U}(1)_X$ and $\mathrm{U}(1)_Z$, $Y=\alpha Z+\beta X$. $X$ commutes with the generators of $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ and so is proportional to the identity, $X=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1,1)$. For the $5^*$ representation, $Z=\operatorname{diag}(2/3,2/3,2/3,-1,-1)$ commutes with the generators of $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}$. Evidently the Georgi–Glashow solution corresponds to $(\alpha,\beta)=(1,0)$ and $Z\equiv Y$, with assignments of the fermions to the $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ representations given in (9.2.9) and (9.2.10). Find a second $\mathrm{SU}(5)'\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_X$ solution for which $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ is embedded in $\mathrm{U}(1)_Z\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_X$, with $(\alpha,\beta)=(-1/5,1/5)$. Show that for this alternative case, the fermion assignments to $\mathrm{SU}(5)'$ multiplets are flipped from the Georgi–Glashow assignments by the interchanges $u\leftrightarrow d$, $e_{\mathrm{L}}\leftrightarrow\nu_{\mathrm{L}}$, and $e_{\mathrm{L}}^c\leftrightarrow N_{\mathrm{L}}^c$. [S. M. Barr, *Phys. Lett. B* **112**, 219 (1982).]

## 9.4

The groups $\mathrm{SO}(N)$ are generated by antisymmetric tensors $T_{ij}=-T_{ji}$ for $(i,j=1,\ldots,N)$, which satisfy the commutation relations $[T_{ij},T_{kl}]=i(\delta_{ik}T_{jl}-\delta_{il}T_{jk}-\delta_{jk}T_{il}+\delta_{jl}T_{ik})$. Show that for $N>6$, the quantity $\operatorname{tr}(\{T_{ij},T_{kl}\}T_{mn})$ must vanish, so that all representations of $\mathrm{SO}(N\geq7)$ are anomaly free. This helps to explain the apparently miraculous anomaly cancellation in the $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ model. [H. Georgi and S. L. Glashow, *Phys. Rev. D* **6**, 429 (1972).]

## 9.7

Consider the first stage of spontaneous symmetry breaking in the $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ model, namely, $\mathrm{SU}(5)\xrightarrow{24}\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$. Suppose that the effective potential for the scalars is given by

$$
\energy{V}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^2)=
\frac{\mechanical{\mu}^2}{2}\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^2)
+\frac{a}{4}[\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^2)]^2
+\frac{b}{2}\operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^4),
$$

where $\boldsymbol{\Phi}\in24$.

(a) Show that for $\mechanical{\mu}^2<0$, the asymmetric vacuum state characterized by (9.2.32) corresponds to the absolute minimum of the classical potential, provided that $15a+7b>0$ and $b>0$.

(b) Express the parameter $v_{24}$ of the vacuum expectation value in terms of $\mechanical{\mu}$, $a$, and $b$.

(c) Compute the masses of the superheavy gauge bosons and Higgs scalars that appear at this stage in the symmetry breaking. [A. J. Buras, J. Ellis, M. K. Gaillard, and D. V. Nanopoulos, *Nucl. Phys. B* **135**, 66 (1978).]

$$
\mathrm{SU}(5):\quad
\sin^2\theta_W(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)=\frac{1}{6}
+\frac{5}{9}\cdot\frac{\alpha(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)}{\alpha_s(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)}.

$$
\frac{A}{\alpha_2(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)}
