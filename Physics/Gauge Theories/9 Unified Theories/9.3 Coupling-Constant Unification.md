> [!TLDR]
> Running gauge couplings estimate the SU(5) unification scale and weak mixing parameter, and expose the minimal model’s disagreement with low-energy measurements.

<!-- PDF page 416; printed page 402 -->

In motivating the unification of the strong, weak, and electromagnetic interactions, we remarked on the possibility that the running coupling constants of the $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}$, and $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ gauge groups might evolve to a common value at some large value of $\mechanical{Q}^2$. We have now seen that in the unbroken $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ theory, the equality of the coupling constants indeed emerges when the $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ coupling is suitably normalized. This was exhibited in (9.2.19) and reflected in the prediction (9.2.27) for the weak mixing parameter $\sin^2\theta_W$. It is now appropriate to ask at what value of $\mechanical{Q}^2$ does this equality obtain, or, in other terms, what have the predictions of the unified theory to do with the low-energy world in which we live? The general analysis of these questions was given by Georgi, Quinn, and Weinberg [16].

The evolution of running coupling constants has been investigated in sections 8.2 and 8.3 for Abelian and non-Abelian gauge theories, respectively. In both cases, the coupling-constant evolution is influenced by the spectrum of particles that can appear in quantum corrections to the gauge-boson propagator. For QCD, for example, the number of quark flavors influences the rate of change of the coupling constant. Before transcribing the appropriate results from chapter 8, we need only cite an important technical result from field theory [17]: at each momentum or mass scale $\mechanical{\mu}$, particles that have masses $\mechanical{M}\gg\mechanical{\mu}$ effectively decouple from matrix elements involving ordinary external particles, with masses $\lesssim\mechanical{\mu}$. This means, for present purposes, that we may ignore the contribution of a particle to the coupling-constant evolution when the particle’s mass exceeds the momentum scale of interest. The practical effect of this decoupling theorem is to excuse us from worrying about the effects of superheavy particles [18].

Let us now consider the evolution of the three coupling constants in turn. Writing, as usual, $\alpha\equiv g^2/4\pi$, we have from (8.3.29) that the strong $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}$ coupling constant behaves as

$$
\frac{1}{\alpha_3(\mechanical{Q}^2)}=
\frac{1}{\alpha_3(\mechanical{\mu}^2)}+
b_3\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{\mu}^2}\right), \tag{9.3.1}
$$

with

$$
4\pi b_3=11-\frac{2n_f}{3}, \tag{9.3.2}
$$

where $n_f$ is the number of flavors with masses less than $\sqrt{\mechanical{Q}^2}$. It will be more apt for present purposes to count the number of fermion generations $n_g$ rather than the

<!-- PDF page 417; printed page 403 -->

number of individual flavors, so that

$$
4\pi b_3=11-\frac{4n_g}{3}. \tag{9.3.3}
$$

Now referring to (8.3.28), we may write the evolution of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}$ weak-isospin coupling constant as

$$
\frac{1}{\alpha_2(\mechanical{Q}^2)}=
\frac{1}{\alpha_2(\mechanical{\mu}^2)}+
b_2\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{\mu}^2}\right), \tag{9.3.4}
$$

with

$$
4\pi b_2=\frac{22-4n_g}{3}, \tag{9.3.5}
$$

neglecting a small contribution from Higgs bosons. The first term in $b_2$ is the standard gauge-boson contribution for an $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ theory. The second is the contribution of fermion loops, which is composed as follows. In each generation there are four left-handed fermion doublets: one lepton doublet and the quark doublet in three colors. Each of these doublets contributes the standard QED value $(-4/3)$ times $1/2$ from the group factor

$$
\operatorname{tr}\left(\frac{\tau^a}{2}\cdot\frac{\tau^b}{2}\right)
=\frac{\delta^{ab}}{2}, \tag{9.3.6}
$$

analogous to (8.3.10), times a spin factor $1/2$ because only left-handed fermions appear in the loop (cf. problem 9.8). Thus we have a total fermion contribution of

$$
4\cdot n_g\cdot\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)\cdot
\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)
=-\frac{4n_g}{3}. \tag{9.3.7}
$$

Finally, for the $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ weak-hypercharge coupling, which evolves as

$$
\frac{5}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha_1(\mechanical{Q}^2)}
=\frac{1}{\alpha_Y(\mechanical{Q}^2)}
=\frac{1}{\alpha_Y(\mechanical{\mu}^2)}
+b_Y\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{\mu}^2}\right), \tag{9.3.8}
$$

we simply have the QED contribution of the fermion loops weighted by the square of the hypercharge rather than the square of the electric charge for each species:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4\pi b_Y&=\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)
\left[\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(Y_{\mathrm{L}}^2)
+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(Y_{\mathrm{R}}^2)\right]\\
&=\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)\cdot\frac{5}{3}\cdot n_g
=-\frac{20n_g}{9}.
\end{aligned} \tag{9.3.9}
$$

The factor $5/3$ recovered here simply reflects the now-familiar ratio (9.2.21) between the normalizations of $\alpha_Y$ and $\alpha_1$.

If we now impose the equality of the $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}$, and $\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ coupling constants at the unification scale $\mechanical{u}$, we have, in the conventional $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ unified theory,

$$
\frac{1}{\alpha_3(\mechanical{Q}^2)}-
\frac{1}{\alpha_2(\mechanical{Q}^2)}
=\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}\right)\cdot
\begin{cases}
(11/12\pi),\\
(23/24\pi),
\end{cases} \tag{9.3.10}
$$

where the two coefficients omit (to make contact with much of the literature) and include the standard-model Higgs-boson contribution. Then, with the definition of the electromagnetic coupling constant introduced in (6.3.49),

$$
\frac{1}{\alpha}\equiv\frac{1}{\alpha_Y}+\frac{1}{\alpha_2}, \tag{9.3.11}
$$

which has only a formal meaning for momenta above the scale of electroweak symmetry breaking, we may write

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{\alpha(\mechanical{Q}^2)}
&=\frac{1}{\alpha_Y(\mechanical{u}^2)}+
\frac{1}{\alpha_2(\mechanical{u}^2)}+
(b_Y+b_2)\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}\right)\\
&=\frac{8}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha_3(\mechanical{u}^2)}
+(b_Y+b_2)\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}\right).
\end{aligned} \tag{9.3.12}
$$

We may then form the combination

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{8}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha_3(\mechanical{Q}^2)}
-\frac{1}{\alpha(\mechanical{Q}^2)}
&=\left(\frac{8b_3}{3}-b_Y-b_2\right)
\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}\right)\\
&=\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}\right)\cdot
\begin{cases}
(11/2\pi),\\
(67/12\pi),
\end{cases}
\end{aligned} \tag{9.3.13}
$$

(excluding and including Higgs-boson contributions), which does not depend on the number of (light) fermion generations. Let us now use the evolution equations just derived, together with experimentally determined values of the strong and electromagnetic coupling constants, to estimate the scale at which unification—in the sense of coincident values of the coupling constants—is achieved.[^9-3-refined-treatment]

The unification scale $\mechanical{u}$ is exponentially sensitive to the values of the combination of couplings on the left-hand side of (9.3.13), so it is important to take into account the $\mechanical{Q}^2$-evolution of $\alpha$ itself, which we considered in the discussion surrounding figure 8.13. At the electroweak scale characterized by $\mechanical{M_Z}=(91.1875\pm0.0021)\,\unit{\mathrm{GeV}}$ [20], the inverse of the fine structure constant is [21]

$$
\alpha^{-1}(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)=128.957\pm0.020. \tag{9.3.14}
$$

Then, with the value of the strong coupling noted in section 8.3,

$$
\alpha_s(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)=0.1184\pm0.0007 \tag{8.3.31}
$$

(so that $\alpha_s^{-1}(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)\approx8.446$), we deduce that the unification scale is

$$
\mechanical{u}\approx1.44\times10^{15}\,\unit{\mathrm{GeV}}. \tag{9.3.15}
$$

Although this is enormous on the scale of laboratory energies (a fact that underlines the riskiness of insouciant extrapolations), it lies well below the Planck mass

$$
\mechanical{M_{\mathrm{Pl}}}\equiv
\left(\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{c}}{G_{\mathrm{Newton}}}\right)^{1/2}
=1.22\times10^{19}\,\unit{\mathrm{GeV}}, \tag{9.3.16}
$$

at which the gravitational interactions become relevant for individual particles. This may be seen to justify a posteriori our decision to defer the inclusion of gravity in the construction of the unified theory. How two mass scales as different as $\mechanical{u}$ and $\mechanical{M_Z}$ may arise and be preserved remains mysterious (cf. §7.11). It is worth noting (cf. figure 7.26) that, depending on the mass of a standard-model Higgs boson, consistency of the electroweak theory might require new physics to intervene below the putative $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ unification scale.

Having roughly estimated the unification mass, we are now in a position to calculate the values of the $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}\otimes\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ coupling constants at low energy, in the *idealized* $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ unified theory. These are shown, in one-loop approximation, for three fermion generations in figure 9.3. There we show the evolution of the $\mathrm{SU}(3)_{\mathrm{c}}$, $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}$, and $\mathrm{U}(1)$ couplings $\alpha_3$, $\alpha_2$, and $\alpha_1$, which would attain a common value at the unification scale $\mechanical{u}$, as well as the weak hypercharge coupling $\alpha_Y$ and, as a derived quantity, the electromagnetic coupling constant $\alpha$ defined through (9.3.11). Problem 9.11 tests this version of coupling-constant unification and explores how the evolution of couplings would be changed by the introduction of supersymmetric partners of the known particles. $$
x_W=\sin^2\theta_W=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha_2}, \tag{9.3.17}

$$
\frac{8}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha_2}

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left[1-\frac{8x_W(\mechanical{Q}^2)}{3}\right]

&=\frac{1}{\alpha_Y(\mechanical{Q}^2)}
-\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha_2(\mechanical{Q}^2)}\\

This may be solved at once to yield

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_W(\mechanical{Q}^2)&=\frac{3}{8}
-\alpha(\mechanical{Q}^2)\frac{(3b_Y-5b_2)}{8}
\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}\right)\\
&=\frac{3}{8}+\frac{55\alpha(\mechanical{Q}^2)}{48\pi}
\ln\left(\frac{\mechanical{Q}^2}{\mechanical{u}^2}\right).
\end{aligned} \tag{9.3.20}
$$

The implied value of the weak mixing parameter at the electroweak scale,

$$
x_W(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)\approx0.203, \tag{9.3.21}
$$

approaches, but does not quite match, the value extracted from experiments. In the “on-shell” scheme, we determine from measured masses of the electroweak gauge bosons that

$$
x_W^{(\mathrm{os})}(\mechanical{M_Z}^2)\equiv
1-\mechanical{M_W}^2/\mechanical{M_Z}^2=0.2226. \tag{9.3.22}
$$

On the assumption that we have already discovered the relevant particle spectrum, the running of the coupling constants reduces the $\mathrm{SU}(5)$ prediction from the value $x_W=3/8$ at the unification scale to the low-energy value given in (9.3.21). This is a remarkable qualitative triumph—but quantitative failure—of the minimal unification hypothesis. Problem 9.13 explores how the prediction for $x_W$ would be changed by the introduction of superpartners at a low scale.

[^9-3-refined-treatment]: A refined treatment would make use of evolution equations beyond leading-logarithmic approximation as well as attention to the renormalization scheme in which the couplings are defined, but a leading-order approach allows us to extract the essential results with a minimum of fuss.
