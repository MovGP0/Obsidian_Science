> These nine exercises apply the variational method, conservation laws, classical action, and isospin symmetry.

### 2.1

Verify explicitly that changing the Lagrangian density by a total divergence leaves the Euler–Lagrange equations unchanged.

<!-- PDF page 48; printed page 34 -->

### 2.2

Using the freedom to add a total divergence to the Lagrangian, show that the Dirac Lagrangian for a free massless fermion may be written in the form

$$
\energy{\mathcal L'}=\frac12\bar\psi i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi
-\frac12(\partial_\mu\bar\psi)i\gamma^\mu\psi.
$$

### 2.3

Beginning from the Lagrangian for the free electromagnetic field (2.2.8), derive the equations of motion by independently varying each component of the electromagnetic field $\magnetic{A_\mu}$.

### 2.4

Use the requirement that the Lagrangian be invariant under a continuous symmetry to deduce the conserved quantity that corresponds to a particular transformation. Show that invariance under (i) translations in space, (ii) translations in time, and (iii) spatial rotations implies conservation of (i) momentum, (ii) energy, and (iii) angular momentum.

### 2.5

Consider a gravitating system of $N$ particles in nonrelativistic motion, for which the Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{L}=\energy{T}-\energy{V}=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^N\mechanical{m_i}\mechanical{\dot{\mathbf x}_i}^2
-G_{\mathrm N}\sum_{i\ne j}\frac{\mechanical{m_i}\mechanical{m_j}}{(\mechanical{\mathbf x_i}-\mechanical{\mathbf x_j})^2},
$$

where $G_{\mathrm N}$ is Newton’s constant and $\mechanical{m_i}$ and $\mechanical{\mathbf x_i}$ represent, respectively, the masses and positions of the individual particles. Show that invariance of the Lagrangian under an infinitesimal boost implies that the center of mass $\mechanical{\mathbf X}\equiv\sum_i\mechanical{m_i}\mechanical{\mathbf x_i}/\sum_i\mechanical{m_i}$ of the $N$-particle system moves with constant velocity along the boost direction. [For a relativistic generalization, see L. D. Landau and E. M. Lifshitz, *The Classical Theory of Fields*, 4th ed., trans. M. Hamermesh, Butterworth–Heinemann, Oxford, 1980, §14.]

### 2.6

The Lagrangian for a free particle in one space dimension is $\energy{L}=\frac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot x}^2$. Show that the action $\energy{S}$ corresponding to the classical motion of a free particle is

$$
\energy{S}=\frac{\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{x_2}-\mechanical{x_1})^2}{2(t_2-t_1)}.
$$

### 2.7

For a harmonic oscillator with Lagrangian $\energy{L}=\frac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot x}^2-\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}^2)$, show that the classical action is

$$
\energy{S}=\frac{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}}{2\sin\signal{\omega}\signal{T}}
\left[(\mechanical{x_1}^2+\mechanical{x_2}^2)\cos\signal{\omega}\signal{T}-2\mechanical{x_1}\mechanical{x_2}\right],
$$

where $\signal{T}=(t_2-t_1)$.

### 2.8

In the absence of the electroweak interactions, neither electric charge nor a small mass difference would distinguish neutron from proton. What observations might provide evidence for the existence of two nucleon species? For an isospin doublet of nucleons?

<!-- PDF page 49; printed page 35; text before For Further Reading -->

### 2.9

The near degeneracy of the neutron and proton masses, which differ by only 1.4 parts per mille [P. J. Mohr, B. N. Taylor, and D. B. Newell, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* **80**, 633 (2008)], is one hint that the neutron and proton may be taken as two states of a nucleon, with the $n$-$p$ mass difference attributed to electroweak effects. A second element of the case for isospin invariance is the charge independence of nuclear forces. Compare the binding energies of the tritium ($^3\mathrm H$) and $^3\mathrm{He}$ ground states [D. R. Tilley, H. R. Weller, and H. H. Hasan, *Nucl. Phys.* **A474**, 1 (1988)]. Use the $^3\mathrm{He}$ charge radius [D. C. Morton, Q. Wu, and G. W. F. Drake, *Phys. Rev.* **A73**, 034502 (2006)] to estimate the Coulomb repulsion in $^3\mathrm{He}$, and so test the charge independence of forces in the three-nucleon system.
