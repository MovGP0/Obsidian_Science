Let us begin to explore the consequences of the electroweak theory built on three generations of quarks and leptons. The results that we derived in chapter 6 for the

<!-- PDF page 209; printed page 195 -->

masses of the gauge bosons,

$$
\mechanical{M_W}^2=\frac{\pi\alpha}{G_{\mathrm{F}}\sqrt2\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm{W}}} \tag{6.3.58}
$$

and

$$
\mechanical{M_Z}^2=\frac{\mechanical{M_W}^2}{\cos^2\theta_{\mathrm{W}}}, \tag{6.3.59}
$$

are unaffected, to leading order, by the introduction of quarks into the theory.[^quark-loops] To compute the nonleptonic decay rates of the intermediate bosons, we appeal to the parton model in analogy with the successful description of the reaction

$$
e^+e^-\to\text{hadrons} \tag{7.2.1}
$$

in terms of the elementary process

$$
e^+e^-\to\gamma_{\mathrm{virtual}}\to q\bar q, \tag{7.2.2}
$$

in which the quark and antiquark in the semifinal state are assumed to materialize with probability one into the observed jets of hadrons. Hadronic decays are expected to occur as back-to-back jets in the $W$-boson rest frame. The normalized angular distribution of the product quark, the direction of which defines a jet axis, is the same as that of the electron described in (6.2.19), (6.2.23), and (6.2.24).

The partial widths for nonleptonic $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ decays may then be related to those for the leptonic decays. For the charged intermediate boson, we have

$$
\left.\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to u\bar d)&=3|V_{ud}|^2\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\ell^+\nu_\ell)\\
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to u\bar s)&=3|V_{us}|^2\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\ell^+\nu_\ell)\\
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to c\bar d)&=3|V_{cd}|^2\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\ell^+\nu_\ell)\\
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to c\bar s)&=3|V_{cs}|^2\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\ell^+\nu_\ell)
\end{aligned}\right\}, \tag{7.2.3}
$$

where the factor of 3 accounts for quark colors. As in the discussion of leptonic decays, we have idealized the decay products as massless, an approximation that may readily be undone. As before [cf. (6.2.20)], the leptonic decay width is given by

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\ell^+\nu_\ell)=\frac{G_{\mathrm{F}}\mechanical{M_W}^3}{6\pi\sqrt2}\approx227\,\unit{MeV}, \tag{7.2.4}
$$

where the numerical value is here derived from the observed mass (Ref. [3]),

$$
\mechanical{M_W}=(80.399\pm0.023)\,\unit{GeV}. \tag{7.2.5}
$$

The experimental value (Ref. [3]) of the leptonic width is $225\pm11\,\unit{MeV}$, in excellent agreement with the estimate (7.2.4).

If $D_q$ is the number of color-triplet $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}$ doublets into which the intermediate boson can decay, the rate for the inclusive decay of $W^+\to\text{hadrons}$ is given by

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\text{hadrons})=3D_q\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\ell^+\nu_\ell). \tag{7.2.6}
$$

[^quark-loops]: In section 7.7, we consider radiative corrections to the gauge-boson masses that arise from quark loops.

Similarly, the total width is given by

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\text{all})=(D_\ell+3D_q)\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\ell^+\nu_\ell), \tag{7.2.7}
$$

where $D_\ell$ is the number of kinematically accessible lepton doublets. Consequently, for the observed values of $D_\ell=3$ and $D_q=2$, we anticipate the branching fractions for each lepton flavor and for nonleptonic decays as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\mu^+\nu_\mu)}{\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\text{all})}&=\frac19,\\
\frac{\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\text{hadrons})}{\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\text{all})}&=\frac23,
\end{aligned} \tag{7.2.8}
$$

to be compared with the experimental values of $(10.80\pm0.09)\%$ and $(67.60\pm0.27)\%$, respectively (Ref. [3]). These results imply a total width

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(W^+\to\text{all})=2.05\,\unit{GeV}, \tag{7.2.9}
$$

in excellent agreement with the measured value (Ref. [3]), $2.085\pm0.042\,\unit{GeV}$.

Apart from the prediction (6.3.58) for the $W$-boson mass in terms of the weak mixing parameter $\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm{W}}$, none of the results for decays of the charged intermediate boson depends sensitively upon the specifics of the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{Y}}$ theory of the weak and electromagnetic interactions. Rather, they all follow from the intermediate boson picture developed in section 6.2, augmented by the idea of quark-lepton universality and by the parton model for nonleptonic decays. It is worthwhile to remark that the latter has been tested for virtual $W^\pm$ decays by the successful estimate of the branching ratios of the $\tau$-lepton.

The properties of the neutral electroweak gauge boson, in contrast, are evidently quite specific to the $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{Y}}$ theory, although no less easy to compute. Comparing the Feynman rules for $Z^0\to\nu\bar\nu$ and $Z^0\to e^+e^-$ given in figure 6.12 with those given for $Z^0\to q\bar q$ given in figure 7.2, we have, at once,

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to q\bar q)=3(L_q^2+R_q^2)\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\nu\bar\nu) \tag{7.2.10}
$$

for each quark flavor. Summing the kinematically allowed decays of $Z^0$ into $u\bar u$, $d\bar d$, $s\bar s$, $c\bar c$, and $b\bar b$, we find

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{hadrons})}{\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\nu\bar\nu)}
&\approx3[2\cdot\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to u\bar u)+3\cdot\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to d\bar d)]\\
&=3\left[2\left(1-\frac{8x_{\mathrm{W}}}{3}+\frac{32x_{\mathrm{W}}^2}{9}\right)
+3\left(1-\frac{4x_{\mathrm{W}}}{3}+\frac{8x_{\mathrm{W}}^2}{9}\right)\right]\\
&=3\left(5-\frac{28x_{\mathrm{W}}}{3}+\frac{88x_{\mathrm{W}}^2}{9}\right),
\end{aligned} \tag{7.2.11}
$$

whereas [cf. (6.3.65)]

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to e^+e^-)=(1-4x_{\mathrm{W}}+8x_{\mathrm{W}}^2)\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\nu\bar\nu). \tag{7.2.12}
$$

The scale of the partial widths is set by the neutrinic decay rate,

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\nu\bar\nu)=\frac{G_{\mathrm{F}}\mechanical{M_Z}^3}{12\pi\sqrt2}\approx166\,\unit{MeV}, \tag{6.3.64}
$$

which we evaluated using the observed mass (Ref. [3]), $\mechanical{M_Z}=91.1876\pm0.0021\,\unit{GeV}$.

We may compute the total width of the neutral gauge boson as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{all})
&\approx3\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\nu\bar\nu)+3\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to e^+e^-)+\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{hadrons})\\
&=\left(21-40x_{\mathrm{W}}+\frac{160x_{\mathrm{W}}^2}{3}\right)\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\nu\bar\nu).
\end{aligned} \tag{7.2.13}
$$

Now, using the value of the weak mixing parameter inferred from $\mechanical{M_Z}$ in section 6.3, $\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm{W}}=0.2122$, we predict at lowest order

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{all})=2.474\,\unit{GeV}, \tag{7.2.14}
$$

to be compared with the experimental value, $(2.4952\pm0.0023)\,\unit{GeV}$;

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{hadrons})=1.722\,\unit{GeV}, \tag{7.2.15}
$$

to be compared with $(1.7444\pm0.0020)\,\unit{GeV}$, and

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to e^+e^-)=84.8\,\unit{MeV}, \tag{7.2.16}
$$

to be compared with $(83.984\pm0.086)\,\unit{MeV}$. It is also useful to observe the expected and observed branching fractions

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\ell^+\ell^-)}{\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{all})}
&=3.43\%\ [(3.3658\pm0.0023)\%],\\
\frac{\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{hadrons})}{\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{all})}
&=69.59\%\ [(69.91\pm0.06)\%],\\
\frac{3\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\nu\bar\nu)}{\energy{\Gamma}(Z^0\to\text{all})}
&=20.1\%\ [(20.00\pm0.06)\%].
\end{aligned} \tag{7.2.17}
$$

The $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{Y}}$ electroweak theory successfully anticipates the existence and gross properties of the gauge bosons $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$. Indeed, the close agreement between lowest-order predictions and experiment is most remarkable. Although attention to quantum corrections is required to extract the most information from experiment and to test the theory with high precision, it is significant that lowest-order calculations suffice for a rather comprehensive appreciation of electroweak phenomena. We turn next to a number of more-detailed applications of the electroweak theory.
