$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Yukawa}}={}&-\zeta_1[(\bar{\mathsf{L}}_u\bar\phi)u_{\mathrm{R}}
+\bar u_{\mathrm{R}}(\bar\phi^\dagger\mathsf{L}_u)]
-\zeta_2[(\bar{\mathsf{L}}_u\phi)d_{\mathrm{R}}
+\bar d_{\mathrm{R}}(\phi^\dagger\mathsf{L}_u)]\\
&-\zeta_3[(\bar{\mathsf{L}}_u\phi)s_{\mathrm{R}}
+\bar s_{\mathrm{R}}(\phi^\dagger\mathsf{L}_u)]

+\bar c_{\mathrm{R}}(\bar\phi^\dagger\mathsf{L}_c)]\\
&-\zeta_5[(\bar{\mathsf{L}}_c\phi)d_{\mathrm{R}}
+\bar d_{\mathrm{R}}(\phi^\dagger\mathsf{L}_c)]
-\zeta_6[(\bar{\mathsf{L}}_c\phi)s_{\mathrm{R}}

\end{aligned}
$$

$$

## 7.3

A standard convention for the $3\times3$ quark-mixing matrix has become

$$
\mathsf{V}=\begin{pmatrix}
c_{12}c_{13}&s_{12}c_{13}&s_{13}e^{-i\delta}\\
-s_{12}c_{23}-c_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}
&c_{12}c_{23}-s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&s_{23}c_{13}\\
s_{12}s_{23}-c_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}
&-c_{12}s_{23}-s_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&c_{23}c_{13}
\end{pmatrix},
$$

with the angles $\theta_{ij}$ in the first quadrant and $\delta$ in $(-\pi,\pi]$. [L.-L. Chau and W.-Y. Keung, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **53**, 1802 (1984).] The hierarchy $s_{13}\ll s_{23}\ll s_{12}\ll1$ is a useful idealization of experimental information. A compact parametrization, informed by the rough magnitudes of the elements, aids in anticipating and integrating experimental results.

(a) Note that the quark-mixing matrix is in first approximation diagonal, symmetrical, and with a hierarchical structure for the mixing between generations.

Now take $V_{us}\equiv\lambda$ as an expansion parameter for deviations from the identity matrix. Express $V_{ub}=A\lambda^2$, where [cf. (7.1.28)] $A\approx0.808$ is of order unity. Set $V_{ub}=0$, and use the requirement of unitarity, $\mathsf{V}^\dagger\mathsf{V}=\mathsf{I}$, to solve for the diagonal elements. Show that, to $O(\lambda^2)$,

$$
\mathsf{V}=\begin{pmatrix}
1-\tfrac12\lambda^2&\lambda&0\\
-\lambda&1-\tfrac12\lambda^2&A\lambda^2\\
0&-A\lambda^2&1
\end{pmatrix}+O(\lambda^3).
$$

(b) Now extend the parametrization to $O(\lambda^3)$. Assign $s_{12}=\lambda$, $s_{23}=A\lambda^2$, and $s_{13}e^{-i\delta}=A\lambda^3(\rho-i\eta)$. By imposing the unitarity condition, show that

$$
\mathsf{V}=\begin{pmatrix}
1-\tfrac12\lambda^2&\lambda&A\lambda^3(\rho-i\eta)\\
-\lambda&1-\tfrac12\lambda^2&A\lambda^2\\
A\lambda^3(1-\rho-i\eta)&-A\lambda^2&1
\end{pmatrix}+O(\lambda^4).
$$

[L. Wolfenstein, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **51**, 1945 (1983).]

## 7.4

The unitarity condition $\mathsf{V}^\dagger\mathsf{V}=\mathsf{I}$ may be expressed through the equations

$$
\sum_iV_{ij}V_{ik}^*=\delta_{jk}\quad\text{and}\quad
\sum_jV_{ij}V_{kj}^*=\delta_{ik}.
$$

It can be informative to express the orthogonality conditions (for $j\ne k$ and $i\ne j$, respectively) as triangles in a complex plane.

(a) Starting from the Wolfenstein parametrization at $O(\lambda^3)$, compute the equations for the six unitarity triangles. V_{ud}V_{ub}^*+V_{cd}V_{cb}^*+V_{td}V_{tb}^*=0, \tag{⋆}

$$
R_te^{-i\beta}+R_ue^{i\gamma}=1,
$$

where the nontrivial lengths of the sides are

$$
R_t=\left|\frac{V_{td}V_{tb}^*}{V_{cd}V_{cb}^*}\right|
\quad\text{and}\quad
R_u=\left|\frac{V_{ud}V_{ub}^*}{V_{cd}V_{cb}^*}\right|.

## 7.21

A fine-tuning problem may be seen to arise even when the scale $\energy{\Lambda}$ in (7.11.1) is not extremely large. What has been called the “LEP paradox” refers to a tension within the precise measurements of electroweak observables carried out at LEP and elsewhere. On one hand, the global fits of Refs. [87] and [75] point to a light standard-model Higgs boson. On the other hand, a straightforward effective-operator analysis of possible beyond-the-standard-model contributions to the same observables gives no hint of any new physics—of the kind needed to resolve the hierarchy problem—below about $5\,\unit{TeV}$. The one-loop quantum corrections to the Higgs-boson mass shown schematically in figure 7.27 contribute

$$

(6\mechanical{M_W}^2+3\mechanical{M_Z}^2+\mechanical{M_H}^2-12\mechanical{m_t}^2),
$$

where $\energy{\Lambda}$ represents the cutoff scale at which new physics might be presumed to intervene.

\frac{|V_{ud}|^2}{\sin^2\theta_{\mathrm{W}}}
\left[\left(\frac{1}{1+\mechanical{\hat t}/\mechanical{\hat u}}+\electric{Q_d}\right)^2
\frac{\mechanical{\hat t}^2+\mechanical{\hat u}^2+2\mechanical{M_W}^2\energy{\hat s}}
{\mechanical{\hat t}\mechanical{\hat u}}\right],
$$

where $V_{ud}$ is an element of the quark-mixing matrix, $\electric{Q_d}$ is the electric charge of the down quark (in units of the proton charge), and $\mechanical{\hat t}$ measures the momentum transfer between $d$ and $W^-$. [K. O. Mikaelian, M. A. Samuel, and D. Sahdev, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **43**, 746 (1979).]

(b) Express the Mandelstam invariants in terms of the c.m. scattering angle variable $\cos\theta^*$ and show that the vanishing of the differential cross section at $\mechanical{\hat t}/\mechanical{\hat u}=2$ corresponds to $\cos\theta^*=\electric{Q_d}=-\tfrac13$. The extinction is understood in terms of classical radiation zeros [S. J. Brodsky and R. W. Brown, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **49**, 966 (1982)]. ## 7.27

Within the standard $\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{Y}}$ electroweak theory, the decay of the Higgs boson into two photons proceeds through fermion loops or $W$-boson loops:

![[gauge-theories-problem-7-27-higgs-two-photon-loops.svg|**Fig. 7.P27** Fermion and $W$-boson loop contributions to $H\to\gamma\gamma$ in problem 7.27. The source diagram has no figure number.]]

(a) Show that decay rate may be written as

$$
\energy{\Gamma}(H\to\gamma\gamma)
=\frac{G_{\mathrm{F}}\alpha^2\mechanical{M_H}^3}{8\pi^3\sqrt2}
\left|\sum_iN_c^{(i)}\electric{Q_i}^2\mathcal{F}(\epsilon_i)
+\mathcal{W}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{W}})\right|^2,
$$

where $N_c^{(i)}$ is the number of colors of fermion $i$ and $\electric{Q_i}$ is its charge. The scaling variables are $\epsilon_i=4\mechanical{m_i}^2/\mechanical{M_H}^2$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{W}}=4\mechanical{M_W}^2/\mechanical{M_H}^2$, and the loop contributions are

$$
\mathcal{F}(\epsilon_i)=\frac{\epsilon_i}{2}[1+(\epsilon_i-1)\varphi(\epsilon_i)],
$$

$$
\mathcal{W}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{W}})
=-\tfrac14[2+3\epsilon_{\mathrm{W}}
+3(\epsilon_{\mathrm{W}}^2-2\epsilon_{\mathrm{W}})\varphi(\epsilon_{\mathrm{W}})],
$$

where

$$
\varphi(\epsilon)=\begin{cases}
-[\sin^{-1}(1/\sqrt{\epsilon})]^2,&\epsilon>1,\\
\tfrac14[\ln(\zeta_+/\zeta_-)+i\pi]^2,&\epsilon<1,
\end{cases}
$$

and $\zeta_\pm=1\pm\sqrt{1-\epsilon}$.

(b) For a light Higgs boson, with $\mechanical{M_H}\approx120\,\unit{GeV}$, which fermion makes the dominant contribution?

(c) How would $\energy{\Gamma}(H\to\gamma\gamma)$ change if the Higgs boson did not couple to fermions?

[A convenient reference is M. Spira et al., *Nucl. Phys. B* **453**, 17 (1995). See also W. J. Marciano, C. Zhang, and S. Willenbrock, *Phys. Rev. D* **85**, 013002 (2012).]
