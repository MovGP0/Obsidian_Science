title: "Covectors and tensors"

> [!TLDR]
> Each upper tensor index receives a positive connection term, and each lower index receives a negative term, so scalar contractions differentiate correctly.

## The pairing determines the sign

A covector field $\alpha$ assigns a linear functional on each tangent space. In coordinates, write $\alpha=\alpha_j\,dx^j$ and $V=V^j\partial_j$, where $dx^j(\partial_k)=\delta^j_k$. Their pairing is the scalar function $\alpha(V)=\alpha_jV^j$. Summation over repeated upper and lower indices is understood.

Extend the connection to scalar functions by $\nabla_i f=\partial_i f$. Require the product rule for the scalar pairing:

$$
\partial_i\bigl(\alpha(V)\bigr)
=(\nabla_i\alpha)(V)+\alpha(\nabla_iV).
$$

Use the convention $\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_j=\Gamma^k{}_{ij}\partial_k$ from [[21.03 Connection coefficients]]. Expanding the left side and the known vector derivative gives

$$
(\partial_i\alpha_j)V^j+\alpha_j\partial_iV^j
=(\nabla_i\alpha_j)V^j+
\alpha_k(\partial_iV^k+\Gamma^k{}_{ij}V^j).
$$

Cancel the component-derivative terms for $V$. Since $V$ is arbitrary, compare its coefficients:

$$
\boxed{\nabla_i\alpha_j=\partial_i\alpha_j-\Gamma^k{}_{ij}\alpha_k.}
$$

The negative sign is necessary. With a positive sign, the two basis contributions would add rather than cancel in a scalar contraction.

## The dual basis and general tensors

Take $\alpha=dx^j$ and differentiate its pairing with $\partial_k$. Since that pairing is the constant $\delta^j_k$, the same rule yields

$$
\nabla_i dx^j=-\Gamma^j{}_{ik}\,dx^k.
$$

A tensor of type $(p,q)$ has $p$ upper indices and $q$ lower indices. Extend $\nabla$ by the product rule for tensor products and by compatibility with contractions. For a tensor of type $(1,1)$ this gives

$$
\nabla_i T^a{}_b
=\partial_iT^a{}_b+
\Gamma^a{}_{ic}T^c{}_b-
\Gamma^c{}_{ib}T^a{}_c.
$$

For a covariant two-tensor $S$ it gives

$$
\nabla_i S_{ab}
=\partial_iS_{ab}-\Gamma^c{}_{ia}S_{cb}-\Gamma^c{}_{ib}S_{ac}.
$$

There is one correction for each original tensor index. The new derivative index $i$ labels the operation; do not add a further correction for it during this first derivative. A second covariant derivative acts on the resulting tensor, including its derivative index.

## Worked example: the differential of x in polar coordinates

In the Euclidean plane, $x=r\cos\theta$, so

$$
\alpha=dx=\cos\theta\,dr-r\sin\theta\,d\theta.
$$

This is a constant covector in Cartesian coordinates. Check its angular covariant derivative with the flat polar coefficients $\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}=-r$ and $\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}=1/r$:

$$
\nabla_\theta\alpha_r
=-\sin\theta-\frac1r(-r\sin\theta)=0,
$$

$$
\nabla_\theta\alpha_\theta
=-r\cos\theta-(-r)\cos\theta=0.
$$

For the radial derivative, $\nabla_r\alpha_r=0$ and

$$
\nabla_r\alpha_\theta=-\sin\theta-\frac1r(-r\sin\theta)=0.
$$

Thus $\nabla\alpha=0$ in this chart as well. The partial derivatives of its components do not vanish, but the dual-basis terms cancel them.

## Contraction and raising indices

For $T^a{}_a$, the trace of a $(1,1)$ tensor, the two correction sums cancel after renaming summed indices. Hence $\nabla_i(T^a{}_a)=\partial_i(T^a{}_a)$. If a metric $g$ is present, raising an index commutes with covariant differentiation only when $\nabla g=0$. An arbitrary affine connection need not have this property; see [[21.06 Levi-Civita connection]].

## Practice

1. Show directly that the identity tensor $I^a{}_b=\delta^a_b$ is parallel for every connection.
2. For a scalar function $f$, derive the coordinate formula for its Hessian $H_{ij}=(\nabla_i df)_j$. Under what condition is it symmetric?

## Worked answers

1. The components of $I$ are constant. Therefore $\nabla_iI^a{}_b=\Gamma^a{}_{ic}\delta^c_b-\Gamma^c{}_{ib}\delta^a_c=\Gamma^a{}_{ib}-\Gamma^a{}_{ib}=0$. No metric assumption is needed.
2. Since $(df)_j=\partial_jf$, the covector rule gives $H_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_jf-\Gamma^k{}_{ij}\partial_kf$. Smoothness makes the mixed partials equal. Thus $H_{ij}-H_{ji}=-(\Gamma^k{}_{ij}-\Gamma^k{}_{ji})\partial_kf$. It is symmetric for every smooth $f$ when the connection is torsion-free in a coordinate frame.
