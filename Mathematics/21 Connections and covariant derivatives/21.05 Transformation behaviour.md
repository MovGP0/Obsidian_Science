> [!TLDR]
> A change of coordinates adds second derivatives to the connection coefficients, and these terms cancel the extra terms in a component derivative.

## Two charts, one connection

Let $x^i$ and $y^a$ be two smooth coordinate systems on the same region, with a smooth invertible change of coordinates. Set

$$
A^i{}_b=\frac{\partial x^i}{\partial y^b},
\qquad
B^a{}_k=\frac{\partial y^a}{\partial x^k}.
$$

These matrices are inverses at each point. The coordinate fields satisfy $\partial_{y^b}=A^i{}_b\partial_{x^i}$. Define coefficients using the derivative direction as the first lower index:

$$
\nabla_{\partial_{x^i}}\partial_{x^j}
=\Gamma^k{}_{ij}\partial_{x^k},
\qquad
\nabla_{\partial_{y^b}}\partial_{y^c}
=\widetilde\Gamma^a{}_{bc}\partial_{y^a}.
$$

Repeated upper and lower indices are summed. Tildes mark coefficients in the $y$ chart; they do not mark a different connection.

## Derive the transformation rule

Use linearity in the direction, then differentiate the coefficient in the second field:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_{\partial_{y^b}}\partial_{y^c}
&=\nabla_{A^i{}_b\partial_{x^i}}
 (A^j{}_c\partial_{x^j})\\
&=A^i{}_b(\partial_{x^i}A^k{}_c)\partial_{x^k}
+A^i{}_bA^j{}_c\Gamma^k{}_{ij}\partial_{x^k}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The chain rule turns $A^i{}_b\partial_{x^i}A^k{}_c$ into $\partial^2x^k/(\partial y^b\partial y^c)$. Change the output basis with $\partial_{x^k}=B^a{}_k\partial_{y^a}$. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
\widetilde\Gamma^a{}_{bc}
=B^a{}_kA^i{}_bA^j{}_c\Gamma^k{}_{ij}
+B^a{}_k\frac{\partial^2x^k}{\partial y^b\partial y^c}.
}
$$

A tensor of type $(1,2)$ would have only the first term. The second term is the precise reason that the connection coefficients are not tensor components. It vanishes for affine coordinate changes but usually remains for nonlinear changes.

## Why the covariant derivative transforms as a tensor

Vector components obey $\widetilde V^a=B^a{}_kV^k$. Differentiating this expression produces both $B^a{}_k\partial V^k$ and $(\partial B^a{}_k)V^k$. The latter is the unwanted derivative of the changing transformation matrix. Insert the coefficient rule above into the covariant derivative. Differentiating the inverse relation $B^a{}_kA^k{}_c=\delta^a_c$ shows that the unwanted term cancels the second-derivative term. The result is

$$
\widetilde{(\nabla V)}^a{}_b
=B^a{}_kA^i{}_b(\nabla V)^k{}_i.
$$

This is the transformation rule of a $(1,1)$ tensor. The word tensor here refers to $\nabla V$ for one fixed vector field $V$. It does not assert function-linearity of $\nabla_XV$ in $V$.

## Worked example on a line

On $x>0$, take the standard connection $\Gamma^x{}_{xx}=0$ and use $y=\log x$, so $x=e^y$. In one dimension the transformation rule reduces to

$$
\widetilde\Gamma^y{}_{yy}
=\frac{dy}{dx}\frac{d^2x}{dy^2}
=\frac1x x=1.
$$

The same connection has zero coefficients in $x$ and nonzero coefficients in $y$. A tensor which vanishes in one basis must vanish in every basis, so this also gives a direct counterexample to tensoriality.

Now take $V=\partial_x$. Its $y$ component is $\widetilde V^y=dy/dx=e^{-y}$. Hence

$$
\widetilde\nabla_y\widetilde V^y
=\frac{d}{dy}e^{-y}+1\cdot e^{-y}=0,
$$

in agreement with $\nabla_xV^x=0$.

## What is tensorial about two connections?

Subtract the transformation rules for two connections. The second-derivative terms are identical and cancel. Therefore $\widehat\Gamma^k{}_{ij}-\Gamma^k{}_{ij}$ transforms as a $(1,2)$ tensor, consistent with the intrinsic argument in [[21.02 Affine connections]].

## Practice

1. On $x>0$, set $y=x^2$. Starting with $\Gamma^x{}_{xx}=0$, find $\widetilde\Gamma^y{}_{yy}$.
2. For the same coordinates, verify that $V=\partial_x$ still has zero covariant derivative.

## Worked answers

1. The inverse map is $x=\sqrt y$. Its second derivative is $-1/(4y^{3/2})$, while $dy/dx=2\sqrt y$. Their product is $-1/(2y)$, so $\widetilde\Gamma^y{}_{yy}=-1/(2y)$.
2. The new vector component is $\widetilde V^y=2\sqrt y$. Its ordinary derivative is $1/\sqrt y$. The connection term is $[-1/(2y)]2\sqrt y=-1/\sqrt y$. The sum is zero, as the tensor transformation law requires.
