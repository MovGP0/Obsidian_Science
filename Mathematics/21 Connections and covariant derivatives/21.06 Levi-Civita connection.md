title: "Levi-Civita connection"

> [!TLDR]
> A smooth nondegenerate metric determines one torsion-free connection that preserves the metric.

## The two defining requirements

Let $g$ be a smooth, symmetric, nondegenerate metric on a smooth manifold $M$. Nondegenerate means that its coordinate matrix $g_{ij}$ has an inverse $g^{ij}$ at every point. The signature can be positive definite or indefinite. The **Levi-Civita connection** is the unique connection with metric compatibility and zero torsion.

Metric compatibility means that, for vector fields $X,Y,Z$,

$$
X\bigl(g(Y,Z)\bigr)
=g(\nabla_XY,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_XZ).
$$

Equivalently, $\nabla g=0$. In coordinates, with $\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_j=\Gamma^k{}_{ij}\partial_k$, it says

$$
\partial_i g_{jk}=\Gamma^\ell{}_{ij}g_{\ell k}
+\Gamma^\ell{}_{ik}g_{j\ell}.
$$

This is the covariant two-tensor rule from [[21.04 Covectors and tensors]]. Zero torsion means

$$
T(X,Y)=\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX-[X,Y]=0,
$$

where $[X,Y]$ is the Lie bracket. Coordinate fields commute, so this condition becomes $\Gamma^k{}_{ij}=\Gamma^k{}_{ji}$. For a non-coordinate frame, zero torsion does not generally mean symmetry of the lower coefficients.

## Derive the Christoffel formula

Write metric compatibility for the three index orders $(i,j,k)$, $(j,i,k)$, and $(k,i,j)$. Add the first two equations and subtract the third. Symmetry of $g$ and of the two lower coefficient indices cancels all terms except the two equal copies with output paired against $k$. Thus

$$
\partial_i g_{jk}+\partial_j g_{ik}-\partial_k g_{ij}
=2g_{k\ell}\Gamma^\ell{}_{ij}.
$$

Multiply by $g^{mk}/2$ and use $g^{mk}g_{k\ell}=\delta^m_\ell$. The result is

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma^m{}_{ij}
=\frac12g^{mk}
\left(\partial_i g_{jk}+\partial_j g_{ik}-\partial_k g_{ij}\right).
}
$$

Every torsion-free compatible connection must have these coefficients, which proves uniqueness. The formula is symmetric in $i,j$. Substitution back into the compatibility equation verifies that the required connection exists in each chart.

To see that the local constructions agree on overlaps, one can use the intrinsic **Koszul formula**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2g(\nabla_XY,Z)
={}&Xg(Y,Z)+Yg(Z,X)-Zg(X,Y)\\
&-g(X,[Y,Z])+g(Y,[Z,X])+g(Z,[X,Y]).
\end{aligned}
$$

The right side depends only on the metric and vector fields. Its function-linearity in $Z$ and nondegeneracy of $g$ determine $\nabla_XY$. The derivative rules give the connection axioms. In a coordinate frame the bracket terms vanish, and the formula reduces to the coordinate calculation above. Thus it gives the same globally defined connection.

## Worked example: the polar metric

On a polar chart of the Euclidean plane,

$$
(g_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&r^2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
(g^{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&r^{-2}\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Only $\partial_r g_{\theta\theta}=2r$ is nonzero. Therefore

$$
\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}=-\frac12\partial_r(r^2)=-r,
\qquad
\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}=\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}
=\frac12r^{-2}\partial_r(r^2)=\frac1r.
$$

All other coefficients vanish. Check compatibility in one nontrivial component:

$$
\nabla_r g_{\theta\theta}
=2r-\frac1r r^2-\frac1r r^2=0.
$$

The metric components change with $r$, but their covariant derivative vanishes. This is precisely what compatibility asserts.

## Limitations and common errors

Metric compatibility alone does not force zero torsion. Zero torsion alone does not select a connection without a metric. A degenerate bilinear form has no inverse $g^{ij}$, so this theorem does not apply in that form. Also, the polar formula is valid only for $r>0$: the apparent singularity at $r=0$ comes from the coordinate chart.

## Practice

1. On a line with metric $g=a(x)^2\,dx^2$, where $a(x)>0$ is smooth, find the Levi-Civita coefficient.
2. Show that a metric-compatible connection preserves the inner product of two parallel vector fields along a curve.

## Worked answers

1. Here $g_{xx}=a^2$ and $g^{xx}=a^{-2}$. The formula gives $\Gamma^x{}_{xx}=\tfrac12a^{-2}(2aa')=a'/a$. Positivity ensures that the metric is nondegenerate.
2. For parallel fields $V,W$ along $\gamma$, compatibility gives $d[g(V,W)]/dt=g(DV/dt,W)+g(V,DW/dt)$. Both covariant derivatives are zero, so the scalar derivative is zero. Thus the inner product is constant on each connected parameter interval.
