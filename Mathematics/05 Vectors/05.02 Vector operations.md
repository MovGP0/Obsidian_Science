> [!TLDR]
> Add vectors by successive displacements, subtract by adding an opposite vector, and use scalar multiplication to change length and direction.

## Addition joins displacements

Let $\mathbf u$ and $\mathbf v$ be vectors in the same real Euclidean space. To construct $\mathbf u+\mathbf v$, draw an arrow for $\mathbf u$. Put the tail of an arrow for $\mathbf v$ at its head. The vector from the first tail to the last head is the sum. This is the **head-to-tail rule**.

In a fixed basis, addition acts on corresponding components:

$$
(u_1,u_2)+(v_1,v_2)=(u_1+v_1,u_2+v_2).
$$

To see why, let $A$, $B$, and $C$ be successive points. Then

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}+\overrightarrow{BC}
=(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)+(\mathbf c-\mathbf b)
=\mathbf c-\mathbf a
=\overrightarrow{AC}.
$$

The intermediate position $\mathbf b$ cancels. Thus the total displacement depends only on the initial and final points.

In the figure, the translated copy of $\mathbf v$ has the same direction and length as the arrow from the origin. The diagonal therefore gives both constructions of the sum.

## Subtraction and multiplication by a number

The opposite vector $-\mathbf v$ has every component negated. Define subtraction by

$$
\mathbf u-\mathbf v=\mathbf u+(-\mathbf v).
$$

If the arrows for $\mathbf u$ and $\mathbf v$ share a tail, $\mathbf u-\mathbf v$ points from the head of $\mathbf v$ to the head of $\mathbf u$. Reversing that arrow gives $\mathbf v-\mathbf u$, which is generally different.

A **scalar** is a number that multiplies a vector. For a real scalar $a$,

$$
a(u_1,u_2)=(au_1,au_2).
$$

If $a>0$, multiplication preserves the direction and multiplies the length by $a$. If $a<0$, it reverses the direction and multiplies the length by $|a|$. If $a=0$, the result is $\mathbf 0$. The zero vector itself has no direction, so these direction statements apply to nonzero vectors.

## Algebraic rules and their meaning

Vector addition is commutative and associative:

$$
\mathbf u+\mathbf v=\mathbf v+\mathbf u,
\qquad
(\mathbf u+\mathbf v)+\mathbf w=\mathbf u+(\mathbf v+\mathbf w).
$$

Commutativity follows from the parallelogram: either pair of sides reaches the same opposite corner. Associativity means that grouping successive displacements does not change the total. Scalars distribute over sums:

$$
a(\mathbf u+\mathbf v)=a\mathbf u+a\mathbf v,
\qquad
(a+b)\mathbf u=a\mathbf u+b\mathbf u.
$$

These rules also follow component by component from the real-number rules. You can therefore simplify vector expressions without repeatedly drawing their arrows. However, a vector cannot be added to a scalar, and there is no general operation of division by a vector.

## Worked example: a combined operation

Take $\mathbf u=(3,1)$ and $\mathbf v=(-1,2)$. First calculate the scaled vectors:

$$
2\mathbf u=(6,2),\qquad 3\mathbf v=(-3,6).
$$

Then subtract:

$$
2\mathbf u-3\mathbf v=(6-(-3),2-6)=(9,-4).
$$

As a separate check, distribute first:

$$
2(3,1)+(-3)(-1,2)=(6,2)+(3,-6)=(9,-4).
$$

The negative scalar reverses the direction of $\mathbf v$. A frequent error is to negate only one component when subtracting a vector. Parentheses prevent this error.

The same rules work in three or more dimensions and in any basis. The geometry of length needs more information, as explained in [[05.04 Magnitude and unit vectors]] and [[05.06 General bases]].

## Practice

1. For $\mathbf a=(2,-3)$ and $\mathbf b=(4,1)$, calculate $3\mathbf a-2\mathbf b$ and $\mathbf b-\mathbf a$.
2. Solve $2\mathbf x+\mathbf a=\mathbf b$ with $\mathbf a=(1,5,-2)$ and $\mathbf b=(7,-1,4)$.

## Worked answers

1. Scaling gives $3\mathbf a=(6,-9)$ and $2\mathbf b=(8,2)$. Therefore $3\mathbf a-2\mathbf b=(-2,-11)$. For the second expression, subtract components in the stated order: $\mathbf b-\mathbf a=(4-2,1-(-3))=(2,4)$.
2. Subtract $\mathbf a$ from both sides: $2\mathbf x=\mathbf b-\mathbf a=(6,-6,6)$. Multiply by $1/2$ to obtain $\mathbf x=(3,-3,3)$. Substitution gives $2(3,-3,3)+(1,5,-2)=(7,-1,4)$.
