title: "Dot products"

> [!TLDR]
> The dot product connects vector components with lengths, angles, perpendicularity, and orthogonal projection.

## Definition in an orthonormal basis

For real vectors $\mathbf u=(u_1,\ldots,u_n)$ and $\mathbf v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ expressed in the same orthonormal basis, define their **dot product** by

$$
\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_i v_i.
$$

The result is a real number, not a vector. Multiply matching components, then add. The dot product is symmetric and linear in each argument. In particular,

$$
(a\mathbf u+b\mathbf v)\cdot\mathbf w
=a(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w)+b(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w).
$$

It also gives squared length: $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v=\|\mathbf v\|^2$. In a basis that is not orthonormal, the simple component formula needs extra coefficients; see [[05.06 General bases]].

## Angle and orthogonality

For two nonzero vectors, let $\theta\in[0,\pi]$ be the smaller angle between arrows with a common tail. The relation between angle and dot product is

$$
\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=\|\mathbf u\|\,\|\mathbf v\|\cos\theta.
$$

To obtain this relation, consider the triangle with sides $\mathbf u$, $\mathbf v$, and $\mathbf u-\mathbf v$. Expanding by the component definition gives

$$
\|\mathbf u-\mathbf v\|^2
=\|\mathbf u\|^2+\|\mathbf v\|^2-2\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v.
$$

The cosine rule for the same triangle replaces its last term by $-2\|\mathbf u\|\|\mathbf v\|\cos\theta$. Comparing gives the relation above. Consequently,

$$
\theta=\arccos\left(\frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf u\|\|\mathbf v\|}\right).
$$

The denominator must be nonzero. The dot product is positive for an acute angle, zero for a right angle, and negative for an obtuse angle. Vectors are **orthogonal** when their dot product is zero. Algebraically, the zero vector is orthogonal to every vector, although an angle involving it is undefined. The angle formula applies only to nonzero vectors.

## Orthogonal projection

Let $\mathbf a\neq\mathbf0$ specify a line through the origin. Seek the point on the line represented by $t\mathbf a$ for which the remainder $\mathbf v-t\mathbf a$ is perpendicular to the line. Set its dot product with $\mathbf a$ equal to zero:

$$
(\mathbf v-t\mathbf a)\cdot\mathbf a=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t=\frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a}{\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf a}.
$$

Thus the vector projection is

$$
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf a}\mathbf v
=\frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a}{\|\mathbf a\|^2}\mathbf a.
$$

The signed scalar component along the unit vector $\widehat{\mathbf a}$ is instead $\mathbf v\cdot\widehat{\mathbf a}$. Distinguish this number from the vector projection. A negative scalar component means that the projection points opposite to $\mathbf a$.

## Worked example: projection and angle

Take $\mathbf v=(3,4)$ and $\mathbf a=(1,1)$. Their dot product is $3+4=7$, with norms $5$ and $\sqrt2$. Therefore $\cos\theta=7/(5\sqrt2)$ and $\theta\approx8.13^\circ$. For the projection, $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf a=2$, so

$$
\mathbf p=\frac72(1,1)=\left(\frac72,\frac72\right),
\qquad
\mathbf r=\mathbf v-\mathbf p=\left(-\frac12,\frac12\right).
$$

Check perpendicularity: $\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf a=-1/2+1/2=0$. The squared lengths also satisfy $\|\mathbf p\|^2+\|\mathbf r\|^2=49/2+1/2=25=\|\mathbf v\|^2$.

Do not use $\|\mathbf a\|$ in the projection denominator when multiplying by $\mathbf a$ itself. That denominator must be squared. Also, zero dot product means perpendicularity, not that either vector must be zero.

## Practice

1. Find the angle between $(1,0,1)$ and $(0,1,1)$.
2. Project $(2,-1)$ onto the line spanned by $(2,1)$, and verify that the remainder is orthogonal to the line.

## Worked answers

1. The dot product is $1$, and both norms are $\sqrt2$. Thus $\cos\theta=1/2$ and $\theta=\pi/3=60^\circ$.
2. The numerator is $2(2)+(-1)(1)=3$, and the denominator is $2^2+1^2=5$. Hence $\mathbf p=(3/5)(2,1)=(6/5,3/5)$. Subtracting gives $\mathbf r=(4/5,-8/5)$; its dot product with $(2,1)$ is $8/5-8/5=0$.
