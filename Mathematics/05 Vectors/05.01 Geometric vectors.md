---
title: "Geometric vectors"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A geometric vector gives a directed displacement; its value does not depend on where an arrow starts.

## Points and directed displacements

A **point** specifies a position in a Euclidean plane or space. A **vector** specifies a displacement from one point to another. If the displacement starts at a point $A$ and ends at a point $B$, write it as $\overrightarrow{AB}$. The order matters: $A$ is the tail, and $B$ is the head. A drawn arrow represents the vector, but the vector is not tied to that particular arrow.

Two arrows represent the same vector if they have equal lengths and the same direction. You can move one arrow parallel to itself until its tail is at the tail of the other. The heads must then agree. Such vectors are called **free vectors**. Rotating an arrow normally changes its vector; moving it without rotation does not.

Choose an origin $O$ and perpendicular coordinate axes with the same scale. If $A=(a_1,a_2)$ and $B=(b_1,b_2)$, the displacement has components

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=(b_1-a_1,\,b_2-a_2).
$$

Each component is an end coordinate minus a start coordinate. This subtraction removes the absolute positions and leaves their relative displacement. In three dimensions, add the third component $b_3-a_3$.

## Equality and the zero vector

In one fixed coordinate system, vectors are equal precisely when their corresponding components are equal:

$$
(u_1,u_2)=(v_1,v_2)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
u_1=v_1\text{ and }u_2=v_2.
$$

The **zero vector**, written $\mathbf 0$, has all components zero. It gives no displacement. Thus $\overrightarrow{AA}=\mathbf 0$ for every point $A$. Its length is zero, and it has no defined direction. It is incorrect to describe it as a very short arrow with an arbitrary direction.

The displacement in the reverse direction is the negative vector:

$$
\overrightarrow{BA}=-\overrightarrow{AB}.
$$

The two vectors have equal lengths. Except when both are zero, they point in opposite directions.

## Worked example: compare three arrows

Let $A=(1,-2)$, $B=(4,2)$, $C=(-3,5)$, and $D=(0,9)$. First calculate

$$
\overrightarrow{AB}=(4-1,\,2-(-2))=(3,4).
$$

The second coordinate increases by four, because subtracting $-2$ adds two. For the other arrow,

$$
\overrightarrow{CD}=(0-(-3),\,9-5)=(3,4).
$$

Therefore $\overrightarrow{AB}=\overrightarrow{CD}$ even though none of their endpoints agree. In contrast, $\overrightarrow{BA}=(-3,-4)$ is the opposite vector. The common length of these arrows is $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$.

## Position vectors depend on an origin

Once $O$ is chosen, the **position vector** of a point $P$ is $\mathbf p=\overrightarrow{OP}$. This associates a vector with a point, but does not make the two concepts identical. Moving the origin changes the position vector of the same point.

For example, a point with coordinates $(4,2)$ relative to $O=(0,0)$ has position vector $(4,2)$. Relative to a new origin $O'=(1,-2)$, its position vector has components $(3,4)$ in axes with the same directions. The point stays fixed. Its displacement from any other fixed point also stays fixed.

Adding a vector $\mathbf v$ to a point $P$ means finding the endpoint $Q$ of an arrow with tail $P$ and vector $\mathbf v$. Write $Q=P+\mathbf v$. Subtracting two points gives a vector. Adding two points has no origin-independent meaning in this elementary setting. [[05.02 Vector operations]] defines the separate operation of adding two vectors.

## Practice

1. Let $P=(-2,3)$ and $Q=(5,-1)$. Find $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ and the endpoint $R$ of an equal vector starting at $S=(4,4)$.
2. Two points have position vectors $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$. Explain why their displacement is unchanged when the origin is translated by a vector $\mathbf c$.

## Worked answers

1. Subtract the start coordinates: $\overrightarrow{PQ}=(5-(-2),-1-3)=(7,-4)$. Add this displacement to $S$: $R=(4+7,4-4)=(11,0)$. Checking gives $\overrightarrow{SR}=(11-4,0-4)=(7,-4)$.
2. Relative to the translated origin, the position vectors are $\mathbf a-\mathbf c$ and $\mathbf b-\mathbf c$. Their difference is $(\mathbf b-\mathbf c)-(\mathbf a-\mathbf c)=\mathbf b-\mathbf a$. The origin terms cancel, as required for a displacement.
