title: "Angular measure"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Radians measure a directed angle by arc length divided by radius; one full turn is 2π radians or 360 degrees.

## Angles and their orientation

An angle describes a turn from an initial ray to a final ray with the same endpoint. In an oriented Cartesian plane, take a counterclockwise turn as positive and a clockwise turn as negative. For a standard-position angle, the endpoint is the origin and the initial ray is the positive $x$-axis.

The terminal ray alone does not record how many turns occurred. For example, $30^\circ$, $390^\circ$, and $-330^\circ$ reach the same ray. These angles are coterminal, but their directed turns are different. Select a representative interval only when the question requires one. Common choices are $[0,2\pi)$ and $(-\pi,\pi]$ in radians.

A degree is $1/360$ of a full turn. Thus a straight angle is $180^\circ$ and a right angle is $90^\circ$. Degree measure is convenient for many geometric diagrams, but it inserts conversion factors in formulas involving circular lengths.

## Definition of radian measure

Take a circle of positive radius $r$ centered at the angle's endpoint. Let $s$ be the length of the circular arc traced by a nonnegative turn. Define its radian measure by

$$
\theta=\frac{s}{r}.
$$

If the radius changes by a factor $k>0$, similarity multiplies the corresponding arc by the same factor. The ratio $s/r$ is unchanged. Radian measure therefore describes the angle and does not depend on the chosen circle.

The circumference is $2\pi r$, so one full turn has measure $(2\pi r)/r=2\pi$. This gives the conversions

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{rad}}=\frac{\pi}{180}\theta_{\mathrm{deg}},
\qquad
\theta_{\mathrm{deg}}=\frac{180}{\pi}\theta_{\mathrm{rad}}.
$$

The subscripts identify the numerical value in each convention. An angle of one radian is about $57.30^\circ$. When no degree symbol is present, trigonometric formulas in mathematics normally use radians.

## Arc length and chord length

Rearranging the definition gives $s=r\theta$ for a nonnegative angle measured in radians. For a directed angle, the signed arc parameter is $r\theta$, while the length traced without reversing direction is $r|\theta|$. If the angle records several turns, include all their arc lengths. Reducing the angle modulo $2\pi$ would lose this information.

An arc follows the circle. A chord is the straight segment joining its endpoints. For a central angle $\alpha$ with $0\leq\alpha\leq\pi$, divide the isosceles triangle into two right triangles to obtain

$$
\text{chord length}=2r\sin(\alpha/2).
$$

The arc length is $r\alpha$ and is greater than the chord length for $0<\alpha\leq\pi$. Thus $r\alpha$ must not be used for the straight distance between two points on a circle. See [[04.03 Triangles]] for the right-triangle relations.

## Worked example

A circle has radius $r=6$, and the turn is $150^\circ$. First convert the angle:

$$
150^\circ=150\frac{\pi}{180}=\frac{5\pi}{6}\text{ radians}.
$$

The corresponding arc length is $s=6(5\pi/6)=5\pi\approx15.708$. The chord length is

$$
2(6)\sin75^\circ
=12\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt2}{4}
=3(\sqrt6+\sqrt2)\approx11.591.
$$

Both expressions describe the same endpoints but different paths. If the turn were clockwise through $150^\circ$, its signed angle would be $-5\pi/6$ and its signed arc parameter would be $-5\pi$. The length of that arc would still be $5\pi$.

## Choosing and checking a measure

Before calculating, state whether the angle is directed and whether a principal representative is needed. Keep exact multiples of $\pi$ until a numerical answer is required. In particular, $s=r\theta$ expects radians. Substituting the number $150$ for $150^\circ$ would give an incorrect arc length of $900$.

## Practice

1. Convert $-225^\circ$ to radians and find its representative in $[0,2\pi)$.
2. An arc has length $7\pi/3$ on a circle of radius $4$. Find its nonnegative angle in radians and degrees. How long is the arc for the same angle on radius $10$?

## Worked answers

1. Conversion gives $-225\pi/180=-5\pi/4$. Add one full turn: $-5\pi/4+2\pi=3\pi/4$. This lies in $[0,2\pi)$ and has the same terminal ray, although the directed turns differ.
2. The angle is $s/r=(7\pi/3)/4=7\pi/12$. Its degree value is $(7\pi/12)(180/\pi)=105$. On the larger circle, $s=10(7\pi/12)=35\pi/6$. The radius changed by $10/4$, and so did the arc length.
