> [!TLDR]
> Interpret definite integrals as accumulated quantities and connect integration to differentiation.

## Scope

Integral calculus starts with sums over a partition and develops methods for evaluating their limits. The central connection is the fundamental theorem of calculus, which links accumulation to antiderivatives.

## Starting knowledge

Use continuity, derivatives, and the chain and product rules from [[Differential calculus - Overview]].

## Topics to cover

- Definite integrals: partitions, Riemann sums, signed area, and dependence on the integration bounds.
- Antiderivatives: indefinite integrals, constants of integration, and families of functions with the same derivative.
- The fundamental theorem: differentiate an accumulation function and evaluate an integral with an antiderivative.
- Substitution: change the integration variable, transform the differential, and update definite bounds.
- Integration by parts: derive the rule from the product rule and keep the boundary contribution.
- Elementary methods: polynomial, exponential, trigonometric, and selected rational integrals.
- Improper integrals: limits at infinite bounds or singular integrands and the question of convergence.
- Checks and approximations: use signs, symmetry, differentiation, and elementary numerical sums to check an integral.

## Learning outcomes

After studying this topic, you should be able to:

- Construct a Riemann sum and explain what its limit represents.
- Evaluate definite integrals by substitution and integration by parts, including their boundary terms.
- Decide whether a simple improper integral converges and verify an antiderivative by differentiation.
