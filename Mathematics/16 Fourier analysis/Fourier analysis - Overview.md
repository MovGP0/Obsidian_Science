> [!TLDR]
> Expand periodic functions in orthogonal modes and use projection integrals to recover their coefficients.

## Scope

Fourier analysis expresses functions in a basis of oscillatory functions. Its main tools are periodicity, orthogonality, normalization, and the distinction between a finite approximation and an infinite expansion.

## Starting knowledge

Use complex exponentials from [[Complex numbers - Overview]], integrals from [[Integral calculus - Overview]], convergence from [[Sequences and series - Overview]], and separated modes from [[Partial differential equations - Overview]].

## Topics to cover

- [[16.01 Periodic functions|Periodic functions]]: period, rescaling of the independent variable, and the interval used for one complete period.
- [[16.02 Trigonometric and complex bases|Trigonometric and complex bases]]: translate between sine–cosine sums and integer complex-exponential modes.
- [[16.03 Function inner products|Function inner products]]: orthogonality, norms, and normalization factors on a finite interval.
- [[16.04 Fourier coefficients|Fourier coefficients]]: derive projection formulas and calculate coefficients for elementary examples.
- [[16.05 Reality and symmetry|Reality and symmetry]]: relate positive and negative indices for real functions and use even or odd symmetry.
- [[16.06 Convergence|Convergence]]: distinguish pointwise and mean-square statements and describe behaviour at a jump discontinuity.
- [[16.07 Energy of an expansion|Energy of an expansion]]: Parseval’s identity as an equality of a squared function norm and a coefficient sum.
- [[16.08 Operations on expansions|Operations on expansions]]: state conditions for differentiating or integrating a series and understand how derivatives act on modes.
- [[16.09 Boundary-value applications|Boundary-value applications]]: determine expansion coefficients from initial data and explain why periodicity gives integer labels.
- [[16.10 The nonperiodic extension|The nonperiodic extension]]: introduce the Fourier-transform idea and distinguish continuous wave numbers from discrete series indices.

## Learning outcomes

After studying this topic, you should be able to:

- Derive the orthogonality integral for integer exponential modes and use it to recover a coefficient.
- Expand an elementary periodic function with a consistent normalization and check a finite partial sum.
- Explain which part of a mode spectrum follows from periodicity and which part requires a differential equation.
