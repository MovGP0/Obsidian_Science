---
title: "Checks for proposed PDE solutions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Verify the equation in the interior, each prescribed datum on its own domain, and the regularity needed to make all these statements meaningful.

## What a full check contains

A proposed formula solves a PDE problem only when it satisfies every part of that problem. Write down the domain, equation, boundary values, initial values, and required regularity before differentiating. A verification can establish that the formula is a solution. It establishes that the formula is the only solution only when a uniqueness argument is also available; see [[15.08 Well-posedness]].

For a linear equation $L[u]=f$, define the residual

$$
R(x,t)=L[u](x,t)-f(x,t).
$$

The candidate satisfies the equation when $R=0$ throughout the interior. A few numerical samples cannot establish that identity. Simplify it symbolically or prove it by an argument valid at every point. For nonlinear equations, define the residual by subtracting the two sides in the same way.

Check each edge condition by substituting its edge coordinate. Check initial data by setting $t=0$, or by taking the limit in the norm required by the problem. If the problem specifies $u_t(x,0)$, compute that derivative separately. Checking $u(x,0)$ does not check its derivative.

## Worked complete verification

Solve the stated verification problem on $0<x<1$, $t>0$:

$$
u_t-u_{xx}=4,\qquad
u(0,t)=u(1,t)=2t,\qquad
u(x,0)=x(1-x).
$$

Test $u(x,t)=x(1-x)+2t$. It is a polynomial, so every required derivative exists and is continuous even at the corners. First differentiate:

$$
u_t=2,\qquad u_x=1-2x,\qquad u_{xx}=-2.
$$

The residual is $2-(-2)-4=0$. For the left edge, $u(0,t)=0+2t=2t$. For the right edge, $u(1,t)=1(1-1)+2t=2t$. For the initial edge, $u(x,0)=x(1-x)$. At the lower corners, the initial value is zero and the boundary data also tend to zero.

There is also derivative compatibility. Each boundary derivative in $t$ is $2$. The PDE at $t=0$ requires $u_t=f''+4=-2+4=2$. Thus this check is consistent with the candidate's smoothness up to the corners. All prescribed conditions hold.

If the candidate had instead been $v=x(1-x)+t$, its initial profile would still be correct, but its residual would be $1-(-2)-4=-1$. Its boundary values would also be $t$ instead of $2t$. A correct initial profile alone says little about later values.

## A candidate can satisfy the PDE and fail the data

For the zero-endpoint heat problem $u_t=u_{xx}$ on $(0,\pi)$ with $u(x,0)=\sin(2x)$, test

$$
v(x,t)=e^{-4t}\sin(2x)+1.
$$

The added constant has both $t$ derivative and second $x$ derivative equal to zero. Thus $v_t-v_{xx}=0$. But $v(0,t)=v(\pi,t)=1$, and $v(x,0)=\sin(2x)+1$. It fails both kinds of data. Removing the constant gives the required candidate $u=e^{-4t}\sin(2x)$.

## Series and regularity checks

For a finite sum, differentiation term by term is automatic. For an infinite series, first justify any exchange of a derivative with the sum. Uniform convergence of the relevant derivative series on compact interior sets is one sufficient route. A formula can solve the PDE for every $t>0$ while reaching its initial profile only in $L^2$, not pointwise everywhere.

If a proposed expression contains a denominator, logarithm, or square root, check its allowed domain. For example, $x/(1+t)$ is smooth for $t>0$ but not across $t=-1$. If a calculation divides by a factor that can vanish, return to the undivided equation at its zeros. Boundary compatibility must match the claimed regularity, as explained in [[15.03 Initial and boundary data]].

Finally, distinguish an exact identity from an approximation. A truncated series may solve the homogeneous PDE and endpoints exactly but match the initial data only approximately. State the remaining error as $f-S_N$ in the relevant norm rather than declaring all conditions exact.

## Practice

1. Verify $u=\cos(2t)\sin x$ for $u_{tt}=4u_{xx}$ on $(0,\pi)$ with zero endpoints, $u(x,0)=\sin x$, and $u_t(x,0)=0$.
2. A proposed solution of $u_t=2u_{xx}$ is $v=e^{-t}\sin x$. It has zero endpoints and initial profile $\sin x$. Compute its residual and correct its exponential factor.

## Worked answers

1. The derivatives are $u_{tt}=-4\cos(2t)\sin x$ and $u_{xx}=-\cos(2t)\sin x$, so $u_{tt}-4u_{xx}=0$. Both endpoint sines vanish. At $t=0$, the cosine equals one. Also $u_t=-2\sin(2t)\sin x$, which is zero at $t=0$. Every prescribed datum holds.
2. Here $v_t=-e^{-t}\sin x$ and $v_{xx}=-e^{-t}\sin x$. Thus $v_t-2v_{xx}=e^{-t}\sin x$, which is not identically zero. Use $u=e^{-2t}\sin x$ instead: now $u_t=-2e^{-2t}\sin x=2u_{xx}$, with the same endpoints and initial value.
