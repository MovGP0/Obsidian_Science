---
title: "Eigenvalue problems on an interval"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The interval and its endpoint conditions determine which nonzero functions solve a differential eigenvalue equation.

## The operator and its domain

An eigenvalue problem asks for a number $\lambda$ and a nonzero function $X$ such that an operator maps $X$ to $\lambda X$. For the negative second derivative on an interval, write

$$
-X''=\lambda X,\qquad 0<x<L.
$$

The constant $L>0$ fixes the interval length. The function $X=0$ satisfies the equation for every $\lambda$, so it is excluded. A nonzero scalar multiple of an eigenfunction has the same eigenvalue. Endpoint conditions are part of the operator's domain and therefore part of the eigenvalue problem.

For real-valued $X$ with either zero values or zero derivatives at both endpoints, integration by parts gives

$$
\lambda\int_0^L X^2\,dx
=\int_0^L(-X'')X\,dx
=\int_0^L(X')^2\,dx-[X'X]_0^L
=\int_0^L(X')^2\,dx.
$$

The boundary term vanishes under either of those conditions. Since the denominator is positive for a nonzero continuous $X$, $\lambda\geq0$. This argument excludes negative eigenvalues without solving hyperbolic-function equations.

## Dirichlet endpoint conditions

Let $X(0)=X(L)=0$. For $\lambda=0$, $X=A+Bx$; the endpoints force $A=B=0$. Thus zero is not an eigenvalue. For $\lambda=k^2>0$, solve

$$
X=A\cos(kx)+B\sin(kx).
$$

The left endpoint gives $A=0$. The right gives $B\sin(kL)=0$. Since $B\ne0$, the permitted numbers are

$$
\lambda_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2}{L^2},\qquad
X_n(x)=\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right),\qquad n=1,2,\ldots.
$$

The eigenvalues form a discrete sequence. Increasing the interval length reduces each eigenvalue by the factor $L^{-2}$. The integer $n$ counts the number of half-periods across the interval.

## Neumann and periodic conditions

For $X'(0)=X'(L)=0$, the constant function is allowed, so $\lambda_0=0$ has eigenfunction $X_0=1$. For $k>0$, the condition at zero gives $B=0$ in the same sine-cosine expression. The condition at $L$ gives $\sin(kL)=0$. Therefore

$$
X_n(x)=\cos(n\pi x/L),\qquad
\lambda_n=(n\pi/L)^2,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.
$$

For periodic conditions $X(0)=X(L)$ and $X'(0)=X'(L)$, a nonconstant sinusoid must complete an integer number of full periods. The eigenvalues are $(2\pi n/L)^2$, $n\geq1$, and each has two independent real eigenfunctions, cosine and sine. The constant is again the zero mode. Endpoint conditions thus change both the allowed values and the dimension of an eigenspace.

## Worked mixed-endpoint problem

Now prescribe $X(0)=0$ and $X'(L)=0$. The zero eigenvalue again gives only the zero function. The integration identity still excludes negative values because $X'X$ vanishes at both endpoints. For positive $\lambda=k^2$, the first condition gives $X=B\sin(kx)$. Then

$$
X'(L)=Bk\cos(kL)=0.
$$

A nonzero solution requires $kL=(n+\tfrac12)\pi$ for $n=0,1,2,\ldots$. Thus

$$
\lambda_n=\left(\frac{(n+1/2)\pi}{L}\right)^2.
$$

These functions satisfy a value condition at one end and a derivative condition at the other. Using the Dirichlet list here would miss the correct half-integer spacing.

## Practice

1. Find the first two Dirichlet eigenvalues on $(0,2)$ and give corresponding eigenfunctions.
2. Under Neumann conditions on $(0,1)$, show that $X=7$ is an eigenfunction. Explain why the same function fails under Dirichlet conditions.

## Worked answers

1. Set $L=2$ and $n=1,2$. The eigenvalues are $\pi^2/4$ and $\pi^2$. Corresponding eigenfunctions are $\sin(\pi x/2)$ and $\sin(\pi x)$. Both vanish at $x=0,2$, and their negative second derivatives give the stated multiples.
2. Since $X''=0$, $-X''=0\cdot X$, so the eigenvalue is zero. The derivative $X'=0$ satisfies both Neumann conditions. But $X(0)=X(1)=7$, which violates zero Dirichlet values. The differential expression alone cannot decide whether a function is an allowed eigenfunction.
