> [!TLDR]
> Solve elementary partial differential equations by separation of variables and explain how domain and boundary conditions select solutions.

## Scope

A partial differential equation relates several partial derivatives of an unknown function. The equation alone usually leaves many solutions; the domain and supplied initial and boundary data are part of the mathematical problem.

## Starting knowledge

Use partial derivatives and multiple integrals from [[Multivariable calculus - Overview]], inner products from [[Bilinear and quadratic forms - Overview]], and linear equation methods from [[Ordinary differential equations - Overview]].

## Topics to cover

- [[15.01 Basic language|Basic language]]: independent variables, order, linearity, and homogeneous versus inhomogeneous equations.
- [[15.02 Model equations|Model equations]]: the Laplace, heat, and wave equations and the different data associated with them.
- [[15.03 Initial and boundary data|Initial and boundary data]]: Dirichlet, Neumann, and periodic conditions, including compatibility at intersections of boundaries.
- [[15.04 Separation of variables|Separation of variables]]: use product solutions to reduce a PDE to ordinary differential equations and separation constants.
- [[15.05 Eigenvalue problems|Eigenvalue problems]]: show how an interval and its endpoint conditions select permitted eigenfunctions and eigenvalues.
- [[15.06 Superposition|Superposition]]: combine solutions of a linear homogeneous equation and identify where this principle fails for nonlinear equations.
- [[15.07 Orthogonality and coefficients|Orthogonality and coefficients]]: use an inner product to separate modes and prepare for Fourier expansions.
- [[15.08 Well-posedness|Well-posedness]]: distinguish existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence on the supplied data.
- [[15.09 Checks|Checks]]: verify a proposed solution in the differential equation and in every initial and boundary condition.

## Learning outcomes

After studying this topic, you should be able to:

- Separate a simple wave or Laplace problem and obtain its ordinary differential equations.
- Derive permitted sinusoidal modes from fixed or periodic boundary conditions.
- Explain why a proposed solution that satisfies the PDE can still fail to solve the complete boundary-value problem.
