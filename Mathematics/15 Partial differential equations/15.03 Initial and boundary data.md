---
title: "Initial and boundary data for partial differential equations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Specify values or normal derivatives on the boundary, and check that all prescribed data agree where their domains meet.

## A domain is part of the problem

Consider $u_t=\kappa u_{xx}$ on $0<x<L$, $t>0$, with constants $L>0$ and $\kappa>0$. Initial data prescribe $u(x,0)=f(x)$. Boundary data constrain $u$ for each $t>0$ at $x=0$ and $x=L$. An initial profile does not supply those endpoint conditions for later values of $t$.

Dirichlet conditions prescribe the function itself:

$$
u(0,t)=a(t),\qquad u(L,t)=b(t).
$$

Neumann conditions prescribe the outward normal derivative $\partial_nu=\nabla u\cdot n$, where $n$ is the outward unit normal. In one dimension, the outward normal is $-1$ at the left endpoint and $+1$ at the right endpoint. Thus

$$
\partial_nu(0,t)=-u_x(0,t),\qquad
\partial_nu(L,t)=u_x(L,t).
$$

If a problem states $u_x$ directly, use that derivative without adding an outward-normal sign. The two conventions must remain distinct.

Periodic conditions identify the two endpoints. For a second-order spatial equation, use

$$
u(0,t)=u(L,t),\qquad u_x(0,t)=u_x(L,t).
$$

Equality of values alone does not guarantee a smooth periodic function. Higher spatial regularity can require agreement of further derivatives.

## Compatibility at corners

For a solution continuous on the closed strip, Dirichlet data must satisfy

$$
f(0)=a(0),\qquad f(L)=b(0).
$$

Otherwise the same corner would have two different limits. These are the first compatibility conditions. If the solution has continuous $u_t$ and $u_{xx}$ up to the corner, the PDE also requires

$$
a'(0)=\kappa f''(0),\qquad
b'(0)=\kappa f''(L).
$$

These stronger conditions depend on the claimed regularity. A solution can be smooth for $t>0$ without having all these derivatives continuous at $t=0$. Do not reject such a solution merely because it lacks a stronger corner property that was never required.

For Neumann data $q_0(t),q_L(t)$ defined as outward derivatives, differentiable initial data must satisfy $-f'(0)=q_0(0)$ and $f'(L)=q_L(0)$. For a smooth wave solution with Dirichlet data, both initial profiles must match: $f(0)=a(0)$ and $g(0)=a'(0)$, with the corresponding relations at $L$.

## Worked compatibility check

Take $L=1$, zero Dirichlet data, and $f(x)=x(1-x)$. The value conditions hold because $f(0)=f(1)=0$. But $f''=-2$, whereas $a'=b'=0$. Thus a solution cannot have $u_t$ and $u_{xx}$ continuous at both lower corners while satisfying this PDE there.

Replace the initial profile by $f(x)=\sin(\pi x)$. Now $f''=-\pi^2\sin(\pi x)$ vanishes at both endpoints. The explicit solution

$$
u(x,t)=e^{-\kappa\pi^2t}\sin(\pi x)
$$

has both the correct values and the correct derivative behavior at the corners. This comparison shows why checking only endpoint values can miss a regularity issue.

## A Neumann integral condition

For the one-dimensional Poisson equation $u''=h$ on $(0,L)$, integrate once:

$$
\int_0^L h(x)\,dx=u'(L)-u'(0)=q_L+q_0.
$$

The prescribed source and outward derivatives must obey this identity. For $h=2$ and $L=1$, choose $q_0=0$, $q_L=2$. Then $u=x^2+C$ solves the problem for every constant $C$. Derivative data cannot distinguish these constants. A normalization such as $\int_0^1u\,dx=0$ selects $C=-1/3$.

## Practice

1. Are $f(x)=x$ and periodic conditions on $[0,1]$ compatible with a continuous periodic initial profile? Is $f(x)=\cos(2\pi x)$ compatible through its first derivative?
2. Can $u''=3$ on $(0,2)$ have zero outward derivative at both endpoints? Explain by integration.

## Worked answers

1. For $f=x$, the values $f(0)=0$ and $f(1)=1$ differ, so it is incompatible. For the cosine, both endpoint values equal $1$. Its derivative is $-2\pi\sin(2\pi x)$, which is zero at both endpoints. Thus the cosine meets both periodic conditions.
2. The integral of the source is $\int_0^2 3\,dx=6$. Zero outward derivatives would give $q_0+q_L=0$. Since $6\ne0$, no twice differentiable solution can satisfy all the data.
