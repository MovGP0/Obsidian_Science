title: "Separation of variables"

> [!TLDR]
> A product trial separates a PDE into ordinary differential equations because functions of independent variables can agree everywhere only through a constant.

## The product trial

For the heat equation $u_t=\kappa u_{xx}$ on $0<x<L$, $t>0$, assume a nonzero solution of the form

$$
u(x,t)=X(x)T(t),\qquad\kappa>0.
$$

The functions $X$ and $T$ are unknown. Differentiate and substitute:

$$
XT'=\kappa X''T.
$$

Where $XT\ne0$, division gives

$$
\frac{T'}{\kappa T}=\frac{X''}{X}.
$$

The left side depends only on $t$, and the right side only on $x$. On a product domain, fix one variable and vary the other. Their common value must be a constant. Call it $-\lambda$, with $\lambda\in\mathbb R$. This sign convention produces

$$
X''+\lambda X=0,\qquad T'+\kappa\lambda T=0.
$$

The divided equation need not hold at zeros of $X$. The resulting undivided ordinary differential equations extend the construction across those zeros. Direct substitution then verifies the product globally. Division at an isolated zero is not a valid step by itself.

## Boundary conditions select the constant

Impose $u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$. For a nontrivial factor $T$, this requires $X(0)=X(L)=0$. Examine all signs of $\lambda$.

If $\lambda=0$, then $X=A+Bx$. The two endpoint conditions imply $A=B=0$, so no nonzero product results. If $\lambda=-\mu^2<0$, the solutions are $X=A\cosh(\mu x)+B\sinh(\mu x)$. The first endpoint gives $A=0$ and the second gives $B\sinh(\mu L)=0$. Since $\mu L>0$, again only the zero function remains.

If $\lambda=k^2>0$, write $X=A\cos(kx)+B\sin(kx)$. Now $A=0$ and $B\sin(kL)=0$. A nonzero $B$ requires $kL=n\pi$ for a positive integer $n$. Hence

$$
\lambda_n=\left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2,
\qquad X_n(x)=\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right).
$$

Solving the equation for $T$ gives $T_n=C_ne^{-\kappa\lambda_nt}$. The endpoint conditions permit only selected separation constants. This is the eigenvalue problem developed in [[15.05 Eigenvalue problems]].

## Worked initial-value problem

Take $L=1$, $\kappa=2$, and

$$
u(x,0)=3\sin(\pi x)-2\sin(3\pi x).
$$

The initial profile is not one product mode. It is the sum of modes $n=1$ and $n=3$. Because the PDE and zero boundary data are linear and homogeneous, combine their separate solutions:

$$
u(x,t)=3e^{-2\pi^2t}\sin(\pi x)
-2e^{-18\pi^2t}\sin(3\pi x).
$$

For the first term, differentiation in $t$ multiplies it by $-2\pi^2$, while $2\partial_{xx}$ does the same. For the second term, both operations multiply it by $-18\pi^2$. Each sine vanishes at $0$ and $1$, and setting $t=0$ restores the prescribed profile. All parts of the problem therefore hold.

## Other equations and limits of the method

For the wave equation $u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}$, separation with the same spatial convention gives $X''+\lambda X=0$ and $T''+c^2\lambda T=0$. For the Laplace equation $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$, a product $X(x)Y(y)$ gives $X''+\lambda X=0$ and $Y''-\lambda Y=0$. The sign in the second equation follows from substitution; do not copy the heat equation's formula.

One product rarely matches arbitrary initial data. A series of separated products is often required, with coefficients found by [[15.07 Orthogonality and coefficients]]. Variable coefficients, non-product domains, and nonlinear terms can prevent elementary separation. The product trial is a construction method, not a claim that every solution has one product factorization.

## Practice

1. On $(0,\pi)$, solve $u_t=u_{xx}$ with zero endpoint values and initial profile $5\sin(2x)$.
2. Separate $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ using $u=X(x)Y(y)$ and $X''=-4X$. Find the general equation and solution for $Y$.

## Worked answers

1. Here $X=\sin(2x)$ has $X''=-4X$. Therefore $T'=-4T$ and $T(0)=5$. Thus $u=5e^{-4t}\sin(2x)$, which has the required endpoints and initial value.
2. Substitution gives $X''Y+XY''=X(Y''-4Y)=0$. For nonzero $X$, this requires $Y''-4Y=0$. Its general solution is $Y=Ae^{2y}+Be^{-2y}$. Boundary data in $y$ would determine $A$ and $B$.
