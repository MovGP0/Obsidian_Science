> [!TLDR]
> Sine and cosine repeat values around the unit circle; scale and translation parameters determine amplitude, period, and phase shift.

## The unit-circle definitions

Use **radians** for the real input $x$. An angle of $x$ radians subtends an arc of signed length $x$ on the unit circle. Positive angles turn counterclockwise from $(1,0)$; negative angles turn clockwise. The point reached is

$$
(\cos x,\sin x).
$$

Thus cosine is the horizontal coordinate and sine is the vertical coordinate. Both functions have domain $\mathbb R$ and range $[-1,1]$. Since every point of the unit circle satisfies $u^2+v^2=1$, their coordinates give $\cos^2x+\sin^2x=1$.

A complete turn has angle $2\pi$. It returns to the same point, so $\sin(x+2\pi)=\sin x$ and $\cos(x+2\pi)=\cos x$. The smallest positive period of each is $2\pi$. Reflection of the circle in the horizontal axis gives $\cos(-x)=\cos x$ and $\sin(-x)=-\sin x$: cosine is even, and sine is odd.

Define tangent by $\tan x=\sin x/\cos x$. It is undefined where $\cos x=0$, so its domain excludes $\pi/2+k\pi$ for all integers $k$. Its range is $\mathbb R$. A half-turn changes the signs of both numerator and denominator, leaving their quotient unchanged. Thus tangent has period $\pi$. Unlike sine and cosine, tangent has no finite amplitude because it is unbounded.

## Amplitude, period, and shift

Consider the family

$$
f(x)=A\sin\bigl(B(x-h)\bigr)+D,
\qquad A\ne0,\quad B\ne0.
$$

The number $D$ is the vertical midline. The **amplitude** $|A|$ is the maximum distance of the graph from that midline. The range is $[D-|A|,D+|A|]$. The **period** is $T=2\pi/|B|$, because changing $x$ by $T$ changes the angle by $2\pi$ or $-2\pi$. The number $h$ is a horizontal or phase shift. For $B>0$, the reference angle zero occurs at $x=h$.

If the rule is written as $A\sin(Bx+C)+D$, factor its argument first: $Bx+C=B(x+C/B)$. Therefore $h=-C/B$, not $-C$. Phase shifts that differ by a full period represent the same graph. A negative $A$ reflects the sine curve across its midline. A negative $B$ reverses the input direction; for sine, oddness allows that sign to be absorbed into $A$.

## Worked example: construct one complete cycle

Let

$$
g(x)=2\sin\left(3\left(x-\frac\pi6\right)\right)-1.
$$

Its domain is $\mathbb R$, midline is $-1$, amplitude is $2$, period is $2\pi/3$, and range is $[-3,1]$. Start at $h=\pi/6$. Each quarter-period step has length $T/4=\pi/6$. Insert successive standard angles:

| Input $x$ | Angle $3(x-\pi/6)$ | Output $g(x)$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\pi/6$ | $0$ | $-1$ |
| $\pi/3$ | $\pi/2$ | $1$ |
| $\pi/2$ | $\pi$ | $-1$ |
| $2\pi/3$ | $3\pi/2$ | $-3$ |
| $5\pi/6$ | $2\pi$ | $-1$ |

![[math-03-sine-cycle.svg|**Fig. 03.3** A full cycle of the transformed sine function, with its midline and five quarter-period points.]]

The graph repeats this cycle in both directions. These exact points establish scale and placement; they are not the only points of the function. A smooth curve connects them according to the sine rule, rather than by straight segments. Degree-mode calculator input would give different values unless the angles were converted consistently.

## Practice

1. Find the domain, range, period, amplitude, and a phase shift for $p(x)=-3\cos(2x+\pi)+4$.
2. Solve $\sin x=1/2$ on $[0,2\pi)$ and then give all real solutions.

## Worked answers

1. Factor $2x+\pi=2(x+\pi/2)$. A phase shift is $h=-\pi/2$. The amplitude is $|-3|=3$, the midline is $4$, and the range is $[4-3,4+3]=[1,7]$. The period is $2\pi/2=\pi$, and the domain is $\mathbb R$. The negative coefficient reflects the cosine wave across $y=4$.
2. The unit-circle height is $1/2$ at $x=\pi/6$ and $x=5\pi/6$ in the stated interval. Both have positive sine; the other half of the circle has negative sine. Periodicity gives all solutions $x=\pi/6+2k\pi$ or $x=5\pi/6+2k\pi$, where $k\in\mathbb Z$.
