> [!TLDR]
> Represent bilinear and quadratic forms by matrices and distinguish positive, indefinite, and degenerate forms.

## Scope

A bilinear form combines two vectors into a scalar. A quadratic form evaluates such a pairing on the same vector twice. Their structure explains inner products, orthogonality, and the meaning of a matrix that represents a squared interval.

## Starting knowledge

Use bases, matrix products, transpose, determinants, and eigenvectors from [[Linear algebra - Overview]].

## Topics to cover

- [[11.01 Bilinear maps|Bilinear maps]]: linearity in each argument and construction from matrix coefficients.
- [[11.02 Quadratic forms|Quadratic forms]]: write a quadratic expression as a matrix product and recover its symmetric bilinear part.
- [[11.03 Symmetry|Symmetry]]: separate symmetric and antisymmetric parts and explain why an antisymmetric part drops out of a quadratic form.
- [[11.04 Inner products|Inner products]]: positivity, norms, angles, orthogonality, and orthogonal projections.
- [[11.05 Orthonormal bases|Orthonormal bases]]: construct them by Gram–Schmidt and state when this positive-definite procedure applies.
- [[11.06 Basis changes|Basis changes]]: derive the congruence transformation of a form and distinguish it from the similarity transformation of a linear map.
- [[11.07 Classification|Classification]]: diagonal forms, positive and negative directions, signature, degeneracy, and nondegeneracy.
- [[11.08 Indefinite forms|Indefinite forms]]: nonzero null vectors and the limits of interpreting every pairing as an ordinary Euclidean length.
- [[11.09 Complex inner products|Complex inner products]]: conjugate linearity, Hermitian symmetry, and the distinction between bilinear and sesquilinear forms.

## Learning outcomes

After studying this topic, you should be able to:

- Recover the symmetric matrix of a quadratic expression, including its cross-term factors.
- Transform the expression to another basis and verify that its value is unchanged.
- Classify a real symmetric form and explain why a nonzero null vector does not necessarily imply degeneracy.
