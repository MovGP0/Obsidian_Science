title: "Tensor components"

> [!TLDR]
> Basis evaluations extract tensor components, and multilinearity reconstructs every evaluation from those components.

## Components are evaluations

Fix a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of a real vector space $V$, and let $\varepsilon^1,\ldots,\varepsilon^n$ be its dual basis. The identity $\varepsilon^i(e_j)=\delta^i{}_j$ is the coefficient-extraction rule. For a vector $v=v^i e_i$, evaluation gives $v^i=\varepsilon^i(v)$. For a covector $\alpha=\alpha_j\varepsilon^j$, the component is $\alpha_j=\alpha(e_j)$.

A covariant two-tensor is a bilinear form $B:V\times V\to\mathbb R$. Define $B_{ij}=B(e_i,e_j)$. If $v=v^i e_i$ and $w=w^j e_j$, apply linearity in the first input and then in the second:

$$
B(v,w)=\sum_i v^i B(e_i,w)
=\sum_{i,j}v^i w^j B(e_i,e_j)
=B_{ij}v^i w^j.
$$

Repeated upper and lower indices in the last expression are summed. Thus $B=B_{ij}\varepsilon^i\otimes\varepsilon^j$. It is enough to know the values on basis pairs because all other values follow from multilinearity.

## General extraction and reconstruction

Use the convention that type $(r,s)$ means $r$ vector factors and $s$ covector factors. Its multilinear arguments are $r$ covectors followed by $s$ vectors. Its components are

$$
T^{i_1\cdots i_r}{}_{j_1\cdots j_s}
=T(\varepsilon^{i_1},\ldots,\varepsilon^{i_r},e_{j_1},\ldots,e_{j_s}).
$$

Insert these numbers into the expansion

$$
T=T^{i_1\cdots i_r}{}_{j_1\cdots j_s}
e_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_r}
\otimes\varepsilon^{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon^{j_s}.
$$

To check this equality, evaluate its right side on basis arguments. Every pairing becomes a Kronecker delta, so only the selected coefficient remains. Two multilinear maps that agree on all basis arguments agree everywhere after expanding their inputs. This proves reconstruction rather than merely defining a notation.

For type $(1,1)$, a linear map $A:V\to V$ gives $T_A(\alpha,v)=\alpha(Av)$. Therefore $A^i{}_j=\varepsilon^i(Ae_j)$. The $j$th column consists of the coordinates of the image of $e_j$. This column convention is useful when converting between maps and mixed tensors.

## Worked example: extract a bilinear form

In the standard basis of $\mathbb R^2$, suppose

$$
B((x,y),(u,z))=2xu-xz+3yu+4yz.
$$

Substitute the four basis pairs. They give $B_{11}=2$, $B_{12}=-1$, $B_{21}=3$, and $B_{22}=4$. Hence

$$
B=2\varepsilon^1\otimes\varepsilon^1
-\varepsilon^1\otimes\varepsilon^2
+3\varepsilon^2\otimes\varepsilon^1
+4\varepsilon^2\otimes\varepsilon^2.
$$

For $v=(1,2)$ and $w=(-1,3)$, reconstruction gives

$$
B(v,w)=2(1)(-1)-(1)(3)+3(2)(-1)+4(2)(3)
=-2-3-6+24=13.
$$

The equivalent matrix calculation is $v^{\mathsf T}Bw=(1,2)\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\3&4\end{pmatrix}(-1,3)^{\mathsf T}$. First $Bw=(-5,9)^{\mathsf T}$, then the scalar is $-5+18=13$. The nonsymmetric entries show that reversing the input order can change the value.

## What components do not tell you alone

A component list needs its basis, index order, and tensor type. The same four numbers can represent a bilinear form or an endomorphism in dimension two, but these objects transform differently. An array becomes a coordinate description of one tensor only when its descriptions in other bases satisfy the laws in [[18.05 Transformation laws]]. Do not confuse a coefficient such as $B_{12}$ with the value $B(v,w)$ on arbitrary vectors.

## Practice

1. If $Ae_1=2e_1-e_2$ and $Ae_2=3e_1+4e_2$, reconstruct $A$ as a mixed tensor and compute $A(e_1+2e_2)$.
2. For $T=e_1\otimes\varepsilon^2\otimes\varepsilon^1$, find its type and evaluate $T(2\varepsilon^1-\varepsilon^2,3e_1+4e_2,e_1-e_2)$.

> [!example]- Worked answers
> 1. The columns are $(2,-1)^{\mathsf T}$ and $(3,4)^{\mathsf T}$. Thus $A=2e_1\otimes\varepsilon^1-e_2\otimes\varepsilon^1+3e_1\otimes\varepsilon^2+4e_2\otimes\varepsilon^2$. By linearity, the image is $(2e_1-e_2)+2(3e_1+4e_2)=8e_1+7e_2$.
> 2. The type is $(1,2)$. Pair the first argument with $e_1$ to get $2$, the second with $\varepsilon^2$ to get $4$, and the third with $\varepsilon^1$ to get $1$. The product is $8$.
