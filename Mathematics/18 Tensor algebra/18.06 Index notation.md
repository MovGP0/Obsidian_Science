> [!TLDR]
> Free indices specify output components; repeated upper and lower indices specify summed contractions.

## Explicit sums and Einstein notation

Work in a finite-dimensional real vector space with a chosen basis. A vector has components $v^i$, a covector has components $\alpha_i$, and a linear map has components $A^i{}_j$. Each index ranges from $1$ to the dimension $n$. The Einstein convention suppresses a sum when an index occurs exactly twice in one term, once above and once below:

$$
\alpha_i v^i=\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_i v^i,
\qquad A^i{}_j v^j=\sum_{j=1}^n A^i{}_j v^j.
$$

The repeated index is **dummy**: it is local to the sum and has no output meaning. An index that occurs once is **free**. In $w^i=A^i{}_jv^j$, $i$ is free and $j$ is dummy. This is $n$ equations, one for each possible $i$.

Every term of a tensor equation must have the same free indices in the same upper or lower positions. Thus $A^i{}_jv^j+B^i{}_k u^k$ is a valid vector expression. The two dummy letters differ, but the free index $i$ matches. The expression $v^i+\alpha_i$ does not give a coordinate-independent tensor sum without an extra map between the two types.

## Safe renaming and legal contraction

A dummy label can be changed everywhere within its term: $A^i{}_jv^j=A^i{}_k v^k$. The new label must not already be free or used for another sum in that term. Changing this to $A^i{}_i v^i$ is invalid: the letter $i$ appears three times and its roles conflict.

Free indices can also be renamed, but rename them consistently throughout the full equation. For example, $w^a=A^a{}_j v^j$ is the same family of equations as the one with free index $i$.

A contraction pairs one vector slot with one covector slot. In components this is one upper-lower repetition. The trace $A^i{}_i$ is a scalar. A type $(2,1)$ tensor can contract to $C^i=T^{ij}{}_j$. Two lower indices cannot be contracted by repetition alone. If an inverse metric $g^{ij}$ is supplied, $g^{ij}B_{ij}$ is a valid scalar contraction with two separate sums. The metric performs the needed type conversion explicitly.

## Worked example: matrix multiplication

Let

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\-1&3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\4&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Composition $C=AB$ means $C^i{}_k=A^i{}_jB^j{}_k$. The free indices are $i,k$ and $j$ is summed. Evaluate the entries:

$$
C^1{}_1=1(2)+2(4)=10,\quad
C^1{}_2=1(0)+2(1)=2,
$$

$$
C^2{}_1=(-1)(2)+3(4)=10,\quad
C^2{}_2=(-1)(0)+3(1)=3.
$$

Thus $C=\begin{pmatrix}10&2\\10&3\end{pmatrix}$. Its trace is $C^i{}_i=13$. Written directly, this is $A^i{}_jB^j{}_i$, with both $i$ and $j$ dummy. Expand the double sum to get $1(2)+2(4)+(-1)(0)+3(1)=13$.

The notation also proves cyclic trace for two maps: rename dummy labels in $A^i{}_jB^j{}_i$ to obtain $A^j{}_iB^i{}_j$, then commute the scalar factors to obtain $B^i{}_jA^j{}_i$. This proves $\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)$ without claiming $AB=BA$.

## Common notation traps

Do not sum a free index merely because its numerical range is known. Do not use a repeated index three or four times in a term. Use distinct letters for independent sums. A numerical expression such as $\sum_i v^i w^i$ can be valid in a fixed orthonormal coordinate system, but the two upper indices do not define an intrinsic contraction on their own. [[18.07 Tensor operations]] gives the abstract operations behind these rules.

## Practice

1. Identify the free and dummy indices in $R^{ab}{}_d=S^{ab}{}_c A^c{}_d$. What is the type of the output?
2. Correct the dummy-label collision in the intended product $(A^i{}_jv^j)(\alpha_j u^j)$, then state the number of free indices. Explain why $A^i{}_j v^i$ fails as a contraction.

> [!example]- Worked answers
> 1. The indices $a,b,d$ are free. The index $c$ occurs once up and once down and is summed. The output has two upper and one lower free indices, so its type is $(2,1)$.
> 2. The letter $j$ occurs four times, so rename the second pair to $k$: $(A^i{}_jv^j)(\alpha_k u^k)$. The two independent sums now have distinct labels. The only free index is the upper index $i$, so the product is a vector. In $A^i{}_jv^i$, the repeated $i$ occurs twice above, so it does not pair a vector slot with a covector slot. The usual matrix-vector expression is $A^i{}_jv^j$.
