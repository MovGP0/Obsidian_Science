> [!TLDR]
> Tensor algebra combines like types, forms products, contracts opposite types, and separates symmetric and alternating parts.

## Addition and tensor products

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space. Use type $(r,s)$ for $r$ vector factors and $s$ covector factors. Tensors of the same type can be added and multiplied by scalars. In any fixed basis, their corresponding components add. Different types require an explicit identification before addition makes sense.

If $T$ has type $(r,s)$ and $U$ has type $(p,q)$, their tensor product has type $(r+p,s+q)$ after collecting vector factors before covector factors with the natural slot permutation. Its components are products. For example,

$$
(v\otimes\alpha)^i{}_j=v^i\alpha_j,
\qquad (B\otimes C)_{ijkl}=B_{ij}C_{kl}.
$$

The product keeps slots separate. It does not sum any indices until a contraction is applied. Changing factor order permutes the slots; it can change the tensor.

## Contraction

The natural pairing $V^*\times V\to\mathbb R$ removes one vector factor and one covector factor. For a simple mixed tensor $v\otimes\alpha$, contraction is $\alpha(v)$. Extend this rule linearly. Since evaluation is independent of basis, contraction is also independent of basis.

For a type $(2,1)$ tensor, contracting the second upper slot with the lower slot gives

$$
C^i=T^{ij}{}_j.
$$

The result has type $(1,0)$. A general contraction changes type $(r,s)$ to $(r-1,s-1)$. Contracting a type $(1,1)$ tensor yields its trace. This rule does not allow two lower slots to be paired without additional data such as an inverse metric.

## Slot permutation and projections

For a covariant two-tensor $B$, the transpose is $B^{\mathsf T}(v,w)=B(w,v)$, so $(B^{\mathsf T})_{ij}=B_{ji}$. Define

$$
S_{ij}=\frac12(B_{ij}+B_{ji}),
\qquad K_{ij}=\frac12(B_{ij}-B_{ji}).
$$

Then $S_{ji}=S_{ij}$, $K_{ji}=-K_{ij}$, and $B=S+K$. The factors $1/2$ ensure that symmetrizing an already symmetric tensor leaves it unchanged. They also make the operations projections: applying either operation twice gives the same result.

For $k$ covariant slots, write $S_k$ for all permutations of $1,\ldots,k$ and $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)$ for the sign of a permutation. Normalized symmetrization and antisymmetrization are

$$
T_{(i_1\cdots i_k)}=\frac1{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_k}T_{i_{\sigma(1)}\cdots i_{\sigma(k)}},
$$

$$
T_{[i_1\cdots i_k]}=\frac1{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_k}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)T_{i_{\sigma(1)}\cdots i_{\sigma(k)}}.
$$

These formulas permute slots of the same kind. Do not interchange an upper and lower index as if their input spaces were identical. Over $\mathbb R$ the factorials are invertible. Over a field of small positive characteristic these normalized formulas can fail.

## Worked example: split a bilinear form

Take $B=\begin{pmatrix}2&5\\1&4\end{pmatrix}$. Add and subtract its transpose:

$$
S=\begin{pmatrix}2&3\\3&4\end{pmatrix},
\qquad K=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\-2&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Their sum is the original matrix. For $v=(x,y)$, direct evaluation gives $K(v,v)=2xy-2yx=0$. Thus $B(v,v)=S(v,v)=2x^2+6xy+4y^2$. The diagonal quadratic function loses the alternating part. By contrast, $K(e_1,e_2)=2$ retains it.

Now take $v=(1,2)$ and $\alpha=(3,-1)$. The product $v\otimes\alpha$ has matrix $\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\6&-2\end{pmatrix}$. Contracting gives its trace $3-2=1$, which agrees with $\alpha(v)=3-2=1$. This shows how product and contraction differ in the same calculation.

## Practice

1. Find the symmetric and alternating parts of $C=\begin{pmatrix}0&-2\\6&1\end{pmatrix}$.
2. If $T^{ij}{}_k=v^i w^j\alpha_k$, with $v=(2,1)$, $w=(1,3)$, and $\alpha=(4,-1)$, compute $T^{ij}{}_j$.

> [!example]- Worked answers
> 1. Average $C$ with $C^{\mathsf T}$ to get $C_{(ij)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$. Half the difference is $C_{[ij]}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-4\\4&0\end{pmatrix}$. Adding these matrices recovers $C$.
> 2. The repeated pair gives $T^{ij}{}_j=v^i\sum_jw^j\alpha_j=v^i\alpha(w)$. Here $\alpha(w)=4(1)-1(3)=1$, so the result is $v=(2,1)$. Contracting the other upper slot instead would produce $w\alpha(v)$, a different vector in general.
