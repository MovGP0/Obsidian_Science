title: "Coordinate changes for metrics"

> [!TLDR]
> Metric components transform with two Jacobian factors, so the pairing of two fixed tangent vectors stays unchanged.

## Fix the Jacobian convention

Let $x=(x^1,\ldots,x^n)$ and $y=(y^1,\ldots,y^n)$ be two smooth coordinate systems on the same open set. Write the old coordinates as functions $x=x(y)$ and define

$$
J^i{}_a=\frac{\partial x^i}{\partial y^a}.
$$

The change is valid where $J$ is invertible. Latin indices $i,j$ label the old coordinates, and $a,b$ label the new coordinates. All formulas below are evaluated at the same manifold point. Component functions in the old chart must also be composed with $x(y)$.

The chain rule gives the new basis in terms of the old basis:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial y^a}=J^i{}_a\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}.
$$

A metric $g$ is a symmetric bilinear pairing on tangent vectors. Apply bilinearity to these new basis vectors:

$$
g^{(y)}_{ab}=g\left(\partial_{y^a},\partial_{y^b}\right)
=J^i{}_a J^j{}_b g^{(x)}_{ij}.
$$

In matrix notation this is

$$
G_y=J^T G_x J.
$$

This is a congruence transformation. A similarity transformation $J^{-1}G_xJ$ describes a linear operator under a basis change and is the wrong rule for a bilinear form.

## Why the scalar pairing is unchanged

Suppose $v$ is one fixed tangent vector. Express it in both bases:

$$
v=v_x^i\partial_{x^i}=v_y^a\partial_{y^a}
=v_y^a J^i{}_a\partial_{x^i}.
$$

Comparison of coefficients gives $v_x=Jv_y$, so $v_y=J^{-1}v_x$. The same relation holds for a second vector $w$. Substitution now gives

$$
v_y^T G_y w_y
=v_y^T J^T G_x J w_y
=(Jv_y)^T G_x(Jw_y)
=v_x^T G_x w_x.
$$

The components change together. Keeping the old vector components while changing only the metric matrix would usually change the result. That operation would describe different vectors.

## Worked example: a shear chart

On the Euclidean plane, the old coordinates $(x,y)$ have $G_x=I$. Introduce $(u,v)$ by

$$
x=u+v,\qquad y=v,\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The determinant is $1$, so this is a valid global change. Multiplying the matrices gives

$$
G_{(u,v)}=J^TJ=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad ds^2=du^2+2\,du\,dv+2\,dv^2.
$$

The cross term reflects the nonorthogonal coordinate basis. It does not indicate a change to the Euclidean metric itself.

Take vectors with new components $a=(1,2)$ and $b=(2,-1)$. Their old components are $Ja=(3,2)$ and $Jb=(1,-1)$. Their Euclidean pairing is $3\cdot1+2(-1)=1$. In the new coordinates, $G_{(u,v)}b=(1,0)$, and $a^TG_{(u,v)}b=1$ again. The self-pairing of $a$ is $13$ in both descriptions.

## Coordinate validity and pullbacks

The same two-Jacobian formula defines the pullback of a metric by a smooth map. If the map is not a local diffeomorphism, the pullback can be degenerate. For example, $x=u$, $y=0$ maps a plane into a line; its Jacobian has rank one, and the pulled-back Euclidean form cannot measure the $v$ direction. This is not a coordinate change on the plane.

The determinant law $\det G_y=(\det J)^2\det G_x$ shows that a valid coordinate change preserves nondegeneracy. It also preserves positive and negative direction counts, although individual entries and eigenvalues can change; see [[20.05 Signature]].

## Practice

1. In the Euclidean plane, set $x=2u$, $y=3v$. Find the new metric and the length of the vector with components $(1,1)$ in the new basis.
2. In one dimension, let $x=e^u$ on the positive half-line with $ds^2=dx^2$. Find the metric coefficient in $u$, and verify the pairing of two vectors with $u$ components $2$ and $-1$.

## Worked answers

1. Here $J=\operatorname{diag}(2,3)$, so $G_y=\operatorname{diag}(4,9)$. The self-pairing is $4+9=13$ and the length is $\sqrt{13}$. The old components are $(2,3)$, which give the same Euclidean length.
2. Since $dx=e^u du$, the coefficient is $g_{uu}=e^{2u}$. The new pairing is $e^{2u}(2)(-1)=-2e^{2u}$. The old components are $2e^u$ and $-e^u$, whose product is also $-2e^{2u}$.
