title: "Implicit and inverse differentiation"

> [!TLDR]
> The chain rule gives derivatives of implicit relations and local inverses when the required nonzero derivative conditions hold.

## Differentiate a relation

An implicit equation specifies a relation between variables without solving for one in terms of the other. To differentiate it with respect to $x$, first assume that a differentiable local branch $y=y(x)$ exists. Each occurrence of $y$ then depends on $x$. For example,

$$
\frac d{dx}y^2=2y\,y',\qquad
\frac d{dx}(xy)=y+xy'.
$$

The factor $y'=dy/dx$ comes from the chain rule. The product $xy$ requires the product rule too. These rules are developed in [[07.04 Differentiation rules]].

A sufficient existence condition comes from the implicit function theorem. Suppose $F(x,y)$ has continuous first partial derivatives near $(a,b)$, $F(a,b)=0$, and $F_y(a,b)\ne0$. Then $F(x,y)=0$ defines a unique differentiable local branch $y(x)$ near that point. Here $F_x$ means differentiation with respect to $x$ holding $y$ fixed, and $F_y$ means differentiation with respect to $y$ holding $x$ fixed. Along the branch,

$$
F_x+F_y y'=0,\qquad y'=-\frac{F_x}{F_y}.
$$

These are local statements. A curve can fail to be one graph over the whole $x$-axis while having valid local branches. The theorem is stated here as an existence result; the derivative formula follows from the chain rule once that branch exists.

## Worked example: a circular relation

For $x^2+y^2=25$, differentiate both sides:

$$
2x+2yy'=0.
$$

Where $y\ne0$, solve to obtain $y'=-x/y$. The point $(3,4)$ lies on the curve because $9+16=25$. There the slope is $-3/4$, so the tangent is

$$
y-4=-\frac34(x-3).
$$

There was no need to solve and differentiate $y=\sqrt{25-x^2}$, although this is the local branch through $(3,4)$.

For a second derivative, differentiate $2x+2yy'=0$ again. The product $yy'$ gives two terms:

$$
2+2(y')^2+2yy''=0.
$$

Substitute $y'=-x/y$ and the original relation:

$$
y''=-\frac{1+x^2/y^2}{y}
=-\frac{x^2+y^2}{y^3}=-\frac{25}{y^3}.
$$

At $(3,4)$ the result is $-25/64$. At $(5,0)$, the denominator of $y'=-x/y$ vanishes. The local graph condition fails for $y(x)$ there. The tangent is vertical, and we can instead use a local graph $x(y)$ because $F_x=2x\ne0$. An invalid denominator requires an examination of the curve, not an ordinary infinite derivative value.

## Derivative of an inverse

A global inverse requires a one-to-one function; a domain restriction can provide this property. Suppose $f$ is continuously differentiable near $a$ and $f'(a)\ne0$. The inverse function theorem gives a differentiable local inverse $g=f^{-1}$ near $b=f(a)$. Differentiating $f(g(y))=y$ gives

$$
f'(g(y))g'(y)=1,\qquad
g'(y)=\frac1{f'(g(y))}.
$$

At paired points, $g'(b)=1/f'(a)$. The denominator is evaluated at the original input $a$, not at $b$. Confusing these inputs is a common error.

For $f(x)=x^3+x$, the derivative is $3x^2+1>0$, so the function is strictly increasing. Its limits at the two ends of $\mathbb R$ are $-\infty$ and $+\infty$, so continuity gives an inverse on all real outputs. Since $f(1)=2$,

$$
(f^{-1})'(2)=\frac1{f'(1)}=\frac14.
$$

No explicit cubic inverse formula is needed. A zero original derivative prevents use of the reciprocal theorem: $x^3$ is invertible, but its inverse has no finite derivative at zero.

## Practice

1. Find the tangent slope to $xy+y^2=6$ at $(1,2)$.
2. Restrict $f(x)=x^2$ to $x>0$. Find the derivative of its inverse at $y=9$ using corresponding points.

## Worked answers

1. The point satisfies $1\cdot2+2^2=6$. Differentiation gives $y+xy'+2yy'=0$, so $y'=-y/(x+2y)$. At $(1,2)$, the denominator is $5\ne0$ and the slope is $-2/5$. The nonzero denominator also verifies the implicit theorem's local condition.

2. The restriction makes $f$ one-to-one. The original input paired with $y=9$ is $x=3$, and $f'(3)=6$. Thus $(f^{-1})'(9)=1/6$. The explicit inverse $\sqrt y$ gives the same result, since its derivative is $1/(2\sqrt y)$ for $y>0$. The negative domain restriction would produce a different branch and a negative inverse derivative.
