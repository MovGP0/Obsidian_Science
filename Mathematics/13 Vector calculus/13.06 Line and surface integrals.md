---
title: "Line and surface integrals"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Scalar integrals add values with length or area; circulation and flux pair a vector field with an oriented tangent or normal.

## Two line integrals

Let $C$ be a piecewise regular curve traced once by $\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb R^3$. For a continuous scalar field $f$, its scalar line integral is

$$
\int_C f\,ds=\int_a^b f(\gamma(t))\|\gamma'(t)\|\,dt.
$$

It adds scalar values weighted by arc length. If $f=1$, the result is the curve length. Reversing the curve does not change this integral because length is nonnegative.

For a continuous vector field $\mathbf F$, its oriented line integral, or **circulation**, is

$$
\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r
=\int_a^b\mathbf F(\gamma(t))\cdot\gamma'(t)\,dt.
$$

Here $d\mathbf r=T\,ds$, where $T$ is the unit tangent. Reversing the orientation changes the sign. In coordinates the integrand is $P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz$ for $\mathbf F=(P,Q,R)$. This is different from $\|\mathbf F\|\,ds$: the dot product selects the signed tangential component.

## Two surface integrals

Let a regular patch $X:D\to S$ cover a surface once apart from possible boundary identifications. Its scalar surface integral is

$$
\iint_S f\,dS
=\iint_D f(X(u,v))\|X_u\times X_v\|\,du\,dv.
$$

It is independent of orientation. On an oriented surface with unit normal $n$, the **flux** integral is

$$
\iint_S\mathbf F\cdot n\,dS
=\iint_D\mathbf F(X(u,v))\cdot(X_u\times X_v)\,du\,dv,
$$

provided the cross product matches the chosen orientation. Reverse the normal to reverse the flux. For a closed surface, outward orientation is standard. For an open surface, state the normal choice. If several patches are needed, add their integrals without counting overlap twice. See [[13.05 Parameterized curves and surfaces]] for the length and area factors.

## Worked example: the same circle, two integrals

Let $C$ be the unit circle in $z=0$, counterclockwise from $+z$, and let $f=x^2$, $\mathbf F=(-y,x,0)$. Set $\gamma(t)=(\cos t,\sin t,0)$, $0\le t\le2\pi$. Its speed is one. Therefore

$$
\int_C x^2\,ds=\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2t\,dt=\pi.
$$

The identity $\cos^2t=(1+\cos2t)/2$ gives the final value. For circulation,

$$
\mathbf F(\gamma(t))\cdot\gamma'(t)
=(-\sin t,\cos t,0)\cdot(-\sin t,\cos t,0)=1.
$$

Thus $\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=2\pi$. Under reversed orientation, the scalar integral stays $\pi$, while the circulation becomes $-2\pi$.

## Worked example: flux through a graph

Let $S$ be $z=x+y$ over $0\le x,y\le1$, with upward orientation, and let $\mathbf F=(0,0,z)$. Use $X(u,v)=(u,v,u+v)$. Then

$$
X_u\times X_v=(-1,-1,1),\qquad
\mathbf F(X)=(0,0,u+v).
$$

Their dot product is $u+v$, so

$$
\iint_S\mathbf F\cdot n\,dS
=\int_0^1\int_0^1(u+v)\,du\,dv=1.
$$

For comparison, $dS=\sqrt3\,du\,dv$ and $n=(-1,-1,1)/\sqrt3$. Using both factors gives the same integrand. Using a unit normal without the $\sqrt3$ area factor would give an incorrect result.

## Practical checks

Write the parameter interval or region, then compute tangents, then choose orientation, and only then integrate. A closed curve symbol $\oint$ specifies closure, not automatically counterclockwise orientation. A closed surface symbol also does not replace a stated outward convention. Singular fields must be continuous on the integration set for these ordinary integral formulas; excluded interior points become a separate concern when an integral theorem is used.

## Practice

1. Along the segment from $(0,0)$ to $(1,1)$, evaluate $\int_C(x+y)\,ds$ and $\int_C(x,y)\cdot d\mathbf r$.
2. Find the upward flux of $(0,0,2)$ through $z=x^2+y^2$ over the unit disk.

## Worked answers

1. Use $\gamma(t)=(t,t)$ for $0\le t\le1$. The speed is $\sqrt2$, so the scalar integral is $\sqrt2\int_0^1 2t\,dt=\sqrt2$. The vector integral has integrand $(t,t)\cdot(1,1)=2t$ and equals $1$.
2. For $X(u,v)=(u,v,u^2+v^2)$, the upward area vector is $(-2u,-2v,1)\,du\,dv$. Its dot product with $(0,0,2)$ is $2$. Integrating over the parameter disk gives $2\pi$. The increased graph area is exactly offset by the smaller normal component in this calculation.
