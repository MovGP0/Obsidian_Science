title: "Parameterized curves and surfaces"

> [!TLDR]
> Derivatives of a parameterization give tangent directions, while their lengths and cross products give length and area elements.

## Regular curves

A parameterized curve in $\mathbb R^3$ is a map $\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathbb R^3$. Assume it is $C^1$, or piecewise $C^1$ with finitely many pieces. At a regular point, $\gamma'(t)\ne0$. The derivative is tangent to the curve, and

$$
T(t)=\frac{\gamma'(t)}{\|\gamma'(t)\|}
$$

is the oriented unit tangent. Increasing $t$ fixes an orientation. The speed is $\|\gamma'(t)\|$, and the arc-length element is

$$
ds=\|\gamma'(t)\|\,dt,\qquad
L=\int_a^b\|\gamma'(t)\|\,dt.
$$

The local reason is $\gamma(t+\Delta t)-\gamma(t)=\gamma'(t)\Delta t+o(|\Delta t|)$. The small chord therefore has length approximately $\|\gamma'(t)\||\Delta t|$. Summing and taking a limit gives arc length under these regularity assumptions.

A monotone regular change of parameter preserves length. A decreasing change reverses the tangent orientation. A map that traces the same curve twice counts its length twice in the parameter integral, so distinguish the geometric image from the traced curve.

## Regular surfaces

A surface patch is a $C^1$ map $X:D\to\mathbb R^3$ from a plane parameter domain $D$. Write its parameters as $(u,v)$. The coordinate tangent vectors are $X_u$ and $X_v$. The patch is regular when they are linearly independent, equivalently

$$
X_u\times X_v\ne0.
$$

An orientation on the patch is given by

$$
n=\frac{X_u\times X_v}{\|X_u\times X_v\|}.
$$

Swapping $u$ and $v$ reverses this normal. A small parameter rectangle maps approximately to the parallelogram spanned by $X_u\,du$ and $X_v\,dv$. Thus

$$
dS=\|X_u\times X_v\|\,du\,dv,
\qquad n\,dS=(X_u\times X_v)\,du\,dv.
$$

If $E=X_u\cdot X_u$, $F=X_u\cdot X_v$, and $G=X_v\cdot X_v$, the cross-product identity gives $dS=\sqrt{EG-F^2}\,du\,dv$. Here $F$ denotes a scalar metric coefficient, not a vector field. The area formula assumes a one-to-one patch in its interior; overlapping charts or repeated coverage require separate accounting.

## Worked examples

For the helix $\gamma(t)=(2\cos t,2\sin t,3t)$, $0\le t\le2\pi$,

$$
\gamma'(t)=(-2\sin t,2\cos t,3),
\qquad \|\gamma'(t)\|=\sqrt{4+9}=\sqrt{13}.
$$

Its length is $2\pi\sqrt{13}$. The tangent is $\gamma'/\sqrt{13}$. Constant speed does not mean constant tangent: the first two components change with $t$.

For the graph $z=u^2+v^2$, choose $X(u,v)=(u,v,u^2+v^2)$. Then

$$
X_u=(1,0,2u),\qquad X_v=(0,1,2v),
\qquad X_u\times X_v=(-2u,-2v,1).
$$

The positive third component gives an upward orientation. On the disk $u^2+v^2\le1$, its area is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=\iint_D\sqrt{1+4u^2+4v^2}\,du\,dv\\
&=2\pi\int_0^1 r\sqrt{1+4r^2}\,dr
=\frac{\pi}{6}(5\sqrt5-1).
\end{aligned}
$$

The factor $r$ comes from polar area in the parameter plane; it is additional to the graph's area factor. Substitution $w=1+4r^2$, $dw=8r\,dr$, yields the last equality.

## Common errors

A normal is not necessarily a unit normal. Normalize it when the formula needs $n$, but do not normalize $X_u\times X_v$ in an integral that already uses $du\,dv$: that would discard its area factor. A failed parameterization does not always mean a singular geometric surface; spherical coordinates degenerate at the poles of a smooth sphere.

## Practice

1. Find the length of $\gamma(t)=(3t,4t,0)$ for $-1\le t\le2$.
2. For $X(u,v)=(u,v,2u-v)$ over $0\le u,v\le1$, find the upward unit normal and surface area.

## Worked answers

1. The derivative is $(3,4,0)$ and its norm is $5$. Thus $L=\int_{-1}^2 5\,dt=15$. This also equals the distance between the two endpoints because the curve is a straight segment traced once.
2. The tangent vectors are $(1,0,2)$ and $(0,1,-1)$. Their cross product is $(-2,1,1)$, with magnitude $\sqrt6$. Thus the upward unit normal is $(-2,1,1)/\sqrt6$, and the area is $\int_0^1\int_0^1\sqrt6\,du\,dv=\sqrt6$.
