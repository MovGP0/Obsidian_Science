---
title: "Tangent and cotangent spaces"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Tangent vectors form a vector space at each point, and covectors form its dual space of linear real-valued maps.

## Two spaces at one point

For a smooth $n$-dimensional manifold $M$, the tangent space $T_pM$ consists of all tangent vectors at $p$. Addition and scalar multiplication are defined through their derivation actions: $(av+bw)[f]=av[f]+bw[f]$. In coordinates $(x^1,\ldots,x^n)$ near $p$, the basis vectors are

$$
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right|_p[f]
=\frac{\partial(f\circ x^{-1})}{\partial x^i}(x(p)).
$$

Every vector has a unique expression $v=\sum_i v^i\partial_i|_p$. Therefore $\dim T_pM=n$. A coordinate basis vector means differentiation while the other coordinate variables are held fixed. It need not be a unit vector in any chosen metric.

The cotangent space is the linear dual

$$
T_p^*M=\operatorname{Hom}(T_pM,\mathbb R).
$$

Its elements are covectors. For a smooth function $f$, the differential $df_p$ is the covector defined by $df_p(v)=v[f]$. The coordinate differentials $dx^i|_p$ are dual to the tangent basis because

$$
dx^i|_p(\partial_j|_p)=\partial_j[x^i]=\delta^i_j.
$$

Here $\delta^i_j$ is one when $i=j$ and zero otherwise. Every covector is $\alpha=\sum_i\alpha_i dx^i|_p$, and its pairing with a vector is $\alpha(v)=\sum_i\alpha_i v^i$. No metric is needed for this pairing. Identifying a covector with a vector requires extra structure, such as an inner product.

## Coordinate changes and inverse rules

For new coordinates $y^a$, the bases satisfy

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial y^a}=\sum_i\frac{\partial x^i}{\partial y^a}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i},
\qquad dy^a=\sum_i\frac{\partial y^a}{\partial x^i}dx^i.
$$

The vector components use the forward Jacobian, while covector components use the inverse Jacobian. These opposite rules preserve the scalar $\alpha(v)$. A row of numbers and a column of numbers can look similar, but their transformation rules encode different mathematical roles.

## Worked example: polar bases

On a region of the punctured plane with a continuous angle, write $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, with $r>0$. Differentiating the inverse coordinate map gives

$$
\partial_r=\cos\theta\,\partial_x+\sin\theta\,\partial_y,
\qquad \partial_\theta=-r\sin\theta\,\partial_x+r\cos\theta\,\partial_y.
$$

Solving for the dual forms yields

$$
dr=\cos\theta\,dx+\sin\theta\,dy,
\qquad d\theta=-\frac{\sin\theta}{r}dx+\frac{\cos\theta}{r}dy.
$$

At $p=(0,2)$, take $r=2$, $\theta=\pi/2$. Then $\partial_r=\partial_y$, $\partial_\theta=-2\partial_x$, $dr=dy$, and $d\theta=-dx/2$. For $v=3\partial_r+2\partial_\theta=-4\partial_x+3\partial_y$ and $\alpha=5dr-d\theta=dx/2+5dy$, both descriptions give

$$
\alpha(v)=5\cdot3-1\cdot2=13
=\frac12(-4)+5\cdot3.
$$

This is a useful direct check of the dual transformation rules.

## Spaces at distinct points

The symbols $T_pM$ and $T_qM$ denote different spaces when $p\ne q$. A chart can compare component tuples, but that comparison depends on the chart. There is generally no distinguished subtraction of vectors based at different points. On an embedded sphere, tangent planes at different points even have different normal directions. A connection or a specified map can provide a comparison, but smooth manifold structure alone does not select one. The tangent bundle is the collection of these spaces with each vector's base point retained.

## Practice

1. In a two-dimensional coordinate basis, let $v=2\partial_1-3\partial_2$ and $\alpha=4dx^1+dx^2$. Find $\alpha(v)$ and $df(v)$ for $f=(x^1)^2+x^2$ at $(1,2)$.
2. With $u=2x$ on a line, find $\partial_u$ and $du$ in the $x$ basis and verify duality.

## Worked answers

1. Linearity gives $\alpha(v)=4(2)+1(-3)=5$. At $(1,2)$, $df=2dx^1+dx^2$, so $df(v)=2(2)-3=1$. The two covectors are different even though they act on the same vector.
2. Since $x=u/2$, the chain rule gives $\partial_u=(1/2)\partial_x$. Differentiating $u=2x$ gives $du=2dx$. Consequently $du(\partial_u)=2(1/2)dx(\partial_x)=1$. Using the same scaling for both bases would fail this check.
