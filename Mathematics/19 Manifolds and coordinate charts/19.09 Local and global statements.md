title: "Local and global statements"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A failed coordinate formula can be repaired by another chart; a failure of local manifold structure cannot.

## Identify what fails

A manifold statement is local if it can be checked in a neighbourhood of each point. Existence of coordinates and smoothness of a function are local statements. Compactness, connectedness, and the existence of a single chart covering the whole space are global questions. Every circle point has an interval neighbourhood, but the entire circle is not an interval. Local Euclidean structure does not erase global topology.

A coordinate singularity is a failure of a chosen coordinate system, such as a many-to-one parametrization, a discontinuous coordinate inverse, or a degenerate derivative. It does not by itself show that the underlying space is singular. To prove a manifold fails to be smooth as an embedded submanifold, one must examine the space near the point, not merely one formula for it. Topological manifold structure and embedded smooth structure are also different questions.

## Worked example: the origin of polar coordinates

Consider the map

$$
P(r,\theta)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta).
$$

Its Jacobian and determinant are

$$
DP=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-r\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&r\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det DP=r.
$$

For $r>0$, a sufficiently short angle interval makes $P$ one-to-one, with smooth local inverse. At $r=0$, every angle gives $(0,0)$, and the angle column of $DP$ vanishes. These facts prevent polar coordinates from forming a chart at the origin. However, the Cartesian identity map is a smooth chart there. The plane has no singular point at zero.

The Euclidean metric provides another check. In Cartesian coordinates it is $dx^2+dy^2$, where a squared differential denotes the tensor product with itself. In polar coordinates it is $dr^2+r^2d\theta^2$. Substituting $r=0$ into the latter coefficient matrix gives determinant zero, but that matrix is not a valid chart representation at zero. It cannot prove degeneracy of the metric there.

## A true local obstruction and an inconclusive test

For the crossing $X=\{(x,y):xy=0\}$, the origin has four local branches. An interval has two. If a one-dimensional chart existed at the origin, restrict its image to a small interval and its domain to a sufficiently small cross neighbourhood. The punctured chart domain would have two connected components, but each of the four arms must meet that neighbourhood and no two arms can connect without the origin. This contradicts the chart property. Other points of $X$ do have line charts.

By contrast, a zero derivative of a defining equation is not a proof of singularity. The set defined by $F(x,y)=y^3=0$ is simply the horizontal line. Although $DF$ vanishes on it, the line has the smooth coordinate $x$. The regular level set theorem fails to apply to this *equation*, but the set remains smooth. Look for another description before concluding that a space is singular.

## Global obstructions need global reasons

A sphere cannot have one chart onto an open subset of $\mathbb R^2$: the sphere is compact, while no nonempty Euclidean open subset is compact. Nevertheless it has a smooth atlas. Similarly, a local inverse of a smooth map need not be a global inverse. The map $t\mapsto(\cos t,\sin t)$ has nonzero derivative everywhere, but repeats after $2\pi$. Local derivative information does not establish global injectivity.

## Practice

1. Longitude is undefined at the north pole of $S^2$. Give a valid local chart there and explain why longitude failure is a coordinate issue.
2. For $C=\{(x,y):y=|x|\}$, distinguish topological manifold structure from smooth embedded structure at the vertex.

## Worked answers

1. On $z>0$, projection $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y)$ has inverse $(u,v)\mapsto(u,v,\sqrt{1-u^2-v^2})$ on the open unit disk. The north pole has coordinates $(0,0)$. These smooth coordinates exist where longitude does not, so the pole is a regular manifold point.
2. Projection to $x$ is a homeomorphism $C\to\mathbb R$ with continuous inverse $t\mapsto(t,|t|)$. Thus $C$ is a topological line and can receive a smooth abstract structure through this chart. As a subset of the plane it is not a smooth embedded one-dimensional submanifold at zero: the two one-sided secant directions span distinct lines, whereas a smooth embedded curve has one tangent line. A corner can therefore be topologically regular while its specified embedding is not smooth.
