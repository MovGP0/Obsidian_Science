title: "Examples of manifolds"

> [!TLDR]
> Explicit charts and the regular level set theorem produce lines, circles, spheres, products, and many other manifolds.

## Lines and circles

The space $\mathbb R^n$ is a smooth manifold with its identity chart. Every open subset inherits this chart locally. A straight line in $\mathbb R^m$ has the form $L=\{a+tb:t\in\mathbb R\}$, where $b\ne0$. The map $t\mapsto a+tb$ has inverse

$$
a+tb\longmapsto\frac{b\cdot((a+tb)-a)}{b\cdot b}=t.
$$

Thus the line has dimension one regardless of $m$. A circle is also locally one-dimensional, but needs more than one chart. Near the upper half of the unit circle, projection to $x$ has inverse $(t,\sqrt{1-t^2})$. Near the right half, projection to $y$ has inverse $(\sqrt{1-t^2},t)$. Together with the lower and left halves, these charts cover the circle. The square roots are smooth on their open coordinate intervals.

## Sphere graph charts

The unit sphere is $S^2=\{(x,y,z):x^2+y^2+z^2=1\}$. On the northern open hemisphere $z>0$, use coordinates $(u,v)=(x,y)$. The inverse is

$$
(u,v)\longmapsto(u,v,\sqrt{1-u^2-v^2}),\qquad u^2+v^2<1.
$$

The image domain is the open unit disk in $\mathbb R^2$. The square root is smooth because its argument is positive. Five more signed coordinate hemispheres cover the rest of the sphere. At any sphere point, at least one coordinate is nonzero, so at least one of these six charts applies. This gives an explicit local description at the poles as well as the equator.

## Products add dimensions

If $M$ has an $m$-dimensional chart $\varphi:U\to A$ and $N$ has an $n$-dimensional chart $\psi:V\to B$, define

$$
\varphi\times\psi:U\times V\to A\times B,\qquad(p,q)\mapsto(\varphi(p),\psi(q)).
$$

The target is open in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$. Product transitions act separately on the two blocks and are smooth. Therefore $M\times N$ has dimension $m+n$. The torus $S^1\times S^1$ has local coordinates $(\theta,\phi)$, with each angle restricted to a short open interval. Globally the angles are periodic, so the coordinate pair is not a single chart on the whole torus. The product $S^1\times\mathbb R$ is a two-dimensional cylinder.

## Worked example: a regular level set

Let $F:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^k$ be smooth. If $DF_p$ has rank $k$ at every $p\in F^{-1}(c)$, the regular level set theorem says that $F^{-1}(c)$ is an embedded manifold of dimension $m-k$. The rank condition means that the $k$ constraints are locally independent. The implicit function theorem then solves for $k$ ambient coordinates in terms of the other $m-k$ coordinates. This is the source of the local graph charts; the theorem is used here without a full proof.

Take $F(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2-z$. Its derivative is the row $(2x,2y,-1)$, which has rank one everywhere. Therefore $F^{-1}(0)$ is two-dimensional. In this case no implicit solution is needed:

$$
(u,v)\longmapsto(u,v,u^2+v^2)
$$

is a global parametrization with smooth inverse $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y)$. The paraboloid is curved in $\mathbb R^3$, but its smooth coordinates cover all of $\mathbb R^2$.

A failed rank condition is inconclusive. The equation $x^3=0$ has derivative zero at its level set, yet that set is the single point $\{0\}$, a zero-dimensional manifold. The regular level theorem is sufficient, not necessary, for a particular defining equation.

## Practice

1. Show that $x^2+y^2+z^2=4$ defines a two-dimensional manifold and give a chart near $(0,0,2)$.
2. Find the dimension and local coordinates of $S^2\times S^1$. Explain why five ambient coordinates do not imply dimension five.

## Worked answers

1. For $G=x^2+y^2+z^2$, the derivative is $(2x,2y,2z)$. It is nonzero on $G^{-1}(4)$ because the origin is not there. The dimension is $3-1=2$. On $z>0$, the inverse coordinate map is $(u,v)\mapsto(u,v,\sqrt{4-u^2-v^2})$ on $u^2+v^2<4$.
2. Take a sphere graph chart $(u,v)$ and a local circle angle $\theta$. Then $(u,v,\theta)$ gives three independent coordinates. The ambient realization lies in $\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^2$, but the sphere and circle equations impose two independent constraints. Hence the local dimension is $5-2=3$.
