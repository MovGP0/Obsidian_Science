title: "Covariant derivatives"

> [!TLDR]
> A connection adds a matrix-valued one-form to component differentiation so the result transforms as the section does.

## Definition and local form

Let $E\to M$ be a smooth real or complex vector bundle. A **connection** is a linear map $\nabla$ from sections of $E$ to $E$-valued one-forms with the Leibniz rule

$$
\nabla(fs)=df\otimes s+f\nabla s
$$

for every smooth scalar function $f$ and section $s$. In a local frame $e=(e_1,\ldots,e_r)$, define the matrix of one-forms $A=(A^a{}_b)$ by $\nabla e_b=\sum_a e_a A^a{}_b$. Then for $s=e\psi$, the Leibniz rule gives

$$
\nabla s=eD\psi,\qquad D\psi=d\psi+A\psi.
$$

Thus $A$ records the derivative of the frame. For a tangent vector $X$, $D_X\psi=X(\psi)+A(X)\psi$. In base coordinates $x^i$, write $A=\sum_i A_i dx^i$, so $D_i=\partial_i+A_i$. A basis index labels rows and columns of $A_i$; the index $i$ labels a base direction.

## Derive the transformation law

Use the passive frame convention $e'=eg$, $\psi'=g^{-1}\psi$. The same geometric derivative must satisfy $e'D'\psi'=eD\psi$, hence $D'\psi'=g^{-1}D\psi$. Expand the left side:

$$
D'\psi'=d(g^{-1}\psi)+A'g^{-1}\psi
=g^{-1}d\psi-g^{-1}(dg)g^{-1}\psi+A'g^{-1}\psi.
$$

The required right side is $g^{-1}d\psi+g^{-1}A\psi$. Cancel the ordinary derivative term. The equality must hold for every column $\psi$, which implies

$$
A'g^{-1}=g^{-1}A+g^{-1}(dg)g^{-1}.
$$

Right multiplication by $g$ yields

$$
\boxed{A'=g^{-1}Ag+g^{-1}dg.}
$$

The second term makes $A$ a connection form rather than an ordinary tensorial one-form. The difference of two connection forms is tensorial: if $B$ is another connection, then $A'-B'=g^{-1}(A-B)g$ because the derivative terms cancel.

## Worked example: a nonorthonormal moving frame

Use the trivial rank-two real bundle over $\mathbb R$. Let the original frame be constant and choose the connection $A=0$. Set

$$
g(x)=\begin{pmatrix}e^x&0\\0&e^{-x}\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Then $g^{-1}dg=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)dx$, so this is $A'$ in the moving frame. For the section with original components $\psi=(x,1)^T$, we have $D\psi=(1,0)^Tdx$. The new components are $\psi'=(xe^{-x},e^x)^T$. Differentiate and add the correction:

$$
D'\psi'=
\begin{pmatrix}(1-x)e^{-x}\\e^x\end{pmatrix}dx
+\begin{pmatrix}xe^{-x}\\-e^x\end{pmatrix}dx
=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-x}\\0\end{pmatrix}dx.
$$

This equals $g^{-1}D\psi$, as covariance requires. The connection is unchanged as a geometric operation, although its local matrix is no longer zero.

## Restrictions and common errors

An arbitrary connection need not preserve a fibre metric. In an orthonormal complex frame, metric compatibility means $A^\dagger=-A$: the matrices are anti-Hermitian. In real orthonormal frames, they are skew-symmetric. These conditions ensure that parallel transport preserves inner products.

Do not use $A'=gAg^{-1}-(dg)g^{-1}$ with the convention $\psi'=g^{-1}\psi$; that formula belongs to a different transformation convention. Also, a zero matrix at one point does not imply a zero connection on a neighborhood. Derivatives of $A$ can still give curvature there; see [[26.06 Curvature or field strength]].

## Practice

1. In a real line, take $D=d+x\,dx$ and $g=e^{-x^2/2}$. Compute $A'$ and explain the result.
2. Let $A=J\,dx$ with $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and take constant $g=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)$. Find $A'$.

## Worked answers

1. Scalars commute, so $A'=A+g^{-1}dg$. Since $dg=-xg\,dx$, $A'=x\,dx-x\,dx=0$. The selected nonzero frame scaling removes this particular connection on $\mathbb R$.
2. Since $dg=0$, only conjugation remains. Compute $Jg=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\2&0\end{pmatrix}$ and then $g^{-1}Jg=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1/2\\2&0\end{pmatrix}$. Hence $A'$ is this matrix times $dx$. Its lack of ordinary skew symmetry reflects the nonorthonormal new frame.
