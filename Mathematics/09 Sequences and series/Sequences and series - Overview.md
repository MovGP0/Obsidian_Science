> [!TLDR]
> Distinguish convergent infinite processes from finite approximations and control the error of a truncated expansion.

## Scope

A sequence describes an ordered set of values; a series describes the limit of partial sums. Taylor expansions and other series are useful only when their convergence or approximation error is understood.

## Starting knowledge

Use elementary functions from [[Functions - Overview]], derivatives from [[Differential calculus - Overview]], and definite and improper integrals from [[Integral calculus - Overview]].

## Topics to cover

- Sequences: limits, boundedness, monotonicity, and examples that fail to converge.
- Infinite series: partial sums, geometric series, and the difference between convergence of terms and convergence of their sum.
- Convergence tests: comparison, ratio, and integral tests, including their limits of applicability.
- Absolute and conditional convergence: explain why rearrangement is not harmless for every series.
- Power series: centre, radius of convergence, endpoint checks, and operations within a justified interval.
- Taylor and Maclaurin expansions: construct coefficients from derivatives and expand common elementary functions.
- Remainders and error: estimate a truncation error and state the small parameter and the approximation order.
- Asymptotic notation: distinguish a local finite-order approximation from a claim that an infinite Taylor series equals its function.

## Learning outcomes

After studying this topic, you should be able to:

- Calculate a geometric sum and state the condition under which its infinite form converges.
- Construct a Taylor polynomial and bound or estimate its remainder near the expansion point.
- Find a power series’ convergence interval, checking its endpoints separately.
