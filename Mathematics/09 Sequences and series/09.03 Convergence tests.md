title: "Convergence tests"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Comparison, ratio, and integral tests prove convergence under different hypotheses; an inconclusive test gives no conclusion.

## Start with the terms

For a real series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n$, first check whether $a_n\to0$. If this fails, the series diverges. If it holds, select a test that fits the terms. Convergence refers to the limit of partial sums, as defined in [[09.02 Infinite series]]. For positive terms, these partial sums increase, so convergence is equivalent to an upper bound on all partial sums.

## Direct and limit comparison

Suppose $0\leq a_n\leq b_n$ for all sufficiently large $n$. If $\sum b_n$ converges, then $\sum a_n$ converges: its increasing partial sums have an upper bound. Conversely, if $\sum a_n$ diverges, then $\sum b_n$ diverges. Finitely many exceptions do not affect convergence.

The direction matters. Bounding terms above by those of a divergent series proves nothing. Bounding them below by those of a convergent series also proves nothing. For example, $0\leq1/n^2\leq1/n$, but the two series have different behavior.

If $a_n,b_n>0$ eventually and

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n}{b_n}=c,
\qquad 0<c<\infty,
$$

then the series have the same convergence behavior. For sufficiently large $n$, the ratio lies between $c/2$ and $3c/2$. Direct comparison with constant multiples of $b_n$ proves the result. A ratio limit of zero or infinity is outside this version of the test, though a suitable one-sided comparison can still help.

## The ratio test

Assume the terms are nonzero eventually and the limit

$$
L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|
$$

exists, possibly as $+\infty$. If $L<1$, the series converges absolutely: choose $q$ with $L<q<1$. Beyond some index, $|a_{n+1}|\leq q|a_n|$, so the tail is bounded by a convergent geometric series. If $L>1$, the magnitudes eventually increase by a factor greater than $1$, so they do not tend to zero and the series diverges. If $L=1$, the test gives no conclusion.

Factorials and powers often fit this test well. For both $a_n=1/n$ and $a_n=1/n^2$, however, the ratio tends to $1$. Their different behavior confirms that the boundary case needs another argument. Absolute convergence is explained in [[09.04 Absolute and conditional convergence]].

## The integral test

Suppose $a_n=f(n)$, where $f$ is positive, continuous, and nonincreasing for $x\geq1$. Then $\sum a_n$ and the improper integral $\int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx$ either both converge or both diverge. A finite shift in the starting point is allowed. The decreasing graph gives rectangle bounds

$$
\int_1^{N+1} f(x)\,dx
\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(n)
\leq f(1)+\int_1^N f(x)\,dx.
$$

These inequalities prove the test. They do not say that the series sum equals the integral. Applied to $f(x)=x^{-p}$ for $p>0$, integration gives convergence exactly when $p>1$. For $p=1$, the integral is $\log x$, which grows without bound. For $p\leq0$, the series already fails the term condition.

## Worked examples

For $a_n=n/2^n$,

$$
\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\frac{n+1}{2n}\longrightarrow\frac12<1.
$$

Thus $\sum n/2^n$ converges. For $c_n=(3n+1)/(n^3+5)$, compare with $b_n=1/n^2$:

$$
\frac{c_n}{b_n}=\frac{3n^3+n^2}{n^3+5}
=\frac{3+1/n}{1+5/n^3}\longrightarrow3.
$$

Both term sequences are positive. Since $\sum1/n^2$ converges by the integral test, the limit comparison proves convergence of $\sum c_n$. Each conclusion uses the stated hypotheses, not only the numerical limit.

## Practice

1. Determine the behavior of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n!/3^n$.
2. Determine the behavior of $\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}1/[n(\log n)^2]$.

## Worked answers

1. Successive positive terms have ratio $(n+1)/3$, which tends to infinity. For $n\geq3$ the ratio exceeds $1$, so the terms do not approach zero. The series diverges by the ratio test.
2. For $x\geq2$, $f(x)=1/[x(\log x)^2]$ is positive and continuous. Its denominator has derivative $(\log x)^2+2\log x>0$, so $f$ decreases. With $u=\log x$ and $du=dx/x$, the integral becomes $\int_{\log2}^{\infty}u^{-2}\,du=1/\log2$, which is finite. The integral test therefore proves convergence.
