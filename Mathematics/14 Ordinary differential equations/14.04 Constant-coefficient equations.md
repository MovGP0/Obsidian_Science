> [!TLDR]
> Roots of the characteristic polynomial determine exponential modes; repeated roots add polynomial factors, and conjugate roots give real sine and cosine modes.

## From a differential operator to a polynomial

Consider the real homogeneous equation

$$
a_ny^{(n)}+a_{n-1}y^{(n-1)}+\cdots+a_0y=0,
\qquad a_n\ne0,
$$

where every $a_k$ is constant. Define $D=d/dt$ and the characteristic polynomial $p(r)=a_nr^n+\cdots+a_0$. For a trial function $y=e^{rt}$, every differentiation supplies a factor $r$. Therefore

$$
p(D)e^{rt}=p(r)e^{rt}.
$$

Since an exponential is never zero, the trial function solves the equation exactly when $p(r)=0$. The roots may be real or complex. Real roots give real exponentials directly; complex roots are combined to obtain real solutions.

For an equation of order $n$, the homogeneous solution space has dimension $n$. The root construction below supplies $n$ independent real functions when roots are counted with multiplicity. Initial values then determine their coefficients through a linear system. Continuous constant coefficients ensure a unique solution for any initial data on all of $\mathbb R$.

## Repeated roots

If $r$ is a root of multiplicity $m$, the corresponding functions are

$$
e^{rt},\quad t e^{rt},\quad\ldots,\quad t^{m-1}e^{rt}.
$$

To see why the polynomial factors appear, use the product rule:

$$
(D-r)(e^{rt}v)=e^{rt}v',\qquad
(D-r)^m(e^{rt}v)=e^{rt}v^{(m)}.
$$

Every polynomial $v$ of degree at most $m-1$ has $v^{(m)}=0$. As $p(D)$ contains the factor $(D-r)^m$, all these functions solve the equation. Repeating the same exponential $m$ times would give only one function, so it could not fit arbitrary $m$ pieces of data.

For example, $y''-4y'+4y=0$ has $p(r)=(r-2)^2$. Thus $y=(A+Bt)e^{2t}$. If $y(0)=1$ and $y'(0)=0$, then $A=1$ and $B+2A=0$, so $B=-2$. The solution is $(1-2t)e^{2t}$. The factor $t$ is essential even though the equation has only one distinct root.

## Complex roots and real forms

For a conjugate pair $r=\alpha\pm i\beta$, with $\beta>0$, Euler's identity gives

$$
e^{(\alpha+i\beta)t}=e^{\alpha t}(\cos\beta t+i\sin\beta t).
$$

Taking real and imaginary parts yields the real basis $e^{\alpha t}\cos\beta t$ and $e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t$. The factor $e^{\alpha t}$ controls growth or decay; $\beta$ controls oscillation. If the conjugate roots each have multiplicity $m$, include $t^j$ times both real functions for $j=0,\ldots,m-1$. Coefficients of a real equation force conjugate roots to occur together.

## Worked example: complex roots and initial data

Solve $y''+2y'+5y=0$ with $y(0)=1$, $y'(0)=0$. Complete the square in the characteristic polynomial:

$$
r^2+2r+5=(r+1)^2+4,
\qquad r=-1\pm2i.
$$

Therefore $y=e^{-t}(A\cos2t+B\sin2t)$. At zero, $y(0)=A=1$. Differentiate the entire product:

$$
y'=e^{-t}\bigl[(-A+2B)\cos2t+(-B-2A)\sin2t\bigr].
$$

The second initial value gives $-A+2B=0$, hence $B=1/2$. Thus

$$
y(t)=e^{-t}\left(\cos2t+\frac12\sin2t\right).
$$

The sine coefficient is not zero merely because $y'(0)=0$. The derivative of the exponential also contributes. This is a common source of an incorrect initial slope.

## Inhomogeneous equations and limitations

For $p(D)y=g(t)$, first obtain the homogeneous family above, then find one particular function $y_p$. The complete answer is $y=y_p+y_h$, because subtracting $y_p$ from any solution leaves a homogeneous solution. A right side that already matches a homogeneous mode requires extra polynomial factors in a trial particular solution; [[14.05 Harmonic equations]] develops a resonant example. Characteristic polynomials in this form apply to constant coefficients. Replacing $a_k$ by functions of $t$ generally invalidates the exponential trial.

## Practice

1. Solve $y''-y=0$ with $y(0)=2$ and $y'(0)=0$.
2. Give the full real homogeneous family for $(D^2+1)^2y=0$.

## Worked answers

1. The roots of $r^2-1$ are $1$ and $-1$, so $y=Ae^t+Be^{-t}$. The data give $A+B=2$ and $A-B=0$. Thus $A=B=1$, and $y=e^t+e^{-t}=2\cosh t$.
2. The roots $i$ and $-i$ each have multiplicity two. Each supplies a constant and a linear polynomial factor. The real family is $y=(A+Bt)\cos t+(C+Et)\sin t$. These four independent functions match the fourth order of the equation; the letter $E$ here is just a fourth arbitrary real constant.
