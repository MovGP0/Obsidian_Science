> [!TLDR]
> A harmonic equation combines oscillatory homogeneous modes with a forced response; damping and equality of forcing and natural frequencies determine its long-term form.

## Oscillation and the two constants

Use the mathematical equation

$$
y''+2\gamma y'+\omega_0^2y=F\cos(\Omega t),
\qquad \gamma\ge0,\quad\omega_0>0,\quad\Omega>0.
$$

Here $t$ is a real variable, $y$ is a real-valued function, and the coefficients are real mathematical parameters. The coefficient $\gamma$ is the damping parameter, $\omega_0$ is the undamped natural angular frequency, $F$ is the forcing coefficient, and $\Omega$ is the forcing angular frequency. The word forcing denotes the specified right-side function.

With $F=0$ and $\gamma=0$, the family is $y=A\cos(\omega_0t)+B\sin(\omega_0t)$. The constants represent independent data: $A=y(0)$ and $B=y'(0)/\omega_0$. Alternatively,

$$
y=R\cos(\omega_0t-\phi),\qquad
R=\sqrt{A^2+B^2},\quad A=R\cos\phi,\quad B=R\sin\phi.
$$

Choose $\phi$ using both signs of $A$ and $B$, for example with the two-argument arctangent. If $R=0$, the phase is arbitrary. The period of a nonzero solution is $2\pi/\omega_0$.

## Three damping cases

The characteristic roots are $r=-\gamma\pm\sqrt{\gamma^2-\omega_0^2}$. If $0<\gamma<\omega_0$, define $\omega_d=\sqrt{\omega_0^2-\gamma^2}$. Then

$$
y_h=e^{-\gamma t}(A\cos\omega_dt+B\sin\omega_dt).
$$

This is a decaying oscillation. Its sine coefficient follows from $y'(0)=-\gamma A+\omega_dB$, so $B=(y'(0)+\gamma y(0))/\omega_d$.

If $\gamma=\omega_0$, the repeated root gives $y_h=(A+Bt)e^{-\gamma t}$. If $\gamma>\omega_0$, both roots are distinct, real, and negative, so the solution is a sum of two decaying exponentials. These last two cases do not have a sustained sine or cosine factor. The three forms follow directly from [[14.04 Constant-coefficient equations]].

## A periodic particular solution

Try $y_p=a\cos\Omega t+b\sin\Omega t$. Set $\Delta=\omega_0^2-\Omega^2$. Matching cosine and sine coefficients gives

$$
\Delta a+2\gamma\Omega b=F,\qquad
\Delta b-2\gamma\Omega a=0.
$$

When $\Delta^2+4\gamma^2\Omega^2\ne0$, solve this two-by-two system:

$$
a=\frac{F\Delta}{\Delta^2+4\gamma^2\Omega^2},\qquad
b=\frac{2\gamma\Omega F}{\Delta^2+4\gamma^2\Omega^2}.
$$

For positive damping, this response is bounded, and $y_h$ decays as $t\to\infty$. The total solution is $y_p+y_h$; the two arbitrary constants belong to $y_h$ and are fixed only after the complete sum is used in the data.

For zero damping and $\Omega=\omega_0$, the denominator vanishes. The trial functions are already homogeneous modes, so this trial cannot work. This exact frequency match is the undamped resonance condition. For positive damping the response remains finite; a largest-amplitude frequency need not equal $\omega_0$.

## Worked example: exact resonance

Solve $y''+4y=\cos2t$, $y(0)=y'(0)=0$. Because $\cos2t$ is a homogeneous mode, try $y_p=c t\sin2t$. Its derivatives give

$$
y_p'=c\sin2t+2ct\cos2t,\qquad
y_p''=4c\cos2t-4ct\sin2t.
$$

Adding $4y_p$ leaves $4c\cos2t$, so $c=1/4$. Both $y_p(0)$ and $y_p'(0)$ are zero. The remaining homogeneous coefficients therefore vanish, and

$$
y(t)=\frac t4\sin2t.
$$

For $t\ge0$, its envelopes are $y=\pm t/4$. The response is unbounded in amplitude, although it crosses zero repeatedly. The right panel of Fig. 14.1 shows this precise statement.

## Practice

1. Solve $y''+9y=0$, $y(0)=0$, $y'(0)=6$. Find its amplitude and period.
2. Find a particular solution of $y''+4y=\cos t$. Then impose zero initial value and slope.

## Worked answers

1. Write $y=A\cos3t+B\sin3t$. The data give $A=0$ and $3B=6$, so $y=2\sin3t$. The amplitude is $2$, and the period is $2\pi/3$.
2. Try $y_p=a\cos t$. Substitution gives $3a\cos t=\cos t$, hence $a=1/3$. Add $A\cos2t+B\sin2t$. At zero, $A=-1/3$; the derivative condition gives $2B=0$. Thus $y=(\cos t-\cos2t)/3$. There is no factor $t$ because the two frequencies differ.
