> [!TLDR]
> Read mathematical statements precisely and distinguish definitions, assumptions, examples, and proofs.

## Scope

Mathematical notation compresses statements about objects and their relations. This topic develops the language needed to read an argument, state its assumptions, and decide whether its conclusion follows.

## Starting knowledge

Bring basic arithmetic with whole numbers and fractions. No earlier topic in this collection is required.

## Topics to cover

- Numbers and sets: membership, subsets, unions, intersections, and common number sets.
- Variables and expressions: distinguish an object, its name, an expression, and an equation.
- Functions and relations: inputs, outputs, domains, codomains, and the meaning of a function value.
- Logical statements: and, or, not, implication, equivalence, and the difference between a statement and its converse.
- Quantifiers: read and write statements that use “for every” and “there exists,” including their order and negation.
- Definitions and assumptions: separate a definition from an identity, a theorem, a hypothesis, and a convention.
- Indexed notation: subscripts, superscripts, finite sums, products, and the role of a summation index.
- Proof methods: direct arguments, counterexamples, contradiction, and elementary induction.

## Learning outcomes

After studying this topic, you should be able to:

- Translate a quantified statement between symbols and plain English, then negate it correctly.
- Evaluate an indexed sum and change its dummy index without changing its value.
- Identify the assumptions in a short argument and give either a proof or a counterexample for its claim.
