> [!TLDR]
> Solve equations with reversible steps, and reverse an inequality when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.

## Statements and solution sets

An equation states that two expressions are equal. An inequality compares their order. A solution is an allowed value that makes the statement true; the solution set contains all such values. Here the unknown $x$ is real. The symbols $<$ and $>$ exclude equality, while $\le$ and $\ge$ include it.

Two equations are equivalent on a domain if their solution sets on that domain agree. Adding or subtracting the same defined expression on both sides preserves equality. Multiplication or division by the same nonzero number also preserves equality. Multiplying by zero loses information: $x=4$ implies $0=0$, but the latter does not imply $x=4$.

If a multiplying expression contains $x$, first determine whether it can be zero or undefined. Division by $x$ in $x(x-2)=0$ would discard the valid solution $x=0$. Use the zero-product rule instead: a product of real numbers is zero precisely when at least one factor is zero.

## Linear equations

A linear equation has the form $ax+b=0$ after expansion and collection. If $a\ne0$, subtract $b$ and divide by $a$ to get $x=-b/a$. If $a=0$ and $b=0$, every allowed input is a solution. If $a=0$ and $b\ne0$, there is no solution.

For example,

$$
3(2x-1)=2x+9
\iff6x-3=2x+9
\iff4x=12
\iff x=3.
$$

Each double arrow denotes equivalence. Substitution gives $3(6-1)=15$ and $6+9=15$, which confirms the solution.

## Quadratic equations

A quadratic equation is $ax^2+bx+c=0$ with $a\ne0$. Factoring can reduce it to linear cases:

$$
x^2-5x+6=0
\iff(x-2)(x-3)=0
\iff x=2\text{ or }x=3.
$$

When factors are not apparent, the quadratic formula gives

$$
x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}.
$$

The discriminant $D=b^2-4ac$ determines the real possibilities: two distinct roots if $D>0$, one repeated root if $D=0$, and no real roots if $D<0$. [[02.07 Completing the square]] derives the formula and explains why these cases occur.

## Inequalities and signs

Adding the same real number preserves order. Multiplication by a positive number preserves order; multiplication by a negative number reverses it. To see the reversal, if $a<b$, then $b-a>0$. For $c<0$, $c(b-a)<0$, so $cb-ca<0$ and $cb<ca$.

For a linear example,

$$
7-3x\le1
\iff-3x\le-6
\iff x\ge2.
$$

The final division is by $-3$, so the comparison reverses. The set is the closed interval $[2,\infty)$, where a square bracket includes a finite endpoint and a round bracket excludes it. Infinity is never an included endpoint.

For a quadratic inequality, factor and test the sign on intervals between the roots. Solve $x^2-x-6>0$. Since it equals $(x-3)(x+2)$, the critical inputs are $-2$ and $3$:

| Interval | Sign of $x+2$ | Sign of $x-3$ | Product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x<-2$ | Negative | Negative | Positive |
| $-2<x<3$ | Positive | Negative | Negative |
| $x>3$ | Positive | Positive | Positive |

The solution is $(-\infty,-2)\cup(3,\infty)$. The strict comparison excludes both zeros. For a non-strict comparison $\ge0$, include them. A factor cannot change sign within one of these intervals because its only zero lies at an endpoint.

Do not multiply an inequality by an expression of unknown sign without separate cases. To solve $(x-1)/(x+2)>0$, a sign analysis is safer than multiplying directly by $x+2$. The input $-2$ must also stay outside the domain.

## Practice

1. Solve $2-5x>12$ and write the solution as an interval.
2. Solve $x^2-4x\le0$ and verify one interior input and one exterior input.

## Worked answers

1. Subtraction gives $-5x>10$. Dividing by $-5$ reverses the sign, so $x<-2$. The interval is $(-\infty,-2)$; the endpoint is excluded because the original inequality is strict.
2. Factor as $x(x-4)\le0$. For $x<0$ both factors are negative, so their product is positive. Between $0$ and $4$ the signs differ, so the product is negative. For $x>4$ both are positive. Include the zero endpoints to get $[0,4]$. At $x=2$, $4-8=-4\le0$; at $x=5$, $25-20=5>0$.
