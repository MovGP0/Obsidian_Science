> [!TLDR]
> A differential form assigns an alternating multilinear function to tangent vectors, with one independent component for each increasing set of indices.

## Covectors and alternating maps

Let $V$ be a real vector space of dimension $n$. A **covector** is a linear map $\alpha:V\to\mathbb R$. The set of covectors is the dual space $V^*$. If $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ is a basis of $V$, its dual basis $e^1,\ldots,e^n$ satisfies $e^i(e_j)=\delta^i_j$, where the Kronecker delta is one when $i=j$ and zero otherwise. Thus a covector $\alpha=\sum_i a_i e^i$ evaluates $v=\sum_i v^i e_i$ as $\alpha(v)=\sum_i a_i v^i$.

A covariant $k$-tensor is a multilinear map $V^k\to\mathbb R$. It is **alternating** if an exchange of any two arguments changes its sign. An alternating covariant $k$-tensor is a $k$-form; its space is written $\Lambda^k V^*$. For $k=1$, every covector is alternating because there is no pair of arguments to exchange. By convention, $\Lambda^0V^*=\mathbb R$: a zero-form is a scalar, with no vector arguments.

If two arguments of an alternating map are equal, an exchange leaves the inputs unchanged but changes the sign of the output. Over the real numbers this gives

$$
\omega(v,v,v_3,\ldots,v_k)=-\omega(v,v,v_3,\ldots,v_k)=0.
$$

More generally, an alternating form vanishes on any linearly dependent list. Express one vector as a combination of the others, use multilinearity, and observe that every resulting term has a repeated argument. Consequently every $k$-form is zero when $k>n$.

## Components and their count

Define components by $\omega_{i_1\cdots i_k}=\omega(e_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_k})$. Repeated indices give zero; a permutation of distinct indices changes the component by the sign of that permutation. It is therefore sufficient to specify components for

$$
1\le i_1<i_2<\cdots<i_k\le n.
$$

There are $\binom nk$ such choices, so $\dim\Lambda^kV^*=\binom nk$. This is a count of independent components, rather than the $n^k$ entries in an unrestricted component array. In dimension four, the counts for degrees zero through four are $1,4,6,4,1$. The total dimension of the exterior algebra is $2^4=16$.

The basis elements are written $e^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_k}$. Their evaluation is the determinant of the matrix whose $(a,b)$ entry is $e^{i_a}(v_b)$. The multiplication symbol is developed in [[23.02 Wedge products]]. For two arguments,

$$
(e^1\wedge e^2)(u,v)=u^1v^2-u^2v^1.
$$

This measures an oriented coordinate area. It uses only the first two coordinates and does not require a length or angle on $V$.

## Worked example: reconstruct a two-form

In $\mathbb R^3$, suppose the independent components are $\omega_{12}=2$, $\omega_{13}=-1$, and $\omega_{23}=3$. Then

$$
\omega=2e^1\wedge e^2-e^1\wedge e^3+3e^2\wedge e^3.
$$

For $u=(1,2,0)$ and $v=(0,1,1)$, evaluate each determinant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega(u,v)
&=2(1\cdot1-2\cdot0)-(1\cdot1-0\cdot0)
  +3(2\cdot1-0\cdot1)\\
&=2-1+6=7.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $\omega(v,u)=-7$ without a second full calculation. The component $\omega_{31}=1$ also follows from $\omega_{13}=-1$; it is not a fourth independent value.

On a smooth manifold $M$, a differential $k$-form chooses such an object in $\Lambda^kT_p^*M$ at each point $p$, with coefficients that vary smoothly in each coordinate chart. Here $T_pM$ is the tangent space. A differential zero-form is therefore a smooth real function. Smoothness concerns the coefficients; alternation concerns the vector arguments at one point.

## Practice

1. How many independent components does a three-form have in dimension five? What changes in dimension two?
2. Let $\alpha=2e^1-e^2$ on $\mathbb R^2$. Evaluate $\alpha(3e_1+4e_2)$. Can a nonzero two-form have the same formula as $\alpha$?

### Worked answers

1. Choose three distinct indices from five: $\binom53=10$. All other component entries follow by signs or are zero. In dimension two every list of three vectors is dependent, so every three-form is zero.
2. Linearity gives $\alpha(3e_1+4e_2)=2(3)-4=2$. This is a one-form because it takes one vector. A two-form must take two vectors and alternate in those arguments; the number of arguments is part of its definition, independent of its coefficients.
