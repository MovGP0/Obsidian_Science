---
title: "Integration of differential forms"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Integrate a form by pulling it back to oriented parameters, then integrate its coefficient with the sign fixed by the parameter order.

## What is integrated

An orientation of an $m$-dimensional real vector space chooses one of the two classes of ordered bases, where two bases are equivalent if their change-of-basis determinant is positive. An oriented manifold makes this choice consistently in its tangent spaces. An $m$-form can then be integrated on an oriented $m$-dimensional region. The degree must equal the dimension of the region of integration.

Let $\phi:D\subseteq\mathbb R^m\to M$ be a smooth parameterization of an oriented piece of $M$. Assume it is regular and one-to-one on its interior, and respects the chosen orientation. If

$$
\phi^*\omega=g(u^1,\ldots,u^m)\,du^1\wedge\cdots\wedge du^m,
$$

define

$$
\int_{\phi(D)}\omega=\int_D g(u^1,\ldots,u^m)\,du^1\cdots du^m.
$$

The right side is an ordinary multiple integral. The wedge on the left-hand expression records the parameter order; the multiple-integral notation on the right uses the ordinary positive coordinate measure on $D$. If $\phi$ reverses the chosen orientation, insert a minus sign. Several charts can be combined with a partition of unity. In the elementary examples below a single parameterization is sufficient, apart from boundary sets of measure zero.

## Orientation and change of parameters

For a curve $c:[a,b]\to M$ and a one-form $\alpha$, the pullback is

$$
c^*\alpha=\alpha_{c(t)}(c'(t))dt.
$$

Reversing the traversal replaces the integral by its negative. Similarly, a change of surface parameters multiplies the coefficient of a two-form by the signed Jacobian determinant. In an integral of a differential form, its sign must remain visible. The absolute Jacobian used for scalar area or volume integrals belongs to a different convention: it does not record orientation.

For an orientation-preserving reparameterization $h:E\to D$, the pullback identity $(\phi\circ h)^*=h^*\phi^*$ and ordinary change of variables show that the integral is unchanged. For an orientation-reversing $h$, the signed determinant is negative, which gives the required reversal. See [[23.05 Pullbacks]] for the substitution rule.

## Worked example: integrate on a triangle

Let $T$ be the triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, and $(0,1)$, oriented by $dx\wedge dy$. Integrate $\omega=(x+y)dx\wedge dy$. The parameter domain is

$$
D=\{(u,v):0\le u\le1,\ 0\le v\le1-u\},
\qquad \phi(u,v)=(u,v).
$$

The parameterization preserves orientation and $\phi^*\omega=(u+v)du\wedge dv$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_T\omega
&=\int_0^1\int_0^{1-u}(u+v)\,dv\,du\\
&=\int_0^1\left[u(1-u)+\tfrac12(1-u)^2\right]du\\
&=\int_0^1\tfrac12(1-u^2)du=\tfrac13.
\end{aligned}
$$

Now use $\psi(u,v)=(v,u)$ on the same triangular domain. Its derivative determinant is $-1$, so $\psi^*\omega=-(u+v)du\wedge dv$. The parameter integral is $-1/3$ and describes the opposite orientation. To compute the original oriented integral using this chart, negate that parameter integral.

## A surface in three dimensions

Let $S$ be the graph parameterized by $\phi(u,v)=(u,v,u+v)$ for $0\le u,v\le1$, oriented by the ordered tangent pair $(\partial_u\phi,\partial_v\phi)$. For $\eta=z\,dx\wedge dy$,

$$
\phi^*\eta=(u+v)du\wedge dv,
\qquad \int_S\eta=\int_0^1\int_0^1(u+v)\,dv\,du=1.
$$

No separate surface-area factor is needed: the two-form itself has been pulled back. Introducing a square-root area factor here would change the problem. Forms encode their dependence on tangent directions through their arguments.

## Practice

1. Integrate $\alpha=x\,dy$ on $c(t)=(t,t^2)$ for $0\le t\le1$, and then on the reversed curve.
2. Parameterize the annulus $1\le x^2+y^2\le4$ by polar coordinates and integrate $dx\wedge dy$ with its usual orientation.

### Worked answers

1. The pullback is $t\,d(t^2)=2t^2dt$. Its integral is $[2t^3/3]_0^1=2/3$. Reversing the curve changes the sign to $-2/3$ because its tangent direction is reversed.
2. Use $\phi(r,\theta)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)$ with $1\le r\le2$ and $0\le\theta\le2\pi$. The seam is counted only on a measure-zero boundary. Since $\phi^*(dx\wedge dy)=r\,dr\wedge d\theta$ and $r>0$, orientation is preserved. The integral is $\int_0^{2\pi}\int_1^2r\,dr\,d\theta=3\pi$.
