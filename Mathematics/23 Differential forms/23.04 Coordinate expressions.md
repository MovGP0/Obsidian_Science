> [!TLDR]
> Increasing-index expressions list each independent component once; full-index expressions need factorial factors to remove repeated copies.

## Two component conventions

In coordinates $x^1,\ldots,x^n$, the vectors $\partial_i=\partial/\partial x^i$ form a tangent basis and $dx^i$ is its dual basis. For a $k$-form $\omega$, define the tensor components by

$$
\omega_{i_1\cdots i_k}=\omega(\partial_{i_1},\ldots,\partial_{i_k}).
$$

Then two equivalent expansions are

$$
\omega=\sum_{i_1<\cdots<i_k}\omega_{i_1\cdots i_k}
dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_k}
=\frac1{k!}\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_k=1}^n
\omega_{i_1\cdots i_k}dx^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_k}.
$$

The first sum uses only increasing indices. In the full sum, each set of $k$ distinct indices occurs $k!$ times. A permutation changes the sign of both the coefficient and the basis wedge, so the resulting terms are equal. Dividing by $k!$ removes this repetition. Terms with repeated indices vanish.

For a two-form in three dimensions, this distinction is

$$
\omega=\omega_{12}dx^1\wedge dx^2+
\omega_{13}dx^1\wedge dx^3+\omega_{23}dx^2\wedge dx^3
=\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\omega_{ij}dx^i\wedge dx^j.
$$

Do not insert another factor of $1/2$ into the increasing-index expression. The convention is attached to the summation range, rather than to the fact that the object has degree two.

## Evaluation and derivatives

If $u=u^i\partial_i$ and $v=v^j\partial_j$, a two-form evaluates as

$$
\omega(u,v)=\sum_{i,j}\omega_{ij}u^iv^j.
$$

The factor $1/2$ is absent here. To derive this, insert $(dx^i\wedge dx^j)(u,v)=u^iv^j-v^iu^j$ into the full expansion. Exchange $i,j$ in the second term and use $\omega_{ji}=-\omega_{ij}$. The two halves add.

For a $k$-form, the exterior derivative has components

$$
(d\omega)_{i_0\cdots i_k}
=\sum_{r=0}^k(-1)^r\partial_{i_r}
\omega_{i_0\cdots\widehat{i_r}\cdots i_k}.
$$

The hat means that the indicated index is omitted. For a one-form $a=a_i dx^i$, this becomes $(da)_{ij}=\partial_i a_j-\partial_j a_i$. For a two-form,

$$
(d\omega)_{ijk}=\partial_i\omega_{jk}-\partial_j\omega_{ik}+\partial_k\omega_{ij}.
$$

These formulas follow by differentiating coefficients and moving the newly inserted $dx^{i_r}$ into its ordered position. The $r$ moves give the sign $(-1)^r$. See [[23.03 Exterior derivatives]] for the coefficient definition of $d$.

## Worked example: components, evaluation, and differentiation

Let

$$
\omega=x\,dy\wedge dz+z\,dx\wedge dy
$$

on $\mathbb R^3$, with $x^1=x$, $x^2=y$, $x^3=z$. The independent components are $\omega_{12}=z$, $\omega_{13}=0$, and $\omega_{23}=x$. The matrix of all components is

$$
(\omega_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}0&z&0\\-z&0&x\\0&-x&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

At $x=2$, $z=3$, take $u=(1,1,0)$ and $v=(0,1,2)$. The increasing-index expression gives

$$
\omega(u,v)=3(1\cdot1-1\cdot0)+2(1\cdot2-0\cdot1)=7.
$$

For the derivative, $(d\omega)_{123}=\partial_x(x)-\partial_y(0)+\partial_z(z)=2$. Thus $d\omega=2dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$. A full three-index expansion would require the factor $1/6$ and contain six signed component entries that combine to the same coefficient two.

Under a change $x=x(y)$, covectors satisfy $dx^i=\sum_a(\partial x^i/\partial y^a)dy^a$. Substitute this into each wedge to transform components. This procedure preserves the form and changes its numerical array. It also prevents the mistake of transforming covectors by the same matrix as tangent-vector components.

## Practice

1. If $\eta=4dx^1\wedge dx^3$, give $\eta_{13}$, $\eta_{31}$, and its full-index expansion.
2. For $a=y^2dx+xy\,dy$, calculate $(da)_{12}$ and then $da$.

### Worked answers

1. Evaluation gives $\eta_{13}=4$ and $\eta_{31}=-4$. Hence the full expression is $\tfrac12(4dx^1\wedge dx^3-4dx^3\wedge dx^1)$, which reduces to the given form after reordering.
2. Here $a_1=y^2$ and $a_2=xy$. Thus $(da)_{12}=\partial_x(xy)-\partial_y(y^2)=y-2y=-y$. The increasing-index expression is $da=-y\,dx\wedge dy$; no extra factor of one half is needed.
