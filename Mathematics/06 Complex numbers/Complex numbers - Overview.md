> [!TLDR]
> Represent complex numbers by magnitude and phase and use complex exponentials to describe rotations and periodic functions.

## Scope

Complex numbers extend real arithmetic without losing the usual algebraic rules. Their rectangular and polar forms provide complementary ways to understand multiplication, roots, and rotation.

## Starting knowledge

Use powers and quadratic equations from [[Algebra - Overview]], function composition from [[Functions - Overview]], and radians and the unit circle from [[Geometry and trigonometry - Overview]].

## Topics to cover

- Complex arithmetic: the imaginary unit, real and imaginary parts, addition, multiplication, and division.
- The complex plane: points, vectors, distances, and the geometric meaning of addition.
- Conjugation and modulus: calculate magnitudes, reciprocals, and products using conjugates.
- Polar representation: magnitude, argument, and the non-uniqueness of phase modulo a full turn.
- Euler’s formula: connect the complex exponential to sine and cosine.
- Rotations and products: add phases and multiply magnitudes, including the geometry of powers.
- Roots: find all roots of a nonzero complex number and describe the roots of unity.
- Complex-valued functions: distinguish their real part, imaginary part, magnitude, and phase as separate functions.

## Learning outcomes

After studying this topic, you should be able to:

- Convert a nonzero complex number between rectangular and polar forms.
- Explain complex multiplication as a rotation and rescaling, then calculate a set of roots.
- Show that adding a full turn to a phase leaves its complex exponential unchanged.
