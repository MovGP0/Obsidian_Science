> [!TLDR]
> Row reduction preserves a linear system’s solutions and shows whether there is no solution, one solution, or a family with free parameters.

## The matrix equation

A linear system with $m$ equations and $n$ unknowns has the form $Ax=b$, where $A\in\mathbb F^{m\times n}$, $x\in\mathbb F^n$ is the unknown column, and $b\in\mathbb F^m$ is specified. Work over $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$. The **augmented matrix** $[A\mid b]$ places the right side beside the coefficient matrix.

Three elementary row operations preserve the solution set: interchange two rows; multiply one row by a nonzero scalar; add a scalar multiple of one row to a different row. Each operation is reversible. For the third operation, subtract the same multiple to recover the original equation. Multiplication by zero is excluded because it can destroy a constraint.

## Pivots and free variables

In row echelon form, zero rows are at the bottom and each leading nonzero entry, or **pivot**, is to the right of the pivot above it. Entries below pivots are zero. In reduced row echelon form, pivots equal one and are the only nonzero entries in their columns.

A pivot in the augmented column produces a row $[0\ \cdots\ 0\mid c]$ with $c\ne0$. This asserts $0=c$, so the system is **inconsistent**. Otherwise it is consistent. A pivot column of $A$ determines a leading variable. Each other column gives a free variable that can take any value in $\mathbb F$.

For a consistent system of rank $r$, there are $n-r$ free variables. There is a unique solution exactly when $r=n$. Over $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, one or more free variables give infinitely many solutions. The number of equations alone does not establish uniqueness, because equations can repeat the same constraint.

## Worked example: a family of solutions

Solve

$$
\begin{aligned}
x+y+z&=2,\\
2x+3y+z&=5,\\
3x+4y+2z&=7.
\end{aligned}
$$

Begin with the augmented matrix and subtract multiples of its first row:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&\mid&2\\
2&3&1&\mid&5\\
3&4&2&\mid&7
\end{pmatrix}
\xrightarrow{R_2-2R_1,\ R_3-3R_1}
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&\mid&2\\
0&1&-1&\mid&1\\
0&1&-1&\mid&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Now use $R_3\leftarrow R_3-R_2$ and then $R_1\leftarrow R_1-R_2$:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&2&\mid&1\\
0&1&-1&\mid&1\\
0&0&0&\mid&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The coefficient rank is two. The third variable is free, so put $z=t$. The first two rows give $x=1-2t$ and $y=1+t$. Thus

$$
x_{\rm vector}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}
+t\begin{pmatrix}-2\\1\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad t\in\mathbb R.
$$

Substitute into the second original equation: $2(1-2t)+3(1+t)+t=5$. The $t$ terms cancel, which checks the parametrization. The third equation was the sum of the first two, so it adds no independent condition.

## Why every family is a translated kernel

If $x_p$ is one solution and $h\in\ker A$, then $A(x_p+h)=b+0=b$. Conversely, if $x$ is any solution, $A(x-x_p)=b-b=0$. Hence

$$
\{x:Ax=b\}=x_p+\ker A
$$

whenever a solution exists. A homogeneous system, where $b=0$, always contains zero and has a subspace as its solution set. An inhomogeneous system generally gives an affine subspace. This is the map viewpoint of [[10.02 Linear maps]].

Consistency can also be expressed as $b\in\operatorname{im}A$ or as $\operatorname{rank}[A\mid b]=\operatorname{rank}A$. In exact arithmetic these conditions agree. With decimal data, a very small pivot requires a numerical tolerance and can make a computed rank sensitive to round-off. Do not treat a rounded decimal zero as an exact theorem about the original data.

## Practice

1. Classify $x+2y=3$, $2x+4y=7$.
2. Solve $x+y=3$, $2x-y=0$, and explain why the solution is unique.

## Worked answers

1. Subtract twice the first equation from the second. The left side becomes zero and the right side becomes $7-6=1$. The contradiction $0=1$ proves inconsistency. Although the coefficient rank is one, the augmented rank is two.
2. Add the equations to obtain $3x=3$, so $x=1$. Substitution into the first equation gives $y=2$. Equivalently, eliminating $2x$ from the second row gives $-3y=-6$, a nonzero second pivot. Both variable columns have pivots, so there is no free parameter and the solution is unique.
