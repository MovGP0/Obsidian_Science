---
title: "Reading components from curvature forms"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Evaluate a curvature two-form on an ordered pair of frame vectors to read the corresponding Riemann component without a factor-of-two error.

## Specify the four slots

Fix a frame $e_a$ with dual coframe $\theta^a$, and use the convention

$$
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.
$$

Define the Riemann tensor components by

$$
R(e_c,e_d)e_b=R^a{}_{bcd}e_a.
$$

The first index gives the output vector component, the second names the vector acted on, and the last two name the ordered differentiation directions. By definition, the curvature form $\Omega^a{}_b$ satisfies

$$
R^a{}_{bcd}=\Omega^a{}_b(e_c,e_d).
$$

Every curvature two-form therefore holds the last two indices in its arguments. Antisymmetry gives $R^a{}_{bdc}=-R^a{}_{bcd}$. Some references use another placement of the four indices or the opposite sign for $R$. Compare defining equations before comparing a component list.

## Where the factor one-half comes from

The wedge product is normalized by

$$
(\theta^c\wedge\theta^d)(e_i,e_j)
=\delta^c_i\delta^d_j-\delta^c_j\delta^d_i.
$$

If all ordered pairs $c,d$ are summed, expansion of the two-form gives

$$
\Omega^a{}_b=\tfrac12\sum_{c,d}R^a{}_{bcd}\,
\theta^c\wedge\theta^d.
$$

Evaluate this expression on $(e_i,e_j)$. The first Kronecker term contributes $R^a{}_{bij}/2$ and the second contributes $-R^a{}_{bji}/2$. Antisymmetry makes the latter equal the former, so their sum is $R^a{}_{bij}$. Thus the factor compensates for the duplicate ordered pairs; it is not an extra factor in the value obtained by evaluation.

Equivalently, use only increasing pairs:

$$
\Omega^a{}_b=\sum_{c<d}R^a{}_{bcd}\,
\theta^c\wedge\theta^d.
$$

For instance, if $\Omega^1{}_2=A\theta^1\wedge\theta^2+B\theta^2\wedge\theta^3$, then $R^1{}_{212}=A$, $R^1{}_{221}=-A$, $R^1{}_{223}=B$, and $R^1{}_{232}=-B$. The coefficients are $A,B$, not $2A,2B$.

## Worked example: the complete surface tensor

For a positive definite surface with an orthonormal frame, suppose Cartan’s calculation gives

$$
\Omega^1{}_2=K\theta^1\wedge\theta^2,
\qquad\Omega^2{}_1=-K\theta^1\wedge\theta^2,
\qquad\Omega^1{}_1=\Omega^2{}_2=0.
$$

Then evaluation gives the four possibly nonzero mixed components:

$$
R^1{}_{212}=K,\quad R^1{}_{221}=-K,
\qquad R^2{}_{112}=-K,\quad R^2{}_{121}=K.
$$

To check their geometric meaning, use the ordered pair $(e_1,e_2)$. The formulas give $R(e_1,e_2)e_2=Ke_1$ and $R(e_1,e_2)e_1=-Ke_2$. Therefore $g(R(e_1,e_2)e_2,e_1)=K$, the Gaussian curvature for this convention.

For $g=dr^2+\sin^2r\,d\phi^2$ on $0<r<\pi$, the coframe $dr$, $\sin r\,d\phi$ gives $\Omega^1{}_2=\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$. Thus $K=1$. In coordinate vectors, $\partial_\phi=\sin r\,e_2$ and $\partial_r=e_1$, so tensoriality yields

$$
R(\partial_r,\partial_\phi)\partial_\phi
=\sin^2r\,\partial_r.
$$

Hence the coordinate component $R^r{}_{\phi r\phi}$ is $\sin^2r$, even though the corresponding orthonormal component is one. The curvature tensor is the same; the components refer to different bases.

## Lowering indices and checking symmetry

In a frame with metric matrix $G$, define $R_{abcd}=G_{ae}R^e{}_{bcd}$. For a Levi-Civita connection,

$$
R_{abcd}=-R_{bacd}=-R_{abdc},\qquad
R_{abcd}=R_{cdab}.
$$

The first symmetry follows from metric compatibility, the second from the two-form arguments, and pair exchange also uses the torsion-free condition. In a positive definite orthonormal frame, lowering the first index does not change its numerical value. In indefinite signature it can change a sign. Never lower an index by simply deleting its raised position.

## Practice

1. If $\Omega^2{}_3=5\theta^1\wedge\theta^3-2\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$, find $R^2{}_{313}$, $R^2{}_{331}$, and $\Omega^2{}_3(e_1+e_2,e_3)$.
2. Expand $\Omega^1{}_2=7\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$ as a sum over all ordered pairs, including the factor $1/2$.

## Worked answers

1. Evaluation on $(e_1,e_3)$ gives $R^2{}_{313}=5$. Reversing the arguments gives $R^2{}_{331}=-5$. Bilinearity gives $\Omega^2{}_3(e_1+e_2,e_3)=\Omega^2{}_3(e_1,e_3)+\Omega^2{}_3(e_2,e_3)=5+0=5$. The coefficient $-2$ contributes only when the argument pair has an $(e_1,e_2)$ part.
2. The nonzero terms are $\frac12[7\theta^1\wedge\theta^2+(-7)\theta^2\wedge\theta^1]$. Since $\theta^2\wedge\theta^1=-\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$, the two terms add to $7\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$. [[24.08 Contractions]] uses these component conventions to compute Ricci curvature.
