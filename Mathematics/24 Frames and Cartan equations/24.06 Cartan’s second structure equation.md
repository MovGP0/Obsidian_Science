> [!TLDR]
> Curvature forms equal the exterior derivative of the connection matrix plus its matrix-valued wedge square.

## Define the curvature convention

For a connection $\nabla$, use

$$
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.
$$

The order of $X,Y$ fixes the sign. Let $e_a$ be a local frame and $\theta^a$ its dual. With $\nabla_Xe_b=\omega^a{}_b(X)e_a$, define the curvature two-forms by

$$
R(X,Y)e_b=\Omega^a{}_b(X,Y)e_a.
$$

Each entry of $\Omega$ is a two-form, rather than a scalar function. It accepts the two directions in which the covariant derivatives are compared. Curvature is tensorial even though the connection coefficients depend on the derivative of the chosen frame.

## Derive the equation

Expand the first double derivative:

$$
\nabla_X\nabla_Ye_b
=\left[X\bigl(\omega^a{}_b(Y)\bigr)
+\omega^a{}_c(X)\omega^c{}_b(Y)\right]e_a.
$$

Subtract the expression with $X,Y$ exchanged, then subtract $\nabla_{[X,Y]}e_b$. The terms with derivatives combine to $d\omega^a{}_b(X,Y)$. The remaining products are the wedge product evaluated on $(X,Y)$. Thus

$$
\Omega^a{}_b=d\omega^a{}_b+\omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b,
\qquad \Omega=d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega.
$$

For form-valued matrices, multiplication means matrix multiplication with wedge products in each entry. In particular, $(\omega\wedge\omega)^a{}_b=\sum_c\omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b$. It does not mean taking the wedge of each entry with itself. Distinct entries can produce nonzero products. The identity holds with or without torsion. These conventions agree, after reordering the one-form factors, with the derivation in [On Cartan Structure Equations, Nagoya University](https://www.math.nagoya-u.ac.jp/~richard/teaching/f2024/SML_Rifqi_5.pdf).

## Worked example: curvature from one function

For the positive definite metric $g=dr^2+f(r)^2d\phi^2$, assume $f>0$. Use the orthonormal coframe $\theta^1=dr$, $\theta^2=f\,d\phi$. The torsion-free calculation gives $\omega^1{}_2=-f'\,d\phi$, $\omega^2{}_1=f'\,d\phi$, with zero diagonal entries.

There are no off-diagonal product terms in dimension two for this matrix. Therefore

$$
\Omega^1{}_2=d(-f'\,d\phi)=-f''\,dr\wedge d\phi
=-\frac{f''}{f}\theta^1\wedge\theta^2.
$$

Also $\Omega^2{}_1=-\Omega^1{}_2$. The diagonal forms vanish because each possible product contains $d\phi\wedge d\phi=0$. Define Gaussian curvature by $K=g(R(e_1,e_2)e_2,e_1)$. Evaluation of the last display gives

$$
K=-\frac{f''}{f}.
$$

If $f=r$, then $f''=0$ and $K=0$. If $f=\sin r$ on $0<r<\pi$, then $f''=-f$ and $K=1$. If $f=\sinh r$ on $r>0$, then $f''=f$ and $K=-1$. These results follow from derivatives, not from an interpretation of the drawing.

![[math-24-warp-function-curvature.svg|**Fig. 24.2** Three positive scale functions on 0 < r < 2. For the metric dr² + f(r)²dφ², their second derivatives give zero, positive, and negative Gaussian curvature through K = −f″/f.]]

The graph shows why the sign changes: for positive $f$, downward bending gives positive $K$, while upward bending gives negative $K$. The value of $f'$ alone does not determine curvature.

## Why the wedge square matters

In Euclidean three-space, use spherical coordinates on $r>0$, $0<\vartheta<\pi$. An orthonormal coframe is $dr$, $r\,d\vartheta$, $r\sin\vartheta\,d\phi$. Its independent connection forms are

$$
\omega^1{}_2=-d\vartheta,\qquad
\omega^1{}_3=-\sin\vartheta\,d\phi,\qquad
\omega^2{}_3=-\cos\vartheta\,d\phi.
$$

For the $(1,3)$ entry,

$$
\Omega^1{}_3=-\cos\vartheta\,d\vartheta\wedge d\phi
+(-d\vartheta)\wedge(-\cos\vartheta\,d\phi)=0.
$$

Discarding the product term would falsely assign curvature to Euclidean space. This cancellation is a useful check on matrix order and signs.

## Practice

1. Compute all independent curvature forms for $g=dx^2+e^{2x}dy^2$.
2. For $f(r)=r^2$ on $r>0$, find $K$ and explain why it is not constant.

## Worked answers

1. With $\theta^1=dx$, $\theta^2=e^xdy$, the connection form is $\omega^1{}_2=-e^xdy$. Hence $\Omega^1{}_2=-e^x dx\wedge dy=-\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$. The other off-diagonal form is its negative, and the diagonal forms vanish. Thus $K=-1$ everywhere.
2. The derivatives are $f'=2r$, $f''=2$. Therefore $\Omega^1{}_2=-2\,dr\wedge d\phi=-(2/r^2)\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$, so $K=-2/r^2$. Its value varies with $r$; negative sign alone does not imply constant negative curvature. [[24.07 Reading components]] explains how to recover the full tensor from these forms.
