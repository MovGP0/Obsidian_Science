---
title: "Cartan’s first structure equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The first structure equation expresses torsion in a coframe and determines the Levi-Civita connection when metric compatibility is also imposed.

## Define torsion and its forms

Let $e_a$ be a local frame with dual coframe $\theta^a$. Define the connection forms by $\nabla_Xe_b=\omega^a{}_b(X)e_a$. The **torsion** of this connection is

$$
T(X,Y)=\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX-[X,Y].
$$

The product rules for a connection and a Lie bracket cancel derivatives of multiplying functions. Hence $T$ is linear over smooth functions in both arguments: it is a vector-valued two-form. Its frame components are the ordinary two-forms $T^a(X,Y)=\theta^a(T(X,Y))$.

Write $X=x^be_b$ and $Y=y^be_b$. Expanding the covariant derivatives yields

$$
T^a(X,Y)=X(y^a)-Y(x^a)-\theta^a([X,Y])
+\omega^a{}_b(X)y^b-\omega^a{}_b(Y)x^b.
$$

The first three terms equal $d\theta^a(X,Y)$ by the definition of the exterior derivative. The last two terms are $(\omega^a{}_b\wedge\theta^b)(X,Y)$. Thus **Cartan’s first structure equation** is

$$
T^a=d\theta^a+\omega^a{}_b\wedge\theta^b.
$$

This identity holds for every linear connection. A connection is torsion free exactly when every right-hand side vanishes. In an orthonormal frame, the Levi-Civita connection satisfies this equation together with $\omega_{ab}=-\omega_{ba}$. The coframe alone does not determine a unique connection unless these metric and torsion requirements are included.

## Worked example: solve a two-dimensional family

Consider $g=dr^2+f(r)^2d\phi^2$ on a region where the smooth function $f$ is positive. Choose

$$
\theta^1=dr,\qquad\theta^2=f\,d\phi.
$$

The metric is positive definite and the coframe is orthonormal. Its exterior derivatives are

$$
d\theta^1=0,\qquad
d\theta^2=f'\,dr\wedge d\phi
=\frac{f'}{f}\theta^1\wedge\theta^2.
$$

Metric compatibility makes the diagonal connection forms zero and gives $\omega^2{}_1=-\omega^1{}_2$. Write the remaining unknown as

$$
\omega^1{}_2=A\theta^1+B\theta^2.
$$

The first torsion equation becomes $0=\omega^1{}_2\wedge\theta^2=A\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$, so $A=0$. The second becomes

$$
0=\frac{f'}{f}\theta^1\wedge\theta^2
-B\theta^2\wedge\theta^1
=\left(\frac{f'}{f}+B\right)\theta^1\wedge\theta^2.
$$

Therefore $B=-f'/f$, and the complete answer is

$$
\omega^1{}_2=-\frac{f'}{f}\theta^2=-f'\,d\phi,
\qquad\omega^2{}_1=f'\,d\phi.
$$

Substitute both forms back into both torsion equations to check the signs. For $f=\sin r$ on $0<r<\pi$, the answer is $\omega^1{}_2=-\cos r\,d\phi$. For $f=r$ on $r>0$, it is $-d\phi$. The second case has a nonzero connection form although the Euclidean metric is flat. Curvature needs the second structure equation.

## A connection with different torsion

On the same region one could instead define a connection by declaring $\nabla_Xe_a=0$ for all $X$. Its matrix $\omega$ is zero in this frame. Since the frame metric is constant, this connection is metric compatible. But its torsion is $T^1=0$ and $T^2=(f'/f)\theta^1\wedge\theta^2$. It is not the Levi-Civita connection when $f'\ne0$. This example shows why $d\theta^a$ alone cannot be called torsion: torsion depends on a chosen connection.

## Limits and checks

Do not discard a coefficient merely because one wedge product vanishes: the equation for another row can determine it. Also, $f=0$ makes the displayed coframe singular. A smooth metric can still extend across an omitted point, as in ordinary polar coordinates, but this frame calculation must remain inside its stated domain. See [[24.03 Non-coordinate frames]] for the structure functions and [[24.06 Cartan’s second structure equation]] for the next calculation.

## Practice

1. Find the Levi-Civita connection forms for $g=dx^2+e^{2x}dy^2$ in $\theta^1=dx$, $\theta^2=e^xdy$.
2. For the polar coframe $\theta^1=dr$, $\theta^2=r\,d\phi$, choose $\omega^1{}_2=-\lambda d\phi$, $\omega^2{}_1=\lambda d\phi$, with constant $\lambda$. Compute the torsion and find the torsion-free value.

## Worked answers

1. Here $f=e^x$ and $f'/f=1$. The calculation above gives $\omega^1{}_2=-\theta^2=-e^xdy$, $\omega^2{}_1=\theta^2$. Substitution gives $T^2=\theta^1\wedge\theta^2+\theta^2\wedge\theta^1=0$, while $T^1=0$ because $\theta^2\wedge\theta^2=0$.
2. The first torsion form is $T^1=-\lambda d\phi\wedge r\,d\phi=0$. The second is $T^2=dr\wedge d\phi+\lambda d\phi\wedge dr=(1-\lambda)dr\wedge d\phi$. It vanishes exactly when $\lambda=1$.
