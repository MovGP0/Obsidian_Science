title: "Connection one-forms"

> [!TLDR]
> Connection one-forms record the derivatives of a frame, and metric compatibility constrains their matrix.

## Fix the index convention first

Let $\nabla$ be a linear connection on the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold. For a local frame $e_a$ with dual coframe $\theta^a$, define one-forms $\omega^a{}_b$ by

$$
\nabla_Xe_b=\omega^a{}_b(X)e_a.
$$

The lower index $b$ names the vector being differentiated; the upper index $a$ names the output component. The argument $X$ is the differentiation direction. Each coefficient is linear over smooth functions in $X$, so it is a one-form. We put these one-forms in a matrix $\omega=(\omega^a{}_b)$, with $a$ the row index and $b$ the column index.

If $\nabla_{e_c}e_b=\Gamma^a{}_{bc}e_a$, our coefficient convention is

$$
\omega^a{}_b=\Gamma^a{}_{bc}\theta^c,\qquad
\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=\omega^a{}_b(e_c).
$$

Other texts can put the direction index first in $\Gamma$. The defining covariant derivative removes that ambiguity. Repeated indices are summed.

## Differentiating a vector with changing components

For $V=v^be_b$, the product rule gives

$$
\nabla_XV=X(v^a)e_a+v^b\nabla_Xe_b
=\bigl[X(v^a)+\omega^a{}_b(X)v^b\bigr]e_a.
$$

The first term differentiates the component functions. The second accounts for the changing basis. With the component column $v$, the expression is $X(v)+\omega(X)v$. This explains why a vector with constant frame components need not be parallel. Conversely, a parallel vector can need changing components to compensate for frame rotation.

The induced connection on the dual basis has the opposite sign:

$$
\nabla_X\theta^a=-\omega^a{}_b(X)\theta^b.
$$

To verify this, differentiate the constant pairing $\theta^a(e_b)=\delta^a_b$. The derivative of the form acting on $e_b$ must cancel $\theta^a(\nabla_Xe_b)$.

## Metric compatibility

Let $G_{ab}=g(e_a,e_b)$ be the metric matrix in the chosen frame. A connection is **metric compatible** if

$$
X(g(U,V))=g(\nabla_XU,V)+g(U,\nabla_XV)
$$

for all vector fields. Apply this to frame vectors. It gives

$$
dG_{ab}=G_{cb}\omega^c{}_a+G_{ac}\omega^c{}_b,
\qquad dG=\omega^T G+G\omega.
$$

In an orthonormal frame $G=\eta$ is constant. Define $\omega_{ab}=\eta_{ac}\omega^c{}_b$. Then

$$
\omega_{ab}=-\omega_{ba},\qquad
\omega^T\eta+\eta\omega=0.
$$

For a positive definite orthonormal frame, the original matrix $\omega$ is skew-symmetric. In indefinite signature, skew-symmetry applies after lowering the first index. For $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$, the off-diagonal mixed forms obey $\omega^1{}_2=\omega^2{}_1$, rather than opposite signs. Metric compatibility alone does not impose zero torsion.

## Worked example: rotating polar directions

In the Euclidean plane on $r>0$, choose

$$
e_1=\cos\phi\,\partial_x+\sin\phi\,\partial_y,
\qquad e_2=-\sin\phi\,\partial_x+\cos\phi\,\partial_y.
$$

The Cartesian basis has zero covariant derivatives for the Euclidean connection. Differentiate only the coefficient functions:

$$
\nabla_Xe_1=X(\phi)e_2,\qquad
\nabla_Xe_2=-X(\phi)e_1.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\omega=\begin{pmatrix}0&-d\phi\\d\phi&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The dual coframe is $\theta^1=dr$, $\theta^2=r\,d\phi$. Since $d\phi(e_2)=1/r$, the derivatives include $\nabla_{e_2}e_1=r^{-1}e_2$ and $\nabla_{e_2}e_2=-r^{-1}e_1$. In contrast, $d\phi(e_1)=0$, so $\nabla_{e_1}e_1=\nabla_{e_1}e_2=0$.

Take $V=re_1$. In direction $e_2$, the component derivative vanishes because $e_2(r)=0$, but the basis contribution gives $\nabla_{e_2}V=r(r^{-1}e_2)=e_2$. In direction $e_1$, differentiating the component gives $\nabla_{e_1}V=e_1$. Both agree with the Cartesian expression $V=x\partial_x+y\partial_y$.

## Practice

1. For a positive definite orthonormal frame in dimension three, how many independent one-form entries can a metric compatible connection matrix have? Write its general form.
2. In the polar example, compute $\nabla_{e_2}(2e_1+re_2)$, including derivatives of its components.

## Worked answers

1. The diagonal entries vanish and each pair of opposite off-diagonal entries has one independent choice. There are $3(3-1)/2=3$ choices. With arbitrary one-forms $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$, the matrix is $\begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha&\beta\\-\alpha&0&\gamma\\-\beta&-\gamma&0\end{pmatrix}$. Additional torsion conditions can constrain these forms.
2. Both $e_2(2)$ and $e_2(r)$ vanish. The basis terms give $2\nabla_{e_2}e_1+r\nabla_{e_2}e_2=(2/r)e_2-e_1$. A component-only derivative would incorrectly give zero. [[24.05 Cartan’s first structure equation]] uses the same connection matrix to compute torsion.
