---
title: "Ricci and scalar curvature in a frame"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Ricci curvature traces the output and first direction slots of the Riemann tensor; scalar curvature then takes the metric trace of Ricci.

## Fix the contraction convention

Let $R(e_c,e_d)e_b=R^a{}_{bcd}e_a$, where

$$
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.
$$

For fixed vectors $U,V$, define $\operatorname{Ric}(U,V)$ as the trace of the linear map $X\mapsto R(X,V)U$. In a frame this is

$$
\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}=R^a{}_{bad}.
$$

The repeated index contracts an upper slot with a lower slot. This trace needs no additional metric factor. If the Riemann tensor has already been lowered, write the same operation as $\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}=G^{ac}R_{abcd}$, where $G^{ac}$ is the inverse metric matrix. Do not insert another inverse metric into the mixed-index formula.

The **scalar curvature** is

$$
S=G^{bd}\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}.
$$

For an orthonormal frame with $G=\eta=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_n)$ and $\varepsilon_a=\pm1$, this becomes $S=\sum_a\varepsilon_a\operatorname{Ric}_{aa}$. Thus in indefinite signature the scalar is a signed trace. For a Levi-Civita connection, Ricci is symmetric.

## Worked example: a surface from Cartan forms

Take $g=dr^2+f(r)^2d\phi^2$ with $f>0$. In the orthonormal coframe $\theta^1=dr$, $\theta^2=f\,d\phi$, the curvature forms are

$$
\Omega^1{}_2=K\theta^1\wedge\theta^2,\qquad
\Omega^2{}_1=-K\theta^1\wedge\theta^2,
\qquad K=-\frac{f''}{f}.
$$

The diagonal forms vanish. Since $R^a{}_{bcd}=\Omega^a{}_b(e_c,e_d)$, contraction gives

$$
\operatorname{Ric}_{11}=R^2{}_{121}=K,
\qquad\operatorname{Ric}_{22}=R^1{}_{212}=K,
\qquad\operatorname{Ric}_{12}=0.
$$

For the first equality, both the minus sign in $\Omega^2{}_1$ and the reversed arguments $(e_2,e_1)$ occur, so they cancel. The Ricci tensor is $\operatorname{Ric}=Kg$ and the scalar is $S=2K$. Scalar curvature is twice Gaussian curvature on a surface with these conventions.

For $f=\sin r$ on $0<r<\pi$, this gives $\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}=\delta_{ab}$ and $S=2$. The coordinate Ricci matrix is not the identity because the coordinate frame is not orthonormal.

## Check with the coordinate method

For the same metric, the nonzero coordinate metric entries are $g_{rr}=1$, $g_{\phi\phi}=f^2$. The Christoffel formula

$$
\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12g^{\rho\sigma}
(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\nu}+\partial_\nu g_{\sigma\mu}
-\partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu})
$$

gives $\Gamma^r{}_{\phi\phi}=-ff'$ and $\Gamma^\phi{}_{r\phi}=\Gamma^\phi{}_{\phi r}=f'/f$. These denote the coefficients of $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu$. There are no $\phi$ derivatives of the metric.

The two nonzero Ricci entries follow directly:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Ric}_{rr}
&=-\partial_r\!\left(\frac{f'}f\right)-\left(\frac{f'}f\right)^2
=-\frac{f''}f,\\
\operatorname{Ric}_{\phi\phi}
&=\partial_r(-ff')-(-ff')\frac{f'}f
=-(f'^2+ff'')+f'^2=-ff''.
\end{aligned}
$$

These are the components of $Kg$, exactly as the frame method predicted. For $f=\sin r$, they are $1$ and $\sin^2r$. Taking the inverse-metric trace gives

$$
S=\operatorname{Ric}_{rr}+f^{-2}\operatorname{Ric}_{\phi\phi}
=-2\frac{f''}f=2K.
$$

Simply adding coordinate diagonal entries would give a position-dependent expression even for the unit sphere. A tensor trace must use the inverse metric unless the basis is positive definite orthonormal.

## Higher-dimensional check

For a metric of constant sectional curvature $K$ in dimension $n$, the components take the form

$$
R^a{}_{bcd}=K(\delta^a_cG_{bd}-\delta^a_dG_{bc}).
$$

Set $c=a$ and sum. The first term contributes $nKG_{bd}$; the second contributes $KG_{bd}$. Hence $\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}=(n-1)KG_{bd}$ and $S=n(n-1)K$. The metric trace of $G$ is $n$ even for indefinite signature. This formula is a consequence of constant sectional curvature, not a formula for every metric.

## Practice

1. For $g=dr^2+\sinh^2r\,d\phi^2$ on $r>0$, give Ricci in both frames and compute $S$.
2. A three-dimensional metric has constant sectional curvature $K=2$. Find its Ricci tensor and scalar. Repeat for $g=dr^2+9\sin^2(r/3)d\phi^2$ on $0<r<3\pi$.

## Worked answers

1. Since $f''=\sinh r=f$, $K=-1$. The orthonormal Ricci matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1)$. The coordinate matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(-1,-\sinh^2r)$ because $\operatorname{Ric}=-g$. Its inverse-metric trace is $-1+(\sinh r)^{-2}(-\sinh^2r)=-2$.
2. With $n=3$, $\operatorname{Ric}=4g$ and $S=3\cdot2\cdot2=12$. For the second metric, $f=3\sin(r/3)$, $f'=\cos(r/3)$, and $f''=-\frac13\sin(r/3)=-f/9$. Thus $K=1/9$, $\operatorname{Ric}=g/9$, and $S=2/9$. The dimensions differ, so the same trace multiplier cannot be used for both examples.
