title: "Transition functions"

> [!TLDR]
> Transition functions convert fibre coordinates between local bundle charts; their overlap equations make the resulting global object consistent.

## Fix a direction convention

Let $\pi:E\to B$ be a fibre bundle with model fibre $F$. Choose open sets $U_i$ covering $B$ and trivializations $\Phi_i:\pi^{-1}(U_i)\to U_i\times F$. On an overlap $U_i\cap U_j$, the same point $e\in E$ has coordinates $(b,f_i)$ and $(b,f_j)$. Adopt the convention

$$
\Phi_i\circ\Phi_j^{-1}(b,f_j)
=(b,g_{ij}(b)f_j),\qquad f_i=g_{ij}(b)f_j.
$$

Here $g_{ij}(b)$ is a transformation of the fibre. In a rank-$r$ real vector bundle it is an invertible $r\times r$ matrix and its action is matrix multiplication. More generally it belongs to a structure group $G$ that acts on $F$. For the following derivation, assume the action is faithful: distinct elements of $G$ induce distinct transformations of $F$. The matrix action above is faithful. Products below mean composition, with the right-hand transformation applied first. For general topological bundles, the coordinate-change map must be continuous as a map of $(b,f)$; common finite-dimensional structure groups encode this in continuous $g_{ij}$.

Apply the coordinate conversion twice on a triple overlap:

$$
f_i=g_{ij}f_j=g_{ij}g_{jk}f_k.
$$

Direct conversion from $k$ to $i$ gives $f_i=g_{ik}f_k$. Therefore compatibility requires

$$
g_{ii}=1,\qquad g_{ji}=g_{ij}^{-1},\qquad
g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}.
$$

The last equation is the **cocycle condition**. Faithfulness lets us infer equality of group elements from equality of their actions on every fibre coordinate. If a chosen structure group has a nonfaithful action, the group-valued cocycle equation must instead be imposed on the transition data; equality of the actions alone gives a weaker statement. The cocycle condition prevents a fibre coordinate from changing merely because a third chart was used. In a noncommutative group the order cannot be reversed.

## Glue products into a bundle

Conversely, start with copies $U_i\times F$ and suitable continuous $g_{ij}$ satisfying these equations. Identify

$$
(b,f_j)\text{ in chart }j
\sim (b,g_{ij}(b)f_j)\text{ in chart }i.
$$

The identity equation makes this relation reflexive, the inverse equation makes it symmetric, and the cocycle equation makes it transitive. With the usual quotient topology, these local products give a bundle in the standard setting of manifold bases and Lie-group actions. This construction explains why transition data carry global information.

A section is expressed by local functions $s_i:U_i\to F$ satisfying $s_i=g_{ij}s_j$ on overlaps. A collection that fails this equation represents different total-space points, not one section. See [[27.06 Bundle structure]] for the definition of a section.

## Worked example: line bundles around a circle

Cover a circle with two arcs $U$ and $V$, each slightly longer than a semicircle. Their overlap has two components, call them $A$ and $C$. For a real line fibre, set

$$
g_{UV}(b)=
\begin{cases}
+1,&b\in A,\\
-1,&b\in C.
\end{cases}
$$

This map is continuous because each overlap component is open in the overlap and the value is constant there. Its inverse is itself. A fibre coordinate returns with a sign reversal after one circuit. The resulting line bundle is the Möbius line bundle. If both signs were $+1$, the charts would instead join into the product line bundle.

![[math-27-bundle-gluing.svg|**Fig. 27.2** Equal seam arrows give a product strip; opposite seam arrows give a Möbius strip. The arrows describe the fibre-coordinate identification.]]

The strip is a picture of bounded fibre coordinates, such as $-1\leq v\leq1$; the full vector bundle uses all $v\in\mathbb R$. The twist is in the gluing rule, not in a local failure to look like a product.

Changing local fibre coordinates to $f'_i=h_i(b)f_i$ changes the transition matrices. Substitute $f_i=h_i^{-1}f'_i$ into $f_i=g_{ij}f_j$ to obtain

$$
f'_i=h_i g_{ij}h_j^{-1}f'_j,\qquad
g'_{ij}=h_i g_{ij}h_j^{-1}.
$$

Thus transition functions depend on the chosen charts. A nonconstant-looking transition function alone does not prove nontriviality: one must ask whether changes of local coordinates can make all transitions identity. For the Möbius line bundle they cannot, as the nonzero-section obstruction in [[27.08 Bundle types]] shows.

## Practice

1. On a triple overlap of a line bundle, suppose $g_{12}=2$ and $g_{23}=-3$. Find $g_{13}$ and $g_{31}$. Then find $g'_{12}$ when $h_1=4$ and $h_2=2$.
2. A proposed rank-two atlas has $g_{12}=A$, $g_{23}=B$, and $g_{13}=BA$. What additional condition is needed for compatibility?

## Worked answers

1. The cocycle equation gives $g_{13}=g_{12}g_{23}=-6$. The reverse conversion is its inverse, so $g_{31}=-1/6$. The new coordinate conversion is $g'_{12}=h_1g_{12}h_2^{-1}=4\cdot2\cdot(1/2)=4$. All values are nonzero, as required for invertible line maps.
2. The required equation is $g_{13}=AB$. The proposed $BA$ works only if $AB=BA$ on that overlap. For example, take $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$. Then $AB=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$ whereas $BA=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$. They differ, so those proposed charts fail compatibility.
