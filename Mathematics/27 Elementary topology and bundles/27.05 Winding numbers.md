title: "Winding numbers"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The winding number is the integer net turn of a closed path about an excluded point; it cannot change during a deformation that avoids that point.

## Lift an angle continuously

Let $\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$ be a continuous loop. Write $r(t)=|\gamma(t)|>0$ and $u(t)=\gamma(t)/r(t)\in S^1$. A continuous **lifted angle** is a continuous real function $\theta$ with

$$
u(t)=e^{i\theta(t)}.
$$

Such a lift exists on an interval once $\theta(0)$ is chosen. One construction divides the interval into finitely many pieces on which $u$ lies in an open arc with a continuous angle branch. Add integer multiples of $2\pi$ at joins so adjacent branches agree. Any two lifts differ by a constant multiple of $2\pi$, since their difference is continuous and takes values in the discrete set $2\pi\mathbb Z$.

Since $u(1)=u(0)$, the endpoint difference is $2\pi n$ for an integer $n$. Define

$$
\operatorname{wind}(\gamma,0)=\frac{\theta(1)-\theta(0)}{2\pi}=n.
$$

Counterclockwise turns are positive. The definition counts net turns, so a path can make forward and backward excursions that cancel. It does not count the number of points where the drawn curve crosses a ray unless orientations and endpoint conventions are handled correctly.

## Why deformation cannot change the integer

Let $H(t,s)$ be a continuous family of closed loops in $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$, so $H(0,s)=H(1,s)$. Normalize it to $u(t,s)=H(t,s)/|H(t,s)|$. The compact square and continuity give uniform control: for sufficiently close $s$ and $s'$, the quotient $u(t,s')/u(t,s)$ stays in an arc about $1$ that avoids $-1$ for every $t$.

This quotient has a unique continuous angle $\delta(t)$ in $(-\pi,\pi)$. Its endpoint values agree because the quotient is itself a closed loop and this branch is single valued. If $\theta_s(t)$ lifts $u(t,s)$, then $\theta_s(t)+\delta(t)$ lifts $u(t,s')$. Their net angle increments agree. Hence the winding is locally constant in $s$. Because $[0,1]$ is connected, it is constant over the entire deformation. A loop cannot lose a turn unless it passes through the excluded point or ceases to be closed.

For loops on $S^1$ based at $1$, a lift can start at $0$. If its endpoint is $2\pi n$, then

$$
H(t,s)=\exp\bigl(i[(1-s)\theta(t)+s\,2\pi nt]\bigr)
$$

is a based homotopy to $e^{2\pi int}$. Both endpoint angles remain $0$ and $2\pi n$. Thus winding completely classifies based circle loops up to homotopy. In more complicated spaces, one integer may not classify all loops.

## Worked calculation and integral form

Consider

$$
\gamma(t)=(2+\cos2\pi t)\exp\bigl(i[4\pi t+\tfrac12\sin2\pi t]\bigr).
$$

Its radial factor lies between $1$ and $3$, so it never reaches zero. It is closed because both radial endpoint values are $3$ and the endpoint angles differ by $4\pi$. The displayed angle is a continuous lift; therefore the winding is $4\pi/(2\pi)=2$. Oscillation of either radius or angle does not change this result.

For a piecewise continuously differentiable loop $\gamma(t)=x(t)+iy(t)$, differentiate the local polar expression to obtain

$$
d\theta=\frac{x\,dy-y\,dx}{x^2+y^2},\qquad
\operatorname{wind}(\gamma,0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_\gamma
\frac{x\,dy-y\,dx}{x^2+y^2}.
$$

For $x=R\cos2\pi nt$, $y=R\sin2\pi nt$, the numerator is $2\pi nR^2\,dt$ and the denominator is $R^2$. Integration over $0\leq t\leq1$ gives $n$. The radius cancels. The integral formula requires the stated differentiability; the lifted-angle definition only requires continuity.

Around a different excluded point $a\in\mathbb C$, use $\operatorname{wind}(\gamma,a)=\operatorname{wind}(\gamma-a,0)$. Winding also explains the global potential obstruction in [[27.10 Local and global potentials]].

## Practice

1. Compute the winding of $\gamma(t)=3e^{-6\pi it}$ and of its reverse. Explain the signs.
2. Let $\gamma(t)=2+\tfrac12 e^{2\pi it}$. Prove that its winding about $0$ is zero without choosing a branch of the inverse tangent.

## Worked answers

1. A lift is $\theta(t)=-6\pi t$, so the net angle is $-6\pi$ and the winding is $-3$. Reversal changes the endpoint difference to $6\pi$, giving $3$. Clockwise traversal has the negative sign in the chosen convention.
2. Use $H(t,s)=2+(1-s)\tfrac12 e^{2\pi it}$. It is closed for every $s$, and $|H(t,s)|\geq2-(1-s)/2\geq3/2$, so it never reaches zero. At $s=1$ it is the constant loop $2$, whose winding is zero. Homotopy invariance gives zero for the original loop.
