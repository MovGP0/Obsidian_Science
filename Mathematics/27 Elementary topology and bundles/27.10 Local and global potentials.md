> [!TLDR]
> Local primitives or frames can exist on every patch while nonzero periods or twisted gluing prevent a compatible global choice.

## Closed and exact one-forms

On a smooth manifold $M$, a smooth one-form $\alpha$ is **closed** if $d\alpha=0$. It is **exact** if there is a globally defined smooth real function $f$ with $\alpha=df$. Such an $f$ is a scalar potential, also called a primitive. Since $d^2=0$, exact forms are closed. The reverse implication is local on a sufficiently small coordinate ball, but it can fail globally.

On a star-shaped open set in $\mathbb R^n$, a closed one-form $\alpha=\sum_j a_j(x)\,dx^j$ has a primitive. If the centre is $0$, set

$$
f(x)=\int_0^1\sum_j a_j(tx)x^j\,dt.
$$

Closedness means $\partial_k a_j=\partial_j a_k$. Differentiating under the integral gives

$$
\partial_k f
=\int_0^1\left[a_k(tx)+t\sum_jx^j\partial_k a_j(tx)\right]dt
=\int_0^1\frac{d}{dt}[t a_k(tx)]dt=a_k(x).
$$

In the middle step, replace $\partial_k a_j$ by $\partial_j a_k$. The boundary term at $t=0$ is zero, so $df=\alpha$. This calculation gives the local result directly and states the geometric condition that makes the radial segments stay in the domain.

## Worked example on a punctured plane

Let $M=\mathbb R^2\setminus\{(0,0)\}$ and define

$$
\alpha=\frac{-y\,dx+x\,dy}{x^2+y^2}.
$$

Put $P=-y/(x^2+y^2)$ and $Q=x/(x^2+y^2)$. A direct derivative calculation gives

$$
\partial_x Q=\frac{y^2-x^2}{(x^2+y^2)^2}
=\partial_y P,
\qquad d\alpha=(\partial_x Q-\partial_y P)\,dx\wedge dy=0.
$$

On a patch with a smooth real angle $\theta$, polar coordinates give $\alpha=d\theta$. Thus local potentials exist. However, on the counterclockwise unit circle $\gamma(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)$, $0\leq t\leq2\pi$, substitution gives

$$
\gamma^*\alpha
=\frac{\sin^2t+\cos^2t}{1}\,dt=dt,
\qquad \oint_\gamma\alpha=2\pi.
$$

If $\alpha=df$ globally, the chain rule would give

$$
\oint_\gamma df=f(\gamma(2\pi))-f(\gamma(0))=0,
$$

contradicting $2\pi$. Therefore closedness is insufficient for a global potential. Applying Stokes' theorem to the filled unit disk would be invalid: that disk contains the removed point, where $\alpha$ is undefined.

More generally, on a connected smooth manifold a closed one-form with zero integrals around all piecewise smooth closed paths has a global primitive. Fix $p$ and define $f(x)=\int_p^x\alpha$ along a path. Two choices differ by a closed-path integral, so zero periods make the result path independent. On a small coordinate ball, the local primitive calculation shows $df=\alpha$. Nonzero periods are therefore the exact obstruction for real one-form potentials in this setting. [[27.05 Winding numbers]] identifies the periods of this example as $2\pi$ times winding.

## Why local choices fail to join

If $\alpha=df_i$ on patches $U_i$, then $d(f_i-f_j)=0$ on overlaps. The difference is constant on each connected component of $U_i\cap U_j$. Adding constants to the $f_i$ can sometimes remove all differences. Around a circle, a nonzero accumulated change of angle prevents this. A branch cut supplies one real angle on a smaller domain, but it removes part of the original domain and does not solve the original global problem.

A similar obstruction occurs for frames. The Möbius real line bundle has local nonzero basis sections everywhere. A proposed global basis would correspond to a continuous $f:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ with $f(1)=-f(0)$ and $f(t)\ne0$. The intermediate value theorem makes that impossible. Here the incompatible local choices are bases related by sign changes, rather than potentials related by additive constants. [[27.07 Transition functions]] and [[27.08 Bundle types]] give the gluing conventions.

Finally, distinguish a real potential from a circle-valued change of frame. For the connection $\nabla=d+ia\alpha$ on the circle, a parallel unit frame would have local coefficient $u(\theta)=e^{-ia\theta}$. It is globally single valued exactly when $a\in\mathbb Z$. For nonzero integer $a$, the real form $a\alpha$ still has no real global primitive, but this circle-valued frame exists. Zero periods and periods in $2\pi\mathbb Z$ answer different questions. The transport calculation is in [[27.09 Connections and holonomy]].

## Practice

1. On $M=\{(x,y):x>0\}$, find a global primitive of the punctured-plane form and verify it by differentiation.
2. Let $\beta=3\alpha$ on the punctured plane. Does it have a real global primitive? On the circle, does $d+i\,3\alpha$ have a global parallel unit frame?

## Worked answers

1. Set $f(x,y)=\arctan(y/x)$. Because $x>0$, this is a smooth single-valued function. Differentiation gives $\partial_x f=-y/(x^2+y^2)$ and $\partial_y f=x/(x^2+y^2)$, so $df=\alpha$. The half-plane excludes the angular seam and all loops encircling the origin.
2. Its period around the unit circle is $3\cdot2\pi=6\pi\ne0$, so it has no real global primitive. On the circle, $u(\theta)=e^{-3i\theta}$ is periodic and never zero. Moreover $du+i\,3\,d\theta\,u=(-3i+3i)e^{-3i\theta}d\theta=0$, so it is a global parallel unit frame. There is no contradiction: $u$ is circle valued and its real argument need not be a single global real function.
