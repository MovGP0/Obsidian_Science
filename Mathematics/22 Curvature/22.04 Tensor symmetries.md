---
title: "Symmetries of the curvature tensor"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Curvature is antisymmetric in its direction slots; metric compatibility adds another antisymmetry, and torsion-free metric connections also have pair interchange symmetry.

## State the connection assumptions

Let $R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$. In coordinates write $R(e_i,e_j)e_b=R^a{}_{bij}e_a$. If a metric $g$ is present, set $R_{abij}=g_{ac}R^c{}_{bij}$.

Three assumptions must remain separate. An arbitrary affine connection defines $R$. A metric-compatible connection satisfies $\nabla g=0$ and preserves inner products under parallel transport. A torsion-free connection satisfies $\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX=[X,Y]$. The Levi-Civita connection has both latter properties.

The first symmetry needs no metric:

$$
R(X,Y)Z=-R(Y,X)Z,\qquad R^a{}_{bij}=-R^a{}_{bji}.
$$

This follows by interchanging $X,Y$ in the definition, including the sign change of the bracket.

## What metric compatibility adds

If $\nabla g=0$, apply the curvature commutator to the scalar $g(Z,W)$. The scalar commutator after the bracket correction is zero. The product rule and $\nabla g=0$ then give

$$
g(R(X,Y)Z,W)+g(Z,R(X,Y)W)=0.
$$

Thus $R(X,Y)$ is skew-adjoint with respect to $g$. In lowered components this is

$$
R_{abij}=-R_{baij}.
$$

This identity does not say that every individual Christoffel matrix in a coordinate basis is skew-symmetric. It says that the curvature endomorphism is skew-adjoint at each point. Coordinate bases need not be orthonormal.

## Pair interchange needs the full setting

For the Levi-Civita connection, one also has

$$
R_{abij}=R_{ijab}.
$$

Metric compatibility alone does not guarantee this identity when torsion is present. One way to prove it in the Levi-Civita case is to use normal coordinates at the point. There $\Gamma^a{}_{ib}=0$ and the first derivatives of $g$ vanish. Differentiating the Christoffel formula gives

$$
R_{abij}=\frac12\bigl(
\partial_i\partial_b g_{aj}-\partial_i\partial_a g_{bj}
-\partial_j\partial_b g_{ai}+\partial_j\partial_a g_{bi}
\bigr).
$$

Interchange $(a,b)$ with $(i,j)$. The symmetry $g_{ab}=g_{ba}$ and equality of mixed partial derivatives reproduce the same expression. Since both sides are tensors, proof in normal coordinates at each point proves the identity in every coordinate system.

The same formula gives the algebraic Bianchi identity

$$
R^a{}_{bij}+R^a{}_{ijb}+R^a{}_{jbi}=0.
$$

Every second derivative cancels in the cyclic sum. See [[22.05 Bianchi identities]] for its role and its relation to differential identities.

## Worked example: all surface components

At a point of a Riemannian surface, select an orthonormal basis $e_1,e_2$ and let $K=R_{1212}$. Antisymmetry makes any component with $a=b$ or $i=j$ vanish. The remaining possibilities are

$$
R_{1212}=K,\quad R_{2112}=-K,\quad
R_{1221}=-K,\quad R_{2121}=K.
$$

The last value can be obtained by swapping both pairs, which introduces two minus signs. Pair interchange is consistent with these values. Hence one number determines all sixteen entries of the surface curvature tensor at a point in this basis.

Contracting gives $\operatorname{Ric}_{11}=R^2{}_{121}=K$ and $\operatorname{Ric}_{22}=R^1{}_{212}=K$. The off-diagonal terms vanish. Thus $\operatorname{Ric}=Kg$ on a surface, a result that would fail if a component sign were copied into the wrong slot.

## Practice

1. For a Levi-Civita connection, suppose $R_{1234}=5$. Determine $R_{2134}$, $R_{1243}$, $R_{3412}$, and $R_{2143}$.
2. A metric-compatible connection has torsion. Which of the three symmetries proved above can be used without an additional argument?

## Worked answers

1. Swapping the first pair gives $R_{2134}=-5$. Swapping the final pair gives $R_{1243}=-5$. Pair interchange gives $R_{3412}=5$. Swapping both antisymmetric pairs gives $R_{2143}=5$. None of these operations permits an arbitrary single-index exchange between different pairs.
2. Direction antisymmetry and skew-adjointness remain valid, so $R_{abij}=-R_{abji}=-R_{baij}$. The stated normal-coordinate proof and the ordinary algebraic Bianchi identity use torsion freedom. Pair interchange must not be assumed from metric compatibility alone.
