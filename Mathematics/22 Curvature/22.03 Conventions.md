---
title: "Curvature signs and index conventions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A curvature formula is unambiguous only after the derivative order, operator sign, component slots, and Ricci contraction are specified.

## One complete convention

Curvature notation differs between mathematical texts. A visible minus sign can result from a different operator definition or from a different order of tensor slots. Write the complete convention before comparing two results.

Throughout this article let $\nabla$ be the Levi-Civita connection of a Riemannian metric $g$. Define

$$
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.
$$

In coordinates, define coefficients and tensor slots by

$$
\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_b=\Gamma^a{}_{ib}\partial_a,
\qquad
R(\partial_i,\partial_j)\partial_b=R^a{}_{bij}\partial_a.
$$

The upper index is the output. The next index is the input vector. The final two indices are the ordered derivative directions. Lower the output index and define the Ricci tensor by

$$
R_{abij}=g_{ac}R^c{}_{bij},
\qquad
\operatorname{Ric}_{bj}=R^a{}_{baj}.
$$

Thus Ricci contracts the output with the first derivative direction. For independent tangent vectors $X,Y$, sectional curvature is

$$
K(X,Y)=\frac{g(R(X,Y)Y,X)}{g(X,X)g(Y,Y)-g(X,Y)^2}.
$$

The denominator is the squared area of their parallelogram and is positive in a Riemannian metric. These choices give positive curvature for a round sphere.

## Index slots are not the order of arguments

For a four-linear form $C(X,Y,Z,W)=g(R(X,Y)Z,W)$, its coordinate components are

$$
C_{ijba}=R_{abij}.
$$

This equation is a slot conversion. It does not claim that the written sequences $ijba$ and $abij$ have the same meaning. As an example, $R_{1212}=g(R(e_1,e_2)e_2,e_1)$ in an orthonormal basis. By contrast, $C_{1212}=g(R(e_1,e_2)e_1,e_2)=-R_{1212}$. A formula copied without its slot definition can therefore acquire the wrong sign.

There is a second subtlety in derivative notation. The operator $\nabla_X\nabla_Y$ differentiates the vector field $\nabla_YZ$. The covariant Hessian also corrects the direction slot by subtracting $\nabla_{\nabla_XY}Z$. For a torsion-free connection their antisymmetric parts agree with $R(X,Y)Z$ after the bracket is accounted for. With torsion $T(X,Y)=\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX-[X,Y]$, the antisymmetric covariant Hessian is $R(X,Y)Z-\nabla_{T(X,Y)}Z$.

## Worked example: translating a sphere calculation

For a constant-curvature metric, our convention gives

$$
R(X,Y)Z=K\bigl(g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y\bigr).
$$

Take orthonormal vectors $e_1,e_2$ on a unit sphere. Then

$$
R(e_1,e_2)e_2=e_1,\qquad
R(e_1,e_2)e_1=-e_2.
$$

Consequently $R_{1212}=1$, while $R_{2112}=-1$. Now suppose another author defines $\widehat R(X,Y)Z=-R(X,Y)Z$ and uses the same component slots. Then $\widehat R_{1212}=-1$ and $\widehat{\operatorname{Ric}}=-\operatorname{Ric}$ if that author keeps the same contraction. Their sphere can still have positive reported sectional curvature if they define it using $-g(\widehat R(X,Y)Y,X)$.

The safe translation is a sequence: identify the operator, identify the slots, convert the contraction, then test one known example. The component sign alone does not determine the author's sectional-curvature sign.

## Transport orientation

For a coordinate rectangle followed as $+X,+Y,-X,-Y$, parallel transport is $I-stR(X,Y)+\cdots$ with our convention. If the rectangle is traversed in the opposite direction, its oriented area changes sign. If the operator sign is reversed, the transport formula changes sign too. See [[22.01 Curvature definitions]] for the direct matrix calculation. Never use an unlabeled loop sketch as the only definition of a curvature sign.

## Practice

1. A text uses $Q_{ijkl}=g(R(e_i,e_j)e_k,e_l)$ with our operator. Express $Q_{1221}$ and $Q_{1212}$ on a sphere with curvature $K$ in an orthonormal frame.
2. An author writes $\widetilde R(X,Y)=R(Y,X)$ and $\widetilde{\operatorname{Ric}}_{bj}=\widetilde R^a{}_{baj}$. Translate its Ricci tensor and scalar curvature into our convention.

## Worked answers

1. The constant-curvature formula gives $R(e_1,e_2)e_2=Ke_1$, so $Q_{1221}=K$. It also gives $R(e_1,e_2)e_1=-Ke_2$, so $Q_{1212}=-K$. The slot conversion is $Q_{ijkl}=R_{lkij}$.
2. Antisymmetry gives $\widetilde R=-R$. Its fixed contraction therefore gives $\widetilde{\operatorname{Ric}}=-\operatorname{Ric}$. Taking the trace with the same inverse metric gives $\widetilde S=-S$. Changing notation does not change the metric, but it changes these signed formulas.
