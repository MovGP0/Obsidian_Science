> [!TLDR]
> Curvature measures the failure of covariant derivatives to commute and the first change caused by parallel transport around a small loop.

## A derivative that acts on tangent vectors

Let $M$ be a smooth manifold and let $\nabla$ be an affine connection on its tangent bundle. If $X$ and $Z$ are vector fields, then $\nabla_XZ$ is the derivative of $Z$ in direction $X$, corrected for the change of the local frame. The connection is linear in $X$ over smooth functions and satisfies the product rule in $Z$. These properties let us compare tangent vectors at nearby points through parallel transport.

Two directions do not always commute. Their Lie bracket $[X,Y]$ measures this failure as differential operators on smooth functions. Thus the correct curvature operator is

$$
R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_X\nabla_YZ-\nabla_Y\nabla_XZ-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z.
$$

Read the inner derivative first. In the first term, differentiate $Z$ in direction $Y$, and then in direction $X$. The bracket correction removes the failure already present in the directions themselves. This choice fixes the sign for this article and for [[22.03 Conventions]].

Although this definition contains derivatives, its value at a point depends only on $X,Y,Z$ at that point. For example, expand $R(X,Y)(fZ)$ with the product rule. Terms containing first derivatives of $f$ cancel in pairs. The remaining second derivatives give

$$
\bigl(X(Yf)-Y(Xf)-[X,Y]f\bigr)Z=0.
$$

Therefore $R(X,Y)(fZ)=fR(X,Y)Z$. Similar expansions show linearity over functions in $X$ and $Y$. Thus $R$ is a tensor, not an operator that requires the values of the input fields throughout a neighbourhood. Also $R(Y,X)=-R(X,Y)$ directly from the definition.

## A small transport loop

Choose commuting coordinate directions $X=\partial_x$ and $Y=\partial_y$ near a point $p$. Follow a coordinate rectangle in the order $+X,+Y,-X,-Y$, with side parameters $s,t>0$. Let $P_{\square}$ be parallel transport around this closed curve, acting on $T_pM$. With the convention above,

$$
P_{\square}v=v-stR(X,Y)v+O(s^2t+st^2).
$$

The leading change is proportional to the oriented parameter area. The minus sign follows from the equation for parallel transport, $\nabla_{\dot\gamma}V=0$. It is not an extra choice once the direction around the rectangle is fixed. Reverse the path and the leading sign reverses. For noncommuting fields, four flow segments need not close; an additional bracket correction is needed.

![[math-22-small-loop.svg|**Fig. 22.1** An oriented coordinate rectangle and the first transport change. The vector arrows are a schematic comparison in the tangent space at the start point.]]

Curvature is the first local term in this transport comparison. A zero result for one vector or one pair of directions does not prove that the full tensor vanishes.

## Worked example: constant connection matrices

On $\mathbb R^2$, use a fixed frame and define $\nabla_{\partial_x}=\partial_x+A$, $\nabla_{\partial_y}=\partial_y+B$, where

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The matrices are constant, so derivative terms vanish. Apply the two derivatives to a field $v$ and cancel its ordinary second and first derivative terms. This gives

$$
R(\partial_x,\partial_y)v=(AB-BA)v
=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}v.
$$

For $v=e_1$, the curvature value is $e_1$. Direct transport gives the same sign check: the four segments act successively by $e^{-sA}$, $e^{-tB}$, $e^{sA}$, and $e^{tB}$. Their composite is

$$
e^{tB}e^{sA}e^{-tB}e^{-sA}=I-st[A,B]+O(s^2t+st^2).
$$

Thus $P_{\square}e_1=e_1-st e_1+\cdots$. This connection need not be metric compatible or torsion free. Neither condition is required to define curvature.

## Practice

1. In the same example, calculate $R(\partial_y,\partial_x)e_2$ and the leading change in $e_2$ around the stated loop.
2. Replace $B$ with $2A$. Determine the curvature and explain whether nonzero connection matrices alone prove curvature.

## Worked answers

1. The commutator matrix sends $e_2$ to $-e_2$. Reversing the derivative directions changes the sign, so $R(\partial_y,\partial_x)e_2=e_2$. For the original loop, $-stR(\partial_x,\partial_y)e_2=st e_2$. Hence $P_{\square}e_2=(1+st)e_2+\cdots$.
2. Since $[A,2A]=2A^2-2A^2=0$ and the coefficients are constant, $R=0$. Nonzero coefficients can describe a flat connection. Curvature measures a particular derivative and product combination, derived in [[22.02 Coordinate expressions]].
