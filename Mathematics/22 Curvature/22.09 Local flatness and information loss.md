---
title: "Local flatness and information lost by contraction"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Vanishing connection coefficients at one point, vanishing curvature on a neighbourhood, and vanishing curvature traces are different statements.

## Three levels of information

Let $g$ be a smooth Riemannian metric and $\nabla$ its Levi-Civita connection. Use $R(X,Y)=\nabla_X\nabla_Y-\nabla_Y\nabla_X-\nabla_{[X,Y]}$ and $\operatorname{Ric}_{bj}=R^a{}_{baj}$. At any point $p$, normal coordinates satisfy

$$
g_{ij}(p)=\delta_{ij},\qquad
\partial_k g_{ij}(p)=0,\qquad
\Gamma^a{}_{ib}(p)=0.
$$

These conditions remove the first-order coordinate effects at that point. They do not set the second derivatives of $g$ to zero. Indeed, at $p$,

$$
R^a{}_{bij}(p)=\partial_i\Gamma^a{}_{jb}(p)
-\partial_j\Gamma^a{}_{ib}(p).
$$

The products of coefficients vanish, but their derivatives need not. A round sphere therefore has normal coordinates at each point and still has $K=1/a^2$. Even $R(p)=0$ at one point is weaker than $R=0$ throughout a neighbourhood.

If the full curvature vanishes on an open neighbourhood, a Riemannian metric is locally isometric to Euclidean space. To understand the neighbourhood condition, shrink to a simply connected coordinate region. Flatness makes local parallel transport path independent. Transport an orthonormal basis from one point to obtain parallel fields $E_i$. Torsion freedom gives $[E_i,E_j]=\nabla_{E_i}E_j-\nabla_{E_j}E_i=0$. Commuting independent fields provide local coordinates. In those coordinates their inner products are constant, so the metric is Euclidean. This argument is local; a flat torus need not have one global Euclidean chart.

## Scalar curvature can cancel

Scalar curvature is $S=g^{bj}\operatorname{Ric}_{bj}$, a trace of a trace. Positive and negative contributions can cancel. Take the product of two surfaces, one with constant curvature $+1$ and one with constant curvature $-1$. Give the product its product metric. Its tangent space splits orthogonally into the two factors, and the product connection differentiates each factor separately. Therefore factor two-planes retain their curvatures, while mixed two-planes have zero curvature.

Choose orthonormal vectors $e_1,e_2$ in the first factor and $e_3,e_4$ in the second. Then

$$
K_{12}=1,\qquad K_{34}=-1,\qquad K_{13}=K_{14}=K_{23}=K_{24}=0.
$$

Thus $\operatorname{Ric}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)$ and $S=0$. The full tensor is nonzero because $R_{1212}=1$. A zero scalar curvature is not a flatness test.

## Worked example: Ricci can also lose algebraic information

Consider an inner-product vector space of dimension four with orthonormal basis $e_1,\ldots,e_4$. Define an algebraic curvature tensor by its independent pair entries

$$
R_{1212}=1,\quad R_{1313}=-1,\quad R_{1414}=0,
\quad R_{2323}=0,\quad R_{2424}=-1,\quad R_{3434}=1.
$$

Extend by pair antisymmetries and pair interchange, and set entries with different unordered pairs to zero. The algebraic Bianchi identity holds: when three distinct indices occur, all terms vanish; when indices repeat, antisymmetric terms cancel. Hence this is a valid algebraic curvature tensor.

Its Ricci diagonal entries are the sums of pair values containing each index:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Ric}_{11}&=1-1+0=0,&
\operatorname{Ric}_{22}&=1+0-1=0,\\
\operatorname{Ric}_{33}&=-1+0+1=0,&
\operatorname{Ric}_{44}&=0-1+1=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Off-diagonal Ricci entries vanish because distinct pair entries were set to zero. Yet $R_{1212}=1$. This explicit pointwise example proves that contraction is not injective on algebraic curvature tensors in dimension four. It is not a construction of a metric that is Ricci flat throughout an open set; that stronger existence problem needs additional work.

In two dimensions $\operatorname{Ric}=Kg$ and $S=2K$, so either trace determines the full tensor. In three dimensions Ricci determines the full tensor but scalar curvature alone does not. Always include the dimension in a conclusion about information loss. See [[22.06 Contractions]] for the trace definitions.

## Practice

1. A metric has $\Gamma(p)=0$ and $\partial_1\Gamma^1{}_{22}(p)=3$, $\partial_2\Gamma^1{}_{12}(p)=1$. Compute $R^1{}_{212}(p)$ and assess local flatness.
2. For the product surface example, compute $G=\operatorname{Ric}-\tfrac12Sg$. Does $S=0$ imply $G=0$ here?

## Worked answers

1. Products vanish at $p$, so $R^1{}_{212}(p)=3-1=2$. The tensor is nonzero at $p$, so no neighbourhood containing $p$ is Euclidean with this metric. Vanishing coefficients at the point do not change this conclusion.
2. Since $S=0$, the correction term is zero and $G=\operatorname{Ric}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)$ in the chosen orthonormal basis. Hence $S=0$ does not imply $G=0$. The contracted Bianchi identity still gives zero covariant divergence, as explained in [[22.07 The Einstein tensor]].
