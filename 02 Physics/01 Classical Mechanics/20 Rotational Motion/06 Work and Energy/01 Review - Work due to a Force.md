---
title: "6.1 Review: Work due to a Force"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Force transfers energy through work equal to its integral along the displacement.

This chapter applies the concepts of work, kinetic energy, and conservation of energy to rotational motion. Start with the work done by a force along a path from $\mechanical S_i$ to $\mechanical S_f$.

$$
\energy W=\int d\energy W
=\int_{\mechanical S_i}^{\mechanical S_f}\mechanical{\vec F}\cdot d\mechanical{\vec S}
=\int_{\mechanical S_i}^{\mechanical S_f}\mechanical F\cos\mechanical\phi\,d\mechanical S .
\tag{6.1}
$$

The force $\mechanical{\vec F}$ does an amount of work $d\energy W$ over an infinitesimal displacement $d\mechanical{\vec S}$. Only the force component along the instantaneous displacement contributes, since $\mechanical\phi$ is the angle between the two vectors. If $\mechanical F$ and $\cos\mechanical\phi$ stay constant, $\energy W=\mechanical F\,\Delta\mechanical S\cos\mechanical\phi$. The SI unit of work is the joule, $\unit J=\unit{N\,m}$.

Work expresses the energy transferred between objects or transformed from one form to another. In linear motion, combining work with Newton’s second law gives the work–kinetic energy theorem. The same reasoning applies to rotation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy W$ | $\unit J$ | Work | Energy transferred by a force. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit N$ | Force | Force acting on the object. |
| $d\mechanical{\vec S}$ | $\unit m$ | Displacement | Infinitesimal change in position along the path. |
| $\mechanical\phi$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angle | Angle between the force and the displacement. |
