---
title: "7.1 General Motion of a Rigid Body: Chasles’ Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Chasles’ theorem describes rigid-body motion as translation of a reference point combined with rotation.

A spinning tennis ball or flying disc translates and rotates at the same time. To see how these motions combine, imagine throwing a uniform rod so it spins in flight.

![[rotational-motion-fig-7-1.svg|**Fig. 7.1** Successive positions of a spinning rod in flight. Its center of mass follows a projectile trajectory, and the rod rotates about its center of mass.|620]]

With air resistance neglected, the center of mass obeys the equations for a point projectile. Relative to the center of mass, the rod rotates about an axis through that point. Chasles’ theorem lets us describe rigid-body motion as a translation of a chosen point and a rotation about that point. Here we take the chosen point to be the center of mass. The next sections apply this decomposition to rolling motion.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}_{\mathrm{cm}}$ | $\unit m$ | Center-of-mass position | The point that follows the projectile trajectory. |
| $\mechanical\theta$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Rotation angle | Orientation of the rod about its center of mass. |
