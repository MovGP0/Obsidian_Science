> [!TLDR]
> Definitions of terms used in the classical mechanics notes.

- **Acceleration.** The time derivative of velocity, $\mechanical{\vec a}=d\mechanical{\vec v}/dt$.
- **Action–angle variables.** Canonical coordinates $(\theta,J)$ in which $\energy{H}=\energy{H}(J)$.
- **Angular momentum.** For one particle, $\mechanical{\vec L}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec v}$.
- **Archimedes' principle.** The upward buoyant force equals the weight of the displaced fluid.
- **Atwood machine.** Two masses connected by a rope over a pulley. A standard example of a constrained system.
- **Bernoulli's equation.** $\mechanical{p}+\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}^2+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}=\mathrm{const.}$ along a streamline in steady, incompressible, inviscid flow.
- **Canonical conjugate momentum.** $p_k=\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q_k$.
- **Canonical transformation.** A change of coordinates $(q,p)\to(Q,P)$ that preserves the form of Hamilton's equations.
- **Center of mass.** The mass-weighted position $\mechanical{\vec R}=(1/\mechanical{M})\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec r}_i$.
- **Central force.** A radial force with respect to a fixed center, with magnitude depending only on distance from that center.
- **Centripetal acceleration.** The inward acceleration component in circular motion, $\mechanical{a}_c=\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R}=\mechanical{\omega}^2\mechanical{R}$.
- **Chaos.** Exponential sensitivity to initial conditions in deterministic nonlinear systems.
- **Conservative force.** A force whose work between two points depends only on the endpoints.
- **Constraint.** A restriction on the motion of a system.
- **Coriolis force.** An inertial force in a rotating frame, $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{Co}}=-2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec v}'$.
- **d'Alembert's principle.** The dynamical extension of the virtual-work principle, $\sum_i(\mechanical{\vec F}_i-\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec a}_i)\cdot\delta\mechanical{\vec r}_i=0$.
- **Degrees of freedom.** The number of independent parameters needed to specify a configuration.
- **Escape speed.** The minimum launch speed needed to reach arbitrarily large distances from a gravitating mass without propulsion, $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}}$.
- **Euler–Lagrange equations.** $d(\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q_k)/dt-\partial\energy{L}/\partial q_k=0$.
- **Euler's equations for a rigid body.** The rotation equations in principal axes, $\mechanical{I}_i\mechanical{\dot\omega}_i-(\mechanical{I}_j-\mechanical{I}_k)\mechanical{\omega}_j\mechanical{\omega}_k=\mechanical{\tau}_i$ for cyclic $(i,j,k)$.
- **Friction.** A tangential force that opposes relative sliding or impending sliding of surfaces. Static friction has maximum magnitude $\mechanical{\mu}_s\mechanical{N}$, and kinetic friction has magnitude $\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{N}$ in the Coulomb model.
- **Galilean transformation.** $\mechanical{\vec r}'=\mechanical{\vec r}-\mechanical{\vec V}t$ and $t'=t$ for constant frame velocity $\mechanical{\vec V}$.
- **Generalized coordinates.** The $n$ independent parameters $q_k$ that describe a system with $n$ degrees of freedom.
- **Generating function.** A function $F$ that generates a canonical transformation.
- **Hamilton–Jacobi equation.** $\energy{H}(q,\partial\energy{S}/\partial q,t)+\partial\energy{S}/\partial t=0$.
- **Hamilton's equations.** $\dot q_k=\partial\energy{H}/\partial p_k$ and $\dot p_k=-\partial\energy{H}/\partial q_k$.
- **Hamilton's principle.** $\delta\energy{S}=0$ with $\energy{S}=\int\energy{L}\,dt$ for variations with fixed endpoints.
- **Hamiltonian.** $\energy{H}(q,p,t)=\sum_k p_k\dot q_k-\energy{L}$, obtained by a Legendre transformation of $\energy{L}$.
- **Harmonic oscillator.** A system with $\mechanical{\ddot x}+\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}=0$ and period $\signal{T}=2\pi/\signal{\omega}$.
- **Holonomic constraint.** A relation of the form $f(\mechanical{\vec r},t)=0$ that depends on position and possibly time.
- **Inertia tensor.** The symmetric $3\times3$ matrix $\mechanical{I}_{ij}=\int\mechanical{\rho}(\mechanical{r}^2\delta_{ij}-\mechanical{r}_i\mechanical{r}_j)\,d\mechanical{V}$.
- **Inertial frame.** A reference frame in which Newton's first law holds.
- **Kepler's laws.** Elliptical orbits with the attracting center at one focus, constant areal velocity, and $\signal{T}^2\propto\mechanical{a}^3$.
- **Kinetic energy.** $\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2$ for a particle. For a rigid body with fixed-axis rotation relative to its center of mass, $\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{M}\mechanical{V}^2+\tfrac12\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\omega}^2$.
- **Lagrangian.** $\energy{L}=\energy{T}-\energy{U}$, a function of the generalized coordinates, their time derivatives, and time.
- **Legendre transformation.** $g(p)=px-f(x)$ with $p=f'(x)$ on an invertible branch. It relates the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations.
- **Linear momentum.** $\mechanical{\vec p}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}$ for a particle and $\mechanical{\vec P}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{\vec V}$ for a system with center-of-mass velocity $\mechanical{\vec V}$.
- **Liouville's theorem.** Hamiltonian evolution preserves phase-space volume.
- **Moment of inertia.** $\mechanical{I}=\int\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{d}_\perp^2\,d\mechanical{V}$.
- **Newton's laws.** Inertia, $\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec a}$ for constant mass, and action and reaction.
- **Noether's theorem.** Each continuous variational symmetry has an associated conserved quantity.
- **Nonholonomic constraint.** A constraint that cannot be reduced to an algebraic position-time relation $f(\mechanical{\vec r},t)=0$. An example is a sphere rolling on a plane without slipping.
- **Normal modes.** Harmonic solutions of the linearized problem around an equilibrium.
- **Parallel-axis theorem (Huygens–Steiner theorem).** $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{I}_G+\mechanical{M}\mechanical{d}^2$ for parallel axes with separation $\mechanical{d}$, where $\mechanical{I}_G$ is measured about the center-of-mass axis.
- **Pendulum.** A system that oscillates under gravity. The simple pendulum has small-angle period $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{\ell}/\mechanical{g}}$. A physical pendulum is an extended body.
- **Phase space.** The $2n$-dimensional space of canonical variables $(q,p)$.
- **Poisson bracket.** $\{f,g\}=\sum_k[(\partial f/\partial q_k)(\partial g/\partial p_k)-(\partial f/\partial p_k)(\partial g/\partial q_k)]$.
- **Potential energy.** A function $\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r})$ with $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\nabla\energy{U}$ for a conservative force.
- **Pressure.** Normal force per unit area, $\mechanical{p}=d\mechanical{F}_\perp/d\mechanical{A}$.
- **Resonance.** The enhanced response of a driven oscillator near its natural frequency. For weak damping the displacement amplitude peaks near $\signal{\Omega}\simeq\signal{\omega}_0$.
- **Rigid body.** A system of points whose mutual distances remain constant.
- **Rocket equation.** $\Delta\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{u}\ln(\mechanical{m}_0/\mechanical{m}_f)$ for constant exhaust speed without external forces.
- **Stability.** An equilibrium is stable if initially small perturbations remain small over time.
- **Stable equilibrium.** A nondegenerate potential minimum in one coordinate satisfies $\energy{U}'(q_0)=0$ and $\energy{U}''(q_0)>0$.
- **Steiner.** See **Parallel-axis theorem**.
- **Tension.** The internal tensile force transmitted along a rope or rod.
- **Universal gravitation.** $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{M}\hat r/\mechanical{r}^2$.
- **Velocity.** $\mechanical{\vec v}=d\mechanical{\vec r}/dt$.
- **Virial theorem.** $2\langle\energy{K}\rangle=n\langle\energy{U}\rangle$ for bounded motion in a homogeneous potential $\energy{U}\propto\mechanical{r}^n$.
- **Virtual work.** The work a force would do through a virtual displacement.
- **Viscosity.** The resistance of a fluid to shear deformation. In a Newtonian fluid under simple shear, $\mechanical{\tau}=\mechanical{\eta}\,d\mechanical{v}/d\mechanical{z}$.
