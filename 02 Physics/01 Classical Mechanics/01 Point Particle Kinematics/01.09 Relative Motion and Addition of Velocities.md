---
title: "Relative Motion and Addition of Velocities"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For frames that translate without rotating, add the velocity of the moving frame to the velocity measured in that frame.

Let $S$ and $S'$ be two reference frames. Frame $S'$ moves relative to $S$. Let $\mechanical{\vec R}(t)$ be the position of the origin of $S'$ relative to $S$. The velocity of this origin is $\mechanical{\vec V}=\mechanical{\dot{\vec R}}$. For a point $P$ with position $\mechanical{\vec r}$ in $S$ and $\mechanical{\vec r}\,'$ in $S'$, the Galilean relations for nonrotating axes are

$$
\mechanical{\vec r}=\mechanical{\vec R}+\mechanical{\vec r}\,',
\qquad
\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\vec V}+\mechanical{\vec v}\,'.
$$

For uniform rectilinear translation of $S'$ relative to $S$, the accelerations are equal, $\mechanical{\vec a}=\mechanical{\vec a}\,'$.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source states that the accelerations are equal for rectilinear translation without rotation. The equality also requires constant translational velocity. If the moving origin accelerates along a straight line, its acceleration must be added.

**Rotating frames.** If $S'$ rotates relative to $S$ with angular velocity $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$, extra terms enter the velocity and acceleration relations, including the Coriolis and centrifugal terms. [[02.07 Noninertial Reference Frames|Chapter 2]] returns to them in the dynamics of noninertial frames.

### Example — A Boat Crossing a River

The river flows at $\mechanical{\vec V}=\mechanical{V}\hat{\imath}$ relative to the bank. The boat points across the river and travels at $\mechanical{\vec v}\,'=\mechanical{v}'\hat{\jmath}$ relative to the water.

Relative to the bank, the total velocity is $\mechanical{\vec v}=(\mechanical{V},\mechanical{v}')$. Its magnitude is $\sqrt{\mechanical{V}^2+\mechanical{v}'^{\,2}}$, and its direction makes an angle $\arctan(\mechanical{V}/\mechanical{v}')$ with the $y$ axis. To cross a river of width $\mechanical{L}$, the boat takes time $t=\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{v}'$. In that time, it drifts downstream by $\mechanical{V}\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{v}'$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\vec R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Frame origin position | The position of the moving frame's origin in the reference frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}, \mechanical{\vec r}\,'$ | $\unit{m}$ | Particle position | The position of the point in each frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec V}, \mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Frame velocity | The velocity of the moving origin and its magnitude in the river example. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}, \mechanical{\vec v}\,', \mechanical{v}'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Particle velocity | The velocity in each frame and the boat's speed relative to the water. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}, \mechanical{\vec a}\,'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Particle acceleration | The acceleration of the point in each frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Frame angular velocity | The rate and axis of rotation of the moving frame. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{m}$ | River width | The distance between the banks measured perpendicular to the flow. |
| — | $\unit{rad}$ | Direction angle | The angle between the boat's velocity relative to the bank and the cross-river direction. |
| — | $\unit{m}$ | Downstream drift | The distance the boat moves downstream during the crossing. |
