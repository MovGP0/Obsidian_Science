---
title: "Motion in Two and Three Dimensions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In a uniform gravitational field, horizontal projectile motion and vertical free fall can be calculated separately.

With constant acceleration, the three components of motion evolve independently. For a projectile in a uniform gravitational field,

$$
\mechanical{\vec a}=-\mechanical{g}\hat{\jmath},
\qquad \mechanical{\vec v}(t)=\mechanical{\vec v}_0-\mechanical{g}t\hat{\jmath},
\qquad \mechanical{\vec r}(t)=\mechanical{\vec r}_0+\mechanical{\vec v}_0t-\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{g}t^2\hat{\jmath}.
$$

For a launch from the origin, $\mechanical{\vec r}_0=\vec 0$. If the launch angle above the horizontal is $\mechanical{\alpha}$, the initial velocity is $\mechanical{\vec v}_0=(\mechanical{v}_0\cos\mechanical{\alpha},\,\mechanical{v}_0\sin\mechanical{\alpha},\,0)$. The path is a parabola.

**Range.** The projectile lands at $\mechanical{y}=0$ after time $t_c=2\mechanical{v}_0\sin\mechanical{\alpha}/\mechanical{g}$. The range is

$$
\mechanical{x}_g=\mechanical{v}_0\cos\mechanical{\alpha}\,t_c
=\frac{\mechanical{v}_0^2\sin(2\mechanical{\alpha})}{\mechanical{g}}.
$$

The maximum range occurs at $\mechanical{\alpha}=45\unit{^\circ}$ and is $\mechanical{x}_g^{\max}=\mechanical{v}_0^2/\mechanical{g}$.

**Maximum height.** At the highest point, $\mechanical{\dot y}=0$. Thus $t_h=\mechanical{v}_0\sin\mechanical{\alpha}/\mechanical{g}$ and $\mechanical{y}_h=(\mechanical{v}_0\sin\mechanical{\alpha})^2/(2\mechanical{g})$.

### Example — Horizontal Launch from a Height

A ball leaves a platform of height $\mechanical{h}$ with horizontal speed $\mechanical{v}_0$. The fall time is $t_c=\sqrt{2\mechanical{h}/\mechanical{g}}$, and the range is $\mechanical{x}_g=\mechanical{v}_0\sqrt{2\mechanical{h}/\mechanical{g}}$. The vertical and horizontal components of motion evolve separately. This is a general property of a uniform field.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The time elapsed since launch. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The constant downward acceleration of the projectile. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}, \mechanical{\vec v}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The instantaneous and initial velocities of the projectile. |
| $\mechanical{v}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Launch speed | The magnitude of the initial velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}, \mechanical{\vec r}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The instantaneous and initial positions of the projectile. |
| $\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Vertical coordinate | The vertical position relative to the origin. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Launch angle | The angle above the horizontal. |
| $t_c$ | $\unit{s}$ | Flight time | The time from launch to landing. |
| $\mechanical{x}_g, \mechanical{x}_g^{\max}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Range | The horizontal distance traveled and its maximum over launch angles. |
| $t_h$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time to maximum height | The time at which the vertical velocity is zero. |
| $\mechanical{y}_h$ | $\unit{m}$ | Maximum height | The greatest vertical position during flight. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Platform height | The height of the platform above the landing level. |
