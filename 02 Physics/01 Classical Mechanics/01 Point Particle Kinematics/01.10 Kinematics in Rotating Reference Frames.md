---
title: "Kinematics in Rotating Reference Frames"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A rotating frame adds terms to velocity and acceleration because its basis vectors change direction with time.

Let reference frame $S'$ rotate with angular velocity $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ relative to $S$. Both frames have the same origin. For any vector $\vec A$,

$$
\left(\frac{d\vec A}{dt}\right)_S
=\left(\frac{d\vec A}{dt}\right)_{S'}+\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\vec A.
$$

Apply this relation to $\vec A=\mechanical{\vec r}$ to obtain the velocity.

$$
\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\vec v}\,'+\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec r},
$$

where $\mechanical{\vec v}\,'$ is the velocity relative to $S'$. Apply the relation to $\mechanical{\vec v}$ to obtain the acceleration.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\vec a}
&=\mechanical{\vec a}\,'+2\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec v}\,'\\
&\quad+\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times(\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec r})
+\mechanical{\dot{\vec\omega}}\times\mechanical{\vec r}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The four terms are:

- $\mechanical{\vec a}\,'$, the relative acceleration.
- $2\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec v}\,'$, the *Coriolis acceleration*.
- $\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times(\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec r})$, the centripetal acceleration due to the rotation of the frame. In the dynamics of $S'$, the apparent *centrifugal force* points in the opposite direction.
- $\mechanical{\dot{\vec\omega}}\times\mechanical{\vec r}$, the Euler acceleration. It occurs only when $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ changes.

To describe the dynamics in rotating frames, include the corresponding apparent forces. See [[02.07 Noninertial Reference Frames]].

### Example — Apparent Acceleration at Earth's Surface

Earth rotates with angular speed $\mechanical{\omega}_T\approx\quantity{7.27\times10^{-5}}{rad\,s^{-1}}$. At latitude $\mechanical{\lambda}$ and radial distance $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{R}_T$, the distance from the rotation axis is $\mechanical{R}_T\cos\mechanical{\lambda}$. The centrifugal acceleration magnitude is $\mechanical{\omega}_T^2\mechanical{R}_T\cos\mechanical{\lambda}\approx\quantity{0.034\cos\mechanical{\lambda}}{m\,s^{-2}}$. At the equator, this is approximately $0.34\%$ of $\mechanical{g}$. Rotation contributes to the difference between apparent $\mechanical{g}$ at the equator, $\quantity{9.78}{m\,s^{-2}}$, and at the poles, $\quantity{9.83}{m\,s^{-2}}$.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source attributes the difference to rotation. The centrifugal term accounts for part of it. Earth's shape also affects gravitational acceleration at the surface.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for differentiation in both frames. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The displacement from the common origin to the point. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}, \mechanical{\vec v}\,'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The velocity of the point measured in $S$ and $S'$ respectively. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}, \mechanical{\vec a}\,'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The acceleration of the point measured in $S$ and $S'$ respectively. |
| $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate and axis of rotation of $S'$ relative to $S$. |
| $\mechanical{\dot{\vec\omega}}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The rate of change of the frame's angular velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}_T$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Earth's angular speed | The magnitude of Earth's angular velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\lambda}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Latitude | The angle north or south of the equator. |
| $\mechanical{r}, \mechanical{R}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance | The distance from Earth's center, equal to its radius at the surface in this model. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of local gravitational or apparent free-fall acceleration, as specified. |
