---
title: "Motion in Polar Coordinates"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In polar coordinates, the unit vectors rotate with the particle. Their time derivatives contribute to the velocity and acceleration.

For planar motion in polar coordinates $(\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\theta})$, the radial and angular unit vectors $\hat e_r$ and $\hat e_\theta$ rotate as the point moves. Their time derivatives are

$$
\dot{\hat e}_r=\mechanical{\dot\theta}\hat e_\theta,
\qquad
\dot{\hat e}_\theta=-\mechanical{\dot\theta}\hat e_r.
$$

Differentiate $\mechanical{\vec r}=\mechanical{r}\hat e_r$ to obtain

$$
\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\dot r}\hat e_r+\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\dot\theta}\hat e_\theta.
$$

The first term gives the radial velocity. The second gives the tangential velocity $\mechanical{v}_\theta=\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\dot\theta}$.

Differentiate again to obtain the acceleration in polar coordinates.

$$
\mechanical{\vec a}=
\bigl(\mechanical{\ddot r}-\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}\bigr)\hat e_r
+\bigl(\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\ddot\theta}+2\mechanical{\dot r}\mechanical{\dot\theta}\bigr)\hat e_\theta.
$$

The term $-\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}$ is the centripetal acceleration component. It is present even when $\mechanical{r}$ varies. The term $2\mechanical{\dot r}\mechanical{\dot\theta}$ is the **kinematic Coriolis term**. It occurs when the point moves both radially and angularly.

### Example — Archimedean Spiral

An ant follows the spiral $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\theta}$ with constant angular velocity $\mechanical{\dot\theta}=\mechanical{\omega}$. Its velocity is $\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\omega}\hat e_r+\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\theta}\mechanical{\omega}\hat e_\theta$. For $\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\omega}>0$, its speed is $\lVert\mechanical{\vec v}\rVert=\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\omega}\sqrt{1+\mechanical{\theta}^2}$. The acceleration is

$$
\mechanical{\vec a}=-\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\theta}\mechanical{\omega}^2\hat e_r
+2\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\omega}^2\hat e_\theta.
$$

The term $2\mechanical{c}\mechanical{\omega}^2$ is the kinematic Coriolis term.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{r}, \mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance and position vector | The distance and directed displacement from the polar origin. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Polar angle | The angle of the position vector relative to the reference direction. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\theta}, \mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The time derivative of the polar angle. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The time derivative of the angular velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The time derivative of the position vector. |
| $\mechanical{\dot r}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Radial velocity | The rate of change of radial distance. |
| $\mechanical{v}_\theta$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Tangential velocity | The velocity component along the angular unit vector. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The time derivative of velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot r}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Second derivative of radial distance | One contribution to the radial acceleration component. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{m\,rad^{-1}}$ | Spiral scale | The increase in radial distance per unit increase in polar angle. |
