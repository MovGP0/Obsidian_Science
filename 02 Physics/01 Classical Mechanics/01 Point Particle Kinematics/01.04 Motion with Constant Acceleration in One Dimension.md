---
title: "Motion with Constant Acceleration in One Dimension"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For motion along a straight line with constant acceleration, velocity changes linearly and position changes quadratically with time.

In **motion with constant acceleration**, $\mechanical{a}(t)=\mechanical{a}_0$. Integrate the acceleration to find the velocity. Then integrate the velocity to find the position.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{v}(t) &= \mechanical{v}_0 + \int_0^t \mechanical{a}_0\,dt'
                 = \mechanical{v}_0 + \mechanical{a}_0t,\\
\mechanical{x}(t) &= \mechanical{x}_0 + \int_0^t \mechanical{v}(t')\,dt'
                 = \mechanical{x}_0 + \mechanical{v}_0t + \frac{1}{2}\mechanical{a}_0t^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

The three kinematic equations are

$$
\mechanical{v}(t)=\mechanical{v}_0+\mechanical{a}_0t,
\qquad \mechanical{x}(t)=\mechanical{x}_0+\mechanical{v}_0t+\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{a}_0t^2,
\qquad \mechanical{v}^2=\mechanical{v}_0^2+2\mechanical{a}_0(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_0).
$$

To obtain the third equation, eliminate $t$ from the first two. For $\mechanical{a}_0\ne 0$, the first equation gives $t=(\mechanical{v}-\mechanical{v}_0)/\mechanical{a}_0$. Substitute this into $\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_0=\mechanical{v}_0t+\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{a}_0t^2$ and simplify to get $\mechanical{v}^2-\mechanical{v}_0^2=2\mechanical{a}_0(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_0)$. This equation is particularly useful when the problem does not specify time.

**Limiting case.** If $\mechanical{a}_0=0$, the motion is uniform along a straight line. The velocity $\mechanical{v}(t)=\mechanical{v}_0$ is constant, and $\mechanical{x}(t)=\mechanical{x}_0+\mechanical{v}_0t$.

> [!note] Physics I
> These three equations are basic tools for solving Physics I problems. Choose the sign of $\mechanical{a}_0$ to agree with the direction of the axis. In free fall, for example, $\mechanical{a}_0=-\mechanical{g}$ when the positive $\mechanical{y}$ axis points up.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The time elapsed since the initial state. |
| $\mechanical{x}, \mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinate | The position on the selected axis. |
| $\mechanical{x}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Initial position | The position at $t=0$. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The signed rate of change of position. |
| $\mechanical{v}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Initial velocity | The velocity at $t=0$. |
| $\mechanical{a}, \mechanical{a}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Constant acceleration | The signed rate of change of velocity. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
