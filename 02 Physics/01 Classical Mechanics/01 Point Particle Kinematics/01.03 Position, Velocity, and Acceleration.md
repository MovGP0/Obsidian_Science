---
title: "Position, Velocity, and Acceleration"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Velocity is the time derivative of position. Acceleration is the time derivative of velocity.

The motion of a point is given by its position as a function of time.

$$
\mechanical{\vec r}(t) = \bigl(\mechanical{x}(t), \mechanical{y}(t), \mechanical{z}(t)\bigr).
$$

> [!definition] Definition 1.1 — Velocity and Acceleration
> The **velocity** of a point is the time derivative of its position.
>
> $$
> \mechanical{\vec v} = \frac{d\mechanical{\vec r}}{dt} = \mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}.
> $$
>
> The **acceleration** is the derivative of the velocity with respect to time.
>
> $$
> \mechanical{\vec a} = \frac{d\mechanical{\vec v}}{dt} = \mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}.
> $$

In Cartesian coordinates, $\mechanical{\vec v} = (\mechanical{\dot x}, \mechanical{\dot y}, \mechanical{\dot z})$ and $\mechanical{\vec a} = (\mechanical{\ddot x}, \mechanical{\ddot y}, \mechanical{\ddot z})$. The magnitude of velocity, $\mechanical{v} = \lVert\mechanical{\vec v}\rVert$, is called **speed**. It is a nonnegative scalar.

**Average velocity and average acceleration.** Over the time interval $\Delta t = t_2-t_1$, define the averages as follows.

$$
\langle\mechanical{\vec v}\rangle = \frac{\mechanical{\vec r}(t_2)-\mechanical{\vec r}(t_1)}{\Delta t},
\qquad
\langle\mechanical{\vec a}\rangle = \frac{\mechanical{\vec v}(t_2)-\mechanical{\vec v}(t_1)}{\Delta t}.
$$

In the limit $\Delta t \to 0$, these averages give the instantaneous values $\mechanical{\vec v}(t)$ and $\mechanical{\vec a}(t)$.

### Example — Free Fall

A ball falls from rest through a height $\mechanical{h}$. Choose the vertical $\mechanical{y}$ axis to point down, so the acceleration due to gravity is $+\mechanical{g} > 0$. Place the origin at the initial height, so $\mechanical{y}(0)=0$. The ball starts from rest, so $\mechanical{\dot y}(0)=0$.

The equation of motion $\mechanical{\ddot y}=\mechanical{g}$ and these initial conditions give $\mechanical{\dot y}(t)=\mechanical{g}t$ and $\mechanical{y}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{g}t^2$. To find the fall time $t_c$, set $\mechanical{y}(t_c)=\mechanical{h}$.

$$
\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{g}t_c^2=\mechanical{h},
\qquad t_c=\sqrt{\frac{2\mechanical{h}}{\mechanical{g}}}.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The position of the point at time $t$. |
| $\mechanical{x}, \mechanical{y}, \mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Cartesian coordinates | The components of the position vector. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable that describes when the point is at a given position. |
| $\Delta t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time interval | The elapsed time between $t_1$ and $t_2$. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The rate of change of position. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The magnitude of the velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The rate of change of velocity. |
| $\langle\mechanical{\vec v}\rangle$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Average velocity | The change in position divided by the time interval. |
| $\langle\mechanical{\vec a}\rangle$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Average acceleration | The change in velocity divided by the time interval. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Fall height | The vertical distance that the ball falls. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $t_c$ | $\unit{s}$ | Fall time | The time for the ball to fall through the specified height. |
