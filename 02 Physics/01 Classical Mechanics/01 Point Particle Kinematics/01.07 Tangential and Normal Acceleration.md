---
title: "Tangential and Normal Acceleration"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Tangential acceleration changes speed. Normal acceleration changes the direction of motion.

For motion along a curve, resolve acceleration into one component along the path and another perpendicular to it. The *arc length* $\mechanical{s}(t)$ measures the distance traveled along the path. The unit tangent vector is

$$
\hat\tau=\frac{d\mechanical{\vec r}}{d\mechanical{s}}.
$$

The velocity is $\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\dot s}\hat\tau$, where $\mechanical{\dot s}=d\mechanical{s}/dt$ is the speed. Differentiation gives

$$
\mechanical{\vec a}=\mechanical{\ddot s}\hat\tau+\mechanical{\dot s}\frac{d\hat\tau}{dt}.
$$

The first term gives the **tangential component**, $\mechanical{a}_\tau=\mechanical{\ddot s}$. For the second term, $d\hat\tau/dt=\mechanical{\dot s}\,d\hat\tau/d\mechanical{s}=(\mechanical{\dot s}/\mechanical{R})\hat n$, where $\hat n$ is the **principal unit normal** and $\mechanical{R}$ is the local **radius of curvature**. Therefore,

$$
\mechanical{\vec a}=\mechanical{\ddot s}\hat\tau+\frac{\mechanical{\dot s}^{\,2}}{\mechanical{R}}\hat n.
$$

**Normal component.** The normal acceleration $\mechanical{a}_n=\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R}$ is present when a particle moves along a curved path, even at constant speed. It changes the direction of the velocity without changing its magnitude.

**Tangential component.** The tangential acceleration $\mechanical{a}_\tau$ changes the speed. It is positive when speed increases, negative when speed decreases, and zero at constant speed.

### Example — A Car Accelerating on a Curve

A car follows a curve of radius $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{100}{m}$. Its initial speed is $\mechanical{v}_0=\quantity{20}{m\,s^{-1}}$, and its tangential acceleration is $\mechanical{a}_\tau=\quantity{2}{m\,s^{-2}}$. The initial acceleration components are

$$
\mechanical{a}_n=\frac{\mechanical{v}_0^2}{\mechanical{R}}=\quantity{4}{m\,s^{-2}},
\qquad
\mechanical{a}_\tau=\quantity{2}{m\,s^{-2}}.
$$

The magnitude is $\mechanical{a}=\sqrt{\mechanical{a}_n^2+\mechanical{a}_\tau^2}\approx\quantity{4.47}{m\,s^{-2}}$. The angle with the unit tangent is $\arctan(\mechanical{a}_n/\mechanical{a}_\tau)\approx 63\unit{{}^\circ}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The position of the particle along the curve. |
| $\mechanical{s}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Arc length | The distance traveled along the curve. |
| $\mechanical{v}, \mechanical{\dot s}, \mechanical{v}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The rate of change of arc length, including the initial speed. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The rate of change of the position vector. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The rate of change of velocity. |
| $\mechanical{a}_\tau, \mechanical{\ddot s}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Tangential acceleration | The rate of change of speed. |
| $\mechanical{a}_n$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Normal acceleration | The acceleration component along the principal normal. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration magnitude | The magnitude of the total acceleration vector. |
| — | $\unit{rad}$ | Acceleration angle | The angle between the total acceleration and the unit tangent. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radius of curvature | The radius of the circle that locally matches the curvature of the path. |
