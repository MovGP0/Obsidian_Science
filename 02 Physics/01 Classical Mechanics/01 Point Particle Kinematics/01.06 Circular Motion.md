---
title: "Circular Motion"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In circular motion, velocity is tangential and acceleration has radial and tangential components.

For circular motion with radius $\mechanical{R}$ and angular position $\mechanical{\theta}(t)$, the position relative to the center is

$$
\mechanical{\vec r}(t)=\mechanical{R}\bigl(\cos\mechanical{\theta}(t)\,\hat{\imath}+\sin\mechanical{\theta}(t)\,\hat{\jmath}\bigr).
$$

The angular velocity is $\mechanical{\omega}=\mechanical{\dot\theta}$. The angular acceleration is $\mechanical{\alpha}=\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$.

Use the chain rule, $\frac{d}{dt}\cos\mechanical{\theta}=-\mechanical{\dot\theta}\sin\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\sin\mechanical{\theta}=\mechanical{\dot\theta}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$. This gives

$$
\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\dot\theta}(-\sin\mechanical{\theta},\cos\mechanical{\theta})
=\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\omega}\hat e_\theta,
$$

where $\hat e_\theta=(-\sin\mechanical{\theta},\cos\mechanical{\theta})$ is the tangential unit vector. For positive $\mechanical{\omega}$, the speed is $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\omega}$.

Differentiate again to obtain

$$
\mechanical{\dot{\vec v}}
=\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\ddot\theta}(-\sin\mechanical{\theta},\cos\mechanical{\theta})
+\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}(-\cos\mechanical{\theta},-\sin\mechanical{\theta}).
$$

The acceleration has a tangential component and a centripetal component.

$$
\mechanical{\vec a}=\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\alpha}\hat e_\theta-\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\omega}^2\hat e_r,
$$

where $\hat e_r=(\cos\mechanical{\theta},\sin\mechanical{\theta})$ points from the center to the particle. The signed radial component is $-\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\omega}^2=-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R}$. It is present even in uniform circular motion, where $\mechanical{\omega}$ is constant and $\mechanical{\alpha}=0$. It continuously changes the direction of the velocity while the speed stays constant. The magnitude of the centripetal acceleration is

$$
\boxed{\mechanical{a}_c=\mechanical{\omega}^2\mechanical{R}=\frac{\mechanical{v}^2}{\mechanical{R}}.}
$$

**Period and frequency.** In uniform circular motion starting at zero angle, $\mechanical{\theta}(t)=\mechanical{\omega}t$. For positive angular velocity, the period is $\signal{T}=2\pi/\mechanical{\omega}$, and the frequency is $\signal{f}=1/\signal{T}=\mechanical{\omega}/(2\pi)$.

### Example — Satellite in a Low Circular Orbit

A satellite in a circular orbit at height $\mechanical{h}\ll\mechanical{R}_T$ has $\mechanical{a}_c\approx\mechanical{g}$, where $\mechanical{g}$ is the acceleration due to gravity at Earth's surface. From $\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R}_T=\mechanical{g}$, its speed is $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}_T}\approx\quantity{7.9}{km\,s^{-1}}$. The period is $\signal{T}=2\pi\mechanical{R}_T/\mechanical{v}\approx\quantity{5063}{s}\approx\quantity{84}{min}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radius | The distance from the center to the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angular position | The angle from the positive horizontal axis to the radius vector. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The displacement from the center to the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of angular position. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The rate of change of angular velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The tangential rate of change of position. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The magnitude of the velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The sum of the tangential and radial acceleration vectors. |
| $\mechanical{a}_c$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Centripetal acceleration magnitude | The magnitude of the inward radial acceleration. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The time for one revolution. |
| $\signal{f}$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Frequency | The number of revolutions per unit time. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Orbit height | The height of the satellite above Earth's surface. |
| $\mechanical{R}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Earth radius | The radius of Earth, used as an approximation of the orbit radius. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of gravitational acceleration at Earth's surface. |
