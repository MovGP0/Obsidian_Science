---
title: "Historical Background"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Galileo used ideal experiments and mathematics to establish the study of motion as a separate field.

Kinematics began as a separate science in the seventeenth century. Galileo Galilei studied falling bodies and pendulums systematically. He introduced the *ideal experiment*, which sets aside the effects of friction and air resistance, and the *mathematical* formulation of the laws of motion. He described free fall as motion with constant acceleration and tested this description on inclined planes at different angles.

His description and tests are among the first examples of physical science in the modern sense.

Isaac Newton extended kinematics to vectors and united it with dynamics in his *Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica* (1687). Newton's formulation gave one mathematical description for both the motion of the Moon and the fall of an apple from a tree.

In the twentieth century, Einstein (1905) extended kinematics to speeds near the speed of light $c$. Classical kinematics remains an excellent approximation at speeds much lower than $c$.
