> [!TLDR]
> Apply Newton's second law to each body and use the constraints to relate their accelerations.

## Two Blocks Coupled by Gravity

Two blocks with masses $\mechanical{m}_1$ and $\mechanical{m}_2$ are connected by an inextensible cord. The first block slides on a horizontal surface. The second hangs from the cord, which passes over a pulley at the edge. Neglect friction and the masses of the pulley and cord.

- On $\mechanical{m}_1$, tension $\mechanical{T}$ acts horizontally toward the pulley. Weight $\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{g}$ acts down, and normal force $\mechanical{N}$ acts up. The equations are $\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{T}$ and $\mechanical{N}=\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{g}$.
- On $\mechanical{m}_2$, tension acts up, and weight $\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}$ acts down. The equation is $\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}-\mechanical{T}$. The downward acceleration of the hanging block equals the horizontal acceleration of the first block in magnitude.

The solution is

$$
\mechanical{a}=\frac{\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2},
\qquad
\mechanical{T}=\frac{\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}.
$$

For $\mechanical{m}_2\gg\mechanical{m}_1$, the acceleration tends to $\mechanical{g}$, as in free fall. For $\mechanical{m}_1\gg\mechanical{m}_2$, it tends to zero, and the system is nearly static.

## Skier on a Slope with Friction

A skier of mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{70}{kg}$ descends a slope of angle $\mechanical{\alpha}=15\unit{{}^\circ}$ with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mechanical{\mu}_d=0.05$. The net force down the slope is

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\sin\mechanical{\alpha}
-\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\mechanical{\alpha}
\approx\quantity{145}{N}.
$$

The acceleration is $\mechanical{a}\approx\quantity{2.07}{m\,s^{-2}}$. At $\mechanical{\alpha}=\arctan\mechanical{\mu}_d\approx2.86\unit{{}^\circ}$, the acceleration is zero and the skier maintains a constant speed.

## Car on a Banked Curve

A curve of radius $\mechanical{R}$ is banked at angle $\mechanical{\beta}$. At the speed that requires no friction, the horizontal component of the normal force gives the centripetal force. The vertical component balances the weight.

$$
\mechanical{N}\sin\mechanical{\beta}=\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2}{\mechanical{R}},
\qquad
\mechanical{N}\cos\mechanical{\beta}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}.
$$

Thus $\mechanical{v}^2=\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}\tan\mechanical{\beta}$. For $\mechanical{\beta}=30\unit{{}^\circ}$ and $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{100}{m}$, $\mechanical{v}\approx\quantity{23.8}{m\,s^{-1}}$. Race tracks are designed for typical race speeds.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}, \mechanical{m}_1, \mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The masses of the skier, car, and blocks. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The acceleration of a block or the skier along its direction of motion. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{T}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Tension | The force transmitted by the cord. |
| $\mechanical{N}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Normal force | The magnitude of the supporting force perpendicular to the surface. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Slope angle | The angle of the slope above the horizontal. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}_d$ | 1 | Coefficient of kinetic friction | The ratio of sliding friction magnitude to normal force. |
| — | $\unit{N}$ | Net force down the slope | The component of weight along the slope minus kinetic friction. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Curve radius | The radius of the car's path. |
| $\mechanical{\beta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Bank angle | The angle of the road surface above the horizontal. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Design speed | The speed at which the car can follow the curve without friction. |
