title: "Second Law – 𝐅 = m𝐚"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In an inertial frame, the net force on a body of constant mass equals its mass times its acceleration.

> [!definition] Definition 2.2 — Newton's Second Law
> In an inertial frame, the net force $\mechanical{\vec F}$ on a body of mass $\mechanical{m}$ determines its acceleration.
>
> $$
> \mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec a}.
> $$
>
> In momentum form,
>
> $$
> \mechanical{\vec F}=\frac{d\mechanical{\vec p}}{dt},
> \qquad
> \mechanical{\vec p}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v},
> $$
>
> where $\mechanical{\vec p}$ is the momentum.

The form $\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec a}$ applies to constant mass. The momentum form provides the starting point for systems with changing mass, such as a [[05.08 Variable-Mass Systems and the Rocket Equation|rocket]].

> [!note] Source wording
> The source states that $\mechanical{\vec F}=d\mechanical{\vec p}/dt$ applies to systems with changing mass. For an open system such as the rocket alone, the momentum balance must include the momentum carried by matter that enters or leaves the system.

**Inertial mass.** The mass $\mechanical{m}$ in the second law is *inertial mass*. It measures the body's resistance to acceleration. The mass in the [[06.02 Law of Gravitation|law of gravitation]] is *gravitational mass*. The equivalence principle in general relativity states their equivalence. The source gives an experimental agreement of approximately $10^{-13}$.

**Units.** In SI, mass is measured in $\unit{kg}$, acceleration in $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$, and force in $\unit{N}=\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}$.

### Example — Free Fall in a Uniform Gravitational Field

Near Earth's surface, a body of mass $\mechanical{m}$ has weight $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\hat{\jmath}$, with $\mechanical{g}\approx\quantity{9.81}{m\,s^{-2}}$. Newton's second law gives $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\hat{\jmath}$. Therefore $\mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}=-\mechanical{g}\hat{\jmath}$, independent of $\mechanical{m}$. In free fall, all bodies at the same location have the same acceleration. This is a manifestation of the equivalence principle.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Net force | The vector sum of the forces on the body. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Inertial mass | The proportionality between net force and acceleration for a body of constant mass. |
| $\mechanical{\vec a}, \mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The rate of change of velocity in an inertial frame. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The product of mass and velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The rate of change of position in the inertial frame. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the momentum derivative. |
| — | $\unit{kg}$ | Gravitational mass | The mass that enters the law of gravitation. |
| — | 1 | Relative precision | The reported precision of the comparison between inertial and gravitational mass. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration near Earth's surface. |
