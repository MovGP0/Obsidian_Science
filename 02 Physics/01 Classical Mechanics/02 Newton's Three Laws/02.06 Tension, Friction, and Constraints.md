---
title: "Tension, Friction, and Constraints"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Tension, friction, and reaction forces depend on the constraints and the motion of the system.

**Tension.** In an ideal cord with negligible mass, the tension $\mechanical{T}$ is the same at every point. In a heavy cord that hangs under its own weight, the tension varies as $\partial\mechanical{T}/\partial\mechanical{s}=\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{g}$. The distance $\mechanical{s}$ is measured upward from the bottom, and $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is the mass per unit length. Tension is zero at the free end and greatest at the suspension point.

**Static friction.** For surfaces in contact without sliding, the static friction force $\mechanical{\vec f}_s$ opposes the force that would start sliding. Its magnitude has an upper limit.

$$
\lVert\mechanical{\vec f}_s\rVert\le\mechanical{\mu}_s\mechanical{N},
$$

where $\mechanical{\mu}_s$ is the coefficient of static friction and $\mechanical{N}$ is the magnitude of the normal force.

**Kinetic friction.** When the surfaces slide, kinetic friction opposes the relative motion. Its magnitude is

$$
\lVert\mechanical{\vec f}_d\rVert=\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{N},
$$

with $\mechanical{\mu}_d<\mechanical{\mu}_s$ in general. In the standard Coulomb model, kinetic friction is independent of sliding speed.

### Example — Inclined Plane with Friction

A block of mass $\mechanical{m}$ rests on a plane inclined at angle $\mechanical{\alpha}$ with coefficient of static friction $\mechanical{\mu}_s$. It stays in equilibrium if $\tan\mechanical{\alpha}\le\mechanical{\mu}_s$. If $\tan\mechanical{\alpha}>\mechanical{\mu}_s$, the block slides. Its acceleration down the plane is $\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{g}(\sin\mechanical{\alpha}-\mechanical{\mu}_d\cos\mechanical{\alpha})$, where $\mechanical{\mu}_d$ is the coefficient of kinetic friction.

**Constraints.** A smooth surface is a *constraint* that limits motion to the surface while contact persists. It exerts a reaction force $\mechanical{\vec N}$ perpendicular to the surface. The kinematics of the problem determines the magnitude of this force. [[09.00 Constraints, Virtual Work, and d’Alembert’s Principle|Chapter 9]] treats constraints systematically.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{T}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Tension | The tensile force transmitted at a point of the cord. |
| $\mechanical{s}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Distance along the cord | The distance measured upward from the free end. |
| $\mechanical{\lambda}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-1}}$ | Linear mass density | The mass per unit length of the cord. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward gravitational acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{\vec f}_s, \mechanical{\vec f}_d$ | $\unit{N}$ | Friction force | The static or kinetic friction force at the contact. |
| $\mechanical{N}, \mechanical{\vec N}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Normal force | The magnitude and vector of the reaction perpendicular to the surface. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}_s, \mechanical{\mu}_d$ | 1 | Friction coefficients | The coefficients of static and kinetic friction. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the block on the incline. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Inclination | The angle of the plane above the horizontal. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The acceleration of the sliding block along the plane. |
