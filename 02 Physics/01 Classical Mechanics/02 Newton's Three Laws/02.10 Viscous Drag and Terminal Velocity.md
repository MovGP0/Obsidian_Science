---
title: "Viscous Drag and Terminal Velocity"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A falling body reaches terminal velocity when drag balances its weight. The drag law depends on the flow regime.

A body moving in a fluid experiences drag. At low speed in the Stokes regime, a common model is

$$
\mechanical{\vec F}_r=-\mechanical{b}\mechanical{\vec v}.
$$

At higher speeds, a common model is

$$
\mechanical{\vec F}_r=-\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{C}_d\mechanical{A}\mechanical{v}\mechanical{\vec v},
$$

where $\mechanical{C}_d$ is the drag coefficient and $\mechanical{A}$ is the frontal area.

**Stokes regime.** For a sphere of radius $\mechanical{R}$ in a fluid of dynamic viscosity $\mechanical{\eta}$, $\mechanical{b}=6\pi\mechanical{\eta}\mechanical{R}$.

**Terminal velocity.** For vertical fall with linear drag, take downward as positive. The equation of motion is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot v}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}-\mechanical{b}\mechanical{v}$. At terminal velocity, $\mechanical{\dot v}=0$, so

$$
\mechanical{v}_\infty=\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}}{\mechanical{b}}.
$$

For a body that starts from rest, the solution is $\mechanical{v}(t)=\mechanical{v}_\infty(1-e^{-\mechanical{b}t/\mechanical{m}})$ with characteristic time $\tau=\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{b}$. When $t\gg\tau$, the speed is nearly constant.

### Example — Raindrop

A spherical water drop of radius $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{1}{mm}$ falls through air with viscosity $\mechanical{\eta}\approx\quantity{1.8\times10^{-5}}{Pa\,s}$. For water density $\mechanical{\rho}_a=\quantity{1000}{kg\,m^{-3}}$, the mass is $\mechanical{m}=(4/3)\pi\mechanical{R}^3\mechanical{\rho}_a\approx\quantity{4.19\times10^{-6}}{kg}$. The Stokes coefficient is $\mechanical{b}=6\pi\mechanical{\eta}\mechanical{R}\approx\quantity{3.39\times10^{-7}}{kg\,s^{-1}}$.

The Stokes model gives $\mechanical{v}_\infty=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{b}\approx\quantity{121}{m\,s^{-1}}$. A drop of this size is already in the regime where drag scales approximately as $\mechanical{v}^2$. The source cites Foote and Du Toit (1969) for a measured terminal speed of approximately $\quantity{6.5}{m\,s^{-1}}$.

## Why linear drag changes a projectile’s horizontal motion

In still air with linear isotropic drag, the only horizontal force has component $-\mechanical{b}\mechanical{v}_x$. The horizontal velocity decays as $\mechanical{v}_x(t)=\mechanical{v}_x(0)e^{-t/\tau}$ with the same $\tau=\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{b}$ as vertical fall. In the vertical equation, gravity adds a constant force. The two equations can be solved independently in this model. In the quadratic law the factor $\mechanical{v}$ is the total speed relative to the fluid, so each component generally depends on the other. At fixed time the horizontal solution tends to constant velocity as $\mechanical{b}$ tends to zero.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_r$ | $\unit{N}$ | Drag force | The force opposing motion relative to the fluid. |
| $\mechanical{b}$ | $\unit{kg\,s^{-1}}$ | Linear drag coefficient | The proportionality between linear drag magnitude and speed. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}, \mechanical{v}, \mechanical{v}_x$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity and speed | Velocity relative to the fluid, its magnitude or downward component, and its horizontal component. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Ambient fluid density | The density of the fluid through which the body moves. |
| $\mechanical{C}_d$ | 1 | Drag coefficient | The dimensionless coefficient in the quadratic drag law. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Frontal area | The projected area normal to the flow. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Sphere radius | The radius of the sphere or drop. |
| $\mechanical{\eta}$ | $\unit{Pa\,s}$ | Dynamic viscosity | The viscosity of the ambient fluid. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the falling body. |
| $\mechanical{\dot v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Vertical acceleration | The rate of change of downward velocity. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{v}_\infty$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Terminal velocity | The steady falling speed at which drag balances weight. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The time since the body was released. |
| $\tau$ | $\unit{s}$ | Characteristic time | The timescale for the approach to terminal velocity under linear drag. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}_a$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Water density | The density used to calculate the drop mass. |
