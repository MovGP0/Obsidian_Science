---
title: "Kinetic Energy and the Work–Energy Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The work done by the net force equals the change in kinetic energy.

> [!definition] Definition 3.2 — Kinetic Energy
> A point particle of mass $\mechanical{m}$ and velocity $\mechanical{\vec v}$ has **kinetic energy**
>
> $$
> \energy{K}=\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2
> =\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}\cdot\mechanical{\vec v}.
> $$

Kinetic energy is a nonnegative scalar measured in joules ($\unit{J}$).

> [!theorem] Theorem 3.1 — Work–Energy Theorem
> A point particle moves from $A$ to $B$ under net force $\mechanical{\vec F}$. The work done by the net force equals the change in kinetic energy.
>
> $$
> \energy{W}_{A\to B}=\energy{K}_B-\energy{K}_A=\Delta\energy{K}.
> $$

**Proof.** Use $\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{m}\,d\mechanical{\vec v}/dt$ for a particle of constant mass.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{W}&=\int_A^B \mechanical{\vec F}\cdot d\mechanical{\vec r}
=\mechanical{m}\int_A^B\frac{d\mechanical{\vec v}}{dt}\cdot\mechanical{\vec v}\,dt\\
&=\mechanical{m}\int_A^B\frac{d}{dt}
\left(\tfrac12\mechanical{\vec v}\cdot\mechanical{\vec v}\right)\,dt
=\left.\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2\right|_A^B
=\Delta\energy{K}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\boxed{\energy{W}_{A\to B}=\energy{K}_B-\energy{K}_A.}
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v},\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity and speed | The particle's velocity and its magnitude. |
| $\energy{K},\energy{K}_A,\energy{K}_B,\Delta\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The energy of motion, its values at the endpoints, and its change. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Net force | The vector sum of the forces on the particle. |
| $\energy{W},\energy{W}_{A\to B}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Work | The work done by the net force along the particle's path. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The position of the particle along the path. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
