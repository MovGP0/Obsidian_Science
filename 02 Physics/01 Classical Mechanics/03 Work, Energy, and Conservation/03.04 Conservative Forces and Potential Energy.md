---
title: "Conservative Forces and Potential Energy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The work done by a conservative force depends only on the endpoints and equals the decrease in potential energy.

> [!definition] Definition 3.3 — Conservative Force
> A force $\mechanical{\vec F}$ is **conservative** if its work $\energy{W}_{A\to B}$ depends only on the endpoints $A$ and $B$.

Equivalently, $\oint\mechanical{\vec F}\cdot d\mechanical{\vec r}=0$ on every closed path. For a differentiable force in a simply connected region, conservativity is equivalent to $\nabla\times\mechanical{\vec F}=\vec 0$.

A conservative force has a **potential energy** $\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r})$ such that $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\nabla\energy{U}$. Its work between $A$ and $B$ is

$$
\energy{W}_{A\to B}=\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r}_A)-\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r}_B)
=-\Delta\energy{U}.
$$

Potential energy has units of energy. It is determined up to an additive constant.

**Examples.**

- Weight near the ground: $\energy{U}(\mechanical{y})=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{y}$.
- Elastic force: $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\mechanical{k}\mechanical{\vec x}$ and $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{x}^2$.
- Gravitation between two masses: $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})=-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}$.
- Coulomb force between two charges: $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})=\electric{q}_1\electric{q}_2/(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\mechanical{r})$.

**Nonconservative forces.** The work done by kinetic friction and viscous drag depends on the path. These forces have no position-dependent potential. When the surface or fluid is at rest in the chosen frame, they do negative work on a body that moves relative to it.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source describes the work of friction and drag as always negative for an unbound system. Work on an individual body depends on the frame and the motion of the surface or fluid. The dissipation of mechanical energy in relative motion is the frame-independent point of the distinction.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | The force whose work is evaluated. |
| $\energy{W}_{A\to B}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Work | The energy transferred by the force along the path. |
| $\energy{U},\Delta\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The energy associated with position and its change between endpoints. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{\vec r}_A,\mechanical{\vec r}_B$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The position along the path or at its endpoints. |
| $\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Height | The vertical position relative to the chosen zero. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The masses entering the gravitational potential energies. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the uniform near-surface acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The stiffness of the spring. |
| $\mechanical{\vec x},\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Spring displacement | The displacement from the unloaded position. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The distance between the two masses or charges. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant in Newton's law of gravitation. |
| $\electric{q}_1,\electric{q}_2$ | $\unit{C}$ | Electric charge | The charges of the two bodies. |
| $\electric{\varepsilon}_0$ | $\unit{F\,m^{-1}}$ | Vacuum permittivity | The permittivity used in Coulomb's law in vacuum. |
