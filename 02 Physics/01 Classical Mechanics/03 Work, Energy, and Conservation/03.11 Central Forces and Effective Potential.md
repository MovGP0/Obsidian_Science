---
title: "Central Forces and Effective Potential"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In central-force motion, conservation of angular momentum reduces the planar problem to radial motion in an effective potential.

For a particle in a central potential $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})$, the angular momentum $\mechanical{L}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}$ is conserved. The **effective potential** reduces the two-dimensional problem to a one-dimensional radial problem.

$$
\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r})=\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})
+\frac{\mechanical{L}^2}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2}.
$$

The total energy is $\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}+\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r})$.

**Physical meaning.** The term $\mechanical{L}^2/(2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2)$ is the *centrifugal barrier*. It diverges as $\mechanical{r}\to 0$ when $\mechanical{L}\ne0$. If the central potential diverges to $-\infty$ more slowly than $1/\mechanical{r}^2$, the barrier still prevents the particle from reaching the origin.

Analyze the radial motion as in a one-dimensional problem. Plot $\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r})$ and identify the turning points and minima. See [[06.00 Gravitation and Motion Under Central Forces|Chapter 6]] for the full treatment.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance | The distance from the center of force. |
| $\mechanical{\dot r}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Radial velocity | The rate of change of radial distance. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The conserved angular momentum component perpendicular to the orbital plane. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\phi}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of the polar angle. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Central potential energy | The potential energy associated with the central force. |
| $\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Effective potential energy | The central potential energy plus the centrifugal barrier. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Mechanical energy | The conserved sum of radial kinetic energy and effective potential energy. |
