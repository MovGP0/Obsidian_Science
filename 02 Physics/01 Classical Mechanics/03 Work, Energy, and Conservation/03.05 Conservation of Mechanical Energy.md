---
title: "Conservation of Mechanical Energy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> When only conservative forces act on a point particle, the sum of its kinetic and potential energies stays constant.

> [!theorem] Theorem 3.2 — Conservation of Mechanical Energy
> For a point particle subject only to conservative forces, the total mechanical energy $\energy{E}=\energy{K}+\energy{U}$ is constant in time.

**Proof.** The work–energy theorem gives $\energy{W}=\Delta\energy{K}$, and conservativity gives $\energy{W}=-\Delta\energy{U}$. Thus $\Delta(\energy{K}+\energy{U})=0$.

**Practical consequence.** Energy conservation can solve problems without integrating the equation of motion. If the total energy and potential energy are known, the speed at each position is

$$
\mechanical{v}(\mechanical{\vec r})=
\sqrt{\frac{2[\energy{E}-\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r})]}{\mechanical{m}}}.
$$

Motion is restricted to the region in which $\energy{E}\ge\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r})$.

### Example — Ball Sliding Down a Smooth Incline

A ball starts from rest and slides down a smooth incline from height $\mechanical{h}$. Initially $\energy{E}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}$. At the bottom $\energy{U}=0$, so $\energy{K}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}$ and $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}$. The result is the same as in free fall and is independent of the slope angle.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source heading describes a rolling ball, but the text and equations treat it as sliding without rotational kinetic energy.

### Example — Ball on an Incline with Friction

Now include kinetic friction with coefficient $\mechanical{\mu}_d$. The friction is nonconservative, and the mechanical energy decreases. The loss $\energy{E}_A-\energy{E}_B$ equals the energy dissipated by friction, $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{fr}}=\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\mechanical{\alpha}\,\mechanical{L}$, where $\mechanical{L}$ is the distance traveled along the incline.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{E},\energy{E}_A,\energy{E}_B$ | $\unit{J}$ | Mechanical energy | The sum of kinetic and potential energy, including values at the endpoints. |
| $\energy{K},\Delta\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The energy of motion and its change. |
| $\energy{U},\Delta\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The energy associated with position and its change. |
| $\energy{W}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Work | The energy transferred by forces along the path. |
| $\energy{W}_{\mathrm{fr}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy dissipated by friction | The positive magnitude of the mechanical energy loss. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The magnitude of the particle's velocity at a given position. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The point at which the potential energy is evaluated. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the moving body. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Initial height | The vertical distance above the bottom of the incline. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}_d$ | 1 | Coefficient of kinetic friction | The ratio of kinetic friction magnitude to normal force. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Slope angle | The angle of the incline above the horizontal. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Distance along the incline | The length of the path traveled against friction. |
