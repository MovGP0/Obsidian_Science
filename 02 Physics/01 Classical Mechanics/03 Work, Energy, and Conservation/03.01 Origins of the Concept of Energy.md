---
title: "Origins of the Concept of Energy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The modern concepts of work and energy grew from studies of motion, hydraulics, and mechanical forces.

The concept of energy developed gradually. The main early steps were:

- **Gottfried Leibniz (1646–1716)** introduced *vis viva*, $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2$ without the factor $1/2$, in 1686. He proposed it as a conserved quantity as an alternative to Descartes's *quantity of motion*. The dispute between the followers of Descartes and Leibniz lasted for decades.
- **Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782)** developed hydraulics and introduced hydraulic work. He formulated the principle that bears his name.
- **Gustave Coriolis (1792–1843)** introduced the factor $1/2$ in the modern expression $\energy{K}=\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2$ in 1829. He defined work as $\energy{W}=\mechanical{\vec F}\cdot\mechanical{\vec d}$.
- **William John Macquorn Rankine (1820–1872)** introduced the term *potential energy* in 1853.
- **Hermann von Helmholtz (1821–1894)** formulated energy conservation in a general form that included heat in 1847.

The unification of kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, and electromagnetic energy was a central result of nineteenth-century physics. The first law of thermodynamics, formulated by Mayer, Joule, Helmholtz, and Clausius, established energy conservation as a universal law.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the moving body. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The speed of the moving body. |
| $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2$ | $\unit{J}$ | Vis viva | Leibniz's quantity, equal to twice modern kinetic energy. |
| $\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The energy associated with the motion of a body. |
| $\energy{W}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Work | The scalar product of constant force and displacement. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | The constant force that does work. |
| $\mechanical{\vec d}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement | The change in position of the body. |
