title: "Worked Examples in Energy Methods"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Use energy conservation and contact conditions to find the motion of a body along a guide.

## Ball in a Vertical Loop

A ball starts from rest at height $\mechanical{h}$ and enters a vertical loop of radius $\mechanical{R}$ at the bottom. Find the minimum height at which it can complete the loop while staying in contact with the guide.

At the top, the weight and normal reaction $\mechanical{N}$ both point toward the center. Newton's second law along the radius gives $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}+\mechanical{N}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R}$. The guide can push the ball, so contact requires $\mechanical{N}\ge 0$. Thus $\mechanical{v}^2\ge\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}$, with gravity alone supplying the centripetal force in the limiting case.

Energy conservation from the start to the top gives

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}
=2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2
\ge 2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}.
$$

Thus $\mechanical{h}\ge 5\mechanical{R}/2$. The result is independent of mass.

## Pulley with Masses and Friction

A mass $\mechanical{m}_1=\quantity{3}{kg}$ hangs from a cord that passes over a pulley at the edge of a horizontal surface. The cord connects it to a mass $\mechanical{m}_2=\quantity{5}{kg}$ on the surface, with coefficient of kinetic friction $\mechanical{\mu}_d=0.2$.

The system starts from rest, and $\mechanical{m}_1$ descends through $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{1}{m}$. Find the speed of the system. The energy balance includes the work lost to friction.

$$
\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}-\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}
=\tfrac12(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{v}^2.
$$

Substitution gives $29.43\unit{J}-9.81\unit{J}=\quantity{19.62}{J}$ for the net energy transferred to the motion. Thus $\mechanical{v}^2=\quantity{4.91}{m^2\,s^{-2}}$ and $\mechanical{v}\approx\quantity{2.21}{m\,s^{-1}}$.

## Pendulum with a Breaking Cord

A pendulum of length $\mechanical{\ell}$ oscillates with amplitude $\mechanical{\theta}_0$. At the bottom of its swing the cord breaks. The ball continues as a projectile with horizontal speed $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta}_0)}$. From the break point, taken as zero height, the ball follows a parabolic path. The horizontal range depends on the vertical distance from the break point to the ground. Without this distance, the range is undetermined. The motion combines constant horizontal velocity with vertical free fall.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source also describes the ground as being at the height of the break point. A horizontal launch has a nonzero flight time to a horizontal surface only if that surface lies below the break point.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the ball or of either load in the pulley system. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Vertical distance | The release height above the loop base or the distance descended by the hanging mass. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Loop radius | The distance from the center of the loop to the guide. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The speed evaluated at the point specified in each example. |
| $\mechanical{N}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Normal force | The inward reaction from the guide at the top of the loop. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}_d$ | 1 | Coefficient of kinetic friction | The ratio of the friction force magnitude to the normal force. |
| — | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The gravitational energy released, the energy dissipated, or the resulting kinetic energy of the pulley system. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum length | The distance from the pivot to the pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}_0$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angular amplitude | The largest angle between the cord and the downward vertical. |
