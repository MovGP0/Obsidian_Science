---
title: "Two-Body Problem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Center-of-mass coordinates separate the motion of two bodies into uniform center-of-mass motion and relative motion with the reduced mass.

Two masses $\mechanical{m}_1$ and $\mechanical{m}_2$ interact through a central force that depends on $\mechanical{\vec r}=\mechanical{\vec r}_1-\mechanical{\vec r}_2$. In center-of-mass and relative coordinates, Newton's equations separate.

$$
\mechanical{M}\mechanical{\ddot{\vec R}}=\vec 0,
\qquad
\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}=\mechanical{\vec F}(\mechanical{\vec r}),
$$

where $\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{m}_2/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)$ is the **reduced mass**. The relative motion is a one-body problem of mass $\mechanical{\mu}$ in a field centered at the origin.

**Polar coordinates.** In the plane of motion, use $(\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\phi})$.

$$
\mechanical{\mu}(\mechanical{\ddot r}-\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\dot\phi}^{\,2})=\mechanical{F}(\mechanical{r}),
\qquad
\mechanical{\mu}(\mechanical{r}\mechanical{\ddot\phi}+2\mechanical{\dot r}\mechanical{\dot\phi})=0.
$$

The second equation gives constant angular momentum $\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}=\mechanical{L}$. The first becomes

$$
\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{\ddot r}=\mechanical{F}(\mechanical{r})+\frac{\mechanical{L}^2}{\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r}^3}
=-\frac{d\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}}{d\mechanical{r}}.
$$

The **effective potential** is $\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r})=\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})+\mechanical{L}^2/(2\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{r}^2)$. The planar problem is now a one-dimensional radial problem.

### Example — Classical Hydrogen Atom

An electron and proton interacting through $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})=-\electric{k}\electric{e}^2/\mechanical{r}$ give the same mathematical form as the gravitational problem. An accurate model of the atom requires quantum mechanics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2,\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The two particle masses and their sum. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Reduced mass | The effective mass for relative motion. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}_1,\mechanical{\vec r}_2,\mechanical{\vec R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The particle positions and center-of-mass position. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The relative displacement and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot{\vec R}},\mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The center-of-mass and relative accelerations. |
| $\mechanical{\dot r},\mechanical{\ddot r}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}},\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Radial derivatives | The first and second time derivatives of separation. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F},\mechanical{F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Central force | The interaction force and its radial component. |
| $\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Polar angle | The angle in the plane of relative motion. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\phi},\mechanical{\ddot\phi}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}},\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular derivatives | The first and second time derivatives of the polar angle. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The conserved component perpendicular to the plane. |
| $\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Effective potential energy | The potential energy for the reduced radial problem. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Interaction potential energy | The potential energy of the central interaction. |
| $\electric{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^2\,C^{-2}}$ | Coulomb constant | The proportionality constant for the electrostatic interaction. |
| $\electric{e}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Elementary charge | The magnitude of the electron and proton charges. |
