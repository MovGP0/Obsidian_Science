---
title: "Gravitational Field and Gravitational Potential"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The gravitational field is force per unit test mass. It is the negative gradient of the gravitational potential.

**Gravitational field.** The field of a point mass $\mechanical{M}$ is $\mechanical{\vec g}(\mechanical{\vec r})=-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}\hat r/\mechanical{r}^2$. The force on a test mass $\mechanical{m}$ is $\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec g}(\mechanical{\vec r})$.

**Gravitational potential.** The field is conservative, with $\mechanical{\vec g}=-\nabla\mechanical{\Phi}$ and

$$
\mechanical{\Phi}(\mechanical{\vec r})=-\frac{\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}}.
$$

The potential energy of the test mass is $\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r})=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\Phi}(\mechanical{\vec r})=-\mechanical{G}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}$.

**Sign convention.** Choose $\mechanical{\Phi}(\infty)=0$. The potential is negative at finite distance and decreases as the test mass approaches the source.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The source and test masses. |
| $\mechanical{\vec g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational field | The gravitational force per unit test mass. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The displacement from the source and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant of proportionality in Newton's law. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Gravitational force | The force on the test mass. |
| $\mechanical{\Phi}$ | $\unit{J\,kg^{-1}}$ | Gravitational potential | The gravitational potential energy per unit test mass. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Gravitational potential energy | The potential energy of the test mass relative to infinity. |
