---
title: "Central Forces and Conservation of Angular Momentum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A central force has no torque about its center, so angular momentum about that point is conserved.

A **central force** points toward or away from a fixed point $O$. It depends only on the distance from that point.

$$
\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{F}(\mechanical{r})\hat r.
$$

> [!theorem] Theorem 6.1 — Conservation of Angular Momentum
> Under a central force, $\mechanical{\vec L}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec v}$ about $O$ stays constant.

**Proof.** The torque about $O$ is $d\mechanical{\vec L}/dt=\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec F}=\mechanical{F}(\mechanical{r})\mechanical{\vec r}\times\hat r=\vec 0$.

**Consequences.**

- **Planar motion.** By definition, $\mechanical{\vec r}\perp\mechanical{\vec L}$. If $\mechanical{\vec L}\ne\vec 0$, the plane perpendicular to it is fixed and the entire trajectory lies in that plane.
- **Constant areal velocity.** The radius vector sweeps area at the constant rate $d\mechanical{A}/dt=\mechanical{L}/(2\mechanical{m})$. This is Kepler's second law.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F},\mechanical{F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Central force | The radial force vector and its signed radial component. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance | The distance from the center of force. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position vector | The displacement from the center of force to the particle. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The velocity of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec L},\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The angular momentum vector and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Swept area | The area swept by the radius vector in the orbital plane. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
