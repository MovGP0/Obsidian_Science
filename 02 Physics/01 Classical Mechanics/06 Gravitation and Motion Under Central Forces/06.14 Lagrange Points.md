---
title: "Lagrange Points"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Lagrange points are equilibrium positions in a frame rotating with two orbiting masses.

The restricted three-body problem treats a test body of negligible mass under the gravitational influence of two bodies of masses $\mechanical{m}_1$ and $\mechanical{m}_2$.

When the two masses orbit each other on circular paths, there are five **Lagrange points** $L_1$ to $L_5$. A test body can stay in equilibrium at these points in the frame that rotates with the pair.

- **Collinear points $L_1$, $L_2$, and $L_3$.** These lie on the line between the primary masses or on its extensions. They are unstable.
- **Triangular points $L_4$ and $L_5$.** These form an equilateral triangle with the two masses on either side of the line. They are linearly stable under small disturbances when $(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)^2/(\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{m}_2)>27$. For $\mechanical{m}_1\ge\mechanical{m}_2$, the threshold mass ratio is approximately $24.96$.

> [!note] Source threshold
> The source includes equality in the stability condition. The usual linear stability condition is strict. Equality is the transition point.

**Example — Trojan asteroids.** Thousands of asteroids occupy stable regions near $L_4$ and $L_5$ in the Sun–Jupiter system. They are called Trojans. Other planets also have Trojans. The source gives asteroid 2010 TK₇ as an example for Earth.

**Space observatories.** The James Webb Space Telescope uses an orbit around the Sun–Earth $L_2$ region, which requires station-keeping. Planck, Herschel, and Gaia also used the Sun–Earth $L_2$ region.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source describes Webb as being at $L_2$. Its orbit is around the region near the equilibrium point.
