---
title: "Cosmic Velocities"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The cosmic velocities are conventional speed scales for orbit and escape from Earth and the Solar System.

Historically, several characteristic speeds are defined for a body launched from Earth's surface.

**First cosmic velocity.** This is the speed of a low circular orbit around Earth in the near-surface approximation.

$$
\mechanical{v}_1=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}_E}\approx\quantity{7.91}{km\,s^{-1}}.
$$

**Second cosmic velocity.** This is the escape speed from Earth's surface.

$$
\mechanical{v}_2=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}_E}\approx\quantity{11.19}{km\,s^{-1}}.
$$

**Third cosmic velocity.** The minimum ideal launch speed for escape from the Solar System starting at Earth is $\mechanical{v}_3\approx\quantity{16.6}{km\,s^{-1}}$. The calculation includes Earth's orbital velocity around the Sun.

**Fourth cosmic velocity.** The source gives $\mechanical{v}_4\approx\quantity{525}{km\,s^{-1}}$ as a model-dependent estimate for escape from the Galaxy. The estimate depends on the adopted Galactic halo.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |

| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of Earth's near-surface gravitational acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{R}_E$ | $\unit{m}$ | Earth radius | The distance from Earth's center to its surface in the spherical approximation. |
| $\mechanical{v}_2$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Second cosmic velocity | The ideal escape speed from Earth's surface. |
