---
title: "Tidal Forces"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Tidal effects arise because gravity varies across an extended body. A strong enough variation can disrupt a nearby moon.

In a gravitationally bound system, the field gradient produces differences in gravitational acceleration across a body. Earth's ocean tides result from the variation of the Moon's attraction between the near and far sides of Earth.

**Estimate.** For a body of radial extent $\Delta\mechanical{r}$ at distance $\mechanical{r}$ from a mass $\mechanical{M}$, the difference in gravitational acceleration is approximately

$$
\Delta\mechanical{g}\approx\frac{2\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{r}^3}\Delta\mechanical{r}.
$$

**Roche limit.** A moon that orbits too close to its planet can break apart. Estimate the limit by balancing the tidal acceleration against the moon's self-gravity.

$$
\mechanical{d}_R\approx\mechanical{R}_P
\left(\frac{2\mechanical{\rho}_P}{\mechanical{\rho}_M}\right)^{1/3},
$$

where $\mechanical{R}_P$ and $\mechanical{\rho}_P$ are the radius and density of the planet, and $\mechanical{\rho}_M$ is the moon's density. This estimate neglects deformation of the moon. The particles in Saturn's rings orbit within the Roche limit, where tides inhibit their formation into a stable moon.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{r},\Delta\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance and extent | The distance from the source and the size of the body along that direction. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Source mass | The mass that produces the external gravitational field. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant in Newton's law of gravitation. |
| $\Delta\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Tidal acceleration difference | The change in gravitational acceleration across the radial extent of the body. |
| $\mechanical{d}_R$ | $\unit{m}$ | Roche limit | The estimated distance where the tidal effect balances the moon's self-gravity. |
| $\mechanical{R}_P$ | $\unit{m}$ | Planet radius | The radius of the primary planet. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}_P,\mechanical{\rho}_M$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mean density | The mean mass densities of the planet and moon. |
