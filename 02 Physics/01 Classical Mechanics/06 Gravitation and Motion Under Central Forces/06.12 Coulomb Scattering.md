> [!TLDR]
> A repulsive inverse-distance potential deflects a particle along a hyperbola. Rutherford's formula gives the differential cross section.

For a repulsive potential $\energy{U}=\alpha/\mechanical{r}$, as in Rutherford scattering between charges of the same sign, the orbit is a hyperbola. The **impact parameter** $\mechanical{b}$ is the perpendicular distance from the force center to the incoming asymptote.

**Deflection angle.** For reduced mass $\mechanical{\mu}$, incident relative speed $\mechanical{v}_\infty$, and impact parameter $\mechanical{b}$,

$$
\tan\!\left(\frac{\mechanical{\theta}}{2}\right)=
\frac{\alpha}{\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{b}\mechanical{v}_\infty^2}.
$$

**Differential cross section.** For a solid-angle element $d\Omega$ at scattering angle $\mechanical{\theta}$, the Rutherford formula is

$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\Omega}=
\left(\frac{\alpha}{4\energy{E}\sin^2(\mechanical{\theta}/2)}\right)^2,
$$

where $\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}_\infty^2$ is the kinetic energy of the relative motion in the center-of-mass frame. Rutherford derived the formula classically in 1911 to explain alpha-particle scattering from gold foil. The experimental agreement gave evidence for the nuclear structure of the atom.

### Example — Rutherford Scattering

Alpha particles of energy $\energy{E}=\quantity{5}{MeV}$ strike gold nuclei ($Z=79$). For backscattering angles $\mechanical{\theta}>90\unit{{}^\circ}$, the cross section is small but nonzero. Thomson's model with diffuse positive charge predicted negligible backscattering. The discrepancy showed that the positive charge is concentrated in a compact nucleus.

## What large-angle events tell you about the charge distribution

In a thin-foil scattering experiment, rare large deflections probe close encounters. In the single-collision, nonrelativistic model with a heavy target, a repulsive point charge predicts a strong angular dependence and permits backscattering. Compare counts at different angles after correcting for detector solid angle and exposure. A diffuse charge distribution changes the short-distance force and can suppress the large deflections. Multiple collisions in a thick foil or a projectile energetic enough to probe nuclear size would require an extension of the single point-charge model.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The repulsive interaction energy. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Coulomb coupling | The positive strength of the inverse-distance potential. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | The distance from the force center. |
| $\mechanical{b}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Impact parameter | The distance from the force center to the incoming asymptote. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Reduced mass | The effective mass of the relative motion. |
| $\mechanical{v}_\infty$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Incident relative speed | The speed at large separation. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Scattering angle | The change in direction between the incoming and outgoing velocities. |
| $d\Omega$ | $\unit{sr}$ | Solid-angle element | The small range of outgoing directions. |
| $d\mechanical{\sigma}/d\Omega$ | $\unit{m^2\,sr^{-1}}$ | Differential cross section | The effective scattering area per unit solid angle. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Relative kinetic energy | The kinetic energy at large separation in the center-of-mass frame. |
| $Z$ | 1 | Atomic number | The number of protons in the target nucleus. |
