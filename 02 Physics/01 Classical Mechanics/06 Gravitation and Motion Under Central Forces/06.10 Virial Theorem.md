---
title: "Virial Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For bounded motion in a power-law potential, the virial theorem relates the time-averaged kinetic and potential energies.

For bounded motion with potential $\energy{U}\propto\mechanical{r}^n$, the time averages satisfy

$$
2\langle\energy{K}\rangle=n\langle\energy{U}\rangle.
$$

For $n=-1$, as in Coulomb and gravitational potentials, $\langle\energy{K}\rangle=-\tfrac12\langle\energy{U}\rangle$. Thus $\langle\energy{E}\rangle=\langle\energy{K}\rangle+\langle\energy{U}\rangle=\tfrac12\langle\energy{U}\rangle=-\langle\energy{K}\rangle$.

For $n=2$, as in the harmonic oscillator, $\langle\energy{K}\rangle=\langle\energy{U}\rangle$. The two forms of energy contribute equally on average.

> [!note] Analytical Mechanics
> The virial theorem gives average quantities without a full solution of the equations of motion. Applications include estimates of galaxy cluster masses, kinetic theory of gases, and binding energies in nuclear physics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{K},\langle\energy{K}\rangle$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The energy of motion and its time average. |
| $\energy{U},\langle\energy{U}\rangle$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The power-law potential energy and its time average. |
| $\langle\energy{E}\rangle$ | $\unit{J}$ | Average total energy | The sum of average kinetic and potential energies. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance | The coordinate that enters the power-law potential. |
