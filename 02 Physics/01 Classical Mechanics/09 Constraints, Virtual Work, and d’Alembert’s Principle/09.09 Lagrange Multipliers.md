---
title: "Lagrange Multipliers"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A Lagrange multiplier gives the magnitude of an ideal reaction from the gradient of the constraint.

For a holonomic constraint $f(\mechanical{\vec r},t)=0$, write the ideal reaction as $\mechanical{\vec\Phi}_i=\lambda(t)\nabla_{\vec r_i}f$. The multiplier $\lambda(t)$ is an unknown. This method keeps the Cartesian coordinates and calculates the reaction explicitly.

1. Write $\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}_i=\mechanical{\vec F}_i^{\,\mathrm{act}}+\lambda\nabla_{\vec r_i}f$ for each particle.
2. Add the constraint $f=0$.
3. Solve the equations for $\mechanical{\vec r}_i(t)$ and $\lambda(t)$.

### Example — Simple Pendulum

The constraint is $f=\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2-\mechanical{\ell}^2=0$. With upward positive, $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot x}=2\lambda\mechanical{x}$ and $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot y}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}+2\lambda\mechanical{y}$. Solving with the constraint gives $\lambda(t)=-\mechanical{m}[\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})\cos\mechanical{\theta}]/2$. The tension is

$$
\mechanical{T}=-2\lambda\mechanical{\ell}
=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}[\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})\cos\mechanical{\theta}]
=\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{\ell}+\mechanical{g}\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$

This agrees with [[02.13 Dynamics of the Simple Pendulum]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec\Phi}_i,\mechanical{\vec F}_i^{\,\mathrm{act}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Forces | The ideal reaction and the active force on particle $i$. |
| $\lambda$ | — | Lagrange multiplier | The coefficient of the constraint gradient. Its unit depends on the constraint. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{\vec r}_i,\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The Cartesian position or its components. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot{\vec r}}_i,\mechanical{\ddot x},\mechanical{\ddot y}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The second time derivative of position. |
| $\mechanical{m}_i,\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of each particle or the pendulum bob. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum length | The distance from the pivot to the bob. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Pendulum angle | The angular displacement from the downward vertical. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rate of change of the pendulum angle. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The speed of the pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward gravitational acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{T}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Tension | The force the cord exerts on the bob. |
