---
title: "Unilateral Constraints and Inequalities"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A unilateral constraint can push at contact. If the required reaction would pull, the body loses contact.

A constraint of the form $f(\mechanical{\vec r})\ge0$ is **unilateral**. For a ball above a floor, $\mechanical{z}\ge0$. The constraint can act only at $\mechanical{z}=0$. Above the floor the ball is in free flight.

- If $f>0$, the constraint is inactive. Only the other forces act.
- If $f=0$ during sustained contact, the normal constraint force must have nonnegative magnitude $\mechanical{\Phi}\ge0$.

If the contact equations require a negative reaction, the body loses contact. This describes separation from a convex surface.

### Example — Ball Sliding on a Smooth Spherical Cap

A ball starts from rest at the top of a spherical cap of radius $\mechanical{R}$. Once it starts to slide, it moves without friction. At angle $\mechanical{\theta}$ from the vertical, energy conservation gives $\mechanical{v}^2=2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})$. With inward positive, the radial equation is

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\mechanical{\theta}-\mechanical{N}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2}{\mechanical{R}},
$$

where the outward normal force must satisfy $\mechanical{N}\ge0$. It vanishes at $\cos\mechanical{\theta}=2/3$, or $\mechanical{\theta}\approx48.2\unit{{}^\circ}$. The ball then follows free projectile motion.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The particle position and its height above the floor. |
| $\mechanical{\Phi},\mechanical{N}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Constraint reaction | The magnitude of the normal contact force. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Cap radius | The radius of the spherical surface. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angle | The angle of the ball's position from the vertical through the cap center. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The speed of the ball while it slides. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the ball. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
