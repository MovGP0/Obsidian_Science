---
title: "Rotation About a Fixed Axis"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For rotation about a fixed axis, the moment of inertia relates angular speed to angular momentum and angular acceleration to torque.

A rigid body rotates about a fixed axis with angular velocity $\mechanical{\omega}(t)=\mechanical{\dot\theta}$. Its kinetic energy is

$$
\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\omega}^2.
$$

The angular momentum component along the axis is $\mechanical{L}_z=\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\omega}$. The torque equation about the axis is

$$
\mechanical{\tau}_{\mathrm{ext},z}=\frac{d\mechanical{L}_z}{dt}=\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\ddot\theta}.
$$

This is the rotational analogue of $\mechanical{F}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{a}$.

**Work and energy.** A constant torque $\mechanical{\tau}$ acting through angle $\Delta\mechanical{\theta}$ does work $\energy{W}=\mechanical{\tau}\Delta\mechanical{\theta}$.

### Example — Physical Pendulum

A compound pendulum is a rigid body that oscillates about a horizontal axis a distance $\mechanical{d}$ from the center of mass. Its equation is $\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\ddot\theta}=-\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}\sin\mechanical{\theta}$. For small amplitudes,

$$
\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{I}}{\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}}}.
$$

For a uniform rod of length $\mechanical{L}$ suspended at one end, $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{L}^2/3$, $\mechanical{d}=\mechanical{L}/2$, and $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{2\mechanical{L}/(3\mechanical{g})}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\omega},\mechanical{\dot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The signed rate of rotation about the axis. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The rate of change of angular velocity. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the rotation. |
| $\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Rotational kinetic energy | The energy of rotation about the axis. |
| $\mechanical{I}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia | The rotational inertia about the fixed axis. |
| $\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Axial angular momentum | The angular momentum component along the axis. |
| $\mechanical{\tau}_{\mathrm{ext},z}$ | $\unit{N\,m}$ | Axial torque | The external torque component along the axis. |
| $\mechanical{\tau}$ | $\unit{N\,m}$ | Torque | The constant torque that does work during rotation. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\Delta\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angular position and change | The angle of rotation or the angle through which torque acts. |
| $\energy{W}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Work | The energy transferred by the torque. |
| $\mechanical{M},\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the pendulum or the mass in the translation analogy. |
| $\mechanical{F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | The force in the translational equation. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Linear acceleration | The acceleration in the translational equation. |
| $\mechanical{d},\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum dimensions | The axis-to-center-of-mass distance and the length of the rod. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The time for one small oscillation. |
