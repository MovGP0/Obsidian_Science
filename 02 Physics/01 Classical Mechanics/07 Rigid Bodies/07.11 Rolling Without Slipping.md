---
title: "Rolling Without Slipping"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For rolling without slipping on a stationary surface, the center speed is the angular speed times the radius.

When a rigid body **rolls without slipping** on a stationary surface, the contact point is instantaneously at rest. For a wheel of radius $\mechanical{R}$ with center speed $\mechanical{v}$ and angular speed $\mechanical{\omega}$,

$$
\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{\omega}\mechanical{R}.
$$

**Kinetic energy.** König's decomposition gives

$$
\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{M}\mechanical{v}^2+\tfrac12\mechanical{I}_G\mechanical{\omega}^2
=\tfrac12\mechanical{M}\mechanical{v}^2
\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{I}_G}{\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2}\right).
$$

The factor $1+\mechanical{I}_G/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2)$ increases the effective inertia. For a disk it is $3/2$, for a solid sphere $7/5$, and for a thin spherical shell $5/3$.

### Example — Sphere, Disk, and Ring on an Incline

Three bodies with the same mass and radius roll without slipping down an incline from rest. Take the vertical drop to be $\mechanical{h}$. Conservation of energy gives $\mechanical{M}\mechanical{v}^2(1+\beta)/2=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}$, where $\beta=\mechanical{I}_G/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2)$. Thus

$$
\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{\frac{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}{1+\beta}}.
$$

- Solid sphere: $\beta=2/5$, so $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{10\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}/7}$.
- Disk: $\beta=1/2$, so $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{4\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}/3}$.
- Ring: $\beta=1$, so $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}$.

The sphere is fastest, and the ring is slowest. The speed for each shape is independent of its mass and radius.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source describes a smooth incline. Rolling from rest under gravity requires sufficient static friction to prevent slipping. Static friction does no work at the contact point on the stationary incline in this model.

**Friction.** Static friction maintains the rolling condition. The contact point is instantaneously at rest, so the friction is nondissipative. Without friction, the body released from rest would slide.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radius | The distance from the rotation axis to the contact point. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Center speed | The speed of the center of mass. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular speed | The rotation rate about the center. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the rolling body. |
| $\mechanical{I}_G$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia | The moment about the center-of-mass axis. |
| $\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The sum of translational and rotational kinetic energies. |
| $\beta$ | 1 | Inertia factor | The moment of inertia divided by mass times radius squared. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Vertical drop | The difference in height between release and the bottom. |
