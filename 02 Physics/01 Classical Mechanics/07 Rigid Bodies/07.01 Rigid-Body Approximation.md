---
title: "Rigid-Body Approximation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Use the rigid-body approximation when deformation is negligible for the dimensions and timescales of the problem.

A **rigid body** is a model for a macroscopic object that deforms only slightly. The model assumes that every distance between two points of the body stays constant during motion.

All real bodies deform under load. The amount depends on elastic properties such as Young's modulus and the shear modulus. The rigid-body approximation applies when deformation is negligible compared with the dimensions of the object and over the timescales of interest.

**Examples.** A car, bicycle, satellite, or billiard ball can be modeled as a rigid body for ordinary mechanical motion.

For phenomena on very short timescales, such as impacts and acoustic vibrations, the approximation can fail. Use continuum mechanics when the deformation matters.

**Six degrees of freedom.** A rigid body in three dimensions has three coordinates $(\mechanical{X}_G,\mechanical{Y}_G,\mechanical{Z}_G)$ for the position of its center of mass and three degrees of freedom for orientation. Euler angles or a unit quaternion can represent the orientation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{X}_G,\mechanical{Y}_G,\mechanical{Z}_G$ | $\unit{m}$ | Center-of-mass coordinates | The position components of the center of mass. |
