---
title: "Equivalent Lagrangians"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Adding the total time derivative of a function of coordinates and time leaves the Euler–Lagrange equations unchanged.

Given a function $f(q,t)$, the equivalent Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{L}'(q,\dot q,t)=\energy{L}(q,\dot q,t)+\frac{df(q,t)}{dt}.
$$

The added term integrates to a boundary term in the action. With fixed endpoints it has zero variation, so it does not change the equations of motion. See [[11.02 Action Functional]].

**Consequence.** A Lagrangian is determined only up to such a total derivative. Different Lagrangians can describe the same dynamics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{L},\energy{L}'$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangians | The two functions that give the same equations of motion. |
| $q,\dot q$ | — | Generalized coordinates and velocities | The configuration variables and their time derivatives. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $f$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Boundary function | The function whose total time derivative is added to the Lagrangian. |
