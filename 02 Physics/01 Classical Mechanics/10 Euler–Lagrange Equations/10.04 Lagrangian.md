---
title: "Lagrangian"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For conservative forces from a position-dependent potential, subtract potential energy from kinetic energy to obtain the Lagrangian.

> [!definition] Definition 10.1 — Lagrangian
> For kinetic energy $\energy{T}$ and a conservative potential energy $\energy{U}$,
>
> $$
> \energy{L}(q,\dot q,t)=\energy{T}(q,\dot q,t)-\energy{U}(q,t).
> $$

> [!theorem] Theorem 10.1 — Euler–Lagrange Equations
> For forces derived from the potential $\energy{U}$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\right)
> -\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial q_k}=0,\qquad k=1,\ldots,n.
> $$

The single function $\energy{L}$ gives all $n$ equations by differentiation. Their form is independent of the choice of generalized coordinates.

**Nonconservative forces.** If some forces do not come from a potential, write

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\right)
-\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial q_k}=Q_k^{\mathrm{nc}},
$$

where $Q_k^{\mathrm{nc}}$ accounts for nonconservative generalized forces such as friction or excitation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{L},\energy{T},\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian and energies | The Lagrangian is kinetic energy minus potential energy. |
| $q,\dot q$ | — | Generalized coordinates and velocities | The configuration variables and their rates of change. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable in the Lagrangian. |
| $Q_k^{\mathrm{nc}}$ | — | Nonconservative generalized force | The generalized force outside the potential model. |
