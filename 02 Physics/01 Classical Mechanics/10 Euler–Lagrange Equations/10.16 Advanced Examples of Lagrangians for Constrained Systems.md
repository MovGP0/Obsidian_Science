---
title: "Advanced Examples of Lagrangians for Constrained Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A reduced Lagrangian can encode a fixed cord length or an imposed motion directly in the coordinates.

## Atwood Machine

Two masses $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ are connected over a fixed pulley, with downward coordinates satisfying $\mechanical{y}_1+\mechanical{y}_2=\mathrm{const}$. Use $\mechanical{y}_1$ as the free coordinate. Then $\energy{T}=\tfrac12(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{\dot y}_1^2$ and $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{g}\mechanical{y}_1+\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{y}_1+\mathrm{const}$. The equation gives $\mechanical{\ddot y}_1=(\mechanical{m}_1-\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{g}/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)$.

## Pendulum with a Moving Pivot

A mass $\mechanical{m}$ hangs from a cord of length $\mechanical{\ell}$. The pivot moves horizontally as $\mechanical{X}(t)=\mechanical{A}\sin(\signal{\Omega}t)$. For angle $\mechanical{\theta}$ from the downward vertical, $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{X}(t)+\mechanical{\ell}\sin\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\mechanical{y}=-\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$. Thus $\mechanical{\dot x}=\mechanical{\dot X}+\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta}\,\mechanical{\dot\theta}$.

$$
\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot x}^2+\mechanical{\dot y}^2)
=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot X}^2+
2\mechanical{\dot X}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta}\,\mechanical{\dot\theta}+
\mechanical{\ell}^2\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}),
\qquad \energy{U}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta}.
$$

The constraint is rheonomic. The equation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\mechanical{\ddot\theta}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}\mechanical{\ddot X}\cos\mechanical{\theta}+\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\sin\mechanical{\theta}=0$. For small angles, $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})\mechanical{\theta}=-\mechanical{\ddot X}/\mechanical{\ell}$, a driven oscillator.

## Particle on a Rotating Guide

A particle moves along a straight guide that rotates about a vertical axis at constant angular speed $\mechanical{\Omega}$ in uniform gravity. The guide is inclined at $\mechanical{\alpha}$ above horizontal. For coordinate $\mechanical{s}$ along the guide from the axis, $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot s}^{\,2}+\mechanical{\Omega}^2\mechanical{s}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\alpha})$ and $\energy{U}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{s}\sin\mechanical{\alpha}$. The Euler–Lagrange equation gives

$$
\mechanical{\ddot s}-\mechanical{\Omega}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\alpha}\,\mechanical{s}+\mechanical{g}\sin\mechanical{\alpha}=0.
$$

The term $-\mechanical{\Omega}^2\cos^2\mechanical{\alpha}\,\mechanical{s}$ on the left represents the centrifugal contribution along the guide.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the pendulum bob or pulley loads. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{y}_1,\mechanical{y}_2,\mechanical{X}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The positions of the masses and the moving pivot. |
| $\mechanical{\dot x},\mechanical{\dot y},\mechanical{\dot y}_1,\mechanical{\dot X}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The specified position derivatives. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot y}_1,\mechanical{\ddot X}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The acceleration of the first pulley load or the moving pivot. |
| $\mechanical{A},\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Amplitude and length | The pivot amplitude and the length of the pendulum. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\energy{T},\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic and potential energy | The energies used to construct the reduced Lagrangian. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Pendulum angle | The angle from the downward vertical. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\theta},\signal{\Omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular rates | The pendulum angle rate and the pivot's driving angular frequency. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The second time derivative of the pendulum angle. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the prescribed motion. |
| $\mechanical{\Omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Guide angular speed | The constant rotation rate of the guide. |
| $\mechanical{\alpha}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Guide inclination | The angle of the guide above horizontal. |
| $\mechanical{s},\mechanical{\dot s},\mechanical{\ddot s}$ | $\unit{m},\unit{m\,s^{-1}},\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Guide coordinate and derivatives | The particle position along the guide and its time derivatives. |
