---
title: "History of Analytical Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The calculus of variations and the principle of action led to a general description of motion in generalized coordinates.

The formulation of the Euler–Lagrange equations was a major result of eighteenth-century mechanics.

- **Pierre-Louis de Maupertuis (1698–1759)** proposed the *principle of least action* in 1744, motivated by metaphysical and theological ideas.
- **Leonhard Euler (1707–1783)** developed the calculus of variations. He applied least action to the motion of a particle and obtained the equations that now bear his name.
- **Joseph-Louis Lagrange (1736–1813)** generalized the approach to constrained systems. In *Mécanique analytique* (1788), he gave a systematic formulation of the equations now called the Euler–Lagrange equations.
- **William Rowan Hamilton (1805–1865)** gave a variational formulation of Lagrange's approach in 1834–1835. See [[11.00 Hamilton's Principle and Symmetries|Chapter 11]] for Hamilton's principle.

The analytical approach handles constraints systematically, preserves the form of the equations under coordinate changes, and helps identify conserved quantities. It is a starting point for Hamiltonian mechanics and canonical quantization.
