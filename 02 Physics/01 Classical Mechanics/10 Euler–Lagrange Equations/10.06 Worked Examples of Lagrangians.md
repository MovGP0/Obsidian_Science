---
title: "Worked Examples of Lagrangians"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Construct a Lagrangian by expressing the kinetic and potential energies in coordinates adapted to the constraints.

## Particle on a Sphere

A particle of mass $\mechanical{m}$ is constrained to the surface of a sphere of radius $\mechanical{R}$ in vertical gravity. Use spherical coordinates $(\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi})$ with $\mechanical{\theta}$ from the downward vertical. The energies are $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{R}^2(\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}+\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mechanical{\dot\phi}^{\,2})$ and $\energy{U}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$.

Take the $z$ axis upward. Differentiating $\energy{L}=\energy{T}-\energy{U}$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{R}^2\mechanical{\ddot\theta}
&=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{R}^2\sin\mechanical{\theta}\cos\mechanical{\theta}\,\mechanical{\dot\phi}^{\,2}
-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}\sin\mechanical{\theta},\\
\frac{d}{dt}(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{R}^2\sin^2\mechanical{\theta}\,\mechanical{\dot\phi})&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The second equation is conservation of angular momentum about the vertical. This is a spherical pendulum.

## Double Atwood Machine

Two pulleys, one moving below the other, connect three masses $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2,\mechanical{m}_3$ by two inextensible cords. With downward positive, use $q_1=\mechanical{y}_1$ and $q_2=\mechanical{y}_3-\mechanical{y}_c$, where $\mechanical{y}_c$ is the moving pulley's position and $\mechanical{y}_c=-\mechanical{y}_1+\mathrm{const}$. The cord lengths determine the other coordinates. Substitute into $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\dot y}_i^2$ and $\energy{U}=-\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{g}\mechanical{y}_i$. The resulting two-coordinate Lagrangian gives $\ddot q_1$ and $\ddot q_2$ in terms of gravity and the mass ratios. The tensions need not be calculated.

## Pendulum with an Oscillating Pivot

The pivot moves vertically as $\mechanical{z}_0(t)=\mechanical{A}\cos(\signal{\Omega}t)$. Measure $\mechanical{\theta}$ from the downward vertical. Then $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{\ell}\sin\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{z}_0-\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$. The energies are

$$
\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\ell}^2\mechanical{\dot\theta}^{\,2}
+2\mechanical{\ell}\mechanical{\dot z}_0\sin\mechanical{\theta}\,\mechanical{\dot\theta}+\mechanical{\dot z}_0^2),
\quad
\energy{U}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}(\mechanical{z}_0-\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta}).
$$

The equation is $\mechanical{\ell}\mechanical{\ddot\theta}+(\mechanical{g}+\mechanical{\ddot z}_0)\sin\mechanical{\theta}=0$. For an inverted pendulum, measure $\mechanical{\theta}$ from the upward vertical. Now $\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{z}_0+\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta}$ and $\energy{U}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}(\mechanical{z}_0+\mechanical{\ell}\cos\mechanical{\theta})$. The same method gives $\mechanical{\ell}\mechanical{\ddot\theta}=(\mechanical{g}+\mechanical{\ddot z}_0)\sin\mechanical{\theta}$. When $\mechanical{\ddot z}_0=0$, the positive sign gives instability about the upright position.

If the pivot oscillates rapidly with $\signal{\Omega}^2\gg\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell}$ and small amplitude $\mechanical{A}\ll\mechanical{\ell}$, the oscillating term can stabilize the inverted position. In the averaged approximation, $\mechanical{A}^2\signal{\Omega}^2>2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}$ gives an effective restoring force. This is the Kapitza phenomenon.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Sphere radius | The distance from the particle to the sphere center. |
| $\mechanical{\theta},\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The polar angle and azimuth of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\theta},\mechanical{\dot\phi}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocities | The rates of change of the two angles. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\energy{T},\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic and potential energy | The energy of motion and the gravitational potential energy. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The kinetic energy minus the potential energy. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The second time derivative of the polar or pendulum angle. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for motion. |
| $\mechanical{m}_i,\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2,\mechanical{m}_3$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Pulley loads | The masses in the double Atwood machine. |
| $\mechanical{y}_i,\mechanical{y}_1,\mechanical{y}_3,\mechanical{y}_c,q_1,q_2$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pulley positions | The vertical positions or generalized coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{\dot y}_i$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Load velocity | The downward velocity of a pulley load. |
| $\ddot q_1,\ddot q_2$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Generalized acceleration | The second time derivatives of the pulley coordinates. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z}_0,\mechanical{A},\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum positions and lengths | The bob and pivot positions, pivot amplitude, and pendulum length. |
| $\mechanical{\dot z}_0,\mechanical{\ddot z}_0$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}},\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Pivot velocity and acceleration | The first and second time derivatives of pivot height. |
| $\signal{\Omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Driving angular frequency | The angular frequency of vertical pivot oscillation. |
