---
title: "Dissipative Forces and the Rayleigh Dissipation Function"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A Rayleigh dissipation function generates generalized forces for models such as linear viscous damping.

Dissipative forces do not follow from an ordinary position-dependent potential. Viscous friction and some other models can represent them as derivatives of a dissipation function with respect to generalized velocities.

The **Rayleigh dissipation function** $\energy{\mathcal R}$ gives $Q_k^{\mathrm{nc}}=-\partial\energy{\mathcal R}/\partial\dot q_k$.

**Quadratic case.** For linear friction $\mechanical{\vec F}_r=-\mechanical{b}\mechanical{\vec v}$ in Cartesian coordinates, use $\energy{\mathcal R}=\tfrac12\sum_i\mechanical{b}_i\dot q_i^2$, with the same coefficient in each direction for isotropic friction. The equations with dissipation are

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}\right)
-\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial q_k}+\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal R}}{\partial\dot q_k}=0.
$$

### Example — Damped Harmonic Oscillator

Use $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot x}^2-\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{x}^2$ and $\energy{\mathcal R}=\tfrac12\mechanical{b}\mechanical{\dot x}^2$. The equation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot x}+\mechanical{k}\mechanical{x}+\mechanical{b}\mechanical{\dot x}=0$, as in [[04.06 Damped Oscillator]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal R}$ | $\unit{W}$ | Rayleigh dissipation function | The function whose velocity gradient gives the negative damping force. |
| $Q_k^{\mathrm{nc}}$ | — | Nonconservative generalized force | The damping force conjugate to the generalized coordinate. |
| $q_k,\dot q_k$ | — | Generalized coordinate and velocity | The coordinate and its time derivative. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The conservative part of the equations. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_r$ | $\unit{N}$ | Friction force | The force that opposes motion. |
| $\mechanical{b},\mechanical{b}_i$ | $\unit{kg\,s^{-1}}$ | Damping coefficient | The coefficient of linear friction. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v},\mechanical{\dot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The particle velocity or its scalar component. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{\ddot x}$ | $\unit{m},\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Oscillator displacement and acceleration | The position from equilibrium and its second time derivative. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The restoring stiffness. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
