title: "Euler–Lagrange Equations"

> [!TLDR]
> The Euler–Lagrange equations describe motion in generalized coordinates and account systematically for ideal constraints.

The Euler–Lagrange equations are central to analytical mechanics. For a regular system with $n$ generalized coordinates, they give $n$ ordinary differential equations of second order, one for each coordinate. Rewrite d’Alembert’s equation in generalized coordinates to obtain them. They express the same physical laws as Newton's equations in a form adapted to the constraints.

## Chapter Summary

- In generalized coordinates, [[10.02 Kinetic Energy in Generalized Coordinates|kinetic energy]] is $\energy{T}=\energy{T}_2+\energy{T}_1+\energy{T}_0$. For time-independent coordinates with scleronomic constraints, $\energy{T}=\energy{T}_2$.
- For conservative forces, the [[10.04 Lagrangian|Lagrangian]] is $\energy{L}=\energy{T}-\energy{U}$. The Euler–Lagrange equations are $d(\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q_k)/dt=\partial\energy{L}/\partial q_k$.
- A [[10.07 Cyclic Coordinates and Conjugate Momenta|cyclic coordinate]] satisfies $\partial\energy{L}/\partial q_k=0$ and has conserved conjugate momentum $p_k=\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q_k$.
- [[10.08 Equivalent Lagrangians|Adding a total derivative]], $\energy{L}\to\energy{L}+df/dt$, leaves the equations unchanged.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{T},\energy{T}_2,\energy{T}_1,\energy{T}_0$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The total and its parts by degree in generalized velocity. |
| $\energy{L},\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian and potential energy | The dynamical function and the energy of the conservative forces. |
| $q_k,\dot q_k$ | — | Generalized coordinate and velocity | The coordinate and its time derivative. |
| $p_k$ | — | Conjugate momentum | The derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to generalized velocity. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for motion. |
| $f$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Boundary function | The function whose time derivative can be added. |
