---
title: "Cyclic Coordinates and Conjugate Momenta"
---

> [!TLDR]
> When a coordinate does not appear explicitly in the Lagrangian, its conjugate momentum is conserved.

> [!definition] Definition 10.2 — Canonically Conjugate Momentum
> For each generalized coordinate $q_k$, define its conjugate momentum by
>
> $$
> p_k=\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k}.
> $$

For central motion, $\mechanical{p}_\phi=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\dot\phi}=\mechanical{L}_z$. For a simple pendulum, $\mechanical{p}_\theta=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\mechanical{\dot\theta}$.

> [!definition] Definition 10.3 — Cyclic Coordinate
> A coordinate $q_k$ is **cyclic** if $\partial\energy{L}/\partial q_k=0$. Its conjugate momentum $p_k$ is conserved.

**Practical consequence.** Identifying cyclic coordinates gives conserved quantities and simplifies integration. In central motion, $\mechanical{\phi}$ is cyclic. The constant $\mechanical{p}_\phi$ reduces the problem to one-dimensional motion in $\mechanical{r}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q_k,\dot q_k$ | — | Generalized coordinate and velocity | An independent coordinate and its time derivative. |
| $p_k$ | — | Conjugate momentum | The derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to the generalized velocity. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The function that gives the equations of motion. |
| $\mechanical{p}_\phi,\mechanical{p}_\theta,\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momenta | The conjugate momenta in the examples and the conserved axial component in central motion. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle or pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial distance and pendulum length | The central-force distance and the fixed cord length. |
| $\mechanical{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Polar angle | The cyclic coordinate for central motion. |
| $\mechanical{\dot\phi},\mechanical{\dot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The time derivative of the indicated angle. |
