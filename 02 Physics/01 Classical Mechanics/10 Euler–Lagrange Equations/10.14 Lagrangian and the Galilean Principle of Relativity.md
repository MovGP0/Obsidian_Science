---
title: "Lagrangian and the Galilean Principle of Relativity"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Galilean symmetry fixes the quadratic velocity dependence of the free-particle Lagrangian.

A Galilean boost sends $\mechanical{\vec r}\to\mechanical{\vec r}+\mechanical{\vec V}t$ and $\mechanical{\vec v}\to\mechanical{\vec v}+\mechanical{\vec V}$. The Lagrangian can change by a total derivative:

$$
\energy{L}(\mechanical{\vec v}+\mechanical{\vec V})=\energy{L}(\mechanical{\vec v})+\frac{df}{dt}.
$$

To first order in $\mechanical{\vec V}$, $\delta\energy{L}=(\partial\energy{L}/\partial\mechanical{\vec v})\cdot\mechanical{\vec V}$. This must equal the total derivative of $f(\mechanical{\vec r},t)$, which is at most linear in velocity. For arbitrary $\mechanical{\vec V}$, the velocity derivative of $\energy{L}$ is therefore linear in velocity up to a constant. Rotational symmetry and removal of a total derivative give $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\lVert\mechanical{\vec v}\rVert^2+\mathrm{const}$. The coefficient $\mechanical{m}$ defines the mass, taken positive for stability.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The position of the free particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v},\mechanical{\vec V}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocities | The particle velocity and the constant boost velocity. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable under the boost. |
| $\energy{L},\delta\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian and its change | The dynamical function and its variation under the boost. |
| $f$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Boundary function | The function whose derivative accounts for the change. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The coefficient of the quadratic velocity term. |
