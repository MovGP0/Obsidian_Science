---
title: "Normal Modes of Rigid-Body Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The normal-mode method also applies to rotations, as in a rigid bar connected to a torsion spring.

Normal modes apply to systems with rotational degrees of freedom. As an example, suspend a rigid bar at its center of mass and connect it to a restoring torsion spring with stiffness $\mechanical{\kappa}$. The equation of motion is $\mechanical{I}\ddot\theta=-\mechanical{\kappa}\theta$. The oscillator has angular frequency $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{\kappa}/\mechanical{I}}$.

In the Cavendish torsion-balance experiment, two small masses on a suspended horizontal bar interact gravitationally with two larger external masses. The measured gravitational torque can be used to determine the gravitational constant $\mechanical{G}$.

**Example. Three coupled pendulums.** Three bobs of mass $\mechanical{m}$ hang side by side on strings of length $\mechanical{\ell}$. Two lateral springs of stiffness $\mechanical{k}$ couple them. For small angles $\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3$,

$$
M=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2 I_3,\qquad
V=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\frac{\mechanical{g}}{\mechanical{\ell}}I_3
+\mechanical{k}\mechanical{\ell}^2\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&0\\-1&2&-1\\0&-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

- $\signal{\omega}_0=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell}}$. The pendulums move together, with no spring deformation.
- $\signal{\omega}_1=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell}+\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$. The outer pendulums move in opposite phase and the middle one stays at rest.
- $\signal{\omega}_2=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell}+3\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$. The middle pendulum moves in opposite phase to the outer ones.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\kappa}$ | $\unit{N\,m\,rad^{-1}}$ | Torsional stiffness | The restoring torque per unit angle. |
| $\mechanical{I},M$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia and mass matrix | The inertia of the bar and matrix for the pendulum angles. |
| $V$ | $\unit{J\,rad^{-2}}$ | Stiffness matrix | The Hessian with respect to the pendulum angles. |
| $\theta,\theta_i$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The bar angle and pendulum angles. |
| $\signal{\omega},\signal{\omega}_0,\signal{\omega}_1,\signal{\omega}_2$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The torsion frequency and three pendulum mode frequencies. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant inferred from the measured attraction. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of each pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length | The length of each pendulum string. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring stiffness | The stiffness of each lateral coupling. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The local acceleration magnitude. |
