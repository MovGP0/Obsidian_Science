---
title: "Acoustic Spectrum of a Vibrating String"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A string fixed at both ends has standing-wave frequencies at integer multiples of its fundamental frequency.

The vibrating string is a historical application of normal modes. Take a string of length $\mechanical{L}$ under constant tension $\mechanical{T}$, with linear mass density $\mechanical{\lambda}$ and both ends fixed.

**Normal modes.** For a mode of amplitude $\mechanical{A}_n$ and phase $\signal{\phi}_n$,

$$
\eta_n(\mechanical{x},t)=\mechanical{A}_n\sin\left(\frac{n\pi\mechanical{x}}{\mechanical{L}}\right)\cos(\signal{\omega}_nt+\signal{\phi}_n),\qquad
\signal{\omega}_n=\frac{n\pi\mechanical{c}}{\mechanical{L}},\qquad
\mechanical{c}=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{T}}{\mechanical{\lambda}}}.
$$

The harmonic spectrum has fundamental $\signal{f}_1=\mechanical{c}/(2\mechanical{L})$ and frequencies $\signal{f}_n=n\signal{f}_1$.

**Mode energy.** A mode with amplitude $\mechanical{A}_n$ has energy $\energy{E}_n=\tfrac14\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{L}\signal{\omega}_n^2\mechanical{A}_n^2$. The total energy is $\energy{E}=\sum_n\energy{E}_n$.

**Perceived spectrum.** The ear perceives the fundamental as pitch and the pattern of harmonic amplitudes as timbre. Instruments such as the violin, flute, and piano produce different overtone spectra and characteristic sounds.

**Example. Guitar string.** An A string has $\signal{f}_1=\quantity{110}{Hz}$ and a typical length $\mechanical{L}=\quantity{0.65}{m}$. Then $\mechanical{c}=2\mechanical{L}\signal{f}_1=\quantity{143}{m\,s^{-1}}$. For a typical tension $\mechanical{T}\simeq\quantity{80}{N}$, the linear mass density is $\mechanical{\lambda}=\mechanical{T}/\mechanical{c}^2\simeq\quantity{4}{g\,m^{-1}}$.

> [!info] Analytical mechanics
> The classical mechanics of nonlinear systems, developed extensively in the twentieth century, is a starting point for dynamical systems theory and nonequilibrium statistical physics. Fractal structures, strange attractors, and oscillator synchronization all draw on this framework.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{L},\mechanical{x},\eta_n,\mechanical{A}_n$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length and displacement | The string length, position, transverse displacement, and modal amplitude. |
| $\mechanical{T}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Tension | The constant tensile force in the string. |
| $\mechanical{\lambda}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-1}}$ | Linear mass density | The mass per unit length. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Wave speed | The speed of a transverse wave on the string. |
| $\signal{\omega}_n$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The angular frequency of mode $n$. |
| $\signal{f}_1,\signal{f}_n$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Frequency | The fundamental and its integer multiples. |
| $\signal{\phi}_n$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Phase | The initial phase of a mode. |
| $\energy{E}_n,\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The mode energy and total string energy. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the wave. |
