---
title: "Normal Modes"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Each normal mode oscillates at one frequency. Normal coordinates turn the quadratic Lagrangian into independent oscillators.

> [!definition] Definition 14.1 | Normal Mode
> A **normal mode** of a system with $n$ degrees of freedom is a solution
>
> $$
> \vec\eta_\alpha(t)=\vec u_\alpha\bigl(A_\alpha\cos(\signal{\omega}_\alpha t)+B_\alpha\sin(\signal{\omega}_\alpha t)\bigr).
> $$
>
> All degrees of freedom in this mode oscillate at the same angular frequency $\signal{\omega}_\alpha$. The mode vector $\vec u_\alpha$ gives the amplitude ratios.

The general solution is the superposition

$$
\vec\eta(t)=\sum_{\alpha=1}^n\vec u_\alpha\bigl(A_\alpha\cos(\signal{\omega}_\alpha t)+B_\alpha\sin(\signal{\omega}_\alpha t)\bigr).
$$

**Normal coordinates.** Define $Q_\alpha=\vec u_\alpha^TM\vec\eta$. Substitution into the Lagrangian gives

$$
V\vec u_\alpha=\signal{\omega}_\alpha^2M\vec u_\alpha,\qquad
\energy{L}=\frac12\sum_\alpha(\dot Q_\alpha^2-\signal{\omega}_\alpha^2Q_\alpha^2).
$$

Each normal coordinate is an independent harmonic oscillator with its own frequency.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\vec\eta,\vec\eta_\alpha,\vec u_\alpha$ | — | Deviations and mode vectors | The full motion, its individual modal contribution, and the normalized eigenvector. |
| $A_\alpha,B_\alpha,Q_\alpha$ | $\unit{J^{1/2}\,s}$ | Normal-coordinate amplitudes and coordinate | The coefficients and coordinate in the mass-normalized basis. |
| $M,V$ | — | Mass and stiffness matrices | The quadratic coefficients in the equations of motion. |
| $\signal{\omega}_\alpha$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The natural frequency of mode $\alpha$. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The sum of independent modal Lagrangians. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
