---
title: "Simultaneous Diagonalization of M and V"
---

> [!TLDR]
> First normalize the mass matrix to the identity, then diagonalize the resulting symmetric stiffness matrix.

The equation $V\vec u=\signal{\omega}^2M\vec u$ is a generalized eigenvalue problem. The solution has two steps.

1. Diagonalize the mass matrix as $M=OBO^T$, with $O$ orthogonal and $B$ diagonal and positive. The transformation $\vec\xi=B^{1/2}O^T\vec\eta$ makes the mass matrix the identity.
2. Diagonalize the symmetric matrix $\tilde V=B^{-1/2}O^TVOB^{-1/2}$. Its eigenvalues are $\signal{\omega}_\alpha^2$ and its eigenvectors are $\vec v_\alpha$.

The normal modes in the original variables are $\vec u_\alpha=OB^{-1/2}\vec v_\alpha$. With an orthonormal basis for $\tilde V$, they satisfy

$$
\vec u_\alpha^TM\vec u_\beta=\delta_{\alpha\beta},\qquad
\vec u_\alpha^TV\vec u_\beta=\signal{\omega}_\alpha^2\delta_{\alpha\beta}.
$$

Thus they diagonalize $M$ and $V$ simultaneously. With normal coordinates $Q_\alpha=\vec u_\alpha^TM\vec\eta$, each mode contributes independently to the energy.

$$
\energy{E}=\sum_\alpha\frac12\bigl(\dot Q_\alpha^2+\signal{\omega}_\alpha^2Q_\alpha^2\bigr).
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $M,B,V$ | — | Mass and stiffness matrices | The original mass matrix, its diagonal form, and the original stiffness matrix. |
| $\tilde V$ | $\unit{s^{-2}}$ | Mass-normalized stiffness matrix | The symmetric matrix after the mass matrix becomes the identity. |
| $\vec\eta,\vec u_\alpha$ | — | Deviation and mode vectors | The original displacements and normalized modes in the original coordinates. |
| $\vec\xi,Q_\alpha$ | $\unit{J^{1/2}\,s}$ | Mass-normalized coordinates | The intermediate and modal coordinates. |
| $\signal{\omega},\signal{\omega}_\alpha$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The trial frequency and the natural frequency of mode $\alpha$. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Total energy | The sum of the modal energies. |
