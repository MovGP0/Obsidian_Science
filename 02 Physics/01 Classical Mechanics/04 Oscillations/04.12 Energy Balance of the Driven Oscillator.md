---
title: "Energy Balance of the Driven Oscillator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In steady motion, the average power supplied by the driving force equals the average power lost to damping.

For a driven oscillator in the steady regime, the average power absorbed from the external force equals the power dissipated by friction.

$$
\langle\energy{P}_{\mathrm{abs}}\rangle
=\tfrac12\signal{\Omega}\mechanical{F}_0\mechanical{A}(\signal{\Omega})\sin\signal{\delta}(\signal{\Omega})
=\mechanical{m}\signal{\gamma}\signal{\Omega}^2\mechanical{A}^2(\signal{\Omega}).
$$

**Maximum transfer.** At the power peak, $\signal{\Omega}=\signal{\omega}_0$ and $\sin\signal{\delta}=1$. For weak damping the displacement amplitude is also near its maximum.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source places the power and displacement peaks at the same frequency. Their separation at nonzero damping is described in [[04.07 Driven Oscillator and Resonance]].

**Bandwidth.** Near resonance for weak damping, the absorbed power has a Lorentzian profile centered at $\signal{\omega}_0$. Its full width at half maximum is $\Delta\signal{\Omega}=2\signal{\gamma}$. With energy decay time $\tau_E=1/(2\signal{\gamma})$, $\Delta\signal{\Omega}\,\tau_E=1$. The source calls this a classical uncertainty relation.

### Example — Building Vibrations

A building subject to seismic excitation has a natural frequency $\signal{f}_0\approx\quantity{1}{Hz}$, or $\signal{\omega}_0\approx\quantity{2\pi}{rad\,s^{-1}}$, and $\signal{Q}\approx30$. Seismic waves near $\signal{f}_0$ can amplify the motion by a factor of approximately $\signal{Q}$ relative to the static deformation in the driven-oscillator model. To reduce this risk, engineers add dampers to lower $\signal{Q}$ or design the natural frequencies to differ from typical frequencies of seismic excitation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\langle\energy{P}_{\mathrm{abs}}\rangle$ | $\unit{W}$ | Average absorbed power | The mean rate at which the drive supplies energy. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical{F}_0$ | $\unit{N}$ | Driving force amplitude | The maximum magnitude of the sinusoidal force. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement amplitude | The magnitude of the steady displacement response. |
| $\signal{\Omega},\signal{\omega}_0$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The driving and undamped natural angular frequencies. |
| $\signal{\delta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Phase lag | The lag of the displacement behind the driving force. |
| $\signal{\gamma}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Damping rate | The coefficient that sets the resonance bandwidth and the energy decay time. |
| $\Delta\signal{\Omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular bandwidth | The full width of the absorbed power curve at half its maximum. |
| $\tau_E$ | $\unit{s}$ | Energy decay time | The time for the energy envelope to fall by a factor $e^{-1}$. |
| $\signal{f}_0$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Natural frequency | The undamped natural frequency in cycles per second. |
| $\signal{Q}$ | 1 | Quality factor | The ratio of natural angular frequency to twice the damping rate. |
