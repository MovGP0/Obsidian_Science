---
title: "Energy Balance of the Simple Oscillator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Over one cycle of free harmonic motion, the average kinetic and potential energies each equal half of the total energy.

In a free harmonic oscillator, the total energy $\energy{E}$ is constant and is shared between kinetic and potential energy. The time averages over one period are

$$
\langle\energy{K}\rangle=\langle\energy{U}\rangle=\frac{\energy{E}}{2}.
$$

**Derivation.** With $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{A}\cos(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\phi})$, the velocity is $\mechanical{\dot x}=-\mechanical{A}\signal{\omega}\sin(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\phi})$. The energies are

$$
\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{A}^2\signal{\omega}^2\sin^2(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\phi}),
\qquad
\energy{U}=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{A}^2\cos^2(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\phi}).
$$

Taking the averages gives $\langle\energy{K}\rangle=\tfrac14\mechanical{m}\mechanical{A}^2\signal{\omega}^2=\energy{E}/2$ and $\langle\energy{U}\rangle=\tfrac14\mechanical{k}\mechanical{A}^2=\energy{E}/2$. This agrees with the [[06.10 Virial Theorem|virial theorem]] for a potential proportional to the square of displacement.

**Position probability density.** Observe the oscillator at a time sampled uniformly over a period. The probability of finding the oscillator in an interval of width $dx$ is proportional to the time it spends there. Thus $P(\mechanical{x})\,dx\propto dx/\mechanical{v}(\mechanical{x})$, where $\mechanical{v}$ is the speed. For the harmonic oscillator,

$$
P(\mechanical{x})=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{A}^2-\mechanical{x}^2}},
\qquad -\mechanical{A}<\mechanical{x}<\mechanical{A}.
$$

The density is smallest at $\mechanical{x}=0$. It diverges at the turning points $\mechanical{x}=\pm\mechanical{A}$, where the speed is zero. This is the classical probability density along the position axis.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Mechanical energy | The conserved sum of kinetic and potential energies. |
| $\energy{K},\langle\energy{K}\rangle$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The instantaneous kinetic energy and its average over one period. |
| $\energy{U},\langle\energy{U}\rangle$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The instantaneous spring energy and its average over one period. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement and amplitude | The displacement from equilibrium and its maximum magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{\dot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The rate of change of displacement. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The stiffness of the spring. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The natural angular frequency. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Initial phase | The phase at zero time. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the oscillation. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed | The magnitude of the velocity at a given position. |
| $P(\mechanical{x})$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Position probability density | The probability per unit position interval for an observation at a random time. |
