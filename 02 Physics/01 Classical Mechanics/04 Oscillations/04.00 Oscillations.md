> [!TLDR]
> The harmonic oscillator models small departures from a stable equilibrium when the restoring force is linear to first order.

Oscillations occur throughout physics, from electrical systems and crystal lattices to electromagnetic waves and quantum mechanics. Near a stable equilibrium with nonzero restoring stiffness, a system behaves to first approximation like a harmonic oscillator. Studying the oscillator therefore helps explain how systems behave close to equilibrium.

## Chapter Summary

- The [[04.02 Simple Harmonic Oscillator|harmonic oscillator]] obeys $\mechanical{\ddot x}+\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}=0$ with $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$ and solution $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{A}\cos(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\varphi})$.
- The period $\signal{T}=2\pi/\signal{\omega}$ is independent of amplitude. This is isochronism.
- The total energy $\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{A}^2$ stays constant for a free undamped oscillator.
- Near a potential minimum with positive curvature, the [[04.04 Harmonic Approximation for Small Oscillations|harmonic approximation]] gives $\signal{\omega}^2=\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)/\mechanical{m}$.
- [[04.06 Damped Oscillator|Damped motion]] has three regimes: underdamping, critical damping, and overdamping.
- A [[04.07 Driven Oscillator and Resonance|driven oscillator]] has steady amplitude $\mechanical{A}(\signal{\Omega})$ and a resonance near $\signal{\Omega}_r\approx\signal{\omega}_0$ for weak damping.
- For [[04.09 Simple and Compound Pendulums|small pendulum oscillations]], $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{\ell}/\mechanical{g}}$ for a simple pendulum and $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{I}/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d})}$ for a compound pendulum.
- [[04.10 Superposition of Oscillations|Superposition]] of oscillations at nearby frequencies produces beats.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{x}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The oscillator displacement coordinate and its equilibrium value. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The second time derivative of displacement. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator or compound pendulum. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The linear restoring stiffness. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Amplitude | The maximum displacement in free or steady driven motion. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\signal{\varphi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Initial phase | The phase at zero time. |
| $\signal{\omega},\signal{\omega}_0$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Natural angular frequency | The angular frequency of undamped free motion. |
| $\signal{\Omega},\signal{\Omega}_r$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Driving angular frequency | The angular frequency of the drive or its value at the amplitude peak. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The time for one complete oscillation. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Mechanical energy | The conserved energy of the free undamped oscillator. |
| $\energy{U}''(\mechanical{x}_0)$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Potential curvature | The second derivative of potential energy at the equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{\ell},\mechanical{d}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pivot distance | The simple pendulum length or the distance to the center of mass of the compound pendulum. |
| $\mechanical{I}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia | The rotational inertia of the compound pendulum about its pivot. |
