---
title: "Oscillators in Multiple Dimensions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An isotropic two-dimensional harmonic oscillator traces an ellipse in general. Anisotropic oscillations trace Lissajous figures.

An **isotropic harmonic oscillator** in two dimensions has potential energy $\energy{U}=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}(\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2)$ with the same stiffness in both directions. Its Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot x}^2+\mechanical{\dot y}^2)
-\tfrac12\mechanical{k}(\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2).
$$

The equations of motion are two independent one-dimensional oscillators with common angular frequency $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$.

**Trajectories.** The general solution is

$$
\mechanical{x}(t)=\mechanical{A}_x\cos(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\phi}_x),
\qquad
\mechanical{y}(t)=\mechanical{A}_y\cos(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\phi}_y).
$$

If $\signal{\phi}_x=\signal{\phi}_y$, the path is a line segment. If $\signal{\phi}_y-\signal{\phi}_x=\pi/2$ and $\mechanical{A}_x=\mechanical{A}_y$, it is a circle. In general the path is an ellipse whose axes need not align with the coordinate axes.

**Conserved quantities.** In addition to energy, the isotropic oscillator conserves angular momentum $\mechanical{L}_z=\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{x}\mechanical{\dot y}-\mechanical{y}\mechanical{\dot x})$ because the potential is central. Further conserved quantities fix the orientation of the ellipse axes. This is an example of superintegrability.

**Anisotropic oscillator.** For $\signal{\omega}_x\ne\signal{\omega}_y$, the trajectories are Lissajous figures. The path closes if $\signal{\omega}_x/\signal{\omega}_y$ is rational. If the ratio is irrational, the path is dense in the rectangle defined by the two amplitudes.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The quadratic energy associated with the displacement. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The kinetic energy minus the potential energy. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement | The two Cartesian displacement components. |
| $\mechanical{\dot x},\mechanical{\dot y}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The rates of change of the Cartesian displacement components. |
| $\mechanical{A}_x,\mechanical{A}_y$ | $\unit{m}$ | Amplitude | The maximum displacement magnitude along each axis. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The common restoring stiffness in the isotropic model. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator. |
| $\signal{\omega},\signal{\omega}_x,\signal{\omega}_y$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The common or separate angular frequencies along the two axes. |
| $\signal{\phi}_x,\signal{\phi}_y$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Initial phase | The phases of the two oscillations at zero time. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The conserved component perpendicular to the plane. |
