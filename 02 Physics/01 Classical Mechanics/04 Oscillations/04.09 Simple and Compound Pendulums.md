---
title: "Simple and Compound Pendulums"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The period of a simple pendulum depends on its length. For a compound pendulum, it also depends on the distribution of mass about the pivot.

**Simple pendulum.** A point mass $\mechanical{m}$ hangs from an inextensible cord of length $\mechanical{\ell}$. For small amplitudes, $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{\ell}/\mechanical{g}}$ independently of $\mechanical{m}$.

**Compound pendulum.** A rigid body oscillates about a fixed axis that does not pass through its center of mass $G$. Let $\mechanical{I}$ be the moment of inertia about the axis, $\mechanical{d}$ the distance from the axis to $G$, and $\mechanical{M}$ the total mass. Its equation of motion is

$$
\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\ddot\theta}
=-\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}\sin\mechanical{\theta}.
$$

For small amplitudes, $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{I}/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d})}$. See [[07.00 Rigid Bodies|Chapter 7]] for the dynamics of rigid bodies.

**Reduced length.** Comparing the periods gives the equivalent simple-pendulum length $\mechanical{\ell}_{\mathrm{red}}=\mechanical{I}/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{d})$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pendulum length | The distance from the pivot to the bob. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The time for one small oscillation. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Total mass | The mass of the compound pendulum. |
| $\mechanical{I}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia | The rotational inertia of the body about the fixed axis. |
| $\mechanical{d}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Center-of-mass distance | The shortest distance from the axis to the center of mass. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Pendulum angle | The angular displacement from the downward equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The second time derivative of the pendulum angle. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}_{\mathrm{red}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Reduced length | The length of a simple pendulum with the same small-amplitude period. |
