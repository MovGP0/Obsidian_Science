> [!TLDR]
> A torsion balance uses a stable equilibrium to measure a small force. An inverted pendulum illustrates exponential departure from an unstable equilibrium.

## Cavendish Torsion Balance

In 1798 Henry Cavendish used a sensitive torsion balance. A thin wire suspends a light horizontal rod with two small lead masses of about $\quantity{0.7}{kg}$ each at its ends. Two larger masses of about $\quantity{158}{kg}$ each attract the small masses. This turns the rod. Measure the equilibrium angle and use the oscillation period to calibrate the torsional stiffness of the wire. These measurements determine the gravitational constant $\mechanical{G}$. The value inferred from Cavendish's result is close to the accepted value.

## Inverted Pendulum

A thin rod of length $\mechanical{L}$ pivots freely about a hinge at its base. Its upright position is an unstable equilibrium. For a small angular disturbance $\mechanical{\theta}$ from the vertical, the equation is

$$
\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\ddot\theta}
=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}\sin\mechanical{\theta}
\approx\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}\mechanical{\theta},
$$

where $\mechanical{I}$ is the moment of inertia about the hinge, $\mechanical{M}$ is the mass, and $\mechanical{d}$ is the distance from the hinge to the center of mass. The solution is

$$
\mechanical{\theta}(t)=\mechanical{A}e^{t/\tau}+\mechanical{B}e^{-t/\tau},
\qquad
\tau=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{I}}{\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}}}.
$$

The growing exponential shows the instability. For a uniform rod, $\tau=\sqrt{2\mechanical{L}/(3\mechanical{g})}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}$ | Gravitational constant | The constant inferred from the torsion balance measurement. |
| — | $\unit{kg}$ | Lead masses | The masses used in the torsion balance. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Rod length | The length of the inverted pendulum. |
| $\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Rod mass | The mass of the inverted pendulum. |
| $\mechanical{I}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2}$ | Moment of inertia | The rotational inertia of the rod about the hinge. |
| $\mechanical{d}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Center-of-mass distance | The distance from the hinge to the center of mass of the rod. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angle | The angular displacement from the upward vertical. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\theta}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-2}}$ | Angular acceleration | The second time derivative of the angle. |
| $\mechanical{A},\mechanical{B}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angular coefficients | The weights of the growing and decaying terms. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\tau$ | $\unit{s}$ | Characteristic time | The time constant for exponential growth or decay. |
