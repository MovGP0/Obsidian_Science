---
title: "Simple Harmonic Oscillator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A linear restoring force produces sinusoidal motion with a natural angular frequency set by the spring constant and mass.

A spring of constant $\mechanical{k}$ exerts the restoring force $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\mechanical{k}\mechanical{\vec x}$ on a body of mass $\mechanical{m}$. In one dimension, Newton's equation becomes

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ddot x}=-\mechanical{k}\mechanical{x},
\qquad \mechanical{\ddot x}+\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}=0,
\qquad \signal{\omega}^2=\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}.
$$

> [!definition] Definition 4.1 — Harmonic Oscillator Equation
> The equation $\mechanical{\ddot x}+\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}=0$ is the equation of the **simple harmonic oscillator**, with natural angular frequency $\signal{\omega}$.

**General solution.** Substitute a trial solution $\mechanical{x}(t)\propto e^{\lambda t}$. The characteristic equation $\lambda^2+\signal{\omega}^2=0$ has roots $\lambda=\pm i\signal{\omega}$. The general real solution is

$$
\mechanical{x}(t)=\mechanical{A}\cos(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\varphi}),
$$

where $\mechanical{A}$ is the amplitude and $\signal{\varphi}$ is the initial phase. The initial conditions $\mechanical{x}(0)$ and $\mechanical{\dot x}(0)$ determine them.

$$
\mechanical{A}=\sqrt{\mechanical{x}(0)^2+\frac{\mechanical{\dot x}(0)^2}{\signal{\omega}^2}},
\qquad
\tan\signal{\varphi}=-\frac{\mechanical{\dot x}(0)}{\signal{\omega}\mechanical{x}(0)}.
$$

**Period and frequency.** The period is $\signal{T}=2\pi/\signal{\omega}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{k}}$, and the frequency is $\signal{f}=1/\signal{T}$. The period depends only on mass and spring stiffness. Independence from amplitude is called *isochronism*.

**Energy.** The total energy is constant.

$$
\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot x}^{\,2}
+\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{x}^2=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{A}^2.
$$

Energy alternates between kinetic and potential forms while their sum stays constant.

$$
\boxed{\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{m}}{\mechanical{k}}},
\qquad \energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{A}^2.}
$$

### Example — Mass Suspended from a Spring

A mass $\mechanical{m}$ on a vertical spring has equilibrium position $\mechanical{y}_0=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{k}$ relative to the unloaded position, with upward positive. For $\mechanical{\xi}=\mechanical{y}-\mechanical{y}_0$, the equation is $\mechanical{\ddot\xi}+\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{\xi}=0$ with $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$, as in the horizontal case. Weight shifts the equilibrium position.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The stiffness of the spring. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillating body. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Restoring force | The spring force directed toward equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{\vec x},\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement | The displacement from equilibrium and its signed component along the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\dot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The rate of change of displacement. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot x}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration | The rate of change of velocity. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Natural angular frequency | The angular frequency of free oscillation. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Amplitude | The maximum magnitude of displacement. |
| $\signal{\varphi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Initial phase | The phase at $t=0$. |
| $\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Period | The time for one complete oscillation. |
| $\signal{f}$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Frequency | The number of oscillations per unit time. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Mechanical energy | The constant sum of kinetic and spring potential energy. |
| $\mechanical{y},\mechanical{y}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Vertical position | The position of the mass and its loaded equilibrium value. |
| $\mechanical{\xi}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Displacement from equilibrium | The vertical displacement measured from the loaded equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot\xi}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Vertical acceleration | The second time derivative of the displacement from equilibrium. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
