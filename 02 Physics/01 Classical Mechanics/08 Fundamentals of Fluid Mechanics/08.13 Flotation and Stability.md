---
title: "Flotation and Stability"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A floating body is stable against small tilts if its metacenter lies above its center of mass.

A floating body needs both a balance between weight and buoyancy and a restoring response to small tilts. The location of the **metacenter** determines this angular stability.

**Center of buoyancy.** The point $G_b$ is the centroid of the submerged volume. The buoyant force acts through it.

**Metacenter.** For a small tilt, the new line of action of the buoyant force intersects the body's original vertical axis at the metacenter $M$. Stability requires $M$ to be above the center of mass $G$. The signed metacentric height $\overline{MG}$ is then positive.

**Ship design.** A low center of mass and a sufficiently high metacenter give roll stability. A large metacentric height gives fast rolls that can be uncomfortable. A small positive height gives a slow restoring response.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\overline{MG}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Metacentric height | The signed height of the metacenter above the center of mass. |
