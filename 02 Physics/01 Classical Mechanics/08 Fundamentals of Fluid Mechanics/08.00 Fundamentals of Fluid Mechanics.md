> [!TLDR]
> Fluid mechanics treats liquids and gases as continua and studies their equilibrium and flow.

Liquids and gases contain very many particles. It is useful to describe them as continua. Fluid mechanics has two main branches: statics and dynamics. This chapter introduces both at the Physics I level.

## Chapter Summary

- [[08.02 Pressure and Hydrostatics|Pressure]] is $\mechanical{p}=d\mechanical{F}_\perp/d\mechanical{A}$. It is isotropic in a fluid at equilibrium.
- Hydrostatic equilibrium gives $d\mechanical{p}/d\mechanical{z}=-\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}$ and $\Delta\mechanical{p}=\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}$ for constant density.
- [[08.03 Pascal's Principle|Pascal's principle]] gives the transmission of a pressure change through an incompressible liquid. It is the basis of the hydraulic press.
- [[08.04 Archimedes' Principle|Archimedes' principle]] gives the buoyant force $\mechanical{\vec F}_A=-\mechanical{\rho}_f\mechanical{V}\mechanical{\vec g}$.
- The [[08.05 Continuity Equation|continuity equation]] gives constant $\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{A}\mechanical{v}$ along a steady streamtube, or constant $\mechanical{A}\mechanical{v}$ at constant density.
- [[08.06 Bernoulli's Equation|Bernoulli's equation]] gives constant $\mechanical{p}+\tfrac12\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}^2+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}$ along a streamline in ideal steady flow.
- In [[08.07 Viscosity and Laminar Flow|viscous pipe flow]], the Poiseuille flow rate scales as $\mechanical{Q}\propto\mechanical{R}^4$. The transition from laminar flow can occur around $\mathrm{Re}\sim2000$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p},\Delta\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The normal force per unit area and its change. |
| $\mechanical{F}_\perp,\mechanical{\vec F}_A$ | $\unit{N}$ | Force | The normal force on an area element and the net buoyant force. |
| $\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Area | The surface or streamtube cross-sectional area. |
| $\mechanical{\rho},\mechanical{\rho}_f$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The mass per unit volume of the fluid. |
| $\mechanical{\vec g},\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The downward acceleration vector and its magnitude. |
| $\mechanical{z},\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Height and depth | The upward position and the depth increase. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Displaced volume | The volume of fluid displaced by the immersed part of the body. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Flow speed | The speed along a streamline or through a section. |
| $\mechanical{Q}$ | $\unit{m^3\,s^{-1}}$ | Volume flow rate | The volume passing through a section per unit time. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Pipe radius | The internal radius of the pipe. |
| $\mathrm{Re}$ | 1 | Reynolds number | The ratio of inertial to viscous effects. |
