---
title: "Navier–Stokes Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The incompressible Navier–Stokes equation adds a viscous term to the momentum balance for fluid flow.

For an incompressible Newtonian fluid of constant dynamic viscosity $\mechanical{\eta}$, the **Navier–Stokes equation** is

$$
\mechanical{\rho}\left[\frac{\partial\mechanical{\vec v}}{\partial t}
+(\mechanical{\vec v}\cdot\nabla)\mechanical{\vec v}\right]
=-\nabla\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{\vec g}+\mechanical{\eta}\nabla^2\mechanical{\vec v}.
$$

The additional condition $\nabla\cdot\mechanical{\vec v}=0$ enforces incompressibility and constrains the pressure. The transition from laminar to turbulent flow depends on the dimensionless Reynolds number $\mathrm{Re}=\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{v}\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{\eta}$, with characteristic length $\mechanical{L}$.

**Stokes limit.** In steady flow at $\mathrm{Re}\ll1$, neglect convective acceleration. The linear equation is $\nabla\mechanical{p}=\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{\vec g}+\mechanical{\eta}\nabla^2\mechanical{\vec v}$. This describes small particles moving slowly relative to a viscous fluid. For a sphere of radius $\mechanical{R}$, the Stokes drag is $\mechanical{\vec F}_r=-6\pi\mechanical{\eta}\mechanical{R}\mechanical{\vec v}$, using the sphere's velocity relative to the fluid far away.

**High Reynolds number.** At $\mathrm{Re}\gg1$, convective inertia can dominate. Turbulent structures can form, including eddies of different sizes and a Kolmogorov cascade. Statistical models describe turbulence when detailed motion is impractical to calculate.

The source identifies existence and regularity for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations as an unsolved Millennium Prize Problem.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The mass per unit volume of the fluid. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v},\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity and speed | The fluid velocity field, characteristic flow speed, or relative particle velocity in the drag law. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the flow. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The local pressure in the fluid. |
| $\mechanical{\vec g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The external acceleration due to gravity. |
| $\mechanical{\eta}$ | $\unit{Pa\,s}$ | Dynamic viscosity | The coefficient of viscous momentum transport. |
| $\mathrm{Re}$ | 1 | Reynolds number | The ratio of inertial to viscous effects. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Characteristic length | The length scale used in the Reynolds number. |
| $\mechanical{R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Sphere radius | The radius of the particle in the Stokes drag formula. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_r$ | $\unit{N}$ | Drag force | The force opposing the sphere's motion relative to the fluid. |
