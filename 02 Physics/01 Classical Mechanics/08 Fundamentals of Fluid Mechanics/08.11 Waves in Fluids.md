---
title: "Waves in Fluids"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Pressure disturbances in a compressible fluid propagate as sound waves.

Disturbances in a fluid can travel as waves. For a small pressure disturbance in a compressible fluid, the speed of sound is

$$
\signal{c}_s=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\partial\mechanical{p}}{\partial\mechanical{\rho}}\right)_s}.
$$

The derivative is at constant specific entropy for adiabatic sound propagation.

For an ideal gas, $\signal{c}_s=\sqrt{\gamma\mechanical{p}/\mechanical{\rho}}$, where $\gamma=c_p/c_v$. For air at $20\unit{{}^\circ C}$, $\signal{c}_s\approx\quantity{343}{m\,s^{-1}}$.

**Surface gravity waves.** Let water depth be $\mechanical{h}$ and wavelength $\signal{\lambda}$. In deep water, $\mechanical{h}\gg\signal{\lambda}$, the dispersion relation is $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}\signal{k}}$ with $\signal{k}=2\pi/\signal{\lambda}$. In shallow water, $\mechanical{h}\ll\signal{\lambda}$, $\signal{\omega}=\signal{k}\sqrt{\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}$. These waves are nondispersive in the shallow-water limit and travel at speed $\sqrt{\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}$.

### Example — Tsunami

A tsunami in a $\quantity{4}{km}$ deep ocean can have a wavelength of about $\quantity{200}{km}$. The water is shallow relative to the wavelength. The speed is $\signal{v}=\sqrt{\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}=\sqrt{9.81\cdot4000}\,\unit{m\,s^{-1}}\approx\quantity{198}{m\,s^{-1}}\approx\quantity{713}{km\,h^{-1}}$. As the wave approaches the coast, its speed decreases as the depth decreases. Conservation of energy flux in the idealized model explains the increase in wave height.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{c}_s$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Sound speed | The speed of a small pressure disturbance. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The local pressure of the fluid. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The local mass per unit volume. |
| $s$ | $\unit{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Specific entropy | The entropy per unit mass held constant in the derivative. |
| $\gamma$ | 1 | Heat capacity ratio | The ratio of specific heat capacities. |
| $c_p,c_v$ | $\unit{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$ | Specific heat capacity | The heat capacities per unit mass at constant pressure and constant volume. |
| — | $\unit{{}^\circ C}$ | Air temperature | The temperature for the sound speed in the example. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Water depth | The depth of the water in the wave model. |
| $\signal{\lambda}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Wavelength | The distance between equivalent points of consecutive wave cycles. |
| $\signal{k}$ | $\unit{rad\,m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | The phase change per unit distance. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The phase change per unit time. |
| $\signal{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Wave speed | The propagation speed in the shallow-water model. |
