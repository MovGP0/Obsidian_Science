---
title: "Worked Example of an Oscillator Using Hamilton–Jacobi Theory"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Integrate Hamilton's characteristic function to recover the motion of the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator.

For the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator, $\energy{H}=p^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2q^2$. The time-independent Hamilton–Jacobi equation is

$$
\frac{1}{2\mechanical{m}}\left(\frac{d\energy{W}}{dq}\right)^2+\frac12\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2q^2=\energy{E}.
$$

On the positive-momentum branch, solve for the derivative and integrate.

$$
\frac{d\energy{W}}{dq}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\omega}^2q^2},\qquad
\energy{W}(q)=\int_0^q\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\omega}^2q'^2}\,dq'.
$$

Substitute $q'=\sqrt{2\energy{E}/(\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2)}\sin\phi$ with the corresponding integration angle. At the upper limit,

$$
\energy{W}(q,\energy{E})=\frac{\energy{E}}{\signal{\omega}}(\phi+\sin\phi\cos\phi),\qquad
\sin\phi=q\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2}{2\energy{E}}}.
$$

Hamilton's principal function is $\energy{S}(q,\energy{E},t)=\energy{W}(q,\energy{E})-\energy{E}t$. Differentiating with respect to $\energy{E}$ at fixed $q$ gives

$$
\beta=\frac{\partial\energy{S}}{\partial\energy{E}}=\frac{\phi}{\signal{\omega}}-t.
$$

Thus $\phi=\signal{\omega}(t+\beta)$. Substitution gives the motion, continued across the turning points,

$$
q(t)=\sqrt{\frac{2\energy{E}}{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2}}\sin\bigl(\signal{\omega}(t+\beta)\bigr).
$$

The amplitude is $\mechanical{A}=\sqrt{2\energy{E}/(\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2)}$, and the initial phase is $\signal{\omega}\beta$. The Hamilton–Jacobi method is particularly convenient when the coordinates match the symmetries of the problem.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{H},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energy | The Hamiltonian and its constant value on the trajectory. |
| $\energy{W},\energy{S}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Characteristic and principal functions | The action functions used to solve the oscillator. |
| $q,q',\mechanical{A}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and amplitude | The displacement, integration coordinate, and maximum displacement. |
| $p$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The canonical momentum of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The oscillator mass. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The constant angular frequency of the oscillation. |
| $\phi$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Phase angle | The angle introduced in the integration. |
| $t,\beta$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time and time offset | The time variable and the integration constant. |
