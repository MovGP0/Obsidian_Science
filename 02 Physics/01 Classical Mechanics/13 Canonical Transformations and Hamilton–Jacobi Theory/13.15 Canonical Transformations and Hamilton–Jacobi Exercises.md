---
title: "Canonical Transformations and Hamilton–Jacobi Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Practice canonical transformations, generating functions, and solutions of the Hamilton–Jacobi equation.

1. **Basic.** Verify that $Q=p$, $P=-q$ is canonical by calculating $\{Q,P\}$.
   **Result.** $\{Q,P\}=1$, so the transformation is canonical.
2. **Basic.** Find $\energy{K}(Q,P)$ for the type 2 generating function $F_2(q,P)=qP+\varepsilon q^2P$.
   **Result.** The type 2 relations give $p=\partial F_2/\partial q=(1+2\varepsilon q)P$ and $Q=\partial F_2/\partial P=q+\varepsilon q^2$. Since $F_2$ has no time dependence, $\energy{K}(Q,P)=\energy{H}(q(Q),p(Q,P))$. Solve $Q=q+\varepsilon q^2$ for $q$ on an invertible branch and substitute into the first relation.
3. **Basic.** Prove that $F_2=\sum_k q_kP_k$ generates the identity transformation.
4. **Basic.** Calculate $J=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint p\,dq$ for the harmonic oscillator and prove $J=\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}$.
5. **Intermediate.** Write the Hamilton–Jacobi equation for a free particle in one dimension and find $\energy{S}$ explicitly.
   **Result.** $\energy{S}=\alpha q-\alpha^2t/(2\mechanical{m})$ and $q=\alpha t/\mechanical{m}+\beta$.
6. **Intermediate.** Verify that $Q=q\cos\alpha+p\sin\alpha$, $P=-q\sin\alpha+p\cos\alpha$ is canonical for every constant $\alpha$. Use canonical variables scaled to compatible units.
7. **Intermediate.** Prove that the harmonic oscillator is isochronous using action–angle variables.
8. **Intermediate.** Calculate the action $J(\energy{E})$ of a nonlinear pendulum in the small-oscillation limit. Measure $\energy{E}$ from the minimum of the potential.
   **Result.** $J=\energy{E}/\signal{\omega}_0+O(\energy{E}^2)$, so $\energy{E}\simeq J\signal{\omega}_0$ to leading order.
9. **Challenge.** Write the Hamilton–Jacobi equation for the Kepler problem in spherical coordinates and separate the variables.
10. **Challenge.** Show that the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector is the conserved quantity associated with the hidden symmetry of the Kepler problem. For bound motion, this symmetry has the $SO(4)$ form.
11. **Intermediate.** Verify that $G=p^2/2$ generates $\delta q=\varepsilon p$, $\delta p=0$. The free-particle time evolution has the generator $G/\mechanical{m}$.
    **Result.** $\delta q=\varepsilon\{q,p^2/2\}=\varepsilon p$ and $\delta p=\varepsilon\{p,p^2/2\}=0$.
12. **Intermediate.** Apply Bohr–Sommerfeld quantization to the harmonic oscillator and derive $\energy{E}_n=\hbar\signal{\omega}(n+\tfrac12)$.
13. **Intermediate.** For the effective one-dimensional Kepler problem, write the radial action $J_r=(2\pi)^{-1}\oint\mechanical{p}_r\,d\mechanical{r}$ in terms of $\energy{E}$ and $\mechanical{L}$. Verify $\energy{E}=-\mechanical{\mu}\alpha^2/[2(J_r+\mechanical{L})^2]$.

14. **Challenge.** For the harmonic oscillator $\energy{H}=p^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2q^2$, introduce $a=\sqrt{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}/2}\,q+ip/\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}}$ and $a^*=\bar a$. Verify $\{a,ia^*\}=1$ and $\energy{H}=\signal{\omega}a^*a$. Explain the analogy with the creation and annihilation operators, including the correspondence $a^*a\leftrightarrow\hat a^\dagger\hat a$ up to normalization.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,p,Q,P$ | — | Canonical variables | The original and transformed coordinates and momenta. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{K},\energy{E},\energy{E}_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonians and energies | The original and transformed Hamiltonians, classical energy, and quantized levels. |
| $F_2,\energy{S},J,J_r,\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action quantities | The type 2 generator, principal function, actions, and reduced Planck constant. |
| $\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon,G$ | — | Parameters and generator | The constants and infinitesimal generator, with meanings and units specific to each exercise. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass and reduced mass | The mass of the particle and reduced mass of the Kepler system. |
| $\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Radial coordinate | The separation of the Kepler bodies. |
| $\mechanical{p}_r$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Radial momentum | The momentum conjugate to separation. |
| $\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The magnitude of the Kepler angular momentum. |
| $\signal{\omega},\signal{\omega}_0$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The oscillator frequency and the small-amplitude pendulum frequency. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $a,a^*$ | $\unit{J^{1/2}\,s^{1/2}}$ | Complex oscillator variables | The canonical variables used in the operator analogy. |
