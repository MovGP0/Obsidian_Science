---
title: "Examples of Useful Canonical Transformations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Canonical transformations can exchange phase-space coordinates, use complex oscillator variables, or move to a rotating frame.

## Exchange of coordinates

The transformation $(q,p)\to(p,-q)$ exchanges the coordinate and momentum with a sign change. In suitably scaled axes it is a quarter-turn in the phase plane. It is canonical because $\{Q,P\}=\{p,-q\}=-\{p,q\}=1$.

## Complex oscillator variables

For the harmonic oscillator, set

$$
a=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}}{2}}q+\frac{ip}{\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}}},\qquad a^*=\bar a.
$$

Then $a^*a=(\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}/2)q^2+p^2/(2\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega})$, so $\energy{H}=\signal{\omega}a^*a$. The complex variables correspond to the annihilation and creation operators of the quantum oscillator. The dimensionless operators obey $[\hat a,\hat a^\dagger]=1$ after normalization by $\sqrt{\hbar}$.

> [!note] Source convention
> The source uses the unit commutator for the variables as written. This assumes normalization by $\sqrt{\hbar}$ or units with $\hbar=1$.

## Rotating coordinates for the three-body problem

To study the three-body problem near the Lagrange points, use a frame that rotates with the orbital angular speed $\mechanical{\Omega}$ of the two primary bodies. The canonical transformation gives $\energy{H}'=\energy{H}-\mechanical{\Omega}\mechanical{L}_z$, where $\mechanical{L}_z$ is the axial angular momentum. The Lagrange points are critical points of the effective Jacobi potential. In the source's sign convention, $L_1,L_2,L_3$ are saddle points and $L_4,L_5$ are maxima. The Coriolis term permits dynamic stability at $L_4,L_5$ when the ratio of the larger primary mass to the smaller one exceeds about $24.96$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,p,Q,P$ | — | Canonical variables | The original and transformed canonical pairs. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator. |
| $\signal{\omega},\mechanical{\Omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The oscillator frequency and the rotation rate of the orbital frame. |
| $a,a^*$ | $\unit{J^{1/2}\,s^{1/2}}$ | Complex oscillator variables | The variables before normalization by the square root of the reduced Planck constant. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The normalization scale for the quantum operators. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{H}'$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonians | The original Hamiltonian and the rotating-frame Hamiltonian. |
| $\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Axial angular momentum | The component along the rotation axis. |
