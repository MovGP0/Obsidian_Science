---
title: "Hamilton–Jacobi Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A generating function that makes the transformed Hamiltonian zero gives constant transformed coordinates and momenta.

Seek a canonical transformation $(q,p)\to(Q,P)$ such that $\energy{K}(Q,P,t)=0$. Then Hamilton's equations reduce to $\dot Q=\dot P=0$, so $Q$ and $P$ give $2n$ constants of motion.

Use the type 2 generating function $\energy{S}(q,P,t)$, with $\energy{K}=\energy{H}+\partial\energy{S}/\partial t=0$. Substitute $p_k=\partial\energy{S}/\partial q_k$ into $\energy{H}$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 13.1 | Hamilton–Jacobi Equation
> The generating function $\energy{S}(q,P,t)$ satisfies
>
> $$
> \energy{H}\left(q,\frac{\partial\energy{S}}{\partial q},t\right)+\frac{\partial\energy{S}}{\partial t}=0.
> $$

This is a first-order partial differential equation in $\energy{S}$. The new momenta $P$ are integration constants of a complete solution.

**Hamilton's principal function.** A complete solution $\energy{S}(q,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n,t)$ has $n$ independent constants $\alpha_k$. The equations $\beta_k=\partial\energy{S}/\partial\alpha_k$ give the other $n$ constants and determine $q(t)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,p,Q,P,\alpha_k,\beta_k$ | — | Canonical variables and constants | The old and new variables, and the constants in the complete solution. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonians | The old and new Hamiltonians. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Hamilton's principal function | The generating function of the transformation. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
