---
title: "Canonical Transformations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Canonical transformations preserve the form of Hamilton's equations and the fundamental Poisson brackets.

> [!definition] Definition 13.1 | Canonical Transformation
> A transformation $(q,p)\to(Q,P)$ with $Q=Q(q,p,t)$ and $P=P(q,p,t)$ is **canonical** when it preserves the form of Hamilton's equations. There is a Hamiltonian $\energy{K}(Q,P,t)$ such that
>
> $$
> \dot Q_k=\frac{\partial\energy{K}}{\partial P_k},\qquad
> \dot P_k=-\frac{\partial\energy{K}}{\partial Q_k}.
> $$

**Characterization.** A transformation is canonical if and only if it preserves the fundamental Poisson brackets, computed in the original variables $(q,p)$.

$$
\{Q_k,Q_l\}=0,\qquad \{P_k,P_l\}=0,\qquad \{Q_k,P_l\}=\delta_{kl}.
$$

**Consequence.** Poisson brackets are canonical invariants. In the two coordinate systems, $\{f,g\}_{q,p}=\{f,g\}_{Q,P}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,p,Q,P$ | — | Canonical variables | The original and transformed coordinates and momenta. |
| $\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Transformed Hamiltonian | The function that generates motion in the new variables. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
