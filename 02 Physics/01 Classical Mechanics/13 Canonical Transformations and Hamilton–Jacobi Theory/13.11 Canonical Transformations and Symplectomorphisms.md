---
title: "Canonical Transformations and Symplectomorphisms"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A canonical transformation preserves the symplectic two-form on phase space.

Geometrically, a canonical transformation is a **symplectomorphism**. It is a diffeomorphism of phase space that preserves the form $\omega=\sum_k dq_k\wedge dp_k$.

**Characterization.** For the Jacobian matrix $M$ of the transformation, canonicity is equivalent to

$$
M^TJM=J,\qquad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_n\\-I_n&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The matrix $M$ belongs to the symplectic group $\operatorname{Sp}(2n,\mathbb R)$.

- Every symplectic matrix has determinant $+1$. The symplectic form determines an orientation, and the matrix preserves it.
- Phase-space volume is preserved.
- Poisson brackets are invariant.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
