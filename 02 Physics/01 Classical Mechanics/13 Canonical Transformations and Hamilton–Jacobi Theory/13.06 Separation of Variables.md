---
title: "Separation of Variables"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Separation of variables can reduce the Hamilton–Jacobi equation to simpler equations in individual coordinates.

The Hamilton–Jacobi equation can be integrated by separation of variables if $\energy{S}$ has a separable form, such as $\energy{S}=\sum_k\energy{S}_k(q_k,t)+\cdots$. Coordinates adapted to the symmetries often give such a form.

**Time-independent Hamiltonian.** If $\energy{H}$ has no explicit time dependence, set $\energy{S}=\energy{W}(q,\alpha)-\energy{E}t$. Here $\energy{W}$ is **Hamilton's characteristic function**. The equation becomes

$$
\energy{H}\left(q,\frac{\partial\energy{W}}{\partial q}\right)=\energy{E}.
$$

**Example. Harmonic oscillator.** For $\energy{H}=p^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2q^2$, the separated equation is

$$
\frac{1}{2\mechanical{m}}\left(\frac{d\energy{W}}{dq}\right)^2+\frac12\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2q^2=\energy{E}.
$$

On the positive-momentum branch, $d\energy{W}/dq=\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}\energy{E}-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\omega}^2q^2}$. Integrate to find $\energy{W}(q,\energy{E})$. Then $\beta=\partial\energy{S}/\partial\energy{E}=\partial\energy{W}/\partial\energy{E}-t$ gives $q(t)=\sqrt{2\energy{E}/(\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2)}\sin(\signal{\omega}t+\signal{\varphi})$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{S},\energy{W}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Principal and characteristic functions | The action functions with and without the time term. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energy | The Hamiltonian and its constant value on the trajectory. |
| $q,p$ | — | Canonical variables | The generalized coordinate and conjugate momentum. |
| $\alpha$ | — | Separation constants | The constants on which the characteristic function depends. |
| $t,\beta$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time and integration constant | The time variable and its additive constant in the oscillator example. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The oscillator mass. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The oscillator's natural frequency. |
| $\signal{\varphi}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Phase | The constant phase offset of the oscillation. |
