---
title: "Hamilton–Jacobi Theory for Nonseparable Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Near an integrable Hamiltonian, perturbation theory can describe nonseparable motion. Resonances can limit its validity.

If the Hamilton–Jacobi equation does not separate, the integration is more difficult. When the Hamiltonian is close to an integrable one, use perturbation methods. Lindstedt's method constructs recursive action–angle expansions in a small perturbation parameter $\varepsilon$. Away from resonances, these expansions can describe the perturbed motion.

**Small denominators.** Near resonances, small denominators can make the formal perturbation series diverge. Under suitable nonresonance conditions and for sufficiently small perturbations of a nondegenerate integrable system, the KAM theorem ensures that many invariant tori survive. Strong resonant perturbations can produce chaotic motion.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source describes KAM as guaranteeing convergence of the perturbation series away from resonances and links series divergence directly to chaos. KAM guarantees persistence of tori under its hypotheses. Divergence alone does not establish chaotic motion.

**Example. Solar System.** The masses of the other planets cause small perturbations to each planetary orbit. Laplace–Lagrange theory gives integrable equations at first order in its secular approximation. Higher-order calculations and numerical simulations show weak chaotic behavior. The source gives a Lyapunov time of order $\quantity{10^7}{yr}$, shorter than the system age of about $\quantity{4.5\times10^9}{yr}$.
