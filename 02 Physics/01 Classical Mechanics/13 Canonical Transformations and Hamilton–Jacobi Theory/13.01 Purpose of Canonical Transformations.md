---
title: "Purpose of Canonical Transformations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A canonical change of coordinates preserves the symplectic structure and gives Hamilton's equations in the new variables.

The Hamiltonian formulation gives considerable freedom in the choice of phase-space coordinates. Any change $(q,p)\to(Q,P)$ that preserves the symplectic structure gives Hamilton's equations in the new coordinates. This includes transformations that mix coordinates and momenta. In the Lagrangian formulation, the usual change of generalized coordinates $q$ determines the corresponding velocities $\dot q$ by differentiation.

The purpose is to find coordinates that simplify the Hamiltonian. For an integrable system, it can depend only on the new momenta. If the transformed Hamiltonian vanishes, the new coordinates and momenta are constant. The Hamilton–Jacobi equation gives this last simplification.

Canonical transformations have these uses.

- Construct action–angle variables for integrable systems.
- Separate variables in Hamilton–Jacobi theory.
- Analyze perturbations of integrable systems, as in KAM theory and Lindstedt's method.
- Formulate semiclassical quantization.
