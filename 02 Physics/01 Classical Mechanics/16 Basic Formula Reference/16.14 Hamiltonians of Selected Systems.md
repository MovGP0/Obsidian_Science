title: "Hamiltonians of Selected Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Reference Hamiltonians for a free particle, oscillator, pendulum, central force, and electromagnetic field.

| System | Hamiltonian |
| --- | --- |
| Free particle in three dimensions | $\energy{H}=\mechanical{\vec p}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical{m})$ |
| One-dimensional harmonic oscillator | $\energy{H}=p^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2q^2$ |
| Plane pendulum | $\energy{H}=p_\theta^2/(2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2)-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\theta$ |
| Central motion | $\energy{H}=\mechanical{p}_r^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\mechanical{L}^2/(2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2)+\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})$ |
| Particle in an electromagnetic field | $\energy{H}=\lvert\mechanical{\vec p}-\electric{q}\magnetic{\vec A}\rvert^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\electric{q}\electric{\Phi}$ |

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{H},\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and potential | The Hamiltonian and central potential energy. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle or pendulum bob mass. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p},p,\mechanical{p}_r$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Linear momentum | The canonical vector, oscillator momentum, and radial momentum. |
| $p_\theta,\mechanical{L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momenta | The pendulum's conjugate momentum and central angular momentum. |
| $q,\mechanical{\ell},\mechanical{r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lengths | The oscillator displacement, pendulum length, and radial distance. |
| $\theta$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Pendulum angle | The angle from the downward vertical. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The oscillator's natural frequency. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The local field magnitude. |
| $\electric{q}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Charge | The electric charge of the particle. |
| $\magnetic{\vec A}$ | $\unit{T\,m}$ | Vector potential | The magnetic vector potential. |
| $\electric{\Phi}$ | $\unit{V}$ | Scalar potential | The electric scalar potential. |
