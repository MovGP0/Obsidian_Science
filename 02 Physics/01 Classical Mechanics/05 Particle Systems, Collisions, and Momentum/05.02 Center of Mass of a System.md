---
title: "Center of Mass of a System"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The center of mass is a mass-weighted position. Its acceleration is the net external force divided by total mass.

> [!definition] Definition 5.1 — Center of Mass
> For a system of $N$ particles of masses $\mechanical{m}_i$ at positions $\mechanical{\vec r}_i$, the **center of mass** is
>
> $$
> \mechanical{\vec R}=\frac{\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec r}_i}{\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i}
> =\frac{1}{\mechanical{M}}\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec r}_i,
> $$
>
> where $\mechanical{M}=\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i$ is the total mass.

For a continuous body with density $\mechanical{\rho}(\mechanical{\vec r})$ throughout volume $\mechanical{V}$,

$$
\mechanical{\vec R}=\frac{1}{\mechanical{M}}\int_V\mechanical{\rho}(\mechanical{\vec r})\mechanical{\vec r}\,dV.
$$

**Velocity.** The velocity of the center of mass is $\mechanical{\vec V}=\mechanical{\dot{\vec R}}=(\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec v}_i)/\mechanical{M}=\mechanical{\vec P}/\mechanical{M}$, where $\mechanical{\vec P}$ is the total momentum.

**Acceleration and Newton's second law.** Sum Newton's equations for all particles. By the third law, the internal forces cancel in the sum.

$$
\mechanical{M}\mechanical{\ddot{\vec R}}=\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{ext}},
$$

where $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is the net external force.

The center of mass moves like a point particle of mass $\mechanical{M}$ subject to the net external force.

### Example — Projectile Explosion

A projectile follows a parabolic trajectory and breaks into two fragments in flight. The explosion creates internal forces. In the same uniform gravitational field with drag neglected, the center of mass of the fragments continues on the original parabola regardless of the details of the explosion.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}_i,\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of an individual particle and the total mass of the system. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}_i,\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The position of a particle or volume element. |
| $\mechanical{\vec R}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Center-of-mass position | The mass-weighted mean position. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Mass density | The mass per unit volume in a continuous body. |
| $\mechanical{V}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The region occupied by the continuous body. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}_i$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Particle velocity | The velocity of particle $i$. |
| $\mechanical{\vec V}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Center-of-mass velocity | The rate of change of the center-of-mass position. |
| $\mechanical{\vec P}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Total momentum | The sum of the individual momenta. |
| $\mechanical{\ddot{\vec R}}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Center-of-mass acceleration | The second time derivative of the center-of-mass position. |
| $\mechanical{\vec F}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Net external force | The vector sum of the forces exerted from outside the system. |
