title: "Worked Examples of Collisions"

> [!TLDR]
> Apply momentum conservation during a short collision. Apply kinetic or mechanical energy conservation in the stages where it holds.

## Ballistic Pendulum

A projectile of mass $\mechanical{m}$ and horizontal speed $\mechanical{v}_0$ strikes a stationary wooden block of mass $\mechanical{M}$ suspended from a cord of length $\mechanical{\ell}$. The projectile embeds in the block in a perfectly inelastic collision.

**Stage 1 — Impact.** Momentum conservation gives $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}_0=(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})\mechanical{v}'$, so $\mechanical{v}'=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}_0/(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})$. The kinetic energy change is $\Delta\energy{K}=-\tfrac12\mechanical{\mu}\mechanical{v}_0^2$, with $\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{M}/(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})$.

**Stage 2 — Swing.** The combined pendulum rises through height $\mechanical{h}$. Energy conservation gives $\tfrac12(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})(\mechanical{v}')^2=(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}$, so $\mechanical{h}=(\mechanical{v}')^2/(2\mechanical{g})$. Solving for the incoming speed gives

$$
\mechanical{v}_0=\frac{\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{m}}\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}.
$$

For $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{0.01}{kg}$, $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{1}{kg}$, and $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{0.05}{m}$, the result is $\mechanical{v}_0\approx101\sqrt{0.981}\,\unit{m\,s^{-1}}\approx\quantity{100}{m\,s^{-1}}$.

## Elastic Collisions Between Identical Balls

Five identical balls stand in a row. A ball at one end hits the stationary row at speed $\mechanical{v}$. The ball at the other end leaves at speed $\mechanical{v}$, and the intermediate balls remain at rest. This observed result conserves total momentum and kinetic energy. It follows from applying the equal-mass elastic collision equations to successive pairs, assuming short contact times.

## Projectile Explosion in Flight

A projectile of mass $\mechanical{M}$ and velocity $\mechanical{\vec v}$ follows a parabolic trajectory. It explodes into two fragments of equal mass.

One fragment is at rest relative to the ground just after the explosion. Find the velocity of the other. Momentum conservation gives $\mechanical{M}\mechanical{\vec v}=(\mechanical{M}/2)\vec 0+(\mechanical{M}/2)\mechanical{\vec v}_2$. Thus $\mechanical{\vec v}_2=2\mechanical{\vec v}$. The second fragment moves in the original direction at twice the speed. The explosion exerts internal forces, so the center of mass continues on the original parabola.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{M}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The masses of the projectile and block, or the mass before fragmentation. |
| $\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Reduced mass | The reduced mass of the projectile and block. |
| $\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Cord length | The length of the pendulum cord. |
| $\mechanical{v}_0,\mechanical{v}'$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed before and after impact | The speed of the projectile and the speed of the joined bodies. |
| $\mechanical{v},\mechanical{\vec v},\mechanical{\vec v}_2$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed or velocity | The speed of a ball, the velocity before the explosion, or the velocity of the second fragment. |
| $\Delta\energy{K}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Change in kinetic energy | The decrease in the kinetic energy during the inelastic collision. |
| $\mechanical{h}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Rise height | The vertical rise of the joined bodies. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Acceleration due to gravity | The magnitude of the downward acceleration in free fall. |
