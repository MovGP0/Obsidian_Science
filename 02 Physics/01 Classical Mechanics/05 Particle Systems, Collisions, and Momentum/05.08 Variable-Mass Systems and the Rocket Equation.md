---
title: "Variable-Mass Systems and the Rocket Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A rocket gains speed by expelling mass. For constant exhaust speed and zero external force, its speed gain is proportional to the logarithm of the mass ratio.

In some systems the mass changes with time. A rocket expels matter, while a growing drop takes matter in. Use the momentum form of Newton's second law, $\mechanical{\vec F}=d\mechanical{\vec p}/dt$, to balance momentum for the system including the transferred matter.

**Rocket equation (Tsiolkovsky).** A rocket of mass $\mechanical{m}(t)$ ejects gas backward at speed $\mechanical{u}$ relative to the rocket, with $\mechanical{\dot m}<0$. The momentum balance in a fixed inertial frame between $t$ and $t+dt$ gives

$$
\mechanical{m}\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}
=-\mechanical{u}\frac{d\mechanical{m}}{dt}+\mechanical{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}.
$$

With constant exhaust speed and no external force, integration gives

$$
\mechanical{v}(t)-\mechanical{v}_0=\mechanical{u}\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}_0}{\mechanical{m}(t)}.
$$

The speed gain depends logarithmically on the mass ratio.

$$
\boxed{\Delta\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{u}\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}_0}{\mechanical{m}_f}.}
$$

### Example — Single-Stage Rocket

An exhaust speed of $\mechanical{u}=\quantity{4}{km\,s^{-1}}$ and mass ratio $\mechanical{m}_0/\mechanical{m}_f=10$ give $\Delta\mechanical{v}=\quantity{4\ln 10}{km\,s^{-1}}\approx\quantity{9.2}{km\,s^{-1}}$.

This is of the order of the speed gain needed to reach low Earth orbit and is below the escape speed from Earth's surface, about $\quantity{11.2}{km\,s^{-1}}$. Interplanetary missions use multiple stages.

> [!note] Analytical Mechanics
> The conservation of linear and angular momentum in an isolated system follows from the invariance of the system under spatial translations and rotations. See [[11.07 Noether's Theorem]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec F},\mechanical{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ | $\unit{N}$ | Net force | The net force or its signed external component in the rocket balance. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The momentum included in the balance. |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{m}_0,\mechanical{m}_f$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Rocket mass | The instantaneous, initial, and final masses of the rocket. |
| $\mechanical{\dot m}$ | $\unit{kg\,s^{-1}}$ | Mass change rate | The negative rate at which the rocket loses mass. |
| $\mechanical{u}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Exhaust speed | The speed of the ejected gas relative to the rocket. |
| $\mechanical{v},\mechanical{v}_0,\Delta\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Rocket velocity and change | The instantaneous and initial velocities along the thrust axis and their difference. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| — | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Escape speed | The minimum launch speed to escape Earth's gravity in the ideal model. |
