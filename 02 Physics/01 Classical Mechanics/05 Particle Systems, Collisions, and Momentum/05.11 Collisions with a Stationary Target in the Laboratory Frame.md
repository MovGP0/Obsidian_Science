---
title: "Collisions with a Stationary Target in the Laboratory Frame"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Transform an elastic collision to the center-of-mass frame, rotate the velocity vectors by the scattering angle, and transform back to the laboratory.

In an elastic collision with particle 2 initially at rest, the center-of-mass velocity in the laboratory is $\mechanical{\vec V}=\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{\vec v}_1/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)$. In the center-of-mass frame, the initial velocities are

$$
\mechanical{\vec u}_1=\frac{\mechanical{m}_2}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}\mechanical{\vec v}_1,
\qquad
\mechanical{\vec u}_2=-\frac{\mechanical{m}_1}{\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2}\mechanical{\vec v}_1.
$$

The collision rotates these velocities through the center-of-mass scattering angle $\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}$. Their magnitudes remain constant.

**Laboratory scattering angle.** For the projectile,

$$
\tan\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{lab}}=
\frac{\sin\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}}{\cos\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}+\mechanical{m}_1/\mechanical{m}_2}.
$$

Use the signs of the laboratory velocity components to select the quadrant.

**Target recoil energy.** Particle 2 gains laboratory kinetic energy

$$
\energy{K}'_2=\frac{4\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{m}_2}{(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)^2}
\sin^2(\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}/2)\,\energy{K}_1.
$$

At the maximum, $\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}=\pi$, which is a head-on collision. Then $\energy{K}'_2=[4\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{m}_2/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)^2]\energy{K}_1$. For equal masses, $\energy{K}'_2=\energy{K}_1$, so all the incident kinetic energy is transferred to the target.

### Example — Energy Transfer in a Neutron Moderator

In a reactor, repeated collisions with a moderator slow fast neutrons from the $\unit{MeV}$ range to thermal energies in the $\unit{meV}$ range. For a neutron of mass $\mechanical{m}_n$ and a nucleus of mass $A\mechanical{m}_n$, the maximum energy fraction transferred in a head-on collision is $4A/(A+1)^2$. The source gives $100\%$ for hydrogen ($A=1$) and about $28\%$ for carbon ($A=12$). Moderator materials include hydrogen-rich water and polyethylene, and materials that contain deuterium.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The masses of the projectile and target. |
| $\mechanical{\vec V}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Center-of-mass velocity | The velocity of the center of mass in the laboratory. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}_1$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Incident velocity | The initial projectile velocity in the laboratory. |
| $\mechanical{\vec u}_1,\mechanical{\vec u}_2$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Center-of-mass frame velocities | The velocities of the two particles relative to the center of mass. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{CM}}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Center-of-mass scattering angle | The change in direction of the projectile velocity in the center-of-mass frame. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}_{\mathrm{lab}}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Laboratory scattering angle | The angle between the initial and final projectile velocities in the laboratory. |
| $\energy{K}_1,\energy{K}'_2$ | $\unit{J}$ | Kinetic energy | The incident projectile energy and the final target energy. |
| $\mechanical{m}_n$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Neutron mass | The reference mass used for the collision with a nucleus. |
| $A$ | 1 | Nuclear mass ratio | The approximate mass of the nucleus relative to a neutron. |
