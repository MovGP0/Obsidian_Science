> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions cover constant acceleration, projectile motion, and other problems in point-particle kinematics.

See [[01.11 Kinematics Exercises]] for the exercise list.

## Exercise 1. Uniform acceleration in a straight line

*A car starts from rest with acceleration $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{2.5}{m\,s^{-2}}$. How long does it take to reach $\mechanical{v}=\quantity{30}{m\,s^{-1}}$, and how far does it travel?*

From $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{a}t$, the time is $t=30/2.5=\quantity{12}{s}$. Then $\mechanical{x}=\tfrac12\mechanical{a}t^2=0.5\cdot2.5\cdot144=\quantity{180}{m}$. The relation $\mechanical{v}^2=2\mechanical{a}\mechanical{x}$ gives the same result, $\mechanical{x}=900/5=\quantity{180}{m}$.

## Exercise 2. Projectile motion

*A projectile is launched at speed $\mechanical{v}_0=\quantity{20}{m\,s^{-1}}$ and angle $\alpha=30^\circ$. Find the range and maximum height.*

The range is $\mechanical{x}_g=\mechanical{v}_0^2\sin(2\alpha)/\mechanical{g}=400\cdot0.866/9.81\simeq\quantity{35.3}{m}$. The height is $\mechanical{y}_h=\mechanical{v}_0^2\sin^2\alpha/(2\mechanical{g})=400\cdot0.25/19.62\simeq\quantity{5.10}{m}$.

## Exercise 3. Uniform circular motion

*A wheel of radius $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{0.30}{m}$ turns at $300\,\mathrm{rpm}$. Find its angular speed, rim speed, and centripetal acceleration.*

The angular speed is $\mechanical{\omega}=300\cdot2\pi/60=10\pi\,\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}\simeq\quantity{31.4}{rad\,s^{-1}}$. Then $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{\omega}\mechanical{R}=\quantity{9.42}{m\,s^{-1}}$, and $\mechanical{a}_c=\mechanical{\omega}^2\mechanical{R}=100\pi^2\cdot0.30\simeq\quantity{296}{m\,s^{-2}}$.

## Exercise 4. Relative motion in a river

*A river flows at $\mechanical{V}=\quantity{1.5}{m\,s^{-1}}$. A boat moves at $\mechanical{v}'=\quantity{2}{m\,s^{-1}}$ relative to the water, perpendicular to the bank. Find its velocity relative to the bank and its angle.*

The velocity components are $\mechanical{\vec v}=(\mechanical{V},\mechanical{v}')$. Its magnitude is $\sqrt{2.25+4}=\quantity{2.5}{m\,s^{-1}}$. The angle to the perpendicular is $\theta=\arctan(\mechanical{V}/\mechanical{v}')=\arctan(0.75)\simeq36.9^\circ$.

## Exercise 5. Vertical launch

*Launch a body vertically upward at $\mechanical{v}_0=\quantity{25}{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find the time to return to the starting point.*

The ascent time is $\mechanical{v}_0/\mechanical{g}$. The total time is $2\mechanical{v}_0/\mechanical{g}=50/9.81\simeq\quantity{5.10}{s}$.

## Exercise 6. Last second of free fall

*A ball falls from height $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{80}{m}$. Find its impact speed and the fraction of the height it falls in the last second.*

The impact speed is $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}=\sqrt{1569.6}\simeq\quantity{39.6}{m\,s^{-1}}$. The total time is $t_c=\sqrt{2\mechanical{h}/\mechanical{g}}=\sqrt{16.31}\simeq\quantity{4.04}{s}$. After about $\quantity{3.04}{s}$, the ball has fallen $\tfrac12\mechanical{g}(t_c-\quantity{1}{s})^2\simeq\quantity{45.3}{m}$. It falls $\quantity{34.7}{m}$ in the last second, about $0.43$ of the height.

## Exercise 7. Average velocity

*For uniform acceleration, show that the average velocity on $[0,T]$ equals $(\mechanical{v}_0+\mechanical{v}(T))/2$.*

Integrate the velocity over time.

$$
\langle\mechanical{v}\rangle=\frac1T\int_0^T(\mechanical{v}_0+\mechanical{a}t)\,dt
=\mechanical{v}_0+\frac{\mechanical{a}T}{2}
=\frac{\mechanical{v}_0+\mechanical{v}(T)}2.
$$

The last equality uses $\mechanical{v}(T)=\mechanical{v}_0+\mechanical{a}T$. In general, the average position is different from the mean of the endpoint positions.

## Exercise 8. Orbital period in low orbit

*A satellite has altitude $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{400}{km}$ and speed $\mechanical{v}\simeq\quantity{7.7}{km\,s^{-1}}$. Find the orbital period.*

The orbital radius is $\mechanical{R}=\mechanical{R}_T+\mechanical{h}=\quantity{6.78\times10^6}{m}$. The period is $\signal{T}=2\pi\mechanical{R}/\mechanical{v}\simeq\quantity{5532}{s}\simeq\quantity{92.2}{min}$.

## Exercise 9. Projectile range from a height

*Launch a projectile from height $\mechanical{h}_0$ at speed $\mechanical{v}_0$ and angle $\alpha$. Find the horizontal range.*

Use $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{v}_0t\cos\alpha$ and $\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{h}_0+\mechanical{v}_0t\sin\alpha-\tfrac12\mechanical{g}t^2$. Solve the quadratic equation $\mechanical{y}=0$ for the positive flight time.

$$
t_c=\frac{\mechanical{v}_0\sin\alpha+\sqrt{\mechanical{v}_0^2\sin^2\alpha+2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}_0}}{\mechanical{g}}.
$$

Thus the range $\mechanical{x}_g=\mechanical{v}_0t_c\cos\alpha$ is

$$
\mechanical{x}_g=\frac{\mechanical{v}_0\cos\alpha}{\mechanical{g}}\left(\mechanical{v}_0\sin\alpha+\sqrt{\mechanical{v}_0^2\sin^2\alpha+2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}_0}\right).
$$

## Exercise 10. Polar coordinates in circular motion

*For uniform circular motion, verify $\mechanical{\dot r}=0$, $\dot\theta=\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{R}$, and $\mechanical{\vec a}=-\mechanical{R}\dot\theta^2\hat e_r$.*

The radius $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{R}$ is constant, so $\mechanical{\dot r}=\mechanical{\ddot r}=0$. Since $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{R}\dot\theta$ is constant, $\dot\theta$ is constant. In the polar-coordinate acceleration
$\mechanical{\vec a}=(\mechanical{\ddot r}-\mechanical{r}\dot\theta^2)\hat e_r+(\mechanical{r}\ddot\theta+2\mechanical{\dot r}\dot\theta)\hat e_\theta$, only $-\mechanical{R}\dot\theta^2\hat e_r$ remains.

## Exercise 11. Tangential and normal components

*Show that $\mechanical{\vec a}=\mechanical{a}_\tau\hat\tau+(\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R})\hat n$.*

Differentiate $\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{v}\hat\tau$ with $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{\dot s}$. Then $\mechanical{\vec a}=\mechanical{\dot v}\hat\tau+\mechanical{v}\dot{\hat\tau}$. The Frenet relation gives $\dot{\hat\tau}=(\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{R})\hat n$, so $\mechanical{\vec a}=\mechanical{\dot v}\hat\tau+(\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R})\hat n$.

## Exercise 12. Polar curve with oscillating radius

*For $\mechanical{r}(t)=\mechanical{R}\cos(\mechanical{\omega}t)$ and $\theta(t)=\mechanical{\omega}t$, find the Cartesian trajectory.*

Use $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{r}\cos\theta=\mechanical{R}[1+\cos(2\mechanical{\omega}t)]/2$ and $\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{r}\sin\theta=(\mechanical{R}/2)\sin(2\mechanical{\omega}t)$. This is a circle of radius $\mechanical{R}/2$ centered at $(\mechanical{R}/2,0)$, traversed at angular speed $2\lvert\mechanical{\omega}\rvert$.

## Exercise 13. Braking

*The tangential acceleration is $\mechanical{a}_\tau=\quantity{-3}{m\,s^{-2}}$ and the initial speed is $\mechanical{v}_0=\quantity{20}{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find the stopping distance and time.*

The stopping distance is $\mechanical{d}=\mechanical{v}_0^2/(2\lvert\mechanical{a}_\tau\rvert)=400/6\simeq\quantity{66.7}{m}$. The time is $t=\mechanical{v}_0/\lvert\mechanical{a}_\tau\rvert=20/3\simeq\quantity{6.67}{s}$.

## Exercise 14. Oncoming trains

*Trains with speeds $\mechanical{v}_1=\quantity{25}{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $\mechanical{v}_2=\quantity{30}{m\,s^{-1}}$ travel in opposite directions. The second train has length $\mechanical{L}=\quantity{200}{m}$. Find its relative speed and the time to pass an observer on the first train.*

The second train's speed relative to the first is $\mechanical{v}_1+\mechanical{v}_2=\quantity{55}{m\,s^{-1}}$. It takes $t=\mechanical{L}/(\mechanical{v}_1+\mechanical{v}_2)=200/55\simeq\quantity{3.64}{s}$ to pass the observer.

## Exercise 15. Logarithmic spiral

*Show that the angle between the tangent and the radial direction is constant.*

For $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{r}_0e^{a\theta}$, the radial velocity is $\mechanical{v}_r=\mechanical{\dot r}=a\mechanical{r}\dot\theta$. The transverse velocity is $\mechanical{v}_\theta=\mechanical{r}\dot\theta$. The angle $\alpha$ between $\mechanical{\vec v}$ and $\hat e_r$ satisfies $\tan\alpha=\mechanical{v}_\theta/\mechanical{v}_r=1/a$, which is constant.

> [!note] Source terminology
> The source calls the logarithmic spiral equidistant. The constant angle makes it an **equiangular** spiral. The radial separation between its turns changes in proportion to the radius.
