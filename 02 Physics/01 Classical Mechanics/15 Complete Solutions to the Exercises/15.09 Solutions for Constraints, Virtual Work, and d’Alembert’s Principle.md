> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions identify constraints and use virtual work and d’Alembert’s principle.

See [[09.14 Constraints and Virtual Work Exercises]] for the exercise list.

## Exercise 1. Degrees of freedom of a double pendulum

*Count the degrees of freedom in a plane and in space.*

In a plane, two angles are free, so $n=2$. In space, each joint needs two angles to specify the link direction, so $n=4$.

## Exercise 2. Lever

*Use lever arms $\mechanical{\ell}_1=\quantity{0.30}{m}$ and $\mechanical{\ell}_2=\quantity{1.20}{m}$ and a load $\mechanical{P}=\quantity{200}{N}$.*

Balance the torques. $\mechanical{F}\mechanical{\ell}_2=\mechanical{P}\mechanical{\ell}_1$, so $\mechanical{F}=200\cdot0.30/1.20=\quantity{50}{N}$.

## Exercise 3. Rolling sphere

*Is the constraint holonomic or nonholonomic?*

For a disk restricted to rolling on a straight line, $\mechanical{\dot x}=\mechanical{R}\dot\theta$ integrates to a position constraint, so it is holonomic. For a sphere rolling on a plane, the constraints couple the horizontal components of $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ to the displacement of the center in a nonintegrable way. They are nonholonomic.

## Exercise 4. Disk rolling along a straight line

*Use the rolling relation $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{R}\theta$.*

The constraint $\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{R}\theta=0$ is holonomic. There is one degree of freedom, and either $\theta$ or $\mechanical{x}$ can serve as its coordinate.

## Exercise 5. Atwood machine constraint

*Two masses are connected by a rope over a pulley.*

With downward coordinates, the fixed rope length gives $\mechanical{y}_1+\mechanical{y}_2=\mathrm{const.}$. One coordinate, such as $\mechanical{y}_1$, is free.

## Exercise 6. Generalized force of a double pendulum

*Find the generalized force for $\theta_1$.*

For the two angles measured from the downward vertical, the gravitational generalized forces are $Q_{\theta_1}=-\partial\energy{U}/\partial\theta_1=-(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}_1\sin\theta_1$ and $Q_{\theta_2}=-\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}_2\sin\theta_2$.

> [!note] Source formula
> The source omits the gravitational acceleration $\mechanical{g}$ from $Q_{\theta_1}$. It is required by the gravitational potential.

## Exercise 7. Particle on a helicoid

*Use $\mechanical{z}=c\phi$ in cylindrical coordinates.*

The two free coordinates are $(\mechanical{\rho},\phi)$. The constraint gives $\mechanical{z}$ as a function of $\phi$.

## Exercise 8. Static equilibrium

*Show $\partial\energy{U}/\partial q_k=0$.*

In static equilibrium, d'Alembert's principle for conservative forces gives $\delta\energy{W}=-\delta\energy{U}=-\sum_k(\partial\energy{U}/\partial q_k)\delta q_k=0$. The variations of the independent coordinates are arbitrary, so every coefficient is zero.

## Exercise 9. Ball on a hemisphere

*Find when a ball sliding from rest at the top loses contact.*

Energy conservation gives $\mechanical{v}^2=2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{R}(1-\cos\theta)$. The radial equation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\theta-\mechanical{N}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R}$. At detachment $\mechanical{N}=0$, so $\cos\theta=2/3$ and $\theta\simeq48.2^\circ$.

## Exercise 10. Pendulum tension

*Find the rope tension as a function of angle $\theta$ from the downward vertical.*

The radial equation is $\mechanical{T}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\theta=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}\dot\theta^2$, so $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{\ell}+\mechanical{g}\cos\theta)$. The tension is greatest at the lowest point.

## Exercise 11. Constraint force on a quadratic surface

*A particle moves on $\mechanical{z}=a\mechanical{x}^2+b\mechanical{y}^2$. Find the normal force at $(0,0)$.*

The upward normal at the origin is $\hat k$. In the direction of motion the normal curvature is $\mechanical{\kappa}=2(a\mechanical{v}_x^2+b\mechanical{v}_y^2)/\mechanical{v}^2$. The vertical equation gives $\mechanical{N}=\mechanical{m}[\mechanical{g}+2(a\mechanical{v}_x^2+b\mechanical{v}_y^2)]$. For $\lvert\mechanical{v}_x\rvert=\lvert\mechanical{v}_y\rvert$, this reduces to $\mechanical{N}=\mechanical{m}[\mechanical{g}+(a+b)\mechanical{v}^2]$.
