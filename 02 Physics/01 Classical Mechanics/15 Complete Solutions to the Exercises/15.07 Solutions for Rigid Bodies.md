> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions use moments of inertia, physical pendulums, rolling motion, and gyroscope dynamics.

See [[07.15 Rigid-Body Exercises]] for the exercise list.

## Exercise 1. Uniform disk

*Find the axial moment of inertia for $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{2}{kg}$ and $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{0.30}{m}$.*

The moment of inertia is $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2/2=\quantity{0.090}{kg\,m^2}$.

## Exercise 2. Parallel-axis theorem

*Move the axis of the same disk by distance $\mechanical{d}=\quantity{0.20}{m}$ without changing its direction.*

The new moment is $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{I}_G+\mechanical{M}\mechanical{d}^2=0.090+2\cdot0.04=\quantity{0.170}{kg\,m^2}$.

## Exercise 3. Rod as a physical pendulum

*A uniform rod of mass $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{1}{kg}$ and length $\mechanical{L}=\quantity{1}{m}$ pivots at one end.*

Its moment is $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{L}^2/3=(1/3)\,\unit{kg\,m^2}$. Its center of mass lies at $\mechanical{d}=\mechanical{L}/2=\quantity{0.5}{m}$ from the pivot. The small-oscillation period is $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{I}/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d})}\simeq\quantity{1.638}{s}$.

## Exercise 4. Gyroscope precession

*Use $\mechanical{I}_3=\quantity{5\times10^{-3}}{kg\,m^2}$, spin speed $\mechanical{\omega}_s=\quantity{200}{rad\,s^{-1}}$, mass $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{0.5}{kg}$, and lever arm $\mechanical{d}=\quantity{0.10}{m}$.*

The slow precession rate is $\mechanical{\Omega}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}/(\mechanical{I}_3\mechanical{\omega}_s)\simeq\quantity{0.49}{rad\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 5. Solid sphere

*Show $\mechanical{I}=(2/5)\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2$.*

In spherical coordinates,

$$
\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{\rho}\int_0^{\mechanical{R}}\mechanical{r}^4\,d\mechanical{r}\int\sin^3\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi
=\frac{8\pi\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{R}^5}{15}.
$$

Use $\mechanical{M}=(4\pi/3)\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{R}^3$ to get the result.

## Exercise 6. Rolling sphere

*A solid sphere rolls without slipping through a height drop $\mechanical{h}$.*

Energy conservation with $\mechanical{I}=2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2/5$ and $\mechanical{\omega}=\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{R}$ gives $\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}=(7/10)\mechanical{M}\mechanical{v}^2$. Thus $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{10\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}/7}$.

## Exercise 7. Impact at the end of a rod

*A particle of mass $\mechanical{m}$ and initial speed $\mechanical{v}_0$ strikes the end of an initially stationary uniform rod of mass $\mechanical{M}$ and length $\mechanical{L}$ perpendicularly in an elastic collision.*

Conservation of linear momentum, angular momentum, and kinetic energy gives the rod's center-of-mass speed $\mechanical{V}$, its angular speed $\mechanical{\omega}$, and the particle's outgoing velocity $\mechanical{v}'$.

$$
\mechanical{V}=\frac{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}_0}{\mechanical{M}+4\mechanical{m}},\qquad
\mechanical{\omega}=\frac{12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}_0}{\mechanical{L}(\mechanical{M}+4\mechanical{m})},\qquad
\mechanical{v}'=\frac{(4\mechanical{m}-\mechanical{M})\mechanical{v}_0}{\mechanical{M}+4\mechanical{m}}.
$$

For $\mechanical{m}=\mechanical{M}$, these become $2\mechanical{v}_0/5$, $12\mechanical{v}_0/(5\mechanical{L})$, and $3\mechanical{v}_0/5$.

## Exercise 8. Braking torque

*A disk has $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{2}{kg}$, $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{0.20}{m}$, and $\mechanical{\omega}_0=\quantity{10}{rad\,s^{-1}}$. It stops in $\Delta t=\quantity{5}{s}$.*

The moment of inertia is $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2/2=\quantity{0.04}{kg\,m^2}$. The mean braking torque has magnitude $\mechanical{\tau}=\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\omega}_0/\Delta t=\quantity{0.080}{N\,m}$.

## Exercise 9. Intermediate-axis instability

*Show that rotation about the intermediate principal axis is unstable.*

Linearize Euler's equations about $\mechanical{\vec\omega}=\mechanical{\omega}_2\hat e_2$. Then $\mechanical{\ddot\omega}_1=k\mechanical{\omega}_1$, with

$$
k=\frac{(\mechanical{I}_2-\mechanical{I}_3)(\mechanical{I}_1-\mechanical{I}_2)}{\mechanical{I}_1\mechanical{I}_3}\mechanical{\omega}_2^2>0.
$$

Thus a small perturbation can grow exponentially.

> [!note] Source sign
> The second difference in the source numerator is $\mechanical{I}_2-\mechanical{I}_1$. Linearizing Euler's equations gives the reversed difference as above, which makes $k$ positive for the intermediate axis.

## Exercise 10. Lagrangian of a symmetric top

*Use Euler angles.*

$$
\energy{L}=\frac{\mechanical{I}_1}{2}(\dot\phi^2\sin^2\theta+\dot\theta^2)
+\frac{\mechanical{I}_3}{2}(\dot\psi+\dot\phi\cos\theta)^2
-\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}\cos\theta.
$$

The angles $\phi$ and $\psi$ are cyclic, so their conjugate momenta $p_\phi$ and $p_\psi$ are constant.

## Exercise 11. Sphere, disk, and ring rolling down an incline

*Rank the speeds after the same vertical drop from rest.*

Use $\mechanical{v}=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}/(1+\beta)}$ with $\beta=\mechanical{I}/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2)$. The values of $\beta$ are $2/5$ for the solid sphere, $1/2$ for the disk, and $1$ for the thin ring. The sphere is fastest, followed by the disk and then the ring.

## Exercise 12. Yo-yo

*Find the downward acceleration of a disk unwinding from a string wound around its rim.*

For $\beta=1/2$, $\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{g}/(1+\beta)=2\mechanical{g}/3\simeq\quantity{6.54}{m\,s^{-2}}$. The string tension is $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{g}/3$.

## Exercise 13. Optimal physical pendulum

*Find the pivot offset that minimizes the period of a uniform rod.*

Minimize $f(\mechanical{d})=\mechanical{L}^2/(12\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d})+\mechanical{d}/\mechanical{g}$. At $f'(\mechanical{d})=0$, $\mechanical{d}^{*2}=\mechanical{L}^2/12$, so $\mechanical{d}^*=\mechanical{L}/\sqrt{12}$.

## Exercise 14. Torque to accelerate a door

*A uniform door of mass $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{30}{kg}$ and width $\mechanical{w}=\quantity{0.9}{m}$ reaches $\mechanical{\omega}=\quantity{1}{rad\,s^{-1}}$ from rest in $t=\quantity{0.5}{s}$.*

The hinge-axis inertia is $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{M}\mechanical{w}^2/3=\quantity{8.1}{kg\,m^2}$. The average torque is $\mechanical{\tau}=\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\omega}/t=\quantity{16.2}{N\,m}$.

## Exercise 15. Chandler period

*Derive $\mechanical{\Omega}=(\mechanical{I}_3-\mechanical{I}_1)\mechanical{\omega}_3/\mechanical{I}_1$.*

For a symmetric body with $\mechanical{I}_1=\mechanical{I}_2$ and no external torque, $\mechanical{\omega}_3$ is constant. The transverse components of $\mechanical{\vec\omega}$ rotate in the body-fixed frame. Their angular rate is $\mechanical{\Omega}=(\mechanical{I}_3-\mechanical{I}_1)\mechanical{\omega}_3/\mechanical{I}_1$. For the rigid-Earth approximation, $\mechanical{\Omega}\simeq\mechanical{\omega}_T/305$, giving a period of about $305$ days. The source compares this with the observed Chandler period of about $433$ days, which is affected by Earth's elasticity.

| $\mechanical{R},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{d},\mechanical{d}^*,\mechanical{L},\mechanical{h},\mechanical{w}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lengths | Radii, pivot offsets, rod length, vertical drop, and door width. |

| $\mechanical{g},\mechanical{a}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Accelerations | The local gravitational magnitude and yo-yo acceleration. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Density | The uniform density of the solid sphere. |
| $t,\Delta t,\signal{T}$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time and period | The elapsed time, braking time, and pendulum period. |

| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The kinetic energy minus the gravitational potential of the top. |

| $k$ | $\unit{s^{-2}}$ | Instability coefficient | The coefficient in the linearized intermediate-axis equation. |
| $\beta$ | $1$ | Inertia factor | The ratio of moment of inertia to mass times squared radius. |
