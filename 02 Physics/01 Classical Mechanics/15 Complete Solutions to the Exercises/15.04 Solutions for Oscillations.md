> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions cover harmonic motion, pendulums, damping, resonance, and coupled oscillators.

## Exercise 1. Harmonic oscillator

*Find angular frequency, period, and frequency for $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{0.5}{kg}$ and $\mechanical{k}=\quantity{200}{N\,m^{-1}}$.*

The angular frequency is $\signal{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}=\sqrt{400}=\quantity{20}{rad\,s^{-1}}$. The period is $\signal{T}=2\pi/\signal{\omega}=\quantity{0.314}{s}$ and the frequency is $\signal{f}=1/\signal{T}\simeq\quantity{3.18}{Hz}$.

## Exercise 2. Simple pendulum

*Find the small-amplitude period for $\mechanical{\ell}=\quantity{1}{m}$.*

The period is $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{\ell}/\mechanical{g}}=2\pi/\sqrt{9.81}\simeq\quantity{2.006}{s}$.

## Exercise 3. Total energy

*Use amplitude $\mechanical{A}=\quantity{0.10}{m}$ and spring stiffness $\mechanical{k}=\quantity{50}{N\,m^{-1}}$.*

The energy is $\energy{E}=\tfrac12\mechanical{k}\mechanical{A}^2=0.5\cdot50\cdot0.01=\quantity{0.25}{J}$.

## Exercise 4. Damping to ten percent

*For $\signal{\omega}_0=\quantity{10}{rad\,s^{-1}}$ and $\signal{\gamma}=\quantity{1}{s^{-1}}$, find the time for the amplitude to fall to ten percent.*

The amplitude ratio is $e^{-\signal{\gamma}t}=0.10$. Thus $t=-\ln(0.10)/\signal{\gamma}=\quantity{2.303}{s}$.

## Exercise 5. Anharmonic correction to the pendulum

*Show $\signal{T}(\theta_0)\simeq\signal{T}_0(1+\theta_0^2/16)$.*

Energy conservation gives $\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\dot\theta^2=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}(\cos\theta-\cos\theta_0)$. With $\sin(\theta/2)=\sin(\theta_0/2)\sin\phi$, the time integral becomes a complete elliptic integral of the first kind with modulus $k=\sin(\theta_0/2)\simeq\theta_0/2$. Expanding to second order gives $\signal{T}\simeq\signal{T}_0(1+\theta_0^2/16)$.

## Exercise 6. Parallel springs

*Use $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{1}{kg}$, $\mechanical{k}_1=\quantity{100}{N\,m^{-1}}$, and $\mechanical{k}_2=\quantity{200}{N\,m^{-1}}$. Find the period.*

The effective stiffness is $\mechanical{k}=\mechanical{k}_1+\mechanical{k}_2=\quantity{300}{N\,m^{-1}}$. Thus $\signal{T}=2\pi\sqrt{\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{k}}\simeq\quantity{0.363}{s}$.

## Exercise 7. Springs in series

*Put the same springs in series and use the same mass.*

Since $1/\mechanical{k}=1/\mechanical{k}_1+1/\mechanical{k}_2$, the effective stiffness is $\mechanical{k}=\mechanical{k}_1\mechanical{k}_2/(\mechanical{k}_1+\mechanical{k}_2)\simeq\quantity{66.7}{N\,m^{-1}}$. Then $\signal{T}=2\pi/\sqrt{66.7}\simeq\quantity{0.770}{s}$.

## Exercise 8. Resonance

*For $\signal{\omega}_0=\quantity{5}{rad\,s^{-1}}$, $\signal{\gamma}=\quantity{0.1}{s^{-1}}$, and $\mechanical{F}_0/\mechanical{m}=\quantity{1}{m\,s^{-2}}$, find the resonance amplitude.*

For $\signal{\gamma}\ll\signal{\omega}_0$, $\mechanical{A}_{\max}\simeq\mechanical{F}_0/(2\mechanical{m}\signal{\gamma}\signal{\omega}_0)=1/(2\cdot0.1\cdot5)=\quantity{1}{m}$.

## Exercise 9. Logarithmic decrement

*Express the logarithmic decrement $\delta=\signal{\gamma}\signal{T}_d$ in terms of $Q$.*

Use $\signal{T}_d=2\pi/\sqrt{\signal{\omega}_0^2-\signal{\gamma}^2}$ and $Q=\signal{\omega}_0/(2\signal{\gamma})$. This gives $\delta=\pi/\sqrt{Q^2-1/4}\simeq\pi/Q$ for $Q\gg1$.

## Exercise 10. Parametric resonance

*Explain exponential growth for a pendulum with length $\mechanical{\ell}(t)=\mechanical{\ell}_0[1+\varepsilon\cos(2\signal{\omega}_0t)]$.*

The source models the modulation with the Mathieu equation $\ddot\theta+\signal{\omega}_0^2[1-\varepsilon\cos(2\signal{\omega}_0t)]\theta\simeq0$. Modulation at twice the natural frequency can transfer energy to the oscillation at the appropriate phase and produce exponential growth in instability bands.

> [!note] Source model
> The written equation modulates only the restoring coefficient. The small-angle equation for a bob with a prescribed varying length also has the term $2\mechanical{\dot\ell}\dot\theta/\mechanical{\ell}$.

## Exercise 11. Quality factor from amplitude decay

*The amplitude halves in $N=50$ oscillations.*

Then $e^{-\signal{\gamma}N\signal{T}}=1/2$, so $\signal{\gamma}N\signal{T}=\ln2$. With the weak-damping approximation $\signal{T}\simeq2\pi/\signal{\omega}_0$, the quality factor is $Q=\signal{\omega}_0/(2\signal{\gamma})\simeq\pi N/\ln2\simeq227$.

## Exercise 12. Pendulum at thirty degrees

*Estimate the period correction at $\theta_0=30^\circ$.*

Use $\theta_0=\pi/6\simeq\quantity{0.524}{rad}$, so $\signal{T}/\signal{T}_0\simeq1+\theta_0^2/16\simeq1.017$. For $\mechanical{\ell}=\quantity{0.5}{m}$, $\signal{T}_0\simeq\quantity{1.42}{s}$ and $\signal{T}\simeq\quantity{1.44}{s}$.

## Exercise 13. Mean power at resonance

*Use $\mechanical{F}_0=\quantity{5}{N}$, $\signal{\omega}_0=\quantity{10}{rad\,s^{-1}}$, $\signal{\gamma}=\quantity{0.5}{s^{-1}}$, and $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{1}{kg}$.*

At drive frequency $\signal{\omega}_0$, the displacement phase lag is $\delta=\pi/2$. The mean power is $\langle\energy{P}\rangle=\mechanical{F}_0^2/(4\mechanical{m}\signal{\gamma})=25/2=\quantity{12.5}{W}$.

## Exercise 14. Spring stiffness from frequency

*Use $\signal{f}=\quantity{4}{Hz}$ and $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{0.1}{kg}$.*

The angular frequency is $\signal{\omega}=2\pi\signal{f}=8\pi\,\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$. Then $\mechanical{k}=\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2=0.1\cdot64\pi^2\simeq\quantity{63.2}{N\,m^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 15. Beats between modes

*Two identical masses are connected by three identical springs, with the outer springs attached to walls.*

The symmetric mode has $\signal{\omega}_1=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$, and the antisymmetric mode has $\signal{\omega}_2=\sqrt{3\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$. Displace only the first mass by $\mechanical{A}$ and release both masses from rest. Superposition gives

$$
\mechanical{x}_1(t)=\mechanical{A}\cos\left(\frac{\signal{\omega}_1+\signal{\omega}_2}{2}t\right)
\cos\left(\frac{\signal{\omega}_2-\signal{\omega}_1}{2}t\right).
$$

The slow signed modulation has angular frequency $(\signal{\omega}_2-\signal{\omega}_1)/2$, and the fast oscillation has frequency $(\signal{\omega}_1+\signal{\omega}_2)/2$. The changing amplitudes show the transfer of energy between the two masses.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The oscillator or pendulum mass. |
| $\mechanical{k},\mechanical{k}_1,\mechanical{k}_2$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring stiffness | The individual and equivalent stiffnesses. |
| $\mechanical{\ell},\mechanical{\ell}_0,\mechanical{A},\mechanical{A}_{\max},\mechanical{x}_1$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length and displacement | The pendulum lengths, amplitudes, and first mass displacement. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The field magnitude at the pendulum. |
| $\signal{\omega},\signal{\omega}_0,\signal{\omega}_1,\signal{\omega}_2$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The natural and modal frequencies. |
| $\signal{f}$ | $\unit{Hz}$ | Frequency | The number of oscillations per second. |
| $t,\signal{T},\signal{T}_0,\signal{T}_d$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time and periods | The time variable and the oscillation periods. |
| $\signal{\gamma}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Damping rate | The exponential amplitude decay rate. |
| $\theta,\theta_0,\phi$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The pendulum angle, its maximum, and the integration angle. |
| $\delta$ | — | Decrement or phase lag | The dimensionless amplitude decrement or the phase lag in radians. |
| $Q,\varepsilon,k$ | $1$ | Dimensionless parameters | The quality factor, relative length modulation, and elliptic modulus. |
| $\mechanical{F}_0$ | $\unit{N}$ | Drive force amplitude | The magnitude of the sinusoidal force amplitude. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The total energy of the undamped oscillator. |
| $\langle\energy{P}\rangle$ | $\unit{W}$ | Mean power | The average power delivered by the drive. |
