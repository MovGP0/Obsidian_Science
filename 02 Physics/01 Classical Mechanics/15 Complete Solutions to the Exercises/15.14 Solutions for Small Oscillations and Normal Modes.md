## Exercise 1. Two masses and three springs

*Find the frequencies and modes.*

With equal masses $\mechanical{m}$ and coupling stiffness $\mechanical{k}'$, the matrices are

$$
M=\mechanical{m}I,\qquad
V=\begin{pmatrix}\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{k}'&-\mechanical{k}'\\-\mechanical{k}'&\mechanical{k}+\mechanical{k}'\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The eigenvalues of $V$ are $\mechanical{k}$ and $\mechanical{k}+2\mechanical{k}'$. For three equal springs, $\mechanical{k}'=\mechanical{k}$, giving $\signal{\omega}_1=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$ for the symmetric mode $(1,1)$ and $\signal{\omega}_2=\sqrt{3\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$ for the antisymmetric mode $(1,-1)$.

## Exercise 2. Periodic chain with four masses

The frequencies are $\signal{\omega}_j=2\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}\lvert\sin(\pi j/4)\rvert$ for $j=0,1,2,3$. In this order they are $0$, $\sqrt{2\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$, $2\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$, and $\sqrt{2\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$.

## Exercise 3. Matrices of the equal double pendulum

For equal masses and lengths, expand to quadratic order in the small angles $\theta_i$. This gives

$$
M=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad V=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

## Exercise 4. Double-pendulum frequencies

The equation $\det(V-\signal{\omega}^2M)=0$ reduces to $2-4x+x^2=0$ with $x=\mechanical{\ell}\signal{\omega}^2/\mechanical{g}$. Its roots are $2\pm\sqrt2$, so $\signal{\omega}_\pm^2=(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})(2\pm\sqrt2)$.

## Exercise 5. Sound speed

At small positive wavenumber $\signal{q}$, the dispersion relation is $\signal{\omega}(\signal{q})\simeq\signal{q}\mechanical{a}\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$. Its slope gives the group speed $\mechanical{c}_s=\mechanical{a}\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$.

## Exercise 6. Nonlinear pendulum at $\pi/4$

Use $\signal{T}(\theta_0)\simeq\signal{T}_0(1+\theta_0^2/16)$. At $\theta_0=\pi/4$, $\signal{T}\simeq1.0386\signal{T}_0$.

## Exercise 7. Pendulum phase space

The Hamiltonian is $\energy{H}=p_\theta^2/(2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2)-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\theta$. At $p_\theta=0$, $\theta=0$ is stable and $\theta=\pi$ is unstable. Between the minimum energy and $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}$, trajectories are closed oscillations. At $\energy{E}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}$ the phase portrait has a separatrix. Above it the pendulum rotates continuously.

## Exercise 8. Logistic map

The nonzero fixed point is $x^*=1-1/r$. The derivative of $rx(1-x)$ there is $2-r$, so this fixed point is stable for $\lvert2-r\rvert<1$, or $1<r<3$. It first doubles its period at $r_1=3$.

## Exercise 9. Two-dimensional tori

On a regular compact connected invariant set, an integrable system with $n$ degrees of freedom has $n$ constant actions $J_k$. For $n=2$, the two angle coordinates give $\mathbb T^2=S^1\times S^1$ with uniform evolution $\dot\theta_k=\signal{\omega}_k$.

## Exercise 10. Three masses with two springs

The stiffness characteristic equation is $\lambda(\lambda-\mechanical{k})(\lambda-3\mechanical{k})=0$. The translation mode $(1,1,1)$ has $\signal{\omega}_0=0$. The mode $(1,0,-1)$ has $\signal{\omega}_1=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$, and $(1,-2,1)$ has $\signal{\omega}_2=\sqrt{3\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$.

## Exercise 11. Chain with alternating masses

The dispersion relation is

$$
\signal{\omega}^2=\frac{\mechanical{k}}{\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}}
\left(\mechanical{M}+\mechanical{m}\pm
\sqrt{(\mechanical{M}+\mechanical{m})^2-4\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}\sin^2(\signal{q}\mechanical{a})}\right).
$$

The minus sign gives the acoustic branch, with $\signal{\omega}\to0$ as $\signal{q}\to0$. The plus sign gives the optical branch, whose frequency remains positive. For unequal masses, the branches have a frequency gap at the boundary of the first Brillouin zone.

## Exercise 12. Lyapunov exponent of the double pendulum

The source estimates $\lambda\sim\sqrt{\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell}}\sim\quantity{3}{s^{-1}}$ in a chaotic regime with $\mechanical{\ell}=\quantity{1}{m}$. This gives a Lyapunov time of about $\quantity{0.3}{s}$, shorter than the small-amplitude period. The estimate depends on the orbit and indicates limited predictability.

## Exercise 13. Feigenbaum bifurcations

The first four period-doubling values are $r_1=3$, $r_2\simeq3.4495$, $r_3\simeq3.5441$, and $r_4\simeq3.5644$. The ratios of consecutive spacings approach the Feigenbaum constant $\delta\simeq4.669$ as more bifurcations are included.
