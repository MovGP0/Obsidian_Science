## Exercise 1. Center of mass of three points

*Masses $\mechanical{m}_1=1$, $\mechanical{m}_2=2$, and $\mechanical{m}_3=3$ in kilograms are at $(0,0)$, $(2,0)$, and $(0,3)$ in meters.*

The total mass is $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{6}{kg}$. The weighted coordinates are $\mechanical{X}=(1\cdot0+2\cdot2+3\cdot0)/6=2/3$ and $\mechanical{Y}=(1\cdot0+2\cdot0+3\cdot3)/6=3/2$. Thus $\mechanical{\vec R}=(2/3,3/2)\,\unit{m}$.

## Exercise 2. One-dimensional elastic collision

*Mass $\mechanical{m}_1=\quantity{2}{kg}$ moves at $\mechanical{v}_1=\quantity{5}{m\,s^{-1}}$ toward stationary $\mechanical{m}_2=\quantity{3}{kg}$. Find the outgoing velocities.*

The elastic-collision formulas give $\mechanical{v}'_1=(\mechanical{m}_1-\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{v}_1/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)=\quantity{-1}{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $\mechanical{v}'_2=2\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{v}_1/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)=\quantity{4}{m\,s^{-1}}$. The momentum is $\quantity{10}{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ and the kinetic energy is $\quantity{25}{J}$ both before and after.

## Exercise 3. Perfectly inelastic collision

*Repeat the previous collision with the two masses sticking together.*

The common velocity is $\mechanical{v}'=\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{v}_1/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)=\quantity{2}{m\,s^{-1}}$. The kinetic energy loss is $\quantity{25}{J}-\quantity{10}{J}=\quantity{15}{J}$.

## Exercise 4. Rocket

*The exhaust speed is $\mechanical{u}=\quantity{3}{km\,s^{-1}}$, and the initial-to-final mass ratio is $5$.*

The rocket equation gives $\Delta\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{u}\ln(\mechanical{m}_0/\mechanical{m}_f)=3\ln5\simeq\quantity{4.83}{km\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 5. Perpendicular velocities for equal masses

*Two equal masses collide elastically with the target initially at rest. Show that nonzero outgoing velocities are perpendicular.*

Momentum conservation gives $\mechanical{\vec v}_1=\mechanical{\vec v}'_1+\mechanical{\vec v}'_2$. Energy conservation gives $\mechanical{v}_1^2=(\mechanical{v}'_1)^2+(\mechanical{v}'_2)^2$. Squaring the vector equation and comparing yields $\mechanical{\vec v}'_1\cdot\mechanical{\vec v}'_2=0$.

## Exercise 6. Ballistic pendulum

*The projectile has mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{0.01}{kg}$, the pendulum has mass $\mechanical{M}=\quantity{1}{kg}$, and their combined rise is $\mechanical{h}=\quantity{0.05}{m}$.*

The speed just after impact is $\mechanical{v}'=\sqrt{2\mechanical{g}\mechanical{h}}\simeq\quantity{0.990}{m\,s^{-1}}$. During impact, $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}=(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})\mechanical{v}'$. Thus $\mechanical{v}=(\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{M})\mechanical{v}'/\mechanical{m}\simeq\quantity{100}{m\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 7. Angular momentum in circular motion

*A ball has mass $\mechanical{m}$, orbital radius $\mechanical{r}$, and angular speed $\mechanical{\omega}$.*

The angular momentum is $\mechanical{\vec L}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec v}$. Since the radius and velocity are perpendicular, its magnitude is $\mechanical{L}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\omega}$. Its direction is perpendicular to the orbital plane.

## Exercise 8. Coupling two disks

*The first disk has inertia $\mechanical{I}_1$ and angular speed $\mechanical{\omega}_1$. The second has inertia $\mechanical{I}_2$ and is at rest. Find their common speed after engagement.*

Angular momentum conservation gives $\mechanical{I}_1\mechanical{\omega}_1=(\mechanical{I}_1+\mechanical{I}_2)\mechanical{\omega}'$. Thus $\mechanical{\omega}'=\mechanical{I}_1\mechanical{\omega}_1/(\mechanical{I}_1+\mechanical{I}_2)$. Some rotational kinetic energy dissipates in this inelastic process.

## Exercise 9. Planar motion under a central force

*Show that the motion is planar.*

A central force conserves $\mechanical{\vec L}$. By the scalar triple product, $\mechanical{\vec r}\cdot\mechanical{\vec L}=\mechanical{\vec r}\cdot(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}\times\mechanical{\vec v})=0$. If $\mechanical{\vec L}\ne0$, the radius vector always lies in the fixed plane normal to it. For zero angular momentum, the motion is radial.

## Exercise 10. Falling chain

*A chain of length $\mechanical{L}$ and linear mass density $\mechanical{\lambda}$ starts from rest with overhang $\mechanical{x}_0$. Find its speed when the end leaves the table.*

The hanging section of length $\mechanical{x}$ has its center of mass $\mechanical{x}/2$ below the table, so its potential energy is $-\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{x}^2/2$. The kinetic energy of the chain is $\tfrac12\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{L}\mechanical{\dot x}^{\,2}$. Conservation of energy gives $\mechanical{\dot x}^{\,2}=\mechanical{g}(\mechanical{x}^2-\mechanical{x}_0^2)/\mechanical{L}$. At $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{L}$, the speed is $\sqrt{\mechanical{g}(\mechanical{L}^2-\mechanical{x}_0^2)/\mechanical{L}}$.

## Exercise 11. Bouncing ball

*For coefficient of restitution $e=0.8$, find how many bounces reduce the height below one percent of its initial value.*

The height ratio after $n$ bounces is $\mechanical{h}_n/\mechanical{h}_0=e^{2n}$. For this ratio to be below $0.01$, require $n>\ln(0.01)/(2\ln0.8)\simeq10.32$. The minimum is $11$ bounces.

## Exercise 12. Transferred energy

*For a one-dimensional elastic collision with mass ratio $A=12$, find the fraction of incident energy transferred to a stationary target.*

For $A=\mechanical{m}_2/\mechanical{m}_1$, the fraction is $\energy{K}'_2/\energy{K}_1=4\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{m}_2/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)^2=4A/(A+1)^2$. At $A=12$ it equals $48/169\simeq28.4\%$.

## Exercise 13. One-dimensional elastic collision

*Use $\mechanical{m}_1=\quantity{1}{kg}$, $\mechanical{v}_0=\quantity{5}{m\,s^{-1}}$, and a stationary mass $\mechanical{m}_2=\quantity{2}{kg}$.*

The incident mass rebounds with $\mechanical{v}'_1=(1-2)\cdot5/3=(-5/3)\,\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$. The target leaves with $\mechanical{v}'_2=2\cdot1\cdot5/3=(10/3)\,\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 14. Two-stage rocket

*Stage 1 has exhaust speed $\mechanical{u}_1=\quantity{3}{km\,s^{-1}}$ and mass ratio $4$. Stage 2 has $\mechanical{u}_2=\quantity{4}{km\,s^{-1}}$ and mass ratio $5$. Find the total change in speed.*

Add the changes from the two stages. $\Delta\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{u}_1\ln4+\mechanical{u}_2\ln5=3\cdot1.386+4\cdot1.609\simeq\quantity{10.6}{km\,s^{-1}}$.

## Exercise 15. König's theorem

*Prove the decomposition of kinetic energy into center-of-mass and relative motion.*

Write each velocity as $\mechanical{\vec v}_i=\mechanical{\vec V}+\mechanical{\vec v}'_i$, where $\mechanical{\vec V}$ is the center-of-mass velocity. Then

$$
\energy{K}
=\frac12\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\lvert\mechanical{\vec V}+\mechanical{\vec v}'_i\rvert^2
=\tfrac12\mechanical{M}\mechanical{V}^2
+\mechanical{\vec V}\cdot\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\vec v}'_i
+\frac12\sum_i\mechanical{m}_i(\mechanical{v}'_i)^2.
$$

The middle term vanishes by the definition of center-of-mass velocity. Thus $\energy{K}=\tfrac12\mechanical{M}\mechanical{V}^2+\energy{K}_{\mathrm{rel}}$.
