> [!TLDR]
> Worked solutions apply Newton's laws to friction, tension, rotating systems, and other forces.

## Exercise 1. Inclined plane with friction

*A block with $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{5}{kg}$ slides down a plane at $\alpha=30^\circ$, with kinetic friction coefficient $\mechanical{\mu}_d=0.2$. Find its acceleration along the plane.*

The downslope weight component is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\sin\alpha$. The normal force is $\mechanical{N}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\alpha$. Kinetic friction opposes sliding with magnitude $\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{N}$. Thus $\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{g}(\sin\alpha-\mechanical{\mu}_d\cos\alpha)=9.81(0.5-0.173)\simeq\quantity{3.20}{m\,s^{-2}}$.

## Exercise 2. Atwood machine

*For $\mechanical{m}_1=\quantity{2}{kg}$ and $\mechanical{m}_2=\quantity{3}{kg}$, find the acceleration and rope tension.*

The force balances are $\mechanical{T}-\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{g}=\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{a}$ and $\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}-\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{a}$. Adding gives $\mechanical{a}(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)=(\mechanical{m}_2-\mechanical{m}_1)\mechanical{g}$. Then $\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{g}/5=\quantity{1.96}{m\,s^{-2}}$ and $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{m}_1(\mechanical{g}+\mechanical{a})\simeq\quantity{23.5}{N}$.

## Exercise 3. Centripetal force on a bend

*A car travels around a level bend of radius $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{50}{m}$ at $\mechanical{v}=\quantity{15}{m\,s^{-1}}$. Find the minimum static friction coefficient.*

Friction supplies the required centripetal force. Thus $\mechanical{\mu}_s\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\ge\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{R}$, so $\mechanical{\mu}_s\ge225/(9.81\cdot50)\simeq0.459$.

## Exercise 4. Horizontal pull

*A mass $\mechanical{m}=\quantity{10}{kg}$ is pulled horizontally by $\mechanical{F}=\quantity{50}{N}$ with $\mechanical{\mu}_d=0.3$. Find its acceleration while sliding.*

The net force is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{F}-\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}=50-29.4=\quantity{20.6}{N}$. Then $\mechanical{a}=\quantity{2.06}{m\,s^{-2}}$.

## Exercise 5. Rotating platform

*A block is at radius $\mechanical{r}=\quantity{0.5}{m}$ on a platform rotating at $\mechanical{\omega}=\quantity{2}{rad\,s^{-1}}$. If $\mechanical{\mu}_s=0.4$, does it remain at rest relative to the platform?*

The required centripetal force is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\omega}^2\mechanical{r}$. The maximum static friction is $\mechanical{\mu}_s\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}$. The corresponding accelerations are $\mechanical{\omega}^2\mechanical{r}=\quantity{2}{m\,s^{-2}}$ and $\mechanical{\mu}_s\mechanical{g}=\quantity{3.92}{m\,s^{-2}}$. The block remains at rest relative to the platform.

## Exercise 6. Terminal velocity

*For vertical motion with linear drag $\mechanical{f}=-\mechanical{b}\mechanical{v}$, find the equation of motion and terminal velocity.*

Take upward as positive. The equation is $\mechanical{\dot v}=-\mechanical{g}-\mechanical{b}\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{m}$. At steady velocity, $\mechanical{\dot v}=0$, so $\mechanical{v}_{\mathrm{lim}}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{b}$. The negative sign indicates downward motion.

## Exercise 7. Two blocks in contact

*Two blocks $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ are in contact on a smooth horizontal surface. Apply a horizontal force $\mechanical{F}$ to the first block. Find the acceleration and contact force.*

The acceleration of both blocks is $\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{F}/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)$. The contact force on the second block is $\mechanical{F}_c=\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{F}/(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)$.

## Exercise 8. Accelerated suspended mass

*A mass $\mechanical{m}$ hangs from a rope and accelerates upward at $\mechanical{a}$. Find the tension.*

The force balance is $\mechanical{T}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{a}$. Thus $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{g}+\mechanical{a})$.

## Exercise 9. Coriolis deflection

*A projectile is launched vertically. Find its deflection due to Earth's rotation.*

Take $\hat z$ upward, $\hat x$ east, and $\hat y$ north. At latitude $\lambda$, $\mechanical{\vec\omega}=\mechanical{\omega}(\cos\lambda\,\hat y+\sin\lambda\,\hat z)$. The Coriolis force is $\mechanical{\vec F}_C=-2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec\omega}\times\mechanical{\vec v}$. To first order in $\mechanical{\omega}$, use the ballistic velocity $\mechanical{\dot z}=\mechanical{v}_0-\mechanical{g}t$ in the eastward acceleration $\mechanical{\ddot x}=-2\mechanical{\omega}\cos\lambda\,\mechanical{\dot z}$. Integrate from launch to $t_f=2\mechanical{v}_0/\mechanical{g}$.

$$
\mechanical{x}(t_f)=-\mechanical{\omega}\cos\lambda\left(\mechanical{v}_0t_f^2-\frac{\mechanical{g}t_f^3}{3}\right)
=-\frac{4\mechanical{\omega}\cos\lambda\,\mechanical{v}_0^3}{3\mechanical{g}^2}.
$$

The deflection is westward. Using the maximum height $\mechanical{h}=\mechanical{v}_0^2/(2\mechanical{g})$, its magnitude is

$$
\Delta\mechanical{x}=\frac83\mechanical{\omega}\mechanical{h}\sqrt{\frac{2\mechanical{h}}{\mechanical{g}}}\cos\lambda.
$$

## Exercise 10. Hanging chain

*A uniform chain of length $\mechanical{L}$ and linear density $\mechanical{\lambda}$ has an initial overhang $\mechanical{x}_0<\mechanical{L}$. Find the equation of motion.*

The hanging mass is $\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{x}$ and its weight is $\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{x}\mechanical{g}$. With no applied tension at the free end and a frictionless support, the weight accelerates the full mass $\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{L}$. Newton's law gives $\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{L}\mechanical{\ddot x}=\mechanical{\lambda}\mechanical{x}\mechanical{g}$, so $\mechanical{\ddot x}=(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{L})\mechanical{x}$. The growing exponential has rate $\sqrt{\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{L}}$.

## Exercise 11. Sphere in glycerin

*A sphere has radius $\mechanical{R}=\quantity{0.5}{mm}$ and density $\mechanical{\rho}_s=\quantity{7800}{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Glycerin has viscosity $\mechanical{\eta}=\quantity{1.5}{Pa\,s}$ and density $\mechanical{\rho}_f=\quantity{1260}{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Find the terminal speed including buoyancy.*

For downward motion the net force component is $\mechanical{F}=(\mechanical{\rho}_s-\mechanical{\rho}_f)\mechanical{V}\mechanical{g}-6\pi\mechanical{\eta}\mechanical{R}\mechanical{v}$, where $\mechanical{V}$ is the sphere volume. At terminal speed,

$$
\mechanical{v}_\infty=\frac{(\mechanical{\rho}_s-\mechanical{\rho}_f)\mechanical{V}\mechanical{g}}{6\pi\mechanical{\eta}\mechanical{R}}
=\frac{2\mechanical{R}^2(\mechanical{\rho}_s-\mechanical{\rho}_f)\mechanical{g}}{9\mechanical{\eta}}
\simeq\quantity{2.4\times10^{-3}}{m\,s^{-1}}.
$$

## Exercise 12. Critical angle

*Show that $\tan\alpha_c=\mechanical{\mu}_s$.*

At impending slip, the downslope weight $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\sin\alpha_c$ equals the maximum static friction $\mechanical{\mu}_s\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\alpha_c$. Divide by $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\cos\alpha_c$ to obtain the result.

## Exercise 13. Two blocks pulled with friction

*Blocks of masses $\mechanical{m}_1=\quantity{3}{kg}$ and $\mechanical{m}_2=\quantity{5}{kg}$ are connected by a rope. A horizontal force $\mechanical{F}=\quantity{30}{N}$ pulls the first block, with kinetic friction coefficient $\mechanical{\mu}_d=0.2$. Find their common acceleration and the rope tension.*

The total friction force has magnitude $\mechanical{\mu}_d(\mechanical{m}_1+\mechanical{m}_2)\mechanical{g}\simeq\quantity{15.7}{N}$. The common acceleration is $\mechanical{a}=(30-15.7)/8\simeq\quantity{1.79}{m\,s^{-2}}$. On the second block, $\mechanical{T}-\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}=\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{a}$. Thus $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{m}_2(\mechanical{a}+\mechanical{\mu}_d\mechanical{g})=\quantity{18.75}{N}$.

## Exercise 14. Vertical launch with viscous drag

*Find the maximum height.*

The equation is $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot v}=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}-\mechanical{b}\mechanical{v}$. Let $\mechanical{v}_\infty=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{b}$ be the positive terminal speed magnitude. Then $\mechanical{v}(t)=(\mechanical{v}_0+\mechanical{v}_\infty)e^{-\mechanical{b}t/\mechanical{m}}-\mechanical{v}_\infty$. The velocity becomes zero at $t_h=(\mechanical{m}/\mechanical{b})\ln(1+\mechanical{v}_0/\mechanical{v}_\infty)$. Integrating gives

$$
\mechanical{h}_{\max}=\int_0^{t_h}\mechanical{v}(t)\,dt=\frac{\mechanical{v}_\infty}{\mechanical{g}}\left[\mechanical{v}_0-\mechanical{v}_\infty\ln\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{v}_0}{\mechanical{v}_\infty}\right)\right].
$$

For positive drag this is below the no-drag height $\mechanical{v}_0^2/(2\mechanical{g})$.

## Exercise 15. Particle on a horizontal circle

*A radial spring acts on a particle constrained to a circle.*

The constraint fixes $\mechanical{r}=\mechanical{R}$. A central spring of zero natural length exerts $\mechanical{\vec F}=-\mechanical{k}\mechanical{R}\hat r$. The spring and the ideal constraint have no tangential component, so the particle moves at constant angular speed $\mechanical{\omega}=\mechanical{v}_0/\mechanical{R}$. The spring and constraint together supply $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\omega}^2\mechanical{R}$. The spring alone supplies it exactly when $\mechanical{\omega}=\sqrt{\mechanical{k}/\mechanical{m}}$.
