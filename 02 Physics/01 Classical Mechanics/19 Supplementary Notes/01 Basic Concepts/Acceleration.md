The **acceleration** $\vec{a}$ of a particle is the time derivative of its velocity: the rate at which the velocity is changing.

$$\vec{a} = (\ddot{x}, \ddot{y}, \ddot{z})$$

If the particle is stationary or is travelling at constant speed *and direction*, then there is no acceleration.

Since position, velocity and acceleration are vector quantities, a change in the direction of the velocity constitutes an acceleration.

> [!note]
> Particles undergoing <ins>circular motion</ins> are <ins>constantly accelerating</ins>, even if the speed is constant.
