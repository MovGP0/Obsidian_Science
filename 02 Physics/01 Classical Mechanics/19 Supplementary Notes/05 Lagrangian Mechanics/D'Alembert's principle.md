**D'Alembert's principle** (after *Jean-Baptiste le Rond d’Alembert*) states that a body is in dynamic equilibrium when you add a "fictitious" inertial force ($\vec F_{I}=-m\vec a$) to the applied forces. For a system of particles with virtual displacements $\delta\vec r_i$ that agree with the constraints:

$$\sum_i \left(\vec F_i^{(a)} - m_i \vec a_i\right)\cdot\delta\vec r_i = 0$$

Only the applied forces $\vec F_i^{(a)}$ occur, because ideal constraint forces do no virtual work.

The principle, which is a restatement of Newton's second law, converts a dynamics problem into a statics problem by introducing an inertial force ($F_{I}=-ma$) that opposes the body's acceleration ($F = ma$). This makes it easier to solve problems, especially those with constraints, by using methods from statics.
