---
title: "Hamiltonian Formulation Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Practice Hamilton's equations, Poisson brackets, conservation laws, and phase-space volume preservation.

1. **Basic.** Write the Hamiltonian of the simple pendulum and derive Hamilton's equations.
   **Result.** $\energy{H}=p_\theta^2/(2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2)-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\theta$, $\dot\theta=p_\theta/(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2)$, and $\dot p_\theta=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\sin\theta$.
2. **Basic.** Write Hamilton's equations for the harmonic oscillator and verify $\ddot q+\signal{\omega}^2q=0$.
3. **Basic.** Calculate $\{\mechanical{L}_x,\mechanical{L}_y\}$ using $\mechanical{L}_x=\mechanical{y}\mechanical{p}_z-\mechanical{z}\mechanical{p}_y$ and $\mechanical{L}_y=\mechanical{z}\mechanical{p}_x-\mechanical{x}\mechanical{p}_z$.
   **Result.** $\mechanical{L}_z$.
4. **Basic.** Verify $\{q,p^2\}=2p$.
5. **Intermediate.** For a particle in a central potential $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})$, calculate $\{\mechanical{L}^2,\energy{H}\}$ and show that $\mechanical{L}^2$ is conserved.
6. **Intermediate.** For a free particle in three dimensions, find all constants of motion that follow from the standard symmetries. Verify them with Poisson brackets and, for any quantity that depends explicitly on time, its explicit time derivative.
   **Source result.** Linear momentum $\mechanical{\vec p}$, angular momentum $\mechanical{\vec L}$, and energy.
7. **Intermediate.** Prove Liouville's theorem for the harmonic oscillator by showing that an ellipse in phase space retains its area.
8. **Intermediate.** Write the Hamiltonian for the double pendulum and list its cyclic coordinates.
   **Result.** There are no cyclic coordinates.
9. **Challenge.** Prove the Jacobi identity $\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\{g,\{h,f\}\}+\{h,\{f,g\}\}=0$ directly from the definition of the Poisson bracket.
10. **Challenge.** Prove Poisson's theorem. If $f$ and $g$ are constants of motion, then $\{f,g\}$ is also a constant of motion.
11. **Intermediate.** Find the Hamiltonian for a particle in an electromagnetic field with $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}^{\,2}-\electric{q}\electric{\Phi}+\electric{q}\mechanical{\vec v}\cdot\magnetic{\vec A}$.
    **Result.** $\mechanical{\vec p}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}+\electric{q}\magnetic{\vec A}$ and $\energy{H}=(\mechanical{\vec p}-\electric{q}\magnetic{\vec A})^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\electric{q}\electric{\Phi}$.
12. **Intermediate.** For the two-dimensional harmonic oscillator $\energy{H}=(\mechanical{p}_x^2+\mechanical{p}_y^2)/(2\mechanical{m})+\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2(\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{y}^2)$, list its independent constants of motion and check integrability.
    **Result.** $\energy{H}$, $\mechanical{L}_z$, and $\energy{A}_{xy}=\mechanical{p}_x\mechanical{p}_y/\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}^2\mechanical{x}\mechanical{y}$ are three independent first integrals on a generic set. Two, for example $\energy{H}$ and $\mechanical{L}_z$, suffice for integrability and are in involution. The third independent integral is a sign of **superintegrability**.
13. **Intermediate.** Prove that the flow of the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator is a rotation in the scaled phase plane $(q,p/(\mechanical{m}\signal{\omega}))$.
14. **Challenge.** Calculate $\{\energy{H},\energy{H}\}$ for an arbitrary Hamiltonian and verify that it is zero.

> [!note] Source result
> Exercise 6 lists the charges for space translations, rotations, and time translations. If Galilean boosts are included among the standard symmetries, they give the additional explicitly time-dependent charge $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}-\mechanical{\vec p}t$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{H},\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and potential energy | The Hamiltonian and the central potential. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the pendulum bob or free particle. |
| $\mechanical{\ell},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Length and position | The pendulum length, radial position, and Cartesian position. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The field magnitude in the pendulum problem. |
| $\theta$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angle | The pendulum angle from the downward vertical. |
| $p_\theta,\mechanical{L},\mechanical{\vec L},\mechanical{L}_x,\mechanical{L}_y,\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The pendulum's conjugate momentum and the angular momentum and its components. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p},\mechanical{p}_x,\mechanical{p}_y,\mechanical{p}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Linear momentum | The momentum vector and its Cartesian components. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The natural frequency of the harmonic oscillator. |
| $q,p$ | — | Canonical variables | The generalized coordinate and its conjugate momentum. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\electric{q}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Electric charge | The charge of the particle in the electromagnetic field. |
| $\electric{\Phi}$ | $\unit{V}$ | Electric potential | The scalar electromagnetic potential. |
| $\magnetic{\vec A}$ | $\unit{T\,m}$ | Magnetic vector potential | The vector potential in the charged-particle Lagrangian. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The velocity of the charged particle. |
| $\energy{A}_{xy}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Oscillator integral | An off-diagonal quadratic first integral of the isotropic oscillator. |
