---
title: "Poisson Brackets"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Poisson bracket gives the time evolution of a phase-space function and expresses the relations between canonical variables.

> [!definition] Definition 12.2 | Poisson Bracket
> For two phase-space functions $f(q,p,t)$ and $g(q,p,t)$, their **Poisson bracket** is
>
> $$
> \{f,g\}=\sum_k\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial q_k}\frac{\partial g}{\partial p_k}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial p_k}\frac{\partial g}{\partial q_k}\right).
> $$

## Basic properties

- Antisymmetry. $\{f,g\}=-\{g,f\}$.
- Linearity in both arguments.
- Leibniz rule. $\{f,gh\}=\{f,g\}h+g\{f,h\}$.
- Jacobi identity. $\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\{g,\{h,f\}\}+\{h,\{f,g\}\}=0$.

## Fundamental brackets

$$
\{q_k,q_l\}=0,\qquad \{p_k,p_l\}=0,\qquad \{q_k,p_l\}=\delta_{kl}.
$$

**Time evolution.** For each function $f(q,p,t)$,

$$
\frac{df}{dt}=\{f,\energy{H}\}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}.
$$

In particular, Hamilton's equations become $\dot q_k=\{q_k,\energy{H}\}$ and $\dot p_k=\{p_k,\energy{H}\}$.

**Constants of motion.** If $f$ has no explicit time dependence and $\{f,\energy{H}\}=0$, then $f$ is a constant of motion.

**Example. Angular momentum components.** The components are $\mechanical{L}_x=\mechanical{y}\mechanical{p}_z-\mechanical{z}\mechanical{p}_y$, $\mechanical{L}_y=\mechanical{z}\mechanical{p}_x-\mechanical{x}\mechanical{p}_z$, and $\mechanical{L}_z=\mechanical{x}\mechanical{p}_y-\mechanical{y}\mechanical{p}_x$. Their brackets are

$$
\{\mechanical{L}_x,\mechanical{L}_y\}=\mechanical{L}_z,\qquad
\{\mechanical{L}_y,\mechanical{L}_z\}=\mechanical{L}_x,\qquad
\{\mechanical{L}_z,\mechanical{L}_x\}=\mechanical{L}_y.
$$

These relations form the Lie algebra of the rotation group $SO(3)$. They have the same form as the quantum angular momentum commutators $[\mechanical{\hat L}_i,\mechanical{\hat L}_j]=i\hbar\varepsilon_{ijk}\mechanical{\hat L}_k$ under the correspondence $\{\cdot,\cdot\}\leftrightarrow[\cdot,\cdot]/(i\hbar)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q_k,p_k$ | — | Canonical variables | The generalized coordinates and conjugate momenta. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The generator of time evolution. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position components | The Cartesian coordinates of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{p}_x,\mechanical{p}_y,\mechanical{p}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum components | The Cartesian components of linear momentum. |
| $\mechanical{L}_x,\mechanical{L}_y,\mechanical{L}_z,\mechanical{\hat L}_i$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum components | The classical components and the quantum operators. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale in the commutator correspondence. |
