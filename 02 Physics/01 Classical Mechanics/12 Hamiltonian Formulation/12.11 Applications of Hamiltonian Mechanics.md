---
title: "Applications of Hamiltonian Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Central-force motion reduces to one radial coordinate. A free particle and a plane pendulum show other types of Hamiltonian motion.

## Particle in a central field

In plane polar coordinates, the Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{H}=\frac{\mechanical{p}_r^2}{2\mechanical{m}}+\frac{p_\phi^2}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2}+\energy{U}(\mechanical{r}).
$$

The angle $\phi$ is cyclic, so $p_\phi=\mechanical{L}_z$ is constant. The radial equations are $\mechanical{\dot r}=\mechanical{p}_r/\mechanical{m}$ and $\mechanical{\dot p}_r=-d\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}/d\mechanical{r}$. This gives an effective one-dimensional problem with

$$
\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mechanical{r})=\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})+\frac{\mechanical{L}_z^2}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}^2}.
$$

## Free particle in three dimensions

For $\energy{H}=\mechanical{\vec p}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical{m})$, the equations are $\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}=\mechanical{\vec p}/\mechanical{m}$ and $\mechanical{\dot{\vec p}}=0$. There are six constants of motion, the three components of momentum $\mechanical{\vec p}$ and the three components of the initial position $\mechanical{\vec r}-\mechanical{\vec p}t/\mechanical{m}$.

## Plane pendulum

The Hamiltonian and its equations are

$$
\energy{H}=\frac{p_\theta^2}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\cos\theta,\qquad
\dot\theta=\frac{p_\theta}{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\ell}^2},\qquad
\dot p_\theta=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}\sin\theta.
$$

They are equivalent to $\ddot\theta+(\mechanical{g}/\mechanical{\ell})\sin\theta=0$. In the $(\theta,p_\theta)$ plane with the angle unwrapped, oscillations give closed trajectories, and complete rotations give open trajectories. The **separatrix** at $\energy{E}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{\ell}$ separates these types of motion.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{H},\energy{U},\energy{U}_{\mathrm{eff}},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energies | The Hamiltonian, potential, effective potential, and energy on a trajectory. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle or pendulum bob. |
| $\mechanical{r},\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{\ell}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and length | The radial coordinate, position vector, and pendulum length. |
| $\mechanical{p}_r,\mechanical{\vec p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Linear momentum | The radial momentum and free-particle momentum vector. |
| $p_\phi,\mechanical{L}_z,p_\theta$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The conserved central-force momentum and the pendulum's conjugate momentum. |
| $\phi,\theta$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angle | The polar angle and pendulum angle. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The gravitational field magnitude in the pendulum example. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
