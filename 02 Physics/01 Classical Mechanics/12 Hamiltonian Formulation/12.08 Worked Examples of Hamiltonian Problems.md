---
title: "Worked Examples of Hamiltonian Problems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Hamiltonian examples include cyclotron motion, motion in a square well, and drift in crossed electric and magnetic fields.

## Particle in a uniform magnetic field

Take $\magnetic{\vec B}=\magnetic{B}\hat z$ and the symmetric gauge $\magnetic{\vec A}=(-\magnetic{B}\mechanical{y}/2,\magnetic{B}\mechanical{x}/2,0)$. The Lagrangian is

$$
\energy{L}=\frac{\mechanical{m}}{2}(\mechanical{\dot x}^{\,2}+\mechanical{\dot y}^{\,2}+\mechanical{\dot z}^{\,2})+\frac{\electric{q}\magnetic{B}}{2}(\mechanical{x}\mechanical{\dot y}-\mechanical{y}\mechanical{\dot x}).
$$

The canonical momenta are $\mechanical{p}_x=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot x}-\electric{q}\magnetic{B}\mechanical{y}/2$, $\mechanical{p}_y=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot y}+\electric{q}\magnetic{B}\mechanical{x}/2$, and $\mechanical{p}_z=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot z}$. The Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{H}=\frac{(\mechanical{p}_x+\electric{q}\magnetic{B}\mechanical{y}/2)^2+(\mechanical{p}_y-\electric{q}\magnetic{B}\mechanical{x}/2)^2+\mechanical{p}_z^2}{2\mechanical{m}}.
$$

In polar coordinates $(\mechanical{\rho},\phi)$ in the transverse plane, with $\mechanical{z}$ along the field,

$$
\energy{H}=\frac{\mechanical{p}_\rho^2}{2\mechanical{m}}+\frac{(p_\phi-\electric{q}\magnetic{B}\mechanical{\rho}^2/2)^2}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\rho}^2}+\frac{\mechanical{p}_z^2}{2\mechanical{m}}.
$$

The coordinates $\phi$ and $\mechanical{z}$ are cyclic, so $p_\phi$ and $\mechanical{p}_z$ are constant. In the transverse plane the trajectory is a Larmor circle about a guiding center. Its signed cyclotron frequency is $\signal{\omega}_c=\electric{q}\magnetic{B}/\mechanical{m}$.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source describes the radial equation as a harmonic oscillator at the cyclotron frequency. The Cartesian velocity components satisfy harmonic equations at that frequency. The polar Hamiltonian generally contains a radial inverse-square term proportional to $p_\phi^2$.

## Particle in a square well

The potential is $\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})=-\energy{V}_0$ for $\lvert\mechanical{x}\rvert<\mechanical{L}$ and zero elsewhere. The Hamiltonian $\energy{H}=\mechanical{p}^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\energy{U}(\mechanical{x})$ is piecewise defined.

For $\energy{E}>0$, the particle moves freely outside the well and travels faster inside it, so the crossing time is finite. For $-\energy{V}_0<\energy{E}<0$, the particle remains in the well and oscillates between its walls.

The period is $\signal{T}=2\mechanical{L}\sqrt{2\mechanical{m}/(\energy{E}+\energy{V}_0)}$. This is the round-trip distance $4\mechanical{L}$ divided by the speed $\sqrt{2(\energy{E}+\energy{V}_0)/\mechanical{m}}$.

## Particle in constant crossed fields

Let $\electric{\vec E}$ and $\magnetic{\vec B}$ be constant and perpendicular. In suitable coordinates, the motion is a superposition of cyclotron motion and uniform drift at velocity

$$
\mechanical{\vec v}_d=\frac{\electric{\vec E}\times\magnetic{\vec B}}{\magnetic{B}^2}.
$$

This drift occurs in plasma physics and in mass spectrometers.
