---
title: "Liouville's Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space volume because the Hamiltonian vector field has zero divergence.

> [!theorem] Theorem 12.2 | Liouville's Theorem
> Hamiltonian flow preserves phase-space volume. For each region $R$ in phase space, the volume transported by the flow of $\energy{H}$ satisfies
>
> $$
> V(R(t))=V(R(0)).
> $$

**Proof.** The Hamiltonian vector field $\vec X_H=(\partial\energy{H}/\partial p_k,-\partial\energy{H}/\partial q_k)$ has zero divergence.

$$
\nabla\cdot\vec X_H=\sum_k\left(\frac{\partial^2\energy{H}}{\partial q_k\partial p_k}-\frac{\partial^2\energy{H}}{\partial p_k\partial q_k}\right)=0.
$$

By the divergence theorem, the transported volume is constant.

## Physical consequences

- **Particle beams.** If a beam occupies volume $V$ in phase space at $t=0$, it occupies the same phase-space volume at every later time.
- **Statistical mechanics.** The probability density in phase space is constant along Hamiltonian trajectories. This is a foundation of statistical mechanics.

> [!info] Analytical mechanics
> Poisson brackets are the classical counterpart of quantum commutators. Under the formal replacement $\{f,g\}\mapsto-i[\hat f,\hat g]/\hbar$, the Poisson algebra corresponds to the algebra of quantum observables. This is Dirac's correspondence principle.
