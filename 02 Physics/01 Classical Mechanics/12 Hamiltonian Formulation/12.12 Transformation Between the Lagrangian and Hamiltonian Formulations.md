---
title: "Transformation Between the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Formulations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Legendre transformation converts between the two formulations when the conjugate momentum equations can be inverted.

The Legendre transformation connects the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations by changing the velocity argument to the conjugate momentum.

$$
\energy{L}(q,\dot q,t)\longleftrightarrow\energy{H}(q,p,t),\qquad
p_k=\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q_k},\qquad
\energy{H}=\sum_k p_k\dot q_k-\energy{L}.
$$

**Inverse transformation.** When the momentum equations are invertible, apply the transformation in reverse using $\dot q_k=\partial\energy{H}/\partial p_k$ to recover $\energy{L}$.

## Advantages of the Hamiltonian formulation

- The equations are first order and symmetric in $(q,p)$.
- The $2n$-dimensional phase space gives a geometric description.
- Poisson brackets provide an algebraic structure.
- When $\energy{H}$ is a first integral, its level surfaces are visible in phase space.
- Canonical transformations are straightforward to apply.

## Advantages of the Lagrangian formulation

- Generalized coordinates can incorporate holonomic constraints.
- Hamilton's principle gives a variational derivation from a physical principle.
- The formulation extends naturally to field theory.
- It is easier to treat local symmetries, such as gauge symmetries.

The choice of formalism depends on the problem.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{L},\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian and Hamiltonian | The two functions related by the Legendre transformation. |
| $q,\dot q_k,p_k$ | — | Generalized variables | The coordinates, velocities, and conjugate momenta. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
