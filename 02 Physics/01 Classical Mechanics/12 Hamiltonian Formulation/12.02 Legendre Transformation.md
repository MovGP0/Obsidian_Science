---
title: "Legendre Transformation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Legendre transformation replaces a variable with the derivative conjugate to it. In mechanics it replaces velocities with conjugate momenta.

> [!definition] Definition 12.1 | Legendre Transformation
> For a convex function $f(x)$, the **Legendre transformation** defines
>
> $$
> g(p)=\sup_x[px-f(x)]=px^*(p)-f(x^*(p)),
> $$
>
> where $f'(x^*)=p$ determines the maximizing point $x^*(p)$ when it exists.

In coordinates where $p=f'(x)$ is invertible, use $x=x(p)$ in $g(p)=px-f(x)$. For a closed convex function, transforming twice recovers $f(x)=\sup_p[xp-g(p)]$.

The transformation changes the independent variable from $x$ to the dual derivative $p=f'(x)$ and preserves the information in the function under these conditions.

**In mechanics.** Transform $\energy{L}(q,\dot q,t)$ to $\energy{H}(q,p,t)$ using $p_k=\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q_k$. Then

$$
\energy{H}(q,p,t)=\sum_k p_k\dot q_k-\energy{L}(q,\dot q,t),
$$

where inversion of the momentum definitions gives $\dot q_k$ as functions of $(q,p,t)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,\dot q_k,p_k$ | — | Generalized variables | The coordinates, velocities, and conjugate momenta. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\energy{L},\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian and Hamiltonian | The two dynamical functions related by the transformation. |
