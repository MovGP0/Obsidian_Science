---
title: "Energy and the Hamiltonian"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Hamiltonian is conserved when it has no explicit time dependence. Its equality to total mechanical energy depends on the system.

In many cases $\energy{H}$ equals the total mechanical energy $\energy{E}=\energy{T}+\energy{U}$.

- With scleronomic constraints and an ordinary potential, $\energy{T}=\energy{T}_2$ is homogeneous quadratic in the generalized velocities. Then $\energy{H}=\energy{T}+\energy{U}=\energy{E}$.
- With rheonomic constraints, $\energy{T}$ can include terms linear in $\dot q$ and terms independent of $\dot q$. In general, $\energy{H}\ne\energy{T}+\energy{U}$.

**Conservation.** If $\partial\energy{H}/\partial t=0$, then $\energy{H}$ is constant along the motion. Hamilton's equations give

$$
\frac{d\energy{H}}{dt}=\sum_k\left(\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial q_k}\dot q_k+\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial p_k}\dot p_k\right)+\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial t}.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{H},\energy{E},\energy{T},\energy{T}_2,\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energies | The Hamiltonian, total mechanical energy, kinetic energy, its quadratic part, and potential energy. |
| $q_k,p_k,\dot q_k,\dot p_k$ | — | Canonical variables and rates | The canonical coordinates, conjugate momenta, and their time derivatives. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
