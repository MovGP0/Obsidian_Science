---
title: "Canonical Variables and Hamilton's Equations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Hamilton's equations give the first-order evolution of the canonical pairs $(q_k,p_k)$.

The **canonical variables** are the pairs $(q_k,p_k)$. The space of these $2n$ variables is the **phase space**.

> [!theorem] Theorem 12.1 | Hamilton's Equations
> For a system with Hamiltonian $\energy{H}(q,p,t)$, the equations of motion are
>
> $$
> \dot q_k=\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial p_k},\qquad \dot p_k=-\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial q_k}.
> $$

**Derivation.** Compute $d\energy{H}$ in two ways. From $\energy{H}=\energy{H}(q,p,t)$,

$$
d\energy{H}=\sum_k\left(\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial q_k}dq_k+\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial p_k}dp_k\right)+\frac{\partial\energy{H}}{\partial t}dt.
$$

Alternatively, differentiate $\energy{H}=\sum_k p_k\dot q_k-\energy{L}(q,\dot q,t)$. The $d\dot q_k$ terms cancel because $p_k=\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q_k$. Thus

$$
d\energy{H}=\sum_k\left(\dot q_k\,dp_k-\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial q_k}dq_k\right)-\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial t}dt
=\sum_k(\dot q_k\,dp_k-\dot p_k\,dq_k)-\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial t}dt.
$$

The second equality uses the Euler–Lagrange relation $\dot p_k=\partial\energy{L}/\partial q_k$. Compare coefficients of $dq_k$, $dp_k$, and $dt$ to get Hamilton's equations and $\partial\energy{H}/\partial t=-\partial\energy{L}/\partial t$.

The $2n$ first-order equations in $2n$ variables require $2n$ initial values $(q,p)$ at $t=0$.

**Example. Harmonic oscillator.** For $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\dot q^2-\tfrac12\mechanical{k}q^2$, the momentum is $p=\mechanical{m}\dot q$, so $\dot q=p/\mechanical{m}$. The Hamiltonian is $\energy{H}=p^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\tfrac12\mechanical{k}q^2$, which gives

$$
\dot q=\frac{p}{\mechanical{m}},\qquad \dot p=-\mechanical{k}q.
$$

Substitution gives the equation of motion $\ddot q=\dot p/\mechanical{m}=-\mechanical{k}q/\mechanical{m}=-\signal{\omega}^2q$.

**Example. Particle in a potential.** For $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}^{\,2}-\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r})$, one has $\mechanical{\vec p}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}$ and $\mechanical{\vec v}=\mechanical{\vec p}/\mechanical{m}$. Thus

$$
\energy{H}=\frac{\mechanical{\vec p}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical{m}}+\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r}).
$$

Hamilton's equations are $\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}=\mechanical{\vec p}/\mechanical{m}$ and $\mechanical{\dot{\vec p}}=-\nabla\energy{U}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q_k,\dot q_k,p_k,\dot p_k$ | — | Generalized variables and rates | The coordinates, momenta, and their time derivatives. |
| $\energy{H},\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and Lagrangian | The two dynamical functions used in the derivation. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{N\,m^{-1}}$ | Spring constant | The force per unit displacement of the oscillator. |
| $\signal{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The natural frequency of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The particle position vector. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The time derivative of the position vector. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The product of mass and velocity. |
| $\energy{U}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy | The position-dependent energy of the particle. |
