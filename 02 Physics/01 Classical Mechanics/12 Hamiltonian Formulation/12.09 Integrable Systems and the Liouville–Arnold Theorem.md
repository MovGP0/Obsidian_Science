---
title: "Integrable Systems and the Liouville–Arnold Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An integrable system has enough independent conserved quantities in involution. Its compact, regular invariant sets are tori.

A Hamiltonian system with $n$ degrees of freedom is **Liouville integrable** if it has $n$ independent integrals of motion $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ in involution. Thus $\{f_i,f_j\}=0$ for all $i,j$.

> [!theorem] Theorem 12.3 | Liouville–Arnold Theorem
> A compact, connected regular common level set of the integrals is an invariant manifold diffeomorphic to the torus $\mathbb T^n$. On this torus there are angle coordinates $\theta_k\in[0,2\pi)$ with $\dot\theta_k=\signal{\omega}_k$, the frequencies of the torus.

> [!note] Source conditions
> The source states the torus conclusion for generic trajectories of an integrable system. The compactness and regularity conditions restrict the conclusion to the relevant level sets.

## Examples

- The one-dimensional harmonic oscillator is integrable with $f_1=\energy{H}$. A positive-energy level set is $\mathbb T^1=S^1$.
- The Kepler two-body problem is integrable with $f_1=\energy{H}$, $f_2=\mechanical{L}^2$, and $f_3=\mechanical{L}_z$. Compact regular bound level sets have torus $\mathbb T^3$.
- The symmetric top is integrable.

**Nonintegrable systems.** Many systems with several degrees of freedom are nonintegrable. Perturbing an integrable system can produce complicated and partly chaotic motion. See the discussion of the KAM theorem in [[14.00 Small Oscillations and Normal Modes]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f_i$ | — | Integral of motion | An independent conserved function in involution with the other integrals. |
| $\theta_k$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angle coordinate | A periodic coordinate on the invariant torus. |
| $\signal{\omega}_k$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The constant rate of an angle on the torus. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The first integral in the oscillator and Kepler examples. |
| $\mechanical{L},\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The magnitude and axial component in the Kepler example. |
