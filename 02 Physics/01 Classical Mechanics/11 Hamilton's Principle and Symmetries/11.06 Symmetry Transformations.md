---
title: "Symmetry Transformations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A continuous variational symmetry changes the Lagrangian by at most a total time derivative.

> [!definition] Definition 11.2 — Symmetry Transformation
> Let $q\to q'(\varepsilon)$ depend continuously on a parameter $\varepsilon$, with $q'(0)=q$. It is a **continuous symmetry** of the Lagrangian if, for every path,
>
> $$
> \energy{L}(q'(\varepsilon),\dot q'(\varepsilon),t)
> =\energy{L}(q,\dot q,t)+\frac{d}{dt}f(q,t;\varepsilon),
> $$
>
> for some function $f$ with $f(q,t;0)=0$.

Equivalently, the action $S$ changes only by a boundary term. This term does not change the equations of motion.

**Examples.**

- Spatial translation $\mechanical{\vec r}\to\mechanical{\vec r}+\varepsilon\hat n$ is a symmetry when $\energy{L}$ is independent of position along $\hat n$.
- Rotation about an axis is a symmetry when $\energy{L}$ is invariant under rotations about that axis.
- Time translation $t\to t+\varepsilon$ is a symmetry when $\energy{L}$ has no explicit time dependence.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q,q',\dot q,\dot q'$ | — | Generalized coordinates and velocities | The original and transformed configuration variables and their time derivatives. |
| $\varepsilon$ | — | Symmetry parameter | The parameter that labels transformations, with zero giving the identity. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The function whose change under the symmetry is a total derivative. |
| $f,S$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Boundary function and action | The primitive of the total derivative and the time integral of the Lagrangian. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The position changed by the spatial translation. |
