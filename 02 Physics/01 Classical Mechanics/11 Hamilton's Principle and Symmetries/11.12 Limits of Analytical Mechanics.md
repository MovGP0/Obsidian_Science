---
title: "Limits of Analytical Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Hamilton's principle extends to fields through a Lagrangian density integrated over space and time.

Hamilton's principle and the action extend to fields, which have infinitely many degrees of freedom. For a field $\phi(\vec x,t)$, the action is

$$
\energy{S}=\int dt\int d^3x\,\mathcal L(\phi,\partial_\mu\phi).
$$

Here $\mathcal L$ is the **Lagrangian density**. The stationarity condition gives the Euler–Lagrange field equation

$$
\partial_\mu\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right)-\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi}=0.
$$

This is the standard variational formulation for classical fields and provides the starting point for quantized field theories. Point mechanics is the time-only case, with a finite set of degrees of freedom.

> [!note] Source wording
> The source describes point mechanics as having one degree of freedom in time. Time is the sole independent variable, and the system can have several generalized coordinates.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\phi$ | — | Field | The dynamical variable at each point in space and time. |
| $\mechanical{\vec x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The spatial argument of the field. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The temporal argument of the field. |
| $\mathcal L$ | $\unit{J\,m^{-3}}$ | Lagrangian density | The integrand of the action in space and time. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action | The space-time integral of the Lagrangian density. |
