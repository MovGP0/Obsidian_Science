---
title: "Variational Equations and Calculus of Variations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The same Euler–Lagrange condition applies to stationary functionals in mechanics and geometry.

The problem $\delta\int\energy{L}\,dt=0$ is one example of the calculus of variations. Other examples include shortest paths or geodesics, the brachistochrone, and minimal surfaces.

For a functional $J[y]=\int F(x,y,y')\,dx$ with fixed endpoints, stationarity gives

$$
\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}-\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y'}\right)=0.
$$

This has the same form as the Euler–Lagrange equations of mechanics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The integrand for mechanical action. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The integration variable in the mechanical example. |
