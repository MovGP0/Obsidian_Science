---
title: "Noether Charges for Discrete Symmetries"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Discrete symmetries map solutions to other solutions. Noether's theorem associates conserved charges with continuous symmetries.

Discrete transformations such as parity, time reversal, and reflection do not generate infinitesimal Noether charges. They still constrain the set of possible solutions.

- **Parity.** Under parity, $\mechanical{\vec r}\to-\mechanical{\vec r}$. If $\energy{L}(\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{\vec v},t)=\energy{L}(-\mechanical{\vec r},-\mechanical{\vec v},t)$, then $-\mechanical{\vec r}(t)$ is also a solution.
- **Time reversal.** Under time reversal, $t\to-t$. If $\energy{L}(\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{\vec v},t)=\energy{L}(\mechanical{\vec r},-\mechanical{\vec v},-t)$, then $\mechanical{\vec r}(-t)$ is also a solution.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The particle position vector. |
| $\mechanical{\vec v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The time derivative of the position vector. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The function whose time integral gives the action. |
