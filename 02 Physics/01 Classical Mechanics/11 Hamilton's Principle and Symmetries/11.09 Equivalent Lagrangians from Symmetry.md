---
title: "Equivalent Lagrangians from Symmetry"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Noether's theorem includes symmetries that change the Lagrangian by a boundary term.

A symmetry can leave $\energy{L}$ exactly invariant or change it by a total time derivative. The Noether charge includes $-f$ when $\varepsilon\,df/dt$ is the infinitesimal change in $\energy{L}$.

### Example — Galilean Boost

For a free particle, $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\lVert\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}\rVert^2$. Under $\mechanical{\vec r}\to\mechanical{\vec r}+\varepsilon\mechanical{\vec V}t$ and $\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}\to\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}+\varepsilon\mechanical{\vec V}$, to first order,

$$
\energy{L}'=\energy{L}+\varepsilon\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}\cdot\mechanical{\vec V}+O(\varepsilon^2)
=\energy{L}+\varepsilon\frac{d}{dt}(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}\cdot\mechanical{\vec V})+O(\varepsilon^2).
$$

The difference at first order is a total derivative. Up to sign, the associated conserved vector is $\mechanical{\vec G}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}-\mechanical{\vec P}t$, which expresses uniform motion of the center of mass.

> [!note] Analytical Mechanics
> Noether's theorem also applies to fields. Global $U(1)$ phase invariance of charged matter in electromagnetism gives electric charge conservation. Invariance under spacetime translations gives energy and momentum conservation. The source refers to the electromagnetic Lagrangian density in this example.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The function whose change is a total derivative. |
| $f,\varepsilon$ | — | Boundary coefficient and symmetry parameter | Their product has the unit of action. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The variable in the total derivative. |
| $\energy{L}'$ | $\unit{J}$ | Transformed Lagrangian | The Lagrangian after the boost. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the free particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The position of the free particle. |
| $\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}},\mechanical{\vec V}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocities | The particle velocity and the boost velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\vec P}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The conserved linear momentum of the free particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec G}$ | $\unit{kg\,m}$ | Boost charge | A constant proportional to the center-of-mass position extrapolated to time zero. |
