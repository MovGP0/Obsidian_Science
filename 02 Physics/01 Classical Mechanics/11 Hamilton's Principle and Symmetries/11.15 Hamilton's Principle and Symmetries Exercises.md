---
title: "Hamilton's Principle and Symmetries Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Practice identifying symmetries, finding conserved quantities, and deriving Noether's theorem.

1. **Basic.** Start with $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}^{\,2}$. Verify that the translation $\mechanical{\vec r}\to\mechanical{\vec r}+\varepsilon\hat{\imath}$ is a symmetry. Identify the conserved charge.
   **Result.** $Q=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot x}=\mechanical{p}_x$.
2. **Basic.** For a particle in a central potential $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})$, verify rotational invariance about the $z$ axis and identify $\mechanical{L}_z$ as the conserved charge.
   **Result.** $\mechanical{L}_z=\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{x}\mechanical{\dot y}-\mechanical{y}\mechanical{\dot x})$.
3. **Basic.** For $\energy{L}$ with no explicit dependence on $t$, verify that $\energy{E}=\dot q\,\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q-\energy{L}$ is conserved.
4. **Basic.** Prove that $\energy{L}'=\energy{L}+df/dt$ gives the same equations of motion as $\energy{L}$.
5. **Intermediate.** For a particle in a central potential $\energy{U}(\mechanical{\vec r})=\energy{U}(\lvert\mechanical{\vec r}\rvert)$, prove that $\mechanical{L}_x$, $\mechanical{L}_y$, and $\mechanical{L}_z$ are conserved.
6. **Intermediate.** Find the conserved quantity associated with a Galilean boost of a free particle.
   **Result.** $\mechanical{\vec G}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec r}-\mechanical{\vec P}t$ is constant.
7. **Intermediate.** For the two-body problem with potential $\energy{U}(\lvert\mechanical{\vec r}_1-\mechanical{\vec r}_2\rvert)$, prove that total momentum $\mechanical{\vec P}$ and total angular momentum $\mechanical{\vec L}$ are conserved. List the associated symmetries.
8. **Intermediate.** The Kepler problem with $\energy{U}(\mechanical{r})=-\alpha/\mechanical{r}$ has a hidden symmetry with an additional conserved quantity, the Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector. Write this vector.
   **Result.** $\mechanical{\vec A}=\mechanical{\vec p}\times\mechanical{\vec L}-\mechanical{\mu}\alpha\hat r$ is constant.
9. **Challenge.** Prove Noether's theorem in its general form. Start from invariance of the action, $\delta\energy{S}=0$, under a continuous infinitesimal transformation.
10. **Challenge.** Write the Lagrangian of a free relativistic particle, $\energy{L}=-\mechanical{m}c^2\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/c^2}$. Verify that the Euler–Lagrange equations give $d\mechanical{\vec p}/dt=0$ with $\mechanical{\vec p}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\vec v}/\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/c^2}$.
11. **Intermediate.** For a bead on a rotating rail, $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}+\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\omega}^2)$. Calculate the Jacobi function $\energy{h}$ and verify $\energy{h}\ne\energy{T}$.
    **Result.** $\energy{h}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}-\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\omega}^2)$ is constant. In general, $\energy{T}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}(\mechanical{\dot r}^{\,2}+\mechanical{r}^2\mechanical{\omega}^2)$ varies with time.
12. **Intermediate.** Show that the free-particle Lagrangian must depend quadratically on velocity for Galilean invariance under $\mechanical{\vec v}\to\mechanical{\vec v}+\mechanical{\vec V}$. Thus $\energy{L}\propto\mechanical{v}^2$ for the usual choice of boundary term.
13. **Intermediate.** For $\energy{L}=\tfrac12\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{g}\mechanical{z}$, verify that $\energy{E}=\energy{T}+\energy{U}$ is conserved. Verify that $\mechanical{L}_z$ is conserved by rotation about $z$ and that $\mechanical{p}_x$ and $\mechanical{p}_y$ are conserved.

> [!note] Source wording
> Exercise 12 calls the Lagrangian invariant. Under a Galilean boost it is invariant up to a total time derivative.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{\mu}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass and reduced mass | The particle mass and the reduced mass in the Kepler problem. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The position vector, its magnitude, and Cartesian components. |
| $\varepsilon$ | $\unit{m}$ | Translation parameter | The shift in the $x$ direction. |
| $\mechanical{\dot{\vec r}},\mechanical{\dot x},\mechanical{\dot y}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The velocity vector and Cartesian components. |
| $\mechanical{\vec p},\mechanical{\vec P},\mechanical{p}_x,Q$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Linear momentum | The particle momentum, total momentum, and component conserved in exercise 1. |
| $\mechanical{\vec L},\mechanical{L}_x,\mechanical{L}_y,\mechanical{L}_z$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum | The angular momentum vector and its components. |
| $\energy{L},\energy{L}',\energy{U},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy and dynamical functions | The Lagrangians, potential energy, and energy function. |
| $\energy{S},f$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action and boundary function | The action and the function whose total derivative changes the Lagrangian. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $q,\dot q$ | — | Generalized coordinate and velocity | The path coordinate and its time derivative. |
| $\mechanical{\vec G}$ | $\unit{kg\,m}$ | Galilean boost charge | The conserved quantity for boost symmetry. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Kepler potential strength | The positive coefficient of the inverse-distance potential. |
| $\mechanical{\vec A}$ | $\unit{kg^2\,m^3\,s^{-2}}$ | Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector | The conserved vector associated with the hidden symmetry. |
| $c,\mechanical{v},\mechanical{\vec v},\mechanical{\vec V},\mechanical{\dot r}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Speed and velocity | The speed of light, particle speed and velocity, boost velocity, and radial velocity. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular velocity | The rotation rate of the rail. |
| $\energy{h},\energy{T}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Jacobi function and kinetic energy | The quantities compared for the bead. |
| $\mechanical{g}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-2}}$ | Gravitational acceleration | The magnitude of the uniform acceleration in exercise 13. |
| $\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Height | The vertical Cartesian position. |
| $\mechanical{p}_y$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum component | The component along the $y$ axis. |
