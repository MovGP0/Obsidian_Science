> [!TLDR]
> Hamilton's principle gives the equations of motion from stationary action. Noether's theorem relates continuous symmetries to conservation laws.

Hamilton's principle formulates dynamics as a variational problem for the action. It gives the Euler–Lagrange equations from an integral condition. In this framework, Noether's theorem connects continuous symmetries to conservation laws. The same framework provides a basis for generalizations to quantum mechanics and field theory.

## Chapter summary

- The action is $\energy{S}=\int\energy{L}\,dt$. Hamilton's principle requires $\delta\energy{S}=0$ for paths with fixed endpoints.
- The Euler–Lagrange equations follow from $\delta\energy{S}=0$.
- A continuous symmetry leaves the Lagrangian unchanged up to a total time derivative.
- Noether's theorem associates a conserved charge with each continuous symmetry.
- Spatial translations give conservation of total linear momentum $\mechanical{\vec P}$.
- Rotations give conservation of total angular momentum $\mechanical{\vec L}$.
- Time translations give conservation of energy $\energy{E}$.
- Galilean boosts give uniform motion of the center of mass.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{S}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action | The time integral of the Lagrangian. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The integrand of the action. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The independent variable for the motion. |
| $\mechanical{\vec P}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Total linear momentum | The conserved charge for spatial translations. |
| $\mechanical{\vec L}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Total angular momentum | The conserved charge for rotations. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The conserved charge for time translations. |
