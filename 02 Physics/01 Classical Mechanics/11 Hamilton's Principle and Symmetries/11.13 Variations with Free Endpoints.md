---
title: "Variations with Free Endpoints"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A free endpoint adds a natural boundary condition to the Euler–Lagrange equations.

In some problems, the variations can move the endpoints. For the action $\energy{S}=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\energy{L}(q,\dot q,t)\,dt$, a general variation with free endpoints gives

$$
\delta\energy{S}=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left(\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial q}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q}\right)\delta q\,dt+\left[\frac{\partial\energy{L}}{\partial\dot q}\delta q\right]_{t_1}^{t_2}.
$$

For $\delta\energy{S}=0$ and arbitrary $\delta q$, the Euler–Lagrange equations must hold, and $\partial\energy{L}/\partial\dot q=0$ at each free endpoint. This condition fixes the endpoint momentum for the free coordinate.

**Application.** For problems such as the free-endpoint brachistochrone or geodesics with endpoints on a boundary, the natural boundary conditions determine the direction of the tangent where the path meets the boundary. In the Euclidean geodesic case, the tangent is perpendicular to the boundary.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{S}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action | The functional varied to find a stationary path. |
| $\energy{L}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Lagrangian | The integrand of the action. |
| $t_1,t_2$ | $\unit{s}$ | Endpoint times | The limits of the action integral. |
| $q,\dot q$ | — | Generalized coordinate and velocity | The path variable and its time derivative. |
