> [!TLDR]
> The author argues that a paradigm grounded in observed cosmic matter may remain fundamental, with later changes refining its cosmological details.

## Physical question and model

Can a theory be shown to be the ultimate foundation of dynamics? Agreement with known data and conceptual economy support a model within a domain. They cannot establish that no future observation will require a different account.

## Lecture account

A new paradigm should explain established observations at least as well as the older one. Strong reasons for change include falsifying evidence, unexplained facts, or repeated added postulates when a simpler common framework is available.

The author claims that Cosmic Relativity meets several of these criteria. Cosmic matter and gravity were available physical facts long before the proposed theory. He cites GPS motional corrections, Sagnac results, and Bergson's criticisms as evidence or logical concerns that he considers poorly handled by the prevailing interpretation. He argues that a preferred cosmic frame explains the known relativistic effects consistently without extra premises.

The proposed resolution of inertia and related dynamics is presented as a physical mechanism, not merely a new formal framework. The author therefore suggests that cosmic gravity may be an ultimate foundation for dynamics and relativity. Future refinements would mainly concern the Universe's slow evolution as observational cosmology becomes more precise. This is the source's assessment of its theory.
