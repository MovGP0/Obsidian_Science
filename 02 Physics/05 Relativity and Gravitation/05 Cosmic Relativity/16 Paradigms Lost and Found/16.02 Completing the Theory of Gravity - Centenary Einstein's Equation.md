> [!TLDR]
> The author proposes retaining a cosmic matter energy–momentum tensor as a permanent background term in Einstein's field equation.

## Physical question and model

Does writing a separate cosmic stress–energy term change Einstein’s theory? The standard field equation can already include local and cosmological matter in its total source. Moving one part to the other side is algebra unless new dynamics or restrictions are added.

## Lecture account

The source seeks a modification that preserves an action linear in scalar curvature and field equations containing no more than second metric derivatives. It adds the energy–momentum tensor of the observed cosmic matter distribution. On large scales this distribution is approximately homogeneous and evolves slowly on local timescales.

The proposed equation is

$$
R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}R-\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}-\kappa\energy{T}^{U}_{\mu\nu}(t)
=\kappa\energy{T}_{\mu\nu}. \tag{16.1}
$$

The author names this the Centenary Einstein's Equation. The cosmic tensor is placed on the left to emphasise that it is permanent and nonremovable, while the right-hand tensor varies with the local gravitational problem.

He claims that including this background rejects the STR, the Minkowski metric, and Lorentz transformations as the theory's physical basis. He assigns light propagation Galilean form in the resulting gravitational description. These are the author's proposed consequences.

The background introduces universal time, making the curvature terms time-dependent as well. When local clumped matter is absent, the right-hand tensor is negligible and the equation describes cosmic gravitational evolution. The source treats the vacuum equation $R_{\mu\nu}=0$ as a useful local approximation near isolated matter rather than a complete account of the matter-filled Universe.

## Interpretation and checks

Define the source split, coupling units, cosmological-constant sign, and metric convention consistently. The contracted Bianchi identity constrains the covariant divergence of the total source; separately chosen terms cannot violate that requirement. State whether cosmic and local components exchange energy and momentum. A homogeneous source still requires a compatible cosmological solution and boundary conditions. The displayed rearrangement alone establishes neither a unique cosmic potential nor an absolute clock law.
