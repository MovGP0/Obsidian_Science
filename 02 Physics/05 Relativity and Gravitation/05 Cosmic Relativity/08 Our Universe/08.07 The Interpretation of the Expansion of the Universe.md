> [!TLDR]
> The author compares matter moving through space with expanding space, and examines the physical interpretation of cosmological redshift.

## Physical question and model

Can a linear recession law distinguish an explosion model from expanding geometry? Ballistic particles released near one origin develop a distance–speed correlation at late times. An FLRW model instead specifies a spacetime and a comoving congruence; matching one linear relation does not establish their full equivalence.

## Lecture account

Imagine an explosion from a small volume of diameter $\mechanical{\delta}$, with particles ejected at all possible random velocities, like the earlier Milne example. After time $t$, distance and velocity become linearly correlated because a particle of speed $\mechanical{v}$ reaches $\mechanical{d}=\mechanical{v}t\pm\mechanical{\delta}$. When $t$ is sufficiently large that the source’s condition $\mechanical{d}\ll\mechanical{\delta}$ holds for almost all particles, the Hubble relation $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{d}/t=\mechanical{H}(t)\mechanical{d}$ follows. The same recession relation is seen from every position.

> [!note]
> The source prints $\mechanical{d}\ll\mechanical{\delta}$, but its argument requires the initial source size to become negligible: $\mechanical{\delta}\ll\mechanical{d}$. The printed inequality is retained above.

Because the linear law preserves homogeneity and isotropy, one can postulate that particles do not move and their space expands instead. Such an interpretation can be carried consistently, as is done for cosmic expansion.

Another artificial example considers a room, its walls, and all contents made of materials with identical thermal expansion. Slow heating changes geometric relations linearly, following a ‘Hubble law’. One could interpret it as expansion of space rather than thermal expansion of matter. The familiar balloon with painted galaxy dots similarly exposes possible overinterpretation.

The preferred present interpretation says galaxies do not really move: their homogeneous space expands and carries them. Local proper motions due to gravity and other causes are comparatively small. Galaxy redshift is then wavelength ‘stretching’ through expanding space rather than a Doppler shift with a fast-motion relativistic correction. The shift equals the expansion factor between emission and reception:

$$
\frac{\signal{\lambda}_r}{\signal{\lambda}_e}\equiv1+z=\frac{\mechanical{a}_r}{\mechanical{a}_e}. \tag{8.26}
$$

Here $\mechanical{a}$ is the FLRW scale factor. This interprets space expansion at wavelength scales below atomic size. Yet atoms and larger bound systems, such as Sun–Earth–Moon, do not expand, for reasons inadequately explained. This is consistent with observations. ‘Space’ is treated as a physical entity that expands or contracts according to the matter density present.

An advantage is handling relative velocities above light speed, since $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{H}\mechanical{r}$ no longer denotes a real relative velocity. Otherwise, real matter exceeds $\mechanical{c}$ for two galaxies separated by $\mechanical{d}>\mechanical{c}/\mechanical{H}_0$, about $4.3\,\unit{Gpc}$. This is not a real problem, since it is the estimated Universe size, 14 billion light years.

An unsatisfactory aspect of space expansion is apparent energy nonconservation. Light emitted about 100 million years ago has its wavelength stretched by about $0.7\%$ on reaching us. Energy changes by $\Delta\energy{E}/\energy{E}=\Delta\signal{\lambda}/\signal{\lambda}\approx0.007$. Where did it go?

The same issue affects particles. Their de Broglie wavelength $\signal{\lambda}=\energy{h}/\mechanical{p}$ stretches by $\mechanical{a}_r/\mechanical{a}_e$ through expanding space. Received momentum is $\mechanical{p}_r=\energy{h}/\signal{\lambda}'=\energy{h}\mechanical{a}_e/(\signal{\lambda}\mechanical{a}_r)=\mechanical{p}\mechanical{a}_e/\mechanical{a}_r$. The source then gives $\delta\energy{E}=\mechanical{p}\delta\mechanical{p}/\mechanical{m}=(1-\mechanical{a}_e/\mechanical{a}_r)\mechanical{p}/\mechanical{m}$. Where did this energy go? One must invent a fantastic account for the anomaly.

> [!note]
> The final printed particle-energy expression is missing a momentum factor: $\mechanical{p}\delta\mechanical{p}/\mechanical{m}$ has energy units, but $(1-\mechanical{a}_e/\mechanical{a}_r)\mechanical{p}/\mechanical{m}$ has velocity units. The printed expression is retained.

The suspicion that matter motion is overinterpreted as space expansion strengthens with collapse of a finite spherical particle cloud, initially uniform at density $\mechanical{\rho}_0$ and pressure-free (‘dust’) [23]. Gravity collapses the homogeneous cloud in time $\mechanical{T}_0=1/\sqrt{\mechanical{G}\mechanical{\rho}_0}$. An internal observer finds evolution and GTR metric identical to an evolving Universe. Contraction obeys Hubble’s law and could be described as finite space contracting instead of the cloud collapsing. The gravitational law is the same for cloud and Universe. Yet in the cloud case, we know its surrounding space is not contracting.

## Interpretation and checks

The ballistic limit requires travelled distances much larger than the initial source size, the opposite of the printed inequality. Cosmological wavelength redshift follows the ratio of scale factors along a null ray under the homogeneous model. It need not be described as light losing energy through a local friction force. Compare luminosity distances, angular distances, and clock-rate observations before deciding whether two redshift interpretations predict the same data.
