## Lecture account

This chapter reviewed observations relevant to the factual Universe’s gravity. The crucial input for a gravitational paradigm of relativity and dynamics is the Universe ‘as it is’, because every physical process, theory, and empirical test occurs in this single Universe.

After the limited observational cosmology before 1930, we reviewed the growth of GTR-based theoretical cosmology pioneered by Einstein, with contributions from de Sitter, Friedmann, Lemaître, and Robertson. Hubble’s systematic galaxy-recession observations transformed the field by establishing a linear velocity–distance relation.

We discussed recent evolution of large-scale homogeneous, isotropic matter–energy density, outlined expansion theory, and derived Friedmann equations from the spatial tidal description of gravity. The Cosmological Principle and Milne model were considered. Expansion and its changing rate were discussed through these equations. A physical reason for expansion was a conceptual highlight. Observational velocity–distance results and refinements of the Hubble constant were reviewed.

We then described quantitative cosmic components: ordinary atomic matter, remnant isotropic CMBR, dark matter, and dark energy, with methods for estimating their densities. Confirmed spatial flatness implies critical density. We discussed its meaning and pointed out the Newtonian gravitational potential $\mechanical{\phi}=-\mechanical{c}^2$.

Finally, we examined interpretation of expansion and redshift, highlighting microscopic energy-conservation concerns. An interpretation with matter and galaxies actually moving is consistent with observations.

This completes the background needed to study the cosmic gravitational paradigm, Cosmic Relativity’s results, and supporting experiments. To highlight current relativity’s inadequacy, the next chapter examines twin-clock time dilation, the ‘twin paradox’. A physically and logically rigorous exposition reveals gaps in standard treatments. The central issue is an absolute motion reference frame, whose existence this chapter has established.
