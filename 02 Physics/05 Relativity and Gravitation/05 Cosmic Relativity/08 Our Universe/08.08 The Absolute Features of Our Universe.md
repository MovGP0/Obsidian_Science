> [!TLDR]
> The author summarises cosmic homogeneity, critical density, expansion, and the CMBR as the factual background for a proposed absolute frame and universal time.

## Physical question and model

Which cosmic properties provide reference structures, and which establish a new law? A mean matter flow and a radiation temperature can define useful cosmological coordinates. Local laws still require separate tests of their symmetry and coupling.

## Lecture account

We conclude by reviewing cosmic physical features and implications relevant to the gravitational paradigm of dynamics and relativity.

1. Average matter–energy distribution is homogeneous and isotropic on large scales. The Universe has the same physical characteristics from every position, the Cosmological Principle.
2. Matter density decreases monotonically through mutual recession obeying $\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{H}(t)\mechanical{d}$. Expansion is slow, below one per cent per 100 million years. Its approximate observational form is the Hubble redshift–distance law, $\delta\signal{\nu}/\signal{\nu}=\mechanical{H}\mechanical{d}/\mechanical{c}$.
3. Mean gravitating matter–energy density is close to critical, $\mechanical{\rho}_c=3\mechanical{H}_0^2/(8\pi\mechanical{G})$. Ordinary baryonic–leptonic matter contributes $\mechanical{\rho}_m/\mechanical{\rho}_c\approx5\%$, dark matter $\mechanical{\rho}_{\mathrm{dm}}/\mechanical{\rho}_c\approx27\%$, and dark energy $\mechanical{\rho}_{\mathrm{de}}/\mechanical{\rho}_c\approx68\%$. Light and neutrinos contribute less than $0.1\%$.
4. Cosmological models use Einstein’s GTR and the FLRW line element. It has universal synchronised time and a time-dependent spatial section preserving homogeneity. Fundamental parameters are $\mechanical{H}=\dot{\mechanical{a}}/\mechanical{a}$, the source’s deceleration parameter $q=\ddot{\mechanical{a}}/(\mechanical{a}\mechanical{H}_0^2)$, and curvature $k$ chosen from $+1,0,-1$. Observations support flat Euclidean space, $k=0$.
5. Directly observable parameters are $\mechanical{H}_0\approx70\pm5\,\unit{km/(s\,Mpc)}$ and $q_0\approx-0.55\pm0.2$. A value near $-0.5$ indicates accelerating expansion and dark energy with $\mechanical{p}=-\mechanical{\rho}\mechanical{c}^2$.
6. The CMBR is an isotropic radiation bath from the hot past. Its temperature, $2.7255\,\unit{K}$, is the same everywhere and slowly decreases with expansion. This temperature gives a universal time synchronised everywhere. Its isotropy for an observer at rest relative to mean matter also provides a motion reference, resolving absolute cosmic-frame velocities to about $1\,\unit{km/s}$.

> [!note]
> Item 4 omits the minus sign in the source’s deceleration definition. Equation (8.17) gives $q_0=-\ddot{\mechanical{a}}/(\mechanical{a}\mechanical{H}_0^2)$, consistent with the negative measured values quoted in item 5. The printed summary definition is retained.

## Interpretation and checks

Build a two-column ledger: observational inputs such as anisotropy or redshift, and additional conclusions such as an absolute clock law or modified inertia. For each conclusion, name the missing dynamical step. A useful transfer test is a laboratory moving relative to a thermal radiation bath: it can detect that environment without establishing a preferred vacuum propagation law.
