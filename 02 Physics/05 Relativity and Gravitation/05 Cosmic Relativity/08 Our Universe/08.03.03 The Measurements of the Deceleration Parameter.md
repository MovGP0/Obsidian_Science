title: "The Measurements of the Deceleration Parameter"

> [!TLDR]
> The deceleration parameter describes the changing expansion rate and depends on matter, curvature, and a cosmological-constant contribution.

## Physical question and model

What does the deceleration parameter measure? It is a dimensionless combination of the scale factor and its first two time derivatives. The minus sign makes positive values correspond to slowing expansion when the expansion rate is positive.

## Lecture account

The Hubble parameter represents expansion rate. Its changing rate is described by the *deceleration parameter*,

$$
q_0\equiv-\frac1{\mechanical{H}_0^2}\frac{\ddot{\mechanical{a}}}{\mechanical{a}}=-\frac{\mechanical{a}\ddot{\mechanical{a}}}{\dot{\mechanical{a}}^2}. \tag{8.17}
$$

With matter only, (8.16) gives $\ddot{\mechanical{a}}/\mechanical{a}=-4\pi\mechanical{G}\mechanical{\rho}/3$. Together with (8.15), this relates deceleration to density and curvature by $q_0=\mechanical{\Omega}_m/2=\tfrac12[1+k\mechanical{c}^2/(\mechanical{H}_0^2\mechanical{a}^2)]$, where $\mechanical{\Omega}_m=\mechanical{\rho}/\mechanical{\rho}_c$. Thus it gives $q_0=1/2$ for flat $k=0$, and $q=0$ without matter, in the empty Milne Universe with $k=-1$. Distant-galaxy redshift measurements can directly give the second-order expansion deceleration term, first stressed by Hoyle and Sandage [16].

A cosmological constant changes the situation greatly. With relatively negligible matter, $\mechanical{H}_0^2=\mechanical{\Lambda}\mechanical{c}^2/3$ and $\ddot{\mechanical{a}}/\mechanical{a}=\mechanical{\Lambda}\mechanical{c}^2/3$, giving $q_0=-1$. With both components, $q_0=\mechanical{\Omega}_m/2-\mechanical{\Omega}_\Lambda$. A mixture with $\mechanical{\Omega}_\Lambda=\mechanical{\Omega}_m/2$, for example $\mechanical{\Omega}_m=0.5$ and $\mechanical{\Omega}_\Lambda=0.25$, gives $q_0=0$ and mimics the empty Milne model, although it is not flat. Until about two decades ago, astronomers broadly favoured $q_0\geq1/2$. This has changed: distance–redshift measurements using distant supernovae as standard candles give $q_0\approx-0.6\pm0.2$, consistent with a component like the cosmological constant, called dark energy. For example, $70\%$ dark energy and $30\%$ matter give $q_0=(0.3\times(1/2)+0.7\times(-1))\simeq-0.55$, preferred by Planck CMBR observations [18].

The favoured consensus model is spatially flat, $k=0$, with $5\%$ atomic matter, $27\%$ dark matter, and $68\%$ dark energy. The Hubble parameter is between $73$ and $68\,\unit{km/(s\,Mpc)}$. For relativity and dynamics, the relevant point is that average density is accurately critical.

## Interpretation and checks

A negative value indicates accelerated expansion, not contraction. Inferring it from distance observations requires a distance–redshift model and calibration. Check a linear scale factor: its second derivative is zero and the parameter vanishes. Check exponential expansion: the parameter is minus one. Historical estimates should remain tied to their data model rather than interpreted as direct acceleration readings.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $q_0,q$ | $1$ | Deceleration parameters | Dimensionless change in the expansion rate. |
| $\mechanical{H}_0$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Present Hubble parameter | Current fractional expansion rate. |
| $\mechanical{a}$ | — | Scale factor | Expansion scale with units set by radial convention. |
| $\mechanical{G}$ | $\unit{m^3/(kg\,s^2)}$ | Gravitational constant | Coupling in the matter-only relation. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}$ | $\unit{kg/m^3}$ | Matter density | Density producing deceleration. |
| $\mechanical{c}$ | $\unit{m/s}$ | Speed of light | Constant in curvature and cosmological-constant terms. |
| $\mechanical{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{m^{-2}}$ | Cosmological constant | Constant contributing to accelerated expansion. |
| $k$ | $1$ | Curvature parameter | Spatial-curvature sign in the stated model relations. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}_c$ | $\unit{kg/m^3}$ | Critical density | Density of the flat model, $3\mechanical{H}_0^2/(8\pi\mechanical{G})$. |
| $\mechanical{\Omega}_m,\mechanical{\Omega}_\Lambda$ | $1$ | Density fractions | Matter and cosmological-constant densities relative to critical density. |
