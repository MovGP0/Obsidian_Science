## Physical question and model

What observations describe the Universe well enough to constrain a dynamical theory? Separate redshift, flux, angular position, and spectrum from distances and densities inferred through a cosmological model. The chapter uses approximate large-scale homogeneity, not a claim that galaxies are uniformly spaced.

## Lecture account

## Abstract

We live in the permanent gravity of all the Universe’s matter and energy. All physical processes, dynamics, and the progress of time are affected by this enormous gravity. Yet its extent and matter content were unknown when our fundamental theories were completed. Thus, current theories miss or misinterpret important cosmic gravitational effects, attributing them to incorrect reasons or postulates. Quantitative knowledge of our vast Universe and its gravity, based on modern observational cosmology, can remedy this situation and establish the true foundations of dynamics and relativity. This chapter reviews the gravitational properties of the real, once ‘given’ Universe.

## Interpretation and checks

A useful evidence ledger links each inferred quantity to an instrument reading and a model assumption. Expansion, matter density, spatial curvature, and dark energy are related by field equations but are not the same observable. The proposed role of cosmic gravity in laboratory inertia is an additional inference beyond those cosmological fits.
