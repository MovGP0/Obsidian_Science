> [!TLDR]
> Uniform cosmic potentials remain spatially homogeneous in inertial motion, which the author uses to derive the local Principle of Relativity despite a detectable cosmic-frame dipole.

## Physical question and model

Can a background matter current be detected without changing local relativity? Its anisotropy can be measured by interacting with that matter or radiation. A uniform vector potential, however, does not automatically create an observable phase difference on two paths.

## Lecture account

Uniformly moving observers have indicators of motion. Measuring galaxy motion could in principle reveal a huge matter current, but is impractical as an inertial-motion reference. The CMBR dipole is the simplest observational signature, discussed in Section 8.5. Matter and its potential stay homogeneous in the moving frame; *only isotropy is broken*. The metric acquires off-diagonal term $-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$, interpreted as vector potential $\mechanical{g}_{0i}\sim\mechanical{A}_i$. The $\mechanical{g}_{00}$ potential changes to $\mechanical{\Phi}'=\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{\Phi}_u$.

*Both $\mechanical{A}_i$ and $\mechanical{\Phi}'_u$ remain homogeneous, without spatial variation.* Although velocity-dependent, they are position-independent. *No experiment, including a multipath interferometer enclosing a constant potential, detects a spatially constant potential.* Only a difference $\mechanical{\phi}_1-\mechanical{\phi}_2$ or gradient $\nabla\mechanical{\phi}$ is detectable, for example through clock differences or forces. Uniform potential dilates every clock identically, so no internal reference reveals it. A vector potential is measurable only through differences of path integrals,

$$
\int\mechanical{A}(\mechanical{x})\cdot d\mechanical{l}_1-\int\mechanical{A}(\mechanical{x})\cdot d\mechanical{l}_2.
$$

At uniform velocity, the modified potentials are time-independent and cannot be detected internally without comparison with another velocity, including rest. *No internal physical experiment can detect the frame’s inertial motion.* This derives the PoR from Cosmic Relativity’s premise of homogeneously distributed cosmic matter.

The PoR is therefore a physical consequence of the real gravitational background rather than a postulate. The author identifies this as a first physical explanation of its strict operation, akin to gauge invariance of the gravitational metric. Its validity is limited by the homogeneity of cosmic potentials, which is smoother than matter distribution itself. Any PoR violation measures departures from gravitational homogeneity.

## Interpretation and checks

For a constant potential and paths with the same endpoints, equal line-integral contributions cancel. Nonzero interference requires the relevant curl, time variation, path topology, or differing interactions. State which is present. This is the boundary check needed before treating a frame-dependent potential component as a measured force. A CMB dipole diagnoses motion relative to radiation, not by itself a preferred fundamental field law.
