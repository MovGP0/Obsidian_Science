## Lecture account

We have found that cosmic potential $\mechanical{\Phi}_u$ in the rest frame becomes the larger-magnitude $\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$ in a frame moving at absolute velocity $\mechanical{v}$. Gravity already affects clock rates according to local potential. Therefore, *clocks moving relative to the cosmic frame occupy a potential deeper by factor $\mechanical{\gamma}$ and undergo gravitational dilation relative to a cosmic-rest clock by factor $\mechanical{\lambda}_r=1/\mechanical{\gamma}$*. For a moving clock relative to one at rest,

$$
\frac{\Delta\mechanical{T}_v}{\Delta\mechanical{T}_0}=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_0}{\mechanical{\Phi}_v}=\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}. \tag{10.13}
$$

The observed motional effect is thus differential gravitational dilation due to cosmic matter. A fast muon lives longer because distant galaxies’ gravity affects its dynamics. Dilation is absolute: the twin moving more relative to the unique cosmic frame ages slower, *with or without acceleration*. Cosmic gravity is the physical cause, and acceleration has no role [8, 9].

Identifying this cause resolves problems debated for a century by physicists and philosophers. The effect has a rational, logical physical explanation [9]. Inconsistent twin-paradox accounts are no longer needed. The author regards the twin-clock problem as completely resolved through cosmic gravity and contrasts this with ambiguous STR dilation lacking a physical cause.

We will use $\mechanical{\Phi}_u$ and $\mechanical{c}^2$ interchangeably in the remaining discussion, with $\mechanical{c}$ now a maximum cosmic-frame velocity controlled by gravity rather than merely a conversion constant. The velocity-dependent potentials’ further effects will reveal new physics and answers to open dynamical and relativistic questions.

## Interpretation and checks

The ratio gives unity at rest and a slower rate at nonzero speed under the source convention. That consistency check does not establish that an absolute potential ratio is measurable. In standard gravity, clock comparisons depend on the metric and paths. Ask whether two clock mechanisms acquire the same proposed correction, and what residual would distinguish it from the standard proper-time prediction.
