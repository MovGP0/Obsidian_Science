## Physical question and model

Why do large low-pressure cyclones favour opposite rotations in the two hemispheres? The model combines pressure-gradient acceleration, Earth rotation, friction, and the scale of the flow. Coriolis deflection affects moving air; it does not supply the storm's energy.

## Lecture account

Tropical cyclones have opposite chiral patterns in the northern and southern hemispheres. Viewed from above the atmosphere, southern winds spiral clockwise and northern winds counterclockwise. This is readily included in formation and development models, but its physical origin needs care. It is usually assigned to Coriolis force, conventionally a kinematic effect from observing motion in rotating coordinates (Fig. 10.4a–b in [[10.08.03 The Coriolis Force]]).

The whole atmosphere rotates with Earth. Cyclones are therefore unlike a freely moving inertial particle, thrown stone, or oscillating pendulum seen from a rotating frame. A fictitious kinematic force does not physically act on the body, whereas real forces act on air and clouds whether viewed from Earth or a nonrotating inertial frame. Equations used for calculation do not by themselves reveal that physical origin. The author argues that a cyclone is not merely a fictitious-force example [14]: a relativistic gravitomagnetic field from noninertial motion relative to cosmic matter acts on moving air (Fig. 10.5).

The caption gives deflecting acceleration $\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g$, with $\mechanical{B}_g\approx-2\mechanical{\Omega}\sin\mechanical{\lambda}$. Rotating atmospheric mass is already in field

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=-\mechanical{\Phi}_i\frac{\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf v}(\mechanical{r})}{\mechanical{c}^2}\approx-2\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}.
$$

Its component at latitude $\mechanical{\lambda}$ is $\mechanical{\Omega}\sin\mechanical{\lambda}$. The rotational field points the same way globally, but its projection on surface area $\mechanical{\mathbf A}$, $\mechanical{\mathbf A}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g$, changes sign between hemispheres. Consider air mass $\mechanical{m}=\mechanical{\rho}\delta\mechanical{V}$ moving radially with $\mechanical{\mathbf v}(t)$, for example from the equatorial region towards northern latitudes. The frame-independent force is $\mechanical{\mathbf F}_g\approx-2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf v}(t)\times\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}\sin\mechanical{\lambda}$. It grows as air accelerates towards the depression. The author identifies this cosmic gravitomagnetic field, induced by Earth’s relative rotation in the matter-filled Universe, as cyclone chirality’s physical cause.

A similar effect may contribute to river meandering, which interested Einstein [15], although local terrain and soil make dense-liquid dynamics more complicated.

Cosmic Relativity also has atmospheric-wind implications still requiring study. Near the equator, the vertical cosmic gravitomagnetic component is small but the horizontal component is nearly $2\mechanical{\Omega}$. Equator-parallel air currents then experience vertical forces whose direction depends on wind direction. Westward winds are pressed towards Earth, while eastward winds are lifted. The author suggests this favours persistent westward trade winds or easterlies, while leaving the predominance of westerly winds for further investigation.

## Interpretation and checks

The vertical component of Earth's rotation changes sign across the equator and becomes small near it. A small rapidly driven vortex can instead be controlled by boundaries and initial conditions. These limits explain why a basin swirl is not a clean cyclone analogue. To test an added cosmic mechanism, compare a prediction beyond the usual rotating-fluid equations, with atmospheric variability included in the uncertainty.
