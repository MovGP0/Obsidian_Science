> [!TLDR]
> Cosmic Relativity derives different source-motion and observer-motion Doppler expressions using absolute motion, Galilean light propagation, and clock dilation.

## Physical question and model

How do source motion and observer motion enter a Doppler comparison? In the source model their speeds are referred to a cosmic frame and their clocks are assigned separate dilation factors. State the ray direction and frame of the emission frequency.

## Lecture account

The author obtains the Doppler shift from Galilean light propagation in the cosmic absolute frame, together with time dilation of the relevant physical clocks. See the absolute Doppler expressions in [[03.05 The Doppler Effect]]. Source motion and observer motion have different expressions because physical motion is measured relative to the cosmic frame.

For source motion,

$$
\signal{\nu}'=\signal{\nu}\frac{\sqrt{1-\mechanical{u}_s^2/\mechanical{c}^2}}{1\pm\mechanical{u}_s/\mechanical{c}}. \tag{10.34}
$$

For observer motion,

$$
\signal{\nu}'=\signal{\nu}\frac{1\pm\mechanical{u}_o/\mechanical{c}}{\sqrt{1-\mechanical{u}_o^2/\mechanical{c}^2}}. \tag{10.35}
$$

The author states that both agree with laboratory and astrophysical experiments. He also identifies the CMB temperature anisotropy, from the Planck spectrum in a frame moving relative to cosmic matter, as evidence of the absolute Doppler effect. See [[08.05 The Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation]], Fig. 8.6. Cosmic Relativity gives

$$
\thermal{T}'(\mechanical{u}_o)=\thermal{T}_0\frac{1+\mechanical{u}_o/\mechanical{c}}{\sqrt{1-\mechanical{u}_o^2/\mechanical{c}^2}}. \tag{10.36}
$$

The author states that the radiation anisotropy matches the anisotropy of the cosmic matter current and spatial metric, and interprets this as an explicit violation of STR Lorentz symmetry.

## Interpretation and checks

Frequency ratios must return to one when source and receiver are comoving under the stated conditions. A moving thermal radiation bath produces a direction-dependent spectrum, so a directional temperature formula is not a universal temperature transformation. Compare collinear receding and approaching limits before extending to arbitrary angles. The existence of a CMB dipole does not distinguish all preferred-frame interpretations from a Lorentz-covariant radiation calculation.
