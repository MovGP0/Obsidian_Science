## Physical question and model

What does the imagined tiny net charge teach about a long-range interaction? The example is explicitly hypothetical. A quantitative version requires the charge distribution, screening, boundary conditions, and how the test system is prepared.

## Lecture account

Imagine a proton–electron charge asymmetry below one part in $10^{40}$, making atoms slightly charged. It would be too small for laboratory detection. When electrodynamics developed rapidly, incomplete cosmology left universal matter content unknown. Such asymmetry in then-known matter—the Solar System, Milky Way, nearby nebulae, and distant stars—would not alter observed electrodynamics. But the real Universe contains vastly more matter than its pioneers knew. A tiny asymmetry would make it a huge charge cloud surrounding every observable electrodynamic phenomenon.

A uniform charge cloud creates a uniform potential, approximately $\electric{\phi}_e\approx3\electric{Q}/(4\pi\electric{\epsilon}_0\mechanical{R})$. At mean mass density $10^{-27}\,\unit{kg/m^3}$, the hypothetical charge density is $\electric{q}\approx10^{-59}\,\unit{C/m^3}$. It seems negligible, but a cosmic size of $10^{26}\,\unit{m}$ gives a potential around $10{,}000\,\unit{V}$. With no spatial gradient, it exerts no force. It is also undetectable in inertial charged-body motion. A moving frame acquires velocity-dependent vector potential $\magnetic{\mathbf A}=\mechanical{\mathbf v}\electric{\phi}/\mechanical{c}$, but local electric and magnetic sources still suffice in the equations and Lorentz force. Uniform motion through a huge spherical charge distribution creates only a locally undetectable constant vector potential.

Acceleration changes this: changing velocity makes the vector potential time-dependent. An unexplained force opposes acceleration, proportional to charge, as if a pseudo-electric field acted. Rotating charges experience pseudo-magnetic forces; a magnetic moment in rotation experiences an unexplained torque. *Charge-proportional electromagnetic pseudo-forces would need postulation.* There would be electromagnetic inertia in addition to ordinary mass inertia. Newton might attribute these to motion relative to special absolute space. A Machian account would seek charge interaction with universal matter instead of fictitious forces.

We see no such fictitious electromagnetic forces because universal matter is essentially neutral. Yet objections arise to identifying *observed inertial ‘fictitious’ forces with the gravitationally charged Universe: its matter and energy are not gravitationally neutral*. Universal matter’s enormous quantity compensates for the small gravitational constant.

This warns against ignoring universal matter’s gravity in fundamental dynamics. Chapter 1 estimated cosmic potential from observed mean gravitating density and found that critical-density matter gives a Newtonian potential numerically equal to squared light speed, $\mechanical{\Phi}_U\simeq\mechanical{c}^2$. This should have suggested a gravitational origin for relativity. It motivates the phenomenological factor $(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{\Phi}_U)^{1/2}$ rather than $(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)^{1/2}$ and light speed (Fig. 10.2).

Einstein speculated around 1924 that atoms might carry slight charge and inspired experiments [5, 6], seeking a link between Earth’s and other bodies’ magnetism and rotation of their large masses. Much later, despite experimental constraints, H. Bondi used the idea to explain cosmic expansion [7]. With hindsight, it is surprising that the gravitational consequences of vast electrically neutral cosmic matter were overlooked, with mass as gravity’s charge.

The electromagnetic analogy and known gravitating matter content suggest that conventional pseudo-forces are gravitational. Stopping there is incomplete, despite earlier rejection of this calculation without empirical or logical objection. *A cosmic gravitational framework with a master frame and real absolute motion requires compatible Galilean space, time, and light propagation.* Invariant relative light speed has no place in it. This opposes current relativity’s foundations, making Cosmic Relativity a complete reconstruction of dynamics and relativity. We proceed to its corrected physics in the gravitationally charged Universe.

## Interpretation and checks

Adding more distant matter does not automatically give a measurable local force: symmetry can cancel the field, while a constant potential needs a reference and coupling. This is the useful part of the analogy with cosmic gravity. Do not transfer electromagnetic signs or screening directly to a gravitational theory. Ask which gauge-independent local measurement would respond to the proposed background.
