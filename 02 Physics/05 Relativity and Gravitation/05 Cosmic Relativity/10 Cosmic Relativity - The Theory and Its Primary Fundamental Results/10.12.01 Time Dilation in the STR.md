---
title: "Time Dilation in the STR"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The author criticises STR for describing motional time dilation kinematically, without identifying an interaction that changes a physical clock's rate.

## Physical question and model

Does time dilation require a local force on a uniformly moving ideal clock? Standard special relativity relates proper time on a worldline to coordinate time without adding a drag or ageing force. The same clock obeys its local rest-frame dynamics.

## Lecture account

The author describes two common responses to time dilation from relative motion in STR. One is immediate acceptance of the theory's hypotheses and Lorentz transformations. This response accepts that a relatively moving clock runs more slowly because of the transformations, without asking for a deeper physical reason. Yet a change in the rate of a physical process usually requires an interaction. The other response is sustained surprise and hesitation: acceptance of a working theory as provisional, without a confident physical explanation.

The author argues that STR provides no physical cause for time dilation. It describes a kinematic effect relative to an arbitrarily chosen rest frame rather than a physical change in the clocks. Two clocks at relative rest have no relative time dilation. STR also gives no relativistic change in the dimensions or clock durations of bodies at relative rest.

He contrasts this with an absolute-frame theory, where relativistic changes are physical effects of an interaction involving that frame. A clock at rest in a frame that moves through the preferred frame has nonzero time dilation. However, this common dilation cannot be measured by comparing clocks within the moving frame, because every reference clock has the same change.

## Interpretation and checks

A physical explanation can concern spacetime symmetry rather than an extra environment interaction. The absence of a proposed microscopic preferred-frame force does not make the proper-time calculation incomplete as a prediction. Compare the same two endpoints and check a boosted description. Universal agreement of ideal clocks is an empirical assumption with a domain, not a statement that every practical clock is immune to acceleration or fields.
