> [!TLDR]
> The author distinguishes potential-gradient gravity from time-dependent cosmic gravitomagnetism and interprets geodesic motion as their local balance.

## Physical question and model

What connects a geodesic equation with a force-balance picture? A geodesic is defined using an affine parameter, usually proper time for a massive test body. Connection coefficients encode the chosen coordinates; curvature determines relative tidal effects.

## Lecture account

The familiar Newtonian attraction between massive bodies is proportional to the gradient of gravitational potential:

$$
\mechanical{F}_g=-\mechanical{m}\nabla\mechanical{\phi}.
$$

The same potential $\mechanical{\phi}$, related to metric component $\mechanical{g}_{00}$, controls the rate of a clock in a gravitational field. This force accelerates massive particles from rest, such as a stone or satellite falling towards Earth.

In Cosmic Relativity, familiar inertial forces are also gravitational, but differ from gradient-type gravity. They arise from time-dependent cosmic gravitomagnetic potentials caused by accelerated relative motion. The rate of change of $\mechanical{g}_{0i}$ produces the gravity-like force felt in an accelerating lift. The spatial variation of $\mechanical{g}_{00}$ and the temporal variation of $\mechanical{g}_{0i}$ have different physical content and effects. Both couple to gravitational mass, the only charge of gravity.

The combination of local gradient gravity and cosmic gravitomagnetic gravity determines dynamics in moving frames. The author claims to derive features that other theories postulate. One example is the geodesic principle, a GTR postulate that generalises Newton's first law. Freely falling particles follow spacetime geodesics:

$$
\frac{d^2\mechanical{x}^{\alpha}}{d\mechanical{s}^2}+\mechanical{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}\frac{d\mechanical{x}^{\mu}}{d\mechanical{s}}\frac{d\mechanical{x}^{\nu}}{d\mechanical{s}}=0. \tag{10.25}
$$

In a freely falling frame, all Christoffel symbols $\mechanical{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}$ vanish locally. The equation reduces to inertial motion, $\ddot{\mechanical{x}}=0$. The geodesic equation follows from the postulate that force-free inertial motion obeys this relation.

Cosmic Relativity instead claims to derive the geodesic principle. Local gravity is balanced by the cosmic gravitational field induced by accelerated motion:

$$
\mechanical{g}_a=-\frac{d\mechanical{g}_{0i}}{dt}=-\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}.
$$

In the author's description, the local Christoffel contribution is exactly balanced by the time derivative of the mixed metric component. Two distinct real fields balance; an existing field does not vanish.

> [!note]
> The source expression is retained. With dimensionless $\mechanical{g}_{0i}$, its time derivative has unit $\unit{s^{-1}}$, whereas $d\mechanical{v}/dt$ has unit $\unit{m/s^2}$. A consistent coordinate normalisation or a factor of $\mechanical{c}$ is required to compare these terms as accelerations.

## Interpretation and checks

A local cancellation of connection terms does not remove curvature. The source's metric potential and velocity have different units unless the coordinate normalisation is fixed, so equating their time derivatives requires the missing conversion factors. Check an unforced straight path written in curvilinear coordinates: connection terms can be nonzero while no new physical interaction has been added.
