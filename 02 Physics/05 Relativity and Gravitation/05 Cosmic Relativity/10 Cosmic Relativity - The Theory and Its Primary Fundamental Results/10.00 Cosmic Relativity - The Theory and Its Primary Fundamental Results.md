## Lecture account

## Abstract

This chapter systematically develops the primary results of Cosmic Relativity’s cosmic gravitational paradigm. All dynamics and relativity are consequences of universal matter and energy’s gravity. Since the Universe is unique, no additional hypotheses or postulated principles are used. Derived results include the Principle of Relativity, Newton’s motion law, inertia’s origin, gravitational centrifugal and Coriolis forces, WEP and universal free fall, EEP, relative dilation in accelerated frames, and moving-clock dilation as cosmic gravitational dilation. Spin’s gravitational significance and its role in cosmic gravity are also mentioned.

## Introduction

The theory begins with the observation that our Universe is filled with matter and energy, homogeneous and isotropic when averaged over large scales, with mean matter density close to critical. The Cosmological Principle postulates homogeneity and isotropy everywhere; applied at large scales, this agrees with observations. Among possible Universes, ours is simple. Critical density separates open and closed evolution in GTR language. Modern measurements find density close to critical to about $3\%$, giving a spatially flat, Euclidean Universe. This observationally justified input is all that is needed for dynamics and relativity from universal matter–energy’s gravitational action. The matter frame is then a preferred absolute frame, and universal cosmic time, encoded for example by CMBR temperature, is absolute time.

This is presented as the only relativity theory consistent with the Universe’s vast matter content. There are no adjustable parameters: gravity’s nature and constant are measured experimentally. There is no freedom for more postulates because cosmic gravity must explain dynamics and relativistic effects.

Chapter 1 outlined the main results. Here we explain the gravitational paradigm [1–4], its physical and logical basis, and fundamental derivations. Later chapters explore its full implications.
