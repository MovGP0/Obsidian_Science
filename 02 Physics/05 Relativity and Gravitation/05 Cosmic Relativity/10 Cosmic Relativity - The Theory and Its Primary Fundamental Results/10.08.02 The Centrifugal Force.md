> [!TLDR]
> The author interprets centrifugal force as the cosmic gravitomagnetic reaction to a change in velocity direction.

## Physical question and model

Why does a rotating description contain an outward centrifugal term? For a point fixed in a rotating frame, its inertial-frame trajectory has inward centripetal acceleration. Moving that acceleration term to the force side gives the outward apparent term.

## Lecture account

The derived reactive cosmic force for a transverse change of uniform velocity is

$$
\mechanical{F}_g=-\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{m}\frac{d\mechanical{v}}{dt}. \tag{10.20}
$$

To leading order, a transverse change alters direction $\hat{\mathbf v}$. The source gives its transverse rate as $K\mechanical{v}=\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{r}$, with local path curvature $K$. It writes the reaction as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{F}_g&=-\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}\frac{d\hat{\mathbf v}}{dt}\\
&=-\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\mechanical{\mathbf a}_{\perp}\\
&=-\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf v}}{\mechanical{r}}=\frac{\mechanical{v}^2}{\mechanical{r}}.
\end{aligned} \tag{10.21}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (10.21) retains the source’s cross-product chain. A velocity crossed with itself is zero, while the final $\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{r}$ is an acceleration rather than a force. The notation for the perpendicular factor also requires clarification to reconcile the intermediate dimensions.

Centrifugal force is the same type of reaction that gave Newton’s law, now for transverse rather than longitudinal acceleration. In both cases, the changing gravitomagnetic potential $\mechanical{g}_{0i}=-\mechanical{v}_i/\mechanical{c}$, tied to relative cosmic matter current, produces the reaction.

## Interpretation and checks

Its magnitude is mass times speed squared divided by radius for circular motion. The source's equation (10.21) contains a cross product of parallel velocity vectors and ends with an acceleration rather than a force. That chain cannot be used literally: the parallel cross product is zero and the mass factor is missing at the end. Derive the circular acceleration from the changing velocity direction before interpreting its cause.
