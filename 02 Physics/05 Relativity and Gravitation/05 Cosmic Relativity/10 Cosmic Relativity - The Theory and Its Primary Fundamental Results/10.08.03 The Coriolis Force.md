> [!TLDR]
> The author develops the Coriolis force as interaction of a particle’s mass current with the circulating cosmic matter current seen in a rotating frame.

## Physical question and model

What determines the direction of a Coriolis term? Define the rotation vector of the laboratory relative to an inertial frame and the particle velocity relative to the laboratory. The cosmic matter current has the opposite rotational sense, so its vector cannot be interchanged without a sign change.

## Lecture account

The relevant first-principles elements are relative cosmic matter and energy currents and the particle’s current at local velocity $\mechanical{u}(t)$. *This is gravitational interaction between two currents.* Ampère’s experiments provide an electromagnetic analogy: similarly directed currents attract with force proportional to $\electric{i}_1\electric{i}_2$, with particle current $\electric{i}=\electric{q}\mechanical{u}$.

Mass currents interact similarly in essential respects, but gravity between masses is always attractive, unlike repulsion between equal-sign electric charges. Correspondingly, *parallel mass currents repel*, with force proportional to their product. The mass current is $\mechanical{I}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{u}(t)$. Strong-current interaction is nonlinear, as gravity generally is. The GTR already describes isolated moving or spinning masses, with Lense–Thirring precession as an example [13]. Cosmic currents create an additional effect outside that usual treatment. A particle moving locally within a frame of absolute velocity $\mechanical{v}$ experiences current–current gravity, but homogeneous matter gives no net force at constant velocity.

The vector potential $\mechanical{g}_{0i}\equiv\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g$ can have nonzero spatial curl. Specifically, $\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g=-\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{\Phi}_u\mechanical{\mathbf v}/\mechanical{c}$. In a rotating frame, cosmic matter forms a circulating current (Fig. 10.3), time-dependent only when angular frequency changes. At constant rotation, $\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g\ne0$ but $\partial\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g/\partial t=0$. There is no force on a stationary particle. A moving particle at $\mechanical{\mathbf u}$ is a gravitational current $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf u}$. It interacts with relativistic field $\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g$:

$$
\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times(\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g)=-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\frac{\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{\Phi}_u}{\mechanical{c}^2}(\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf v})=-2\mechanical{\gamma}\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}. \tag{10.22}
$$

The gravitational mass–potential product has been replaced by inertia $\mechanical{m}_i$. Following convention, this is a ‘gravitomagnetic force’ [13]. At modest rotation rates, $\mechanical{\mathbf F}=-2\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}$, the Coriolis force. It acts perpendicular to velocity in the rotation plane, deflecting the path without changing energy because $\mechanical{\mathbf F}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf u}=0$. It is analogous to electromagnetic Lorentz force $\mechanical{\mathbf F}_L=\electric{q}\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\magnetic{\mathbf B}$.

The author regards this as a full solution of fictitious inertial forces, ending debates from Newton’s time that each new development revived. Einstein could have found it by following Mach, but theoretical preferences prevented this; another century passed before Cosmic Relativity supplied the claimed solution.

An important distinction is needed. An *apparent transverse deflection* occurs when straight inertial motion is viewed in rotating coordinates, even in empty gravity-free space. Its kinematic acceleration is $\mechanical{\mathbf a}_k=-2\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}$. The path stays straight externally although curved in rotating coordinates (Fig. 10.4a–b). Foucault pendulums and a straight line drawn on rotating paper illustrate this coordinate effect.

This is consistent with the proposed physical current interaction. *Its real manifestation occurs when a body corotates with the frame and also moves radially* (Fig. 10.4c), as atmospheric winds do. Without radial motion, a particle rests in rotating coordinates, temporarily ignoring its small centrifugal force. Radial motion introduces real $\mechanical{\mathbf F}=-2\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\mathbf u}\times\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}$. The deflection is absolute and identical in rotating and nonrotating descriptions. A purely kinematic treatment is insufficient for several physical situations, including tropical cyclones.

## Interpretation and checks

The standard rotating-frame term is minus twice mass times the cross product of lab rotation with particle velocity. It vanishes for motion parallel to the rotation axis and reverses when either transverse velocity or rotation reverses. Those are direct sign checks for the proposed cosmic-current expression. Agreement with this kinematic pattern does not alone measure the source of inertia.
