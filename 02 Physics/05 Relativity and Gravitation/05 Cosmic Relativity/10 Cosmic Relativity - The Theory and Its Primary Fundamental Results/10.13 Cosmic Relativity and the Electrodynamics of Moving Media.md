## Physical question and model

Can one theory account for several moving-medium optical effects? Compare Doppler frequency, ray direction, phase drag, and induced voltage separately. Each uses different material response and geometry.

## Lecture account

Puzzles and experiments in electrodynamics and optics motivated and supported the development of relativity. Einstein's STR then supplied a more general framework. The author presents Cosmic Relativity, with the gravity of the encompassing Universe, as the most general account of relativistic effects.

He argues that the original electrodynamic problems are also solved within Cosmic Relativity, with physical explanations in an absolute gravitational frame. The following sections briefly revisit the classical problems discussed in [[03.00 Electrodynamics, Light and Relativity|Chapter 3]] and [[04.00 The Special Theory of Relativity and Its Empirical Foundations|Chapter 4]].

## Interpretation and checks

A successful unifying explanation must state how the same coupling appears in all those observables without silently changing clock or field conventions. Recover vacuum and zero-medium-motion limits. Where standard relativity gives the same first-order expression, that agreement alone does not select a cosmic mechanism. Look for a higher-order or environmental difference with a realistic uncertainty budget.
