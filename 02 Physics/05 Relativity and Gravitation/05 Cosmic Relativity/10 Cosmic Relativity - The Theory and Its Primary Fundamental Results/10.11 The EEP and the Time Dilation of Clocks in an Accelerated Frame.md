> [!TLDR]
> Cosmic Relativity predicts that common acceleration alone produces no relative time dilation between spatially separated clocks, contrary to the source's account of EEP.

## Physical question and model

How do separated clocks compare in an accelerating laboratory? In the weak, approximately uniform-field limit, the fractional rate difference scales as acceleration times separation divided by light speed squared. Specify stationary positions in that lab and the sign of increasing height.

## Lecture account

Einstein's early derivation of relative time dilation between clocks in a uniform gravitational field used the Doppler shift of radiation in a uniformly accelerated frame. See [[06.08 The First Applications of the EEP - Prelude to the GTR]]. This applied EEP: the postulated equivalence between uniform acceleration and a uniform gravitational field. EEP is widely used to derive physical results in weak gravitational fields.

For clocks C1 and C2 at positions $\mechanical{x}_1$ and $\mechanical{x}_2$ in a uniform field $\mechanical{\mathbf g}=-\nabla\mechanical{\phi}$, the source gives

$$
\Delta T=\frac{T\left(\mechanical{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)-\mechanical{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_2)\right)}{\mechanical{c}^2}.
$$

The clock at the deeper potential, with larger potential magnitude, runs more slowly. For small separation $\mechanical{d}=\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_2$, the approximate expression is

$$
\Delta T=\frac{T\mechanical{g}\mechanical{d}}{\mechanical{c}^2}.
$$

Within GTR, local equivalence between an accelerated frame and gravity is total. The author instead describes relativistically induced cosmic gravitational potentials. The induced field in an accelerated frame differs fundamentally from familiar potential-gradient gravity. Its effects need not be identical. This raises the question of relative time dilation between separated clocks in an accelerated frame.

The author predicts no relative time dilation caused by their common acceleration. He therefore claims an EEP violation for clock rates, despite genuine gravitational time dilation. He presents this as a crucial test of Cosmic Relativity. Such experiments are difficult because a sufficiently large common acceleration must be sustained for sufficiently long. Experimental tests are discussed in the next chapter.

The argument is that the accelerated-frame potentials $\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{\Phi}_u$ and $-\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{v}\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}$ depend on time but have no spatial gradient. *At each instant, the potentials remain homogeneous throughout the accelerating frame.* Clocks at different positions thus experience identical potentials and identical time dilations. The author concludes that they have no relative time dilation [9].

> [!note]
> The source's prediction is that acceleration alone causes no relative time dilation between two spatially separated clocks, although a genuine gravitational field does cause it. This is the claimed limit of EEP in this account.

## Interpretation and checks

The effect vanishes as the separation tends to zero, even if the lab has nonzero proper acceleration. A finite rigid accelerating laboratory cannot have the same proper acceleration at every position, so extend the simple formula cautiously. This standard noninertial-clock result can be derived in flat spacetime; a matching cosmic interpretation needs an additional measurable distinction.
