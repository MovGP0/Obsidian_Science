---
title: "The Equivalence Principle and Universality of Free Fall"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The author argues that universality of free fall can hold even with material-dependent gravitational coupling because the same coupling also determines inertia.

## Physical question and model

Can universal trajectories coexist with nonstandard definitions of mass? If a model allows composition-dependent gravitational couplings in both forcing and inertia, their ratio can cancel. This is a statement about the parameterisation as well as the observable.

## Lecture account

The equivalence principle and experimental evidence for universality of free fall (UFF) must be distinguished. WEP implies UFF in the absence of an additional composition-dependent force. The converse does not follow logically or physically. UFF can remain valid even if WEP fails. Cosmic Relativity is presented as an example of this possibility.

Consider hypothetical composition-dependent gravity, with material-dependent coupling $\mechanical{G}_n$. Two objects of different materials have gravitational masses $\mechanical{m}_1$ and $\mechanical{m}_2$. In Earth's field, they experience forces $\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{g}_1$ and $\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}_2$, even if their masses are equal:

$$
\mechanical{m}_n\mechanical{g}_n=\frac{\mechanical{G}_n\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}_n}{\mechanical{R}^2}. \tag{10.26}
$$

Their accelerations are

$$
\mechanical{a}_1=\frac{\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{g}_1}{\mechanical{m}_{i(1)}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mechanical{a}_2=\frac{\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{g}_2}{\mechanical{m}_{i(2)}}. \tag{10.27}
$$

Here $\mechanical{m}_{i(n)}$ denotes inertial mass. The product $\mechanical{m}_n\mechanical{g}_n$ contains the composition-dependent coupling. In the conventional account, inertial mass is independent of gravitational interaction. Different couplings then give different accelerations, which violate UFF. This agrees with the initial assumption of different gravitational couplings for different materials.

The author claims a different result from Cosmic Relativity's account of inertia and WEP: UFF remains valid even if gravitational coupling depends on material.

This follows because inertial mass is gravitational mass multiplied by the cosmic gravitational potential. That potential already contains the hypothetical material-dependent coupling. The source writes

$$
\mechanical{m}_{i(1)}=\mechanical{k}\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{G}_1\quad\text{and}\quad\mechanical{m}_{i(2)}=\mechanical{k}\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{G}_2. \tag{10.28}
$$

The material-independent proportionality constant $\mechanical{k}$ represents the cosmic matter-energy density and other numerical factors. The source states that $\mechanical{k}\mechanical{G}=1$. Thus

$$
\mechanical{a}_1=\frac{\mechanical{G}_1\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}_1}{\mechanical{R}^2\mechanical{k}\mechanical{m}_1\mechanical{G}_1}=\frac{\mechanical{M}}{\mechanical{R}^2\mechanical{k}}=\frac{\mechanical{G}_2\mechanical{M}\mechanical{m}_2}{\mechanical{R}^2\mechanical{k}\mechanical{m}_2\mechanical{G}_2}=\mechanical{a}_2. \tag{10.29}
$$

The material-dependent coupling cancels. The author concludes that *UFF remains valid even with a material-dependent gravitational coupling*.

> [!note]
> Equation (10.28) uses the same symbol $\mechanical{m}_g$ for both bodies. Equation (10.29) instead uses their respective gravitational masses $\mechanical{m}_1$ and $\mechanical{m}_2$. These source symbols are retained.

## Interpretation and checks

State the operational definition of the weak principle before calling such a cancellation a violation of it. Many experimental statements define WEP through universal test-body free fall, so a mere renamed mass ratio is not a distinguishable failure. Require an independent measurement of one coupling. The source's equation (10.28) also changes mass labels; keep each body's own mass in the ratio before performing the cancellation.
