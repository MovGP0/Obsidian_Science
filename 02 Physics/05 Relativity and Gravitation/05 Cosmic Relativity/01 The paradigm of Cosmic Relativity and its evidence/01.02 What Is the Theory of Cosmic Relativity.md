## Physical question and model

What extra explanation does Cosmic Relativity seek beyond a kinematic formula? Its goal is a physical origin for inertia, time dilation, and other motion-dependent effects. Treat the gravitational coupling to the whole Universe as the proposed model, with the cosmic distribution and the local measuring apparatus both specified.

## Lecture account

The theory of Cosmic Relativity asserts and establishes, with calculations and experimental evidence, that the laws of dynamics as well as all the motional relativistic effects that alter the space-time intervals are entirely gravitational effects, due to the matter-energy in the Universe [2]. Thus, the paradigm is based on the undeniable observed fact that our Universe is filled with matter and energy, which are the sources of gravity. The theory has no more postulates; all its results have to consistently follow from this single fact, as observed. Cosmic Relativity provides both the framework and the physical reason for understanding deep principles like the Principle of Relativity and the Principle of Equivalence. The laws of motion (Newton's law in relativistic form, for example), the core relativistic effects like time dilation and length contraction, and the equivalence of inertia and mass are directly derived as the cosmic gravitational effects, on physical entities (matter) moving relative to the average rest frame of the matter-filled Universe. *Cosmic Relativity is presented as the only theory of relativity that is consistent with both cosmological evidence as well as all the laboratory experiments*. The factual Universe and its dynamical nature provide both an absolute spatial reference for motion and an absolute universal time. Of course, Cosmic Relativity subsumes and formalises Ernst Mach's insightful speculation on the origin of Newton's inertial fictitious forces [5]. Cosmic Relativity provides a necessary completion to the General Theory of Relativity, by integrating the Universe as the absolute and privileged gravitational frame for dynamics. The modified and completed GTR encompasses the full scope and predictions of a relativistic theory of gravity in this matter-filled Universe. Cosmic Relativity remedies the gap in Einstein's theory, of not incorporating Mach's principle, in spite of Einstein's conviction of its importance for the GTR [6, 7]. But the Special Theory of Relativity cannot be a theory consistent with the factual situation of the absolute frame of the matter-filled Universe. It is clarified with numerous demonstrations that the STR cannot be a basis for a theory of relativistic gravity that also incorporates Mach's principle. This explains why Einstein's well-motivated and reasoned attempt, as well as several other later attempts, to incorporate the Machian idea into a theory of gravitation failed. Cosmic Relativity succeeds in this task and provides new predictions that have been now verified experimentally. As to any question concerning the fate of the STR, experimental results alone should be the final judge, and a wealth of experimental evidence is the anchor point in the work presented here.

Cosmic Relativity turns out to be the most general framework for relativistic physics in the factual Universe, starting with the cosmos ‘as it is’ and then incorporating the complete physics of General Relativity, rather than trying to enclose and fit new physics into the GTR. *A consistent Machian General Relativity necessarily has the gravitational frame of the Universe as a preferred master frame*. Therefore, the conventional postulate of ‘general coordinate invariance’ is broken when the velocity transformations are considered. Only such a theory is consistent with the observed fact that there is no general coordinate invariance in the factual Universe. In other words, all real velocity-dependent physical effects, like time dilation and length contraction, depend on the ‘absolute’ velocity relative to the cosmic frame, and they can be traced directly to the cosmic gravitational potentials modified by motion. The well known gap in the classical physics of dynamics, of not being able to trace the source of inertial pseudo-forces like the centrifugal force and the Coriolis force, is brought to a closure with the complete understanding of the origin of inertia and inertial forces.

*Cosmic Relativity's most important prediction, characteristic of a theory of relativity with an absolute preferred frame, is that the relative velocity of light is Galilean to first order in $\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$, instead of being an invariant constant. Remarkably, this all-important result is verified in a novel experiment* [2, 3]. The Galilean relative velocity is a direct indication of an absolute determining frame for the propagation of light; in all other frames that move relative to such a master frame, the relative velocity of light is $\mechanical{c}'=\mechanical{c}-\mechanical{v}$. Thus, the ‘light hypothesis’ on which the STR is built is experimentally falsified. This changes the whole fundamental premise of dynamics and relativity. This may be surprising to anybody who believes that the invariance of the relative speed of light is already a proven fact by experiments like the Michelson-Morley test. But it is sufficient to say at this stage that the M-M experiment tested the Principle of Relativity by monitoring the *two-way propagation of light* and did not test the *invariance of the one-way relative speed of light*. It is in fact well known, as asserted by both Michelson and Einstein, in several occasions, that the M-M experiment or similar two-way experiments are consistent with both Einstein's STR and the ether based relativity theory of Lorentz and Poincaré [8]. In the STR, the null result of M-M experiment follows from its fundamental hypothesis that the relative velocity of light is an invariant constant.

In contrast, in the Lorentz-Poincaré theory, the null result follows from the compensation of the delay due to the Galilean (variable) nature of the relative velocity of light by the physical length contraction during absolute motion. In Cosmic Relativity, the length contraction and time dilation are cosmic gravitational effects that mask the anisotropy due to the Galilean propagation of light. So, Cosmic Relativity agrees completely with the null result of M-M experiment. *Most importantly, the theory of Cosmic Relativity not only agrees fully with all relevant experimental evidence to date, but also covers several effects that can be explained only within the new paradigm of cosmic gravity as the determining factor of dynamics*. In particular, the theory is unambiguous and complete in its answers to the questions that naturally arise in the context of the apparent invariance of the fundamental equations governing particles and fields. Briefly put, there is not a single empirical, theoretical or logical inconsistency in its structure, predictions or comparisons with evidence. This verifiable feature is the strength of the gravitational paradigm of Cosmic Relativity.

## Interpretation and checks

Separate explanatory ambition from derivation. To predict a clock rate, the model needs an equation of motion or a clock Hamiltonian and a rule connecting its phase to a reading. To predict inertia, it needs a force law and an identified frame. Check the limit of uniform rest in the proposed cosmic frame. Then ask whether its predictions depart from a standard proper-time calculation.
