## Lecture account

The calculation of the relevant gravitational effects due to the matter-energy in the Universe can also be done in the language of a ‘metric’ and its spatial and temporal derivatives, familiar in the theories of relativity. Of course, this does not change the general features of the gravitational effects. Since the matter in the Universe is distributed approximately uniformly on a large scale, without appreciable coherent motions on such scales, the cosmic gravitational potential has nearly the same value everywhere in the Universe. Then, the metric that describes the spatial and temporal relation in the Universe also is homogeneous. The small variations due to an isolated galaxy or star, in their vicinity, are less than a part in a thousand to a million. *As is well known in fundamental physics, a potential without a spatial or temporal variation cannot be detected by any physical means*. Since the physical changes due to a uniform gravitational potential are identical in every physical system experiencing the potential, there is no way to compare with a reference system and detect the physical effects. For example, all clocks in a similar gravitational environment and interaction potential will suffer from identical gravitational time dilation, making it impossible to detect the time dilation using any one of the clocks as a reference clock. *The fact that there are no locally measurable physical effects of a constant potential field turns out to be the true content of the Principle of Relativity (PoR)*. Thus, the deepest principle underlying dynamics is a consequence of the gravitational Universe. The PoR is a reflection of the observed homogeneity of the matter-energy distribution and its gravitational potentials in the Universe.

One drastic and necessary conclusion is that the Lorentz transformations (LT) of relativity in the empty Minkowski space cannot be a valid mathematical structure in the matter-filled space of the factual Universe. The LT at the velocity $\mechanical{v}$ preserve the isotropy of the empty Minkowski space, and the isotropic metric remains invariant. But, a simple velocity boost in the factual Universe creates an anisotropic space, due to the matter currents (Fig. 1.3). It is clear that the Lorentz transformations (LT) and the invariant Minkowski space-time structure of the empty space are not consistent with the space of the matter-filled Universe. This is the same physically obvious statement that the STR in the empty space is not consistent with the factual matter-filled Universe. Surprisingly, the Galilean transformations (GT) in the absolute space correctly give us, along with the observed anisotropy of space with matter, the most important relativistic feature of motion—time dilation [2]. This ensures the total consistency of the GT with all empirical features of relativity. This fact of great importance is what I demonstrate next.

The factual Universe with its very slow time evolution of expansion can be described with the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) line element. Using the observed feature that the spatial curvature is nearly zero, the metric, for an observer at rest relative to the average distribution of matter, is

$$
ds^2=-\mechanical{c}^2dt^2+a^2(t)(d\mechanical{x}^2+d\mechanical{y}^2+d\mechanical{z}^2). \tag{1.2}
$$

The metric of the factual Universe is simple, $g_{00}=-\mechanical{c}^2$ and $g_{ij}=a^2(t)\delta_{ij}$. The scale factor of the slow expansion is $a(t)\simeq1+Ht=1+t/\tau$, where $\tau=1/H$ is of the order of the age of the Universe. Then the line element for an observer at ‘absolute’ rest is

$$
ds^2=-\mechanical{c}^2dt^2+(1+Hdt)^2(d\mechanical{x}^2+d\mechanical{y}^2+d\mechanical{z}^2). \tag{1.3}
$$

> [!note] Source notation
> Equation (1.3) uses $1+Hdt$, whereas the preceding scale factor uses $1+Ht$. This difference is present in the source and is retained here.

*Moving at a uniform velocity $\mechanical{v}$ through the Universe makes the space and the metric anisotropic, but the homogeneity is preserved*. Since the LT cannot produce the observed anisotropy, we employ the Galilean transformations for the moving frame, $\mechanical{x}'=\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}t$ and $t'=t$. Choosing the direction of motion along the x-axis, at the velocity $\mechanical{v}(t)$, we have the transformed line element (the y and z coordinates are suppressed for brevity),

$$
ds'^2=-\mechanical{c}^2dt'^2+(1+Hdt')^2d\mechanical{x}'^2\simeq-\mechanical{c}^2dt^2+(1+2Hdt)d\mechanical{x}'^2 \tag{1.4}
$$

$$
=-\mechanical{c}^2dt^2+(1+2Hdt)(d\mechanical{x}^2+\mechanical{v}^2dt^2-2\mechanical{v}\,d\mechanical{x}\,dt). \tag{1.5}
$$

Since $H_0\approx1/T_u$, the term $Hdt\approx dt/T_u$ can be ignored when we consider human time scales (this is discussed in more detail in [[10.05 The Gravitational Foundation of Cosmic Relativity|Chapter 10]]). Then the line element is

$$
ds'^2=-\mechanical{c}^2(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)dt^2-2\mechanical{v}\,d\mechanical{x}\,dt+d\mechanical{x}^2. \tag{1.6}
$$

We get the transformed metric as $g'_{00}=-(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)\mechanical{c}^2$, and $g'_{01}=g'_{10}=-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$. It is very impressive to see that the GT have given us the crucial time dilation factor! Also, the anisotropy terms are present, agreeing with the observation of the ‘dipole anisotropy’ of the CMBR, in the factual Universe. The coefficient of the last term, $2Hdt$, is irrelevant for laboratory physics, because it is essentially $2dt/T_u$ where $T_u$ is the age of the Universe.

Summarizing, the Galilean transformations, to a frame moving with the velocity $\mechanical{v}$ relative to the master cosmic frame, transform the metric as

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
g_{00}=-\mechanical{c}^2 & g_{01}=0\\
g_{10}=0 & g_{11}=1
\end{bmatrix}
\xrightarrow{GT(\mechanical{v})}
\begin{bmatrix}
-\mechanical{c}^2(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2) & -\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}\\
-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c} & 1
\end{bmatrix}. \tag{1.7}
$$

The physical duration of time, as measured by a clock that is moving through the Universe at the arbitrary absolute velocity $\mechanical{v}(t)$, is $\sqrt{-g'_{00}}\,dt'=(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)^{1/2}dt$. The GT indeed contain the physics of time dilation with the *absolute velocity, or the velocity relative to the cosmic frame*. The precise measure of the absolute motion is given by the dipole anisotropy of the CMBR, which is easily measured. Hence, there is no ambiguity about which frames of reference are really moving and which are at rest. Therefore, there is a complete elimination of the paradoxes that are frequently debated in the context of the STR. In any case, with the LT shown as inconsistent in the factual matter-filled Universe (because the metric becomes anisotropic in a moving frame), the STR can no more be the theory of motional relativity.

Since the cosmic frame is the master reference frame for all motion, with the Galilean features for the transformations to moving frames, *Cosmic Relativity has the remarkable crucial prediction that the velocity of light relative to an observer is Galilean*. The speed of light relative to the absolute cosmic frame is always $\mechanical{c}$, which is also the upper limit of velocities in the cosmic frame. But the relative velocity depends on the velocity of the observer. The situation is kinematically similar to the one with the hypothetical ether, but the physical implications are entirely different. The decisive experimental evidence for the Galilean nature of the propagation of light is discussed later ([[11.04 The Experiment on the One-Way Relative Velocity of Light|Chapter 11]]). This single experimental result is of course sufficient to replace the STR with Cosmic Relativity, as the fundamental theory of relativity.

In all the formal expressions we derive, the gravitational features of the Universe are contained as the combination $\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2$, where the gravitational potential is nearly $2\pi\mechanical{G}\mechanical{\rho}T_u^2$. Since $|\mechanical{\Phi}_u|=\mechanical{c}^2$, these factors are not explicitly visible in the expressions, but we should always be aware that it is the cosmic gravity that determines all relativistic results. The ratio $\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2=1$ remains invariant (time-independent) at the value ‘unity’, because the average density $\mechanical{\rho}(t)$ decreases as $1/T^2$ in the standard expansion history of the Universe.

While the metric factor $g_{00}=-(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)$ determines the behaviour of moving clocks, the ‘gravitomagnetic potential’ represented by the metric term $g_{0i}=-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$ determines almost the entire basis of dynamics, in the most amazing and unexpected way. It determines the laws of motion, the origin of inertia and the inertial forces, and a host of physical effects involving the spin angular momentum. The entire physical phenomena in rotating frames are determined by the ‘curl’ (circulation) of the relativistic potential $g_{0i}$, acting as a cosmic ‘gravitomagnetic field’,

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf B}_{gU}=-\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf v}=-2\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}. \tag{1.8}
$$

$\mechanical{\Omega}$ is the angular velocity of a reference frame, rotating in the matter-filled Universe (Fig. 1.3). This is similar to the magnetic field in electromagnetism, generated from a current, as the curl of a vector potential, $\nabla\times\magnetic{\mathbf A}=\magnetic{\mathbf B}$. Since $\mechanical{A}_g=-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$ for the cosmic gravitomagnetic potential, we get $\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g=\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=-2\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Omega}$.

The study of the cosmic gravitational potentials in moving frames leads us to several remarkable results. The potential $\mechanical{\Phi}_u$ changes to $\mechanical{\Phi}_u(\mechanical{v})=\gamma\mechanical{\Phi}_u=\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$ in a moving frame. Concurrently, the gravitomagnetic potential $-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$ appears, because of the matter current in a moving frame. Note that

$$
\mechanical{\phi'}_u^{\,2}-\mechanical{A}_g^2
=\gamma^2\mechanical{\Phi}_u^2-\mechanical{v}^2\gamma^2\mechanical{\Phi}_u^2/\mechanical{c}^2
=\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u^2}{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}
-\frac{\mechanical{v}^2}{\mechanical{c}^2}\frac{\mechanical{\Phi}_u^2}{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}
=\mechanical{\Phi}_u^2. \tag{1.9}
$$

Several important results follow naturally, from the single physical premise of the gravity of the matter-energy in the Universe. *Cosmic Relativity eliminates the dominance of postulates and ‘laws’ in fundamental physics, and derives the core results of dynamics and relativity as the physical consequences of the gravity of the matter in the factual Universe*.

## Interpretation and checks

Check the zero-velocity limit and then compute an invariant, such as curvature or proper time along the same path. The cross term alone is not evidence of a new interaction. The printed matrix mixes coordinate normalisations, so it cannot be read as a uniform set of dimensionless components without conversion. A locally constant scale factor can be absorbed into spatial units; cosmological curvature depends on its derivatives, which must be retained for a cosmological claim.
