## Physical question and model

Can the observed matter in the Universe explain local inertia and clock behaviour? Read this chapter as an overview of the author's proposed mechanism. The existence of cosmic matter is observational input; the choice of a cosmic potential, its transformation law, and its coupling to local instruments are additional modelling decisions.

## Lecture account

## Abstract

The central theme of this book is the cosmic gravitational paradigm for the core physical theories of dynamics and relativity. It will be established with numerous proofs and robust experimental support that all the relativistic phenomena related to motion as well as the laws of dynamics are dictated by the enormous gravitational potentials of the matter and energy in the Universe. The current theories of physics do not incorporate cosmic gravity; they were completed well before the vast matter content of the Universe became known. Hence our current theoretical paradigm of dynamics and relativity should be replaced by the new theory of Cosmic Relativity, in which the matter-filled Universe is a genuine absolute frame, of space and time. This chapter is an overview of the gravitational paradigm of Cosmic Relativity, its main results, and the solutions of several major outstanding issues in fundamental physics.

## Introduction

In this introductory chapter, I briefly describe the paradigm of Cosmic Relativity, the physical reasons that necessitate the new cosmic gravitational theory of relativity and dynamics, and the empirical and logical evidence in its strong support. The fundamental premise of Cosmic Relativity is the recognition that the vast amount of matter-energy that is distributed approximately uniformly in the Universe has a correspondingly large gravity, which has significant and observable physical effects on the space and time intervals, and on dynamics in general. A non-physicist reading this assertion might wonder why such an obvious fact should be stated as a novel finding. However, *the knowledge about the extent and the amount of the cosmic matter and its gravity came after 1930, much after our fundamental theories of relativity and dynamics were completed* (pre-1930). Therefore, the current fundamental theories of dynamics and relativity, formulated with the empty infinite space as their background, do not incorporate any of the physical effects of cosmic gravity [1]. This necessitates a reconsideration of the theories, checking and ensuring consistency between the new cosmology and the entire theoretical edifice of physics, which is pre-cosmology. Then, experimental and observational evidence unambiguously shows that Einstein's Special Theory of Relativity in a (non-existent) empty space has to be replaced with an entirely gravitational theory of relativity, with our unique matter-filled Universe as the absolute frame of space and time. The key characteristic prediction of such an absolute dynamics is the Galilean propagation of light, with its relative velocity $\mechanical{c}\pm\mechanical{v}$ for an observer moving at the velocity $\mechanical{v}$; this is confirmed unambiguously in a novel experiment. Several existing experimental results strongly favour the new paradigm. Also, several long-standing problems in relativistic dynamics are solved as straightforward cosmic gravitational effects. Cosmic Relativity is the paradigm that consistently synthesises cosmological gravity and the theories of relativity and dynamics [2, 3]. Its scope and reach are unprecedented in fundamental physics.

## Interpretation and checks

Use the later chapters to test the overview one claim at a time. A successful calculation must connect a specified worldline or apparatus to a measured time, force, or phase. Recovering a familiar formula is a consistency check, not a unique identification of its cause. Ask which prediction differs from standard relativity under the same clock and coordinate conventions.
