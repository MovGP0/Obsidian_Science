## Physical question and model

What does temperature mean for a moving thermal system? Temperature in the comoving equilibrium frame is well defined. A directional radiation spectrum or energy-transfer experiment seen from another frame need not be described by one transformed scalar temperature.

## Lecture account

Einstein was one of the acknowledged master of thermodynamics and statistical physics, right from his early years in physics. Therefore, it was very natural for him to consider the relativistic transformations of thermodynamic quantities. In his Jahrbuch paper, after repeating an argument by M. Planck to show that the entropy remained an invariant in a frame in relative motion, Einstein argued that the temperature transformed as

$$
\thermal{T}'=\thermal{T}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}\equiv\thermal{T}/\gamma \tag{4.23}
$$

and concluded,

> Thus, the temperature of a moving system is always lower with respect to a reference system that is in motion relative to it than with respect to a reference system that is at rest relative to it.

Surprisingly, *there is no consensus on the correctness of this result, even after extended debates that continue till today*. While most experts, including M. Planck and M. von Laue, agreed with Einstein's relativistic expression for the temperature, Einstein himself pulled out of the camp later, disagreeing with his own conclusion! His revised expression was exactly the inverse of Eq. (4.23), $\thermal{T}'=\gamma\thermal{T}$; the temperature of a relatively moving system is always higher [11]. Many post-Einstein authors agree with Einstein's latter conclusion, that $\thermal{T}'=\gamma\thermal{T}$, and many methods of its derivation exist. But a concrete analysis that can dissolve the long-standing confusion is lacking.

Thus, this remains as a serious gap in the STR. *The theory is unable to decide and give an unambiguous answer for the motional transformation of the temperature of a thermodynamical system*. The kind of lacuna is not expected of a fundamental theory. In contrast, Cosmic Relativity has no difficulty in reaching the correct and unambiguous answer because the motion of a thermodynamic system is absolute in the theory. Since temperature represents the average thermal energy, the correct relativistic relation in Cosmic Relativity is $\thermal{T}'=\gamma\thermal{T}$, with $\mechanical{v}$ as the absolute velocity. The ambiguity arises in the STR because it does not, and cannot, distinguish between the motion of the observer and the motion of the thermodynamical system. It is the same root cause that muddled the discussion of the Doppler shift in the STR. It is really significant that Einstein himself could not decide on the right answer in such situations, and vacillated between totally antipodal stands.

## Interpretation and checks

The historical transformation quoted in the lecture belongs to a chosen definition of heat, work, and simultaneous volume. Do not use it as a universal thermometer law. For a moving blackbody, directional Doppler shifts make the observed radiation anisotropic. At zero relative speed all consistent definitions recover the rest temperature. To compare proposals, define the actual detector response and exchange process before assigning a temperature to the moving system.
