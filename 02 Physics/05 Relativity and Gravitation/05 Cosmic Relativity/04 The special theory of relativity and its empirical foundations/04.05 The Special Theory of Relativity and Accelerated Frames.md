## Physical question and model

Can special relativity calculate an accelerated clock's elapsed time? It can: describe the worldline in an inertial chart and integrate its local proper-time increments. The clock need not move inertially throughout the experiment.

## Lecture account

The STR was proposed by Einstein as a theory that was “restricted” to inertially moving frames. However, there are no strictly inertial frames. Our habitat, the Earth, itself is rotating and also moving rather rapidly in a near-circular orbit around the Sun. All the natural and atomic motions are accelerated in various ways. Therefore, the STR had to be taken out of its straight jacket if it had to be connected with the real physical world. Otherwise, the STR cannot be applied in any physical situation! This was formalised in a major step taken by Einstein [9], to generalise the application of the STR in gravitational fields. He conceptualised an ‘instantaneously comoving inertial frame’ at every point of a non-inertial trajectory. This allowed the application of the STR to accelerated frames as well. In each of such inertial frames, the LT of the STR are valid with the instantaneous relative velocity. Then one can find the integrated relativistic effect by adding up the infinitesimal effects, by due attention to the mathematical structure of the sequence of transformations. In particular, one finds that two LT with their velocities in different directions are equivalent to the combination of a single LT and a rotation in space. That is how the STR is used in atomic physics and in the particle accelerators like the LHC at the CERN. The accelerations of the particles in these situations are enormous, and those of the human observer mild, but non-zero. In short, in situations where gravity can be neglected or separated from motional effects, the LT and the STR are routinely used in accelerated frames, contrary to the statements in many textbooks and research papers.

Einstein's reason for assuming the validity of the STR in accelerated frames was to take the leap towards a theory of gravity, using the brilliant insight of the equivalence of the acceleration and a uniform gravitational field as a springboard. We will see some details of this in [[07.01 The Genesis and Its Salient Points|Chapter 7]] and [[09.10 Einstein's Gravitational Resolution of the Paradox|Chapter 9]].

Orbital motions that deviate from a straight line and rotations are ubiquitous in daily life and in the laboratory. All these are handled routinely with the LT (added to the standard rotational transformations). But, these situations pose an unsolved problem in mechanics, remaining open for four centuries. That is the puzzle of the origin of inertial forces, also called the ‘fictitious forces’ or the ‘pseudo-forces’. What is the physical reason for the appearance of the centrifugal and Coriolis forces during non-inertial motion? The STR does not have any answer. We will see later that even the GTR does not have any answer. Instead of attempting to answer this vexing question, the GTR asserts in its major assumption that even gravity is a fictitious force! As I indicated in the opening chapter, the correct answer, which is also the only consistent answer from the point of view of empirical evidence, is given by Cosmic Relativity, which is a theory of relativity with the matter-filled Universe as the absolute gravitational reference frame for dynamics [18].

## Interpretation and checks

Acceleration changes the velocity history and can prevent two clocks from sharing a single comoving inertial frame. It does not by itself invalidate flat-spacetime mechanics. Curvature, not acceleration alone, requires general relativity for the spacetime geometry. A rotating coordinate system also gives nontrivial metric components in flat spacetime. Check the inertial limit and distinguish a coordinate singularity or horizon from a local failure of the clock law.
