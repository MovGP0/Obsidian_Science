## Physical question and model

What would make an experimental result decisive between Cosmic Relativity and standard relativity? State both complete predictions for the same apparatus, events, clock convention, and calibration. A correction term shared by both is not a discriminator.

## Lecture account

## Abstract

Cosmic Relativity makes new predictions that the author regards as decisive tests: contradictory experimental results would refute the theory. This chapter examines Galilean light propagation, with relative velocity depending linearly on observer velocity, and moving-clock time dilation. Both rely on an absolute dynamical reference frame. The author states that these differ directly from STR and that an unambiguous result could distinguish the theories. He presents several experiments, including navigation-system evidence such as GPS, as supporting Cosmic Relativity.

## Introduction

A fundamental theory needs distinctive predictions. An ideal crucial test examines mutually exclusive central postulates of two theories. A result contradicting a defining postulate falsifies the corresponding theory.

The author identifies the main distinction as an absolute reference frame and universal time in Cosmic Relativity. STR instead uses invariant light velocity relative to every inertial frame. In Cosmic Relativity, cosmic gravity defines the unique absolute frame for dynamics. Light has intrinsic speed $\mechanical{c}$ only in the isotropic cosmic frame. For another frame with velocity $\mechanical{\mathbf v}$, the source gives relative velocity $\mechanical{\mathbf c}-\mechanical{\mathbf v}$ to first order in $\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$. This is described as Galilean propagation, analogous to sound in air. In one dimension, it becomes $\mechanical{c}\pm\mechanical{v}$.

The source proposes comparing the light travel duration over distance $\mechanical{L}$. It prints $T=\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}\pm\mechanical{v}$ in contrast to $T=\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}$. The author argues that a Galilean result would falsify STR's light postulate and support Cosmic Relativity. The chapter therefore focuses on one-way propagation and relative light velocity.

> [!note]
> The printed travel-time expression adds a velocity to a time and is dimensionally inconsistent. The preceding relative-speed statement indicates the intended comparison is $T=\mechanical{L}/(\mechanical{c}\pm\mechanical{v})$ versus $T=\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}$.

The author refers to earlier arguments for logical and structural inconsistency in STR, especially between the light hypothesis and experienced simultaneity. He claims that the matter-filled Universe necessarily produces gravitational effects on clocks and physical systems of the measured size, leaving no room for a competing account that omits cosmic gravity.

He also proposes a direct experimental approach independent of accepting Cosmic Relativity: a disproved light hypothesis would, in his argument, falsify STR and its role as the foundation of GTR even without a replacement theory. He then presents cosmic gravity as the remaining physical foundation. The chapter examines one-way light experiments in detail, including practical methods and physical effects relevant to interpretation.

Another proposed decisive test measures dilation of relatively moving clocks. The author argues that transported-clock experiments support an absolute frame and that relative velocity to a stationary reference clock does not determine dilation. After giving Cosmic Relativity's exact predictions, the chapter examines GNSS atomic clocks, including GPS. These systems combine precise moving-clock timing with light propagation used to transfer time information. He presents their position calculations as evidence for both absolute-velocity time dilation and Galilean light propagation.

## Interpretation and checks

Build an evidence table mentally for each test: measured quantity, proposed residual, uncertainty, and alternative explanation. Loop timing, rotating interference, open-path timing, and clock reunion are not interchangeable observables. The lecture reports the author's interpretation; independent raw-data analysis and complete apparatus modelling are required before claiming that one theory is excluded.
