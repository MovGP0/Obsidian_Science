title: "The Propagation of Light in a Moving Frame"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The source compares Galilean and Lorentz predictions for one-way propagation, first along a straight path and then around a loop.

## Physical question and model

Which length enters a Lorentz transformation of a folded light path? Total optical path length is not the same as the vector displacement between emission and reception. Reflections divide the path into segments with different directions.

## Lecture account

The author argues that understanding light and wave propagation is essential to distinguishing absolute-frame relativity from STR. He compares Galilean transformations (GT) and Lorentz transformations (LT) for straight and looped paths made from straight segments.

First let A and B be separated by a straight length $\mechanical{L}$ and move at velocity $\mechanical{v}$ relative to global inertial frame O (Fig. 11.2). Wave speed is independent of source A's velocity, as for sound. Use $\mechanical{c}_w$ for wave speed in a privileged frame, when such a frame is known; for light, $\mechanical{c}_w=\mechanical{c}$. Since B moves away during propagation, the stationary-frame duration is

$$
\Delta t=\frac{\mechanical{L}+\mechanical{v}\Delta t}{\mechanical{c}_w}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\Delta t=\frac{\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)}\approx\frac{\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}_w}\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{v}}{\mechanical{c}_w}\right). \tag{11.3}
$$

Second-order effects are negligible for the stated light speeds. At $\mechanical{v}\approx100\,\unit{m/s}$, the source gives $\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}\approx3\times10^{-7}$ and $\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2\approx10^{-13}$. Even for sound, $\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}_w^2<0.1\%$ when $\mechanical{v}<10\,\unit{m/s}$.

The stationary-frame distance is $\Delta\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{L}+\mechanical{v}\Delta t$, so $\Delta\mechanical{x}/\Delta t=\mechanical{c}_w$, as expected.

Next, GT gives $\Delta\mechanical{x}'=\Delta\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}\Delta t=\mechanical{L}$ and $\Delta t'=\Delta t=\mechanical{L}/[\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)]$. The moving-frame relative speed is therefore

$$
\mechanical{c}'=\frac{\Delta\mechanical{x}'}{\Delta t'}=\frac{\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{L}/[\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)]}=\mechanical{c}_w\left(1-\frac{\mechanical{v}}{\mechanical{c}_w}\right)=\mechanical{c}_w-\mechanical{v}. \tag{11.4}
$$

The author expects the same for light, $\mechanical{c}'=\mechanical{c}-\mechanical{v}$, if a privileged propagation frame exists. In contrast, the source prints the LT relations

$$
\Delta\mechanical{x}'=(\Delta\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}\Delta t)=\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{L}, \tag{11.5}
$$

$$
\Delta t'=\left(\Delta t-\frac{\mechanical{v}\Delta\mechanical{x}}{\mechanical{c}^2}\right)=\signal{\gamma}\left(\frac{\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)}-\frac{\mechanical{v}\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}^2(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)}\right)=\frac{\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{L}(1-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{c}_w/\mechanical{c}^2)}{\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)}. \tag{11.6}
$$

The moving-frame velocity is then

$$
\mechanical{c}'=\frac{\Delta\mechanical{x}'}{\Delta t'}=\frac{\mechanical{c}_w-\mechanical{v}}{1-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{c}_w/\mechanical{c}^2}. \tag{11.7}
$$

For $\mechanical{c}_w=\mechanical{c}$, this gives $\mechanical{c}'=\mechanical{c}$ as expected in STR. For $\mechanical{c}_w\ll\mechanical{c}$, it gives $\mechanical{c}'\approx\mechanical{c}_w-\mechanical{v}$.

> [!note]
> The first transformed expressions in (11.5) and (11.6) omit the Lorentz factor $\signal{\gamma}$, although their later expressions include it. The source equations are retained with that omission visible.

The source notes that $\signal{\gamma}$ cancels in the distance-to-duration ratio, so second-order contraction and dilation factors need not be carried through a relative-velocity calculation. For a general wave with $\mechanical{c}_w\ne\mechanical{c}$, light speed enters through the $\mechanical{v}\Delta\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{c}^2$ term in the time transformation, but not the spatial transformation. The author identifies that time term as solely responsible for invariant relative light speed and argues that a conventional experiment cannot access it without a separated clock.

He then asserts that connecting A and B with a hollow optical fibre and looping it until the endpoints are adjacent cannot change propagation. To first order in $\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$, the moving-frame path distance remains $\mechanical{L}$ in both GT and LT. He further claims that the looped-path durations become equal in the two calculations, which he interprets as LT being generally inconsistent with the light hypothesis. He sets out the detailed first-order calculation below.

The observer remains inertial, and clocks A and B are brought into what the source calls “synchro-proximity”. B is drawn slightly displaced from A for clarity; in the ideal experiment, emission and reception positions coincide in the apparatus frame. The author argues that this tests whether the Lorentz position-dependent time term corresponds to a physical clock effect.

In Fig. 11.2, emission begins at A with $\mechanical{x}=0$, $\mechanical{y}=0$, and $t=0$. The light passes the following fixed positions in stationary frame O:

| Point | Position | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| b | $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{l},\mechanical{y}=0$ | $t=\mechanical{l}/\mechanical{c}$ |
| c | $\mechanical{x}=\mechanical{l},\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{h}$ | $t=(\mechanical{l}+\mechanical{h})/\mechanical{c}$ |
| d | $\mechanical{x}=-\mechanical{l},\mechanical{y}=\mechanical{h}$ | $t=(3\mechanical{l}+\mechanical{h})/\mechanical{c}$ |
| a | $\mechanical{x}=-\mechanical{l},\mechanical{y}=0$ | $t=(3\mechanical{l}+2\mechanical{h})/\mechanical{c}$ |

The path continues to B. Its total length is $4\mechanical{l}+2\mechanical{h}=\mechanical{L}$, with $\mechanical{h}\ll\mechanical{l}$. Each point between emission and reception is crossed only once, while B moves away as light approaches in frame O.

GT gives $\mechanical{x}'=\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}t$ and $t'=t$. Reception is at B, stationary in frame O′ at $\mechanical{x}'=0$. The source tabulates events at A, b, a after most of the loop, and reception B=A, for general wave speed $\mechanical{c}_w\le\mechanical{c}$. Light is the special case $\mechanical{c}_w=\mechanical{c}$. LT uses $\mechanical{x}'=\signal{\gamma}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}t)$ and $t'=\signal{\gamma}(t-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{x}/\mechanical{c}^2)$; the source neglects very small second-order $\signal{\gamma}$ effects when comparing first-order velocities.

The source table is:

| Event | $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},t$ in O | GT: $\mechanical{x}',\mechanical{y}',t'$ in O′ | LT: $\mechanical{x}',\mechanical{y}',t'$ in O′ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | $0,0,0$ | $0,0,0$ | $0,0,0$ |
| b | $\mechanical{l},0,\mechanical{l}/\mechanical{c}_w$ | $\mechanical{l}-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{l}/\mechanical{c}_w,0,\mechanical{l}/\mechanical{c}_w$ | $\signal{\gamma}(\mechanical{l}-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{l}/\mechanical{c}_w),0,(\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{l}/\mechanical{c}_w)(1-\mechanical{v}\mechanical{c}_w/\mechanical{c}^2)$ |
| a | $-\mechanical{l},0,(3\mechanical{l}+2\mechanical{h})/\mechanical{c}_w$ | $-\mechanical{l}-\mechanical{v}(\mechanical{L}-\mechanical{l})/\mechanical{c}_w,0,\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}_w$ | $-\signal{\gamma}(\mechanical{l}+\mechanical{v}(\mechanical{L}-\mechanical{l})/\mechanical{c}_w),0,\signal{\gamma}(\mechanical{L}-\mechanical{l})/\mechanical{c}_w$ |
| B | $\mechanical{v}\mechanical{L}/[\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)],0,\mechanical{L}/[\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)]$ | $0,0,\mechanical{L}/[\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)]$ | $0,0,\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{L}(1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2)/[\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)]$ |

> [!note]
> In row a, the printed GT time is $\mechanical{L}/\mechanical{c}_w$, although the stationary time is $(\mechanical{L}-\mechanical{l})/\mechanical{c}_w$. The printed LT time also omits the position-dependent contribution $\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{v}\mechanical{l}/\mechanical{c}^2$ from the stated transformation. These source entries are retained.

The source gives the stationary-frame sum of path segments as

$$
\Delta\mechanical{x}=2\mechanical{l}+2\mechanical{h}+\frac{\mechanical{v}\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)}\approx\mechanical{L}\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{v}}{\mechanical{c}_w}\right),
$$

with duration printed as $\Delta t=\mechanical{L}(1+\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)/\mechanical{c}$. It states that their ratio is $\mechanical{c}_w$.

For GT, the source again uses $\Delta\mechanical{x}'=\mechanical{L}$. From the stationary-frame path length, it obtains

$$
\Delta\mechanical{x}'=\Delta\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}\Delta t=\mechanical{L}\left(1+\frac{\mechanical{v}}{\mechanical{c}_w}\right)-\frac{\mechanical{v}\mechanical{L}(1+\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)}{\mechanical{c}_w}\approx\mechanical{L}.
$$

The author identifies this with the fixed separation of A and B in their own frame, and thus gives relative velocity $\mechanical{c}_w-\mechanical{v}$.

> [!note]
> The printed segment sum starts with $2\mechanical{l}+2\mechanical{h}$, although the preceding total loop length is $\mechanical{L}=4\mechanical{l}+2\mechanical{h}$. The printed duration also uses $\mechanical{c}$ instead of the general wave speed $\mechanical{c}_w$. The stated length limit and speed ratio require consistent loop length and wave speed.

The source's LT propagation duration in O′ is

$$
\Delta t'=\frac{\mechanical{L}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}}{\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)}. \tag{11.8}
$$

The first-order term contains the wave speed, not necessarily light speed. The square-root factor is negligible at first order. For light, the source gives $\Delta t'\approx\mechanical{L}/(\mechanical{c}-\mechanical{v})$, identical to its Galilean result. It argues that the position-dependent Lorentz time term now contributes only at second order because the endpoint clocks are adjacent.

The author states that two locally accessible clocks leave no scope for imposing a hypothetical synchronisation term on measured time. If relative light speed remained $\mechanical{c}$, the path traversed during this duration would be $\Delta\mechanical{x}'=\mechanical{c}\mechanical{L}/[\mechanical{c}(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c})]\approx\mechanical{L}(1+\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c})$. He regards that as an unacceptable speed-dependent separation between relatively stationary endpoints, and thus claims a contradiction between LT and the light hypothesis for the loop.

The source also directly combines its relative path length $\Delta\mechanical{x}'=\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{L}$ and duration:

$$
\frac{\Delta\mechanical{x}'}{\Delta t'}=\frac{\mechanical{c}\signal{\gamma}\mechanical{L}}{\mechanical{L}/[\signal{\gamma}(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c})]}\approx\mechanical{c}-\mechanical{v}. \tag{11.9}
$$

He interprets this as LT yielding Galilean relative light velocity, contrary to Einstein's postulate.

For Cosmic Relativity, he gives the same duration for straight and looped propagation:

$$
\Delta t'=\frac{\mechanical{L}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}}{\mechanical{c}_w(1-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}_w)}. \tag{11.10}
$$

This is the Galilean result modified by absolute clock dilation, with factor $\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}=\sqrt{-\mechanical{g}_{00}}$. He gives the moving-frame path length as $\Delta\mechanical{x}'=\mechanical{L}\sqrt{1-\mechanical{v}^2/\mechanical{c}^2}$. Their ratio is then exactly $\mechanical{c}'=\mechanical{c}_w-\mechanical{v}$ for all waves, including light. He states that a direct experiment in the next section confirms this.

The author concludes that LT applied to a general light path conflicts with invariant relative speed, while conventional separated-clock arrangements conceal the issue. He distinguishes this claim from Lorentz's own ether theory, which has Galilean propagation and therefore lacks the asserted conflict with an invariant-speed postulate. Before presenting experiments, he introduces a further argument using the principle of relativity alone.

## Interpretation and checks

Transform each reflection event and each segment, including moving-mirror boundary conditions. Adding scalar segment lengths and then treating their sum as one Cartesian displacement can create a spurious first-order result. The unrestricted Lorentz velocity formula returns light speed for a vacuum ray. Several printed intermediate equations omit factors or mix path length with endpoint separation, so the loop conclusion does not follow from those lines alone.
