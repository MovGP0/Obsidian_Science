## Physical question and model

How much time do two clocks accumulate between the same departure and reunion events? Use their complete worldlines, not only one relative speed observed during separation. Ideal clocks measure proper time; the reunion comparison is local and does not depend on a distant synchronisation convention.

## Abstract

Moving-clock time dilation is one of modern relativity’s most striking and readily verified predictions. The STR gives all inertial frames equal status, so only relative motion has physical meaning; there is no unique resting or truly moving clock. Which of two relatively moving clocks therefore experiences real motional time dilation? The twin paradox is among physics’ most debated topics. Einstein’s answer rejected the STR resolutions commonly discussed in textbooks and articles. I examine twin clocks and the inadequacy of usual resolutions, including Einstein’s. The only logically and physically sound resolution is time dilation relative to the matter-filled Universe’s absolute frame.

## Interpretation and checks

The standard twin calculation has a unique result for specified paths in flat spacetime. If one clock travels at three fifths of light speed during long inertial legs, those legs accumulate four fifths of the chosen inertial coordinate duration. This is an illustrative model, not flight data. The author's cosmic explanation must predict a different invariant reading to be distinguished from it.
