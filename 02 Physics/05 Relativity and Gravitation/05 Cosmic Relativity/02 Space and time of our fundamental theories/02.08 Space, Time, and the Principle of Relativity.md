## Physical question and model

What does the relativity principle forbid an isolated inertial laboratory from detecting? It concerns the form of local physical laws under uniform changes of inertial frame. It does not forbid detecting motion relative to an external gas, radiation field, or other material reference.

## Lecture account

I conclude this chapter with a discussion of arguably the most important realisation about dynamics in space without significant amount of matter—the Principle of Relativity (PoR). The PoR asserts the impossibility to distinguish between a state of uniform motion and a state of rest of a reference frame for dynamics. In other words, all physical phenomena in a frame in uniform ‘inertial’ motion, at constant velocity, are identical to those in a frame at rest. Therefore, no physical phenomenon in the frame can be used to decide whether one is moving or not. Underlying the PoR is the unstated assumption of the homogeneity and isotropy of space, which is taken as self-evident in the case of the empty space devoid of any matter.

The PoR is a principle that is manifest to everybody, as evident from the plain fact that, for millennia, we did not realise the Earth's motion through the Universe at the stupendous speed of nearly $400\,\unit{km/s}$. Not even the mildly non-uniform rotational motion of the Earth was recognised. One should realise that in an average lifetime, we travel the enormous distance of nearly a trillion kilometres in the Universe, a tenth of a light year, carrying the impression of being at rest! None of the physical phenomena we come across in our terrestrial frame reveals our state of motion. All phenomena observable within the reference frame are as if one is at rest, independent of any velocity, spatial position, and time.

It was Galileo who clearly articulated the PoR first. There are several passages in his ‘Dialogues concerning Two Chief World Systems’ [22] that emphasise the PoR.

> ...whatever motion comes to be attributed to the earth must necessarily remain imperceptible to us and as if nonexistent, so long as we look only at terrestrial objects; for as inhabitants of the earth, we consequently participate in the same motion.

In a convincing scenario of the empirical method, Galileo laid out the details:

> Shut yourself up with some friend in the main cabin below decks on some large ship, and have with you there some flies, butterflies, and other small flying animals. Have a large bowl of water with some fish in it; hang up a bottle that empties drop by drop into a wide vessel beneath it. With the ship standing still, observe carefully how the little animals fly with equal speed to all sides of the cabin. The fish swim indifferently in all directions; the drops fall into the vessel beneath; and, in throwing something to your friend, you need throw it no more strongly in one direction than another, the distances being equal; jumping with your feet together, you pass equal spaces in every direction. When you have observed all these things carefully (though there is no doubt that when the ship is standing still everything must happen in this way), have the ship proceed with any speed you like, so long as the motion is uniform and not fluctuating this way and that. You will discover not the least change in all the effects named, nor could you tell from any of them whether the ship was moving or standing still.

The PoR was in a shadow of doubt later because of the possibility of its violation if a stationary ether existed, as an absolute medium for the electromagnetic phenomena. Then it is akin to travelling on the open deck of Galileo's ship, through a stationary medium of air; one can then conceive experiments that reveal the motion of the ship. The Michelson-Morley experiment, with its second order sensitivity, and the empirical evidence prior to that, dispelled this doubt; light propagated back and forth, seemingly as ‘indifferently as the fish in a bowl’. That is when Poincaré restated the PoR emphatically [23]:

> The principle of relativity, according to which the laws of physical phenomena must be the same for a stationary observer as for an observer carried along in a uniform motion of translation; so that we have not and can not have any means of discerning whether or not we are carried along in such a motion.

This is reiterated by Einstein [24] as

> The laws of nature are independent of the state of motion of the system of reference, at least if the latter is without acceleration.

and in his monograph, ‘The Meaning of Relativity’ [25] as

> If K is an inertial system, then every other system K which moves uniformly and without rotation relatively to K, is also an inertial system; the laws of nature are in concordance for all inertial systems. This statement we shall call the principle of special relativity.

The implicit assumption of an underlying ‘empty space’ in the STR becomes clear in Max Planck's exposition of the PoR [26], in the context of Einstein's theory.

> The gist of this principle is: it is in no wise possible to detect the motion of a body relative to empty space; in fact, there is absolutely no physical sense in speaking of such a motion. If, therefore, two observers move with uniform but different velocities, then each of the two with exactly the same right may assert that with respect to empty space he is at rest, and there are no physical methods of measurement enabling us to decide in favour of the one or the other.

Once accepted as a founding principle, the PoR is very powerful, as proved in the development of physics in the 20th century. However, if one seeks, as a physicist should, *the reason for the validity of the PoR*, one enters another plane of the foundations of physics. Then one sees that none of the theories of relativity in the history connected the PoR to any physical reason. Lorentz's theory sought for a reason in the electromagnetic phenomena in the ether, but the fact that the PoR is more general than electromagnetic phenomena defeats that attempt. Einstein's STR does not even attempt to find a reason, just as it does not give physical reasons for the relativistic effects like the time dilation in relative motion. We will see that, in contrast to this very unsatisfactory situation, there is a completely intelligible physical reason behind the validity and the universality of the PoR in the theory of Cosmic Relativity. The PoR originates in the universal nature of the gravitational interaction in the homogeneously distributed matter in the Universe, as I already indicated in the first chapter. The homogeneity of the distributed matter translates first to the homogeneity of the gravitational potentials, and then to the homogeneity of space itself. The physical effects of homogeneous (uniform) physical potentials are undetectable. This implies the PoR.

## Interpretation and checks

Seal a mechanical experiment in a uniformly moving ideal cabin and compare its internal outcomes with those in a resting cabin. Then add a window receiving anisotropic external radiation: the environment has changed, so the comparison no longer tests an isolated cabin. This explains why detecting the CMB dipole is not by itself a violation of relativity. Acceleration is different again and can be measured internally with an accelerometer.
