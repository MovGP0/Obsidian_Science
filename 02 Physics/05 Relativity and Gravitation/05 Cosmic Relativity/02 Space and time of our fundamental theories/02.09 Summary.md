## Lecture account

We surveyed the concepts of space and time in modern physics, starting with Newton's discussion in Principia. The distinction between the ‘absolute’ and ‘relative’ was clarified. Newton's operational identification of the absolute motion with the aid of his experiment with water in a rotating bucket, presented as a proof for an absolute space, was described. This was then elaborated by discussing Ernst Mach's pointed criticism, which touched the core of relativity and dynamics, demanding a consistent answer till today. The unsolved problem of the origin of the inertial forces highlights the core issue of absolute vs relative, for space and time.

The significant conceptual shift during the development of electrodynamics and optics, the addition of the hypothetical and all pervading homogeneous medium of the ether to the empty space, was discussed next. An important development in the same context was the introduction of the physical fields of interactions, which was a lasting conceptual advance that enabled the mathematical formulation and the rapid development of the theories of physics, and their applications. While the hypothetical ether slowly vanished from the physical description, the fields survived and became the essential and perhaps the most important unobservable notion in physics.

The mathematical structure of Einstein's STR was put in the form of a geometrical description of space-time transformations by Minkowski, which was further carried by Einstein to the elegant use of the 4-dimensional Riemannian geometry to describe gravity. This enabled Einstein to generalise the theory of relativity to include the complete description of gravity and dynamics, in a way consistent with the nonlinearity stemming from the equivalence of mass and energy (to be discussed later). Then, gravity was described in terms of the curvature of space and time, in proportion to the amount of matter and its motion. However, the GTR continued with its basis in the Minkowski metric of the empty space, because the factual situation of the matter-filled Universe with its enormous gravity emerged much after all our theories of relativity and dynamics were completed.

The absolute space and time of the factual Universe were discussed briefly next, highlighting the fact that all the physical phenomena, and their experience happen in this single Universe, in the gravitational presence of the entire cosmic matter. Then, there is an obvious fundamental conflict between the prevalent theories as they are formulated and the real factual situation, which is the main theme of discussion in this book. The obvious conflict between the absolute reference implied by Mach's principle and the structure of the STR based in an empty space was highlighted. A brief mention was made about an increased content of metaphysical overload in physics, contrary to the announced program by the logical positivists, of purging such notions from the space and time of physics. The necessity of the gravitational paradigm of Cosmic Relativity was then highlighted, by pointing to the amply verified facts of the factual existence of the absolute markers of dynamics and relativity in the cosmic matter and radiation.

In the next chapter, we explore the decisive developments in electrodynamics that culminated in the radical modifications in the concepts of space and time, and in the theories of relativity.
