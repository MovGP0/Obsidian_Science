## Physical question and model

What would make an extra spatial dimension more than a mathematical device? In a compactified model, state the geometry, size, and allowed fields of the extra direction. Additional coordinates can organise interaction fields, but formal unification does not itself establish their physical existence.

## Lecture account

The attempts at the unification of interactions suggested the possibility of higher dimensions of space and time. Of course, we usually consider only one dimension of time, quite sensibly, and all other dimensions are ascribed to space. The need for higher dimensions in a unified theory can be superficially understood as the need to accommodate an increased number of interaction potentials and fields as the different components of the same unified interaction. The first of these attempts at a unification considered the electromagnetic field and gravity, which was passionately pursued by Einstein himself, by a different path. The famous ‘Kaluza-Klein’ 5-dimensional space-time in which this can be formally achieved served as archetype for subsequent speculations to even higher dimensions. In all such attempts, one has to deal with the empirical constraint that we ‘observe’ only 3 spatial dimensions. The usual remedy is to ‘compactify’ the extra dimensions—make them undetectably small in their extent and also curl them up to avoid any boundary edges. This has the additional feature that a physical quantity associated with the compactified dimension, akin to a momentum, is automatically quantised. In the Kaluza-Klein theory, the quantised quantity was the electric charge, which was very impressive.

While it is very straightforward to add extra dimensions to space-time mathematically, I will refrain from considering space beyond 3 dimensions and a single time. The physical reasons for this will be justified during the more detailed discussions later, of the relation between matter and the implied space. The significant point here is that matter itself is 3-dimensional and all our intuitions about space, including its ascribed number of dimensions, have come only from our familiarity with the 3-dimensional matter. In any case, all proposals about extra dimensions of space remained as speculations without even the slightest empirical evidence. Also, it is not relevant for the results discussed in this work, because we are dealing with gravity on the cosmic scale, but the speculated extra dimensions are, in all cases, microscopic or of subatomic nature.

Higher dimensions usually imply corrections to the inverse square law of the long range interactions like gravity and electromagnetism. This is because the exact $1/\mechanical{r}^2$ fall-off of the force can be related to the reduction of the density of radial ‘lines of force’ (Gauss's law), which is unique for the 3-dimensional space. If space was 4-dimensional, for example, a physical gravitational field would spread out as $1/\mechanical{r}^3$ when the distance from the source is increased. Therefore, the tests of the inverse-square law of gravity have been the most popular method to probe proposals of higher dimensions, but with very limited reach of distance and sensitivity. In spite of several experimental searches and theoretical investigations, higher dimensional space has remained as a mere speculation in physics, with no evidence at all. However, several theories need higher dimensions for their (theoretical) consistency. One can always argue that the compact extent of the extra dimension is too tiny to be observable by any feasible method (of the order of the Planck size, $10^{-35}\,\unit{m}$). (The present experiments cannot probe conclusively below a few microns.) In any case, the existence or otherwise of such ultra-compact extra dimensions is not relevant for our discussion and results in relativity.

## Interpretation and checks

A compact direction allows discrete momentum modes. At energies too low to excite those modes, motion can appear effectively lower-dimensional. This is the model's limiting case, not proof that compactification occurred. A falsifiable proposal must identify a new mode, force-law correction, or other measurable departure and the scale where it appears. Predict what happens to the excitation threshold when the compactification length is reduced: it rises.
