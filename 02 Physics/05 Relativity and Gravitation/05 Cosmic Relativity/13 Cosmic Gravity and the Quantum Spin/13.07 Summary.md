> [!TLDR]
> The chapter attributes spin phases, fine structure, chiral spin transport, and exchange statistics to spin interacting with cosmic gravity.

## Physical question and model

Review three separate results: spin transformation under rotations, phases acquired during transport, and symmetry under identical-particle exchange. A common use of angular momentum does not make these statements equivalent.

## Lecture account

The author identifies intrinsic spin as an elementary gravitomagnetic moment: a closed current of mass and energy, the charge of gravity. This identification is applied to both classical angular momentum and quantum spin. As mass-dependent effects are linked to gravity and magnetic-moment effects to electromagnetism, he argues that effects depending only on spin are gravitomagnetic.

The first application is the magnetic phase of a spin-polarised electron in a uniform field. The source assigns a phase $\pi$ and argues that cosmic coupling supplies the extra phase needed for $2\pi$ orbital closure.

The Thomas-precession contribution to atomic fine-structure doublets is then reinterpreted as electron-spin interaction with an induced cosmic field. Photon and neutron geometric phases in helical or cyclic spin transport are likewise treated as dynamical gravitational phases.

For CISS in organic molecules, rapid chiral motion is said to induce a large cosmic field. Its energy splitting favours one spin direction, explaining strong spin filtering and the related directional magnetoresistance.

These examples prepare the proposed SSC proof. Direct and exchange scattering paths differ in turning angle, and their cosmic spin phases are claimed to yield the integer-spin plus sign and half-integer-spin minus sign. The author states that the proof covers massive and massless particles, interacting and noninteracting particles, and identical composite aggregates. He presents it as a physical explanation for the boson–fermion division.

## Interpretation and checks

As a reconstruction task, choose one spin convention and recover the energy, torque, and phase with consistent factors and units. Then explain why a vanishing torque need not mean vanishing phase. Finally, list the extra assumptions needed to turn a scattering-path phase into a universal statistics rule. This identifies which parts of the chapter are established calculations and which depend on the cosmic interpretation.
