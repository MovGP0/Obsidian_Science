> [!TLDR]
> Classical spin is angular momentum, while quantum spin has discrete projections; the source connects their physical effects through ensemble averages.

## Physical question and model

What distinguishes intrinsic spin from orbital angular momentum? Both generate rotations, but intrinsic spin acts on internal state components. An elementary particle need not contain a rigid body or spatial mass current that literally rotates.

## Lecture account

Spin is intrinsic angular momentum. In classical physics, it is usually the vector sum of the constituent particles' angular momenta about a principal axis:

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf s}=\sum_i\mechanical{\mathbf r}_i\times\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i.
$$

Except for particles on the axis, this is the sum of rotational momenta. Spin is a pseudovector. Its projection along a unit direction $\hat n$ is

$$
\mechanical{S}_n=\hat n\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf s}
=\mechanical{S}\cos\theta.
$$

In quantum mechanics, a spin operator replaces the classical component. Its expectation value, an ensemble average, corresponds to the classical projection. Intrinsic spin of an elementary particle does not permit a direct mechanical rotation model.

Historically, electron spin was introduced as self-rotation to explain spectral doublets. A rotating charge forms a magnetic dipole, which can interact with the relative nuclear current and split spectral lines. In quantum theory, components along an axis take discrete values separated by $\energy{\hbar}$:

$$
+\mechanical{s},\quad+\mechanical{s}-\energy{\hbar},\quad+\mechanical{s}-2\energy{\hbar},\quad\ldots,\quad-\mechanical{s}.
$$

For the source's elementary spin $\mechanical{s}=\energy{\hbar}/2$, the two projections are $+\energy{\hbar}/2$ and $-\energy{\hbar}/2$. Their representation requires a spinor. Its rotation law differs from that of a vector. Nevertheless, the source stresses that the ensemble average of spinors has the usual vectorial angular-momentum behaviour [2].

Spin is often called a relativistic concept because the Dirac equation gives electron spin. The source notes that two-component spinors already occur in the nonrelativistic Pauli equation [6]. For cosmic-gravity effects, he requires consistency with the Principle of Relativity and coordinate transformations, rather than a mechanical spin model.

Spin has the same dimension as action. The author argues that action itself is quantised, rather than physical quantities independently [7]. He therefore connects spin quantisation to quantisation of the action associated with spin dynamics.

## Interpretation and checks

Distinguish the spin quantum number, the magnitude of angular momentum, and a measured component. For spin one-half, the component has two outcomes; it is not the continuous classical projection of a pre-existing vector onto every axis. Check the zero-spin case and a change of measurement axis. A composite body can have classical angular momentum, but that limit does not derive the internal structure of an elementary spin.
