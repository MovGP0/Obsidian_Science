> [!TLDR]
> The author derives the exchange sign from the phase difference of identical spins coupled to a cosmic gravitomagnetic field during scattering.

## Physical question and model

Does the calculated spin phase establish the universal exchange rule? The source compares direct and exchange scattering alternatives and assigns a cosmic phase to their turning angles. The crucial assumption is that their angle difference represents every allowed identical-particle exchange.

## Lecture account

Cosmic Relativity is claimed to provide both a physical reason and a dynamical mechanism for the SSC [10, 44, 45]. The proposed difference between bosons and fermions comes from spin phases accumulated in the cosmic field induced by particle motion. The source says that this gives symmetric boson states and antisymmetric fermion states.

Consider two identical particles in identical spin states. Alternative quantum routes interfere when their initial and final states are indistinguishable. Two-particle scattering has direct and exchanged amplitudes, as in the Jordan–Wigner account (Fig. 13.7). The ordinary dynamical phases are the same because the spacetime paths have identical characteristics.

At close approach, however, the deflection angles producing the same final configuration differ by exactly $\pi$. The author attributes a relative dynamical phase to the spin's cosmic gravitational interaction during this difference in turning angle.

![[cosmic-relativity-13-07-exchange-scattering.svg|**Fig. 13.7** Red and blue routes connect the same initial and final two-particle states. Spins are perpendicular to the scattering plane for indistinguishability. The momentum directions turn through θ or θ + π. The relative phase determines the source's exchange statistics.]]

The proposed motion-induced potential is proportional to $\mechanical{\Phi}_u\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$. Particle spins are polarised in the same direction perpendicular to the scattering plane. Turning near closest approach induces a cosmic field and gives each amplitude a factor $\exp(i\signal{\varphi})$.

The relevant quantity is the phase difference for indistinguishable states. Each particle's momentum direction turns by angles differing by $\pi$. The author says that exchanging final momentum vectors makes the process equivalent to particle exchange, for massive or massless particles alike.

During the turning, $\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=-\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf v}(t)=-2\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}(t)$, where $\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}$ is the rate of change of momentum direction. The field exists only while that direction turns. The spin phase is

$$
\delta\signal{\varphi}_g=-\frac1{\energy{\hbar}}\int\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}\,dt
=-\frac1{\energy{\hbar}}\int\mechanical{s}\frac{d\theta}{dt}\,dt
=-\frac{\mechanical{s}\theta}{\energy{\hbar}}. \tag{13.24}
$$

It depends on the turning angle rather than the duration or other scattering details. The author calls it explicitly dynamical despite its geometric form.

![[cosmic-relativity-13-08-cosmic-scattering-phases.svg|**Fig. 13.8** The two indistinguishable scattering amplitudes in cosmic matter. The momentum directions turn through θ and θ + π. Schematic circulating cosmic currents represent the transient gravitomagnetic interaction. The source gives the relative two-particle phase as 2sπ/ℏ.]]

Both momentum vectors turn in the same sense during a scattering event. The product wavefunction $\psi_1(\mechanical{x}_1)\psi_2(\mechanical{x}_2)$ therefore acquires the sum of the two phases. The source writes $\signal{\varphi}_1=2\mechanical{s}\theta_1/\energy{\hbar}$ and, for $\theta_2=\theta_1+\pi$, $\signal{\varphi}_2=-2\mechanical{s}\theta_2/\energy{\hbar}$. It gives their difference as

$$
\Delta\signal{\varphi}=\signal{\varphi}_1-\signal{\varphi}_2
=\frac{2\mechanical{s}(\theta_1-\theta_2)}{\energy{\hbar}}
=\frac{2\mechanical{s}\pi}{\energy{\hbar}}. \tag{13.25}
$$

The source then writes $\exp(i\Delta\signal{\varphi}/\energy{\hbar})=\exp(i2\pi s)$ for particles of spin $s\energy{\hbar}$. For zero spin,

$$
\Delta\signal{\varphi}=2s(\theta_1-\theta_2)=0\times2\pi=0. \tag{13.26}
$$

The amplitudes add with sign $+1$. The source extends this to all integer spins because $\exp(2\pi in)=+1$, giving Bose–Einstein behaviour.

Higher spins can be formed as composites of spin-½ particles, with additive spin projections and phases. For a spin-½ pair, the source gives

$$
\Delta\signal{\varphi}=2s(\theta_1-\theta_2)=2\times\frac12\times\pi=\pi. \tag{13.27}
$$

The exchange sign is then $-1$, producing antisymmetric amplitudes, Pauli exclusion, and Fermi–Dirac statistics.

> [!note]
> The source presents this as a physical proof: collective behaviour is set by spin interaction with the cosmic field induced by motion. Equations (13.25)–(13.27) change spin and phase normalisation. The stated sign of the second phase and the substitution θ₂=θ₁+π do not directly give all printed equalities. These source inconsistencies are retained rather than silently repaired.

The author stresses that only the total turning angle enters, independent of scattering duration or whether particles are massive. Total spin of an aggregate therefore sets its statistics, rather than the fermionic nature of its individual constituents.

For composites of electrons, protons, and neutrons, the additive quantum phases depend on total spin in pair scattering. The author claims that this resolves the composite-particle objection to field-based SSC proofs.

He also claims validity for interacting particles: local interaction phases are identical in the direct and exchange amplitudes and cancel in their difference. A common external gravitomagnetic field likewise supplies no differential phase. In this account only the field generated by differential particle motion changes that difference.

The source presents the result as a general physical explanation rather than a field-consistency condition. It reiterates that every phase change must involve a physical interaction, and states that arbitrary initial and final states can be treated with the same gravitational phase calculation.

## Interpretation and checks

The numerical substitution gives a plus or minus sign for the selected angles, but that is only the final algebraic step. A proof must show independence from trajectory, interaction, frame, and exchange implementation. It must also show that the spin phase was not already included in the state symmetry. Ask what happens when the scattering paths are continuously deformed. Without that argument, the calculation is a proposed mechanism rather than a general proof of spin–statistics.
