> [!TLDR]
> The author argues that a spin-½ electron's magnetic phase prevents orbital phase closure unless an additional cosmic gravitational phase is included.

## Physical question and model

Does orbital phase closure require the extra spin phase proposed here? A consistent calculation must specify the full spinor state, orbital dynamics, magnetic coupling, and boundary conditions. Treating each contribution as an independent scalar winding can double-count a phase already included in the Hamiltonian.

## Lecture account

Chapter 1 introduced this puzzle in sections 1.7–1.11. The author presents it as evidence for cosmic gravity acting on spin and as preparation for the spin–statistics argument.

Consider an electron constrained to a plane in a uniform perpendicular magnetic field. Its cyclotron frequency is $\signal{\omega}=\electric{e}\magnetic{B}/\mechanical{m}$. Quantum Landau levels have energies $(i+1/2)\energy{\hbar}\signal{\omega}$, where $i$ is an integer. The half term is the zero-point energy. Landau orbitals are important in two-dimensional condensed-matter systems, including the quantum Hall effects.

The source requires Bohr–Sommerfeld phase closure over an orbit. For a spinless charge,

$$
\Delta\signal{\phi}=\frac{1}{\energy{\hbar}}\oint\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A}\cdot d\mechanical{\mathbf l}
=\frac{1}{\energy{\hbar}}\iint\electric{e}\nabla\times\magnetic{\mathbf A}\cdot d\mechanical{\mathbf S}
=2\pi\frac{\magnetic{\Phi}}{\magnetic{\Phi}_0}=2\pi n. \tag{13.11}
$$

Here $n$ is an integer and $\magnetic{\Phi}_0=\energy{h}/\electric{e}$ is the flux quantum. The author stresses that the quantised object is the orbital quantum phase, rather than the magnetic field itself as discrete flux lines.

**Fig. 13.2** The source's phase puzzle. An electron has a cyclotron orbit of frequency eB/m. Its orbital phase closes in multiples of 2π, but the additional magnetic spin phase is π. The red arrow indicates spin.

A real electron has spin $\energy{\hbar}/2$ and magnetic moment $\magnetic{\mu}_e$. This adds an interaction energy $\energy{E}_s=\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}_e\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}$. The source argues that paired electrons with total spin 1 or 0 satisfy phase closure, as in superconductivity. It assigns half their phase, namely $\pi$, to one electron and next checks this by direct calculation.

The source's explicit magnetic-phase calculation is

$$
\delta\signal{\phi}=\frac1{\energy{\hbar}}\oint\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}_e\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}\,dt
=\frac1{\energy{\hbar}}\oint g\magnetic{\mu}_B\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}\,dt
=\frac{2}{\energy{\hbar}}\frac{\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}}{2\mechanical{m}}\frac12\frac{2\pi\magnetic{B}}{\signal{\omega}}
=\pi\frac{\magnetic{\Phi}}{\magnetic{\Phi}_0}=\pi n. \tag{13.12}
$$

For odd $n$, including the ground state in the source's argument, this adds a phase $\pi$. The author treats the apparent failure of phase closure as evidence for an unidentified spin interaction.

He assigns the orbital frame a cosmic field $\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g=2\signal{\boldsymbol\omega}$ and interaction energy $\energy{E}_g=\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g/2=\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\omega}$. Its phase magnitude is

$$
\delta\signal{\phi}_g=\frac1{\energy{\hbar}}\oint\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\omega}\,dt
=\frac{2\pi\mechanical{s}}{\energy{\hbar}}=\pi. \tag{13.13}
$$

The relative cosmic matter current in the electron frame is thus said to supply the missing phase. The author presents this as proof of the cosmic gravitational potential and of spin coupling to cosmic matter, with consequences for quantum dynamics and spin transport.

> [!note]
> The source's boxed conclusion is that spin-polarised cyclotron motion satisfies Bohr–Sommerfeld closure only when the motion-induced cosmic gravitomagnetic spin interaction is included. Equation (13.12) is retained as printed: its substitution with a single cyclotron period gives π, while its final expression introduces the orbital integer n. Spin is also used there in units of ℏ. These conventions are not silently corrected.

## Interpretation and checks

Use the Pauli Landau-level problem as a check: orbital zero-point energy and Zeeman energy occur in one spectrum. Their relative shift is not, on its own, a contradiction requiring cosmic gravity. Every phase must be dimensionless and compared between complete alternatives. Derive the claimed residual phase without imposing a second, incompatible closure condition on the same state.
