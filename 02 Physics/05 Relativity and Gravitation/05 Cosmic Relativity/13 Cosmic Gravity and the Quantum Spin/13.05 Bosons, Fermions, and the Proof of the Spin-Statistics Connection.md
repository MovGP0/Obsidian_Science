> [!TLDR]
> The spin–statistics connection links integer spin to bosons and half-integer spin to fermions; the author seeks a physical reason for this division.

## Physical question and model

What physical distinction does the spin–statistics connection express? It links the spin representation of identical particles to the symmetry of their many-particle states. Occupation and exchange rules have consequences for spectra, gases, and condensed matter.

## Lecture account

Particle spin takes values $0,\energy{\hbar}/2,\energy{\hbar},\ldots$. These divide into integer and half-integer multiples of $\energy{\hbar}$. Identical integer-spin particles, called bosons, can share a quantum state in any number. Identical half-integer-spin particles, called fermions, allow only one particle per quantum state. This collective behaviour determines many macroscopic phenomena.

S. N. Bose used the spin-1 photon counting rule to derive Planck's radiation law [25]; Einstein extended it to atoms [26]. W. Pauli proposed the exclusion principle for identical atomic electrons [27]. He later connected spin and statistics through consistency of relativistic quantum field theories [28].

The author argues that such consistency proofs do not explain the physical cause, especially because the same rules hold for a pair of nonrelativistic particles. He cites I. Duck and E. C. G. Sudarshan [29], who distinguish showing that a theory is consistent with spin–statistics from explaining why nature follows it. He also cites Feynman's lectures [30]: Pauli's field-theory argument establishes the connection, but an elementary physical explanation was still lacking.

Despite its name, the spin–statistics connection (SSC) acts already for two identical nonrelativistic particles. Their pair behaviour then controls ensemble statistics. The author argues that previous physical explanations omitted spin's proposed gravitational nature. Outside nuclear processes, he identifies gravity and electromagnetism as the two long-range interactions available for such an explanation.

The decisive difference is the wavefunction phase under exchange of two particles' positions. By analogy with geometric phases, the author proposes that nonuniform motion in the matter-filled Universe generates the exchange phase through cosmic spin coupling. The following sections review the SSC before developing this claim.

## Interpretation and checks

Specify the setting: the usual boson–fermion statement applies to ordinary particles in three spatial dimensions under the assumptions of the relevant theorem. Two-dimensional quasiparticles require a different exchange analysis. Exclusion forbids two identical fermions in the same complete one-particle state; opposite spin labels can make states distinct. A dynamical account must explain the universal exchange rule, not only one specially chosen scattering path.
