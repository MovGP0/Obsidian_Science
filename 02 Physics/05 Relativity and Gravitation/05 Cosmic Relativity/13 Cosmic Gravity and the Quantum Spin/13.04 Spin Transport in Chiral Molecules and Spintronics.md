## Physical question and model

Can a proposed spin–rotation energy explain spin-selective molecular transport? Specify the carrier trajectory, residence time, spin preparation, contacts, and measured polarisation. A local angular-frequency estimate is only one input to a transport model.

## Lecture account

A particle in a rotating frame moves relative to the matter of the whole Universe. In its frame the Universe rotates and has relative matter currents, as on the spinning Earth. The author assigns these currents large, nonuniform gravitational potentials. One consequence is the cosmic gravitomagnetic field $\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=-2\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}$.

The source writes a particle's magnetic moment as $\magnetic{\mu}=g_m\magnetic{\mu}_B\mechanical{s}$, with $\magnetic{\mu}_B=\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}/(2\mechanical{m})$ and dimensionless gyromagnetic factor $g_m$. Cosmic matter currents couple to spin rather than directly to a magnetic moment. Because spin and moment are linked, a gravitational spin effect can resemble a magnetic-moment effect.

For example, a rotating-frame transformation in NMR can be represented by a pseudo-magnetic field [20]. The proposed physical energy is $-\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}$. The source equates this to a magnetic expression involving $g_m\magnetic{\mu}_B\mechanical{s}\magnetic{B}_{eq}$ and gives

$$
\magnetic{B}_{eq}=\frac{\energy{\hbar}\signal{\Omega}}{2g_m\magnetic{\mu}_B}.
$$

This equivalent field is a formal representation of the spin coupling and can be large in microscopic dynamics. The author stresses that a magnetic moment responds to a real magnetic field, not directly to spatial rotation; in his account the rotational effect is always gravitomagnetic spin coupling.

> [!note]
> The source alternates between dimensional spin ℏ/2 and dimensionless spin in its magnetic-moment relation. The equivalent-field expression is retained as printed, including its factor of two.

A helical path projects to circular motion. The source identifies the nonzero velocity curl, $\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf v}=2\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}$, with a cosmic gravitomagnetic field and uses $\energy{E}_g=\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g/2=-\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}$. He proposes this as the cause of chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS) [21].

Molecules such as DNA can form conducting helical paths. Electrons follow the helix, with a typical diameter of about $1\,\unit{nm}$ and a length of several nanometres. Large transmitted spin polarisation has been reported without an applied magnetic environment sufficient to account for it. R. Naaman and colleagues named the observed effect CISS. The source next quotes a review by Naaman and Waldeck [22].

In paraphrase, Naaman and Waldeck report that chiral molecules act as strong spin filters. In DNA at room temperature, transmission probabilities for favoured and unfavoured spins exceeded a ratio of 4:1, greater than many inorganic filters. The effect was unexpected because the organic molecules are nonmagnetic and their conventional spin–orbit coupling is small [22].

The author attributes this directly to rotation-induced cosmic coupling. A helix radius $\mechanical{r}=0.5\,\unit{nm}$ and electron speed near $5\times10^5\,\unit{m/s}$ give $\signal{\Omega}\simeq10^{15}\,\unit{rad/s}$. The proposed splitting is

$$
\Delta\energy{E}=\Delta\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}
\simeq\energy{\hbar}\signal{\Omega}\simeq0.7\,\unit{eV}.
$$

The source compares this with thermal energy $3\times10^{-21}\,\unit{J}$, below $0.03\,\unit{eV}$, and calls the splitting about 30 times larger. It concludes that one spin orientation is strongly favoured, making the molecule a spin valve.

Conventional explanations tend to use electromagnetic spin–orbit coupling. The source argues that CISS lacks the required environmental charges or currents and treats a fictitious magnetic field only as a proxy for the proposed gravitational energy. He writes

$$
\frac{\mechanical{s}\mechanical{B}_g}{2}
=\frac{\energy{\hbar}\signal{\omega}}2
=\frac{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{v}}{2\mechanical{r}},
\qquad
g\magnetic{\mu}_B\magnetic{B}_f
=\frac{\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}\magnetic{B}_f}{\mechanical{m}}
=\frac{\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{v}}{2\mechanical{r}}.
$$

He then gives the equivalent field $\magnetic{B}_f=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}/(2\electric{e}\mechanical{r})$, about $3000\,\unit{T}$, and contrasts it with laboratory transient fields stated as no greater than $100\,\unit{T}$. The actual proposed field is gravitomagnetic, caused by relative cosmic matter currents. Reversing electron motion at fixed chirality reverses its sign. In hole conduction the actual electron motion sets the field; charge sign does not enter this gravitational account.

> [!note]
> The source prints B_f=mv/2er without grouping. The grouped form above is the form consistent with the preceding equality and the quoted field estimate. Its spin normalisation still follows the source's varying conventions. The laboratory-field comparison is a source-era statement.

A related experiment used chiral cysteine layers between gold, nickel, and aluminium oxide [23]. Without the chiral layer the magnetoresistance was independent of applied-field direction. With it, the source reports a strong directional change, continued below.

The magnetoresistance rises or falls with molecular chirality and changes sign when either the magnetic field or molecular handedness reverses. The magnetic field sets the available tunnelling states in nickel. For applied fields below $0.5\,\unit{T}$ and temperatures $15$–$300\,\unit{K}$, the source reports no appreciable spin polarisation without the chiral molecules; both spin states enter, giving a field-symmetric resistance.

The proposed chiral filter instead supplies mainly one spin, with splitting $\Delta\energy{E}=\Delta\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}\simeq\energy{\hbar}\signal{\Omega}$ larger than thermal energy. Conductance increases when the selected spin matches the more numerous available nickel states. Reversing the field or chirality reverses this preference. The author presents the CISS results [24] as a contribution of Cosmic Relativity to nanoscale spintronics.

Unlike the single-particle phase experiments in [[13.03.03 Geometric Phases and Cosmic Gravity]], chiral conduction involves a thermal ensemble with both spin states. The proposed energy difference creates a large barrier for the unfavoured spin. The author contrasts attracting parallel electric currents with repelling parallel mass currents. Cosmic matter current opposes particle motion, consistent with $\mechanical{g}_{0i}=-\mechanical{v}_i/\mechanical{c}$. Spin aligned with the helix gives an attractive current–current interaction and a stable configuration; classically, a torque turns the average spin towards it.

He compares this with a cyclone coupled to the Earth's rotation-induced field. A spinning top or gyroscope on a slowly rotating platform is suggested as a simple test of the stable orientation.

## Interpretation and checks

The energy estimate scales as reduced Planck constant times speed divided by path radius. Check how it changes when speed falls or the path straightens. An equivalent magnetic field is an energy comparison, not evidence that such a magnetic field exists. The cosmic mechanism must predict a transmission probability and its dependence on chirality and experimental controls. A large estimated energy alone does not explain the observed selectivity or exclude other mechanisms.
