> [!TLDR]
> The author adds a cosmic gravitational vector potential to the Pauli equation and derives a mass-independent spin–rotation coupling.

## Physical question and model

What follows from adding a vector potential to the Pauli Hamiltonian? The operator identity separates kinetic energy from spin coupling through momentum commutators. This algebra predicts a coupling only after the potential and its physical normalisation have been chosen.

## Lecture account

The Dirac equation gives the electron's spin and gyromagnetic ratio. The author argues that spin is more basic than the STR [6], and that the Dirac equation's four-dimensional structure does not require the STR; see [[12.08 The Dirac Equation and Relativity]].

The nonrelativistic Pauli equation modifies the Schrödinger equation with electromagnetic potentials [9]:

$$
i\energy{\hbar}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}
=\left[\frac{1}{2\mechanical{m}}\left(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A})\right)^2+\electric{e}\electric{\phi}\right]\psi. \tag{13.7}
$$

The $2\times2$ matrices $\boldsymbol\sigma$ are Pauli matrices. $\magnetic{\mathbf A}$ is the electromagnetic vector potential and $\electric{\phi}$ is the electric potential. Using

$$
(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf a)(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf b)
=\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b+i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b),
\qquad\hat{\mechanical{\mathbf p}}=-i\energy{\hbar}\nabla,
\qquad\nabla\times\magnetic{\mathbf A}=\magnetic{\mathbf B},
$$

gives

$$
i\energy{\hbar}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}
=\left[\frac{(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A})^2}{2\mechanical{m}}+\electric{e}\electric{\phi}
-\frac{\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}}{2\mechanical{m}}\right]\psi. \tag{13.8}
$$

The source identifies the last term with $g\magnetic{\mu}_B\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}$, where $\magnetic{\mu}_B=\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}/(2\mechanical{m})$. For electron spin $s=1/2$ in units of $\energy{\hbar}$, this yields the gyromagnetic ratio $g=2$ without the STR.

To derive a cosmic coupling, the author adds the gravitational potential $\mechanical{g}_{0i}=-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}\equiv\mechanical{A}_g=-\mechanical{\Phi}_u\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$ to the generalised momentum, with gravitational charge $\mechanical{m}$:

$$
i\energy{\hbar}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}
=\left[\frac{1}{2\mechanical{m}}\left(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g)\right)^2+\electric{e}\electric{\phi}\right]\psi. \tag{13.9}
$$

Writing $\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Pi}=\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A}-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g$, the displayed expansion is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2\mechanical{m}}(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Pi})(\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Pi})
&=\frac{\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Pi}^2}{2\mechanical{m}}
+i\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot(\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Pi}\times\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Pi})\\
&=\frac{\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Pi}^2}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\frac{i}{2\mechanical{m}}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot i\energy{\hbar}\nabla\times\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A}
-\frac{1}{2\mechanical{m}}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\energy{\hbar}\nabla\times\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g\\
&=\frac{\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Pi}^2}{2\mechanical{m}}
-\frac{\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\energy{\hbar}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}.
\end{aligned}\tag{13.10}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (13.10) preserves the source's intermediate line, where the cross-product term lacks the common factor $1/(2\mechanical{m})$. The source also uses $\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g$ both for a dimensionless metric-related potential and for a velocity-unit potential in the momentum coupling. These normalisations must be distinguished when checking units. Spin and Pauli-matrix normalisations vary between the explanatory text and these formulas.

Unlike the Bohr magneton in the electromagnetic term, the gravitational term contains only $\energy{\hbar}$. The source attributes cancellation of mass to equality of inertial and gravitational mass.

The Pauli matrices have eigenvalues $\pm1$. The source states that a spin-½ electron or proton in the cosmic field

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=-\frac{2\mechanical{\Phi}_u\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}}{\mechanical{c}^2}=-2\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}
$$

has interaction energy $+\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}$. He compares this with classical spin aligned with or opposite to the axis. An ensemble of $N$ aligned spins must give the classical result for angular momentum $N\energy{\hbar}/2$.

> [!note]
> This positive energy sign follows the source's equation (13.10); equation (13.2) gives the negative sign. The source also switches here to a dimensional cosmic potential, $\mechanical{\Phi}_u=\mechanical{c}^2$, whereas its earlier identification used a normalised potential.

The proposed effects should appear in atomic and condensed-matter physics, where electron spin is important. A charge moving in a magnetic field defines a rotating frame and thus, in the proposed account, an additional cosmic spin interaction. A photon, with spin magnitude $\energy{\hbar}$, can follow a guided non-linear path and acquire a corresponding phase. Slow reactor neutrons provide another laboratory probe.

The following sections first develop a spin-phase puzzle, then discuss Thomas precession and spectral fine structure, photon and neutron geometric phases, and spin-selective transport in chiral molecules. The chapter ends with the proposed gravitational proof of the spin–statistics connection [10].

## Interpretation and checks

Keep dimensional spin distinct from the Pauli matrices: a spin-one-half operator is reduced Planck constant times the Pauli vector divided by two. The printed intermediate expansion loses a kinetic prefactor, and the source changes potential units and energy signs, as the notes below record. Re-derive the commutator with one convention before comparing coefficients. Cancellation of particle mass does not establish that the field is generated by cosmic matter.
