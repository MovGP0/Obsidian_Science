## Physical question and model

How does spin precession affect atomic spectral splittings? In the usual weak-velocity calculation, spin–orbit coupling and Thomas precession must be combined in the same frame. Thomas rotation also follows from the composition of non-collinear Lorentz boosts.

## Lecture account

The author argues that Galilean relative light propagation requires a new relativity paradigm, and that cosmic gravity causes motional relativistic effects. He therefore seeks gravitational explanations for effects usually assigned to relative motion in the STR. Atomic fine structure is a central example: the proposed theory must give the correct electron-spin precession and the Thomas factor in the doublet spectrum.

When G. Uhlenbeck and S. Goudsmit proposed electron spin, simple spin–orbit coupling gave twice the observed fine-structure splitting [11]. L. Thomas's relativistic precession correction produced the observed doublet splitting [12]. Two Lorentz boosts in different directions combine into a boost and a rotation. That rotation gives an extra negative spin precession, opposite to the electromagnetic spin–orbit term.

The conventional Thomas effect is classical and kinematic. Its frequency is not normally calculated from an energy difference between the two spin projections interacting with a physical field.

The usual fine-structure treatment enters the electron's rest frame and calculates the magnetic field due to the apparent motion of the nucleus. The Dirac equation also gives the result directly. To make the physical issue clear, the source writes the magnetic-moment energy due to motion in the nuclear electric field as proportional to

$$
\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}\cdot\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf v}_l\times\electric{\mathbf E}}{\mechanical{c}},
\qquad\electric{\mathbf E}=\hat r\frac{Z\electric{e}}{\mechanical{r}^2}.
$$

The local path need not be curved for this coupling. The radial electric field makes it nonzero only for nonzero orbital angular momentum. The relevant velocity is the tangential component $\mechanical{\mathbf v}_l$ (Fig. 13.3).

> [!note]
> These field and coupling expressions follow the source's electromagnetic unit convention; they omit the SI Coulomb factor and should not be read as complete SI equations.

The electron has radial acceleration $\mechanical{a}_r=d\mechanical{v}_r/dt=-\electric{e}\electric{E}/\mechanical{m}$. In the source's cosmic-potential description, $\mechanical{g}_{0r}=-\mechanical{v}_r/\mechanical{c}$ changes with time and generates a radial field

$$
\mechanical{E}_g=-\frac{d(\mechanical{v}_r/\mechanical{c})}{dt}=-\frac{\mechanical{a}_r}{\mechanical{c}}.
$$

The source writes the induced field as $\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g=\electric{e}\mechanical{\mathbf v}_l/\mechanical{c}\times\mechanical{\mathbf E}_g$, by analogy with the electromagnetic motional field. Equality of inertial and gravitational masses removes their ratio. The stated spin energy is $\energy{E}_s=\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g/2=-\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\mechanical{\mathbf a})/(2\mechanical{c}^2)$.

The author identifies this with the complete Thomas term, without Lorentz transformations. The energy difference between spin-up and spin-down is written as $\Delta\energy{E}=\energy{\hbar}\signal{\omega}_T$. The same frequency describes classical precession of the state $(|+z\rangle+|-z\rangle)/\sqrt2$:

$$
\signal{\boldsymbol\omega}_T=\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf B}_g}{2}
=-\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\mechanical{\mathbf a}}{2\mechanical{c}^2}
\simeq-\frac{\mechanical{v}^2}{2\mechanical{c}^2}\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}. \tag{13.14}
$$

The last form assumes an approximately circular orbit. The author calls the coupling a first-order $\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$ effect, analogous to the Lorentz force, and gives the next correction as order $(\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c})^3$.

With orbital angular momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf l}=\mechanical{\mathbf r}\times\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, $\mechanical{\mathbf p}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf v}$, magnetic moment $\magnetic{\mu}=g\mechanical{s}\magnetic{\mu}_B$, and $\magnetic{\mu}_B=\electric{e}\energy{\hbar}/(2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{c})$ in the convention used here, the source gives

$$
\energy{E}_m=\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}_e\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf B}_r
=\magnetic{\boldsymbol\mu}_e\cdot\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf v}_l\times\electric{\mathbf E}}{\mechanical{c}}
=\frac{Z\electric{e}\magnetic{\mu}_e\mechanical{v}_l}{\mechanical{c}\mechanical{r}^2}
=\frac{Zg\electric{e}^2\energy{\hbar}^2(\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf l})}{2\mechanical{c}^2\mechanical{r}^3\mechanical{m}^2}. \tag{13.15}
$$

The relation $\mechanical{l}\simeq\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}_l\mechanical{r}$ is used. This magnetic contribution alone is twice the observed splitting. The opposing gravitational contribution is

$$
\energy{E}_s=-\frac{\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf v}\times\mechanical{\mathbf a})}{2\mechanical{c}^2}
=-\frac{Z\electric{e}^2\energy{\hbar}^2(\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf l})}{2\mechanical{m}^2\mechanical{r}^3\mechanical{c}^2}. \tag{13.16}
$$

Thus the source's total fine-structure energy is

$$
\energy{E}_{fs}=\frac{Z\electric{e}^2\energy{\hbar}^2(\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf l})}{2\mechanical{c}^2\langle\mechanical{r}^3\rangle\mechanical{m}^2}(g-1)
\simeq\frac{Z\electric{e}^2\energy{\hbar}^2(\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf l})}{2\mechanical{c}^2\langle\mechanical{r}^3\rangle\mechanical{m}^2}. \tag{13.17}
$$

The source calls $\langle\mechanical{r}^3\rangle$ the wavefunction average. It claims that cosmic gravity supplies half of the electromagnetic contribution and thus explains the fine-structure magnitude.

> [!note]
> The printed induced-field expression contains an extra electric charge e. Equations (13.15)–(13.17) also mix dimensional and dimensionless angular momenta, and use the reciprocal of ⟨r³⟩ rather than explicitly writing ⟨r⁻³⟩. These source expressions are retained for traceability; their conventions require care before numerical use.

## Interpretation and checks

The precession scale is acceleration times speed divided by light speed squared, with inverse-time units. It vanishes for collinear acceleration and velocity and is small at low speed. Fix the electromagnetic unit system before comparing the printed factors of light speed. Reproducing the Thomas factor is a consistency requirement for the proposed account; it does not distinguish the account unless another observable differs.
