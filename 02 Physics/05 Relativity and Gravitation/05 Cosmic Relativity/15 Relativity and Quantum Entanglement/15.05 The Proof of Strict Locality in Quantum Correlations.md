> [!TLDR]
> The source proposes that local action-wave interference reproduces singlet correlations while the joint wavefunction describes an ensemble.

## Physical question and model

Does reproducing a joint cosine prove Bell locality? It proves agreement with one target function only after all amplitudes and averaging rules are specified. Bell locality additionally requires the conditional joint probability, at a complete shared state, to factor into two local response probabilities.

## Lecture account

The author presents an explicit locality argument [16], intended to support Cosmic Relativity's absolute time and exclude nonlocal physical influence. The proposed correlations follow from local interference of the action waves introduced in Chapter 12.

The standard spin-singlet state is

$$
|S\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(|u\rangle_1|d\rangle_2-|d\rangle_1|u\rangle_2\right). \tag{15.9}
$$

The author assigns this wavefunction to a statistical ensemble rather than one two-particle history. He notes Einstein's view that an ensemble interpretation removes the need for a physical nonlocal collapse of a joint state.

![[cosmic-relativity-15-04-pair-action-waves.svg|**Fig. 15.4** Source pair dynamics and action alternatives. A central source xₛ emits towards x₁ and x₂. The two action branches on each side are labelled S₁ₐ, S₁ᵦ, S₂ₐ, and S₂ᵦ; total momentum obeys p₁ + p₂ = p₀. Curves distinguish alternatives rather than measured spatial trajectories.]]

The source caption describes a fixed dynamical resource, represented by total momentum $\mechanical{p}=\mechanical{p}_0$, whose action constraints are $\energy{S}_{1a}+\energy{S}_{2b}=0$ and $\energy{S}_{1b}+\energy{S}_{2a}=0$. Individual particle histories are claimed to remain definite and separable.

For alternatives a and b, define four action waves:

$$
\signal{\zeta}_{1a}=e^{i\energy{S}_{1a}/\energy{\hbar}},\quad
\signal{\zeta}_{1b}=e^{i\energy{S}_{1b}/\energy{\hbar}},\quad
\signal{\zeta}_{2a}=e^{i\energy{S}_{2a}/\energy{\hbar}},\quad
\signal{\zeta}_{2b}=e^{i\energy{S}_{2b}/\energy{\hbar}}. \tag{15.10}
$$

For a fixed shared resource, such as momentum, $\mechanical{p}_1=-\mechanical{p}_2$ when the total is zero. Action is $\energy{S}=\int\mechanical{p}\,d\mechanical{x}$, so conservation enters the phases [17]. The first particle's local wave sum is

$$
\signal{\zeta}_1=\frac1{\sqrt2}e^{i\energy{S}_{1a}/\energy{\hbar}}+\frac1{\sqrt2}e^{i\energy{S}_{1b}/\energy{\hbar}}. \tag{15.11}
$$

Its intensity is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\zeta}_1\signal{\zeta}_1^*
&=\frac12\left(1+1+e^{-i\energy{S}_{1a}/\energy{\hbar}}e^{i\energy{S}_{1b}/\energy{\hbar}}+e^{i\energy{S}_{1a}/\energy{\hbar}}e^{-i\energy{S}_{1b}/\energy{\hbar}}\right)\\
&=1+\cos\left(\frac{\energy{S}_{1b}-\energy{S}_{1a}}{\energy{\hbar}}\right).
\end{aligned}\tag{15.12}
$$

Writing the actions as momentum times displacement from source coordinate $\mechanical{x}_s$ gives

$$
1+\cos\left(\frac{\mechanical{p}_{1b}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_s)-\mechanical{p}_{1a}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_s)}{\energy{\hbar}}\right)
=1+\cos\left(\frac{\Delta\mechanical{p}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_s)}{\energy{\hbar}}\right). \tag{15.13}
$$

The source position is uncertain on scale $\energy{\hbar}/\Delta\mechanical{p}$, corresponding to an individual action uncertainty of order $\energy{\hbar}$ [16]. The author states that this washes out the local cosine at fixed apparatus setting, giving uniform single-particle intensity. The second side behaves similarly.

For joint observations, only the action-wave pairs $1a,2b$ and $1b,2a$ satisfy the source conservation constraint. With $\mechanical{p}_{1a}=-\mechanical{p}_{2b}$ and $\mechanical{p}_{1b}=-\mechanical{p}_{2a}$, the printed joint amplitude is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\zeta}_1\signal{\zeta}_2
&=\frac12e^{i\mechanical{p}_{1a}(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_s)/\energy{\hbar}}e^{i\mechanical{p}_{2b}(\mechanical{x}_2-\mechanical{x}_s)/\energy{\hbar}}
+\frac12e^{i\mechanical{p}_{1b}(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_s)/\energy{\hbar}}e^{i\mechanical{p}_{2a}(\mechanical{x}_2-\mechanical{x}_s)/\energy{\hbar}}\\
&=\frac12e^{i\mechanical{p}_{1a}(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_2)/\energy{\hbar}}
+\frac12e^{i\mechanical{p}_{1b}(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_2)/\energy{\hbar}}.
\end{aligned}\tag{15.14}
$$

The common source coordinate cancels. The resulting joint distribution is

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\signal{\zeta}_1\signal{\zeta}_2|^2
&=\frac14\left[2+e^{-i(\mechanical{p}_{1a}-\mechanical{p}_{1b})(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_2)/\energy{\hbar}}+e^{i(\mechanical{p}_{1a}-\mechanical{p}_{1b})(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_2)/\energy{\hbar}}\right]\\
&=\frac12\left[1+\cos\left(\frac{(\mechanical{p}_{1a}-\mechanical{p}_{1b})(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_2)}{\energy{\hbar}}\right)\right]\\
&=\frac12\left[1+\cos\left(\frac{\Delta\mechanical{p}(\mechanical{x}_1-\mechanical{x}_2)}{\energy{\hbar}}\right)\right].
\end{aligned}\tag{15.15}
$$

The source identifies this full-visibility dependence on relative detector position with two-particle coincidence interference. It claims that local interference of correlated action waves produces it without a nonlocal influence.

For spin, the same approach is said to give $C(\theta)=-\cos\theta$ [16]. Spin action is angular momentum times its conjugate angle. The proposed correlated actions are $\mechanical{s}_+(\theta_i-\varphi)$ and $\mechanical{s}_-(\theta_i-\varphi)$, where the common initial uncertainty is $\mechanical{s}\varphi$ and the analyser setting supplies a reference phase.

> [!note]
> The notation ζ₁ζ₂ in equation (15.14) denotes the two conservation-selected joint branches, not the unrestricted product of two two-term local sums, which would have four terms. The source's inference that this construction establishes strict locality is retained as its claim. Cosine arguments are grouped explicitly to show the action divided by ℏ.

At analyser 1, the source superposes the two local spin action waves:

$$
\signal{\zeta}(1,\theta_1)=\frac1{\sqrt2}e^{is_+\theta_1}e^{-is_+\varphi}
+\frac1{\sqrt2}e^{is_-\theta_1}e^{-is_-\varphi}. \tag{15.16}
$$

Similarly, at analyser 2,

$$
\signal{\zeta}(2,\theta_2)=\frac1{\sqrt2}e^{is_+\theta_2}e^{-is_+\varphi}
+\frac1{\sqrt2}e^{is_-\theta_2}e^{-is_-\varphi}. \tag{15.17}
$$

Here the spin projections $s_\pm$ are in units of $\energy{\hbar}$. The same-sign source combinations are excluded by total-spin conservation. Selecting the two opposite-sign source branches gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\zeta}(1,\theta_1)\signal{\zeta}(2,\theta_2)
&=\frac12e^{is_+\theta_1}e^{-is_+\varphi}e^{is_-\theta_2}e^{-is_-\varphi}\\
&\quad+\frac12e^{is_-\theta_1}e^{-is_-\varphi}e^{is_+\theta_2}e^{-is_+\varphi}.
\end{aligned}\tag{15.18}
$$

For measured projections $s_{1i},s_{2i}=\pm1$ in units of $\energy{\hbar}/2$, define the pair correlation

$$
C(\theta_1-\theta_2)=\langle s_{1i}s_{2i}\rangle
=p_+(+1)+p_-(-1)=p_+-p_-. \tag{15.19}
$$

Here $p_+$ is the probability of equal outcomes and $p_-$ of opposite outcomes. The source calculates

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_-&=|\signal{\zeta}(1,\theta_1)\signal{\zeta}(2,\theta_2)|^2\\
&=\frac12+\frac14e^{-i(s_+-s_-)(\theta_1-\theta_2)}
+\frac14e^{i(s_+-s_-)(\theta_1-\theta_2)}\\
&=\frac12[1+\cos(\Delta s(\theta_1-\theta_2))]
=\cos^2\left(\frac{\theta_1-\theta_2}{2}\right).
\end{aligned}\tag{15.20}
$$

The complementary probability is

$$
p_+=1-\cos^2\left(\frac{\theta_1-\theta_2}{2}\right)
=\sin^2\left(\frac{\theta_1-\theta_2}{2}\right). \tag{15.21}
$$

Therefore

$$
C(\theta_1-\theta_2)=p_+-p_-=-\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2). \tag{15.22}
$$

The author claims this reproduces the singlet result from local action-wave interference and common-source conservation [14, 16]. He rejects a nonlocal interpretation of Bell experiments, while acknowledging the resulting advances in photon sources and detectors.

> [!note]
> The source prints the final trigonometric terms in (15.20)–(15.21) with an ambiguously placed /2. They are explicitly grouped as half-angle functions here, consistent with the preceding cosine identity and equation (15.22). Its local and joint amplitudes also use different selected branch sets, as noted after equation (15.15). These formulas transcribe the proposed argument; they do not independently validate its locality claim.

## Interpretation and checks

The source selects two joint branches from local sums whose unrestricted product has four terms. This selection needs a complete preparation and measurement rule; writing the result as a product symbol does not prove conditional factorisation. Shared randomness can correlate outcomes, but with setting-independent sampling and local responses it still obeys CHSH. Check normalisation, every joint outcome, and both local marginals for all settings. [Hensen and colleagues’ 2015 experiment](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.05949) tested a Bell inequality while addressing detection and spacelike-separation conditions. A no-signalling interference construction is not, by itself, a counterexample to that bound.
