> [!TLDR]
> A nonseparable joint state predicts correlations between subsystems, while the source distinguishes wavefunction reduction from a physical change at a distance.

## Physical question and model

How can a joint state contain information absent from a product of individual pure states? A product amplitude factorises; an entangled amplitude generally does not. Normalisation and the measurement basis are part of the physical specification.

## Lecture account

Chapter 12 introduced state representations. Several possible observable eigenvalues are represented by a superposition:

$$
|\Psi\rangle=a|\psi_a\rangle+b|\psi_b\rangle+c|\psi_c\rangle+\cdots. \tag{15.1}
$$

Each basis state represents a definite value, such as energy. Individual measurements give one eigenvalue with Born-rule probability. The usual reduction rule assigns the corresponding state after observation. If outcome b occurs, the state becomes $|\psi_b\rangle$, with coefficient one, while other coefficients vanish.

The author distinguishes this formal updating rule from an ontological wave spread through space. If the wavefunction is only a mathematical description, reduction need not be a physical event. If it is assigned literal spatial reality, its instantaneous disappearance from extended regions is physically problematic in the source's view.

For a two-outcome observable, write

$$
|\psi\rangle=a|\psi_+\rangle+b|\psi_-\rangle. \tag{15.2}
$$

Two independent particles have $\psi_A=a\psi_{A+}+b\psi_{A-}$ and $\psi_B=c\psi_{B+}+d\psi_{B-}$. Their product is

$$
\Psi=\psi_A\psi_B
=ac\psi_{A+}\psi_{B+}+ad\psi_{A+}\psi_{B-}+bc\psi_{A-}\psi_{B+}+bd\psi_{A-}\psi_{B-}. \tag{15.3}
$$

These terms represent the four possible joint outcomes. Measuring A as positive removes the last two terms:

$$
\Psi'=\psi_{A}\psi_B
=ac\psi_{A+}\psi_{B+}+ad\psi_{A+}\psi_{B-}
=a\psi_{A+}(c\psi_{B+}+d\psi_{B-}). \tag{15.4}
$$

This remains factorisable. A has a definite state; B retains its original superposition until measured. The printed expression omits conditional renormalisation.

For particles produced from a zero-total-spin system, same-axis spin outcomes must instead be opposite. Conservation excludes the ++ and −− combinations. The joint state is

$$
\Psi=\alpha\psi_{A+}\psi_{B-}+\beta\psi_{A-}\psi_{B+}. \tag{15.5}
$$

For nonzero coefficients this cannot be factored into separate pure states of A and B. Schrödinger identified this nonseparability as entanglement in 1935 [9]. The source describes the lack of an individual pure wavefunction as the lack of an individual physical state; the joint wavefunction still gives expectation values.

> [!note]
> The source's wording about an individual system having no state refers to the absence of an individual pure-state wavefunction in this discussion. The equations themselves describe a joint entangled state; an individual subsystem can still be represented by a reduced density operator.

## Interpretation and checks

For the equal-weight singlet, either particle alone has an unpolarised reduced state, even though their same-axis outcomes are perfectly anticorrelated. This is a mixed individual state, not the absence of an individual statistical state. A joint pure state and a classical mixture can have the same local statistics but different joint predictions in other bases. Check normalisation with modulus-squared complex coefficients and compare at least two analyser settings before classifying a preparation.
