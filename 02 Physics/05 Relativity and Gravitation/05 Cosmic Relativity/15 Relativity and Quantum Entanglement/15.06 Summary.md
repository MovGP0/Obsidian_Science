> [!TLDR]
> The chapter argues that singlet correlations admit a local action-wave account and that Bell-test results should not be read as instantaneous physical causation.

## Physical question and model

Review the distinction between correlated outcomes and a signal sent from one analyser to the other. Quantum theory gives no controllable remote message through local marginal statistics, yet its joint predictions can violate Bell’s conditional factorisation bound.

## Lecture account

Both the STR and Cosmic Relativity restrict physical influence between separated events by a finite propagation speed. The author regards this locality requirement as essential to causality. He argues that rejecting local hidden-variable models after correlation experiments does not establish an instantaneous influence in nature.

Cosmic Relativity assigns this causal structure to cosmic gravity and global cosmological time. The chapter therefore examines entanglement, EPR completeness, hidden-variable theories, and measured correlations while insisting on strict locality.

The source distinguishes EPR's representational incompleteness from von Neumann's causal incompleteness. In the former, locality prevents a distant measurement from changing a real state, although it changes the conditional wavefunction assignment. In the latter, hidden variables are considered as a route to deterministic outcomes. The author attributes much later confusion to treating these as the same question.

He derives the cosine correlation by assigning angular-momentum-conserving conditional ensemble averages. Quantum mechanics agrees with that correlation. He argues that the different LHVT prediction therefore fails conservation as well as experiment, and interprets Bell-inequality violations as rejecting the tested model class rather than proving nonlocal physical causation.

Finally, the author derives two-particle coincidence interference and spin-singlet correlations with the local action waves introduced in Chapter 12. He presents these calculations as a proof of strict locality and as support for invariant cosmic simultaneity and causal order.

The chapter ends the book's applications of Cosmic Relativity. Its final chapter reviews the proposed paradigm's status and expected durability, with no fundamental separation between classical and quantum dynamics.

## Interpretation and checks

As a transfer task, start with the singlet’s equal-axis anticorrelation and ask what additional rule predicts a rotated analyser. Then test the resulting four-setting combination. Identify whether the model changes factorisation, setting independence, sampling, or another explicit premise. A preferred cosmic time and an ensemble interpretation do not answer this probability test on their own. The chapter’s claim of a strictly local mechanism remains subject to that audit.
