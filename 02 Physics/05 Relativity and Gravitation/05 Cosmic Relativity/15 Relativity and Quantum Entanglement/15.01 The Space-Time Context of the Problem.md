> [!TLDR]
> Comparing separated measurements requires local clock readings and a synchronisation rule, while causal order must remain consistent for genuinely cause-linked events.

## Physical question and model

Does changing the time coordinate resolve the entanglement problem? For spacelike separated measurements, ordinary relativity allows different coordinate orderings while preserving each recorded outcome and the joint statistics. A preferred ordering would be extra structure, not a new correlation calculation.

## Lecture account

Relativity compares physical events at different places and times. Correlation is the quantitative relation between independently measured outcomes on systems that interacted earlier. Questions about simultaneity and temporal order are separate from the numerical outcomes. Einstein's criticism concerned whether quantum mechanics was a complete description of such systems.

A genuine cause must precede its effect for every observer. The meanings of before, simultaneous, and after compare two clock readings. In the source's absolute cosmic-time framework they are observer-independent. In the STR, simultaneity of spacelike-separated events depends on observer motion. Every measurement is recorded by a local clock, so separated clocks must be synchronised to compare the times to the required accuracy; the source refers to *Gravity's Time* [4].

The source caption states that O records A before B, while the moving O′ coordinate description reverses their order. These events must be spacelike separated if their order reverses. Both the STR and Cosmic Relativity preserve the order of genuinely causally connected events.

The author requires a finite propagation delay and expects a causal influence to weaken with distance. He derives an absolute limiting speed $\sqrt{\mechanical{\Phi}_u}=\mechanical{c}$ from the cosmic gravitational potential. Cosmic Relativity assigns an absolute order to all events, while the STR assigns observer-dependent order to spacelike events and an invariant causal order to timelike or lightlike links.

Einstein's locality statement [6] is paraphrased here: the real physical situation of a spatially separated system S2 is independent of what is done to S1. The author claims that there is no empirical violation of this principle.

Quantum theory introduces a wavefunction that the source does not identify with a physical entity in ordinary space. A pair with a common history has a joint wavefunction. If it cannot be split into individual wavefunctions even after separation, the state is nonseparable, or entangled. The chapter examines whether this mathematical nonseparability implies a physical influence.

## Interpretation and checks

The causal question and the probability question require separate answers. Specifying which event is earlier in cosmic time does not derive the observed angle dependence or evade a Bell bound. Conversely, a correlation is not itself a controllable signal. State the preparation, analyser choices, outcome pairing, and separation before asking whether a proposed causal mechanism can reproduce the data.
