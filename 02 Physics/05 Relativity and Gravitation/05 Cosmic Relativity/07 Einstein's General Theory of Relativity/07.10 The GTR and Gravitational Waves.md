> [!TLDR]
> The author discusses gravitational-wave generation and asks how their propagation speed can be expressed through gravitational coupling constants and cosmic matter.

## Physical question and model

What can a propagating gravitational wave do to freely falling test masses? In a weak-field description, a changing quadrupole produces a transverse tidal strain. Its detection compares separated paths or bodies, not a single pointwise force reading.

## Lecture account

Although it was not evident during the GTR’s extended development, Einstein quickly recognised gravitational-wave generation by accelerated masses. Electric- and magnetic-type potentials suggested propagating solutions by analogy with electrodynamics. The waves are fundamentally different, having a quadrupole nature traceable to the equivalence principle and momentum conservation.

Much has been written this decade about gravitational waves, especially after detections from massive binaries such as black holes and neutron stars near the ends of their orbits. As part of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration, it was a busy period for us, with numerous detections, public excitement, and new detector projects such as KAGRA and LIGO-India [25]. Many details are available in articles and lectures. Here I discuss less commonly discussed aspects.

Electrodynamics and gravitation are distinct interactions, as far as we know. Thus, gravitational-wave propagation is governed by gravitation’s physical features without reference to electromagnetism or the ‘velocity of light’. Although transverse waves of interactions expressed in a unified relativistic formalism should share a velocity, its numerical value should be expressible entirely through the specific interaction’s coupling constants. It is often said that weak waves in linearised general relativity propagate at light speed because the GTR is based on the STR. Yet light speed is expressible through electromagnetic constants as $\mechanical{c}^2=1/(\electric{\epsilon}_0\magnetic{\mu}_0)$. Why should gravitational-wave speed depend on electromagnetic constants? *We should be able to represent gravitational-wave speed solely through the gravitational constant and another constant characterising interactions of mass currents, or gravitomagnetism.* This follows from the interactions’ physical independence. Had waves been known before 1900, when the link between electromagnetism and light was established, we would have sought a physical link between electromagnetism and gravity, and a relation between $\mechanical{c}$ and gravitational coupling. Is this possible? The affirmative answer reiterates the role of cosmic matter’s gravity.

Which gravitational constants determine gravitational-wave speed, and how can they be measured? Gravity’s analogue of permittivity $\electric{\epsilon}_0$ is $1/\mechanical{G}$. A measurement between two known gravitomagnetic currents is needed to obtain gravitational permeability $\mechanical{\mu}_G$. Ideally, this would be an Ampère-type experiment measuring forces between gravitational charges’ currents, like measuring magnetic permeability through the force between calibrated electric currents. Without that possibility, the next ideal measurement is *gravitomagnetic interaction between spinning objects*, since spin or angular momentum is a closed circulating gravitational-charge current. Gravity Probe-B’s gyro-precession measurement supplies this (Fig. 7.6). Although its systematic errors are relatively large, it offers the only direct measurements and is suitable here. Knowing Earth’s precession effect and ‘gravitomagnetic moment’, its angular momentum, lets us estimate $\mechanical{\mu}_G$. The interaction between a gyroscope with spin $\mechanical{\mathbf s}$ and Earth with angular momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf J}$ is

$$
\energy{E}=\mechanical{\mu}_G\mechanical{\mathbf s}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf J}/\mechanical{R}^3. \tag{7.28}
$$

For a circular polar orbit, orbit-averaged gyroscope precession is

$$
\langle\mechanical{\omega}\rangle=\frac{\mechanical{\mu}_G\mechanical{J}}{2\mechanical{R}^3}=\frac{\mechanical{\mu}_G\mechanical{I}\mechanical{\Omega}}{2\mechanical{R}^3}\simeq\frac{\mechanical{\mu}_G(0.33)\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}_{\oplus}^2\mechanical{\Omega}}{2\mechanical{R}^3}. \tag{7.29}
$$

The normalised inertia $\mechanical{I}/(\mechanical{M}\mechanical{R}^2)=0.33$ is from the Earth model, and $\mechanical{J}\simeq6\times10^{33}\,\unit{kg\,m^2/s}$. Gravity Probe-B orbited at $\mechanical{R}=\mechanical{R}_E+640\,\unit{km}$. From measured precession of $37.2\pm7.2$ milliarcseconds per year, or $(5.7\pm1.1)\times10^{-15}\,\unit{rad/s}$, we obtain

$$
\mechanical{\mu}_G\simeq6.7\,(\pm1.3)\times10^{-28}\,\unit{m/kg}. \tag{7.30}
$$

Thus, $\mechanical{\mu}_G\mechanical{\epsilon}_G=\mechanical{\mu}_G/\mechanical{G}\simeq(1.0\pm0.2)\times10^{-17}\,\unit{s^2/m^2}$, and $(\mechanical{\mu}_G/\mechanical{G})^{-1}\simeq(9.9\pm1.9)\times10^{16}\,\unit{m^2/s^2}\simeq\mechanical{c}_g^2$. Although precision is low, this describes gravitational-wave speed solely through gravitational constants $\mechanical{G}$ and $\mechanical{\mu}_G$, analogous to electromagnetism through $\electric{\epsilon}_0$ and $\magnetic{\mu}_0$ [26]. Its agreement with electromagnetic light speed reflects the universality of relativity.

Therefore, *we see deeper physics linking gravity and electromagnetism*, to be explored through the new relation

$$
\mechanical{G}/\mechanical{\mu}_G=1/(\electric{\epsilon}_0\magnetic{\mu}_0)=\mechanical{c}^2\approx|\mechanical{\Phi}_U|. \tag{7.31}
$$

This indicates that the small gravitational constant is related to the large electromagnetic constant through matter in this Universe. The relation reiterates the decisive role of cosmic matter and energy’s gravity in relativistic physics.

## Interpretation and checks

Conservation of total mass-energy and momentum restricts monopole and dipole radiation for an isolated source in the usual approximation. The chapter also quotes stationary spin-precession expressions; those are not themselves wave-detection formulas. Inferring a coupling ratio from them needs consistent normalisation. Check that a uniform coordinate acceleration does not produce the same differential strain as a curvature wave.
