## Physical question and model

How should several successful gravity tests be combined? Specify which solution, source distribution, and approximation each experiment probes. General relativity supplies a common framework, while fitted masses, distances, and environmental effects still enter each prediction.

## Lecture account

Classic GTR tests, discussed extensively in textbooks and publications, examine metric terms and their variations in space and time. They include binary-system perihelion precession, light bending near isolated masses and extended structures such as galaxy clusters, gravitational redshift in laboratories and astrophysics, gravitational time dilation, Shapiro delay of light and gravitational waves crossing gravitational potentials, and spin precession in another spinning body’s field, such as Earth’s [27]. I do not review them here, but distinguish what can and cannot be tested. The GTR is extensively and successfully used in astrophysics. Neutron stars and black holes permit precise tests beyond the early triumphs of Mercury’s perihelion precession and solar light bending. Neutron-star and black-hole binaries emit gravitational waves; years of orbital monitoring have tested finer aspects of Einstein’s theory. Direct wave detection has extended these tests. No competing theory today is compelling enough for a serious comparison.

The preceding chapter discussed high-precision foundational tests. Since the theory depends closely on UFF, WEP, and EEP, considerable effort has gone into these tests over the past 50 years. At precision approaching one part in $10^{15}$, the foundations are rock solid.

Assuming these empirically based postulates, the GTR does not explain the origin of inertial forces. Thus, forces and torques acting on a turning cyclist or a spinning top on a slowly rotating table are not ‘explained’ through a source relation. This is a deficiency in a fundamental gravity and dynamics theory. The GTR provides an accurate mathematical description as consequences of fictitious fields arising from coordinate transformations.

One integral aspect that cannot be tested is general coordinate invariance. Compare this with tests of the Principle of Relativity that opened the path to relativity theories: no comparable possibility exists for general coordinate invariance. This matters because the principle determines the theory’s exact form.

As we proceed, we will see that Einstein’s GTR neither recognises nor responds to a matter source uniformly distributed throughout space. The mathematical structure permits such a source, as Einstein’s ‘cosmological constant’ shows, but this possibility was not appreciated during formulation. The Universe’s matter and energy constitute such a source. Yet Einstein’s equation does not include it, as shown by adherence to general coordinate invariance and the STR as its limit. The next sections develop this deficiency and incompleteness.

## Interpretation and checks

A local vacuum region can have curvature sourced by boundary conditions and surrounding matter. Thus a vacuum-solution test does not test an entirely empty Universe. Search for residual patterns that an alternative predicts after standard nuisance terms are included. Label a theory's untested regime separately from a demonstrated inconsistency. Astrophysical data constrain models within their source uncertainties, not by authority of a successful textbook example.
