> [!TLDR]
> The author revisits the hole argument using clock rates and argues that different metrics for the same matter distribution create a physical ambiguity.

## Physical question and model

Can a changed time–time metric component alone demonstrate a changed clock rate? The stationary-clock shortcut applies only to a worldline with fixed spatial coordinates in that chart. Under a general transformation, that same physical clock may no longer be coordinate-stationary.

## Lecture account

Consider a metric $\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{x}_\nu)$ for coordinates $\mechanical{x}_\nu$. Let $\mechanical{\gamma}_{00}=-1$, so a clock rate is determined by $d\mechanical{\tau}=\sqrt{-\mechanical{\gamma}_{00}}\,dt=dt$. Introduce a coordinate change $\mechanical{x}\to\mechanical{x}'$ with $\mechanical{x}_\nu=\mechanical{x}'_\nu$ everywhere except a cavity L. Inside L, $\mechanical{x}_0\ne\mechanical{x}'_0$, such that $\mechanical{\gamma}'_{00}(\mechanical{x}'_\nu)\ne\mechanical{\gamma}_{00}(\mechanical{x}_\nu)$, smoothly becoming equal outside L. This is exactly Einstein’s type of transformation. We then have another solution, whose form in the original coordinates, $\mechanical{\gamma}'_{00}(\mechanical{x}_\nu)$, is also valid. In a generally covariant theory, both are equally valid solutions with the same coordinates. Outside L, the matter distribution is unchanged because $\mechanical{x}'_\nu=\mechanical{x}_\nu$. Inside L, it is unchanged because there is no matter. However, *two different clock rates inside L are associated with the same matter distribution outside L*, since the rate associated with $\mechanical{\gamma}'_{00}$ is $d\mechanical{\tau}=\sqrt{-\mechanical{\gamma}'_{00}}\,dt$. This is unjustified in any physically valid theory invariant under the coordinate transformation considered.

The original hole argument is logically and mathematically transparent, apparently explaining Einstein’s conviction of its validity. It was also consistent with Mach’s principle, which he viewed as a fundamental guiding principle. No discussion since then has addressed it quantitatively. A detailed study with comments is by Stachel [19]. Most past discussions are general philosophical arguments without explicit physical situations, by philosophers interested in physics but not in deciding a physical problem.

One cannot escape Einstein’s hole by comparing it with the *gauge freedom* familiar in electrodynamics. If coordinate freedom corresponds to gauge freedom, it should not affect clocks or interferometers differently. Changing the local gravitational potential affects local clock rates and the phases of light and quantum systems. Restricting the gauge by restricting coordinate transformations violates general coordinate invariance. Einstein’s concern was genuine. Yet he chose general coordinate invariance as the appropriate GTR principle, sacrificing his earlier preference for Mach’s principle, a unique absolute reference frame, and restricted coordinate transformations (see [[07.12 The GTR and Mach's Principle|Section 7.12]]).

Einstein’s shift from conviction to hesitant denial of the hole argument can be traced to inadequate consideration of the scope of relativistic transformations. Treated merely as ‘coordinate transformations’, it is natural to demand physical invariance. But *a transformation involving time is not merely a change of inactive coordinates; it involves motion and velocity relative to the sources of gravitational fields*. A velocity transformation such as $\mechanical{x}'=\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}t$ affects spatial coordinates everywhere and also the matter distribution, for example its isotropy. This was a ‘hole’ in Einstein’s argument that proper physical insight plugs.

The hole argument acquires validity when we consider a velocity transformation in the factual Universe. There is only one Universe, whose matter distribution should determine a unique $\mechanical{\gamma}_{\mu\nu}$. A coordinate transformation changes $\mechanical{\gamma}'_{00}$, as a Galilean transformation in the hole, $\mechanical{x}'=\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{v}t$, shows explicitly. General coordinate invariance can no longer be demanded because the transformed gravitational potentials and clock rates differ in the two coordinate systems, as shown in Section 10.12. *The lesson is that the absolute frame of our single Universe does not allow freedom of general coordinate invariance.*

## Interpretation and checks

Proper time is obtained by contracting the complete metric with the transformed tangent to the worldline. Cross terms and spatial terms can compensate the change in the time–time component. The source's comparison holds selected coordinate conditions fixed while changing the chart. Recalculate along the same physical path before concluding that there are distinct predictions. This is a direct reconstruction exercise for the hole argument.
