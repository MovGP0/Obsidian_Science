> [!TLDR]
> The chapter reviews Einstein’s foundations, geometry, and gravitomagnetic predictions, then argues for completing the theory with the Universe’s gravitational background.

## Physical question and model

Review the geometric argument through three levels: local inertial measurements, curvature determined by field equations, and cosmological solutions with specified sources. The lecture adds a Machian interpretation and a proposed equation change.

## Lecture account

We reviewed fundamental aspects of Einstein’s GTR, following his reasoning during and after its development for conceptual precision. From the generalised relativity and equivalence postulates, we traced the uniquely determined theory. The central issue was whether Mach’s principle or general coordinate invariance should determine its final form. The analysis showed why coordinate invariance won, partly through external circumstances.

We examined Einstein’s abandonment of the hole argument. The argument was shown as physically and logically sound through different clock rates in a hole with the same external matter distribution. Einstein’s justification for coordinate invariance, sacrificing Mach’s principle, appears inadequate. This reinforces the need to include factual cosmic matter and gravity in the formalism as the privileged determining background, or ‘absolute’ reference.

An absolute gravitational reference implies Galilean light propagation and corresponding metrics. I demonstrated the Galilean character of the rotating-frame Langevin metric and the Schwarzschild metric outside a mass, obtained from the absolute background by Galilean transformations.

I described the geometric GTR formulation, treating gravity as fictitious force rather than physical interaction. Facts and Einstein’s own opinion do not support a purely geometric theory of unobservable space and time. Comparing gravity with electrodynamics showed differences and strong similarities. Gravitomagnetism, arising from relative matter currents, is crucial to understanding inertial forces. Their origin is not handled by the GTR because it abandoned Mach’s principle, initially trusted by Einstein as a founding principle. This was traced to the lack of relevant observations and knowledge of the large-scale Universe.

To support universal matter’s gravity as the reason for relativistic physics, I estimated gravitational-wave speed solely from gravitational interaction constants, without referring to light speed or electromagnetic constants. This implies a relation between weak gravitational and large electromagnetic couplings through the Universe.

I highlighted the inconsistency between the single factual Universe with its matter and gravity and a theory relying on coordinate transformations in empty space. This is the GTR’s incompleteness, revealed in its inability to explain inertia and inertial forces. A cosmic gravitational paradigm modifies Einstein’s theory to include the Universe’s factual matter distribution. This ‘Centenary Einstein’s equation’ has the Universe as absolute reference frame. It agrees with known tests, explains inertia and inertial forces, and is consistent with observed Galilean one-way propagation of light and gravitational waves and many other phenomena that an empty-background theory cannot explain.

The next chapter examines the observed Universe’s features relevant to relativity and dynamics.

## Interpretation and checks

Try comparing the same clock worldline in two coordinate systems. If its computed proper time changes, some component or path transformation has been omitted. Then compare two genuinely different stress–energy models: their clock predictions can differ physically. This separates coordinate covariance from empirical model choice and prepares the observational cosmology in Chapter 8.
