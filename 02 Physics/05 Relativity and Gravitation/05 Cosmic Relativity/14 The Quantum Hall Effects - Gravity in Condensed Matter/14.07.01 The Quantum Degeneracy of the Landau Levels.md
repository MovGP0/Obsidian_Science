> [!TLDR]
> The source counts Landau states through action in units of h, then adds the action associated with a cosmic gravitational vector potential.

## Physical question and model

Does an added orbital action change the number of independent quantum states? Standard Landau degeneracy follows from magnetic flux and sample area, per resolved internal component. A modified count requires a Hamiltonian and boundary conditions that support that count.

## Lecture account

A gauge choice changes orbital geometry but not Landau degeneracy $G_q$. All allowed vector potentials must satisfy $\nabla\times\magnetic{\mathbf A}=\magnetic{\mathbf B}$. The standard state density is $g_m=\electric{e}\magnetic{B}/\energy{h}=\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0$ [6, 17]. A level is full when electron sheet density equals this state density.

The author proposes that the induced cosmic vector potential contributes equally to counting states. He first derives the magnetic result directly from orbital action [19], comparing this with Bose's counting in Planck's radiation law [20]. The available action space is quantised in units of $\energy{h}$, giving $G_q=\energy{S}/\energy{h}$. For electromagnetic action $\energy{S}_{em}=\int\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A}\cdot d\mechanical{\mathbf l}$, he gives the degeneracy per area as

$$
g_m=\frac1{\energy{h}}\int\electric{e}\magnetic{\mathbf A}\cdot d\mechanical{\mathbf l}
=\frac1{\energy{h}}\iint\electric{e}(\nabla\times\magnetic{\mathbf A})\cdot d\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Sigma}
=\frac{\electric{e}\magnetic{B}}{\energy{h}}
=\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\magnetic{\phi}_0}. \tag{14.7}
$$

> [!note]
> Equation (14.7) states a density result but does not display division of the loop or surface integrals by their enclosed area. The final equality presumes that per-area normalisation.

Tilting the field by angle $\theta$ to the sample normal reduces degeneracy by $\cos\theta$. The state count is insensitive to field sign.

The author then adds the velocity-dependent cosmic potential $\mechanical{g}_{0i}=\mechanical{A}_g/\mechanical{c}=-\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{c}$, with $\mechanical{\Phi}_u/\mechanical{c}^2=1$. For each electron, its gravitational action is $\energy{S}_g=-\int\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}_i\,d\mechanical{l}$, quantised as $k\energy{\hbar}$. Unlike the externally set magnetic degeneracy, the induced gravitational contribution depends on the number of electrons actually present. The source therefore averages it over the electron ensemble using filling factor $\nu$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g&=\frac{\electric{e}\magnetic{B}}{\energy{h}}-\frac{\nu}{\energy{h}}\int\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g\cdot d\mechanical{\mathbf l}\\
&=\frac{\electric{e}\magnetic{B}}{\energy{h}}-\frac{\mechanical{m}\nu}{\energy{h}}\iint\nabla\times\mechanical{\mathbf A}_g\cdot d\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Sigma}\\
&=\frac{\electric{e}\magnetic{B}}{\energy{h}}-\frac{2\mechanical{m}\nu}{\energy{h}}\iint\signal{\boldsymbol\Omega}\cdot d\mechanical{\boldsymbol\Sigma}\\
&=\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\magnetic{\phi}_0}-\frac{2\mechanical{m}\nu\signal{\Omega}}{\energy{h}}
=\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\magnetic{\phi}_0}-\frac{2\mechanical{m}\nu\electric{e}\magnetic{B}}{\mechanical{m}\energy{h}}\\
&=\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\magnetic{\phi}_0}-2\nu\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\magnetic{\phi}_0}
=\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\magnetic{\phi}_0}-2\semiconductor{\rho}.
\end{aligned}\tag{14.8}
$$

Here $\nu=\semiconductor{\rho}/(\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0)$. By analogy with magnetic state counting, the author writes $g_g=-2\nu\electric{e}\magnetic{B}/\energy{h}=-2\nu\magnetic{b}$. Integer gravitational action gives the more general $\energy{S}_g/\energy{h}=-2k\mechanical{m}\nu\signal{\Omega}/\energy{h}=-2k\semiconductor{\rho}$, relevant below $\nu=1/3$. Thus

$$
g=\magnetic{b}(1-2k\nu)=\magnetic{b}-2k\semiconductor{\rho}.
$$

The source draws three consequences:

1. At complete filling, $\nu=1$, the magnitude of degeneracy remains $|\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0|$, preserving the IQHE. The author calls the filled-level degeneracy protected.
2. At half filling, the total degeneracy vanishes. The source predicts a Fermi sea and no QHE at $1/2$ or $1/4$, stressing that the physical magnetic field remains present.
3. At some fractional fillings, carrier density is an integer multiple of the modified degeneracy, producing the FQHE by the same filling mechanism as the IQHE. At $1/3$, degeneracy becomes the actual carrier density rather than three times it.

The source also states that complementary fractions $\nu=p/q$ and $1-p/q$ have equal degeneracy but different density-to-degeneracy ratios. It attributes the successful effective CF filling formula to this physical cosmic interaction, while rejecting changed magnetic field, attached gauge flux, or a required many-particle origin.

> [!note]
> Equation (14.8) retains the source's signs and implicit per-area normalisation. Its intermediate curl convention differs from B_g=−2Ω used elsewhere. The stated vanishing at 1/4 requires k=2 in the general formula. The filled-level claim uses the magnitude of a signed expression, not an unchanged signed value.

## Interpretation and checks

Distinguish a dimensionless total state count from its density per area; the printed integrals suppress that area normalisation. The filling-factor weighting of gravitational action is a new modelling assumption, not a consequence of Stokes’ theorem. At half filling the proposed effective count vanishes, while the ordinary electronic Hilbert space still exists. Derive how the claimed effective states arise and avoid treating an occupation average as an automatic change in the number of available states.
