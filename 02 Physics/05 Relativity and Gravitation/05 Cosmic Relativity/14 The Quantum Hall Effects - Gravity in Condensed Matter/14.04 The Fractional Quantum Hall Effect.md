title: "The Fractional Quantum Hall Effect"

> [!TLDR]
> Fractional filling factors produce Hall plateaux with the same broad transport signatures as integer plateaux, but conventional theory assigns them a strongly interacting origin.

## Physical question and model

Why can a partially filled Landau level form a distinct Hall state? The nearly degenerate kinetic spectrum makes interactions important in selecting a many-electron ground state. A fractional plateau is then a collective phase, not simply an integer plateau with a relabelled density.

## Lecture account

The source contrasts the similar measured signatures of IQHE and FQHE with their different conventional explanations [9, 10]. In paraphrase, Laughlin's Nobel lecture [11] states that a fractional Hall state cannot be continuously mapped to a noninteracting electron state. Gauge invariance and charge discreteness preserve integer conductance in the noninteracting limit, making the fractional state a new state of matter.

The same lecture notes their nearly identical phenomenology: Hall plateaux, conductance equal to a pure number times $\electric{e}^2/\energy{h}$, and zero longitudinal resistance and conductance on a plateau. Finite-temperature departures follow activated or Mott variable-range-hopping behaviour. The key qualitative difference is the conductance quantum. The author regards this similarity as a reason to seek one physical explanation.

The original observation [7] showed a $\nu=1/3$ plateau as temperature fell through the plotted values $4.15$, $1.65$, $1.00$, and $0.48\,\unit{K}$. A later experiment [12] at about $35\,\unit{mK}$ resolved intermingled integer and fractional states between $\nu=3$ and $1/4$. Visible labels include $7/2$, $5/2$, $2$, $5/3$, $3/2$, $4/3$, $1$, $3/4$, $2/3$, $3/5$, $4/7$, $5/9$, $1/2$, $10/19$, $10/21$, $4/9$, $3/7$, $2/5$, $4/11$, $1/3$, $2/7$, $6/23$, $1/4$, and $6/25$.

The author argues that if Coulomb and many-particle effects are small at $\nu=1$ or $2$, they should also be small at nearby $\nu=4/3$ or $7/3$. He regards their conventional theoretical separation as unresolved and proposes a unified cosmic-gravity theory [13]. Existing fractional theories are described as phenomenologically successful but dependent on different quasiparticle constructions rather than one agreed microscopic account.

## Interpretation and checks

Do not infer that different phases require different fundamental laws. One electronic Hamiltonian can support several phases as field, density, and interaction ratios change. Conversely, an alternative mechanism must calculate the stability and excitation structure of its proposed state. A matching Hall ratio is one check; fractional charge, exchange behaviour, and edge response provide additional tests.
