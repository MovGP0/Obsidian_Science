title: "The Excitation Energy Gaps"

> [!TLDR]
> Measured thermal activation gaps constrain the energies of fractional Hall sublevels, but the source argues that the available trends do not select one microscopic interpretation.

## Physical question and model

What does temperature-dependent longitudinal transport measure? An activated model extracts an energy scale from the slope of logarithmic resistivity against inverse temperature. The factor of two assumes the specified creation or mobility-gap picture.

## Lecture account

The proposed lowest-level FQHE needs Coulomb-separated sublevels. Gaps have been inferred from temperature-dependent longitudinal resistivity [22], assuming

$$
\circuit{\rho}_{xx}=\circuit{\rho}_0\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{2\thermal{k}\thermal{T}}\right). \tag{14.13}
$$

The source regards their magnitudes as consistent with Coulomb energy at the measured density. Gaps approximately linear in $\Delta\magnetic{B}_\nu=\magnetic{B}_\nu-\magnetic{B}_{1/2}$ have been used to support composite fermions. The author argues that similar approximate trends occur against $\nu$ or $\sqrt\nu$ [13], so the measurement range cannot distinguish the interpretations.

He writes magnetic length $\mechanical{l}_0=\sqrt{2\pi\magnetic{\phi}_0/\magnetic{B}}=\sqrt{2\pi/g_m}$ and Coulomb energy $\energy{E}_C\simeq\electric{e}^2/(\epsilon\mechanical{l}_0)\propto\electric{e}^2\sqrt{g_m}$. With $\nu=ng$, the printed scaling is

$$
\energy{E}_C\propto\frac1{\sqrt n}\electric{e}^2\sqrt\nu. \tag{14.14}
$$

The source concludes that the activation gaps are compatible with its gravitational theory but do not prove a unique microscopic configuration.

> [!note]
> The source's magnetic-length normalisation differs from the usual reduced-Planck-constant form. It also uses ν both as a dimensionless filling factor and in the relation ν=ng, where g has density units. Equation (14.14) is retained as printed. Coulomb expressions use the source's non-SI convention.

## Interpretation and checks

The exponent must be dimensionless. Disorder broadening, variable-range hopping, and temperature-dependent prefactors can change the inferred gap or invalidate the fit. The Coulomb scale needs the dielectric constant and unit-system convention when made quantitative. A proportionality with filling or level index is not a numerical prediction without its coefficient and fixed sample parameters. Compare the same temperature window and uncertainty for competing gap models.
