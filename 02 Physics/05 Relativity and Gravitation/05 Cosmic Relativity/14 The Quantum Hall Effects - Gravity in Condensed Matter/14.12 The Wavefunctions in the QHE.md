> [!TLDR]
> The source interprets orbital and Laughlin wavefunction phase factors through action-based state counting rather than fractional carrier charge.

## Physical question and model

What information is encoded in a quantum Hall wavefunction? The polynomial sets phase, angular structure, and correlations, while the Gaussian enforces the magnetic length scale and normalisability. A many-particle state is more than an orbit-counting label.

## Lecture account

The lowest-level single-particle states are written, without normalisation,

$$
\psi_s=z^m\exp\left(-\frac{|z|^2}{4\mechanical{l}_0^2}\right), \tag{14.22}
$$

where $z=\mechanical{x}+i\mechanical{y}$ is the electron's complex planar coordinate and $m$ is the angular-momentum eigenvalue. The many-particle Laughlin wavefunction [8] is

$$
\Psi_L=\prod_{i<j}(z_j-z_i)^m
\exp\left(-\sum_i\frac{|z_i|^2}{4\mechanical{l}_0^2}\right). \tag{14.23}
$$

Odd $m$ describes filling $\nu=1/m$. The author links this to his action-based degeneracy view. He writes $z^m=\mechanical{r}^m\exp(im\energy{S}/\energy{\hbar})$ and argues that $\energy{S}/\energy{h}$ counts states. Applied electromagnetic and motion-induced cosmic gravitational fields both contribute orbital action. Their ensemble effect makes degeneracy depend on actual carrier density.

For quantum cyclotron motion, the source assigns magnetic and gravitational action contributions in the ratio $1:-2\nu$. At $m=3$, he regards the Laughlin state as expressing this modified degeneracy rather than a fractional charge $1/m$ or a reduced physical magnetic field.

> [!note]
> The phase identification in z^m uses action in place of the usual polar angle and is the source's interpretive proposal. Normalising factors and dimensional powers are not supplied in these printed wavefunctions.

## Interpretation and checks

When two coordinates meet, the Laughlin factor makes the amplitude vanish to a power, suppressing close encounters. Exchanging them changes the sign for odd powers, consistent with a fully spin-polarised fermion state. The same power also changes spatial correlations; it cannot be justified by a spin phase alone. Normalise the state and calculate density or pair correlations before asserting equivalence with another proposed microscopic account.
