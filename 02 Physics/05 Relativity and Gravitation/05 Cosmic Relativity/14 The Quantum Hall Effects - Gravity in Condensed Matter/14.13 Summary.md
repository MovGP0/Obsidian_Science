> [!TLDR]
> The chapter proposes a density-dependent cosmic correction to Landau degeneracy as one explanation of integer and fractional Hall effects.

## Physical question and model

Review the chain from the proposed gravitational potential to action, effective state count, candidate fractions, and transport. Each arrow requires an independent physical argument. A shared rational fraction sequence leaves many of these steps untested.

## Lecture account

Clean two-dimensional conductors at strong field and low temperature show Hall plateaux at discrete filling factors. The author contrasts an accepted approximately independent-particle IQHE account with fractional theories based on strong correlations, fractional quasiparticles, attached gauge flux, and hierarchies.

His alternative adds the cosmic gravitational potential induced in cyclotron motion. The proposed state density changes from $g_m=\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0$ to

$$
g=\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\magnetic{\phi}_0}-2k\semiconductor{\rho},
$$

with integer $k$. It therefore depends on actual electron density as well as magnetic field. The author claims that this one change gives all integer and fractional filling factors without changing the field or carrier charge. The chapter identifies conceptual and physical objections to existing hierarchy accounts before developing this proposal.

The source presents this as a unified independent-particle theory. It predicts no ordinary half-filled plateau at $1/2$ or $1/4$ and proposes spatial mixing of nearby states for $5/2$. Graphene's electron and hole branches are treated through the same state-count mechanism, despite their different energy quantisation.

Charge and heat transport along one-dimensional edge channels provide additional tests. The author regards available thermal measurements as encouraging but limited, and states that more data are needed. Finally, he interprets the Laughlin wavefunction exponent as the cosmic modification of action, rather than as fractional particle charge.

The chapter concludes by claiming a major laboratory condensed-matter effect of large-scale gravity and an integrated microscopic explanation of the QHE.

## Interpretation and checks

Reconstruct the one-third filling example, then identify why the same integer-filling calculation fails at one-half. Explain separately what fixes the orbital state count, the many-body gap, and the edge response. Finally, choose a measurement that could distinguish the cosmic correction from an effective many-electron description. Treat the proposed gap and thermal bounds as predictions to evaluate with sample conditions and uncertainty, rather than confirmed universal results.
