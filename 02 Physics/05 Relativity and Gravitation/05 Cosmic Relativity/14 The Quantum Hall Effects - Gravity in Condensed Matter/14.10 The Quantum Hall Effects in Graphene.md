> [!TLDR]
> Monolayer graphene provides a two-dimensional Hall system whose lattice symmetry modifies the allowed state structure.

## Physical question and model

Why does graphene have a different Landau spectrum from a parabolic-band electron gas? Its low-energy band Hamiltonian couples sublattice components with an approximately linear dispersion. Spin and valley supply additional degeneracy in the regime where they remain unresolved.

## Lecture account

A single atomic layer of graphene is a useful two-dimensional material for testing microscopic QHE theories [23]. Carbon atoms form a periodic hexagonal arrangement with a basic triangular spatial periodicity.

The lattice has two equivalent sublattices. Unlike a semiconductor 2DEG, nearest-neighbour hopping dominates conduction. Ordinary nonrelativistic dispersion is $\energy{E}=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2/2=\mechanical{p}^2/(2\mechanical{m})$, while graphene has $\energy{E}=\mechanical{p}\mechanical{v}_F$, with Fermi velocity about $10^6\,\unit{m/s}$. Its conical dispersion resembles the photon relation $\energy{E}=\mechanical{p}\mechanical{c}$.

Graphene's electron and hole Landau energies are

$$
\energy{E}_n=\pm\frac{\sqrt2\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{v}_F\sqrt n}{\mechanical{l}_0}
=\pm\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{v}_F\sqrt{\frac{2\electric{e}\magnetic{B}n}{\energy{\hbar}}}
=\pm\mechanical{v}_F\sqrt{2\energy{\hbar}\electric{e}\magnetic{B}n}. \tag{14.15}
$$

These differ from 2DEG energies $\energy{E}_n=\energy{\hbar}\electric{e}\magnetic{B}(n+1/2)/\mechanical{m}$. Graphene's successive gaps decrease as the level index rises. Despite material differences, its QHE resembles other two-dimensional systems [24].

The author seeks a nonrelativistic action-counting derivation rather than interpreting graphene carriers as freely moving massless Dirac particles. Site-to-site hopping fixes the mean velocity by $\mechanical{v}_F\simeq\mechanical{a}\signal{\nu}_h$, where $\mechanical{a}$ is lattice spacing and $\signal{\nu}_h$ the hopping frequency. Quantising the action gives

$$
2\pi n=\frac1{\energy{\hbar}}\int\energy{E}_n\,dt
=\frac{2\pi\energy{E}_n}{\energy{\hbar}\signal{\omega}_c}
=\frac{2\pi\energy{E}_n\mechanical{r}_n}{\sqrt2\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{v}_F}. \tag{14.16}
$$

The source calls $\mechanical{r}_n$ a notional orbital radius because the hopping path is not literally circular. It states $\signal{\omega}_c=\sqrt2\mechanical{v}_F/\mechanical{r}_0$ and requires successive orbit areas to differ by one flux quantum, giving $\mechanical{r}_n^2=n\mechanical{r}_0^2$. It obtains

$$
\energy{E}_n=\frac{\sqrt2\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{v}_Fn}{\mechanical{r}_n}
=\frac{\sqrt2\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{v}_Fn}{\sqrt n\mechanical{r}_0}
=\energy{\hbar}\mechanical{v}_F\sqrt{\frac{2\electric{e}\magnetic{B}}{\energy{\hbar}}}\sqrt n
=\mechanical{v}_F\sqrt{2\energy{\hbar}\electric{e}\magnetic{B}n}. \tag{14.17}
$$

He attributes the square-root spectrum to fixed velocity [13]. In a 2DEG it is cyclotron frequency, rather than velocity, that remains fixed.

Gate voltage makes density sweeps easy at fixed field. The source prints $\nu=\electric{e}\semiconductor{\rho}/(\energy{h}\magnetic{B})$ and gives magnetic degeneracy $g=\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0$. The zero-density Dirac point is shared by electron and hole branches, so no plateau occurs at the central zero level. Two spin projections and two sublattices add a fourfold degeneracy. The complete-filling relation is $\semiconductor{\rho}/(\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0)=4(\nu+1/2)$, with Hall conductivity in steps of $4\electric{e}^2/\energy{h}$.

The author highlights questions about impurity localisation in a single atomic layer, robust plateaux at relatively high temperature, and the density dependence of longitudinal conductivity. Fractional states in graphene [25] offer further tests when high fields lift the fourfold degeneracy. Observed fractions cited include $1/3$, $2/3$, $3/4$, $4/7$, $4/3$, $5/3$, and $7/3$. Gap estimates and field trends are described as mixed and needing further study. Monolayer, bilayer, Corbino, electron, and hole measurements are all proposed as tests of microscopic theories.

> [!note]
> The printed filling-factor expression eρ/(hB) differs from the earlier definition ρh/(eB). The orbit-frequency statement uses r₀ while equation (14.16) uses rₙ. The magnetic-length convention in this derivation also differs from the one printed in section 14.8.3. These source details are retained.

The author concludes that the similar integer and fractional phenomenology in graphene supports the same single-particle cosmic state-count mechanism, despite graphene's different dispersion.

## Interpretation and checks

The Fermi velocity is a material velocity, not the invariant light speed of spacetime relativity. Fixed speed alone does not derive the two-component Hamiltonian or its zero-energy level. Check the square-root field scaling and the special zero level. The familiar fourfold sequence depends on unresolved spin and valley states; symmetry breaking can alter it. Vanishing net carrier density at neutrality is not the absence of zero-level quantum states.
