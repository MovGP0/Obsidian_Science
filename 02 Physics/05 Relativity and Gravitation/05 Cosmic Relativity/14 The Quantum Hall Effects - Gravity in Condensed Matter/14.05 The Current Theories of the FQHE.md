> [!TLDR]
> Fractional-charge and composite-fermion theories represent fractional Hall states through different effective quasiparticles and strong correlations.

## Physical question and model

What role do Laughlin states and composite fermions play in fractional Hall theory? A correlated trial wavefunction and an effective flux description organise different aspects of the same electronic many-body problem. Their explanatory value depends on calculated observables and their stated approximation.

## Lecture account

The source introduces the first $\nu=1/3$ observation in an ultrapure sample, quoting $5\,\unit{mK}$ and $15\,\unit{T}$ here. At this filling, carrier density is one third of the Landau degeneracy $\electric{e}\magnetic{B}/\energy{h}$. The question is how an effectively filled state can arise when density and external field are fixed by the experiment.

Laughlin proposed fractionally charged quasiparticles [8]. The source writes their charge as $e'=1/3$, meaning one third of the electron charge. This reduces effective degeneracy by three and is implemented through a many-particle wavefunction that accounts for the $1/3$ plateau.

Further plateaux required more structure: fractions included $1/5$, $1/7$, $2/3$, $3/5$, $2/5$, $3/7$, $4/5$, $4/7$, and states above one such as $4/3$, $5/3$, $7/5$, and $7/3$. Hierarchy theories combined fractional quasiparticles, pairing, and condensation to generate these states [14, 15]. Their strong Coulomb correlations contrast with the approximately independent-particle IQHE treatment, which is the author's main criticism.

J. K. Jain's composite-fermion (CF) scheme [16, 17] instead retains the full electron charge while reducing the effective field. For $\nu=1/3$, reduction by three maps the state to effective integer filling. Even numbers of gauge-field flux quanta are attached to electrons to form particle–vortex composites. The even number preserves fermionic character while reducing the effective field. The source stresses that this attached gauge flux is not the physical Maxwell magnetic flux.

> [!note]
> The temperature quoted in this section differs from the 0.48 K lowest trace in the source's Fig. 14.7. Both are retained as source statements.

In Jain's introductory paper [16], the IQHE is approximately noninteracting while the FQHE is a correlated collective state. Parent fractions such as $1/3$ and $1/5$ generate daughter states through successive quasiparticle condensations.

Each composite fermion carries an even number $2p$ of flux quanta $\magnetic{\phi}_0=\energy{h}/\electric{e}$ opposite to the applied field. The effective field is postulated as

$$
\magnetic{B}^*=\magnetic{B}-2p\semiconductor{\rho}\magnetic{\phi}_0. \tag{14.5}
$$

The filling factor becomes

$$
\nu^*=\frac{\semiconductor{\rho}}{\magnetic{B}^*/\magnetic{\phi}_0}
=\frac{\semiconductor{\rho}}{\magnetic{b}-2p\semiconductor{\rho}}
=\frac{\nu}{1-2p\nu}. \tag{14.6}
$$

At $\nu=1/3$ and $p=1$, $\nu^*=1$: the FQHE becomes the IQHE of composite fermions. Even flux attachment both preserves fermionic character and generates odd-denominator fractions. At $\nu=1/2$, the effective field vanishes, removing cyclotron levels and the corresponding plateau. The source notes accurate effective wavefunctions related to Laughlin's.

The author's objections are that gauge flux and real magnetic flux have different physical origins, that reducing a real-space field affects all Landau levels, and that extra hierarchy assumptions remain necessary for the full range of fractions and levels above one. He also questions why attached flux should disappear and reappear as density or field changes slightly, including in parameter ranges treated as weakly interacting for the IQHE.

He regards the effective theories as useful but incomplete, with different quasiparticles and different physical assumptions. Jain's comparison [18] distinguishes the Haldane–Halperin hierarchy from CF theory: they propose different microscopic mechanisms and can make experimentally different predictions. The source therefore calls for a unified integer and fractional theory.

## Interpretation and checks

The effective field in the composite-fermion mapping is not a claim that the externally measured Maxwell field has literally changed. At one-third filling, the displayed mapping gives unit effective filling; at one-half it becomes singular. Check both limits before interpreting it. Matching this algebra with a cosmic correction does not make their microscopic assumptions identical. The source’s quoted temperature also differs from its reproduced experimental caption, so retain the recorded discrepancy when tracing the original experiment.
