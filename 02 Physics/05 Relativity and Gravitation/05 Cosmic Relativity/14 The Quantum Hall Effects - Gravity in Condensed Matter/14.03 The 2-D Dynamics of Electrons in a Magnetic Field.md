> [!TLDR]
> Circular magnetic motion has a speed-independent cyclotron frequency and quantised Landau energies separated by ℏω꜀.

## Physical question and model

How does a perpendicular magnetic field quantise planar orbital motion? For a parabolic band, the Lorentz force sets the cyclotron frequency, and quantisation gives equally spaced oscillator energies. The orbital state count is a separate property from that spacing.

## Lecture account

Continuous magnetic deflection produces a cyclotron orbit. Balancing the magnetic Lorentz force and the circular-motion force gives

$$
\frac{\mechanical{m}\mechanical{v}^2}{\mechanical{r}}=\electric{e}\mechanical{v}\magnetic{B}. \tag{14.3}
$$

With $\signal{\omega}=\mechanical{v}/\mechanical{r}$, this gives $\signal{\omega}=\electric{e}\magnetic{B}/\mechanical{m}$. Different speeds and radii therefore have the same angular frequency. A larger orbit has higher speed and kinetic energy. This degeneracy is crucial to quantum dynamics.

![[cosmic-relativity-14-03-cyclotron-orbit.svg|**Fig. 14.3** Cyclotron motion of a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field between magnet poles. The red arrow indicates the particle's spin direction.]]

Two-dimensional electron motion in a strong field has Landau levels with constant spacing

$$
\Delta\energy{E}=\energy{\hbar}\signal{\omega}_c=\frac{\energy{\hbar}\electric{e}\magnetic{B}}{\mechanical{m}},
\qquad
\energy{E}_n=\left(n+\frac12\right)\energy{\hbar}\signal{\omega}_c.
$$

The lowest level includes a zero-point energy (Fig. 14.4).

> The higher-level labels in Fig. 14.4 do not include the half-quantum shift in the preceding energy formula. The source's labels and its stated equal-spacing description are preserved as separate information.

Electrons repel through a Coulomb energy proportional to $\electric{e}^2/\mechanical{r}$. This alters exact energies, but the source treats typical low-density motion approximately as single-particle dynamics. It states that the cyclotron gap is usually greater than mean Coulomb energy in the dielectric medium.

Many spatial states can have the same energy. Their number is the Landau-level degeneracy. Two opposite spins can normally occupy each orbital; at high field the electrons are instead spin-polarised. Once a level is full, additional electrons occupy the next level. The stated degeneracy per unit area is $\magnetic{B}/\magnetic{\phi}_0$.

At integer filling $\nu$, the carrier sheet density is an integer multiple of this flux-quantum density, so

$$
\circuit{R}_H=\frac{\electric{V}_y}{\electric{i}_x}
=\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\semiconductor{\rho}\electric{e}}
=\frac{\magnetic{B}}{\nu(\electric{e}\magnetic{B}/\energy{h})\electric{e}}
=\frac{\energy{h}}{\nu\electric{e}^2}. \tag{14.4}
$$

Density and field have not disappeared; their ratio is encoded in $\nu$. The Hall conductance at complete filling is an integer multiple of $\electric{e}^2/\energy{h}$. This alone does not explain the observed finite plateau width around integer filling. That requires the role of impurities and localisation.

Impurities localise electronic states and stabilise plateaux over a finite field range. The source emphasises that the conventional IQHE account remains approximately an independent-particle theory with minor interaction effects, rather than invoking strong many-particle correlations [5, 6]. Details follow in [[14.03.01 The Theory of the IQHE Plateaux]].

## Interpretation and checks

Use the effective band mass appropriate to the material. Each orbital Landau level has one state per flux quantum per spin component, with additional spin or valley degeneracy stated separately. In the weak-field limit the spacing tends to zero. Check the zero-point offset: the displayed formula gives the first excited orbital level three halves of the level spacing. A classical circular orbit motivates the frequency but does not alone derive the full quantum spectrum.
