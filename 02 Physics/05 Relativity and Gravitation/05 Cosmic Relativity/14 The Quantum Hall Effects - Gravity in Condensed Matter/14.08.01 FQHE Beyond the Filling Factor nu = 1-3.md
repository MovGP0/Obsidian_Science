> [!TLDR]
> The source extends its state-count formula to smaller filling factors by assigning higher integer gravitational-action quanta.

## Physical question and model

What predicts the extra integer used to extend the fraction sequence? If it labels gravitational action, the model must explain how preparation and energy select that action. Choosing it after observing a fraction weakens the prediction.

## Lecture account

The primary sequence omits states such as $1/5$, $2/7$, and $1/7$. The author invokes gravitational action $\energy{S}_g=k\energy{\hbar}$ with $k>1$ to extend the theory. He prints

$$
\frac{\semiconductor{\rho}}g=\nu^*=\frac{\nu^*}{1-2k\nu}. \tag{14.12}
$$

For $k=2$, the degeneracy changes from $\magnetic{b}(1-2\nu)$ to $\magnetic{b}(1-4\nu)$, described as doubling the gravitational phase. At $\nu=1/5$, the corrected level has $\semiconductor{\rho}/g=1$. Further fillings $2/7$, $3/11$, and, as printed, $4/11$ are associated with effective fillings 2, 3, and 4. Another sequence includes $2/9$ and $3/13$.

With $k=3$, the sequence extends to $1/7$. Adding filled levels gives fractions above one, including $6/5$, $9/7$, $11/9$, $16/13$, $8/7$, and $15/13$. The author claims a unified account without quasiparticle hierarchies or phase transitions: induced cosmic potentials change degeneracy according to field and actual carrier density, allowing independent-particle filling at all observed integer and fractional factors.

> [!note]
> Equation (14.12) prints ν* in its own numerator; the preceding equation (14.10) uses ν there and a magnitude in the denominator. The printed sequence also changes from 4/15 to 4/11, although effective filling four in the stated k=2 expression gives 4/15 on that branch. These are source inconsistencies.

## Interpretation and checks

Use the intended density-to-degeneracy ratio consistently; the printed equation repeats the effective filling on its right-hand numerator, as the transcription note records. For the second correction and one-fifth filling, the intended ratio is one. This arithmetic checks the sequence extension but does not derive which states appear or their relative strength. Compare fractions predicted but absent under the same experimental conditions.
