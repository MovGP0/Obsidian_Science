> [!TLDR]
> The author proposes that some half-integer Hall plateaux are spatial mixtures of nearby filled states rather than distinct fractional states.

## Physical question and model

Can mixed integer domains reproduce a robust half-integer Hall state? The source proposes spatial averaging as a way to account for a state its primary filling formula does not describe. This is an alternative physical hypothesis requiring a transport calculation.

## Lecture account

The spin-polarised $\nu=5/2=2+1/2$ state [21] does not naturally fit the source's theory, which predicts no plateau at half filling. It occurs only in some samples and special conditions. Magnetoresistance at $7/2$ and $9/2$ is usually similar; a rare $3/2$ state is also discussed.

The proposed explanation is microscopic spatial mixing of $\nu=2$ and $3$ IQHE regions, producing an average intermediate plateau rather than a distinct $5/2$ state. Mixing neighbouring $7/3$ and $8/3$ states is offered as another possibility. At very low temperature in clean samples, the measured resistivity could average these regions while longitudinal resistivity remains zero.

The author proposes cycling the field between the neighbouring integer states to look for rearrangement or annealing of this spatial mixture. He expects the $3/2$ case to be harder because the field interval between $1$ and $2$ is wider. The source presents this as a testable proposal, not a confirmed observation of mixed domains.

## Interpretation and checks

A half-integer filling is not an arithmetic mean of the adjacent Hall resistances. Parallel, series, and two-dimensional domain networks also give different effective responses. Derive the measured tensor and longitudinal dissipation for the proposed geometry before assigning a plateau value. Field cycling could test domain rearrangement, but the suggested test is not a reported observation. A robust gap, contact response, and reproducible plateau require explanation beyond a schematic mixture.
