## Physical question and model

Which electron–hole symmetry is under discussion? A valence-band hole in a solid and particle–hole conjugation within a partly filled Landau level are distinct constructions. State the band, level, spin, and valley restrictions before comparing complementary fractions.

## Lecture account

Both electrons and holes show the QHE. In monolayer graphene their branches are especially symmetric as gate voltage scans carrier density across zero. A hole is already a quasiparticle representing collective valence-electron transport. Its microscopic environment differs from that of a conduction electron.

Hole orbits turn oppositely to electron orbits in the same field. The proposed motion-induced cosmic field reverses too. Although it then points along the applied field, the positive hole charge reverses the magnetic coupling, so the two contributions still oppose one another. The author claims electron–hole symmetry without extra assumptions. He criticises flux attachment to holes in effective theories because both the hole and the attached gauge flux are constructed entities.

## Interpretation and checks

In an ideal isolated Landau level, a particle–hole transformation exchanges occupied and empty orbitals. Landau-level mixing and other symmetry-breaking terms can modify that relation. The existence of holes as quasiparticles does not invalidate other emergent excitations. Compare complementary fractions with the same sample and resolved internal components; similar plateau patterns alone do not identify a cosmic mechanism.
