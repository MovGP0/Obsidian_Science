## How to use these notes

These notes preserve a lecture account of Cosmic Relativity and add explanations for an undergraduate reader with calculus, vectors, and basic quantum mechanics. General-relativistic and many-particle sections retain their more advanced material. Review the linked introductory chapters when a mathematical prerequisite is missing.

In a content note, **Physical question and model** states the problem and the assumptions needed to study it. **Lecture account** preserves the source presentation, including its proposed mechanisms and recorded transcription cautions. **Interpretation and checks** adds physical reasoning, limiting cases, and questions about evidence. These added checks are study guidance, not quotations from the lecture. Numbered equations and bracketed reference numbers retain the source numbering.

Keep four categories separate when reading: an observation, an assumed model, a consequence derived within that model, and a proposed interpretation. For example, the observed microwave-background dipole defines a useful cosmic reference, but a universal gravitational origin for inertia requires additional dynamics. Similarly, a correct predicted phase does not uniquely establish the interaction proposed to explain it.
