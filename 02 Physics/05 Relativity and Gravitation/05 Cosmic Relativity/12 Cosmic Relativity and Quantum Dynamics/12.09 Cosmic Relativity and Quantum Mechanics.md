## Physical question and model

How would the proposed action-wave mechanics couple to cosmic gravity? State whether the metric enters the particle action, phase, Hamiltonian, or all three, and keep their units consistent. A phase shift requires an energy-time or momentum-distance action difference.

## Lecture account

The chapter's proposed result is that macroscopic classical mechanics and microscopic atomic dynamics have no fundamental division. Both are governed by one universal mechanics involving action waves.

The modified Hamilton equation is presented as the universal law. Intrinsic uncertainty in the initial action makes all dynamics indeterminate at the corresponding scale. The source gives $\Delta\energy{S}\ge\energy{\hbar}/2$. Since $\energy{\hbar}$ is very small, this matters appreciably only when the action of the physical situation is comparable to it. The author claims that this describes systems from light to planets within one framework.

Particles and action waves occupy real space, so the proposed dynamics can couple directly to cosmic gravity. Cosmic matter-energy affects a microscopic system just as it affects a macroscopic one. The central claim is that *cosmic gravitational potentials alter the system's action phase*. The author argues that all gravitational dynamical results can be obtained from that phase coupling without treating quantum mechanics as a provisional addition to Cosmic Relativity.

## Interpretation and checks

A common phase applied to every interfering branch is unobservable in their probabilities. Only a relative phase survives a comparison. Compute the full path difference before assigning a measurable cosmic effect. A model that agrees with the standard mass-dependent gravitational phase is consistent with that test but not uniquely selected by it. The next chapters must use the same coupling and normalisation.
