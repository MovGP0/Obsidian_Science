## Physical question and model

What does particle diffraction establish about a matter-wave model? It demonstrates phase-dependent probabilities tied to momentum and preparation. It does not require a classical material ripple in a medium, nor does a discrete detection event eliminate an amplitude description.

## Lecture account

Quantum mechanics began with the spectrum of radiation from a heated body. Planck's discrete radiation elements gained a particle interpretation when Einstein explained the photoelectric effect with light quanta. A particle picture was not entirely new: light had been described as a stream of microscopic particles before wave theory and experiments established its wave behaviour.

Quantisation also appeared in the charged-particle dynamics producing radiation. Bohr's discrete electron orbits around a nucleus suggested a generally quantised microscopic world. In 1923, de Broglie proposed that matter combines particle and wave properties [4], matching several atomic and radiation observations. Schrödinger then formulated a spatial wave equation for those proposed matter waves [3].

Born, Heisenberg, and Jordan developed a parallel matrix formulation [17] without directly describing particle or wave dynamics. It gave the same results, while the uncertainty principle supplied physical and philosophical guidance for the theory's statistical character. Born's rule connected wavefunctions to probabilities. Dirac [18] and von Neumann [19] supplied a rigorous mathematical framework.

The author argues that this formal completion did not resolve indeterminism, collapse, measurement, or nonlocality, despite a century of work. He claims that an ensemble interpretation of the Schrödinger equation and a completed action mechanics remove those problems and provide universal dynamics across sizes, masses, and velocities.

In this proposal, matter itself has no wave properties. Only dynamical action is periodic, and this complex-action periodicity produces irreducible indeterminism at all scales. The source gives the unified uncertainty statement here as $\Delta\energy{S}\ge\energy{\hbar}$.

For a fixed momentum $\mechanical{p}$, action-wave periodicity becomes spatial periodicity $\mechanical{l}=\energy{h}/\mechanical{p}$. The source writes the periodicity condition as $\mechanical{l}\mechanical{p}/\energy{h}=1$ for $\exp(i\energy{S}/\energy{\hbar})$. It argues that this spatial dynamical periodicity was misidentified as a matter-wave wavelength.

> [!note]
> This section prints the action-uncertainty bound with $\energy{\hbar}$, while section 12.3 used $\energy{\hbar}/2$. Both source forms are retained.

The author presents removal of matter waves as clarifying dynamics and resolving the divergent vacuum zero-point-energy problem, considered next.

## Interpretation and checks

Separate the empirical wavelength–momentum relation from an ontological picture of what travels between source and detector. A proposed action wave can be compared through the same interference observable. To distinguish interpretations experimentally, identify an additional prediction, such as a preparation or coupling dependence. The title records the source's rejection of a literal matter wave rather than a refutation of observed matter-wave interference.
