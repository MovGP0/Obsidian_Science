> [!TLDR]
> The author revisits earlier continuity-equation formulations and interprets their quantum potential as a manifestation of action-wave dynamics.

## Physical question and model

How are trajectory equations related to quantum probability flow? Define the many-particle configuration space, probability density, phase, and any guidance or force law. A continuity equation alone does not choose those laws.

## Lecture account

The chapter began by expressing ensemble probability continuity as the sum of the Schrödinger equation and its conjugate. The source identifies $\signal{\psi}$ as a single-valued complex square root of density, $\signal{\rho}=\signal{\psi}\signal{\psi}^*$, belonging to an ensemble of dynamical histories.

The relation between continuity and Schrödinger equations was noted early by Madelung [12], Kennard [13], de Broglie [14], and Bohm [15]. The author points out that Kennard also appears in the [[10.14 The Unipolar Induction|unipolar-induction discussion]]. He argues that these researchers instead treated the Schrödinger equation as one history's dynamical law and did not recognise its ensemble role.

In the author's account, that assumption led to a nonlocal quantum potential controlling a particle in real space. He instead interprets the potential as a manifestation of wave-like action dynamics. The following passage quotes Kennard [13].

> Thus each element of the probability moves in the Cartesian space of each particle as that particle should move according to Newton's laws under the classical force plus a “quantum force”...

The source identifies this with

$$
\mechanical{m}_j\frac{d\mechanical{v}_j}{dt}=\frac{\energy{\hbar}^2}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{x}_j}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\mechanical{m}_i\signal{A}}\frac{\partial^2\signal{A}}{\partial\mechanical{x}_i^2}-\frac{\partial\energy{V}}{\partial\mechanical{x}_j}. \tag{12.25}
$$

The author argues that the appearance of $\signal{A}=\sqrt{\signal{\rho}}$ results from replacing the action relation $d\mechanical{p}/dt=\partial(\partial\energy{S}/\partial\mechanical{x})/\partial t$ by $\partial(\partial\energy{S}/\partial t)/\partial\mechanical{x}$, then using Schrödinger evolution to express it through density-amplitude derivatives.

He criticises the resulting dependence of particle $j$'s velocity on all masses $\mechanical{m}_i$, even for mutually independent, noninteracting particles. He instead assigns that dependence to a joint ensemble probability density, rather than to one particle's physical state. In his account, de Broglie–Bohm interpretations [15] mistake an ensemble evolution for individual-particle dynamics.

The source argues that continuity gives the Schrödinger equation, rather than the reverse, and rejects obtaining an ensemble law solely from one particle's equation without averaging or a multi-particle system. He attributes the nonlocality of de Broglie–Bohm theory to this interpretational error [16].

The author then contrasts two approaches to a new fundamental result. One begins with established physical ideas and identifies the new result's differences. The other treats the new result as primary and works backwards to earlier concepts despite unresolved meaning. He argues that quantum theory followed the second route, assuming Schrödinger evolution to be the master law and classical mechanics an approximation.

Connections with continuity, Hamilton action, Newtonian mechanics, Lagrangian mechanics, and Poisson brackets were found, but the author says they did not resolve the wavefunction's meaning, collapse, measurement, or nonlocality. He attributes this to treating the wavefunction as an individual particle's state.

His proposed starting point is Hamilton's action mechanics together with ensemble probability continuity. He cites two Einstein ideas: wave behaviour may belong to particle dynamics rather than to matter itself, and foundational problems may disappear if the wavefunction describes an ensemble. The author presents the resulting interpretation as a consistent account of questions debated since quantum mechanics began.

## Interpretation and checks

The amplitude-dependent term in the displayed trajectory equation has force units after a spatial derivative and depends on the full configuration. For several particles, this can couple separated coordinates even when the ordinary potential is local. An ensemble reinterpretation does not automatically remove that mathematical dependence. Check a product state and an entangled state separately before making a locality claim.
