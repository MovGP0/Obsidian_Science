## Physical question and model

Which equivalence statement is actually being tested? Universality of free fall compares trajectories, the weak principle compares gravitational response with inertia, and the Einstein principle extends local nongravitational physics to freely falling laboratories. The strong principle also concerns gravitational self-energy.

## Lecture account

## Abstract

The Universality of Free Fall (UFF) in a gravitational field found a formal justification in the equivalence of gravitational mass and inertial mass. This Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP) was affirmed by experiments of progressively improved precision. Einstein realised that the UFF and the WEP allowed to assert the local equivalence of physical phenomena in a uniform gravitational field and in an accelerated frame in empty space. Einstein's Equivalence Principle (EEP) is the basis of the General Theory of Relativity (GTR). When the EEP is widened to include the gravitational phenomena, it is called the Strong Equivalence Principle. I explore the ladder of equivalence principles as a prelude to the discussion of the GTR and also for posing the most important open problem in dynamics, namely the origin of inertia.

## Introduction

The dynamics of bodies in a gravitational field is simpler than the dynamics of charged particles in electromagnetic fields, in familiar terrestrial situations. One notable simplicity, which is also the most puzzling feature, is the fact that *all bodies have the same dynamical trajectory in a gravitational field, for the same initial conditions, independent of their mass or other properties*. This single fact of the *Universality of Free Fall* (UFF) is behind the formulation of the theory of gravity as a geometrical theory; the observable gravitational effects are described in terms of the distortions or the curvature of the underlying space and time, in which particles move along the *straightest lines*, or *geodesics*. Then, there is no need to mention the intrinsic properties of the particles.

The refinement in the theoretical generalisations, from the observed UFF to Einstein's Equivalence Principle (EEP) that culminated in the General Theory of Relativity, represents an unprecedented theoretical triumph, with numerous applications from the laboratory to the larger Universe. At the same time, that particular route taken amounted to conceding the lack of a fundamental physical understanding about the origin of inertia and the ‘fictitious’ inertial forces. Gravity itself was finally classified as a fictitious force, being separated conceptually from the other fundamental interactions.

The equivalence principle that underlies the logical and the conceptual basis of the General Theory of Relativity is also the most important pointer to the connection between cosmic gravity and relativistic dynamics. However, the EEP is taken as the founding postulate, without further analysis to derive it from a more fundamental physical reason. Later we will see that the equivalence principle is the direct and exact consequence of the gravitational interaction between a body and the rest of the matter in the Universe. My theory of Cosmic Relativity derives the equivalence of inertia and mass (WEP), as well as the local equivalence of a gravitational field and an accelerated reference frame (EEP), from first principles and the features of the factual Universe. Both the theoretical aspects as well as some experimental tests of the UFF and the equivalence principles are discussed in this chapter.

## Interpretation and checks

Do not treat one null differential-acceleration result as a test of every equivalence principle. State the body composition, size, spin, initial conditions, and external fields. A finite laboratory can detect tidal gravity even while its centre follows free fall. The chapter's proposed cosmic explanation is an additional mechanism, distinct from the empirical constraints it seeks to explain.
