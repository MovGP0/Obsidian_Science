> [!TLDR]
> The author argues that mass-dependent quantum phases do not violate the equivalence principle, which compares gravitational and accelerated-frame dynamics.

## Physical question and model

How should equivalence be formulated for a quantum system? Specify the prepared state, observable, gravitational coupling, and transformation to the accelerated frame. Quantum phase can depend on mass even when the classical trajectory obeys universal free fall.

## Lecture account

We conclude this chapter with firm comments on the status of the equivalence principle in relation to quantum phenomena [24]. This is an area where considerable confusion persists despite several clarifications. I categorically state that all claims or conjectures of potential WEP or EEP violation for quantum systems, at a level larger than already constrained by experiments with classical macroscopic bodies, are incorrect.

Much has been written about whether quantum dynamics obeys the WEP. Several papers claim a small violation of the equivalence principle in a quantum-mechanical context [24]. All these incorrect conclusions arise from an imprecise understanding of the equivalence principle itself. Since classical WEP tests depend on UFF tests, an impression has been created that trajectories and other dynamical characteristics of a particle’s propagation in a gravitational field should not depend on its mass or other internal properties if the WEP is valid. In quantum mechanics, the confusion arises mostly because the quantum dynamical phase depends on energy and mass, and there is an energy-dependent quantum phase in a gravitational field. However, the correct WEP statement is not that trajectories and dynamics are independent of mass. *The correct and precise statement is that dynamics in a uniform gravitational field is the same as in a gravity-free frame moving with acceleration $\mechanical{a}=-\mechanical{g}$.* In other words, any claim of WEP violation in quantum mechanics must prove that the same experiments yield observably different results in a uniform gravitational field and a uniformly accelerated frame with $\mechanical{a}=-\mechanical{g}$. Any other claim is a misinterpretation of the equivalence principle.

In fact, *quantum dynamics obeys the WEP at the same level as classical dynamics, as I have proved rigorously* [24]. Consider evolution from time $t_a$ to $t_b$ of a quantum state consisting of an arbitrary superposition of mass eigenstates. The Lagrangians of the quantum system in a gravitational field and in an accelerating frame are at most quadratic in velocities and coordinates. The quantum propagator is

$$
K(t_b,t_a)\equiv\langle\psi_b|U(t_b,t_a)|\psi_a\rangle=F(t_b,t_a)\exp(i\mechanical{S}_{\mathrm{cl}}/\energy{\hbar}). \tag{6.10}
$$

Here $\mechanical{S}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is the classical action. The quantum path integral with the phase contributed entirely by the classical path gives the exact result in this situation. The classical equivalence principle dictates that *the classical path in a gravitational field $\mechanical{g}$ is exactly the same as the classical path in an accelerated frame with acceleration $-\mechanical{a}$*. Then the classical actions are identical in the two situations. Therefore, the path integrals and phase shifts are identical without approximations,

$$
\left.K(t_b,t_a)\right|_g=\left.K(t_b,t_a)\right|_{a=-g}. \tag{6.11}
$$

The subscripts identify the gravitational field and accelerating frame. Since the propagators are identical, the quantum evolutions are identical for arbitrary quantum states. Thus, there is no ambiguity about the validity of the equivalence principle for quantum systems at the level already tested with macroscopic objects such as a torsion balance.

## Interpretation and checks

The simple propagator form with a classical-action phase is exact for certain quadratic actions and semiclassical more generally. A uniform-field transformation changes phases as well as coordinates; compare measurable transition probabilities after both changes. The lecture's categorical exclusion of quantum violations is a theoretical assertion, not a bound supplied by every classical experiment. Distinct nonminimal couplings or internal-state dependence require their own tests.
