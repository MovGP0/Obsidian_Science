> [!TLDR]
> Equal inertial and gravitational mass makes gravitational acceleration independent of the falling body's mass.

## Physical question and model

What cancels when gravitational and inertial masses have a universal ratio? Use inertial mass in the response law and passive gravitational mass in the force law until experiment justifies identifying them. A universal constant ratio can be absorbed into the gravitational coupling convention.

## Lecture account

A partial understanding of the UFF is obtained when Newton's law of motion, $\mechanical{\mathbf F}=\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\mathbf a}$, is combined with Newton's theory of gravitation in which the gravitational force on a body of mass $\mechanical{m}$ in a uniform gravitational field $\mechanical{\mathbf g}$ is $\mechanical{\mathbf F}_g=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf g}$. The gravitational field itself is produced by another source mass, $\mechanical{M}$ and hence in Newton's theory ‘mass’ is the ‘charge of gravity’, in analogy with the Coulomb's law for the force between electric charges. The radial gravitational field due to an idealised point mass is $\mechanical{\mathbf g}=-\hat{\mathbf r}\mechanical{G}\mechanical{M}/\mechanical{r}^2$. Quite independent of this fact, we have the quantity $\mechanical{m}_i$ appearing in Newton's law of motion, also called ‘mass’ because it coincides with the quantity of matter we call mass in all empirical investigations. It is in fact the ‘inertia’ or the dynamical resistance to any acceleration of motion. The acceleration of a body under any force $\mechanical{\mathbf F}$ is in inverse relation to the inertia of the body, $\mechanical{\mathbf a}=\mechanical{\mathbf F}/\mechanical{m}_i$.

Often, we do not pay enough attention to the distinction between the gravitational mass and the inertia. But, a priori, the trajectories of dynamics and characteristics of gravity are two independent features of the physical world. Hence, the inertia and the gravitational mass are to be considered as independent and unrelated physical quantities, *as unrelated as the inertia and the electric charge*, until we find a physical reason why the two are related universally. In any case, if and only if we identify the two quantities—inertia $\mechanical{m}_i$ and the charge of gravity $\mechanical{m}_g$ of a body—as the same ‘mass’ $\mechanical{m}$, then the acceleration of any body in a gravitational field is

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf a}=\mechanical{\mathbf F}_g/\mechanical{m}_i=\mechanical{m}\mechanical{\mathbf g}/\mechanical{m}=\mechanical{\mathbf g} \tag{6.1}
$$

*which is independent of the mass of the body*. This is the connection between the Universality of Free Fall, an empirical fact, and the postulate of the equivalence of inertial mass (inertia) and gravitational mass (gravitational charge, or simply mass, which we had indicated by $\mechanical{m}_g$). Since we have the Einstein relation in relativistic physics equating any form of energy $\energy{E}$ to a mass $\mechanical{m}=\energy{E}/\mechanical{c}^2$, the WEP and the UFF extend to all forms of energy as well. (The Einstein relation involves the inertial mass $\mechanical{m}_i$, but the WEP implies that any form of energy is also a source of gravity, with mass $\mechanical{m}_g=\mechanical{m}_i$.) A precision test of the UFF is then interpreted as the precision test of the ‘Weak’ Equivalence Principle (WEP). The adjective ‘weak’ is to distinguish it from the more specialised statement of the equivalence of physical phenomena in a gravitational field and in an accelerated frame without gravity (EEP), and also from a stronger statement of the EEP when the physical phenomena include all gravitational phenomena, called the Strong Equivalence Principle (SEP). The SEP is essentially the statement that gravitational energy itself freely falls in a gravitational field at the same acceleration as all bodies and other forms of energy.

The most important fact to be reiterated at this stage is that physicists do not yet have any fundamental understanding why the inertia should be the same as the charge of gravity. Why does ‘mass’ take the double role of being the inertia in dynamics and also the charge of the gravitational field? This gap remains as long as the equivalence principle remains as a postulate, as in the case for the construction of metric theories of gravity including the General Theory of Relativity. It is then clear that finding a convincing and quantitatively justified answer to this open problem will amount to arguably the most profound discoveries connecting dynamics and gravity. What is remarkable, in the context of the contents of this book, is that the definite answer is indeed found in the theory of Cosmic Relativity.

A taste of surprise at the equivalence of inertia and gravitational mass may be obtained when we consider the long range force of electromagnetism; the law of dynamics is the same, $\mechanical{\mathbf F}=\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\mathbf a}$, whereas the law of force is $\mechanical{\mathbf F}_e=\electric{e}\electric{\mathbf E}$. Hence acceleration is

$$
\mechanical{\mathbf a}=\mechanical{\mathbf F}_e/\mechanical{m}_i=\electric{e}\electric{\mathbf E}/\mechanical{m}_i. \tag{6.2}
$$

which depends on the non-universal ratio $\electric{e}/\mechanical{m}_i$. This can be positive or negative.

Some relevant questions pertaining to inertia are then: (a) why is inertia to motion proportional or the same as the charge of gravity, and not the charge of any other interaction? (b) is inertia exactly equal to gravitational mass under all circumstances, especially in relation to the known equivalence of mass and energy? (c) is inertia an invariant quantity or can it be modified by an interaction or motion?

The obvious physical answer to the first question could have been that inertia is generated gravitationally. Then, inertia would naturally be proportional to gravitational mass, with the gravitational constant as the proportionality constant. This by itself, independent of all details, would give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{m}_i&=\mechanical{G}'\mechanical{m}_g\mechanical{\Phi}\\
\mechanical{m}_i/\mechanical{m}_g&=\mechanical{G}'\mechanical{\Phi}
\end{aligned} \tag{6.3}
$$

where $\mechanical{G}'$ is an effective coupling constant and $\mechanical{\Phi}$ is the measure of external gravity (like the total gravitational potential). Now the ratio of inertia and gravitational mass is universal, the same for all bodies. *This is sufficient to understand and explain the universality of free fall*. Note that the UFF requires only that the ratio of inertia and gravitational mass is the same for all bodies; they need not exactly be equal to make the ratio as 1. However, this path for a solution to the puzzle of inertia was never pursued enough in physics, in spite of its simplicity and naturalness, while our fundamental theories were being completed in the early part of the 20th century. This was perhaps because of a single reason: nobody could identify a source for the potential $\mechanical{\Phi}$ that is large enough to make the local gravitational potentials insignificant in comparison, to achieve and ensure the universality required in the UFF. Isolated demonstrations of the possibility along what E. Mach suggested [6], by considering the gravity of all the matter in the Universe (like the work by Sciama [7]), were not considered favourably, for reasons inexplicable.

## Interpretation and checks

The acceleration depends on the ratio of the two masses. Its composition independence is the empirical content; setting that ratio to one also uses a unit convention. Electrical acceleration instead depends on charge divided by inertial mass, so it need not be universal. A proposed cosmic origin must derive the ratio from a specified coupling law rather than place the desired proportionality into its definition.
