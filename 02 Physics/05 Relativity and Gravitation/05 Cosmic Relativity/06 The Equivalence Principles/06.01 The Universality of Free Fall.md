> [!TLDR]
> Universal free fall states that bodies with the same initial conditions follow the same gravitational motion regardless of their material properties.

## Physical question and model

Why do bodies of different composition approximately follow the same free-fall path? Compare test bodies released with the same position and velocity in the same gravitational field. Neglect air drag, charge forces, finite-size tides, and backreaction on the source.

## Lecture account

A central theme, as a basic postulate, that threads through the theories of dynamics and gravitation from Galileo's time is the Universality of Free Fall (UFF). It is just “the extremely strange and confirmed experience that all bodies in the same gravitational field fall with the same acceleration” (to quote Einstein [1]), irrespective of the mass or other material properties and physical state of the bodies, when the initial condition of the drop are the same (position and velocity). This fact was stated clearly and asserted for the first time by Galileo [2]. The experimental verification at the level of a part in thousand ($10^{-3}$) was not difficult even in his times, by comparing the relative phase of two simple pendulums of identical length, with bobs of different materials. In modern times, the UFF is verified in different kinds of experiments to the level of 1 part in $10^{13}$–$10^{14}$ (see [[06.07 A Brief Account of Experimental Tests|Section 6.7]]).

The defining postulate of the Special Theory of Relativity is the invariance of the relative velocity of light, which was postulated in relation to Einstein's interpretation of the postulate of the Principle of Relativity (PoR). The starting fundamental postulates of the General Theory of Relativity are, (a) the validity of the STR in frames that are freely falling in a gravitational field $\mechanical{g}$, and (b) the local equivalence of a uniform gravitational field and the acceleration ‘$\mechanical{a}$’ of a reference frame with $\mechanical{a}=-\mechanical{g}$. The UFF is the basis for the second postulate. The UFF is interpreted as due to the equivalence of the inertial mass and gravitational mass. The inertial mass (or inertia) is the ‘mass’ that appears in Newton's law of motion, $\mechanical{\mathbf F}=\mechanical{m}_i\mechanical{\mathbf a}$. The gravitational mass is the source of a gravitational field; it is the ‘charge’ of gravity. This appears in Newton's law for a gravitational field, $\mechanical{\mathbf F}_g=-\hat{\mathbf r}\mechanical{G}\mechanical{m}_g/\mechanical{r}^2$, for example. (The fact that Newton discovered the law of dynamics as well as the law of gravity was a coincidence of brilliance.) The equivalence of the inertia and gravitational mass is termed the Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP) [4, 5].

If all objects have the same acceleration in a uniform gravitational field, then everything in a freely falling reference frame, including an observer, will be at relative rest, as if there are no forces, including gravity. This is the physical environment, effectively equivalent to empty space, where the STR is valid. Thus the seemingly intractable problem of dealing with the dynamics in a gravitational field is reduced to the easy task of applying the STR, if one chooses a freely falling frame. This was Einstein's great insight. This fact was generalised by Einstein and it was stated as the local equivalence of the physical phenomena in a uniform gravitational field and in an accelerated frame devoid of gravity, to formulate the GTR. This generalised equivalence in now called Einstein's Equivalence Principle (EEP).

Thus the UFF is the fundamental seed from which the whole physics of the gravitational phenomena emerged as the GTR. It is the UFF that is accessible for experimental tests. But the crucial question remains, what is the reason for the UFF? In other words, is there a deeper physical reason why all bodies fall at the same acceleration in a gravitational field? After all, such a universality does not happen in the *free fall* of electrically charged particles in a uniform electric field. This question can be shifted to a level deeper when we say that the UFF is because of the equivalence of the inertia and the gravitational mass.

> [!note] Source force expression
> The printed expression for $\mechanical{\mathbf F}_g$ omits the source mass. With $\mechanical{G}$ as the gravitational constant, the usual point-mass force includes both masses. The source's expression is retained.

## Interpretation and checks

The result concerns acceleration under gravity, not the absence of other forces. Equal initial conditions are essential: two freely falling bodies with different velocities need not trace the same path. Predict how changing surface area affects a drop in air; that probes drag as well as gravity. In a precision test, reverse or modulate the expected violation signal to separate it from environmental acceleration.
