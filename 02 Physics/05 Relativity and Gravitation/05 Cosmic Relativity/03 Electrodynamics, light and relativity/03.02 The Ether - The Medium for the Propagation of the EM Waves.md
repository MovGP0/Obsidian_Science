> [!TLDR]
> The ether was proposed as a medium for electromagnetic waves before relativity removed it from the standard physical description.

## Physical question and model

What extra prediction did an ether supply beyond Maxwell's field equations? Historical models assigned a medium velocity and a rule for interaction with matter. A moving-medium hypothesis must specify whether the medium is dragged, stationary, or partly coupled; the word ether alone is not a model.

## Lecture account

The pioneers who built the edifice of electrodynamics were convinced of the need for a medium for all electromagnetic (EM) phenomena to occur, and the ‘ether’ was that postulated hypothetical medium [5, 6]. The hypothesis was emphasised and strengthened by Thomas Young, who pioneered the wave theory of light with his famous experiment of ‘double slit interference’. This was even before recognizing the connection between light and electrodynamics. The transverse waves of light were imagined to propagate in an invisible medium, similar to all other familiar waves in various material media as their support. The fact that the speed of light is less, $\mechanical{c}'=\mechanical{c}/\signal{n}$, in denser transparent matter with refractive index $\signal{n}$, meant a different density of the ether in such media. While it was clear that the ether itself would not be sensible directly, independent of the electromagnetic phenomena, it became important to deduce whether the ether was stationary and immovable, or whether it was carried along by moving material media like glass or water, in which light propagated at a diminished speed (this fact itself was not known until Fizeau measured the velocity of light in water). Also, it was hoped that an ether ‘wind’, due to the motion of the Earth through the stationary ether, might be detectable as some modification of the electromagnetic phenomena.

The polarisation of light was a strong clue that the oscillations of light-waves were transverse, perpendicular to the direction of propagation. The properties that were necessary for the hypothetical ether, to be consistent with a medium that was capable of supporting the propagation of transverse oscillations at the large speed of light, were rather bizarre. In analogy with a string that can support such waves, the ether had to be of immense elastic rigidity. Yet, it had to be thin enough not to impede the movements of matter. Also, it needed to easily permeate in solid media. Since both electric and magnetic disturbances had to be accounted for, the microscopic picture also needed vortex-like structures. In short, the ether medium was too complex for any satisfactory physical description.

(Rigorously speaking, similar conceptual issues arise if one attributes physical properties to the invisible space, as Einstein's GTR does. The gravity of an external source affects the dynamics of particles in the interior of a dense object. But, does the deformable external space continue with the same physical properties inside dense matter?).

One important testable issue was whether the ether can be carried or affected by moving transparent bodies. The experimental setting for a test was already available in the phenomenon of ‘stellar aberration’, established in observations in the late 17th century, and explained by James Bradley in 1729.

## Interpretation and checks

The analogy with sound identifies a possible test, not a necessary electromagnetic substrate. At rest in a transparent material, refractive index describes the material response and can depend on frequency; it is not a direct measurement of ether density. Test each proposed drag rule against the same propagation observable. Removing the medium should recover the vacuum case.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |

| $\signal{n}$ | $1$ | Refractive index | Ratio of vacuum light speed to light speed in the material. |
