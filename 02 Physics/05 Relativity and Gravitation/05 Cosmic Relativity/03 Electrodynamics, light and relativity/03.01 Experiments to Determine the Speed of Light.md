## Physical question and model

How can distance and elapsed time establish a propagation speed? A direct time-of-flight experiment needs a calibrated path and timing model. A frequency–wavelength determination uses wave phase and length calibration. Both are measurements through a model, with different systematic errors.

## Lecture account

The experimental determinations of the speed of light, from the early determination by Roemer to highly precise modern experiments, served to establish that the velocity of light was finite, with a numerical value of approximately $\mechanical{c}=2.99\times10^8\,\unit{m/s}$. I sketch Roemer's method and then discuss only Michelson's experiments that continued till 1935, which stand as the best direct measurements even now. The modern ‘precise’ value quoted today does not correspond to a measurement of the ‘velocity’, but refers to the product of the measured frequency $\signal{\nu}$ and the wavelength $\signal{\lambda}$ in an optical (or microwave) cavity. Then the velocity is calculated assuming the theoretical definition of a physical wave in space, $\mechanical{c}=\signal{\nu}\signal{\lambda}$.

## Interpretation and checks

In the present SI, the numerical value of vacuum light speed is fixed by definition; length realisation uses frequency and time standards. This is distinct from claiming that no experiment tests propagation. Separate a historical measured estimate, a defined conversion constant, and a modern Lorentz-symmetry test. Ask which quantity remains independently measured if the metre is realised from light travel time.
