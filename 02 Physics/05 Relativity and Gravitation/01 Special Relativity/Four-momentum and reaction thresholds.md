title: "Four-momentum and reaction thresholds"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The total energy–momentum invariant identifies the minimum lab energy needed to produce a final state.

Use metric signature $(+,-,-,-)$. For a system with total energy $\energy{E}$ and total momentum $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, define $s=\energy{E}^2-\signal{c}^2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}|^2$. The center-of-momentum frame has zero total spatial momentum, so $\sqrt{s}$ is its total energy. At the kinematic threshold for massive final particles, they have no relative motion in that frame. Thus the minimum $\sqrt{s}$ is the sum of their rest energies. The threshold gives an energy condition, with the reaction dynamics determining the actual rate.

For a photon of energy $\energy{E}_\gamma$ striking a proton of mass $\mechanical{m}_p$ initially at rest, the total energy is $\energy{E}_\gamma+\mechanical{m}_p\signal{c}^2$ and the momentum magnitude is $\energy{E}_\gamma/\signal{c}$. If the final state is a proton and a neutral pion of mass $\mechanical{m}_\pi$, set

$$
\mechanical{m}_p^2\signal{c}^4+2\mechanical{m}_p\signal{c}^2\energy{E}_\gamma
=(\mechanical{m}_p+\mechanical{m}_\pi)^2\signal{c}^4.
$$

The threshold is $\energy{E}_{\gamma,\mathrm{thr}}=\mechanical{m}_\pi\signal{c}^2\bigl(1+\mechanical{m}_\pi/(2\mechanical{m}_p)\bigr)$. The positive correction supplies the kinetic energy required by momentum conservation in the lab. At threshold the outgoing particles have the same lab velocity. In the limit of a very heavy target the recoil correction tends to zero. The same invariant can be constructed from measured total lab energy and momentum to check the allowed final states in a scattering problem.
