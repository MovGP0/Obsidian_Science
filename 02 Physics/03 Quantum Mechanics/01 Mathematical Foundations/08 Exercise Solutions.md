## Use a solution to check your method

First identify the task: prove a statement, solve an equation, change a representation, or predict measurement statistics. State the required conditions before you calculate. In particular, check whether a vector must be nonzero or normalized, whether a matrix must be Hermitian, and whether a function must satisfy boundary conditions.

Attempt the exercise before opening the worked steps. If you need help, read only far enough to identify the next method, then continue on your own. Compare the reason for each step as well as the final answer. Different valid methods can produce the same result.

Use checks that fit the chapter:

- In Chapters 1–3, substitute a proposed solution into the original equation and check initial or boundary data. A solution of the differential equation can still fail the boundary conditions.
- In Chapters 4–11, test matrix and operator identities on a simple vector, check multiplication order, and check normalization after a change of basis. A successful finite-dimensional example illustrates an identity; it does not prove the identity for every operator.
- In Chapters 12–13, compare the transform convention and normalization factors before comparing answers. Then use an inverse transform or a norm check where the stated hypotheses permit it.
- In Chapters 14–15, check that probabilities sum or integrate to one and that variance is nonnegative. For a normalized eigenstate of the measured observable, its outcome spread must be zero.

After a worked exercise, change one condition and predict what must be checked again. For example, removing a normalization condition changes probability formulas, while changing a boundary condition can change the allowed eigenvalues. Use [[09 Glossary of Terms]] when the difficulty is the meaning of a term rather than the algebra.
