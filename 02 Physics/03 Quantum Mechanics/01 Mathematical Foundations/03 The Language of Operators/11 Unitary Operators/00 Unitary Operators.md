> [!TLDR]
> Unitary operators implement quantum transformations that preserve inner products. Their generators connect continuous symmetries with conserved observables and time evolution.

The question is how a state can change while retaining the normalization needed for probabilities. A unitary map preserves more than each norm: it preserves overlaps between different preparations. Its inverse then recovers the original vector without discarding a component.

Read the definition before the generator theorem. Then compare spatial translation with time evolution: momentum generates one and the Hamiltonian generates the other. The conservation section adds a further hypothesis. A continuous unitary family gives a conserved generator only when that family is a symmetry of the specified dynamics and the generator has no explicit time dependence. An arbitrary unitary transformation need not satisfy this condition.

The exercises on phase evolution and precession show why preserved overlaps do not force every fixed-observable probability to remain constant. The antiunitary challenge marks the boundary of the unitary symmetry description.

Noether’s theorem connects continuous symmetries with conservation laws. In quantum mechanics, unitary operators implement the symmetry transformations considered in this chapter. Their Hermitian generators correspond to the associated conserved quantities.

This chapter completes a three-part structure:

- Hermitian operators describe **observables**.
- Commutators describe **algebraic structure**.
- Unitary operators describe **symmetries and dynamics**.
