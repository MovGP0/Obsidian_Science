> [!TLDR]
> These seven worked exercises connect ordinary differential equations with quantum boundary conditions, degeneracy, and tunnelling.

Each exercise note contains the complete question, all subparts, a worked solution, and the answer.

## A route from initial values to scattering

Exercises 1–2 test solution selection: identify the maximal interval in the nonlinear example, and explain why a repeated characteristic root needs a new basis function. Exercise 3 then checks the equation, boundary conditions, and normalization separately for the same proposed state.

Compare Exercises 4 and 7 before doing their matching algebra. A finite-well bound state must decay at both infinities. A left-incident barrier state instead has an incident and reflected wave on the left, and no incoming wave on the right. These different exterior conditions select different physical questions even though both use piecewise constant potentials and matching of values and derivatives.

Exercise 5 separates two spatial coordinates and distinguishes states from distinct energy values. Exercise 6 returns to time evolution: its amplitudes can change while total probability remains fixed. As a final check, explain why the real exponential inside the barrier is not evidence of probability loss from the complete scattering solution.
