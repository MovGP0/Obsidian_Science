> [!TLDR]
> Measurement connects quantum states and observables with probability distributions. This final chapter brings together expectation values, uncertainty, time evolution, and spectral probabilities.

This final chapter is the closest to applied quantum mechanics. It combines the tools developed so far: inner products, Hermitian operators, eigenvalues, Hilbert space, Dirac notation, and probability.

The central subject is **measurement**: what happens when a quantum system is measured, and how to calculate the associated statistical quantities.

## How the calculations fit together

Start with a normalized state and a specified observable. The spectral distribution answers which results are possible and their probabilities. Its first moment gives the expectation; its centered second moment gives the uncertainty. Neither statistic alone reconstructs the full state.

Read the expectation and variance sections together, then use Ehrenfest’s theorem to ask which means change under the Hamiltonian. The uncertainty principle constrains pairs of outcome spreads. The spectral example returns to the full probability distribution and checks that its weighted mean agrees with the operator calculation.

Keep three questions separate throughout: What is the distribution before measurement? How does the prepared state evolve between measurements? What is the state after a selected result? The last question needs a measurement-update rule in addition to a probability rule.
