---
title: "Postulates of Quantum Mechanics Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Practice normalizing quantum states and applying the Born rule, projection postulate, and unitary time evolution.

1. Normalize $\lvert\psi\rangle=a\lvert1\rangle+b\lvert2\rangle+c\lvert3\rangle$ for $(a,b,c)=(2,i,-1)$ in the orthonormal basis $\{\lvert n\rangle\}$.
   **Result.** $\lvert\psi\rangle=(2\lvert1\rangle+i\lvert2\rangle-\lvert3\rangle)/\sqrt6$.
2. Show that $\hat A^\dagger=\hat A$ implies $\langle\psi\mid\hat A\mid\psi\rangle\in\mathbb R$ for every $\lvert\psi\rangle$ in the domain.
3. Let $\hat A$ have eigenvalues $\{1,2,3\}$ and respective eigenstates $\{\lvert1\rangle,\lvert2\rangle,\lvert3\rangle\}$. Calculate $\langle\hat A\rangle$ and $\Delta A$ for $\lvert\psi\rangle=(\lvert1\rangle+\lvert2\rangle+\lvert3\rangle)/\sqrt3$.
   **Result.** $\langle\hat A\rangle=2$ and $\Delta A=\sqrt{2/3}$.
4. For the same $\hat A$, which state minimizes $\Delta A$? Which state maximizes it among the equal-weight superpositions of two eigenstates?
5. Verify that $\hat U=e^{-i\energy{\hat H}t/\hbar}$ is unitary if $\energy{\hat H}$ is self-adjoint.
6. Find the probability of the spin outcome $\lvert+z\rangle$ for initial states $\lvert+x\rangle$, $\lvert+y\rangle$, and $\lvert-z\rangle$.
   **Result.** $1/2$, $1/2$, and $0$.
7. An observable $\hat A$ on $\mathbb C^3$ has eigenvalues $a_1=1$ (twofold degenerate) and $a_2=-2$. Let $\{\lvert u_1\rangle,\lvert u_2\rangle\}$ be an orthonormal basis of the $a_1$ eigenspace, and let $\lvert v\rangle$ be the normalized eigenstate for $a_2$. For $\lvert\psi\rangle=(\lvert u_1\rangle+2\lvert u_2\rangle+2\lvert v\rangle)/3$, find the probability of $a_1$ and the state after that result.
   **Result.** $P(a_1)=5/9$. The resulting state is $(\lvert u_1\rangle+2\lvert u_2\rangle)/\sqrt5$.
8. Two orthonormal bases of $\mathbb C^2$ are $\{\lvert0\rangle,\lvert1\rangle\}$ and $\{\lvert+\rangle,\lvert-\rangle\}$ with $\lvert\pm\rangle=(\lvert0\rangle\pm\lvert1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. For $\lvert\psi\rangle=\alpha\lvert0\rangle+\beta\lvert1\rangle$, express the two probabilities in terms of $\alpha$ and $\beta$.
   **Result.** $P(+)=\lvert\alpha+\beta\rvert^2/2$ and $P(-)=\lvert\alpha-\beta\rvert^2/2$.
9. A two-state system has Hamiltonian $\energy{\hat H}=\energy{\varepsilon}\sigma_x$. The initial state is $\lvert0\rangle$, an eigenstate of $\sigma_z$. Calculate $\lvert\psi(t)\rangle$ and $P_1(t)=\lvert\langle1\mid\psi(t)\rangle\rvert^2$.
   **Result.** $P_1(t)=\sin^2(\energy{\varepsilon}t/\hbar)$.
10. Write the evolution operator $\hat U(t)$ in spectral form for a time-independent Hamiltonian with eigenvalues $\{\energy{E}_n\}$ and eigenstates $\{\lvert n\rangle\}$. Show that $\lvert c_n\rvert^2$ is constant for an initial state $\lvert\psi(0)\rangle=\sum_n c_n\lvert n\rangle$.
11. **Quantum oscillations.** For $\energy{\hat H}=\energy{\varepsilon}\sigma_z+\energy{\Delta}\sigma_x$ and initial state $\lvert0\rangle$, find the eigenvalues, eigenstates, and probability $P_1(t)$. Discuss the limits $\lvert\energy{\Delta}\rvert\ll\lvert\energy{\varepsilon}\rvert$ and $\lvert\energy{\Delta}\rvert\gg\lvert\energy{\varepsilon}\rvert$.
    **Hint.** The eigenvalues are $\pm\sqrt{\energy{\varepsilon}^2+\energy{\Delta}^2}$. The probability is

    $$
    P_1(t)=\frac{\energy{\Delta}^2}{\energy{\varepsilon}^2+\energy{\Delta}^2}
    \sin^2\left(\frac{\sqrt{\energy{\varepsilon}^2+\energy{\Delta}^2}}{\hbar}t\right).
    $$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a_1,a_2,\langle\hat A\rangle,\Delta A$ | — | Measurement values and statistics | The outcomes, expectation value, and standard deviation have the unit of the observable. |
| $P,P_1$ | $1$ | Measurement probability | The probability of an indicated outcome or state. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy{E}_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energies | The evolution generator and its eigenvalues. |
| $\energy{\varepsilon},\energy{\Delta}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian coefficients | The energy parameters of the two-state Hamiltonians. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The elapsed evolution time. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The conversion between energy and phase rate. |
