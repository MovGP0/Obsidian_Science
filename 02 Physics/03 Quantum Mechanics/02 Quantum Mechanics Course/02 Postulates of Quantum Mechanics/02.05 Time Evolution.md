> [!TLDR]
> Between measurements, a closed quantum system evolves unitarily. The Hamiltonian generates this evolution.

> [!definition] Definition 2.5 | Postulate V. Unitary Time Evolution
> Between measurements, when the system is isolated from the apparatus and other external systems, its state evolves by a unitary operator $\hat U(t,t_0)$.
>
> $$
> \lvert\psi(t)\rangle=\hat U(t,t_0)\lvert\psi(t_0)\rangle.
> $$
>
> The composition law is $\hat U(t_2,t_0)=\hat U(t_2,t_1)\hat U(t_1,t_0)$, and $\hat U(t,t)=\hat I$. The Hamiltonian generates the evolution through $i\hbar\partial_t\hat U=\energy{\hat H}\hat U$.

Differentiate the evolved state and use the generator relation to obtain the **time-dependent Schrödinger equation**.

$$
i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}\lvert\psi(t)\rangle=\energy{\hat H}\lvert\psi(t)\rangle.
$$

[[04.00 Schrödinger Equation|Chapter 4]] develops the dynamics in detail. The main properties are:

- **Determinism.** The initial state determines the evolved state.
- **Linearity.** If $\lvert\psi_j(t_0)\rangle\to\lvert\psi_j(t)\rangle$ for $j=1,2$, a linear combination evolves into the same linear combination of the evolved states.
- **Unitarity.** The norm $\lVert\lvert\psi(t)\rangle\rVert^2$ is constant, so the total probability remains one.
- **Reversibility.** $\hat U^{-1}=\hat U^\dagger$.

For a time-independent Hamiltonian,

$$
\hat U(t,t_0)=\exp\left[-\frac{i\energy{\hat H}(t-t_0)}{\hbar}\right].
$$

If $\lvert\psi(t_0)\rangle=\lvert E\rangle$ is a Hamiltonian eigenstate of energy $\energy{E}$, then $\lvert\psi(t)\rangle=e^{-i\energy{E}(t-t_0)/\hbar}\lvert E\rangle$. The evolution changes only the global phase. The probabilities for a fixed observable are independent of time, so this is a **stationary state**.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t,t_0,t_1,t_2$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The times used to specify the evolution and its composition. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energy | The generator of evolution and its eigenvalue in the stationary state. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The conversion between energy and phase rate. |
