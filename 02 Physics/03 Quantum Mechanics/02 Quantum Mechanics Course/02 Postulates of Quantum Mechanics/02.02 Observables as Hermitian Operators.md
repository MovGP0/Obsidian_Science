---
title: "Observables as Hermitian Operators"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Self-adjoint operators represent observables. Their spectra give the possible measurement values.

> [!definition] Definition 2.2 | Postulate II. Observables
> Each measurable quantity $A$ is represented by a linear self-adjoint operator $\hat A$ on its domain in $\mathcal H$, with $\hat A^\dagger=\hat A$. The possible measurement values belong to the spectrum $\sigma(\hat A)$.

A self-adjoint operator has a real spectrum, so the measured values are real. For a discrete spectrum with a complete set of eigenstates, the spectral theorem gives an orthonormal basis in which to expand $\lvert\psi\rangle$. For a continuous spectrum the corresponding decomposition uses a spectral integral.

In the position representation, quantization gives the position and momentum operators

$$
\mechanical{\hat x}:\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\mapsto\mechanical{x}\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x}),
\qquad
\mechanical{\hat p}:\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\mapsto
-i\hbar\frac{\partial\signal{\psi}}{\partial\mechanical{x}}.
\tag{2.1}
$$

Their **canonical commutation relation** is

$$
[\mechanical{\hat x},\mechanical{\hat p}]=i\hbar\hat I.
\tag{2.2}
$$

This relation implies the Heisenberg uncertainty relation and underlies the quantum description of conjugate position and momentum. In three dimensions, $[\mechanical{\hat x}_i,\mechanical{\hat p}_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}\hat I$ for $i,j=1,2,3$.

Other fundamental observables include these operators.

- **Hamiltonian.** For a particle in a potential, the total energy operator in the position representation is $\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hat p}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical{m})+\energy{V}(\mechanical{\hat x})=-\hbar^2\partial_x^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\energy{V}(\mechanical{x})$.
- **Orbital angular momentum.** $\mechanical{\hat{\vec L}}=\mechanical{\hat{\vec x}}\times\mechanical{\hat{\vec p}}$. See [[09.00 Orbital Angular Momentum]].
- **Spin.** The intrinsic angular momentum $\mechanical{\hat{\vec S}}$ is introduced separately in [[10.00 Spin]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat x},\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The position operator and its coordinate argument. |
| $\mechanical{\hat p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum operator | The generator of translations in the position representation. |
| $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Position wave function | The wave function normalized over one spatial dimension. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the position–momentum commutator. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy{V}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and potential energy | The total energy operator and the potential term. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\vec L}},\mechanical{\hat{\vec S}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular momentum operators | The orbital and intrinsic angular momenta. |
