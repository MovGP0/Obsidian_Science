---
title: "Successive Measurements and the Quantum Zeno Effect"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An intermediate incompatible measurement can change later outcomes. Very frequent checks can inhibit unitary transitions.

Consider three successive measurements of $\hat A,\hat B,\hat A$ for two observables with nondegenerate spectra and $[\hat A,\hat B]\ne0$. Neglect evolution between the measurements. For a specified intermediate result $b_m$, the probability of the sequence $a_n\to b_m\to a_n$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(a_n\to b_m\to a_n)
&=\lvert\langle a_n\mid\psi\rangle\rvert^2
  \lvert\langle b_m\mid a_n\rangle\rvert^2
  \lvert\langle a_n\mid b_m\rangle\rvert^2\\
&=P_n^{(0)}\lvert\langle b_m\mid a_n\rangle\rvert^4,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $P_n^{(0)}=\lvert\langle a_n\mid\psi\rangle\rvert^2$. Sum over $m$ to obtain the joint probability that the first and last outcomes are $a_n$. Conditional on the first outcome $a_n$, the return probability is $\sum_m\lvert\langle b_m\mid a_n\rangle\rvert^4$. It is less than one if the intermediate result is uncertain.

## Quantum Zeno effect

Prepare the system in an eigenstate $\lvert a\rangle$ of $\hat A$ and evolve it with a time-independent Hamiltonian that does not commute with $\hat A$. At short time $\delta t$, the probability of finding the initial state is

$$
P(a,\delta t)=1-\left(\frac{\Delta\energy{E}\,\delta t}{\hbar}\right)^2+O(\delta t^4),
$$

when the expansion through fourth order exists. Take the checked outcome to select $\lvert a\rangle$. Measure every $\delta t$ over $T=N\delta t$. The probability of obtaining $a$ at every check is approximately

$$
P(a,T)\simeq\left[1-\left(\frac{\Delta\energy{E}\,\delta t}{\hbar}\right)^2\right]^N\to1
\quad\text{as }N\to\infty\text{ at fixed }T.
$$

Thus frequent checks can inhibit transitions. The source cites the 1990 experiment of Itano and colleagues with trapped $^9\mathrm{Be}^+$ ions.

**Physical significance.** The quantum Zeno effect distinguishes smooth unitary evolution from the discrete updates of repeated measurements. The quadratic short-time survival probability and the measurement rule together predict the effect. Quantum control and information processing can use this behavior.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a_n,b_m,a$ | — | Measurement values | The eigenvalues in the measurement sequences. The units depend on the observable. |
| $P,P_n^{(0)}$ | $1$ | Measurement probabilities | The sequence or survival probability and the probability of the first result. |
| $\delta t,T$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time intervals | The interval between checks and the total observation time. |
| $\Delta\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy standard deviation | The energy spread of the initial state. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale in the short-time survival probability. |
