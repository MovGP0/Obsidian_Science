---
title: "Complete Set of Commuting Observables (CSCO)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A complete set of commuting observables labels each common eigenstate by a unique tuple of eigenvalues.

If $\hat A$ has a degenerate eigenvalue $a$, the eigenspace $\mathcal H_a$ has more than one dimension. Additional information is necessary to select a state in it.

> [!definition] Definition 3.2 | CSCO
> A set $\{\hat A_1,\hat A_2,\ldots,\hat A_N\}$ of pairwise commuting observables is a **complete set of commuting observables** (CSCO) if the joint eigenvalues $(a_1,\ldots,a_N)$ specify the normalized common eigenstate $\lvert a_1,\ldots,a_N\rangle$ up to a global phase.

## Examples

- For a spinless particle in a three-dimensional central potential, the set is $\{\energy{\hat H},\mechanical{\hat{\vec L}}^{\,2},\mechanical{\hat L}_z\}$ when the spectrum has no remaining degeneracy. See [[12.00 Hydrogen Atom]].
- For a spin-½ particle with spin-independent dynamics, add $\mechanical{\hat S}_z$ to the orbital set. In a coupled basis, use total angular momentum observables $\mechanical{\hat{\vec J}}^{\,2}$ and $\mechanical{\hat J}_z$ in place of the separate projection observables where appropriate.
- For nonrelativistic hydrogen, $\{\energy{\hat H},\mechanical{\hat{\vec L}}^{\,2},\mechanical{\hat L}_z,\mechanical{\hat S}_z\}$ labels the bound-state basis by $(n,\ell,m_\ell,m_s)$.

Finding a CSCO gives a basis for the quantum problem. Once the basis is fixed, each state has a unique expansion in it.

## Physical significance

A CSCO need not be unique. In a rotationally symmetric system one can choose $\mechanical{\hat L}_z$ or $\mechanical{\hat L}_x$ as the projection observable. These two operators do not commute with one another. The choice changes the basis and leaves the physics unchanged, as when one chooses a classical coordinate system.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a_i$ | — | Joint measurement values | The eigenvalues of the commuting observables, with their respective units. |
| $\energy{\hat H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy observable for the central problem. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\vec L}}^{\,2},\mechanical{\hat{\vec J}}^{\,2}$ | $\unit{J^2\,s^2}$ | Squared angular momenta | The squared orbital and total angular momentum operators. |
| $\mechanical{\hat L}_x,\mechanical{\hat L}_z,\mechanical{\hat S}_z,\mechanical{\hat J}_z$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Angular momentum components | The orbital, spin, and total projection observables. |
| $n,\ell,m_\ell,m_s$ | $1$ | Quantum numbers | The principal, orbital, magnetic, and spin-projection labels. |
