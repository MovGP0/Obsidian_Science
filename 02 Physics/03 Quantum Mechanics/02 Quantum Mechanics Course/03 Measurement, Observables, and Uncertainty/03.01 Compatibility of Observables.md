---
title: "Compatibility of Observables"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Compatible observables commute and can have a common basis of eigenstates.

> [!definition] Definition 3.1 | Compatible Observables
> Two observables $\hat A$ and $\hat B$ are compatible if they commute, $[\hat A,\hat B]=0$. For unbounded observables, compatibility requires commuting spectral projections.

> [!theorem] Theorem 3.1 | Common Bases for Compatible Observables
> In finite dimensions, two Hermitian operators $\hat A$ and $\hat B$ have a common orthonormal eigenbasis if and only if $[\hat A,\hat B]=0$. The same conclusion applies to strongly commuting self-adjoint operators with complete discrete eigenbases.

This follows from the spectral theorem. Commutation makes each eigenspace of $\hat A$ invariant under $\hat B$, so diagonalizing $\hat B$ within those eigenspaces gives a common basis.

- A common eigenstate $\lvert a,b\rangle$ satisfies $\hat A\lvert a,b\rangle=a\lvert a,b\rangle$ and $\hat B\lvert a,b\rangle=b\lvert a,b\rangle$.
- For a system in $\lvert a,b\rangle$, ideal measurements give $a$ and $b$ in either order. Each value is reproducible if there is no intervening evolution.
- If $[\hat A,\hat B]\ne0$, a measurement of $\hat A$ can disturb the distribution for $\hat B$. The order of measurements can affect the results.

## Example. Position and momentum

The canonical commutator $[\mechanical{\hat x},\mechanical{\hat p}]=i\hbar\hat I$ prevents common eigenstates. A generalized position eigenstate is a delta distribution $\delta(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_0)$ and is delocalized in momentum. A generalized momentum eigenstate is a plane wave $e^{i\mechanical{p}\mechanical{x}/\hbar}$ and is delocalized in position.

## Example. Two position components

The Cartesian components of position in two dimensions obey $[\mechanical{\hat x},\mechanical{\hat y}]=0$. A wave packet can be localized in both directions. Similarly, $[\mechanical{\hat x}_i,\mechanical{\hat x}_j]=0$ and $[\mechanical{\hat p}_i,\mechanical{\hat p}_j]=0$ for Cartesian components.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a,b$ | — | Measurement values | The eigenvalues of the two observables, with the corresponding units. |
| $\mechanical{\hat x},\mechanical{\hat y},\mechanical{x},\mechanical{x}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The Cartesian observables and the coordinate and eigenvalue in one dimension. |
| $\mechanical{\hat p},\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The momentum observable and its eigenvalue. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale in the position–momentum commutator. |
