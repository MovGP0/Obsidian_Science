---
title: "Order-of-Magnitude Estimates with ℏ"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The uncertainty relation and the Coulomb potential give the characteristic size and binding energy of hydrogen.

Dimensional estimates with $\hbar$ give useful scales for quantum systems. In a classical orbit an electron radiates and would spiral into the nucleus in a time of order $\quantity{10^{-11}}{s}$. In quantum mechanics, confinement increases the required kinetic energy. By $\Delta\mechanical{x}\,\Delta\mechanical{p}\gtrsim\hbar$ at order-of-magnitude accuracy, confining an electron to a region of radius $\mechanical{r}$ gives

$$
\energy{K}\sim\frac{\mechanical{p}^2}{2\mechanical{m}_e}
\sim\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r}^2}.
$$

Adding the Coulomb potential $\energy{U}=-\electric{e}^2/(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\mechanical{r})$ gives

$$
\energy{E}(\mechanical{r})\sim
\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mechanical{m}_e\mechanical{r}^2}
-\frac{\electric{e}^2}{4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\mechanical{r}}.
$$

Minimizing this estimate gives a radius on the scale of the Bohr radius $\mechanical{a}_0$ and an energy on the scale of $\quantity{-13.6}{eV}$. The estimate recovers the Bohr scales without an assumption of stationary classical orbits. The kinetic-energy cost of confinement accounts for a stable atomic size on the order of $\mechanical{a}_0$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale in the uncertainty estimate. |
| $\Delta\mechanical{x},\mechanical{r},\mechanical{a}_0$ | $\unit{m}$ | Confinement lengths | The position uncertainty, confinement radius, and Bohr radius. |
| $\Delta\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum uncertainty and scale | The momentum spread and the typical momentum of the confined electron. |
| $\mechanical{m}_e$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Electron mass | The mass used in the kinetic energy. |
| $\energy{K},\energy{U},\energy{E}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energies | The kinetic, potential, and total energy estimates. |
| $\electric{e}$ | $\unit{C}$ | Elementary charge | The magnitude of the electron charge. |
| $\electric{\varepsilon}_0$ | $\unit{F\,m^{-1}}$ | Vacuum permittivity | The electric constant in the Coulomb potential. |
| — | $\unit{s}$ | Classical collapse time | The order-of-magnitude time for the radiating classical orbit to collapse. |
