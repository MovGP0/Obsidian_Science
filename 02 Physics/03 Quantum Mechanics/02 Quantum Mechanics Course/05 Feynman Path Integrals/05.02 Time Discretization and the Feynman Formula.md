---
title: "Time Discretization and the Feynman Formula"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Divide time evolution into short intervals and insert position identities to obtain an integral over intermediate positions. In the continuum limit this gives the Feynman path integral.

Consider $\energy{\hat H}=\energy{\hat T}+\energy{\hat V}$, with kinetic energy $\energy{\hat T}=\mechanical{\hat p}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical m)$ and potential energy $\energy V(\hat x)$. Divide the interval $T=t_f-t_i$ into $N$ equal steps of duration $\epsilon=T/N$.

In general $[\energy{\hat T},\energy{\hat V}]\ne 0$. The Trotter product formula gives

$$
e^{-i\energy{\hat H}T/\mechanical{\hbar}}
=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(e^{-i\energy{\hat T}\epsilon/\mechanical{\hbar}}e^{-i\energy{\hat V}\epsilon/\mechanical{\hbar}}\right)^N.
$$

Insert $N-1$ position identities between adjacent factors and one momentum identity inside each factor. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal K={}&\lim_{N\to\infty}\int
\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}dx_k\,
\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{d\mechanical p_k}{2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}}\\
&\quad\times\exp\left\{\frac{i}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
\left[\mechanical p_k(x_{k+1}-x_k)-\epsilon\energy H(x_k,\mechanical p_k)\right]\right\},
\end{aligned}
$$

with $x_0=x_i$ and $x_N=x_f$. Each Gaussian integral over $\mechanical p_k$ gives $\sqrt{\mechanical m/(2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon)}\,e^{i\mechanical m(x_{k+1}-x_k)^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon)}$ apart from the potential factor. The configuration-space form is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal K={}&\lim_{N\to\infty}
\left(\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}\epsilon}\right)^{N/2}
\int\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}dx_k\\
&\quad\times\exp\left\{\frac{i\epsilon}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
\left[\frac{\mechanical m}{2}\left(\frac{x_{k+1}-x_k}{\epsilon}\right)^2-\energy V(x_k)\right]\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
$$

The sum, with its factor of $\epsilon$, is the discrete form of the action $\mechanical S[x]=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\energy L(x,\dot x)\,dt$, with Lagrangian $\energy L=\mechanical m\dot x^2/2-\energy V(x)$. In the limit $N\to\infty$, denote the formal measure over paths from $x(t_i)=x_i$ to $x(t_f)=x_f$ by $\mathcal D x$. Then

$$
\signal K(x_f,t_f;x_i,t_i)=
\int_{x(t_i)=x_i}^{x(t_f)=x_f}\mathcal D x\,e^{i\mechanical S[x]/\mechanical{\hbar}}.
\tag{5.3}
$$

> [!note]
> The symbol $\mathcal D x$ denotes the time-slicing limit in Eq. (5.2). A Wick rotation to imaginary time can put the kinetic part in terms of a Wiener measure. The potential then supplies a weight. The time-slicing definition suffices here.

## Physical meaning

In the real-time sum, the action is real and each phase factor has unit magnitude. Its phase is $\mechanical S[x]/\mechanical{\hbar}$. The sum over paths gives transition amplitudes through Eq. (5.3).

The dashed and dotted lines show virtual paths that fluctuate around the classical path. The weight of each path is $e^{i\mechanical S[x]/\mechanical{\hbar}}$.]]

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal K$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Propagator | The transition kernel between fixed endpoints. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy{\hat T},\energy{\hat V}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy operators | The Hamiltonian, kinetic energy, and potential energy. |
| $\energy H,\energy V,\energy L$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian, potential, and Lagrangian | The classical functions in the discrete and continuous actions. |
| $x_k,x(t)$ | $\unit{m}$ | Positions | The discrete coordinates and the path in the continuum. |
| $\mechanical p_k$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The momentum integrated over in each interval. |
| $T,\epsilon,t_i,t_f$ | $\unit{s}$ | Times | The total duration, the step duration, and the endpoint times. |
| $N$ | $1$ | Number of steps | The number of time intervals in the discretization. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical S,\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action and reduced Planck constant | Their ratio sets the path phase. |
