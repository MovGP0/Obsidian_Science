---
title: "Harmonic Oscillator"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The oscillator action is quadratic. Splitting the path into the classical trajectory and fluctuations makes the fluctuation integral Gaussian.

For $\energy V(x)=\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}^{\,2}x^2/2$, write $x(t)=x_{\mathrm{cl}}(t)+y(t)$. The classical path satisfies $\ddot x_{\mathrm{cl}}+\mechanical{\omega}^{\,2}x_{\mathrm{cl}}=0$ with $x_{\mathrm{cl}}(0)=x_i$ and $x_{\mathrm{cl}}(T)=x_f$. The fluctuation $y$ vanishes at both endpoints. The action separates into $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}$ and a quadratic term in $y$. Thus the propagator factors into a classical phase and a Gaussian functional integral that depends only on $T$ for a fixed oscillator.

The classical action is

$$
\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}
=\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}{2\sin(\mechanical{\omega}T)}
\left[(x_f^2+x_i^2)\cos(\mechanical{\omega}T)-2x_fx_i\right].
\tag{5.5}
$$

The fluctuation prefactor can be calculated from the determinant of $-d^2/dt^2-\mechanical{\omega}^{\,2}$ with Dirichlet boundary conditions. It gives the full propagator

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal K_{\mathrm{osc}}(x_f,T;x_i,0)
={}&\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}\sin(\mechanical{\omega}T)}}\\
&\times\exp\left\{\frac{i\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}{2\mechanical{\hbar}\sin(\mechanical{\omega}T)}
\left[(x_f^2+x_i^2)\cos(\mechanical{\omega}T)-2x_fx_i\right]\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
$$

As $\mechanical{\omega}\to 0$, this tends to the [[05.03 Free Particle|free-particle propagator]]. At the focal times $T=n\pi/\mechanical{\omega}$, all classical trajectories from $x_i$ arrive at $(-1)^n x_i$ regardless of their initial velocities. The kernel becomes a delta distribution times a phase. For times beyond the first focus, continue the square-root phase from short positive times across the focal points.

> [!note] Remark 5.1. Spectrum from the trace
> [[08.00 Harmonic Oscillator]] treats the oscillator by algebraic methods. Equation (5.6) contains the same spectral information. In imaginary time, the trace is obtained by the Wick rotation $T\mapsto-i\thermal{\beta}\mechanical{\hbar}$.
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}e^{-i\energy{\hat H}T/\mechanical{\hbar}}
> =\int dx\,\signal K_{\mathrm{osc}}(x,T;x,0)=\sum_n e^{-i\energy E_n T/\mechanical{\hbar}}.
> $$
>
> After rotation this is the partition function, from which one obtains $\energy E_n=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}(n+1/2)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal K_{\mathrm{osc}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Oscillator propagator | The position-space kernel for oscillator evolution. |
| $x,x_i,x_f,x_{\mathrm{cl}},y$ | $\unit{m}$ | Positions and fluctuations | The path, endpoints, classical trajectory, and displacement from it. |
| $T,t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Times | The propagation duration and time along a path. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The oscillator mass. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The natural frequency of the oscillator. |
| $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}},\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Classical action and reduced Planck constant | Their ratio determines the phase from the classical path. |
| $\energy V,\energy{\hat H},\energy E_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The potential, Hamiltonian, and energy eigenvalues. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The inverse temperature parameter after Wick rotation. |
