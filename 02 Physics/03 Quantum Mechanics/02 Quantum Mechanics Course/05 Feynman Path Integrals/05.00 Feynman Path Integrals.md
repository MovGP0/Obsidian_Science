> [!TLDR]
> The Feynman path integral gives a transition amplitude as a sum over paths. The classical action determines the phase of each contribution.

The [[04.00 Schrödinger Equation|Schrödinger formulation]] describes time evolution by a unitary operator acting on the state vector. Feynman's equivalent formulation gives the probability amplitude for a transition from an initial to a final configuration. Add the contributions from all paths compatible with the endpoint conditions. Each path contributes a phase factor determined by the classical action evaluated along it. This gives the [[02.00 Postulates of Quantum Mechanics|superposition principle]] a geometric meaning.

This chapter constructs the path integral by dividing unitary time evolution into short intervals. It calculates the free-particle and harmonic-oscillator propagators in closed form, obtains the classical limit by stationary phase, and recovers the differential Schrödinger equation.

## Chapter summary

- The [[05.01 Transition Amplitude and Propagator|propagator]] $\signal K(x_f,t_f;x_i,t_i)=\langle x_f\lvert\hat U(t_f,t_i)\rvert x_i\rangle$ is the integral kernel of time evolution and specifies the dynamics.
- The [[05.02 Time Discretization and the Feynman Formula|Trotter product formula]] yields $\signal K=\int\mathcal D x\,e^{i\mechanical S[x]/\mechanical{\hbar}}$. Each path with the specified endpoints contributes a phase from its classical action.
- The [[05.03 Free Particle|free kernel]] is $\signal K_{\mathrm{free}}=\sqrt{\mechanical m/(2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}T)}\,e^{i\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}T)}$. Equation (5.6) gives the [[05.04 Harmonic Oscillator|oscillator kernel]].
- The [[05.05 Classical Limit and Stationary Phase|stationary phase condition]] $\delta\mechanical S=0$ selects the classical trajectory in accordance with Hamilton's principle.
- An infinitesimal propagation step [[05.06 Equivalence to the Schrödinger Equation|reproduces the Schrödinger equation]].
- A vector potential can give a [[05.07 Closed Paths and Geometric Phases|relative phase between paths]]. This phase depends on the enclosed magnetic flux and produces observable interference effects.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal K$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Propagator | The kernel of position-space evolution in one dimension. |
| $\hat U$ | $1$ | Time evolution operator | The unitary map between the initial and final states. |
| $x_i,x_f$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position endpoints | The initial and final positions. |
| $t_i,t_f,T$ | $\unit{s}$ | Times | The endpoint times and their difference. |
| $\mechanical S[x],\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action and reduced Planck constant | Their ratio is the phase associated with a path. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the propagating particle. |
| $\magnetic{\Phi}$ | $\unit{Wb}$ | Magnetic flux | The flux enclosed between two paths. |
