---
title: "Dynamical and Geometric Phases"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The dynamical phase depends on time spent at each energy. The geometric phase depends on the path and the choice of eigenstate phases at its endpoints.

Insert Eq. (18.2) of the [[18.01 Adiabatic Theorem|adiabatic theorem]] into the time-dependent Schrödinger equation. Differentiate and project onto $\langle n(R(t))\rvert$. Orthonormality of the instantaneous states gives

$$
\dot\gamma_n(t)=i\langle n(R(t))\rvert\partial_t\lvert n(R(t))\rangle
=i\langle n(R)\rvert\nabla_R\lvert n(R)\rangle\cdot\dot R.
$$

The total phase separates into two terms.

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)=
\underbrace{-\frac{1}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\int_0^t\energy{E}_n(R(t'))\,\mathrm dt'}_{\text{dynamical phase}}
+\underbrace{\gamma_n(t)}_{\text{geometric phase}}.
$$

The dynamical term depends on the speed at which the system traverses the path. For a fixed gauge along the path, the geometric term is independent of traversal speed.

> [!definition] Definition 18.1 · Berry connection
> The Berry connection for level $n$ is the vector field
>
> $$
> \mathbf A_n(R)=i\langle n(R)\rvert\nabla_R\lvert n(R)\rangle.
> $$
>
> The geometric phase accumulated along the path $C$ from $R(0)$ to $R(t)$ is
>
> $$
> \gamma_n(t)=\int_C\mathbf A_n(R)\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf R.
> $$

Differentiating $\langle n\mid n\rangle=1$ shows that $\langle n\rvert\nabla_R\lvert n\rangle$ is purely imaginary. Thus $\mathbf A_n$ is real. Under a change of eigenstate phase $\lvert n(R)\rangle\mapsto e^{i\beta(R)}\lvert n(R)\rangle$, the connection changes as $\mathbf A_n\mapsto\mathbf A_n-\nabla_R\beta$. The phase integral along an open path changes by an endpoint term. A closed path removes that endpoint dependence modulo $2\pi$.

**Fig. 18.1** Closed path $C$ on a unit sphere in parameter space. The vector $\mathbf R(t)$ traces the path. For the rotating spin example, the solid angle $\Omega$ subtended at the origin determines the Berry phase over one cycle.

> [!note] Scope of the solid-angle formula
> The source caption relates the phase to the solid angle. This holds for the spin example. In general, the phase depends on the flux of the Berry curvature through a surface bounded by $C$.

A slower traversal can increase the magnitude of the dynamical phase while leaving the geometric phase fixed. To isolate the geometric term in the laboratory, compare paths that accumulate equal dynamical phases. Another method uses a spin-echo sequence designed to cancel the dynamical terms while retaining a geometric contribution.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\theta_{\mathrm{tot}},\gamma_n$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Total and geometric phases | The overall phase and its path-dependent contribution. |
| $\mathbf A_n$ | Inverse parameter unit | Berry connection | Its line integral along the path gives the geometric phase. |
| $\Omega$ | $\unit{sr}$ | Solid angle | The oriented area enclosed on the unit sphere of directions. |
| $\energy{E}_n(R)$ | $\unit{J}$ | Instantaneous energy | Its time integral determines the dynamical phase. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The duration over which the phase accumulates. |
