title: "Geometric Phases"

> [!TLDR]
> A quantum state can acquire a phase set by the path through parameter space or by the topology of the accessible region. Interference can reveal this phase.

[[17.00 Symmetries and Conservation Laws|Symmetries]] act on quantum states through transformations parameterized by continuous or discrete groups. Geometric phases describe another aspect of quantum evolution. The phase depends on the geometry of a path through Hamiltonian parameter space or on the topology of the region accessible to the particle. Interference can reveal information that the instantaneous energies do not contain.

When external parameters $R$ of the Hamiltonian change sufficiently slowly, a state that begins in an isolated instantaneous eigenstate follows that eigenstate up to a phase. The dynamical phase is the integral of the energy over time. The geometric phase comes from the connection on the eigenstate bundle over parameter space. Its value around a closed path is an example of holonomy.

## Chapter summary

- A state that starts in an isolated instantaneous eigenstate follows that eigenstate if the Hamiltonian parameters vary slowly on the scale set by the energy gap.
- The total phase separates into the dynamical contribution $-\int\energy{E}_n\,\mathrm dt/\mechanical{\hbar}$ and the geometric contribution $\gamma_n$.
- The Berry connection $\mathbf A_n=i\langle n\rvert\nabla_R\lvert n\rangle$ depends on the gauge. Its circulation along a closed path gives the Berry phase, which is gauge invariant modulo $2\pi$.
- The Berry curvature $\mathbf F_n=\nabla_R\times\mathbf A_n$ is gauge invariant. Its flux through a surface bounded by the path gives the Berry phase when Stokes' theorem applies.
- A spin-$1/2$ eigenstate that follows a field around a cone of solid angle $\Omega$ gains the phase $\gamma_\pm=\mp\Omega/2$. The curvature has a monopole form in field parameter space.
- The Aharonov–Bohm phase is $\phi_{\mathrm{AB}}=\electric q\,\magnetic\Phi_B/\mechanical{\hbar}$ even when the paths lie outside the magnetic field.
- The positive flux period is $h/\lvert\electric q\rvert$.
- Both phases are holonomies. The geometric framework extends to matrix-valued transport and to cyclic evolution beyond the adiabatic regime.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\gamma_n,\phi_{\mathrm{AB}}$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Geometric phases | The Berry phase and the relative Aharonov–Bohm phase. |
