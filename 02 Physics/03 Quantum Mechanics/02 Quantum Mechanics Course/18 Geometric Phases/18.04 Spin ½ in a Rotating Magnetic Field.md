---
title: "Spin ½ in a Rotating Magnetic Field"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A spin eigenstate that follows a slowly rotating magnetic field acquires a Berry phase of $\mp\Omega/2$, where $\Omega$ is the oriented solid angle of the field direction.

Let the magnetic field $\magnetic{\mathbf B}(t)$ have constant magnitude and rotate about the $z$ axis on a cone of half-angle $\theta$. The Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{\hat H}(t)=-\magnetic\mu\,\magnetic{\mathbf B}(t)\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol\sigma}.
$$

Its instantaneous eigenstates are $\lvert\pm,\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle$, with the spin projected along $\hat{\mathbf n}=\magnetic{\mathbf B}/\lvert\magnetic{\mathbf B}\rvert$. For $\hat{\mathbf n}=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)$, choose the spin-up eigenstate

$$
\lvert+,\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle
=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(\theta/2)\\e^{i\varphi}\sin(\theta/2)\end{pmatrix}.
$$

In these angular coordinates the Berry connection has $A_\theta=0$ and

$$
A_\varphi=i\langle+,\hat{\mathbf n}\rvert\partial_\varphi\lvert+,\hat{\mathbf n}\rangle
=-\sin^2(\theta/2).
$$

Hold $\theta$ fixed and increase $\varphi$ by $2\pi$. The spin-up eigenstate acquires

$$
\gamma_+=\int_0^{2\pi}A_\varphi\,\mathrm d\varphi
=-2\pi\sin^2(\theta/2)=-\frac{\Omega}{2}.
\tag{18.4}
$$

Here $\Omega=2\pi(1-\cos\theta)$ is the oriented solid angle of the cone. The spin-down eigenstate gives the opposite sign, so $\gamma_\pm=\mp\Omega/2$ modulo $2\pi$. In the adiabatic limit, this result depends on the solid angle and is independent of the speed of traversal.

> [!note] Remark 18.1 · Limits of the phase
> When $\theta\to0$, the loop shrinks to a point and $\gamma_\pm\to0$. When $\theta\to\pi$, the solid angle tends to $4\pi$ and $\gamma_+\to-2\pi$, equivalent to zero modulo $2\pi$. The equator has $\theta=\pi/2$ and gives $\gamma_+=-\pi$, so the geometric factor changes the spinor's sign.

The corresponding Berry curvature in magnetic-field parameter space is a monopole field.

$$
\mathbf F_\pm(\magnetic{\mathbf B})
=\mp\frac12\frac{\magnetic{\mathbf B}}{\lvert\magnetic{\mathbf B}\rvert^3}.
$$

The monopole is at $\magnetic{\mathbf B}=0$, where the energy levels meet. Its effective charge $\mp1/2$ reflects the topology of the spin eigenstates.

> [!theorem] Corollary 18.1 · Berry phase for general spin
> For a Zeeman Hamiltonian linear in $\magnetic{\mathbf B}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S}$, let $m_S$ be the spin projection along the field in units of $\mechanical{\hbar}$. For a closed adiabatic path with oriented solid angle $\Omega$,
>
> $$
> \gamma_{m_S}=-m_S\Omega.
> \tag{18.5}
> $$
>
> This gives Eq. (18.4) at $S=1/2$ with $m_S=\pm1/2$.

More generally, for a rotationally invariant system coupled to a rotating field, the same form uses the total angular momentum projection $m$ along the instantaneous field axis.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\mathbf B},\magnetic\mu$ | $\unit{T},\unit{J\,T^{-1}}$ | Magnetic field and moment coefficient | The field and the coefficient of its coupling to the Pauli vector. |
