> [!TLDR]
> The Aharonov–Bohm phase is periodic in flux with period $h/\lvert\electric q\rvert$. In a superconductor, the Cooper-pair charge sets the flux quantum.

The interference phase $\phi_{\mathrm{AB}}$ is defined modulo $2\pi$. The flux values $\magnetic\Phi_B$ and $\magnetic\Phi_B+nh/\electric q$, where $n\in\mathbb Z$, give the same interference shift. The source writes the signed flux increment as

$$
\magnetic\Phi_0=\frac{h}{\electric q}.
$$

The positive flux period is $\lvert\magnetic\Phi_0\rvert=h/\lvert\electric q\rvert$. For an electron with $\electric q=-\electric e$, this is about $\quantity{4.135\times10^{-15}}{Wb}$. In a superconductor, the charge magnitude of a Cooper pair is $2\electric e$, so the flux quantum is $h/(2\electric e)\approx\quantity{2.067\times10^{-15}}{Wb}$. Flux quantization in superconducting rings gives this scale.

The replacement $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}\mapsto\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}-\electric q\magnetic{\mathbf A}$ is the momentum form of the covariant derivative for the electromagnetic $\mathrm U(1)$ connection. The Aharonov–Bohm phase is its holonomy on a noncontractible loop. For a degenerate eigenspace, a non-Abelian connection can give a matrix-valued Wilczek–Zee holonomy.

## Physical meaning

Both the Berry phase and the Aharonov–Bohm phase are holonomies of connections on bundles. For the Berry phase, the base space consists of Hamiltonian parameters and the fiber is the instantaneous eigenspace. For the Aharonov–Bohm phase, the base space is the region accessible to the particle, and the connection comes from minimal coupling. Parallel transport around a loop can return the state with a phase shift. Interference can measure that phase shift.

> [!note] Holonomy and topology
> The source links observable holonomy to a failure of global trivialization. A connection can have nontrivial holonomy on a trivial bundle. For example, the exterior Aharonov–Bohm connection can be represented by a global potential that is closed and has nonzero circulation.
