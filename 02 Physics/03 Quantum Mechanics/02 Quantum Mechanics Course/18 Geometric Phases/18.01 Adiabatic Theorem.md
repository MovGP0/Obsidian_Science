---
title: "Adiabatic Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A state that starts in an isolated instantaneous eigenstate follows that eigenstate up to a phase if the Hamiltonian changes sufficiently slowly.

Let $\energy{\hat H}(R(t))$ depend on real parameters $R=(R_1,\ldots,R_k)$ that vary along a smooth path. At each fixed $R$, use an orthonormal basis of instantaneous eigenstates.

$$
\energy{\hat H}(R)\lvert n(R)\rangle=\energy{E}_n(R)\lvert n(R)\rangle.
$$

Assume the level of interest is separated from the others by a positive gap $\energy{\Delta}_n(R)=\min_{m\ne n}\lvert\energy{E}_n(R)-\energy{E}_m(R)\rvert$ along the path. Prepare the state as $\lvert\signal{\psi}(0)\rangle=\lvert n(R(0))\rangle$. If $R$ changes sufficiently slowly on the time scale set by the gap, the state follows $\lvert n(R(t))\rangle$ up to a phase.

> [!theorem] Theorem 18.1 · Adiabatic theorem, operational form
> Let $\energy{\hat H}(R(t))$ have a discrete spectrum and a uniformly positive gap around level $n$ for $t\in[0,T]$. The source gives the local adiabatic criterion
>
> $$
> \mechanical{\hbar}\frac{\lVert\dot R\cdot\nabla_R\energy{\hat H}\rVert}{\energy{\Delta}_n^2}\ll1.
> \tag{18.1}
> $$
>
> For slow evolution along a fixed smooth path with an open gap, the Schrödinger solution that starts in level $n$ has the adiabatic form
>
> $$
> \lvert\signal{\psi}(t)\rangle
> \simeq e^{i\gamma_n(t)}
> e^{-\frac{i}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\int_0^t\energy{E}_n(R(t'))\,\mathrm dt'}
> \lvert n(R(t))\rangle.
> \tag{18.2}
> $$
>
> The real phase $\gamma_n(t)$ is determined below.

> [!note] Scope of the error estimate
> The source gives the heuristic scale $O(\mechanical{\hbar}/(\energy{\Delta}_nT))$. For a fixed smooth path and a uniform positive gap, the error can fall as $1/T$, with a coefficient that depends on the gap and derivatives of the Hamiltonian. To use Eq. (18.1), write $R$ as a function of $s=t/T$ and compare $T$ with the maximum of $\mechanical{\hbar}\lVert(\mathrm dR/\mathrm ds)\cdot\nabla_R\energy{\hat H}\rVert/\energy{\Delta}_n^2$. A closing gap invalidates this estimate.

A standard proof expands the state in the instantaneous basis and removes each dynamical phase.

$$
\lvert\signal{\psi}(t)\rangle=
\sum_m c_m(t)e^{-\frac{i}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\int_0^t\energy{E}_m(R(t'))\,\mathrm dt'}
\lvert m(R(t))\rangle.
$$

For a gapped path traversed slowly, the off-diagonal amplitudes remain small. The ratio in Eq. (18.1) indicates their scale in the source's estimate. The phase of the surviving coefficient $c_n$ is the geometric phase derived in the next section.

> [!note] Degeneracy
> The single-level condition fails at a degeneracy where $\energy{\Delta}_n$ vanishes. Such points can act as singularities for the eigenstate connection and can affect the geometric phase of paths around them.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $R$ | Depends on parameter | Control parameters | The real coordinates that specify the Hamiltonian. |
| $\energy{\hat H}(R),\energy{E}_n(R),\energy{\Delta}_n(R)$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian, eigenenergy, and gap | The instantaneous operator, level energy, and separation from the nearest other level. |
| $t,T$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time and duration | The elapsed time and total evolution time. |
| $\gamma_n$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Geometric phase | The phase of the leading amplitude after the dynamical phase is removed. |
