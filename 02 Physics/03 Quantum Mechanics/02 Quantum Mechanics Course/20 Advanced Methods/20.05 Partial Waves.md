---
title: "Partial Waves"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For a central potential, each orbital angular momentum channel has its own phase shift. These shifts determine the elastic scattering amplitude and total cross section.

Expand the incident plane wave with the polar axis along the beam.

$$
e^{ikz}=\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty i^\ell(2\ell+1)j_\ell(kr)P_\ell(\cos\theta),
$$

where $j_\ell$ is a spherical Bessel function and $P_\ell$ is a Legendre polynomial. The central potential changes the asymptotic phase of the partial wave of orbital angular momentum $\ell$ by $\delta_\ell$. The resulting amplitude is

$$
f(\theta)=\frac1{2ik}
\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty (2\ell+1)(e^{2i\delta_\ell}-1)P_\ell(\cos\theta).
$$

The total elastic cross section is

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{4\pi}{k^2}
\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty(2\ell+1)\sin^2\delta_\ell.
$$

At low energy for a short-range potential, the $\ell=0$ channel, or $s$ wave, usually dominates when the symmetry allows it. Its leading phase shift defines the **scattering length** $a$.

$$
\delta_0(k)\simeq-ka \qquad (k\to0).
$$

At sufficiently low energy, this single length gives the leading effective interaction for the $s$-wave channel. Such descriptions are used for dilute ultracold gases of bosons and of fermions in different internal states.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $k$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wave number | Sets the asymptotic wavelength of the particle. |
| $\ell,\delta_\ell$ | $1,\unit{rad}$ | Partial-wave index and phase shift | The orbital angular momentum label and the phase change in its channel. |
| $a$ | $\unit{m}$ | Scattering length | The negative slope of the $s$-wave phase shift at zero wave number. |
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Total elastic cross section | The weighted sum of all partial-wave cross sections. |
