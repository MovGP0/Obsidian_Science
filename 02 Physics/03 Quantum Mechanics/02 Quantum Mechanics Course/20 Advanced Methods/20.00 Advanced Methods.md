> [!TLDR]
> The variational method bounds ground-state energies, WKB describes semiclassical motion, and scattering theory describes transitions between continuum states.

This chapter introduces three approximation methods. The variational method gives upper bounds on the ground-state energy. The WKB approximation applies in semiclassical regimes and complements perturbation theory. Scattering theory treats continuum states. The sections give the main ideas and calculation tools for each method.

## Chapter summary

- The variational bound is $\langle\signal\phi\rvert\energy{\hat H}\lvert\signal\phi\rangle\ge\energy E_0$ for a normalized trial state. Its accuracy depends on the trial family.
- For two smooth turning points, Bohr–Sommerfeld quantization gives $\oint\mechanical p\,\mathrm dx=2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}(n+1/2)$ and is exact for the harmonic oscillator. The hard walls of an infinite well give $\oint\mechanical p\,\mathrm dx=2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}n$ instead.
- The distant scattered wave has amplitude $f(\theta,\phi)$ in front of $e^{ikr}/r$. The differential cross section is $\mathrm d\sigma/\mathrm d\Omega=\lvert f\rvert^2$.
- The first Born amplitude is proportional to the Fourier transform of the potential at the wave-vector transfer $\mathbf q$. Its Coulomb limit gives Rutherford scattering.
- The partial-wave phase shifts give $\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=(4\pi/k^2)\sum_\ell(2\ell+1)\sin^2\delta_\ell$ for elastic scattering. The scattering length sets the leading low-energy $s$-wave response.
- The optical theorem gives $\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=(4\pi/k)\operatorname{Im}f(0)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy E_0$ | $\unit{J}$ | Ground-state energy | The quantity bounded from above by the variational principle. |
| $\mechanical p$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Classical momentum | Its phase-space action gives the WKB quantization rule. |
| $f,\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ | $\unit{m},\unit{m^2}$ | Scattering amplitude and total cross section | Determine angular scattering and total removal from the incident beam. |
| $\delta_\ell,a$ | $\unit{rad},\unit{m}$ | Phase shift and scattering length | The angular-momentum channel phase and the low-energy length scale. |
