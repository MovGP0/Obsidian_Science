---
title: "Born Approximation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In the first Born approximation, the scattering amplitude is proportional to the Fourier transform of the potential at the transferred wave vector.

For a weak potential, first-order scattering theory gives

$$
f^{\mathrm B}(\theta)=-\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}^2}
\int e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r'}\energy V(\mathbf r')\,\mathrm d^3\mathbf r',
$$

where $\mathbf q=\mathbf k_f-\mathbf k_i$ is the wave-vector transfer. The momentum transfer is $\mechanical{\hbar}\mathbf q$. Thus $f^{\mathrm B}$ is a Fourier component of the potential times a constant. For elastic scattering by a spherically symmetric potential, $q=\lvert\mathbf q\rvert=2k\sin(\theta/2)$ and the angular integral gives

$$
f^{\mathrm B}(\theta)=-\frac{2\mechanical m}{\mechanical{\hbar}^2q}
\int_0^\infty r\energy V(r)\sin(qr)\,\mathrm dr.
$$

## Yukawa potential

For $\energy V(r)=V_0e^{-\mu r}/r$, this gives

$$
f^{\mathrm B}(\theta)=-\frac{2\mechanical m V_0}
{\mechanical{\hbar}^2(q^2+\mu^2)}.
$$

The differential cross section is

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\sigma}{\mathrm d\Omega}
=\frac{4\mechanical m^2 V_0^2}
{\mechanical{\hbar}^4(q^2+\mu^2)^2}.
$$

In the Coulomb limit $\mu\to0$, the result is the Rutherford cross section, proportional to $1/\sin^4(\theta/2)$. The exact quantum Coulomb cross section agrees with this result. This agreement concerns the cross section, which uses the magnitude of the amplitude.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathbf q,k,\mu$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wave-vector transfer, wave number, and screening parameter | $1/\mu$ sets the range of the Yukawa potential. |
| $\energy V(r),V_0$ | $\unit{J},\unit{J\,m}$ | Potential energy and Yukawa strength | The factor $1/r$ converts $V_0$ to an energy scale. |
| $f^{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Born scattering amplitude | The first-order amplitude obtained from the Fourier transform. |
| $\mathrm d\sigma/\mathrm d\Omega$ | $\unit{m^2\,sr^{-1}}$ | Differential cross section | The squared magnitude of the amplitude. |
