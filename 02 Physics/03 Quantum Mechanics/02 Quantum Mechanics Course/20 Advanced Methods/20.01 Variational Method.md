title: "Variational Method"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The energy expectation in any normalized trial state is at least the ground-state energy. Minimizing over trial parameters gives an upper bound.

> [!theorem] Theorem 20.1 · Variational principle
> For every normalized state $\lvert\signal\phi\rangle$ with finite energy expectation,
>
> $$
> \langle\signal\phi\rvert\energy{\hat H}\lvert\signal\phi\rangle\ge\energy E_0,
> $$
>
> where $\energy E_0$ is the ground-state energy. Equality holds exactly when the state lies in the ground eigenspace.

**Proof.** Expand $\lvert\signal\phi\rangle=\sum_n c_n\lvert n\rangle$ in an energy eigenbasis for a discrete spectrum. Then

$$
\langle\signal\phi\rvert\energy{\hat H}\lvert\signal\phi\rangle
=\sum_n\lvert c_n\rvert^2\energy E_n
\ge\energy E_0\sum_n\lvert c_n\rvert^2
=\energy E_0.
$$

## Procedure

Choose a family of nonzero trial states $\lvert\signal\phi(\boldsymbol\alpha)\rangle$ with adjustable parameters $\boldsymbol\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)$. Minimize the Rayleigh quotient.

$$
\energy E(\boldsymbol\alpha)
=\frac{\langle\signal\phi(\boldsymbol\alpha)\rvert\energy{\hat H}\lvert\signal\phi(\boldsymbol\alpha)\rangle}
{\langle\signal\phi(\boldsymbol\alpha)\mid\signal\phi(\boldsymbol\alpha)\rangle}.
$$

The minimum is an upper bound on $\energy E_0$. The error tends to zero if the family can approximate a ground state in energy expectation.

## Harmonic oscillator with a Gaussian trial state

Use $\signal\phi_\alpha(x)=(\alpha/\pi)^{1/4}e^{-\alpha x^2/2}$ for $\alpha>0$. Its energy expectation is

$$
\langle\energy{\hat H}\rangle
=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2\alpha}{4\mechanical m}
+\frac{\mechanical m\omega^2}{4\alpha}.
$$

Set $\mathrm d\energy E/\mathrm d\alpha=\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(4\mechanical m)-\mechanical m\omega^2/(4\alpha^2)=0$. The minimum is at $\alpha^*=\mechanical m\omega/\mechanical{\hbar}$ and gives $\energy E_{\min}=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega/2$. This is exact because the Gaussian family includes the true ground state.

## Helium with a hydrogen-like trial state

Use the product $\signal\phi(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2)=\signal\phi_{1s}(\mathbf r_1;Z_{\mathrm{eff}})\signal\phi_{1s}(\mathbf r_2;Z_{\mathrm{eff}})$ for the spatial part, with effective charge number $Z_{\mathrm{eff}}$. The energy expectation derived in Exercise 7 is

$$
\energy E(Z_{\mathrm{eff}})
=\unit{Ha}\left[Z_{\mathrm{eff}}^2-4Z_{\mathrm{eff}}+\frac58 Z_{\mathrm{eff}}\right].
$$

With one variational parameter, the minimum is within $2\%$ of the experimental value $\quantity{-78.98}{eV}$ quoted in the source.

## Excited states

To bound the first excited energy in a system with a nondegenerate ground state, restrict the trial states to the orthogonal complement of the exact ground state. A symmetry such as parity can enforce this orthogonality. Minimizing with a trial state orthogonal only to an approximate ground state gives an estimate and can lose the upper-bound property.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy E_0,\energy E_{\min}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian and energies | The operator, exact ground energy, and minimum trial expectation. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{m^{-2}}$ in the oscillator example | Gaussian parameter | Sets the inverse squared width of the trial state. |
