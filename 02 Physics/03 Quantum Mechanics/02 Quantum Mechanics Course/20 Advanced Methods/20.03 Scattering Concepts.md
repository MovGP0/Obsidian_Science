> [!TLDR]
> In elastic scattering by a short-range potential, the distant wave consists of an incident plane wave and an outgoing spherical wave. The scattering amplitude determines the angular cross section.

To probe a quantum system, direct a beam of particles at the target and measure the outgoing particles. Let the particles have mass $\mechanical m$ and incident momentum $\mechanical{\hbar}\mathbf k_0$. The potential $\energy V(\mathbf r)$ is localized or decays sufficiently fast at large distance. Far from the target,

$$
\signal\psi(\mathbf r)\simeq e^{i\mathbf k_0\cdot\mathbf r}
+f(\theta,\phi)\frac{e^{ik_0 r}}{r}.
$$

The first term is the incident plane wave. The second is the outgoing spherical wave with scattering amplitude $f(\theta,\phi)$. The **differential cross section** is the scattered flux per unit solid angle divided by the incident flux density.

$$
\frac{\mathrm d\sigma}{\mathrm d\Omega}=\lvert f(\theta,\phi)\rvert^2.
$$

Integrating over all directions gives the total elastic cross section $\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=\int\lvert f\rvert^2\,\mathrm d\Omega$ for this elastic model.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathbf k_0$ | $\unit{rad\,m^{-1}}$ | Incident wave vector | Sets the direction and momentum of the incident beam. |
| $\energy V(\mathbf r)$ | $\unit{J}$ | Scattering potential | The interaction with the target. |
| $f(\theta,\phi)$ | $\unit{m}$ | Scattering amplitude | Multiplies the outgoing spherical wave. |
| $\theta,\phi,\Omega$ | $\unit{rad},\unit{rad},\unit{sr}$ | Angular coordinates and solid angle | Specify the direction and angular acceptance of the detector. |
| $\mathrm d\sigma/\mathrm d\Omega,\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ | $\unit{m^2\,sr^{-1}},\unit{m^2}$ | Differential and total cross sections | Give the angular distribution and its integral. |
