> [!TLDR]
> The WKB wave function oscillates in classically allowed regions and is exponential in forbidden regions. Airy functions connect the two forms at a simple turning point.

Following the [[07.03 WKB Approximation for Tunneling|WKB approximation]], consider a particle of mass $\mechanical m$ with energy $\energy E$. Where $\energy E>\energy V(x)$, let $k(x)=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E-\energy V(x))}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. The wave function is approximately

$$
\signal\psi(x)\simeq\frac1{\sqrt{k(x)}}
\left[Ae^{i\int^x k(x')\,\mathrm dx'}+Be^{-i\int^x k(x')\,\mathrm dx'}\right].
$$

Where $\energy E<\energy V(x)$, let $\kappa(x)=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V(x)-\energy E)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. Then

$$
\signal\psi(x)\simeq\frac1{\sqrt{\kappa(x)}}
\left[Ce^{-\int^x\kappa(x')\,\mathrm dx'}+De^{\int^x\kappa(x')\,\mathrm dx'}\right].
$$

The integration reference can be absorbed into the coefficients. At a classical turning point $x_\pm$ with $\energy V(x_\pm)=\energy E$, the local wave number vanishes and the WKB prefactor diverges. For a simple turning point, a local solution in terms of Airy functions matches the two asymptotic forms.

## Bohr–Sommerfeld rule

For a well with two simple turning points $x_1<x_2$, the WKB quantization condition is

$$
\oint\mechanical p\,\mathrm dx
=2\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\mechanical p(x)\,\mathrm dx
=2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}\left(n+\frac12\right),
\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots
$$

Here $\mechanical p(x)=\mechanical{\hbar}k(x)=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E-\energy V(x))}$ denotes the positive momentum on the forward branch. The $1/2$ comes from matching at the two turning points.

### Harmonic oscillator

For $\energy V(x)=\mechanical m\omega^2x^2/2$, the turning points are $x_\pm=\pm\sqrt{2\energy E/(\mechanical m\omega^2)}$. The integral is

$$
\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E-\energy V(x))}\,\mathrm dx=\frac{\pi\energy E}{\omega}.
$$

The quantization condition gives $\energy E_n=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega(n+1/2)$, which equals the exact spectrum.

### Infinite square well

An infinite wall requires $\signal\psi=0$ at the boundary. This replaces the Airy matching used at a smooth turning point. For a well of width $L$ with $\energy V=0$ inside, the condition is $2kL=2\pi n$ with $n=1,2,\ldots$. It gives the exact energies $\energy E_n=n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\mechanical m L^2)$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x,x_\pm,L$ | $\unit{m}$ | Coordinates and well width | The position, turning points, and width of the infinite well. |
| $k(x),\kappa(x)$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Local wave number and decay constant | Set oscillation and decay in the respective regions. |
| $\mechanical p(x)$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Classical momentum magnitude | Its integral across the well sets the quantization condition. |
| $\energy E,\energy V(x)$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy and potential | Their difference determines the local behavior. |
