---
title: "Optical Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The imaginary part of the forward scattering amplitude fixes the total cross section through conservation of probability flux.

> [!theorem] Theorem 20.2 · Optical theorem
> With the scattering amplitude convention of this chapter,
>
> $$
> \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{4\pi}{k}\operatorname{Im}[f(\theta=0)].
> $$

Scattering removes flux from the incident beam. The scattered wave interferes destructively with the incident wave in the forward direction. Conservation of probability flux gives the relation between this interference and the total cross section. If other channels are open, the total includes flux into those channels. The same unitarity principle applies in optics, quantum mechanics, and quantum field theory, with conventions suited to each setting.

## Physical meaning

The variational method applies to bound-state energies and improves with the choice of trial states. WKB applies in semiclassical regimes such as large quantum numbers, thick barriers, and deep wells. Scattering theory gives amplitudes and cross sections for unbound particles. Each method applies to a different part of the quantum problem.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ | $\unit{m^2}$ | Total cross section | Measures flux removed from the incident beam. |
| $k$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Incident wave number | The magnitude of the incident wave vector. |
| $f(0)$ | $\unit{m}$ | Forward scattering amplitude | Its imaginary part gives the total cross section. |
