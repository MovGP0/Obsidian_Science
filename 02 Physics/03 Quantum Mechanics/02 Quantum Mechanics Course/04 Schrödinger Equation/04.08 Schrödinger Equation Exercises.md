---
title: "Schrödinger Equation Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Apply the Schrödinger equation, the continuity equation, and the laws of wave-packet evolution.

1. Calculate $\signal{\mathbf J}$ for $\signal{\psi}(x)=\signal A e^{i\signal kx}+\signal B e^{-i\signal kx}$. Interpret the terms.  
   **Result.** $\signal J=(\mechanical{\hbar}\signal k/\mechanical m)(\lvert\signal A\rvert^2-\lvert\signal B\rvert^2)$. The net flux is the flux to the right minus the flux to the left.
2. Verify that $\signal{\psi}(x,t)=e^{i\signal kx-i\mechanical{\hbar}\signal k^{\,2}t/(2\mechanical m)}$ solves the free Schrödinger equation.
3. For $\signal{\omega}(k)=\mechanical{\hbar}\signal k^{\,2}/(2\mechanical m)$, calculate $\mechanical v_f(k)$ and $\mechanical v_g(k)$ and show that $\mechanical v_g=2\mechanical v_f$.
4. Calculate $\signal{\mathbf J}$ for real $\signal{\psi}(x)$.  
   **Result.** $\signal{\mathbf J}=0$.
5. Show that $\signal{\psi}(x,t)=\signal{\psi}(x)e^{-i\energy E t/\mechanical{\hbar}}$ with real $\signal{\psi}(x)$ satisfies $d\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle/dt=0$. Interpret the result.
6. Verify explicitly that $[\hat x,\energy{\hat H}]=i\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat p}/\mechanical m$ for $\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hat p}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical m)+\energy V(\hat x)$.
7. For the Gaussian wave packet $\signal{\phi}(k)=(2\sigma^2/\pi)^{1/4}e^{-\sigma^2(\signal k-\signal k_0)^2}$, calculate $\signal{\psi}(x,t)$ explicitly and verify $\sigma(t)=\sigma\sqrt{1+(\mechanical{\hbar}t/(2\mechanical m\sigma^2))^2}$.
8. For the harmonic oscillator, prove that
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \langle\hat x(t)\rangle&=\langle\hat x(0)\rangle\cos(\mechanical{\omega}t)
   +\frac{\langle\mechanical{\hat p}(0)\rangle}{\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}}\sin(\mechanical{\omega}t),\\
   \langle\mechanical{\hat p}(t)\rangle&=\langle\mechanical{\hat p}(0)\rangle\cos(\mechanical{\omega}t)
   -\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}\langle\hat x(0)\rangle\sin(\mechanical{\omega}t).
   \end{aligned}
   $$
9. Show that a time-dependent potential can change the energy of a particle according to $d\langle\energy{\hat H}\rangle/dt=\langle\partial_t\energy{\hat H}\rangle$. Apply this result to $\energy V(x,t)=\energy V_0(x)+\mechanical f(t)x$, which describes a time-dependent external force.
10. **Free propagator.** Show that the solution of the free equation $i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_t\signal{\psi}=-\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}\partial_x^2\signal{\psi}/(2\mechanical m)$ can be written as
    $$
    \signal{\psi}(x,t)=\int\signal K(x,t;x',0)\signal{\psi}(x',0)\,dx',
    \qquad
    \signal K=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}t}}e^{i\mechanical m(x-x')^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}t)}.
    $$
    **Hint.** Calculate the Fourier transform.
11. **Effective nonunitary time evolution.** Adding an anti-Hermitian term to $\energy{\hat H}$ changes the evolution. For example, if $\energy{\hat H}\mapsto\energy{\hat H}-i\mechanical{\hbar}\gamma/2$, the norm of $\lvert\signal{\psi}\rangle$ decreases as $e^{-\gamma t/2}$. Interpret how this models exponential decay in nonrelativistic quantum mechanics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}(x,t)$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Position wave function | The probability amplitude over one-dimensional position. |
| $\signal{\phi}(k)$ | $\unit{m^{1/2}}$ | Wavenumber amplitude | The spectral amplitude of the Gaussian packet. |
| $\signal A,\signal B$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Plane-wave coefficients | The amplitudes of the right- and left-moving components. |
| $\signal J$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ in 1D | Probability current | The net probability flux in one dimension. |
| $\signal k,\signal k_0$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | The spatial wave frequency and its value at the center of the spectrum. |
| $\signal{\omega}(k),\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The dispersion relation and the oscillator frequency, respectively. |
| $\mechanical v_f,\mechanical v_g$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Phase and group velocities | The phase and envelope propagation speeds. |
| $\hat x,\sigma,\sigma(t)$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and packet width | The position operator and the initial and evolved standard deviations. |
| $\mechanical{\hat p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum operator | The observable for linear momentum. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy V,\energy E$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The Hamiltonian, potential energy, and stationary energy. |
| $\mechanical f(t)$ | $\unit{N}$ | Potential slope | The negative of the external force from the linear potential term. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\signal K$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Free propagator | The kernel that evolves a one-dimensional wave function. |
| $\gamma$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Decay rate | The rate at which the squared norm decreases. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the Schrödinger equation. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The elapsed time. |
