---
title: "From Unitary Time Evolution to the Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Differentiating the unitary evolution operator gives the time-dependent Schrödinger equation.

Postulate V gives $\lvert\psi(t)\rangle=\hat U(t,t_0)\lvert\psi(t_0)\rangle$, where $\hat U$ is unitary and $\hat U(t,t)=\hat I$. Define the infinitesimal generator $\energy{\hat H}(t)$ by

$$
i\hbar\frac{\partial\hat U(t,t_0)}{\partial t}
=\energy{\hat H}(t)\hat U(t,t_0).
\tag{4.1}
$$

Apply Eq. (4.1) to the initial state to obtain the **time-dependent Schrödinger equation**.

$$
i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\lvert\psi(t)\rangle
=\energy{\hat H}(t)\lvert\psi(t)\rangle.
$$

Unitarity requires the Hamiltonian to be Hermitian. Differentiating $\hat U\hat U^\dagger=\hat I$ and using Eq. (4.1) gives $\energy{\hat H}=\energy{\hat H}^\dagger$ on the relevant domain.

## Position representation

For a nonrelativistic particle of mass $\mechanical{m}$ in a potential $\energy{V}(\mechanical{\vec r},t)$, the Hamiltonian is $\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hat{\vec p}}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical{m})+\energy{V}(\mechanical{\vec r},t)$. Use the wave function $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{\vec r},t)=\langle\vec r\mid\psi(t)\rangle$ and the momentum operator $-i\hbar\nabla$ to obtain

$$
i\hbar\partial_t\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{\vec r},t)=
\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mechanical{m}}\nabla^2+\energy{V}(\mechanical{\vec r},t)\right]\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{\vec r},t).
$$

The equation is linear, first order in time, and second order in space. For the specified Hamiltonian, the initial wave function determines the solution uniquely. Unitary evolution also gives the solution at earlier times.

## Time-independent Hamiltonian

If $\energy{\hat H}$ is independent of time, then

$$
\hat U(t,t_0)=\exp[-i\energy{\hat H}(t-t_0)/\hbar].
\tag{4.2}
$$

For a discrete spectrum, expand the initial state as $\lvert\psi(t_0)\rangle=\sum_n c_n\lvert E_n\rangle$ where $\energy{\hat H}\lvert E_n\rangle=\energy{E}_n\lvert E_n\rangle$. The evolved state is

$$
\lvert\psi(t)\rangle=\sum_n c_n e^{-i\energy{E}_n(t-t_0)/\hbar}\lvert E_n\rangle.
$$

The eigenvalues and eigenstates determine the time evolution. A continuous spectrum uses the corresponding spectral integral.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $t,t_0$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The current time and initial time. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy{V},\energy{E}_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energies | The Hamiltonian, potential energy, and energy eigenvalue. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale in the evolution equation and phase. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\vec r}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The spatial argument of the wave function and potential. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\vec p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum operator | The vector operator in the kinetic energy term. |
| $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{\vec r},t)$ | $\unit{m^{-3/2}}$ | Position wave function | The normalized probability amplitude in three spatial dimensions. |
