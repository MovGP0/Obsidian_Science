---
title: "Ehrenfest Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Ehrenfest theorem gives equations of motion for the mean position and momentum. The rate of change of mean momentum is the mean force.

Take expectation values of the [[04.05 Heisenberg Picture|Heisenberg equations]] to obtain the equivalent result in the Schrödinger picture.

> [!theorem] Theorem 4.1. Ehrenfest theorem
> For a nonrelativistic particle in a potential $\energy V(\hat x)$,
>
> $$
> \frac{d\langle\hat x\rangle}{dt}=\frac{\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle}{\mechanical m},
> \qquad
> \frac{d\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle}{dt}=-\langle\energy{V'}(\hat x)\rangle.
> $$

The expectation values satisfy equations of Newtonian form. The classical force law would give $-\energy{V'}(\langle\hat x\rangle)$, while the quantum equation gives $-\langle\energy{V'}(\hat x)\rangle$. The two are approximately equal for a packet well localized in a region where the force varies smoothly. The difference can be large for a delocalized state, such as a packet spread over a barrier.

## Derivation in the Schrödinger picture

For an observable $\hat A$,

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat A\rangle
=\frac{1}{i\mechanical{\hbar}}\langle[\hat A,\energy{\hat H}]\rangle
+\langle\partial_t\hat A\rangle.
$$

For position, $[\hat x,\energy{\hat H}]=[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical m)]=i\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat p}/\mechanical m$. For momentum, $[\mechanical{\hat p},\energy V(\hat x)]=-i\mechanical{\hbar}\energy{V'}(\hat x)$. Substitute the commutators into the derivative to obtain the theorem.

## Example. Harmonic oscillator

For $\energy V(x)=\frac12\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}^{\,2}x^2$, its derivative is $\energy{V'}(x)=\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}^{\,2}x$. The Ehrenfest equations become

$$
\frac{d\langle\hat x\rangle}{dt}=\frac{\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle}{\mechanical m},
\qquad
\frac{d\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle}{dt}=-\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}^{\,2}\langle\hat x\rangle.
$$

The force is linear, so its expectation equals the force evaluated at the mean position. The mean position oscillates at the same angular frequency as the classical [[08.00 Harmonic Oscillator|harmonic oscillator]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\langle\hat x\rangle$ | $\unit{m}$ | Mean position | The expectation value of the position operator. |
| $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Mean momentum | The expectation value of the momentum operator. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\energy V,\energy{\hat H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy and Hamiltonian | The potential energy function and the total energy operator. |
| $-\langle\energy{V'}(\hat x)\rangle$ | $\unit{N}$ | Mean force | The expectation value of the force in the potential. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The frequency of the harmonic motion. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the commutator and evolution equations. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The elapsed time. |
