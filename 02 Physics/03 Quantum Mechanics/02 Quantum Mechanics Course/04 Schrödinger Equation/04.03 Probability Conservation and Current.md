---
title: "Probability Conservation and Current"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The probability density and probability current obey a local continuity equation.

Unitarity conserves the total probability $\int\lvert\signal{\psi}\rvert^2\,d^3r$. To obtain local conservation for a real scalar potential, multiply the time-dependent Schrödinger equation by $\signal{\psi}^*$ and its conjugate by $\signal{\psi}$. Subtract to get

$$
\partial_t\lvert\signal{\psi}\rvert^2+
\frac{\hbar}{2\mechanical{m}i}\nabla\cdot
(\signal{\psi}^*\nabla\signal{\psi}-\signal{\psi}\nabla\signal{\psi}^*)=0.
$$

Define the probability density $\rho=\lvert\signal{\psi}\rvert^2$ and the probability current density

$$
\vec J=
\frac{\hbar}{2\mechanical{m}i}(\signal{\psi}^*\nabla\signal{\psi}-\signal{\psi}\nabla\signal{\psi}^*)
=\frac1{\mechanical{m}}\operatorname{Re}[\signal{\psi}^*\mechanical{\hat{\vec p}}\signal{\psi}].
$$

The **continuity equation** is $\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\vec J=0$. Probability can flow through the boundary of a region, but there is no source or sink. For a normalized state with no flux at infinity, $\int\rho\,d^3r=1$ at all times.

## Example. Plane-wave current

For $\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})=A e^{i\signal{k}\mechanical{x}}$, the two derivative products are $i\signal{k}\lvert A\rvert^2$ and $-i\signal{k}\lvert A\rvert^2$. Thus

$$
J=\frac{\hbar\signal{k}}{\mechanical{m}}\lvert A\rvert^2=\mechanical{v}\rho,\qquad
\mechanical{v}=\frac{\hbar\signal{k}}{\mechanical{m}}=\frac{\mechanical{p}}{\mechanical{m}}.
$$

The current has the same density-times-speed form as a classical flux.

## A reflection probability is a ratio of fluxes

Consider a real, stationary one-dimensional potential with freely propagating states on each side. Each traveling plane-wave term carries a current with the sign of its direction of travel. Divide the absolute reflected current by the incident current for the reflection probability, and divide the transmitted current by the incident current for the transmission probability. Conservation gives a sum of one. If the wave numbers on the two asymptotic sides differ, the transmitted and incident plane waves carry different currents at equal amplitude, so the transmission probability includes the ratio of their wave numbers. For an evanescent tail on a semi-infinite side, the decaying exponential carries zero current, although its amplitude can be nonzero.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi},A$ | — | Wave function and amplitude | The normalized wave function has unit $\unit{m^{-3/2}}$ in three dimensions. A plane wave uses a chosen normalization. |
| $\rho$ | — | Probability density | Probability per volume in three dimensions or per length in one dimension. |
| $\vec J,J$ | — | Probability current density | Probability flux per area in three dimensions or probability per time in one dimension. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\hbar$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale relating the phase gradient to momentum. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\vec p}},\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | The operator and plane-wave momentum. |
| $\signal{k}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | The spatial phase rate of the plane wave. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position | The one-dimensional coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{v}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Velocity | The plane-wave momentum divided by mass. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The variable in the continuity equation. |
