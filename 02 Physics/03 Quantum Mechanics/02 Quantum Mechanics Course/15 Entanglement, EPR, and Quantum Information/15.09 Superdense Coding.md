---
title: "Superdense Coding"
---

> [!TLDR]
> With a shared Bell pair, Alice can send two classical bits by transmitting one qubit. A Bell measurement recovers the message.

Alice and Bob share $\lvert\signal{\Phi}^+\rangle_{AB}$. To send one of the four messages $\{00,01,10,11\}$, Alice applies $\hat I$, $Z$, $X$, or $XZ$, respectively, to her qubit. Up to global phase, these operations produce the four Bell states.

$$
00\mapsto\lvert\signal{\Phi}^+\rangle,\qquad
01\mapsto\lvert\signal{\Phi}^-\rangle,\qquad
10\mapsto\lvert\signal{\Psi}^+\rangle,\qquad
11\mapsto\lvert\signal{\Psi}^-\rangle.
\tag{15.4}
$$

Alice sends her qubit to Bob through a quantum channel. He then holds both halves of the pair. A measurement in the orthonormal Bell basis identifies the message.

Superdense coding uses one transmitted qubit and one shared Bell pair to communicate two classical bits. [[15.08 Quantum Teleportation|Teleportation]] uses two transmitted classical bits and one shared Bell pair to transfer a qubit state. Both protocols use the shared entanglement as a resource for communication.

Alice's local unitary leaves Bob's reduced state unchanged before the qubit arrives. Bob can read the message only after the physical transfer.
