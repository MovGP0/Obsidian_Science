title: "Entanglement, EPR, and Quantum Information"

> [!TLDR]
> Entangled states produce correlations that can violate Bell inequalities. Quantum information protocols use entanglement for teleportation and superdense coding.

The tensor product describes composite systems. [[13.00 Density Matrix and Decoherence|Density matrices]] describe their subsystems, and [[14.00 Many-Particle Systems and Identical Particles|exchange symmetry]] restricts the states of identical particles. These ideas lead to entanglement. Two subsystems can have a pure joint state that cannot be written as a product of two individual states. Each subsystem then has a mixed reduced density matrix. The correlations can violate Bell inequalities, as experiments show, which rules out local hidden-variable models under the assumptions of those inequalities.

This chapter defines separable and entangled states, discusses the EPR paradox and its reformulation, and derives the CHSH inequality and its quantum value for the singlet. It then introduces the no-cloning theorem, qubits, elementary quantum gates, quantum teleportation, and superdense coding.

## Chapter summary

- A pure bipartite state is separable if it factors into a product. The reduced state of either subsystem of a pure entangled state is mixed.
- The four Bell states $\lvert\signal{\Phi}^\pm\rangle$ and $\lvert\signal{\Psi}^\pm\rangle$ form an orthonormal basis of maximally entangled two-qubit states.
- EPR identified a conflict between local realism, completeness, and quantum mechanics. Bell inequalities turn the conflict into quantitative predictions.
- Local hidden-variable models obey $\lvert S\rvert\le2$ for CHSH. Quantum mechanics attains $\lvert S\rvert=2\sqrt2$ on a singlet, and experiments that close the main loopholes confirm a violation.
- The no-cloning theorem rules out a universal operation that makes a perfect copy of an unknown quantum state.
- The qubit, Bloch sphere, and elementary gates give a language for quantum circuits. Hadamard, phase $T$, and CNOT form a universal set.
- Teleportation and superdense coding use shared entanglement to transfer quantum states and classical bits. Both obey the no-cloning and no-signalling constraints.
