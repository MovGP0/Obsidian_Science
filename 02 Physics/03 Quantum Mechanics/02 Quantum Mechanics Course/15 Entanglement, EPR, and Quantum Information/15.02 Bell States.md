---
title: "Bell States"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The four Bell states form an orthonormal basis for two qubits. Each is maximally entangled and has maximally mixed reduced states.

The vectors

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert\signal{\Phi}^{\pm}\rangle&=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(\lvert00\rangle\pm\lvert11\rangle),\\
\lvert\signal{\Psi}^{\pm}\rangle&=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(\lvert01\rangle\pm\lvert10\rangle)
\end{aligned}
\tag{15.1}
$$

form the **Bell basis**, an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2$. For example, the partial trace over $B$ gives

$$
\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B(\lvert\signal{\Phi}^+\rangle\langle\signal{\Phi}^+\rvert)=\frac12(\lvert0\rangle\langle0\rvert+\lvert1\rangle\langle1\rvert)=\frac{\hat I}{2}.
$$

The same result applies to both reduced states for each Bell state. Each qubit is locally maximally mixed. The information that distinguishes the Bell states is in the correlations between the qubits.

> [!theorem] Proposition 15.1 · Common eigenstates
> The Bell states are simultaneous eigenstates of $\hat\sigma_x\otimes\hat\sigma_x$, $\hat\sigma_y\otimes\hat\sigma_y$, and $\hat\sigma_z\otimes\hat\sigma_z$. The signs of their eigenvalues identify the states.

In particular, the spin singlet satisfies

$$
(\hat\sigma_j\otimes\hat\sigma_j)\lvert\signal{\Psi}^-\rangle=-\lvert\signal{\Psi}^-\rangle,\qquad j=x,y,z.
$$

The singlet is invariant under a common $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ rotation of both spins. This makes it useful for studying spin correlations along arbitrary directions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\lvert\signal{\Phi}^\pm\rangle,\lvert\signal{\Psi}^\pm\rangle$ | $1$ | Bell states | The four normalized two-qubit states. |
| $\rho_A$ | $1$ | Reduced density operator | The state of qubit $A$ after a partial trace over $B$. |
| $\hat\sigma_j$ | $1$ | Pauli operator | The spin operator along axis $j$ in units of $\mechanical{\hbar}/2$. |
