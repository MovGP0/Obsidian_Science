---
title: "Bell and CHSH Inequalities"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Local hidden-variable models bound the CHSH combination by $2$. Quantum measurements on a singlet can give $2\sqrt2$.

In 1964, Bell showed that local hidden-variable models and quantum mechanics give quantitatively different predictions. The version due to Clauser, Horne, Shimony, and Holt (CHSH) uses two possible measurement settings at each detector.

![[quantum-mechanics-fig-15-1-bell-chsh.svg|**Fig. 15.1** Bell–CHSH setup. A central source prepares singlet pairs. The two photons or spins travel in opposite directions to the spacelike separated detectors of Alice and Bob. Alice chooses between axes $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf a'$, and Bob chooses between $\mathbf b$ and $\mathbf b'$. Each records an outcome of $\pm1$. The four correlations $E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)$, $E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b')$, $E(\mathbf a',\mathbf b)$, and $E(\mathbf a',\mathbf b')$ enter the CHSH combination $S$.]]

Suppose a hidden variable $\lambda$ with probability density $\rho(\lambda)$ determines the outcomes for both detectors. In a local model, each outcome belongs to $\{-1,+1\}$ and depends on the detector's own setting and $\lambda$. The four functions are $A(\mathbf a,\lambda)$, $A(\mathbf a',\lambda)$, $B(\mathbf b,\lambda)$, and $B(\mathbf b',\lambda)$. The correlation is

$$
E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=\int \rho(\lambda)A(\mathbf a,\lambda)B(\mathbf b,\lambda)\,\mathrm d\lambda.
$$

Define the CHSH combination

$$
S=E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)-E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b')+E(\mathbf a',\mathbf b)+E(\mathbf a',\mathbf b').
\tag{15.2}
$$

For each $\lambda$, the integrand apart from $\rho(\lambda)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&A(\mathbf a,\lambda)[B(\mathbf b,\lambda)-B(\mathbf b',\lambda)]\\
&\quad+A(\mathbf a',\lambda)[B(\mathbf b,\lambda)+B(\mathbf b',\lambda)].
\end{aligned}
$$

Exactly one square bracket is zero. The other equals $\pm2$. Since $\rho(\lambda)\ge0$ and $\int\rho(\lambda)\,\mathrm d\lambda=1$, the weighted average obeys the **CHSH inequality**.

$$
\lvert S\rvert\le2 \qquad\text{(local hidden-variable model).}
$$

For the quantum spin singlet, the measurements of $\mathbf a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ and $\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ give

$$
E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=\langle\signal{\Psi}^-\rvert(\mathbf a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\otimes(\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\lvert\signal{\Psi}^-\rangle=-\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b.
$$

This follows from rotational invariance of the singlet and the Pauli identity $(\mathbf a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)=(\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)\hat I+i(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$. Choose coplanar axes at angles $0,\pi/4,\pi/2,3\pi/4$ for $\mathbf a,\mathbf b,\mathbf a',\mathbf b'$ respectively. Then

$$
S_{\mathrm{qm}}=-\cos(\pi/4)+\cos(3\pi/4)-\cos(\pi/4)-\cos(\pi/4)=-2\sqrt2.
$$

The magnitude $\lvert S_{\mathrm{qm}}\rvert=2\sqrt2$ reaches the **Tsirelson bound** and exceeds the local bound by a factor of $\sqrt2$. This violates the CHSH inequality for local hidden-variable models.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathbf a,\mathbf a',\mathbf b,\mathbf b'$ | $1$ | Detector axes | Unit vectors for the two settings at each detector. |
| $A,B$ | $1$ | Measurement outcomes | The values $-1$ and $+1$ returned by the detectors. |
| $E,S$ | $1$ | Correlation and CHSH combination | The mean product of two outcomes and the signed sum of four such means. |
| $\lambda,\rho(\lambda)$ | Depends on model | Hidden variable and its probability density | The variable that fixes the local outcomes and the normalized density over its possible values. |
