---
title: "Spin–Orbit Coupling"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Spin–orbit coupling is diagonal in the basis of total angular momentum. It splits a given orbital level into the allowed $j$ levels.

For one electron in an atom, $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}}=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}+\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}$. With $s=1/2$, the triangle rule gives $j=\ell+1/2$ and $j=\ell-1/2$ for $\ell\ge1$. For $\ell=0$, only $j=1/2$ occurs. A $p$ orbital has $\ell=1$, so there are four states with $j=3/2$ and two with $j=1/2$. This agrees with $(2\ell+1)(2s+1)=6$.

The spin–orbit term in the Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{\hat H}_{\mathrm{SO}}=\xi(r)\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}},\qquad
\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}
=\frac12\left(\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}}^{\,2}-\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^{\,2}-\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^{\,2}\right).
$$

The angular operator is diagonal in the coupled basis. For the orbital with quantum numbers $n,\ell$, the level shift is

$$
\Delta\energy E_j^{\mathrm{SO}}
=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}}2[j(j+1)-\ell(\ell+1)-s(s+1)]
\langle\xi(r)\rangle_{n\ell}.
\tag{11.10}
$$

The signed difference between $j=\ell+1/2$ and $j=\ell-1/2$ is $(2\ell+1)\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}\langle\xi\rangle_{n\ell}/2$. This produces, for example, the $2p_{1/2}$ and $2p_{3/2}$ sublevels of [[12.00 Hydrogen Atom|hydrogen]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}},\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}},\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Angular momenta | The total, orbital, and spin operators. |
| $\energy{\hat H}_{\mathrm{SO}},\Delta\energy E_j^{\mathrm{SO}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Spin–orbit energy | The interaction Hamiltonian and its level shift. |
| $\xi(r)$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ | Spin–orbit coupling coefficient | The radial factor multiplying the angular momentum dot product. |
| $r$ | $\unit m$ | Radius | The electron distance from the center. |
| $n,\ell,s,j$ | $1$ | Quantum numbers | The principal, orbital, spin, and total angular momentum labels. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The scale in angular momentum eigenvalues. |
