---
title: "Electric Dipole Selection Rules"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An electric dipole is a rank-one tensor and is odd under parity. Its transitions obey angular momentum selection rules and change parity.

In the dipole approximation, the electromagnetic interaction of an atom contains the electron dipole operator $\electric{\hat{\mathbf d}}=-\electric e\hat{\mathbf r}$. Its spherical components are a rank-one tensor. Thus an electric dipole transition $\lvert njm\rangle\to\lvert n'j'm'\rangle$ can occur only when

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta j&=j'-j\in\{-1,0,+1\},\qquad j=j'=0\text{ forbidden},\\
\Delta m&=m'-m\in\{-1,0,+1\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The $0\to0$ coefficient $\langle0,0;1,q\mid0,0\rangle$ vanishes because a rank-one tensor cannot couple two angular momentum zero states. The parity of the dipole requires opposite parity for the two states. For hydrogen-like orbitals the orbital rank-one condition also requires $\lvert\Delta\ell\rvert\le1$, so the parity change leaves $\Delta\ell=\pm1$. These rules explain which lines can occur in the spectra of [[12.00 Hydrogen Atom|hydrogen]] and the alkali atoms.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{\hat{\mathbf d}}$ | $\unit{C\,m}$ | Electric dipole operator | The rank-one, odd-parity operator that couples to the electric field. |
| $\electric e$ | $\unit C$ | Elementary charge | The positive magnitude of the electron charge. |
| $\hat{\mathbf r}$ | $\unit m$ | Position operator | The electron position relative to the center. |
| $n,j,m,\ell$ | $1$ | State quantum numbers | The principal, total angular momentum, projection, and orbital labels. |
| $\Delta j,\Delta m,\Delta\ell$ | $1$ | Quantum number changes | The differences between the final and initial state labels. |
