title: "Bloch Sphere"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A pure spin-$1/2$ state is a point on the Bloch sphere. Its polar angle and azimuth determine the probabilities and relative phase of the two spin components.

Up to a global phase, any pure state in $\mathbb C^2$ can be written as

$$
\lvert\signal{\psi}\rangle
=\cos\left(\frac\theta2\right)\lvert+\rangle
+e^{i\phi}\sin\left(\frac\theta2\right)\lvert-\rangle,
$$

where $\theta\in[0,\pi]$ and $\phi\in[0,2\pi)$. These angles specify the point on the **Bloch sphere** in Figure 10.1. The north pole is $\lvert+\rangle$, the south pole is $\lvert-\rangle$, and the equator contains $\lvert\pm x\rangle$ and $\lvert\pm y\rangle$.

**Fig. 10.1** Bloch sphere of pure qubit states. The poles are the $\sigma_z$ eigenstates $\lvert\pm z\rangle$. The $\lvert\pm x\rangle$ and $\lvert\pm y\rangle$ states lie on the equator. The unit vector $\hat{\mathbf n}(\theta,\phi)$ represents $\lvert\signal{\psi}\rangle=\cos(\theta/2)\lvert+\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2)\lvert-\rangle$ and equals $\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rangle$. Drag to rotate the sphere.

## Geometric expectation values

Direct calculation gives

$$
\langle\sigma_x\rangle=\sin\theta\cos\phi,\qquad
\langle\sigma_y\rangle=\sin\theta\sin\phi,\qquad
\langle\sigma_z\rangle=\cos\theta.
$$

Thus $\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rangle$ is the Bloch vector. For a pure state it has length one and lies on the surface. For a mixed state it has length less than one and lies inside the sphere.

## Spinor rotations

The rotation operator for angle $\alpha$ about $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat R_{\hat{\mathbf n}}(\alpha)
&=e^{-i\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\alpha/\mechanical{\hbar}}
=e^{-i\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}\alpha/2}\\
&=\cos(\alpha/2)\hat I-i\sin(\alpha/2)\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The last equality follows from $(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n})^2=\hat I$.

## Double covering of SO(3) by SU(2)

For a rotation by $2\pi$, the spinor rotation operator is $-\hat I$. A rotation by $4\pi$ returns the spinor to its original value. This is the double covering $\mathrm{SU}(2)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)$. Spinors distinguish these rotations, while integer angular momentum representations such as scalars, vectors, and ordinary tensors return to their initial value after $2\pi$.

A neutron interferometer can detect the relative sign. Rotate the spin by $2\pi$ in only one arm, then recombine the beams. The interference term reverses sign relative to the experiment without the rotation. The source cites the 1975 measurements of Rauch and colleagues and Werner and colleagues.

## Physical meaning

The Bloch sphere gives a geometric description of a qubit. During unitary single-qubit evolution, a pure state moves along a path on the sphere. Each single-qubit unitary gate acts as a rotation up to a global phase.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\lvert\signal{\psi}\rangle$ | $1$ | Qubit state | The normalized spinor represented on the sphere. |
| $\theta,\phi,\alpha$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Angles | The polar angle, azimuth, and rotation angle. |
| $\hat{\mathbf n},\langle\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rangle$ | $1$ | Direction and Bloch vector | The rotation axis or pure-state direction, and the Pauli expectation vector. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Spin operator | The vector of intrinsic angular momentum components. |
| $\hat R_{\hat{\mathbf n}}$ | $1$ | Rotation operator | The unitary operator on the spinor. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale in the rotation exponent. |
