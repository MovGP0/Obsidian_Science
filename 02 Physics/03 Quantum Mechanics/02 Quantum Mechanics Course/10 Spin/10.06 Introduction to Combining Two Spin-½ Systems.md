---
title: "Introduction to Combining Two Spin-½ Systems"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Two spin-$1/2$ particles combine into a spin-one triplet and a spin-zero singlet. The singlet is maximally entangled.

The two-particle spin space is $\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2\cong\mathbb C^4$. A product basis is $\{\lvert++\rangle,\lvert+-\rangle,\lvert-+\rangle,\lvert--\rangle\}$. The total spin is $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^{(1)}+\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^{(2)}$. Its components satisfy the same angular momentum commutators as for one particle.

The eigenvalues of $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^{\,2}$ are $\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}s(s+1)$ for $s=1$ and $s=0$. The **triplet** has $s=1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lvert1,1\rangle&=\lvert++\rangle,\\
\lvert1,0\rangle&=\frac{\lvert+-\rangle+\lvert-+\rangle}{\sqrt2},\\
\lvert1,-1\rangle&=\lvert--\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

The **singlet** has $s=0$.

$$
\lvert0,0\rangle=\frac{\lvert+-\rangle-\lvert-+\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

The decomposition $4=3+1$ into the $s=1$ and $s=0$ spaces is a special case of the [[11.00 Addition of Angular Momentum|Clebsch–Gordan rule]]. For spins $j_1$ and $j_2$, the tensor product decomposes into the representations from $\lvert j_1-j_2\rvert$ to $j_1+j_2$ in unit steps.

The singlet cannot be expressed as a product of two single-spin states. It is maximally entangled. See [[15.00 Entanglement, EPR, and Quantum Information]] for its measurement correlations.
