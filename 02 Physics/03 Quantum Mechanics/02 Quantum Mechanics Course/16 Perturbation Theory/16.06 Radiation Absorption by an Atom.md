---
title: "Radiation Absorption by an Atom"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Electric dipole matrix elements determine which atomic transitions couple to radiation and set the strengths of absorption and spontaneous emission.

Consider a bound atom in a monochromatic electromagnetic field of angular frequency $\omega$. The electric dipole approximation gives

$$
\energy{\hat V}(t)=-\electric{\mathbf d}\cdot\electric{\mathbf E}(t),
$$

where $\electric{\mathbf d}$ is the electric dipole operator. Let $\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi}=\langle f\rvert\electric{\mathbf d}\lvert i\rangle$. For isotropic, unpolarized radiation with intensity $I(\omega)$ per unit angular frequency, the resonant absorption rate is

$$
\Gamma_{i\to f}
=\frac{\pi}{3\electric{\varepsilon}_0 c\mechanical{\hbar}^2}
I(\omega_{fi})\lvert\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi}\rvert^2.
$$

The orbital selection rules for electric dipole transitions are $\Delta\ell=\pm1$ and $\Delta m=0,\pm1$. The position operator is a vector with odd parity, so the dipole matrix element connects orbital states with the required change in angular momentum.

> [!note] Dipole sign in the source
> The source writes $\electric{\mathbf d}=\electric{e}\mathbf r$. The electron dipole is $-\electric{e}\mathbf r$ when $\electric{e}$ denotes the positive elementary charge, as in the Stark example. The rates below depend on the squared magnitude.

Quantizing the field also gives the spontaneous emission rate from an excited level $f$ to a lower level $i$.

$$
A_{f\to i}=\frac{\omega_{fi}^3}{3\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\mechanical{\hbar}c^3}
\lvert\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi}\rvert^2.
$$

For the hydrogen transition $2p\to1s$, the rate is $A\simeq\quantity{6.3\times10^8}{s^{-1}}$ and the lifetime is $\tau\simeq\quantity{1.6\times10^{-9}}{s}$. A nanosecond is a typical scale for an allowed electric dipole decay.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{\mathbf d},\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi}$ | $\unit{C\,m}$ | Dipole operator and matrix element | The electric dipole and its transition matrix element. |
| $\electric{\mathbf E},\electric{\varepsilon}_0$ | $\unit{V\,m^{-1}},\unit{F\,m^{-1}}$ | Electric field and vacuum permittivity | The applied field and the vacuum constant in the rates. |
| $I(\omega)$ | $\unit{W\,m^{-2}\,(rad\,s^{-1})^{-1}}$ | Spectral intensity | The radiation intensity per unit angular frequency. |
| $\Gamma_{i\to f},A_{f\to i}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Transition rates | The absorption rate and the spontaneous emission rate. |
| $\tau$ | $\unit{s}$ | Lifetime | The reciprocal of the total spontaneous decay rate. |
| $c,\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{m\,s^{-1}},\unit{J\,s}$ | Speed of light and reduced Planck constant | The vacuum light speed and quantum action scale. |
