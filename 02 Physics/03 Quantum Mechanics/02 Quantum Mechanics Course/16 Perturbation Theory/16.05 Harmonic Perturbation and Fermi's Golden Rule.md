---
title: "Harmonic Perturbation and Fermi's Golden Rule"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A harmonic perturbation drives transitions near resonance. Into a continuum, Fermi's golden rule gives a rate proportional to the squared matrix element and the density of final states.

Let $\energy{\hat V}(t)=2\energy{\hat V}_0\cos(\omega t)=\energy{\hat V}_0(e^{i\omega t}+e^{-i\omega t})$, with Hermitian $\energy{\hat V}_0$. Integration gives the first-order amplitude

$$
c_f^{(1)}(t)=\frac{\energy{V}_{0,fi}}{i\mechanical{\hbar}}
\left[
\frac{e^{i(\omega_{fi}+\omega)t}-1}{i(\omega_{fi}+\omega)}
+\frac{e^{i(\omega_{fi}-\omega)t}-1}{i(\omega_{fi}-\omega)}
\right].
$$

The first term is resonant near $\omega=-\omega_{fi}$ for $\energy{E}_f<\energy{E}_i$, which gives stimulated emission. The second is resonant near $\omega=\omega_{fi}$ for $\energy{E}_f>\energy{E}_i$, which gives absorption. Near the absorption resonance,

$$
\lvert c_f^{(1)}(t)\rvert^2\simeq\frac{\lvert\energy{V}_{0,fi}\rvert^2}{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}
\frac{\sin^2[(\omega_{fi}-\omega)t/2]}{[(\omega_{fi}-\omega)/2]^2}.
$$

For large $t$, $\sin^2(xt/2)/x^2$ approaches $(\pi t/2)\delta(x)$ in the distributional sense. The absorption rate per final state is

$$
\Gamma_{i\to f}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{P_{i\to f}(t)}{t}
=\frac{2\pi}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert\energy{V}_{0,fi}\rvert^2
\delta(\energy{E}_f-\energy{E}_i-\mechanical{\hbar}\omega).
$$

This is **Fermi's golden rule**. For a continuum with density $\rho(\energy{E}_f)$, integrate over the final energies. With the matrix element and density evaluated at $\energy{E}_f=\energy{E}_i+\mechanical{\hbar}\omega$,

$$
\Gamma_{i\to f}=\frac{2\pi}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert\energy{V}_{0,fi}\rvert^2\rho(\energy{E}_f).
$$

The density gives the number of available states per unit energy. The squared matrix element measures the strength of the coupling to those states. The rate is constant in the golden-rule approximation, so the transition probability grows linearly while $P_{i\to f}\ll1$.

> [!note] Time dependence in the source
> The source calls the rate linear in time, then describes linear growth of the probability. The probability is linear in this regime and its rate is constant.

The same form appears in calculations of radioactive decay, scattering rates, atomic absorption spectra, photoemission, and tunneling through barriers.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\hat V}_0,\energy{V}_{0,fi}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Perturbation coefficient and matrix element | The operator coefficient of each Fourier component and its entry between $i$ and $f$. |
| $\omega,\omega_{fi}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Drive and transition frequencies | The angular frequency of the drive and the signed gap divided by $\mechanical{\hbar}$. |
| $\Gamma_{i\to f}$ | $\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Transition rate | The probability per unit time in the golden-rule regime. |
| $\rho(\energy{E}_f)$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Density of final states | The number of final states per unit energy. |
