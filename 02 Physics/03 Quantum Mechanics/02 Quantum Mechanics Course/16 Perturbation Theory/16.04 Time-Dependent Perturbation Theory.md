---
title: "Time-Dependent Perturbation Theory"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In first order, integrate the transition matrix element against the phase set by the energy gap. The squared magnitude gives the transition probability to leading order.

Let $\energy{\hat H}(t)=\energy{\hat H}_0+\energy{\hat V}(t)$ with a small perturbation $\energy{\hat V}(t)$. The initial state is $\lvert\signal{\psi}(0)\rangle=\lvert i\rangle$, an eigenstate of $\energy{\hat H}_0$. We want the probability of finding the system in $\lvert f\rangle$ at time $t$ for $f\ne i$.

Expand in the unperturbed energy basis.

$$
\lvert\signal{\psi}(t)\rangle=\sum_n c_n(t)e^{-i\energy{E}_n t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\lvert n\rangle.
$$

The Schrödinger equation gives

$$
i\mechanical{\hbar}\dot c_m(t)=\sum_n\energy{V}_{mn}(t)e^{i\omega_{mn}t}c_n(t),
$$

where $\energy{V}_{mn}(t)=\langle m\rvert\energy{\hat V}(t)\lvert n\rangle$ and $\omega_{mn}=(\energy{E}_m-\energy{E}_n)/\mechanical{\hbar}$.

## First order

Write $c_n=c_n^{(0)}+c_n^{(1)}+\cdots$ with $c_i^{(0)}=1$ and all other zeroth-order coefficients zero. Then

$$
\dot c_f^{(1)}(t)=\frac{1}{i\mechanical{\hbar}}\energy{V}_{fi}(t)e^{i\omega_{fi}t}.
$$

Since $c_f^{(1)}(0)=0$,

$$
c_f^{(1)}(t)=\frac{1}{i\mechanical{\hbar}}\int_0^t\energy{V}_{fi}(t')e^{i\omega_{fi}t'}\,\mathrm dt'.
$$

The leading transition probability is $P_{i\to f}(t)\simeq\lvert c_f^{(1)}(t)\rvert^2$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\hat H}(t),\energy{\hat V}(t),\energy{V}_{mn}(t)$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian, perturbation, and matrix element | The full operator, its time-dependent correction, and the correction between unperturbed levels. |
| $\energy{E}_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Unperturbed energy | The eigenvalue of $\energy{\hat H}_0$ for state $n$. |
| $\omega_{mn}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Transition angular frequency | The signed unperturbed energy difference divided by $\mechanical{\hbar}$. |
| $c_n,P_{i\to f}$ | $1$ | State amplitude and transition probability | The expansion coefficient and the probability for a specified final level. |
| $t$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time | The interval since the initial state was prepared. |
