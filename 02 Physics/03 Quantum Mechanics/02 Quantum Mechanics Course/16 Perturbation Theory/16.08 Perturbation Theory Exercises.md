---
title: "Perturbation Theory Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Calculate stationary energy shifts and driven transitions with the applicable approximation.

1. For an oscillator with perturbation $\energy{\hat V}=\lambda\hat x^2$, calculate the first-order energy shift.  
   **Result.** $\Delta\energy{E}_n^{(1)}=\lambda\langle n\rvert\hat x^2\lvert n\rangle=\lambda\mechanical{\hbar}(n+1/2)/(\mechanical{m}\omega)$.
2. For an infinite well of width $L$ with perturbation $\energy{\hat V}=\lambda\delta(x-L/2)$, calculate the first-order energy shift.  
   **Result.** $\Delta\energy{E}_n^{(1)}=\lambda\lvert\signal{\psi}_n(L/2)\rvert^2=(2\lambda/L)\sin^2(n\pi/2)$. The shift vanishes for even $n$.
3. Verify the sign of the second-order ground-state energy correction.  
   **Source condition.** The source asks for $\energy{E}_0^{(2)}<0$. The inequality is strict when there is nonzero coupling to an excited state, as in [[16.01 Time-Independent Perturbation Theory – Nondegenerate Case|the derivation]].
4. Use $\lvert\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi}\rvert^2\sim(\electric{e}a_0)^2$ and the spontaneous emission formula to estimate the hydrogen $2p\to1s$ decay rate. The reference value is $A\simeq\quantity{6.3\times10^8}{s^{-1}}$.
5. Evaluate $\langle2s\rvert\hat z\lvert2p_0\rangle$ explicitly for the hydrogen $n=2$ level. Verify that it equals $-3a_0$ and derive the Stark splitting.
6. **Normal Zeeman effect.** Add $\energy{\hat V}=(\magnetic{\mu}_B\magnetic B/\mechanical{\hbar})\mechanical{\hat L}_z$ to a hydrogen-like atom and calculate the splitting of the $2p$ level.  
   **Result.** Three levels with $\Delta\energy E=m\magnetic{\mu}_B\magnetic B$ for $m=-1,0,+1$.
7. Apply a full Gaussian pulse $\energy{\hat V}(t)=\energy{\hat V}_0e^{-t^2/\tau^2}$ to a two-level system initially in $\lvert0\rangle$ at $t\to-\infty$. Calculate $P_{0\to1}(\infty)$.  
   **Result.** $P=\lvert\energy{V}_{0,10}\rvert^2\pi\tau^2e^{-\omega_{10}^2\tau^2/2}/\mechanical{\hbar}^2$.
8. Switch on the constant perturbation $\energy{\hat V}(t)=\energy{\hat V}_0$ at $t=0$. Calculate $P_{i\to f}(t)$ from the first-order amplitude and show that it oscillates with period $2\pi/\lvert\omega_{fi}\rvert$.  
   **Result.** $P(t)=4\lvert\energy{V}_{0,fi}\rvert^2\sin^2(\omega_{fi}t/2)/(\mechanical{\hbar}^2\omega_{fi}^2)$ for $\omega_{fi}\ne0$.
9. Derive the resonant Rabi oscillation formula from the two-level Schrödinger equation in the rotating-wave approximation.
10. A one-dimensional oscillator starts in its ground state. At $t=0$, shift the center from $x=0$ to $x=a$. Calculate the probability of finding it in the new ground state.  
    **Result.** $P=e^{-a^2/(2x_0^2)}$ with $x_0=\sqrt{\mechanical{\hbar}/(\mechanical{m}\omega)}$. The coherent-state displacement has magnitude $\lvert\alpha\rvert=\lvert a\rvert/(x_0\sqrt2)$.
11. **Quadratic Stark effect.** For a nondegenerate level such as the hydrogen ground state, the Stark shift is quadratic in $\electric E$. Calculate the second-order energy correction for $\lvert100\rangle$ by estimating the sum over the $np_0$ states.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\lambda$ | $\unit{J\,m^{-2}}$ or $\unit{J\,m}$ | Perturbation coefficient | The coefficient of the oscillator's quadratic term or the well's delta function, respectively. |
| $\energy{\hat V},\energy{\hat V}_0,\Delta\energy E$ | $\unit{J}$ | Perturbations and energy shift | The correction operators and the change in a level. |
| $\magnetic{\mu}_B,\magnetic B,\mechanical{\hat L}_z$ | $\unit{J\,T^{-1}},\unit{T},\unit{J\,s}$ | Zeeman quantities | The Bohr magneton, field, and orbital angular momentum component. |
| $a,a_0,x_0,L$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lengths | The oscillator displacement, Bohr radius, oscillator length, and well width. |
| $\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi},\electric E$ | $\unit{C\,m},\unit{V\,m^{-1}}$ | Dipole matrix element and electric field | The transition dipole and applied Stark field. |
| $t,\tau$ | $\unit{s}$ | Time and pulse width | Elapsed time and the scale of the Gaussian pulse. |
| $\omega,\omega_{fi}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequencies | The oscillator frequency and the signed transition frequency. |
| $P,A$ | $1,\unit{s^{-1}}$ | Transition probability and decay rate | The probability of occupying the specified state and the spontaneous decay rate. |
