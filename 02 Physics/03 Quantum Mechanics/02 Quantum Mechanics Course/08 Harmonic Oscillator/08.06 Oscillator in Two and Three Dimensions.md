---
title: "Oscillator in Two and Three Dimensions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> An isotropic oscillator in several dimensions separates into independent one-dimensional oscillators.

For the three-dimensional isotropic oscillator,

$$
\energy{\hat H}
=\frac{\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical m}
+\frac12\mechanical m\mechanical{\omega}^{\,2}\hat{\mathbf r}^{\,2}
=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\energy{\hat H}_i.
$$

The three Cartesian oscillators are independent. The eigenstates are $\lvert n_1,n_2,n_3\rangle=\lvert n_1\rangle\otimes\lvert n_2\rangle\otimes\lvert n_3\rangle$, with

$$
\energy E=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}\left(n_1+n_2+n_3+\frac32\right).
$$

For fixed $n=n_1+n_2+n_3$, there are $(n+1)(n+2)/2$ ordered triples of nonnegative integers, giving the degeneracy of the level. In two dimensions, the same separation gives $\energy E=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}(n_1+n_2+1)$ with $n+1$ states at fixed $n$.

In spherical coordinates the three-dimensional basis is $\lvert n_r,\ell,m\rangle$, with $\energy E=\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\omega}(2n_r+\ell+3/2)$. It gives the same degeneracy as the Cartesian basis. This illustrates that a system can have more than one [[03.02 Complete Set of Commuting Observables (CSCO)|complete set of commuting observables]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\hat H},\energy{\hat H}_i,\energy E$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonians and energy | The total Hamiltonian, each Cartesian term, and the energy eigenvalue. |
| $\hat{\mathbf r}$ | $\unit m$ | Position operator | The vector of Cartesian position operators. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum operator | The vector of Cartesian momentum operators. |
| $n_i,n,n_r,\ell,m$ | $1$ | Quantum numbers | The Cartesian, total, radial, and angular labels of the states. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The oscillator mass. |
| $\mechanical{\omega}$ | $\unit{rad\,s^{-1}}$ | Angular frequency | The shared natural frequency of all Cartesian modes. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the level spacing. |
