> [!TLDR]
> A density operator describes both pure states and statistical mixtures. It also describes a subsystem whose environment or partner system is ignored.

A single state vector $\lvert\signal{\psi}\rangle$ describes a pure preparation. In that case the state gives the maximum information permitted by quantum mechanics, while measurement outcomes may still be uncertain. Laboratory preparations often also contain classical statistical uncertainty. For example, an atomic beam from an oven at temperature $\thermal T$ contains an ensemble of states, each with a preparation probability. In addition, coupling to an uncontrolled environment can leave the subsystem in a state that has no single state-vector description.

The **density operator** $\hat\rho$, introduced by von Neumann and Landau, generalizes the state description. This chapter develops its properties and time evolution and applies it to decoherence. Interactions with the environment can suppress observable interference in the subsystem, making a superposition behave like a classical mixture for its local measurements.

The source connects decoherence with the [[02.04 Wave Function Collapse|projection postulate]], interpreting the appearance of collapse as an effective consequence of interaction with the environment.

> [!note] Scope of the interpretation
> Decoherence suppresses local interference between alternatives. A reduced density operator can then predict the same statistics as a classical mixture. The selection of one outcome in an individual measurement is a separate part of the measurement problem.

## Chapter summary

- A preparation ensemble gives $\hat\rho=\sum_i p_i\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle\langle\signal{\psi}_i\rvert$. Hermiticity, positivity, and trace one characterize density operators.
- The expectation is $\langle\hat O\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho\hat O)$. In dimension $d$, purity $\mathcal P=\operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho^2)$ ranges from $1/d$ for the maximally mixed state to one for a pure state.
- A qubit has $\hat\rho=(\hat I+\mathbf a\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})/2$ with $\lvert\mathbf a\rvert\le1$.
- The partial trace $\hat\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B\hat\rho_{AB}$ describes local measurements on $A$. A pure entangled joint state can give a mixed local state.
- The von Neumann entropy $\thermal S(\hat\rho)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\hat\rho\log\hat\rho)$ is zero for a pure state and reaches $\log d$ for the maximally mixed state. It is subadditive.
- Unitary evolution obeys $i\mechanical{\hbar}\dot{\hat\rho}=[\energy{\hat H},\hat\rho]$ and preserves the spectrum, purity, and entropy.
- During decoherence, coupling to the environment and tracing it out suppress off-diagonal terms in the pointer basis on a time scale $\tau_d$.
- The Lindblad equation gives the general time-homogeneous completely positive Markovian generator in finite dimension. Its qubit examples give relaxation time $T_1$ and coherence time $T_2\le2T_1$.
- Decoherence explains why interference becomes inaccessible in local measurements. The interpretation of single measurement outcomes remains open.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\hat\rho,\hat\rho_A,\hat\rho_{AB}$ | $1$ | Density operators | The general, reduced, and joint states. |
| $p_i$ | $1$ | Preparation probability | The weight of the corresponding pure state. |
| $\mathcal P,\thermal S$ | $1$ | Purity and entropy | The squared trace measure and spectral entropy. |
| $\mathbf a,\boldsymbol{\sigma},d$ | $1$ | Bloch vector, Pauli matrices, and dimension | The qubit coordinates, operator basis, and state-space dimension. |
| $\hat L_k$ | $\unit{s^{-1/2}}$ in rate-normalized form | Lindblad operator | A dissipative channel operator. |
| $\energy{\hat H},\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J},\unit{J\,s}$ | Hamiltonian and reduced Planck constant | The energy operator and action scale for coherent evolution. |
| $\tau_d,T_1,T_2$ | $\unit s$ | Decoherence, relaxation, and coherence times | The characteristic time scales of the reduced dynamics. |
