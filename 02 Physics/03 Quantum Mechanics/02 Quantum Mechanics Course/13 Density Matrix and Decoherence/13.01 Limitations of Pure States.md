---
title: "Limitations of Pure States"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A coherent superposition and a statistical mixture can agree on one measurement and differ on another. Spin along $x$ distinguishes an equal coherent superposition from an equal mixture in the $z$ basis.

Consider a source of spin-$1/2$ atoms. A calibrated source can prepare $\lvert\uparrow\rangle$ with certainty. An imperfect source or a gas in thermal equilibrium may prepare $\lvert\signal{\psi}_1\rangle$ with probability $p_1$, $\lvert\signal{\psi}_2\rangle$ with probability $p_2$, and so on, with $\sum_i p_i=1$. In this preparation model, each atom is in one of the prepared states and the uncertainty about which preparation occurred is classical.

A **pure state** is specified by one state vector up to global phase. A **mixed state** can be represented by a statistical ensemble $\{p_i,\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle\}$ of distinct state rays. Compare the pure state

$$
\lvert\signal{\psi}\rangle=\frac{\lvert\uparrow\rangle+\lvert\downarrow\rangle}{\sqrt2}
$$

with the mixture having $p_\uparrow=p_\downarrow=1/2$. The pure state is an eigenstate of $\mechanical{\hat S}_x$ and gives $+\mechanical{\hbar}/2$ with certainty. In the mixture, each $\mechanical{\hat S}_x$ outcome has probability $1/2$. Both preparations give $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_z\rangle=0$.

> [!definition] Definition 13.1. Mixed state
> A statistical preparation uses an ensemble $\{p_i,\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle\}$ with $p_i\ge0$, $\sum_i p_i=1$, and normalized states. The states can be nonorthogonal. If at least two distinct state rays occur with positive probability, the ensemble describes a mixed state.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\lvert\signal{\psi}\rangle,\lvert\signal{\psi}_i\rangle$ | $1$ | Pure state vectors | The coherent spinor and the members of a preparation ensemble. |
| $p_i,p_\uparrow,p_\downarrow$ | $1$ | Preparation probabilities | The weights of the states in a statistical ensemble. |
| $\mechanical{\hat S}_x,\mechanical{\hat S}_z$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Spin components | The spin observables along the two measurement axes. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | Twice the magnitude of either spin-$1/2$ component outcome. |
