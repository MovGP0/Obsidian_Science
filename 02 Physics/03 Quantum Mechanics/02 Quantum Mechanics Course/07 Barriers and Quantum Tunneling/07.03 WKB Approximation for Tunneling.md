---
title: "WKB Approximation for Tunneling"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The WKB approximation replaces the rectangular-barrier decay exponent by an integral of the local decay constant across the forbidden region.

For a general nonrectangular barrier, use the Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin (WKB) approximation, named for the 1926 work. Treat $\mechanical{\hbar}$ as a small parameter relative to the action scale.

Write $\signal{\psi}(x)=e^{i\mechanical S(x)/\mechanical{\hbar}}$. Substitution into Eq. (6.1) of [[06.00 One-Dimensional Systems]] gives

$$
\frac{\mechanical S'^{\,2}-i\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical S''}{2\mechanical m}+\energy V=\energy E.
$$

At leading order, drop the term proportional to $\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical S''$. Then

$$
\mechanical S'(x)^2=2\mechanical m(\energy E-\energy V(x))
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mechanical S(x)=\pm\int^x\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E-\energy V(x'))}\,dx'.
$$

For $\energy E>\energy V$, the action is real and the solutions oscillate. For $\energy E<\energy V$, the action is imaginary and $\signal{\psi}\sim e^{\pm\int\signal{\kappa}(x)\,dx}$ with $\signal{\kappa}(x)=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V(x)-\energy E)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. The next order gives a prefactor proportional to $1/\sqrt{\signal{\kappa}(x)}$.

## WKB tunneling formula

For a smooth barrier with $\energy V(x)>\energy E$ between the classical turning points $x_1,x_2$, where $\energy V(x_1)=\energy V(x_2)=\energy E$, the leading transmission is

$$
\signal T\simeq e^{-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\signal{\kappa}(x)\,dx},\qquad
\signal{\kappa}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V(x)-\energy E)}}{\mechanical{\hbar}}.
$$

This **Gamow formula** generalizes $e^{-2\signal{\kappa}a}$. Near a simple turning point, use Airy functions to match the decaying solution to the oscillatory solution. The matching gives factors of order unity at leading exponential accuracy.

## Example. Triangular barrier

Take $\energy V(x)=\energy V_0-\mechanical F x$ for $0<x<\energy V_0/\mechanical F$ and $\energy E=0$. The forbidden segment runs from the entrance at $x_1=0$ to the turning point at $x_2=\energy V_0/\mechanical F$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^{\energy V_0/\mechanical F}
\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V_0-\mechanical F x)}\,\frac{dx}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
&=\frac{1}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
\left[-\frac{[2\mechanical m(\energy V_0-\mechanical F x)]^{3/2}}{3\mechanical m\mechanical F}\right]_0^{\energy V_0/\mechanical F}\\
&=\frac{2\sqrt{2\mechanical m}\,\energy V_0^{\,3/2}}{3\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical F}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $\signal T\sim e^{-4\sqrt{2\mechanical m}\,\energy V_0^{\,3/2}/(3\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical F)}$. This is the leading exponential dependence for cold field emission from a metal in a strong electric field, known as Fowler–Nordheim emission.

> [!note] Entrance of the triangular barrier
> The source calls $x=0$ a turning point. In the specified interior profile, $\energy V(0^+)=\energy V_0>0=\energy E$. The integral starts at the entrance, and only the far end satisfies $\energy V=\energy E$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\psi}$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ with position normalization | Position wave function | The amplitude in the WKB ansatz. |
| $\mechanical S,\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Action and reduced Planck constant | Their ratio gives the WKB exponent. |
| $\energy E,\energy V,\energy V_0$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The particle energy, barrier profile, and triangular barrier height. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Local decay constant | The decay rate in the forbidden region. |
| $x,x_1,x_2,a$ | $\unit m$ | Positions and width | The coordinate, forbidden-region endpoints, and rectangular-barrier width. |
| $\signal T$ | $1$ | Transmission coefficient | The transmitted fraction of incident probability current. |
| $\mechanical F$ | $\unit{N}$ | Potential slope magnitude | The rate at which the triangular barrier decreases with distance. |
