> [!TLDR]
> Tunneling helps determine stellar fusion rates and allows charge carriers to cross thin junctions in tunnel diodes.

## Stellar fusion

In the Sun, the temperature is of order $\thermal T\sim10^7\,\unit K$. The thermal energy $\thermal{k_B}\thermal T$ is of order $\unit{keV}$, far below the typical Coulomb barrier scale of order $\unit{MeV}$ between two protons. Fusion can still occur through tunneling. The product of the Maxwell–Boltzmann factor and the tunneling probability peaks at an energy well above $\thermal{k_B}\thermal T$. This **Gamow peak** gives the energy range that contributes most to the fusion rate.

## Tunnel diodes

In these semiconductor junctions, the conduction and valence bands overlap in energy. Charge carriers tunnel through a thin depletion region from one band to the other. Part of the current–voltage curve has negative differential resistance, which is useful in high-frequency electronics.

## Josephson junctions

A thin insulating barrier separates two superconductors. Cooper pairs tunnel across the barrier while maintaining phase coherence. This is the basis of SQUID magnetometers and superconducting qubits such as transmons.

## Spontaneous vacuum decay

In field theories with a false vacuum, the transition to a lower-energy vacuum can occur by tunneling. Coleman instantons describe this quantum version of bubble nucleation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\thermal T$ | $\unit K$ | Temperature | The temperature used in the stellar fusion example. |
| $\thermal{k_B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion factor from temperature to thermal energy. |
| $\thermal{k_B}\thermal T$ | $\unit{J}$ | Thermal energy scale | The energy scale of the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution. |
| $\electric I,\electric V$ | $\unit A,\unit V$ | Current and voltage | The quantities in the tunnel-diode characteristic. |
