---
title: "Barriers and Quantum Tunneling Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Use exact and approximate transmission formulas to analyze barriers and decay.

1. Calculate $\signal T$ for an electron of energy $\energy E=1\,\unit{eV}$ incident on a barrier of height $\energy V_0=5\,\unit{eV}$ and width $a=0.5\,\unit{nm}$.  
   **Result.** $\signal{\kappa}a\simeq5.13$ and $\signal T\simeq9\times10^{-5}$.
2. An electron of energy $\energy E=1\,\unit{eV}$ hits a barrier with $\energy V_0=2\,\unit{eV}$ and $a=1\,\unit{nm}$. Calculate $\signal{\kappa}$ and $\signal T$.  
   **Result.** $\signal{\kappa}\simeq5.13\times10^9\,\unit{m^{-1}}$ and $\signal T\simeq1.4\times10^{-4}$.
3. Show that $\signal T=1$ when $\energy E>\energy V_0$ and $\signal k'a=n\pi$ for positive integer $n$. Interpret the condition in terms of a standing wave.
4. For a scanning tunneling microscope with work function $\energy{\phi}=4\,\unit{eV}$, by what factor does $\signal T$ change when the tip–sample gap increases by $\Delta a=0.1\,\unit{nm}$?  
   **Result.** $e^{-2\signal{\kappa}\Delta a}\simeq e^{-2}\simeq0.135$.
5. Calculate the exact transmission of a rectangular barrier for $\energy V_0=2\energy E$ and $\signal{\kappa}a=1$.  
   **Result.** $\signal T=1/\cosh^2(1)\simeq0.420$.
6. Use WKB to calculate $\signal T$ for $\energy V(x)=\energy V_0(1-x/a)$ on $0<x<a$, with $\energy E=0$.  
   **Result.** $\signal T\sim\exp[-4\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy V_0}\,a/(3\mechanical{\hbar})]$.
7. Estimate the lifetime $\tau$ for alpha decay of uranium-238 with $Z'=2\cdot90$, $\energy E_\alpha=4.27\,\unit{MeV}$, $R=9.3\,\unit{fm}$, and encounter frequency $\mechanical{\nu}=\mechanical v_\alpha/(2R)$.  
   **Source result.** $\tau\sim3\times10^{17}\,\unit s\sim10^{10}$ years. The source compares this with the measured half-life of about $4.5\times10^9$ years. For exponential decay, the corresponding mean lifetime is the half-life divided by $\ln 2$.
8. **Full Gamow approximation.** Use WKB and a pure Coulomb potential outside the nuclear radius to derive $\log\signal T$ for alpha decay. Show that the leading contribution scales as $-1/\sqrt{\energy E_\alpha}$.
9. **Gamow peak.** Show that the proton–proton fusion factor in a star has a maximum from the competition between the Boltzmann factor $e^{-\energy E/(\thermal{k_B}\thermal T)}$ and the tunneling factor $e^{-b/\sqrt{\energy E}}$. Find the energy $\energy E_G$ and width of the peak.
10. **Ramsauer–Townsend effect.** A slow electron scattered by a noble gas atom has a minimum in its cross section at certain energies. Explain the minimum as a transmission resonance of a well and calculate the resonance energies in that model.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal T$ | $1$ | Transmission coefficient | The fraction of incident current transmitted through the barrier. |
| $\energy V,\energy V_0,\energy E,\energy E_\alpha,\energy E_G,\energy{\phi}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The potential, barrier height, particle and alpha energies, Gamow peak energy, and work function. |
| $\signal k',\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Interior wavenumber and decay constant | The interior oscillation and decay rates. |
| $x,a,\Delta a,R$ | $\unit m$ | Lengths | The position, barrier width, change in tip–sample gap, and nuclear radius. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The mass of the particle. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the tunneling exponent. |
| $\tau,\mechanical{\nu},\mechanical v_\alpha$ | $\unit s,\unit{Hz},\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Lifetime, encounter frequency, and speed | The quantities in the alpha-decay estimate. |
| $Z'$ | $1$ | Charge number product | The product of the alpha and daughter charge numbers. |
| $\thermal{k_B},\thermal T$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}},\unit K$ | Boltzmann constant and temperature | Their product sets the thermal energy scale. |
| $b$ | $\unit{J^{1/2}}$ | Tunneling parameter | The coefficient in the Gamow exponential. |
