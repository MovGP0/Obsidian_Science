---
title: "Rectangular Potential Barrier"
---

> [!TLDR]
> At a rectangular barrier, match the wave function and its derivative at both edges. The evanescent solution inside couples the incident and transmitted waves.

Take a barrier of height $\energy V_0>0$ and width $a$.

$$
\energy V(x)=
\begin{cases}
0, & x<0,\\
\energy V_0, & 0<x<a,\\
0, & x>a.
\end{cases}
$$

For $0<\energy E<\energy V_0$, the exterior wavenumber is $\signal k=\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ and the decay constant inside the barrier is $\signal{\kappa}=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V_0-\energy E)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. Figure 7.1 shows the three regions. Normalize the incident amplitude to one and write

$$
\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm I}=e^{i\signal kx}+\signal r e^{-i\signal kx},\qquad
\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm{II}}=\signal A e^{-\signal{\kappa}x}+\signal B e^{\signal{\kappa}x},\qquad
\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm{III}}=\signal t e^{i\signal kx}.
$$

Match $\signal{\psi}$ and $\signal{\psi}'$ at $x=0$ and $x=a$.

$$
1+\signal r=\signal A+\signal B,\qquad
i\signal k(1-\signal r)=-\signal{\kappa}(\signal A-\signal B).
\tag{7.1}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal A e^{-\signal{\kappa}a}+\signal B e^{\signal{\kappa}a}&=\signal t e^{i\signal ka},\\
-\signal{\kappa}(\signal A e^{-\signal{\kappa}a}-\signal B e^{\signal{\kappa}a})
&=i\signal k\signal t e^{i\signal ka}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.2}
$$

Solve the four equations for the transmitted amplitude.

$$
\signal t=\frac{e^{-i\signal ka}}{\cosh(\signal{\kappa}a)
+\frac i2\left(\frac{\signal{\kappa}}{\signal k}-\frac{\signal k}{\signal{\kappa}}\right)\sinh(\signal{\kappa}a)}.
$$

The wavenumber is the same on both sides, so the transmission coefficient is

$$
\signal T=\lvert\signal t\rvert^2
=\left[1+\frac{\energy V_0^{\,2}}{4\energy E(\energy V_0-\energy E)}\sinh^2(\signal{\kappa}a)\right]^{-1}.
\tag{7.3}
$$

**Fig. 7.1** Rectangular barrier of height $\energy V_0$ and width $a$ for an incident wave of energy $\energy E<\energy V_0$. Part of the incident wave reflects. The amplitude decays inside the classically forbidden region and emerges as a weaker transmitted wave. The curves show the wave amplitudes schematically, with vertical offsets. Equation (7.3) gives $\signal T=\lvert\signal t\rvert^2$.

## Thick or high barrier

For $\signal{\kappa}a\gg1$, use $\sinh(\signal{\kappa}a)\simeq e^{\signal{\kappa}a}/2$ to obtain

$$
\signal T\simeq\frac{16\energy E(\energy V_0-\energy E)}{\energy V_0^{\,2}}e^{-2\signal{\kappa}a}.
$$

The factor $e^{-2\signal{\kappa}a}$ gives the exponential dependence on the area under $\sqrt{\energy V-\energy E}$ across the barrier. The prefactor varies more slowly.

## Complete transmission

For $\energy E>\energy V_0$, substitute $\signal{\kappa}\mapsto i\signal k'$ with $\signal k'=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E-\energy V_0)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. The identity $\sinh(i\signal k'a)=i\sin(\signal k'a)$ gives

$$
\signal T=\left[1+\frac{\energy V_0^{\,2}}{4\energy E(\energy E-\energy V_0)}\sin^2(\signal k'a)\right]^{-1}.
$$

At $\signal k'a=n\pi$ for positive integers $n$, the barrier width fits an integer number of half-wavelengths. These are **transmission resonances**, with $\signal T=1$. The cancellation of the reflected wave is analogous to the vanishing reflection of an antireflection coating in optics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy V(x),\energy V_0,\energy E$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The barrier profile, barrier height, and particle energy. |
| $x,a$ | $\unit m$ | Position and barrier width | The position and separation of the two interfaces. |
| $\signal k,\signal k',\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumbers and decay constant | The exterior wavenumber and the interior oscillation or decay rate. |
| $\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm I},\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm{II}},\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm{III}}$ | $1$ with unit incident amplitude | Relative wave functions | The scattering solutions in the three regions. |
| $\signal r,\signal t,\signal A,\signal B$ | $1$ | Relative amplitudes | The reflected, transmitted, and two interior coefficients. |
| $\signal T$ | $1$ | Transmission coefficient | The transmitted fraction of the incident current. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the wave and decay rates. |
