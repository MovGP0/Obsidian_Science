---
title: "Application to Alpha Decay"
---

> [!TLDR]
> In the alpha-decay model, an alpha particle escapes a nuclear well by tunneling through the surrounding Coulomb barrier.

A heavy nucleus emits an alpha particle, a helium-4 nucleus, during decay. In the simplified model the alpha particle already exists inside the nucleus. The attractive nuclear potential confines it. Outside, the Coulomb interaction repels it. Write the potential as

$$
\energy V(r)=
\begin{cases}
-\energy V_N, & r<R,\\
C/r, & r>R.
\end{cases}
$$

Here $R\simeq1.2A^{1/3}\,\unit{fm}$ is the nuclear radius for mass number $A$. The Coulomb coefficient is $C=Z'\electric e^{\,2}/(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0)$, where $Z'=2(Z-2)$ accounts for the daughter charge $(Z-2)\electric e$ and the alpha charge $2\electric e$.

A typical alpha particle has energy $\energy E_\alpha\simeq5\,\unit{MeV}$. The barrier at the surface is much higher, $\energy V_C(R)=C/R\sim30\,\unit{MeV}$. The particle is classically confined. It escapes by tunneling.

**Fig. 7.2** Potential for alpha decay. The attractive nuclear well $\energy V=-\energy V_N$ for $r<R$ meets the repulsive Coulomb barrier $\energy V(r)=Z'\electric e^{\,2}/(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0r)$ at radius $R$. The alpha particle tunnels from $R$ to the outer turning point $r_2=Z'\electric e^{\,2}/(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\energy E_\alpha)$, where the potential equals its energy $\energy E_\alpha$.

The WKB exponent uses the integral from $R$ to $r_2=C/\energy E_\alpha$. Substitute $r=r_2\sin^2\theta$ to obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_R^{r_2}\sqrt{2\mechanical m_\alpha\left(\frac{C}{r}-\energy E_\alpha\right)}\,\frac{dr}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
&=\frac{\sqrt{2\mechanical m_\alpha\energy E_\alpha}\,r_2}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
\left[\arccos\sqrt{\frac{R}{r_2}}-\sqrt{\frac{R}{r_2}-\left(\frac{R}{r_2}\right)^2}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

For $R\ll r_2$, use $\arccos\sqrt{R/r_2}\simeq\pi/2-\sqrt{R/r_2}$. With $\mechanical v_\alpha=\sqrt{2\energy E_\alpha/\mechanical m_\alpha}$, the leading term is the Gamow–Gurney–Condon result

$$
\log\signal T\simeq-\frac{2\pi Z'\electric e^{\,2}}{4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical v_\alpha}
+\text{correction terms}.
$$

The strong dependence on $\energy E_\alpha$ explains why the lifetimes of alpha-emitting isotopes can differ by more than twenty orders of magnitude for a change in energy by only a factor of two. This is the **Geiger–Nuttall law**. The decay rate is $1/\tau\simeq\mechanical{\nu}\signal T$, or equivalently the energy width is $\energy{\Gamma}\simeq\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\nu}\signal T$. The classical frequency $\mechanical{\nu}\sim10^{21}\,\unit{Hz}$ is the rate at which the alpha particle encounters the nuclear wall.

## Physical meaning

Gamow, Gurney, and Condon gave this explanation in 1928, two years after the Schrödinger equation. The model explains how a metastable nucleus can take seconds, hours, or billions of years to decay even though the times between barrier encounters are very short. A small tunneling probability per encounter multiplied by a large encounter frequency gives the wide range of decay rates.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $r,R,r_2$ | $\unit m$ | Radial coordinates | The radial position, nuclear radius, and outer turning point. |
| $A,Z,Z'$ | $1$ | Mass and charge numbers | The parent mass and proton numbers and the product of the alpha and daughter charge numbers. |
| $\energy V,\energy V_N,\energy V_C,\energy E_\alpha$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The potential, nuclear well depth, Coulomb barrier, and alpha energy. |
| $C$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Coulomb coefficient | The numerator of the exterior potential $C/r$. |
| $\electric e$ | $\unit{C}$ | Elementary charge | The positive magnitude of the electron charge. |
| $\electric{\varepsilon}_0$ | $\unit{F\,m^{-1}}$ | Vacuum permittivity | The constant in the Coulomb coefficient. |
| $\mechanical m_\alpha,\mechanical v_\alpha$ | $\unit{kg},\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ | Alpha mass and speed | The parameters in the tunneling integral and leading exponent. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The action scale in the WKB exponent. |
| $\signal T$ | $1$ | Transmission probability | The probability of crossing the barrier on one encounter. |
| $\mechanical{\nu},\tau,\energy{\Gamma}$ | $\unit{Hz},\unit s,\unit{J}$ | Encounter frequency, lifetime, and energy width | The rate of encounters, the mean decay time, and the corresponding width. |
