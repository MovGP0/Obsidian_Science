---
title: "Hamiltonian and Separation of Center-of-Mass Motion"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The electron–proton Hamiltonian separates into a free center-of-mass term and a relative Coulomb term with the reduced mass.

The proton and electron attract through the Coulomb potential. The Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{\hat H}
=\frac{\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}_e^{\,2}}{2\mechanical m_e}
+\frac{\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}_p^{\,2}}{2\mechanical m_p}
-\frac{\electric e^{\,2}}{4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\lvert\mathbf r_e-\mathbf r_p\rvert}.
$$

Use the center-of-mass coordinate $\mathbf R=(\mechanical m_e\mathbf r_e+\mechanical m_p\mathbf r_p)/\mechanical M$ and the relative coordinate $\mathbf r=\mathbf r_e-\mathbf r_p$. With their canonical momenta $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf P}}$ and $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}$,

$$
\energy{\hat H}
=\frac{\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf P}}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical M}
+\frac{\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical{\mu}}
-\frac{\electric e^{\,2}}{4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0r}.
$$

The total mass is $\mechanical M=\mechanical m_e+\mechanical m_p$ and the reduced mass is $\mechanical{\mu}=\mechanical m_e\mechanical m_p/(\mechanical m_e+\mechanical m_p)$. Since $\mechanical m_p\simeq1836\mechanical m_e$, replacing $\mechanical{\mu}$ with $\mechanical m_e$ gives a fractional error of about $1/1836$.

The center of mass moves freely. The relative Hamiltonian is

$$
\energy{\hat H}_{\mathrm{rel}}=\frac{\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical{\mu}}
-\frac{\electric e^{\,2}}{4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0r}.
$$

This is an effective one-particle problem of mass $\mechanical{\mu}$ in the potential $-\alpha\mechanical{\hbar}\signal c/r$. The **fine-structure constant** is $\alpha=\electric e^{\,2}/(4\pi\electric{\varepsilon}_0\mechanical{\hbar}\signal c)\simeq1/137$. The following sections use $\mechanical m_e$ in place of $\mechanical{\mu}$ unless stated otherwise.
