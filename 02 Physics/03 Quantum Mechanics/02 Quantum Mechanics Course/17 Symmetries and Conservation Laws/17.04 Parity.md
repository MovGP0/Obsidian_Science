> [!TLDR]
> Parity inverts spatial coordinates. Electric dipole transitions connect states of opposite parity.

> [!definition] Definition 17.2 · Parity
> The parity operator $\hat\Pi$ acts by inversion.
>
> $$
> \hat\Pi\signal{\psi}(\mathbf r)=\signal{\psi}(-\mathbf r).
> $$
>
> It satisfies $\hat\Pi^2=\hat I$ and $\hat\Pi=\hat\Pi^\dagger$. Its eigenvalues are $\pm1$.

Parity eigenstates are **even** for $+1$ and **odd** for $-1$. A general state decomposes as $\signal{\psi}=\signal{\psi}_++\signal{\psi}_-$, with $\signal{\psi}_\pm=(\signal{\psi}\pm\hat\Pi\signal{\psi})/2$. If $\energy V(-\mathbf r)=\energy V(\mathbf r)$, then $[\hat\Pi,\energy{\hat H}]=0$. Every nondegenerate energy eigenstate then has definite parity.

## Orbital parity

The spherical harmonics satisfy

$$
\hat\Pi Y_{\ell m}=(-1)^\ell Y_{\ell m}.
$$

The radial factor is unchanged under inversion, so a hydrogen orbital $\signal{\psi}_{n\ell m}$ has parity $(-1)^\ell$. The $s$ and $d$ orbitals are even, and the $p$ and $f$ orbitals are odd.

## Parity selection rule

The position operator is odd, $\hat\Pi\hat{\mathbf r}\hat\Pi=-\hat{\mathbf r}$. For parity eigenstates $\lvert i\rangle$ and $\lvert f\rangle$, $\langle f\rvert\hat{\mathbf r}\lvert i\rangle$ can be nonzero only if their parities are opposite. This requires odd $\Delta\ell$. The vector angular momentum rule also requires $\lvert\Delta\ell\rvert\le1$, so an electric dipole transition has $\Delta\ell=\pm1$. In particular, electric dipole transitions between two $s$ orbitals or two $p$ orbitals are forbidden.

> [!note] Polar and axial vectors
> The source caption applies the sign change to vector operators in general. It applies to polar vectors such as position. Axial vectors such as orbital angular momentum are unchanged under parity.
