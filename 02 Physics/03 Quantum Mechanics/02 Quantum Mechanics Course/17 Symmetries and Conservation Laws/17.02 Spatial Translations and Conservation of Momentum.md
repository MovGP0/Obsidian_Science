---
title: "Spatial Translations and Conservation of Momentum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Momentum generates spatial translations. Translation invariance makes the corresponding component of momentum conserved.

The operator that translates a state by $\mathbf a$ acts as

$$
\hat T(\mathbf a)\signal{\psi}(\mathbf r)=\signal{\psi}(\mathbf r-\mathbf a).
$$

For an infinitesimal displacement $\boldsymbol\epsilon$, expand $\signal{\psi}(\mathbf r-\boldsymbol\epsilon)\simeq\signal{\psi}(\mathbf r)-\boldsymbol\epsilon\cdot\nabla\signal{\psi}(\mathbf r)$. Comparing with $\hat T(\boldsymbol\epsilon)=\hat I-i\boldsymbol\epsilon\cdot\hat{\mathbf G}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ identifies the generator as $\hat{\mathbf G}=-i\mechanical{\hbar}\nabla=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}$. Thus

$$
\hat T(\mathbf a)=e^{-i\mathbf a\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}/\mechanical{\hbar}}.
$$

When $\energy V(\mathbf r+\mathbf a)=\energy V(\mathbf r)$ for every $\mathbf a$, the Hamiltonian commutes with the translation group and $[\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}},\energy{\hat H}]=0$. For a free particle, $\energy{\hat H}=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}^2/(2\mechanical m)$ and every momentum component is conserved.

In a periodic crystal potential, lattice translations preserve the Hamiltonian. The conserved label is quasimomentum modulo reciprocal-lattice momentum. This discrete symmetry is the basis of electronic band theory.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathbf r,\mathbf a$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position and translation vector | The coordinate and the displacement of the state. |
| $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum operator | The generator of spatial translations. |
| $\energy V,\energy{\hat H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential energy and Hamiltonian | The position-dependent energy and full energy operator. |
