> [!TLDR]
> Match wave functions at one-dimensional boundaries to obtain discrete levels, reflection probabilities, and bound states.

Problems are in [[06.08 One-Dimensional Systems Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Infinite well levels and transition

Use $\energy E_n=n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\mechanical m_e L^2)$. With $L=\quantity{1}{nm}$, $\mechanical{\hbar}c\simeq\quantity{197}{eV\,nm}$, and $\mechanical m_e c^2=\quantity{0.511}{MeV}$,

$$
\energy E_1=\frac{(\pi\mechanical{\hbar}c)^2}{2\mechanical m_ec^2 L^2}
\simeq\frac{(\pi\cdot197)^2}{2(5.11\times10^5)}\unit{eV}
\simeq\quantity{0.376}{eV}.
$$

Thus $\energy E_2=4\energy E_1\simeq\quantity{1.50}{eV}$ and $\energy E_3=9\energy E_1\simeq\quantity{3.38}{eV}$. The $2\to1$ transition has $\Delta\energy E=3\energy E_1\simeq\quantity{1.13}{eV}$, giving $\lambda=hc/\Delta\energy E\simeq\quantity{1.10}{\mu m}$ in the near infrared.

## Exercise 2 · Step with V₀ = 3E

For $\energy E<\energy V_0$, $k=\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ and $\kappa=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V_0-\energy E)}/\mechanical{\hbar}=\sqrt2\,k$. Match the wave function and its derivative at the step. The reflection coefficient is

$$
R=\left\lvert\frac{k-i\kappa}{k+i\kappa}\right\rvert^2=1.
$$

The transmitted flux is zero. The evanescent wave still penetrates the forbidden region on the scale $1/\kappa$.

## Exercise 3 · Bound state of an attractive delta potential

For $\energy V(x)=-\alpha\delta(x)$, use the even bound state $\signal u(x)=Ae^{-\kappa\lvert x\rvert}$, with $\kappa=\sqrt{-2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. Integration across $x=0$ gives

$$
-\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m}
[\signal u'(0^+)-\signal u'(0^-)]=\alpha\signal u(0).
$$

The jump is $-2A\kappa$ and $\signal u(0)=A$. Hence $\kappa=\mechanical m\alpha/\mechanical{\hbar}^2$ and $\energy E_0=-\mechanical{\hbar}^2\kappa^2/(2\mechanical m)=-\mechanical m\alpha^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}^2)$.

## Exercise 4 · Orthonormality in the infinite well

For $\signal\psi_n(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$, the product-to-sum identity gives

$$
\int_0^L\signal\psi_m(x)\signal\psi_n(x)\,\mathrm dx
=\frac1L\int_0^L
\left[\cos\left(\frac{(m-n)\pi x}{L}\right)-\cos\left(\frac{(m+n)\pi x}{L}\right)\right]\,\mathrm dx.
$$

For $m\ne n$, both integrals vanish. For $m=n$, the first gives $L$ and the second vanishes. The result is $\delta_{mn}$.

## Exercise 5 · Finite well with V₀a² = 5ℏ²/m

For the well of half-width $a$, $z_0=a\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy V_0}/\mechanical{\hbar}=\sqrt{10}$. The number of bound states is $\lceil2z_0/\pi\rceil=3$, with even, odd, and even parity in increasing energy. The lowest solution of $z\tan z=\sqrt{z_0^2-z^2}$ is near $z=1.185$. It gives

$$
\energy E_0=-\energy V_0+\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2z^2}{2\mechanical m a^2}
=\energy V_0(z^2/z_0^2-1)\simeq-0.86\energy V_0.
$$

## Exercise 6 · Superposition of the first two well states

The state is $\signal\psi(x,t)=(e^{-i\energy E_1t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\signal\psi_1(x)+e^{-i\energy E_2t/\mechanical{\hbar}}\signal\psi_2(x))/\sqrt2$. The diagonal position means are $L/2$ and the off-diagonal matrix element is $\langle\signal\psi_1\rvert\hat x\lvert\signal\psi_2\rangle=-16L/(9\pi^2)$ in the given sine convention. Thus

$$
\langle\hat x(t)\rangle=\frac L2-\frac{16L}{9\pi^2}\cos(\omega_{21}t),
\qquad
\omega_{21}=\frac{\energy E_2-\energy E_1}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
=\frac{3\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}}{2\mechanical mL^2}.
$$

The period is $T_{\mathrm{osc}}=2\pi/\omega_{21}=4\mechanical mL^2/(3\pi\mechanical{\hbar})$.

## Exercise 7 · Mean momentum in the infinite well

The eigenfunction is real and zero at the walls, so $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}\rangle=-i\mechanical{\hbar}[\signal\psi_n^2/2]_0^L=0$. Inside the well the kinetic operator is $\mechanical{\hat p}^2/(2\mechanical m)$, so $\langle\mechanical p^2\rangle=2\mechanical m\energy E_n=n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2/L^2$ and $\Delta\mechanical p=n\pi\mechanical{\hbar}/L$.

## Exercise 8 · Transmission through the attractive delta potential

At $\energy E>0$, write $\signal\psi=e^{ikx}+re^{-ikx}$ on the left and $\signal\psi=te^{ikx}$ on the right, with $k=\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. Continuity gives $t=1+r$. The derivative jump is $-2\mechanical m\alpha\signal\psi(0)/\mechanical{\hbar}^2$. Thus

$$
t=\frac{1}{1-i\mechanical m\alpha/(\mechanical{\hbar}^2k)},\qquad
T=\lvert t\rvert^2
=\frac{\energy E}{\energy E+\energy E_\alpha},
\qquad
\energy E_\alpha=\frac{\mechanical m\alpha^2}{2\mechanical{\hbar}^2}.
$$

The positive $\energy E_\alpha$ is the magnitude of the bound-state energy. As $k\to\infty$, $T\to1$.

## Exercise 9 · Heisenberg product in the ground state

Use $\langle x\rangle=L/2$ and $\langle x^2\rangle=L^2[1/3-1/(2\pi^2)]$. Then $(\Delta x)^2=L^2(\pi^2-6)/(12\pi^2)$. Exercise 7 gives $\Delta\mechanical p=\pi\mechanical{\hbar}/L$ for $n=1$. Therefore

$$
\Delta x\,\Delta\mechanical p=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}}{2\sqrt3}\sqrt{\pi^2-6}
\simeq0.568\mechanical{\hbar}>\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}}2.
$$

The Gaussian saturates the bound. The ground state of the infinite well has a strictly larger uncertainty product.

## Exercise 10 · Repulsive delta barrier

For $\energy V=+\alpha\delta(x)$, replace $\alpha$ by $-\alpha$ in the amplitude of Exercise 8. This gives $t=[1+i\mechanical m\alpha/(\mechanical{\hbar}^2 k)]^{-1}$, $T=\energy E/(\energy E+\energy E_\alpha)$, and $R=\energy E_\alpha/(\energy E+\energy E_\alpha)$. A bound state would require $\kappa=-\mechanical m\alpha/\mechanical{\hbar}^2<0$ for $\alpha>0$, whereas decay requires $\kappa>0$.

## Exercise 11 · Asymmetric well with an infinite wall on the left

For $-a<x<a$, use $\signal\psi=A\sin[k(x+a)]$ with $k=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E+\energy V_0)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. This vanishes at $x=-a$. For $x>a$, use $\signal\psi=Be^{-\kappa x}$ with $\kappa=\sqrt{-2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. Matching $\signal\psi$ and its derivative at $x=a$ gives

$$
k\cot(2ka)=-\kappa.
$$

Set $z=2ka$ and $z_0=2a\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy V_0}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. Then $z\cot z=-\sqrt{z_0^2-z^2}$. The lowest root must lie in $(\pi/2,\pi)$ with $z<z_0$. It exists if $z_0>\pi/2$, or $\energy V_0>\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(32\mechanical m a^2)$. This asymmetric well has a finite threshold for binding.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $L,a$ | $\unit{m}$ | Well dimensions | The width of the infinite well and the half-width in the finite examples. |
| $\energy E_n,\energy V_0,\energy E_\alpha$ | $\unit{J}$ | Level energies, depth, and delta energy scale | The energies that enter the matching conditions. |
| $k,\kappa$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wave number and decay constant | The spatial rates of oscillation and exponential decay. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Delta strength | The coefficient of the Dirac delta. |
