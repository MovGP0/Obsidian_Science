---
title: "Solutions for the Hydrogen Atom"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Integrate hydrogen wave functions and use the Coulomb spectrum to obtain line energies and orbital degeneracies.

Problems are in [[12.08 Hydrogen Atom Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Normalize the 1s state

For $\signal\psi_{100}(r)=(\pi a_0^3)^{-1/2}e^{-r/a_0}$, integrate in spherical coordinates.

$$
\int\lvert\signal\psi_{100}\rvert^2\,\mathrm d^3\mathbf r
=\frac4{a_0^3}\int_0^\infty r^2e^{-2r/a_0}\,\mathrm dr
=\frac4{a_0^3}\frac{2!}{(2/a_0)^3}=1.
$$

## Exercise 2 · Radial moments of the ground state

The integral is

$$
\langle r^n\rangle=\frac4{a_0^3}\int_0^\infty r^{n+2}e^{-2r/a_0}\,\mathrm dr
=\frac4{a_0^3}(n+2)!\left(\frac{a_0}{2}\right)^{n+3}
$$

for the nonnegative integer moments here. Thus $\langle r\rangle=3a_0/2$ and $\langle r^2\rangle=3a_0^2$. Subtracting the squared mean gives $\Delta r=\sqrt3\,a_0/2$.

## Exercise 3 · Inverse radial moments

The same radial probability measure gives $\langle1/r\rangle=(4/a_0^3)(a_0/2)^2=1/a_0$ and $\langle1/r^2\rangle=(4/a_0^3)(a_0/2)=2/a_0^2$.

## Exercise 4 · Lyman-α energy

For $2\to1$, $\Delta\energy E=\energy E_2-\energy E_1=(3/4)\unit{Ry}\simeq(3/4)\quantity{13.6}{eV}=\quantity{10.2}{eV}$.

## Exercise 5 · Lyman-α wavelength

Using $hc\simeq\quantity{1240}{eV\,nm}$, $\lambda=hc/\Delta\energy E\simeq\quantity{121.6}{nm}$ in the ultraviolet.

## Exercise 6 · Orbital states at n = 2

The $2s$ orbital gives one state with $m=0$. The $2p$ orbitals give three with $m=-1,0,1$. There are four orbital states in all. With two spin projections for each, the count is eight, or $2n^2$ at $n=2$.

## Exercise 7 · Helium ion

For a hydrogen-like ion, $\energy E_n=-Z^2\unit{Ry}/n^2$ and the radius scale is $a_0'=a_0/Z$. For $\mathrm{He}^+$ with $Z=2$, the ground energy is approximately $\quantity{-54.4}{eV}$ and $a_0'\simeq\quantity{0.265}{Å}$.

## Exercise 8 · Virial theorem for the ground state

The potential mean is $\langle\energy V\rangle=-\electric e^2\langle1/r\rangle/(4\pi\electric\varepsilon_0)=-2\unit{Ry}$. Since $\energy E_1=-\unit{Ry}$, $\langle\energy T\rangle=\energy E_1-\langle\energy V\rangle=\unit{Ry}$. Thus $2\langle\energy T\rangle+\langle\energy V\rangle=0$, as required for a Coulomb potential.

## Exercise 9 · Probability inside one Bohr radius in the 2p state

The radial function is $\signal R_{21}=(1/\sqrt{24})a_0^{-3/2}(r/a_0)e^{-r/(2a_0)}$. The radial probability density is $r^4 e^{-r/a_0}/(24a_0^5)$. With $u=r/a_0$,

$$
P(0<r<a_0)=\frac1{24}\int_0^1 u^4e^{-u}\,\mathrm du
=\frac{24-65/e}{24}\simeq3.7\times10^{-3}.
$$

About $0.37\%$ of the radial probability lies inside $a_0$. The radial density reaches its maximum at $r=4a_0$. Most of the probability lies between $a_0$ and $6a_0$.

## Exercise 10 · Ionization energy of lithium with two electrons removed

For $\mathrm{Li}^{2+}$ with $Z=3$, the ground energy is $\energy E_1=-9\unit{Ry}\simeq\quantity{-122.4}{eV}$. Ionization from the ground state requires $\quantity{122.4}{eV}$.

## Exercise 11 · Relativistic kinetic correction in the ground state

The unperturbed equation gives $\mechanical{\hat p}^2\signal\psi_{100}=2\mechanical m(\energy E_1-\energy V)\signal\psi_{100}$. Use the squared norm of this expression to get $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}^4\rangle=4\mechanical m^2\langle(\energy E_1-\energy V)^2\rangle$. The inverse moments give $\langle\energy V\rangle=-2\unit{Ry}$ and $\langle\energy V^2\rangle=8\unit{Ry^2}$. Hence $\langle(\energy E_1-\energy V)^2\rangle=(1-4+8)\unit{Ry^2}=5\unit{Ry^2}$.

With $\unit{Ry}/(\mechanical m c^2)=\alpha^2/2$ in the approximation used here,

$$
\Delta\energy E_{\mathrm{rel}}
=-\frac{5\unit{Ry^2}}{2\mechanical m c^2}
=-\frac54\alpha^2\unit{Ry}
\simeq\quantity{-9.05\times10^{-4}}{eV}.
$$
