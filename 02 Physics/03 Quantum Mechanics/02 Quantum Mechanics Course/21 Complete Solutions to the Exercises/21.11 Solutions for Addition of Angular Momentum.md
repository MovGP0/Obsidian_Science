> [!TLDR]
> Combine angular momentum operators and use coupled bases to calculate multiplets, Clebsch–Gordan coefficients, and spin–orbit shifts.

Problems are in [[11.10 Addition of Angular Momentum Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Algebra of total angular momentum

Let $\mechanical{\hat J}_i=\mechanical{\hat J}_{1i}+\mechanical{\hat J}_{2i}$. Components from different factors commute. Components in the same factor obey $[\mechanical{\hat J}_{ai},\mechanical{\hat J}_{aj}]=i\mechanical{\hbar}\varepsilon_{ijk}\mechanical{\hat J}_{ak}$. Thus

$$
[\mechanical{\hat J}_i,\mechanical{\hat J}_j]
=i\mechanical{\hbar}\varepsilon_{ijk}(\mechanical{\hat J}_{1k}+\mechanical{\hat J}_{2k})
=i\mechanical{\hbar}\varepsilon_{ijk}\mechanical{\hat J}_k.
$$

The total satisfies the angular momentum algebra. Its Casimir $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}}^2$ commutes with $\mechanical{\hat J}_z$ and with $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}}_a^2$ for $a=1,2$.

## Exercise 2 · Two spins 1/2

The triangle rule gives $J=0,1$. Their dimensions are $1$ and $3$, which sum to the product dimension $2\cdot2=4$. Write $1/2\otimes1/2=0\oplus1$.

## Exercise 3 · Combining spin 1/2 with 1, and 1 with 1

For $1/2\otimes1$, the allowed values are $J=1/2,3/2$ with dimensions $2+4=6$. For $1\otimes1$, the values are $J=0,1,2$ with dimensions $1+3+5=9$. These give $1/2\oplus3/2$ and $0\oplus1\oplus2$, respectively.

## Exercise 4 · Clebsch–Gordan coefficients for two spins 1/2

The states $\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\rangle$ and $\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\rangle$ both have $M=0$. In the chosen phase convention,

$$
\lvert1,0\rangle=\frac{\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\lvert0,0\rangle=\frac{\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

Therefore $\langle\uparrow\downarrow\mid1,0\rangle=1/\sqrt2$ and $\langle\uparrow\downarrow\mid0,0\rangle=1/\sqrt2$.

## Exercise 5 · Count the coupled states

Assume $j_1\ge j_2$. The arithmetic sum $\sum_{J=j_1-j_2}^{j_1+j_2}(2J+1)$ has $2j_2+1$ terms. The mean of the first and last terms is $2j_1+1$. Their product is the dimension $(2j_1+1)(2j_2+1)$ of the product space.

## Exercise 6 · Spherical tensor rank

An operator $\hat T_q^{(k)}$ transforms like the $q$ component of angular momentum rank $k$. The identity is invariant and has rank zero. The operator $\mechanical{\hat L}_z$ is the $q=0$ component of a rank-one tensor. In the Condon–Shortley convention, the other components are $\mp\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm/\sqrt2$. The scalar product $\mechanical{\mathbf L}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S}$ is invariant under joint rotations generated by $\mechanical{\mathbf J}=\mechanical{\mathbf L}+\mechanical{\mathbf S}$ and has rank zero.

## Exercise 7 · Wigner–Eckart theorem for the raising operator

With the reduced-matrix-element convention in the source,

$$
\langle jm'\rvert\hat T_q^{(k)}\lvert jm\rangle
=\langle jm;kq\mid jm'\rangle
\frac{\langle j\rVert\hat T^{(k)}\lVert j\rangle}{\sqrt{2j+1}}.
$$

Use $\hat T^{(1)}_{+1}=-\mechanical{\hat J}_+/\sqrt2$ and the ladder matrix element $\langle jm'\rvert\mechanical{\hat J}_+\lvert jm\rangle=\mechanical{\hbar}\sqrt{j(j+1)-m(m+1)}\,\delta_{m',m+1}$. Comparing with the Clebsch–Gordan factor gives $\langle j\rVert\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf J}}\lVert j\rangle=\mechanical{\hbar}\sqrt{j(j+1)(2j+1)}$.

## Exercise 8 · Spin–orbit product for j = ℓ ± 1/2

Use $\mechanical{\mathbf L}\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S}=(\mechanical{\mathbf J}^2-\mechanical{\mathbf L}^2-\mechanical{\mathbf S}^2)/2$. For $s=1/2$, the eigenvalue is

$$
\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2}\left[j(j+1)-\ell(\ell+1)-\frac34\right].
$$

At $j=\ell+1/2$ this is $\mechanical{\hbar}^2\ell/2$. At $j=\ell-1/2$ it is $-\mechanical{\hbar}^2(\ell+1)/2$. The lower branch requires $\ell\ge1$.

## Exercise 9 · Spin product on the triplet and singlet

For two spins $1/2$, $\mechanical{\mathbf S}_1\cdot\mechanical{\mathbf S}_2=(\mechanical{\mathbf S}^2-3\mechanical{\hbar}^2/2)/2$. On the triplet, $\mechanical{\mathbf S}^2=2\mechanical{\hbar}^2$ and the product is $\mechanical{\hbar}^2/4$. On the singlet the total spin is zero and the product is $-3\mechanical{\hbar}^2/4$.

## Exercise 10 · Racah recurrence

Let $C^M_{m_1m_2}=\langle j_1m_1;j_2m_2\mid JM\rangle$. Apply $\mechanical{\hat J}_\pm=\mechanical{\hat J}_{1\pm}+\mechanical{\hat J}_{2\pm}$ to the coupled expansion of $\lvert JM\rangle$, project onto the product state labeled by $m_1,m_2$, and cancel $\mechanical{\hbar}$. The result is

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\sqrt{J(J+1)-M(M\pm1)}\,C^{M\pm1}_{m_1m_2}\\
&\quad=\sqrt{j_1(j_1+1)-m_1(m_1\mp1)}\,C^M_{m_1\mp1,m_2}\\
&\qquad+\sqrt{j_2(j_2+1)-m_2(m_2\mp1)}\,C^M_{m_1,m_2\mp1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Terms outside the allowed range are zero. Once a normalized highest-weight state and its phase are fixed, the lowering relation generates the other coefficients in its multiplet.

> [!note] Ladder coefficient in the source
> The source's projected recurrence uses $m_a(m_a\pm1)$ even though $m_a$ labels the final product state. The factor above uses the initial projection $m_a\mp1$ and the final projection $m_a$.

## Exercise 11 · Sum over Clebsch–Gordan coefficients

The Wigner–Eckart theorem gives a Clebsch–Gordan coefficient times $\langle j'\rVert\hat T^{(k)}\lVert j\rangle/\sqrt{2j'+1}$. At fixed $j',m'$, the squared coefficients sum to one over $m,q$. Thus

$$
\sum_{m,q}\lvert\langle j'm'\rvert\hat T_q^{(k)}\lvert jm\rangle\rvert^2
=\frac{\lvert\langle j'\rVert\hat T^{(k)}\lVert j\rangle\rvert^2}{2j'+1}.
$$

The sum is independent of $m'$. In a reduced-element convention that absorbs the denominator into the element, the sum is the squared magnitude of that rescaled element.
