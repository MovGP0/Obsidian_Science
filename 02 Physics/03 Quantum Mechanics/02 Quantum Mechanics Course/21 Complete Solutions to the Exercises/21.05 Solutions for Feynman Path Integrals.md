> [!TLDR]
> Evaluate path-integral propagators and relate their phases to classical actions and semiclassical interference.

Problems are in [[05.08 Feynman Path Integrals Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Numerical free propagator

For mass $\quantity{10^{-26}}{kg}$ and elapsed time $\quantity{10^{-11}}{s}$,

$$
\lvert K\rvert=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}T}}
\simeq\quantity{1.23\times10^9}{m^{-1}}.
$$

The phase follows from Eq. (5.4), with $K\propto e^{i\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}T)}/\sqrt i$. Since $1/\sqrt i=e^{-i\pi/4}$,

$$
\varphi=\frac{\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2}{2\mechanical{\hbar}T}-\frac\pi4
\simeq(4.74-0.785)\unit{rad}\simeq\quantity{3.95}{rad}.
$$

## Exercise 2 · Direct verification of the free Schrödinger equation

Let $\xi=x_f-x_i$. Differentiating the free kernel gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_T K&=\left(-\frac1{2T}-\frac{i\mechanical m\xi^2}{2\mechanical{\hbar}T^2}\right)K,\\
\partial_{x_f}K&=\frac{i\mechanical m\xi}{\mechanical{\hbar}T}K,\\
\partial_{x_f}^2K&=\left(\frac{i\mechanical m}{\mechanical{\hbar}T}
-\frac{\mechanical m^2\xi^2}{\mechanical{\hbar}^2T^2}\right)K.
\end{aligned}
$$

Both $i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_T K$ and $-(\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\mechanical m))\partial_{x_f}^2 K$ equal $[-i\mechanical{\hbar}/(2T)+\mechanical m\xi^2/(2T^2)]K$.

## Exercise 3 · Phase variation scale of the free propagator

The phase $\mechanical m\xi^2/(2\mechanical{\hbar}T)$ changes by one radian from $\xi=0$ when $\lvert\xi\rvert=\ell=\sqrt{2\mechanical{\hbar}T/\mechanical m}$. For an electron and $T=\quantity{10^{-12}}{s}$,

$$
\ell\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2(1.055\times10^{-34})(10^{-12})}{9.11\times10^{-31}}}\unit{m}
\simeq\quantity{1.52\times10^{-8}}{m}.
$$

This is the displacement scale on which the phase begins to vary appreciably. Contributions with rapidly varying phases can cancel when integrated over a range of paths.

## Exercise 4 · Classical action of a free particle

For a path of length $L=vt$ at constant speed, $S_{\mathrm{cl}}=\mechanical m v^2t/2=\mechanical m v L/2$. In the example,

$$
\frac{S_{\mathrm{cl}}}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
\simeq\frac{(9.11\times10^{-31})(10^6)(10^{-7})}{2(1.055\times10^{-34})}
\simeq\quantity{4.32\times10^2}{rad}.
$$

An action much larger than $\mechanical{\hbar}$ indicates the semiclassical scale in this example.

## Exercise 5 · Small-ωT limit

Use $\sin(\omega T)\simeq\omega T-(\omega T)^3/6$ and $\cos(\omega T)\simeq1-(\omega T)^2/2$ in Eq. (5.6). The prefactor differs from that of the free kernel by a relative correction of order $(\omega T)^2$. The action in the phase is

$$
S_{\mathrm{cl}}
=\frac{\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2}{2T}
-\frac{\mechanical m\omega^2T}{6}(x_f^2+x_i^2+x_fx_i)
+O(\mechanical m\omega^4T^3)
$$

at fixed endpoints, with the endpoint length factors understood in the remainder. The second term gives the first potential correction to the free phase.

## Exercise 6 · Double slit as a sum of amplitudes

For paths of equal amplitude magnitude and a monoenergetic beam, $\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}=2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}/(\mechanical m v)$. A path of length $L_j$ has phase $\varphi_j=2\pi L_j/\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}$. Write the sum as $K_{\mathrm{free}}(e^{i\varphi_1}+e^{i\varphi_2})$ with a common factor. The intensity is proportional to

$$
\lvert K_1+K_2\rvert^2
=2\lvert K_{\mathrm{free}}\rvert^2(1+\cos\Delta\varphi),
\qquad
\Delta\varphi=\frac{\mechanical m v(L_2-L_1)}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
=\frac{2\pi\Delta L}{\lambda_{\mathrm{dB}}}.
$$

## Exercise 7 · Composition of the free propagator

The composition integral uses $K_{\mathrm{free}}(x_f,T_2;x,0)K_{\mathrm{free}}(x,T_1;x_i,0)$ and integrates over $x$. Complete the square in $(x_f-x)^2/T_2+(x-x_i)^2/T_1$. The constant part gives the phase $i\mechanical m(x_f-x_i)^2/[2\mechanical{\hbar}(T_1+T_2)]$ in the exponent. The Gaussian integral and kernel factors give

$$
\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}T_1}}
\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}T_2}}
\sqrt{\frac{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}T_1T_2}{\mechanical m(T_1+T_2)}}
=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m}{2\pi i\mechanical{\hbar}(T_1+T_2)}}.
$$

The resulting kernel is $K_{\mathrm{free}}(x_f,T_1+T_2;x_i,0)$.

## Exercise 8 · Numerical oscillator action

For $x_i=0$, Eq. (5.5) gives $S_{\mathrm{cl}}=\mechanical m\omega x_f^2/[2\tan(\omega T)]$. At $\omega T=\pi/4$, the tangent is one. Therefore

$$
S_{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{\mechanical m\omega x_f^2}{2}
=\quantity{5.00\times10^{-31}}{J\,s},\qquad
\frac{S_{\mathrm{cl}}}{\mechanical{\hbar}}\simeq\quantity{4.74\times10^3}{rad}.
$$

## Exercise 9 · Oscillator partition function

Continue Eq. (5.6) to $T=-i\beta\mechanical{\hbar}$, so $\sin(\omega T)=-i\sinh(\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega)$ and $\cos(\omega T)=\cosh(\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega)$. On the diagonal,

$$
K(x,-i\beta\mechanical{\hbar};x,0)
=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m\omega}{2\pi\mechanical{\hbar}\sinh(\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega)}}
\exp\left[-\frac{\mechanical m\omega x^2}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
\frac{\cosh(\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega)-1}{\sinh(\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega)}\right].
$$

Integrate this Gaussian over $x$. The identity $\cosh z-1=2\sinh^2(z/2)$ gives

$$
Z(\beta)=\int K(x,-i\beta\mechanical{\hbar};x,0)\,\mathrm dx
=\frac{1}{2\sinh(\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega/2)}
=\sum_{n=0}^\infty e^{-\beta\mechanical{\hbar}\omega(n+1/2)}.
$$

The exponential weights recover $\energy E_n=\mechanical{\hbar}\omega(n+1/2)$.

## Exercise 10 · Aharonov–Bohm effect with a solenoid

For paths $\gamma_1,\gamma_2$ on opposite sides of the solenoid, traverse $\gamma_1$ and then the reverse of $\gamma_2$. The vector potential gives

$$
\Delta\varphi=\frac{\electric q}{\mechanical{\hbar}}
\oint_{\gamma_1-\gamma_2}\magnetic{\mathbf A}\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf x
=\frac{\electric q\,\magnetic\Phi}{\mechanical{\hbar}}.
$$

The magnetic field is zero on the paths. The enclosed flux produces the observable phase shift. With $\magnetic\Phi=h/(2\electric e)$, the source uses the positive charge convention and obtains $\pi$. For an electron, $\electric q=-\electric e$ gives $-\pi$ with the same orientation. The phases agree modulo $2\pi$. The original maxima become destructive interference for equal-amplitude paths. See [[18.05 Aharonov–Bohm Effect]] for the full treatment.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $K,K_{\mathrm{free}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Propagator | The one-dimensional kernel for position-space evolution. |
| $T,\ell$ | $\unit{s},\unit{m}$ | Propagation time and displacement scale | The time of evolution and length for a phase change of one radian. |
| $\mechanical S_{\mathrm{cl}}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Classical action | The action evaluated along the classical path. |
| $\thermal Z,\thermal\beta$ | $1,\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Partition function and inverse thermal energy | The trace of the imaginary-time kernel and its inverse energy parameter. |
| $\electric q,\magnetic\Phi$ | $\unit{C},\unit{Wb}$ | Charge and magnetic flux | The quantities that set the Aharonov–Bohm phase. |
