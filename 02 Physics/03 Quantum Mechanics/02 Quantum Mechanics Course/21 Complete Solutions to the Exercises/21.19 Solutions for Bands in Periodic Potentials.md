> [!TLDR]
> Apply Bloch periodicity, dispersion, and the Kronig–Penney relation to calculate band properties.

Problems are in [[19.11 Bands in Periodic Potentials Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Width of the first Brillouin zone

For $a=\quantity{4}{Å}$, the first zone is $[-\pi/a,\pi/a)$. Its right boundary is $\pi/a\simeq\quantity{0.785}{Å^{-1}}=\quantity{7.85\times10^9}{m^{-1}}$. The width is $2\pi/a\simeq\quantity{1.571}{Å^{-1}}$, inversely proportional to the lattice period.

## Exercise 2 · Bloch periodicity

Since $\signal u_{nk}(x+a)=\signal u_{nk}(x)$, the Bloch form gives

$$
\signal\psi_{nk}(x+a)=e^{ik(x+a)}\signal u_{nk}(x+a)
=e^{ika}e^{ikx}\signal u_{nk}(x)
=e^{ika}\signal\psi_{nk}(x).
$$

The shift $x\mapsto x+a$ gives the phase $e^{ika}$. The operator in [[19.01 Discrete Translation Symmetry]] is defined with argument $x-a$ and has eigenvalue $e^{-ika}$. The source uses the opposite translation convention for its eigenvalue in this solution.

## Exercise 3 · Kronig–Penney function

For repulsive barriers,

$$
f(q)=\cos(qa)+\frac{\mechanical m\alpha}{\mechanical{\hbar}^2q}\sin(qa),
\qquad
f(0)=1+\frac{\mechanical m\alpha a}{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}>1.
$$

The function oscillates as $qa$ increases. The bands are the intervals with $\lvert f(q)\rvert\le1$ and the gaps have $\lvert f(q)\rvert>1$. The repulsive term raises the start of the first allowed interval above $q=0$. In wave-number units, the bands broaden and gaps narrow toward the nearly free limit at high $q$.

## Exercise 4 · Zone edge for nearly free electrons

Neglect the periodic potential. At $k=\pi/a$ the free energy is $\energy E=\mechanical{\hbar}^2\pi^2/(2\mechanical m a^2)$. Using the source's illustrative silicon lattice length of about $\quantity{5.4}{Å}$ with the electron mass gives about $\quantity{1.3}{eV}$ in this one-dimensional estimate. The periodic potential opens a gap near the crossing energy.

## Exercise 5 · Tight-binding band

The derivative of $\energy E(k)=\energy E_0-2\energy t\cos(ka)$ is $2\energy t a\sin(ka)$. At $\energy t>0$, the minimum is $\energy E_0-2\energy t$ at $k=0$. The maximum is $\energy E_0+2\energy t$ at $k=\pm\pi/a$. The bandwidth is $4\energy t$. Larger hopping gives a wider band and a larger group-velocity scale.

## Exercise 6 · First Kronig–Penney band edge

With $P=\mechanical m\alpha a/\mechanical{\hbar}^2=1.5$,

$$
f(q)=\cos(qa)+1.5\frac{\sin(qa)}{qa}.
$$

The edge satisfies $\lvert f(q)\rvert=1$. At $qa\to0^+$, $f(q)\to1+P=2.5$, so the initial interval is forbidden. The lower edge is the first positive root of $f(q)=1$. At $qa=1.50$, $f\simeq1.068$. At $qa=1.55$, $f\simeq0.988$. Solving gives $qa\simeq1.54272$, which the source quotes as approximately $1.542$. The lower-edge energy is about $1.19\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(\mechanical m a^2)$. The upper edge occurs at $qa=\pi$ with $f=-1$ and energy $\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\mechanical m a^2)\simeq4.93\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(\mechanical m a^2)$. The next gap starts there.

## Exercise 7 · Tight-binding effective mass

Near $k=0$, $\energy E(k)=\energy E_0-2\energy t+\energy t a^2k^2+O(k^4)$. Compare the quadratic term with $\mechanical{\hbar}^2k^2/(2\mechanical m^*)$. This gives $1/\mechanical m^*=2\energy t a^2/\mechanical{\hbar}^2$ and $\mechanical m^*=\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\energy t a^2)$. At fixed $a$, a wider band has smaller mass at the bottom in this model.

## Exercise 8 · Nearly free electron gap

For $\energy V(x)=\energy V_0\cos(2\pi x/a)$, both Fourier coefficients at $G=\pm2\pi/a$ are $\energy V_0/2$. At $k=\pi/a$, this couples the degenerate free states $e^{i\pi x/a}$ and $e^{-i\pi x/a}$. The gap is $\energy\Delta=2\lvert\energy V_{2\pi/a}\rvert=\lvert\energy V_0\rvert$.

## Exercise 9 · Density of states in a parabolic band

The two signs of $k$ and the two spin states give $4L\,\mathrm dk/(2\pi)$ states in an interval on the positive branch. Divide by $L$ and use $\mathrm dk/\mathrm d\energy E=\mechanical m^*/(\mechanical{\hbar}^2 k)$ at $k>0$. For $\energy E=\mechanical{\hbar}^2 k^2/(2\mechanical m^*)$ with $\mechanical m^*>0$,

$$
g(\energy E)=\frac2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical m^*}{2\mechanical{\hbar}^2\energy E}}.
$$

This applies inside the band and diverges as $1/\sqrt{\energy E}$ at its lower edge.

## Exercise 10 · States per band

With $L=Na$ and periodic boundary conditions, $k_j=2\pi j/L$ with integer $j$. There are $N$ distinct wave numbers modulo $2\pi/a$. For even $N$, one choice is $j=-N/2,\ldots,N/2-1$. Each band contains $N$ orbital states and $2N$ states including spin, so a filled spin-degenerate band holds two electrons per cell.

## Exercise 11 · Tight binding from Kronig–Penney

For large $\alpha$, the first band lies close to $qa=\pi$. Put $qa=\pi+\delta$ with small $\delta$. Then $\cos(qa)\simeq-1+\delta^2/2$, $\sin(qa)\simeq-\delta$, and $1/q\simeq a/\pi$. To first order in $1/\alpha$, $f(q)\simeq-1-(\mechanical m\alpha a/(\pi\mechanical{\hbar}^2))\delta$. Equating this to $\cos(ka)$ and expanding the energy gives

$$
\energy E(k)\simeq\energy{\tilde E}_0-2\energy t\cos(ka),\qquad
\energy{\tilde E}_0\simeq\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2\pi^2}{2\mechanical m a^2}-2\energy t,
\qquad
\energy t\simeq\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^4\pi^2}{2\mechanical m^2 a^3\alpha}.
$$

At the zone edge the upper band energy remains $\mechanical{\hbar}^2\pi^2/(2\mechanical m a^2)$. Hopping decreases as $1/\alpha$ as the cells become isolated.
