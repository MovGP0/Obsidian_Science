> [!TLDR]
> Use symmetry generators and commutators to derive conservation laws, parity constraints, and rotational selection rules.

Problems are in [[17.08 Symmetries and Conservation Laws Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Conservation of free momentum

The free Hamiltonian is $\energy{\hat H}_0=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}^2/(2\mechanical m)$. Its commutator with $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}$ vanishes because the momentum components commute. The Heisenberg equation gives $\mathrm d\langle\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}\rangle/\mathrm dt=0$. Spatial translation invariance and momentum conservation are equivalent here.

## Exercise 2 · Conjugation by translation

Use $\hat T(\mathbf a)=e^{-i\mathbf a\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}/\mechanical{\hbar}}$ and $[\mechanical{\hat p}_i,\hat r_j]=-i\mechanical{\hbar}\delta_{ij}$. The first commutator in the exponential conjugation gives $a_j$. Higher commutators vanish because that term is a scalar. Thus $\hat T^\dagger\hat{\mathbf r}\hat T=\hat{\mathbf r}+\mathbf a$. Since the momentum commutes with its own generator, $\hat T^\dagger\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}\hat T=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf p}}$.

## Exercise 3 · Parity is an involution

Parity maps $\signal\psi(\mathbf r)$ to $\signal\psi(-\mathbf r)$. Applying it twice gives the original state, so $\hat\Pi^2=\hat I$. For an eigenvector with eigenvalue $\lambda$, this gives $\lambda^2=1$. Even states have eigenvalue $+1$ and odd states have eigenvalue $-1$.

## Exercise 4 · Parity of hydrogen-like levels

Under inversion, the radial part is invariant and $Y_{\ell m}$ gains $(-1)^\ell$. Thus $1s$ is even. For $n=2$, $2s$ is even and $2p$ is odd. For $n=3$, $3s$ and $3d$ are even and $3p$ is odd.

## Exercise 5 · Conservation of parity

If $\energy V(-x)=\energy V(x)$, then $[\hat\Pi,\energy{\hat H}]=0$ and parity commutes with $\hat U(t)$. For initial parity $\pm1$,

$$
\hat\Pi\signal\psi(t)=\hat U(t)\hat\Pi\signal\psi(0)=\pm\hat U(t)\signal\psi(0)=\pm\signal\psi(t).
$$

The parity is preserved throughout the evolution.

## Exercise 6 · Rotation by π/2 in the ℓ = 1 basis

The operator $\hat R_z(\alpha)=e^{-i\alpha\mechanical{\hat L}_z/\mechanical{\hbar}}$ gives phase $e^{-im\alpha}$ on $\lvert1,m\rangle$. For order $m=-1,0,+1$, $\hat R_z(\pi/2)=\operatorname{diag}(i,1,-i)$. The reverse order gives $\operatorname{diag}(-i,1,i)$.

## Exercise 7 · Magnetic translations and the Aharonov–Bohm phase

For a uniform magnetic field, the kinetic momentum is $\mechanical{\boldsymbol\pi}=\mechanical{\mathbf p}-\electric q\magnetic{\mathbf A}$ and $[\mechanical{\hat\pi}_i,\mechanical{\hat\pi}_j]=i\mechanical{\hbar}\electric q\,\varepsilon_{ijk}\magnetic B_k$. If $\hat T_\pi(\mathbf a)=e^{-i\mathbf a\cdot\mechanical{\boldsymbol\pi}/\mechanical{\hbar}}$, its commutator in the exponent with the corresponding $\mathbf b$ generator is a scalar. Exchanging the two exponentials gives

$$
\hat T_\pi(\mathbf a)\hat T_\pi(\mathbf b)
=e^{-i\electric q\magnetic\Phi_{ab}/\mechanical{\hbar}}
\hat T_\pi(\mathbf b)\hat T_\pi(\mathbf a),
\qquad
\magnetic\Phi_{ab}=\magnetic{\mathbf B}\cdot(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b).
$$

The phase measures the oriented flux through the parallelogram. Reversing the order reverses the loop orientation. This is the flux dependence of an Aharonov–Bohm phase.

> [!note] Momentum and sign conventions
> The source calls $\mechanical{\boldsymbol\pi}$ canonical momentum and gives the positive sign in the exchange phase. The canonical momentum is $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, and the displayed kinetic-momentum exponentials give the negative sign for $\magnetic\Phi_{ab}$ as defined. The symmetry generators of magnetic translations in uniform $\magnetic{\mathbf B}$, $\mechanical{\boldsymbol\pi}+\electric q\magnetic{\mathbf B}\times\mathbf r$, have the opposite commutator and give the positive sign in the same order.

## Exercise 8 · Rotate the raising operator

Use $[\mechanical{\hat L}_z,\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm]=\pm\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm$. Every nested commutator in the conjugation series gives another factor of $\pm i\alpha$, so

$$
\hat R_z^\dagger(\alpha)\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm\hat R_z(\alpha)
=e^{\pm i\alpha}\mechanical{\hat L}_\pm.
$$

These phases correspond to the $q=\pm1$ components of a rank-one tensor.

## Exercise 9 · Approximate conservation of Jz

Assume $[\mechanical{\hat J}_z,\energy{\hat H}_0]=0$. Then $\mathrm d\langle\mechanical{\hat J}_z\rangle/\mathrm dt=(1/(i\mechanical{\hbar}))\langle[\mechanical{\hat J}_z,\energy{\hat V}]\rangle$. If this commutator is nonzero, the operator is not conserved in general. In a bounded sector, its change over time $t$ is bounded by $t\lVert[\mechanical{\hat J}_z,\energy{\hat V}]\rVert/\mechanical{\hbar}$. A weak perturbation can therefore preserve the mean approximately over a finite interval. If a slowly varying perturbation preserves a gap and only weakly mixes the unperturbed sectors, an adiabatically followed state can also retain nearly the same projection.

## Exercise 10 · Hydrogen ground state

The $1s$ wave function depends only on $r$ and has angular part $Y_{00}$. Hence $\mechanical{\hat L}_z\signal\psi_{100}=-i\mechanical{\hbar}\partial_\phi\signal\psi_{100}=0$ and $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf L}}^2\signal\psi_{100}=0$. Under inversion $r$ is unchanged, so the state has even parity and $\ell=m=0$.

## Exercise 11 · Ratio of hydrogen decay rates

The dipole rate scales as $\omega^3\lvert\electric{\mathbf d}_{fi}\rvert^2$. In units of $\unit{Ry}/\mechanical{\hbar}$, the two transition frequencies are $3/4$ for $2p\to1s$ and $8/9$ for $3p\to1s$. Their cubed ratio is

$$
\frac{\omega_{2p\to1s}^3}{\omega_{3p\to1s}^3}
=\left(\frac{3/4}{8/9}\right)^3\simeq0.601.
$$

The radial integrals give $\lvert\langle1s\rvert r\lvert2p\rangle_{\mathrm{rad}}\rvert^2\simeq1.665a_0^2$ and $\lvert\langle1s\rvert r\lvert3p\rangle_{\mathrm{rad}}\rvert^2\simeq0.267a_0^2$. The angular factors agree for the same orbital channels, so the ratio is about $0.601/0.160\simeq3.75$. The rates quoted in the source are $\quantity{6.27\times10^8}{s^{-1}}$ and $\quantity{1.67\times10^8}{s^{-1}}$ respectively. The larger squared matrix element outweighs the lower transition frequency for $2p\to1s$.
