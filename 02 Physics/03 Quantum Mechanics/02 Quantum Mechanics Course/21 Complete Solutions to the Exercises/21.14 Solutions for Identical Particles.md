> [!TLDR]
> Apply exchange symmetry to two-particle states and derive its effects on spin, spatial correlations, and energy.

Problems are in [[14.08 Many-Particle Systems and Identical Particles Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Rotation invariance of the singlet

The spin singlet is $\lvert\signal\Psi^-\rangle=(\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Its total spin is zero, so every component of $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}=\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}_1+\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}_2$ annihilates it. The common rotation $e^{-i\theta\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}/\mechanical{\hbar}}$ therefore leaves it invariant.

## Exercise 2 · Triplet and singlet

The triplet states $\lvert1,1\rangle=\lvert\uparrow\uparrow\rangle$, $\lvert1,0\rangle=(\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$, and $\lvert1,-1\rangle=\lvert\downarrow\downarrow\rangle$ are symmetric. The singlet $\lvert0,0\rangle=(\lvert\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-\lvert\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$ is antisymmetric. The eigenvalues of $\mechanical{\hat{\mathbf S}}^2$ are $2\mechanical{\hbar}^2$ and zero respectively.

## Exercise 3 · Two bosons in an infinite well

Both bosons can occupy $n=1$. The ground state $\signal\Psi(x_1,x_2)=\signal\phi_1(x_1)\signal\phi_1(x_2)$ is already symmetric. Its energy is $2\energy E_1=\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(\mechanical m L^2)$.

## Exercise 4 · Two spinless fermions

The spatial state must be antisymmetric. The lowest distinct orbitals are $1$ and $2$. Thus

$$
\signal\Psi(x_1,x_2)=\frac{\signal\phi_1(x_1)\signal\phi_2(x_2)-\signal\phi_2(x_1)\signal\phi_1(x_2)}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\energy E=\frac{5\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m L^2}.
$$

## Exercise 5 · When the Slater determinant vanishes

The normalized Slater form for orthonormal orbitals is $\signal\Psi(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_N)=\det[\signal\phi_i(\xi_j)]/\sqrt{N!}$. If two complete one-particle states coincide, the corresponding rows are equal. Their determinant is zero, which expresses Pauli exclusion.

## Exercise 6 · Mean squared separation for bosons

For two distinct orthonormal orbitals $a,b$, the symmetric combination gives

$$
\langle(x_1-x_2)^2\rangle_S
=\langle x^2\rangle_a+\langle x^2\rangle_b
-2\langle x\rangle_a\langle x\rangle_b
-2\lvert\langle a\rvert\hat x\lvert b\rangle\rvert^2.
$$

The first three terms give the result for distinguishable particles. The last term reduces the separation when the exchange matrix element is nonzero. For the first two infinite-well orbitals, the distinguishable result is $L^2[1/6-5/(8\pi^2)]$ and the exchange term is $-512L^2/(81\pi^4)$.

## Exercise 7 · Mean squared separation for fermions

The antisymmetric combination reverses the exchange term. Thus

$$
\langle(x_1-x_2)^2\rangle_A
=\langle(x_1-x_2)^2\rangle_{\mathrm{dist}}
+2\lvert\langle a\rvert\hat x\lvert b\rangle\rvert^2.
$$

For the first two infinite-well orbitals the increase is $512L^2/(81\pi^4)$. It follows from exchange antisymmetry even for noninteracting particles.

## Exercise 8 · Orthogonality of the Bell states

Use $\lvert\signal\Psi^\pm\rangle=(\lvert01\rangle\pm\lvert10\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and $\lvert\signal\Phi^\pm\rangle=(\lvert00\rangle\pm\lvert11\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Each state has norm one. The plus and minus signs give $\langle\signal\Psi^-\mid\signal\Psi^+\rangle=(1-1)/2=0$, and similarly for the $\signal\Phi$ pair. The two families have disjoint support in the computational basis. All remaining off-diagonal overlaps are zero, so the Gram matrix is the identity on the four-dimensional space.

## Exercise 9 · Helium variational calculation

For the singlet with spatial product $\signal\phi_{1s}(Z';\mathbf r_1)\signal\phi_{1s}(Z';\mathbf r_2)$, each electron contributes kinetic energy $Z'^2/2$ and nuclear attraction $-ZZ'$ in Hartree units. The repulsion contributes $(5/8)Z'$. Thus

$$
\energy E(Z')=[Z'^2-2ZZ'+(5/8)Z']\unit{Ha}
=[2Z'^2-4ZZ'+(5/4)Z']\unit{Ry}.
$$

The minimum occurs at $Z'_{\mathrm{opt}}=Z-5/16=27/16$ for helium. The energy is $-2(Z'_{\mathrm{opt}})^2\unit{Ry}\simeq\quantity{-77.5}{eV}$. The source quotes the experimental value $\quantity{-78.99}{eV}$.

## Exercise 10 · Ground term of p²

The Pauli-compatible terms are $^1S$, $^1D$, and $^3P$. Hund's first rule gives $S=1$, and the allowed triplet has $L=1$. The $p$ subshell is less than half filled, so the lowest level has $J=\lvert L-S\rvert=0$. The ground term is $^3P_0$.

## Exercise 11 · Fermi energy

At zero temperature, $N$ spin-$1/2$ fermions fill the wave-vector sphere up to $k_F$. The two spin states give

$$
N=2\frac{\mechanical V}{6\pi^2}k_F^3=\frac{\mechanical V k_F^3}{3\pi^2}.
$$

For density $n=N/\mechanical V$, $k_F=(3\pi^2 n)^{1/3}$. Hence

$$
\energy E_F=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2 k_F^2}{2\mechanical m}
=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m}(3\pi^2n)^{2/3}.
$$

The total energy of the filled sphere is $\energy E_{\mathrm{tot}}=(3/5)N\energy E_F$ in the continuum limit.
