## Exercise 1 · Elementary commutators

Use $[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}]=i\mechanical{\hbar}$ and $[\hat A\hat B,\hat C]=\hat A[\hat B,\hat C]+[\hat A,\hat C]\hat B$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\hat x^2,\mechanical{\hat p}]&=\hat x[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}]+[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}]\hat x=2i\mechanical{\hbar}\hat x,\\
[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}^2]&=[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}]\mechanical{\hat p}
+\mechanical{\hat p}[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}]=2i\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat p}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Exercise 2 · Commutator with a function of momentum

Induct on $n$. If $[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}^n]=ni\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat p}^{n-1}$, then the product rule gives

$$
[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}^{n+1}]
=[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}^n]\mechanical{\hat p}
+\mechanical{\hat p}^n[\hat x,\mechanical{\hat p}]
=(n+1)i\mechanical{\hbar}\mechanical{\hat p}^n.
$$

For an analytic expansion $f(\mechanical{\hat p})=\sum_n c_n\mechanical{\hat p}^n$ on a common domain where the operations converge, linearity gives $[\hat x,f(\mechanical{\hat p})]=i\mechanical{\hbar}f'(\mechanical{\hat p})$.

## Exercise 3 · Gaussian minimum-uncertainty state

For $\signal\psi(x)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/4}e^{-x^2/(4\sigma^2)}$, symmetry gives $\langle x\rangle=0$. The Gaussian variance gives $\langle x^2\rangle=\sigma^2$, so $\Delta x=\sigma$. Also $\mechanical{\hat p}\signal\psi=(i\mechanical{\hbar}x/(2\sigma^2))\signal\psi$, which gives $\langle\mechanical p\rangle=0$ and

$$
\langle\mechanical p^2\rangle
=\mechanical{\hbar}^2\int\lvert\signal\psi'(x)\rvert^2\,\mathrm dx
=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{4\sigma^4}\langle x^2\rangle
=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{4\sigma^2}.
$$

Thus $\Delta\mechanical p=\mechanical{\hbar}/(2\sigma)$ and $\Delta x\,\Delta\mechanical p=\mechanical{\hbar}/2$. The Gaussian saturates the uncertainty bound.

## Exercise 4 · Zero uncertainty in an eigenstate

If $\hat A\lvert\signal\psi\rangle=a\lvert\signal\psi\rangle$, then $\langle\hat A^n\rangle=a^n$ and $\Delta A=0$. The Robertson inequality then implies $\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\rangle=0$ for observables with the required common domain and finite variances. A state with nonzero commutator expectation cannot be an eigenstate of $\hat A$.

## Exercise 5 · Spin spread along z

For a spin-$1/2$ state at polar angle $\theta$, $\langle\mechanical{\hat S}_z\rangle=(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)[\cos^2(\theta/2)-\sin^2(\theta/2)]=(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)\cos\theta$. Since $\mechanical{\hat S}_z^2=(\mechanical{\hbar}/2)^2\hat I$,

$$
(\Delta\mechanical S_z)^2=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{4}(1-\cos^2\theta),\qquad
\Delta\mechanical S_z=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}}2\lvert\sin\theta\rvert.
$$

## Exercise 6 · Atomic linewidth

For lifetime $\tau=\quantity{10^{-8}}{s}$, the natural energy width is $\energy\Gamma=\mechanical{\hbar}/\tau=\quantity{1.055\times10^{-26}}{J}\simeq\quantity{6.6\times10^{-8}}{eV}$. At $\lambda_0=\quantity{500}{nm}$, the center energy is $\energy E_0\simeq\quantity{2.48}{eV}$. Linearizing $\lambda=hc/\energy E$ gives $\Delta\lambda/\lambda_0\simeq\energy\Gamma/\energy E_0\simeq2.66\times10^{-8}$ and $\Delta\lambda\simeq\quantity{1.3\times10^{-14}}{m}$.

## Exercise 7 · Gaussian tail beyond 3σ

For a centered Gaussian probability density with standard deviation $\sigma$,

$$
P(\lvert x\rvert>3\sigma)=2\int_{3\sigma}^\infty
\frac{e^{-x^2/(2\sigma^2)}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\,\mathrm dx
=2[1-\Phi(3)]\simeq2.7\times10^{-3}.
$$

Here $\Phi$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution and $\Phi(3)\simeq0.99865$. A classical oscillator at fixed energy has no probability outside its turning points. The quantum Gaussian can extend into a classically forbidden region.

## Exercise 8 · Saturation of the Pauli uncertainty relation

The commutator $[\sigma_x,\sigma_z]=-2i\sigma_y$ gives $\Delta\sigma_x\,\Delta\sigma_z\ge\lvert\langle\sigma_y\rangle\rvert$. In $\lvert\pm y\rangle$, the expectation of $\sigma_y$ is $\pm1$ and the other two expectations vanish. The $y$ basis is mutually unbiased with the $x$ and $z$ bases. Since $\sigma_x^2=\sigma_z^2=\hat I$, both standard deviations equal one and the bound is saturated.

> [!note] Pauli bases in the source
> The source describes the bases as orthogonal to one another. Eigenvectors within a basis are orthogonal. States from different Pauli bases have squared overlap $1/2$.

## Exercise 9 · Spreading of a free Gaussian packet

For the real centered initial Gaussian, the symmetrized position–momentum covariance is zero. The free evolution $\hat x(t)=\hat x(0)+\mechanical{\hat p}(0)t/\mechanical m$ therefore gives

$$
\langle x^2\rangle_t=\langle x^2\rangle_0+\frac{\langle\mechanical p^2\rangle_0}{\mechanical m^2}t^2.
$$

With $\Delta x(0)=\sigma$ and $\Delta\mechanical p=\mechanical{\hbar}/(2\sigma)$,

$$
(\Delta x(t))^2=\sigma^2+\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2t^2}{4\mechanical m^2\sigma^2},
\qquad
\Delta x(t)=\sigma\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}t}{2\mechanical m\sigma^2}\right)^2}.
$$

The characteristic spreading time is $t_*=2\mechanical m\sigma^2/\mechanical{\hbar}$. The variance doubles at $t_*$. The standard deviation doubles at $\sqrt3\,t_*$.

## Exercise 10 · Schrödinger uncertainty inequality

Use the centered operators $\hat a=\hat A-\langle\hat A\rangle\hat I$ and $\hat b=\hat B-\langle\hat B\rangle\hat I$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\langle\hat a^2\rangle\langle\hat b^2\rangle\ge\lvert\langle\hat a\hat b\rangle\rvert^2$. The anticommutator contribution to $\hat a\hat b$ has a real expectation, and the commutator contribution has a purely imaginary expectation. Therefore

$$
(\Delta A)^2(\Delta B)^2
\ge \frac14\lvert\langle[\hat A,\hat B]\rangle\rvert^2
+\frac14\lvert\langle\{\hat a,\hat b\}\rangle\rvert^2.
$$

The second term measures the square of the symmetrized covariance. It is zero, for example, for the pair $\sigma_x,\sigma_z$ in a $\sigma_y$ eigenstate.

## Exercise 11 · Semiclassical oscillator limit

In the energy eigenstate $\lvert n\rangle$, parity gives $\langle x\rangle=\langle\mechanical p\rangle=0$. The second moments are $\langle x^2\rangle=(\mechanical{\hbar}/(\mechanical m\omega))(n+1/2)$ and $\langle\mechanical p^2\rangle=\mechanical m\mechanical{\hbar}\omega(n+1/2)$. Hence

$$
\Delta x\,\Delta\mechanical p=\mechanical{\hbar}(n+1/2)\sim n\mechanical{\hbar}.
$$

The absolute spread grows with $n$. Relative to the classical turning amplitude $x_{\max}=\sqrt{2\energy E_n/(\mechanical m\omega^2)}$, $\Delta x/x_{\max}=1/\sqrt2$, the root-mean-square ratio for classical harmonic motion.
