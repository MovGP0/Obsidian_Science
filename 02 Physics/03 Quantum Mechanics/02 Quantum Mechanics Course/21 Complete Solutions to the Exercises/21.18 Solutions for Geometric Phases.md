---
title: "Solutions for Geometric Phases"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Derive Berry phases from solid angles and Aharonov–Bohm phases from flux. Estimate the time scale for adiabatic evolution.

Problems are in [[18.08 Geometric Phases Exercises]].

## Exercise 1 · Cone with half-angle π/3

The solid angle is

$$
\Omega=\int_0^{2\pi}\mathrm d\varphi\int_0^{\pi/3}\sin\theta\,\mathrm d\theta
=2\pi[1-\cos(\pi/3)]=\pi.
$$

The spin-up eigenstate of spin $1/2$ has $\gamma_+=-\Omega/2=-\pi/2$. For the $m=1$ eigenstate of spin one, the phase is $-\Omega=-\pi$.

## Exercise 2 · Equator of the field sphere

For $\theta=\pi/2$, $\Omega=2\pi$ and $\gamma_-=+\pi$. The spin-up result is $-\pi$. These agree modulo $2\pi$ for this loop, while the curvatures of the two eigenstates have opposite signs. The geometric factor is $-1$ after one circuit and $+1$ after two. This sign change is related to the $4\pi$ rotation property of spinors.

## Exercise 3 · Magnetic flux and flux periods

For $a=\quantity{1}{mm}$ and $\magnetic B=\quantity{0.1}{T}$, $\magnetic\Phi_B=\pi a^2\magnetic B\simeq\quantity{3.14\times10^{-7}}{Wb}$. The electron flux period is $h/\electric e\simeq\quantity{4.14\times10^{-15}}{Wb}$, so the solenoid flux is about $7.6\times10^7$ periods. In a superconductor the Cooper-pair charge magnitude is $2\electric e$, so the flux quantum is $h/(2\electric e)$. Quantization is observable in microscopic superconducting systems.

## Exercise 4 · Adiabatic time scale

In the condition $\mechanical{\hbar}\lvert\langle m\rvert\partial_t\energy{\hat H}\lvert n\rangle\rvert/(\energy E_n-\energy E_m)^2\ll1$, use a gap $\energy\Delta=\quantity{10^{-3}}{eV}$ and $\lVert\partial_t\energy{\hat H}\rVert\sim\energy\Delta/T$. This gives $T\gg\mechanical{\hbar}/\energy\Delta\simeq\quantity{6.6\times10^{-13}}{s}$. The source suggests a factor of $10$ to $100$ as a practical margin. A numerical fidelity guarantee requires the actual Hamiltonian path and schedule.

## Exercise 5 · Aharonov–Bohm phase at half the electron flux period

For an electron with $\electric q=-\electric e$, $\phi_{\mathrm{AB}}=\electric q\magnetic\Phi/\mechanical{\hbar}=-\pi$ at $\magnetic\Phi=h/(2\electric e)$. This is equivalent to $\pi$ modulo $2\pi$. An electron interferometer shifts by half a fringe for one superconducting flux quantum.

## Exercise 6 · Berry phase for a general angular path

For the spinor $(\cos(\theta/2),e^{i\varphi}\sin(\theta/2))^{\mathsf T}$, $A_\theta=0$ and $A_\varphi=-\sin^2(\theta/2)$. Therefore

$$
\gamma_+=-\oint\sin^2(\theta/2)\,\mathrm d\varphi
=-\frac12\oint(1-\cos\theta)\,\mathrm d\varphi.
$$

For fixed $\theta_0$ and one positive circuit, the phase is $-\pi(1-\cos\theta_0)=-\Omega/2$. The line integral also applies to noncircular closed paths in a suitable angular gauge.

## Exercise 7 · Nearly circular path

For $\theta(\varphi)=\theta_0+\alpha\cos\varphi$, expand

$$
\cos\theta(\varphi)
=\cos\theta_0-\alpha\sin\theta_0\cos\varphi
-\frac{\alpha^2}{2}\cos\theta_0\cos^2\varphi+O(\alpha^3).
$$

The first angular moment of $\cos\varphi$ is zero and the integral of $\cos^2\varphi$ over one circuit is $\pi$. All odd powers integrate to zero. Therefore

$$
\gamma_+=-\pi(1-\cos\theta_0)-\frac{\pi\alpha^2}{4}\cos\theta_0+O(\alpha^4).
$$

The correction lowers the phase when $\cos\theta_0>0$ and raises it when $\cos\theta_0<0$.

## Exercise 8 · Half-fringe shift

A half-fringe shift requires $\phi_{\mathrm{AB}}=\pi$ modulo $2\pi$. With the signed charge $\electric q$, one solution is $\magnetic\Phi=\pi\mechanical{\hbar}/\electric q=h/(2\electric q)$. For an electron, the magnitude $h/(2\electric e)$ also gives a half-fringe shift. This magnitude equals the superconducting flux quantum.

## Exercise 9 · Ring threaded by flux

In the angular coordinate $\theta$,

$$
\energy{\hat H}=\frac{(\mechanical{\hat p}_\theta-\electric q\magnetic\Phi/(2\pi))^2}{2\mechanical m R^2}.
$$

The functions $e^{in\theta}$ have $\mechanical{\hat p}_\theta$ eigenvalue $n\mechanical{\hbar}$. Their energies are

$$
\energy E_n(\magnetic\Phi)=\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2}{2\mechanical m R^2}
\left(n-\frac{\electric q\magnetic\Phi}{h}\right)^2.
$$

A shift by $\magnetic\Phi_0=h/\electric q$ relabels the spectrum. The ground branch selects the integer nearest $\electric q\magnetic\Phi/h$, with derivative jumps where two adjacent parabolas cross.

## Exercise 10 · Flux of the Berry monopole

On a sphere of field-space radius $B$ centered at the degeneracy, $\mathbf F_+=-\hat{\mathbf n}/(2B^2)$ and $\mathrm d\mathbf S=\hat{\mathbf n}B^2\sin\theta\,\mathrm d\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi$. Thus

$$
\int_{S^2}\mathbf F_+\cdot\mathrm d\mathbf S=-\frac12\int\mathrm d\Omega=-2\pi.
$$

The radial monopole coefficient is $-1/2$. The flux in units of $2\pi$ gives a Chern number of $-1$. The equatorial loop encloses half the sphere and gives $-\pi$.

> [!note] Flux quantization and loop phases
> The source says that the phase is quantized in units of $\pi$ for a loop around the monopole. The quantized invariant is the flux through the full closed sphere divided by $2\pi$. A general loop has a phase set by its solid angle and can vary continuously.

## Exercise 11 · Total phase on a ring with electric driving

For a cycle with one positive winding about a fixed flux $\magnetic\Phi$, the solution uses

$$
\phi_n^{\mathrm{tot}}=\phi_{\mathrm{AB}}+\phi_{\mathrm{dyn}},\qquad
\phi_{\mathrm{AB}}=2\pi\frac{\magnetic\Phi}{\magnetic\Phi_0},\qquad
\phi_{\mathrm{dyn}}=-\frac1{\mechanical{\hbar}}\int_0^T\energy E_n(t)\,\mathrm dt.
$$

The flux term depends on the winding and enclosed flux. The dynamical term depends on the energy history. For interference between paths with a typical relative energy difference $\Delta\energy E$, the relative dynamical phase is small on the $2\pi$ scale if $T\Delta\energy E/\mechanical{\hbar}\ll2\pi$. A mesoscopic ring with slow electric driving is a possible setting for this comparison. The explicit driving Hamiltonian is needed to calculate a general cycle's phases.
