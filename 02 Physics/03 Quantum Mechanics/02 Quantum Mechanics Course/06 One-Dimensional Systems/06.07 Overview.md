> [!TLDR]
> The five one-dimensional potentials illustrate how confinement and asymptotic behavior determine whether the spectrum is discrete, continuous, or mixed.

| Potential | Spectrum | States |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[06.01 Free Particle\|Free particle]], $\energy V=0$ | Continuous on $[0,\infty)$ | Plane waves $e^{\pm i\signal kx}$. |
| [[06.02 Potential Step\|Step]] of height $\energy V_0$ | Continuous on $[\min(0,\energy V_0),\infty)$ | Incident, reflected, and transmitted components. |
| [[06.03 Infinite Potential Well\|Infinite well]] | Discrete, $\energy E_n=n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical mL^2)$ | Standing waves $\sin(n\pi x/L)$. |
| [[06.04 Finite Potential Well\|Finite well]] | Finite discrete part and a continuum | Bound states oscillate inside and decay exponentially outside. |
| [[06.05 Attractive Delta Potential\|Attractive delta]] | One bound state and a continuum | The bound-state amplitude is proportional to $e^{-\signal{\kappa}\lvert x\rvert}$. |

In these examples, any bound levels form a finite or countably infinite set of discrete energies with normalizable wave functions. The rest of the spectrum, where present, is continuous.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy V,\energy V_0,\energy E_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The potential, step height, and discrete energy eigenvalues. |
| $\signal k,\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber and decay constant | The spatial oscillation and exponential decay rates. |
| $x,L$ | $\unit m$ | Position and well width | The coordinate and the infinite-well width. |
| $n$ | $1$ | Level index | The positive integer identifying a bound mode. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the discrete energy formula. |
