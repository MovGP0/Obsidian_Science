---
title: "Potential Step"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A finite potential step reflects part of an incident wave even above the step energy. Below the step, the transmitted wave is evanescent.

Take $\energy V(x)=0$ for $x<0$ and $\energy V(x)=\energy V_0>0$ for $x\ge0$. A wave of energy $\energy E$ is incident from the left.

## Above the step

For $\energy E>\energy V_0$, let $\signal k_1=\sqrt{2\mechanical m\energy E}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ in region I ($x<0$), and $\signal k_2=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy E-\energy V_0)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$ in region II ($x\ge0$). Write

$$
\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm I}(x)=\signal A e^{i\signal k_1x}+\signal B e^{-i\signal k_1x},\qquad
\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm{II}}(x)=\signal C e^{i\signal k_2x}.
$$

The amplitudes $\signal A$, $\signal B$, and $\signal C$ describe the incident, reflected, and transmitted waves. Continuity of $\signal{\psi}$ and $\signal{\psi}'$ at $x=0$ gives $\signal A+\signal B=\signal C$ and $\signal k_1(\signal A-\signal B)=\signal k_2\signal C$. Solve for

$$
\frac{\signal B}{\signal A}=\frac{\signal k_1-\signal k_2}{\signal k_1+\signal k_2},
\qquad
\frac{\signal C}{\signal A}=\frac{2\signal k_1}{\signal k_1+\signal k_2}.
$$

Use the [[04.03 Probability Conservation and Current|probability currents]] to obtain the reflection and transmission coefficients.

$$
\signal R=\left\lvert\frac{\signal B}{\signal A}\right\rvert^2
=\left(\frac{\signal k_1-\signal k_2}{\signal k_1+\signal k_2}\right)^2,\qquad
\signal T=\frac{\signal k_2}{\signal k_1}\left\lvert\frac{\signal C}{\signal A}\right\rvert^2
=\frac{4\signal k_1\signal k_2}{(\signal k_1+\signal k_2)^2}.
$$

The relation $\signal R+\signal T=1$ expresses probability conservation. The partial reflection above the step is a wave effect. A classical particle with enough energy to cross the step continues forward.

## Below the step

For $0<\energy E<\energy V_0$, $\signal k_2^{\,2}<0$. Set $\signal{\kappa}=\sqrt{2\mechanical m(\energy V_0-\energy E)}/\mechanical{\hbar}$. The admissible wave is $\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm{II}}(x)=\signal C e^{-\signal{\kappa}x}$. The boundary conditions give

$$
\frac{\signal B}{\signal A}=\frac{\signal k_1-i\signal{\kappa}}{\signal k_1+i\signal{\kappa}},
\qquad
\signal R=\left\lvert\frac{\signal B}{\signal A}\right\rvert^2=1.
$$

Reflection is total. The exponential is real up to a constant phase, so it carries no transmitted current. The wave nevertheless penetrates the classically forbidden region. Its amplitude falls by a factor $e$ over the penetration depth $1/\signal{\kappa}$, and its density $\lvert\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm{II}}\rvert^2$ falls by the same factor over $1/(2\signal{\kappa})$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy E,\energy V,\energy V_0$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy quantities | The incident energy, potential, and step height. |
| $\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm I},\signal{\psi}_{\mathrm{II}}$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ for position normalization | Wave functions | The amplitudes on the two sides of the step. |
| $\signal A,\signal B,\signal C$ | Same as $\signal{\psi}$ | Wave coefficients | The incident, reflected, and transmitted amplitudes. |
| $\signal k_1,\signal k_2,\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumbers and decay constant | The oscillation rates in the allowed regions and the exponential rate below the step. |
| $\signal R,\signal T$ | $1$ | Reflection and transmission coefficients | The reflected and transmitted fractions of incident current. |
| $x,1/\signal{\kappa}$ | $\unit m$ | Position and penetration depth | The distance from the interface and the amplitude decay length. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant relating wavenumber and momentum. |
