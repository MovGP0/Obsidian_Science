---
title: "Infinite Potential Well"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The infinite well confines the wave function to a finite interval. The zero boundary values select standing waves with energies proportional to the square of the mode number.

Take $\energy V(x)=0$ for $0<x<L$ and $\energy V(x)=\infty$ outside. The wave function is confined to the interval, with $\signal{\psi}(0)=\signal{\psi}(L)=0$.

Inside the well, Eq. (6.1) gives $\signal{\psi}''=-\signal k^{\,2}\signal{\psi}$, with $\signal k^{\,2}=2\mechanical m\energy E/\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}$. The general solution is $\signal{\psi}(x)=\signal A\sin(\signal kx)+\signal B\cos(\signal kx)$. The first boundary condition gives $\signal B=0$. The second gives $\signal kL=n\pi$ with $n=1,2,\ldots$. Therefore

$$
\energy E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}}{2\mechanical mL^2},
\qquad
\signal{\psi}_n(x)=\sqrt{\frac2L}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right).
$$

The levels are discrete and nondegenerate. The gap between adjacent levels grows with $n$. The ground state has $n=1$ and positive energy. For an estimate, take $\Delta x\sim L/2$. The [[03.03 Robertson Inequality|uncertainty relation]] gives $\Delta\mechanical p\gtrsim\mechanical{\hbar}/L$, so $\langle\mechanical{\hat p}^{\,2}\rangle\gtrsim\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}/L^2$ and $\energy E\gtrsim\mechanical{\hbar}^{\,2}/(2\mechanical mL^2)$. This is of the same order as $\energy E_1$. Figure 6.1 shows the first eigenfunctions.

## Example. Probability in half the well

For $\signal{\psi}_n(x)$, the probability of finding the particle in $0<x<L/2$ is

$$
P=\frac2L\int_0^{L/2}\sin^2\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\,dx=\frac12.
$$

Reflection about $x=L/2$ leaves the density of each eigenstate unchanged, so the two halves have equal probability.

## Parity

Center the well on $[-L/2,L/2]$. Then each eigenstate is even or odd.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\psi}_n^{(+)}(x)&\propto\cos\left(\frac{(2k+1)\pi x}{L}\right),&
n&=2k+1,\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots,\\
\signal{\psi}_n^{(-)}(x)&\propto\sin\left(\frac{2k\pi x}{L}\right),&
n&=2k,\quad k=1,2,\ldots.
\end{aligned}
$$

Odd $n$ gives even parity and even $n$ gives odd parity. Parity is conserved for an even potential, and the energy eigenstates can be chosen to have definite parity.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $x,L$ | $\unit m$ | Position and width | The coordinate and the distance between the walls. |
| $\energy V,\energy E_n$ | $\unit{J}$ | Potential and energy levels | The confinement potential and the discrete energy spectrum. |
| $\signal{\psi}_n$ | $\unit{m^{-1/2}}$ | Eigenfunction | The normalized standing wave of level $n$. |
| $\signal k$ | $\unit{m^{-1}}$ | Wavenumber | The spatial frequency inside the well. |
| $n$ | $1$ | Mode number | The positive integer that selects a level. |
| $\mechanical m$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Mass | The particle mass. |
| $\mechanical{\hbar}$ | $\unit{J\,s}$ | Reduced Planck constant | The constant in the energy quantization. |
| $\Delta x,\Delta\mechanical p$ | $\unit m,\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Position and momentum uncertainties | The standard deviations in the ground-state energy estimate. |
| $P$ | $1$ | Probability | The probability of finding the particle in the left half of the well. |
