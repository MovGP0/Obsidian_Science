---
title: "Effective Equation for uₙₖ"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Inserting the Bloch form into the Schrödinger equation shifts the momentum operator by $\mechanical{\hbar}k$ in the equation on one cell.

Substitute $\signal\psi=e^{ikx}\signal u$ into $\energy{\hat H}\signal\psi=\energy E\signal\psi$. With $\mechanical{\hat p}=-i\mechanical{\hbar}\,\mathrm d/\mathrm dx$, the periodic factor satisfies

$$
\left[
\frac{\mechanical{\hat p}^2}{2\mechanical m}
+\energy V(\hat x)
+\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}k\mechanical{\hat p}}{\mechanical m}
+\frac{\mechanical{\hbar}^2 k^2}{2\mechanical m}
\right]\signal u_{nk}(x)=\energy E_n(k)\signal u_{nk}(x).
\tag{19.4}
$$

This operator acts on periodic functions of period $a$, with the inner product taken over one cell. For each fixed $k$, the operator on this separable Hilbert space has discrete spectrum. Its eigenfunctions $\{\signal u_{nk}\}$ are orthonormal on the cell at fixed $k$, with corresponding energies $\{\energy E_n(k)\}$. The explicit dependence on $k$ is the starting point of $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf p$ expansions. An isolated band typically depends analytically on $k$ locally.

> [!note] Remark 19.1 · Smooth bands and symmetry points
> The ordered band energy $\energy E_n(k)$ is continuous and $2\pi/a$-periodic. For the real scalar potential considered here, it is even in $k$. When the band is differentiable at $k=0$ or $k=\pm\pi/a$, symmetry makes its derivative zero there. A band touching can invalidate the differentiability assumption.
