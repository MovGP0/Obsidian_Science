---
title: "Bands in Periodic Potentials Exercises"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Use Bloch periodicity, band dispersion, and the Kronig–Penney condition to calculate band properties.

1. For a one-dimensional lattice with $a=\quantity{4}{Å}$, find the width of the first Brillouin zone and the wave number at its right edge.  
   **Result.** The width is $2\pi/a\approx\quantity{1.571}{Å^{-1}}$. The right edge is $k=\pi/a\approx\quantity{0.785}{Å^{-1}}$.
2. Show explicitly that $\signal\psi_{nk}(x+a)=e^{ika}\signal\psi_{nk}(x)$ for every Bloch state.  
   **Result.** $e^{ik(x+a)}\signal u_{nk}(x+a)=e^{ika}e^{ikx}\signal u_{nk}(x)$ by periodicity.
3. Sketch $f(q)=\cos(qa)+(\mechanical m\alpha/\mechanical{\hbar}^2q)\sin(qa)$ for the Kronig–Penney model and identify the allowed bands.  
   **Result.** Bands occur where $\lvert f\rvert\le1$ and gaps occur where $\lvert f\rvert>1$. The $1/q$ term narrows the first band in wave number.
4. In the nearly free limit, neglect $\energy V$ and estimate the energy at $k=\pi/a$.  
   **Result.** $\energy E\simeq\mechanical{\hbar}^2(\pi/a)^2/(2\mechanical m)=\mechanical{\hbar}^2\pi^2/(2\mechanical m a^2)$.
5. For $\energy E(k)=\energy E_0-2\energy t\cos(ka)$ with $\energy t>0$, find the minimum, maximum, and bandwidth.  
   **Result.** The minimum is $\energy E_0-2\energy t$ at $k=0$. The maximum is $\energy E_0+2\energy t$ at $k=\pm\pi/a$. The width is $4\energy t$.
6. Solve the Kronig–Penney condition numerically for the bottom of the first band, where $f(q)=+1$, at $\mechanical m\alpha a/\mechanical{\hbar}^2=1.5$.  
   **Result.** The lower edge is at $qa\approx1.543$, with $\energy E\approx1.19\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(\mechanical m a^2)$. The upper edge is exactly $qa=\pi$, where $f(q)=-1$. The source gives $qa\approx1.542$ for the lower edge.
7. For the tight-binding dispersion $\energy E(k)=\energy E_0-2\energy t\cos(ka)$, calculate the effective mass at the bottom of the first band.  
   **Result.** $1/\mechanical m^*=\left.\partial_k^2\energy E\right\rvert_0/\mechanical{\hbar}^2=2\energy t a^2/\mechanical{\hbar}^2$, so $\mechanical m^*=\mechanical{\hbar}^2/(2\energy t a^2)$.
8. Estimate the nearly free electron gap at $k=\pi/a$ for $\energy V(x)=\energy V_0\cos(2\pi x/a)$.  
   **Result.** $\energy V_{2\pi/a}=\energy V_0/2$, so $\energy\Delta=2\lvert\energy V_{2\pi/a}\rvert=\lvert\energy V_0\rvert$.
9. For $\energy E(k)=\mechanical{\hbar}^2k^2/(2\mechanical m^*)$ on $[-\pi/a,\pi/a)$ with $\mechanical m^*>0$, calculate the density of states per unit length for energies inside the band. Include both spin states.  
   **Result.** $g(\energy E)=(2/\pi)\sqrt{\mechanical m^*/(2\mechanical{\hbar}^2\energy E)}$.
10. Count the states in one band for a crystal of length $L=Na$ with periodic boundary conditions.  
    **Result.** $N$ orbital states or $2N$ states including spin.
11. In the limit $\alpha\to+\infty$ of the Kronig–Penney model, derive the tight-binding dispersion $\energy E(k)=\energy E_0-2\energy t\cos(ka)$ for the first band.  
    **Result.** Linearizing Eq. (19.5) about $qa=\pi$ gives $\energy t\simeq\mechanical{\hbar}^4\pi^2/(2\mechanical m^2a^3\alpha)$. The source gives $\energy E_0\simeq\mechanical{\hbar}^2\pi^2/(2\mechanical m a^2)$ at leading order. At the same order as the hopping term, the band center lies $2\energy t$ below that isolated-well energy.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $a,L$ | $\unit{m}$ | Lattice period and crystal length | The model uses $L=Na$. |
| $k,q$ | $\unit{rad\,m^{-1}}$ | Wave numbers | The Bloch wave number and free wave number between barriers. |
| $\alpha$ | $\unit{J\,m}$ | Delta strength | The coefficient of each repulsive barrier. |
| $\energy E_0,\energy t,\energy\Delta$ | $\unit{J}$ | Band center, hopping energy, and gap | The scales of the tight-binding dispersion and the Bragg gap. |
| $\mechanical m,\mechanical m^*$ | $\unit{kg}$ | Bare and effective mass | The particle mass and the mass inferred from band curvature. |
| $g(\energy E)$ | $\unit{J^{-1}\,m^{-1}}$ | Density of states | The number per unit energy and length, including spin in Exercise 9. |
