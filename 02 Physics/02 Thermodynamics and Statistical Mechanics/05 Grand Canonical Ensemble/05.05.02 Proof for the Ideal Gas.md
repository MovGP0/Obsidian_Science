> [!TLDR]
> Eliminating fugacity makes the ideal-gas grand potential reproduce canonical thermodynamics.

For the canonical classical ideal gas:

$$
Z_N=\frac1{{\mechanical{N}}!}\left(\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}\right)^{\mechanical{N}},
\qquad
{\energy{F}}\simeq-{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\left[\ln\left(\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}\right)+1\right].
$$

The free-energy expression uses Stirling's approximation. The grand canonical calculation gives:

$$
\ln\Xi=\frac{z{\mechanical{V}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3},\qquad
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}z}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3},\qquad
\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle=\frac{z{\mechanical{V}}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}.
$$

Eliminate fugacity with $z=\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3/{\mechanical{V}}$:

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=\frac{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}},\qquad
{\energy{\Phi}}=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}=-\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}.
$$

Since ${\energy{\mu}}={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln z$:

$$
{\energy{\Phi}}+{\energy{\mu}}\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle
=-\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle {\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\left[\ln\left(\frac {\mechanical{V}}{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3}\right)+1\right].
$$

This is the canonical free energy at ${\mechanical{N}}=\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle$, to the same thermodynamic accuracy. The equation of state agrees as well.

The comparison has two approximation layers. The translational canonical function is the classical continuum result; replacing its factorial by Stirling's leading terms then gives the extensive free energy. The exponential grand sum can be performed without that second approximation. At a matched integer mean count, the canonical expression still contains the subleading factorial contribution, so an exact finite-size equality of free energies is not claimed.

Pressure provides a separate check: differentiating the canonical logarithm with respect to volume gives particle count times thermal energy divided by volume, which agrees with the grand canonical pressure at matched mean count. Thus pressure can agree already within the finite-count classical model, while the transformed free energies agree only to the stated extensive accuracy. Neither agreement proves equality of count fluctuations.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Particle number | Actual or mean number; subscripts identify the subsystem. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | Volume of the specified system. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}_T$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal de Broglie wavelength | Thermal wavelength of a particle; a scale factor of the same letter is not a wavelength. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | Converts temperature to energy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature. |
| ${\energy{F}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | Thermodynamic potential at fixed temperature and volume. |
| ${\energy{\mu}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Chemical potential | Energy per added particle; subscript or superscript identifies species or reference state. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | Bulk or partial gas pressure, as indicated. |
| ${\energy{\Phi}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Grand potential | Helmholtz free energy minus chemical work. |
| $z$ | $\unit{1}$ | Fugacity | Chosen to match the canonical particle density. |
