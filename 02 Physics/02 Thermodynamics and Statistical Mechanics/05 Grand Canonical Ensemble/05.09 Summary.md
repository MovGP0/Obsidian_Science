> [!TLDR]
> The grand canonical ensemble describes equilibrium with energy and particle exchange.

The main results of this chapter are:

1. **Chemical potential:** ${\energy{\mu}}=(\partial {\energy{U}}/\partial {\mechanical{N}})_{S,V}$ controls matter equilibrium. For the classical monatomic ideal gas, ${\energy{\mu}}={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln({\mechanical{n}}{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3)<0$ in the dilute regime.
2. **Grand canonical distribution:** $p_r=\Xi^{-1}e^{-\beta({\energy{E}}_r-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}}_r)}$.
3. **Partition function and grand potential:** $\Xi=\sum_{N,r}e^{-\beta({\energy{E}}_r-{\energy{\mu}} {\mechanical{N}})}$ and ${\energy{\Phi}}=-{\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\ln\Xi$. For a homogeneous extensive bulk system, ${\energy{\Phi}}=-{\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}$.
4. **Fluctuations:** $(\Delta {\mechanical{N}})^2={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}(\partial\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle/\partial{\energy{\mu}})_{T,V}$. Equivalently, $(\Delta {\mechanical{N}})^2={\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}}\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle^2{\mechanical{\kappa}}_T/{\mechanical{V}}$. Away from singular states, relative fluctuations decrease as $1/\sqrt{\langle {\mechanical{N}}\rangle}$.
5. **Ensemble equivalence:** the microcanonical, canonical, and grand canonical descriptions give the same bulk thermodynamics in the usual additive thermodynamic limit.
6. **Ideal gas:** $\Xi=\exp(z{\mechanical{V}}/{\signal{\lambda}}_T^3)$ and particle number has a Poisson distribution.
7. **Langmuir adsorption:** $\theta={\mechanical{P}}/({\mechanical{P}}+{\mechanical{P}}_0)$ gives the Henry limit at low pressure and saturation at high pressure.
8. **Chemical equilibrium:** $K_P=\exp[-\Delta {\energy{G}}^\circ/({\thermal{k}_{B}} {\thermal{T}})]$ relates partial pressures to standard chemical potentials.

The next chapter applies the grand canonical distribution to quantum particles. The Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein distributions follow from the allowed occupations of their quantum states.

## Reconstruct the argument

Start with an isolated composite, not with a probability formula. Conservation assigns the reservoir energy and particle count left over from each subsystem microstate. The first-order reservoir entropy change gives its relative weight. Normalizing over every allowed microstate and particle-number sector gives the grand partition function. Its logarithmic derivatives then give means and responses; the variance tests whether the mean is representative.

Three checks prevent common errors. At fixed particle number, chemical-potential factors cancel from microstate probability ratios. For the classical ideal gas, the number variance equals the mean. For independent adsorption sites, the variance vanishes at complete occupation because a site cannot accept another molecule. These different fluctuation laws come from different allowed states, not from a change in the reservoir weighting.

The summary assumes equilibrium and weak reservoir coupling. The pressure identity additionally requires homogeneous bulk extensivity, and the small-relative-fluctuation estimate requires finite response at fixed density. Keep those assumptions attached to the results when using the next chapter's quantum distributions.
