> [!TLDR]
> A weak magnetic field slightly shifts the two electron spin populations and produces Pauli paramagnetism.

The question is why conduction-electron spin response stays finite at zero temperature instead of following the inverse-temperature Curie law for independent localized spins. A weak field lowers one spin branch and raises the other. Exclusion permits only a small transfer near the occupied boundary, so the density of states there determines the response.

Follow the signs in the population note before taking the weak-field limit. Electron spin and electron magnetic moment point in opposite directions. This section isolates the spin response of noninteracting itinerant electrons. It does not include orbital magnetism or exchange interactions, so its susceptibility is a component of a metal's response rather than a prediction of every contribution.
