> [!TLDR]
> The Sommerfeld expansion gives controlled low-temperature corrections to a degenerate Fermi gas.

At finite temperature, the Fermi surface is broadened over an energy interval of order ${\thermal{k}}_B{\thermal{T}}$.

The main constraint is unchanged particle density. Heating creates occupied states above the old boundary and vacancies below it. Since the number of available states changes with energy, these two changes do not in general cancel at the old chemical potential. Determine the new chemical potential from the number integral before evaluating the energy.

Use the expansion only when the thermal width is small compared with the Fermi energy and the energy weight is smooth across that width. It is a local expansion near the occupation boundary, not a high-temperature formula. Near the classical regime, use the full Fermi integrals instead. The final checks are recovery of the zero-temperature energy and a positive energy increase on heating at fixed density.
