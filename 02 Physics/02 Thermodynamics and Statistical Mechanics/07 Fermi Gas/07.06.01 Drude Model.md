> [!TLDR]
> The Drude model relates electrical conductivity to electron density and a relaxation time.

The Drude model (1900) treats conduction electrons as a classical gas of free particles that undergo scattering, represented by a mean relaxation time ${\semiconductor{\tau}}$. Despite its classical assumptions, it gives a useful form for electrical conductivity:

$$
{\electric{\sigma}}=\frac{{\mechanical{n}}{\electric{e}}^2{\semiconductor{\tau}}}{{\mechanical{m}}_e}.
$$

The conductivity follows by balancing the electric force on an electron against loss of its mean drift momentum over the relaxation time. Because the electron charge is negative, its drift is opposite to the field. Conventional current has the opposite direction to electron motion and therefore points along the field. These two signs give the positive squared charge in the conductivity.

The relaxation time describes decay of directed momentum, not necessarily the time between all microscopic collisions. Small-angle scattering illustrates the distinction: it can change a state while removing little directed momentum. [Manfra et al., Quantum and transport lifetimes, Applied Physics Letters 85 (2004)](https://manfragroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Quantum-and-transport-lifetimes-in-a-tunable-low-density-AlGaN-GaN-two-dimensional-electron-gas1.pdf) gives the separate angular weights for these lifetimes. The displayed result assumes a weak steady field, a uniform isotropic carrier gas, and a single relaxation time. With no momentum relaxation, this model has no finite steady drift: the divergence as the relaxation time increases signals the loss of its stationary assumption.

Here ${\mechanical{n}}$ is the conduction-electron density and ${\electric{e}}$ is the positive magnitude of the electron charge. For copper at room temperature, the source gives ${\electric{\sigma}}\approx6\times10^7\,\unit{S\,m^{-1}}$. This corresponds to ${\semiconductor{\tau}}\approx2.5\times10^{-14}\,\unit{s}$.

The simple relaxation-time form is retained in the Sommerfeld model, while the equilibrium state and relevant velocities are treated with Fermi statistics.
