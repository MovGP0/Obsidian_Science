> [!TLDR]
> The zero-temperature density of states gives the total energy and degeneracy pressure.

The density of states answers a counting question: how many complete single-particle states occur in a small energy interval? At zero temperature, integrate this count up to the Fermi energy to recover the particle density. Weight the same count by energy to obtain the internal energy. This common normalization is essential; a density of states per volume must not be combined with a total particle number without the volume factor.

Read the three notes in order. The pressure calculation then varies volume while holding particle number fixed, so the Fermi energy changes during compression. Holding the Fermi energy fixed instead would describe a different constraint and would give the wrong ground-state pressure. The resulting positive pressure is a response to compression, even though the temperature is zero.
