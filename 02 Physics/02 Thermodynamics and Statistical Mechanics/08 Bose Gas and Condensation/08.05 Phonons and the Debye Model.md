> [!TLDR]
> The Debye model treats crystal vibrations as bosonic modes and explains the low-temperature cubic law for heat capacity.

Phonons are the excitation quanta of the normal vibration modes of a crystal. Their Bose–Einstein occupation determines the thermal energy and heat capacity of the crystal.

This section develops the Debye model, its temperature limits, and its comparison with the Einstein model:

Follow the distinction between atoms and excitations throughout this section. The atom number fixes the number of vibration modes, but it does not fix the number of phonons. Start with one normal mode as a quantum oscillator, then build the Debye mode density and impose its total mode count. Only after that step should the thermal occupation be integrated.

The high-temperature check counts all oscillator degrees of freedom. The low-temperature check probes the available long-wavelength modes, which is why it distinguishes the Debye continuum from a single Einstein frequency. Use the tabulated Debye temperatures to compare temperature scales, and retain the model boundary: this calculation gives the lattice contribution at fixed volume, not every contribution to measured solid heat capacity.
