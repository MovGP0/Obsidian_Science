> [!TLDR]
> A photon gas gives the Planck spectrum, blackbody emission, and radiation pressure.

Photons are bosons whose number can change when the cavity walls emit or absorb radiation. Their equilibrium distribution gives blackbody radiation.

The atomic-gas calculation fixed the atom number and solved for chemical potential. Here the walls can create or absorb photons, so equilibrium fixes the chemical potential instead. Begin with this change of constraint before using the mode count. Then follow the spectrum to its integral, its peak, and its momentum flux; these are different measurable aspects of the same radiation field.

Use the spectral variable stated by a detector. Energy per frequency interval and energy per wavelength interval have different peak locations. Also distinguish energy stored in a cavity from power leaving a surface: the latter requires an angular-flux factor. This model assumes thermal equilibrium, isotropy inside a large cavity, and a continuum of modes; a narrow driven optical mode need not have a Planck population.
