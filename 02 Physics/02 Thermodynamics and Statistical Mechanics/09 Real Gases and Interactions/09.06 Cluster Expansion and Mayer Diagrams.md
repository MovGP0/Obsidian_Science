> [!TLDR]
> Mayer graphs organize the configurational integral and connect molecular interactions to virial coefficients.

Graph edges represent Mayer functions, and graph nodes represent molecules.

The purpose of a diagram is to organize integrals, not to show a molecular path or a permanent chemical bond. Begin with the product expansion to see why each edge can occur only once. Then compare disconnected pairs, chains, and triangles. The cluster-integral convention fixes the numerical factors before the density virial coefficients are formed.

The final cancellation is the key check: a connected three-node chain belongs to a cluster integral but cancels from the third density virial coefficient. Counting only connected graphs is therefore not enough. The ideal-gas limit is even simpler: with zero potential, every edge weight vanishes and only the one-particle term remains.
