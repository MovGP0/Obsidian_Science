> [!TLDR]
> Throttling changes a real gas temperature at constant enthalpy; the sign depends on its state.

The Joule–Thomson effect is used in gas liquefaction.

Begin with the flow balance. A valve has pressure work at its inlet and outlet, so the conserved energy combination differs from that of an isolated gas expanding into a vacuum. The coefficient note then converts this constant-enthalpy condition into a local temperature response. Only after this step is it useful to insert the van der Waals or virial equation of state.

The inversion temperature separates cooling and heating for a small pressure decrease. It does not by itself guarantee liquefaction: a finite flow path must reach a two-phase state, and the apparatus must remove compression heat and recover cooling. Read the application after the coefficient so that each stage of the cycle has a stated energy-transfer role.
