> [!TLDR]
> The second virial coefficient gives the leading density correction to the ideal-gas law.

This coefficient is particularly useful for dilute real gases.

The main question is how the accessible configurations of a pair change the pressure of a dilute gas. Start with the radial integral to identify the positive core and negative attractive contributions. Then expand the van der Waals equation and compare its coefficient with the microscopic result. This comparison tests the meaning of its two parameters without assuming that the complete mean-field equation is exact.

Finish with the Boyle temperature, where the two contributions cancel at leading density order. This is a useful boundary test: ideal leading pressure does not imply ideal microscopic structure. The square-well exercise gives a fully analytic example of that cancellation; the Lennard–Jones section shows why a soft core changes the high-temperature limit.
