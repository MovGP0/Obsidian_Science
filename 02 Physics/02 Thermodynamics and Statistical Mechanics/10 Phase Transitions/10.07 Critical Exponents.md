> [!TLDR]
> Critical exponents describe power laws near a continuous transition. Scaling relations connect them.

Near a critical point, thermodynamic functions and correlation functions often follow power laws in the reduced temperature $t=(T-T_c)/T_c$. The following articles define the exponents, give their relations, and compare universality classes.

Critical exponents compare how different observables approach the same equilibrium critical point. First specify the path: zero-field temperature dependence and field dependence exactly at the critical temperature define different exponents. Then separate a singular contribution from any regular background and from material-dependent amplitudes.

Read the definitions before using the scaling identities. The identities are consistency tests under stated scaling assumptions, rather than six independent empirical laws. Use the universality table only after matching dimension, symmetry, interaction range, and observable definitions. Rounded source values need not satisfy each identity to all printed digits. A finite range that appears straight on logarithmic axes is suggestive but does not establish the asymptotic exponent without checks for crossover, rounding, and background terms.
