> [!TLDR]
> The molar van der Waals equation accounts for attraction and excluded volume.

This model asks how attraction and the finite size of molecules change an ideal-gas isotherm. The attractive correction lowers the pressure relative to the ideal-gas expression at a given molar volume. Excluded volume reduces the space available for motion and raises the repulsive contribution. Assume a uniform classical fluid and treat both corrections through bulk average parameters.

For a real fluid, the molar form is

$$
\left({\mechanical{P}}+\frac{{\energy{a}}}{{\mechanical{v}}^2}\right)({\mechanical{v}}-{\mechanical{b}})={\thermal{R}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

Here ${\energy{a}}>0$ represents attraction and ${\mechanical{b}}>0$ represents excluded volume. In this article, ${\mechanical{v}}$ is the molar volume and ${\thermal{R}}$ is the molar gas constant.

Introduce the reduced variables $\widetilde {\mechanical{P}}={\mechanical{P}}/{\mechanical{P}}_c$, $\widetilde {\mechanical{v}}={\mechanical{v}}/{\mechanical{v}}_c$, and $\widetilde {\thermal{T}}={\thermal{T}}/{\thermal{T}}_c$. The critical values are

$$
{\mechanical{P}}_c=\frac{{\energy{a}}}{27{\mechanical{b}}^2},\qquad {\mechanical{v}}_c=3{\mechanical{b}},\qquad {\thermal{T}}_c=\frac{8{\energy{a}}}{27{\thermal{R}}{\mechanical{b}}}.
$$

Substitution gives

$$
\left(\widetilde {\mechanical{P}}+\frac{3}{\widetilde {\mechanical{v}}^2}\right)
\left(\widetilde {\mechanical{v}}-\frac13\right)=\frac83\widetilde {\thermal{T}}.
\tag{10.4}
$$

See [[09.02.05 Critical Point]] for the derivative conditions that determine the critical values.

Solve the equation for pressure to check the signs: the thermal term uses molar volume minus excluded molar volume, while the attraction term is subtracted. The physical domain is molar volume greater than the excluded-volume parameter. At large volume both corrections become small and the ideal-gas equation is recovered. The attraction parameter divided by squared molar volume has pressure units, while gas constant times temperature has pressure–molar-volume units.

The critical values arise when the first and second volume derivatives of pressure vanish on one isotherm. Substituting the stated values gives unit reduced pressure, volume, and temperature in Eq. (10.4). This is an algebraic property of the model. It does not establish quantitative accuracy for a real fluid near criticality, and the subcritical uniform loop still requires the Maxwell coexistence construction.
