> [!TLDR]
> Six main critical exponents describe heat capacity, magnetization, susceptibility, and correlations.

These definitions describe limiting behavior, not complete equations of state. Use equilibrium bulk matter and select a symmetry-broken phase below the transition before removing its conjugate field. Each line then specifies a distinct path or separation limit. The field-power line is written for approach from positive field; negative field requires the corresponding symmetry-related sign of magnetization.

## Definition 10.7 — Critical exponents

Use the dimensionless reduced temperature $t=({\thermal{T}}-{\thermal{T}}_c)/{\thermal{T}}_c$. The main definitions are:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\thermal{C}}_H&\sim |t|^{-\alpha} &&({\energy{H}}=0),\\
{\magnetic{m}}&\sim(-t)^\beta &&({\energy{H}}=0,\ {\thermal{T}}\to {\thermal{T}}_c^-),\\
{\magnetic{\chi}}&\sim |t|^{-\gamma} &&({\energy{H}}=0),\\
{\magnetic{m}}&\sim {\energy{H}}^{1/\delta} &&({\thermal{T}}={\thermal{T}}_c),\\
{\mechanical{\xi}}&\sim |t|^{-\nu},\\
G({\mechanical{r}})&\sim {\mechanical{r}}^{-(d-2+\eta)} &&({\thermal{T}}={\thermal{T}}_c).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here ${\energy{H}}$ is the energy-like field conjugate to the dimensionless magnetization. The exponents $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\nu,\eta$ and the spatial dimension $d$ are dimensionless. In the last line, $G$ is a connected correlation function, not Gibbs free energy.

The amplitudes above and below the transition can differ. In the usual scaling description, the exponent $\alpha$ is the same on the two sides. A zero exponent can describe a finite jump or a logarithmic singularity. A negative $\alpha$ permits a finite heat-capacity cusp with a regular background.

The heat-capacity power law describes its singular part, with an analytic background added where needed. All proportionalities omit amplitudes with the necessary physical units. In particular, raising an energy-valued field to a fractional power does not directly produce a dimensionless magnetization; its amplitude supplies the inverse energy power.

To reconstruct the definitions, ask which variable changes while the others are constrained. The magnetization temperature exponent uses zero field below the transition. The critical-isotherm exponent uses a varying field at exactly the critical temperature. Confusing these paths gives different apparent powers. For a dimensionless order parameter, the connected correlation is also dimensionless; its distance power requires a microscopic scale in the omitted amplitude. No fitted dataset is supplied by these definitions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $G$ | $\unit{1}$ | Connected correlation | Correlation of a dimensionless order parameter at separated points. |
| ${\thermal{C}}_H$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity at constant field | Temperature response with the conjugate field held fixed. |
| ${\magnetic{m}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Reduced magnetization | Mean dimensionless Ising spin per site. |
| ${\energy{H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Ising conjugate field | Energy coefficient multiplying a dimensionless spin; not an SI field strength. |
| ${\magnetic{\chi}}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Reduced susceptibility | Response of a dimensionless order parameter to an energy-like field. |
| ${\mechanical{\xi}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Correlation length | Length over which fluctuations remain correlated. |
| ${\mechanical{r}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | Distance between the two points of a correlation function. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | Absolute temperature of the system. |
| ${\thermal{T}}_c$ | $\unit{K}$ | Critical temperature | Temperature of the stated phase transition. |
