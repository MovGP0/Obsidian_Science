> [!TLDR]
> An order parameter is zero in the disordered phase and nonzero in the ordered phase.

A candidate order parameter should answer a physical question: can it distinguish two equilibrium states that the relevant symmetry exchanges? For an Ising magnet, reversing every spin reverses the magnetization. Its sign therefore identifies which ordered phase has been selected, while its magnitude measures the strength of that order.

## Definition 10.4 — Order parameter

An **order parameter** ${\mechanical{\phi}}$ is a quantity that is zero in the disordered phase, usually at high temperature, and nonzero in the ordered phase, usually at low temperature. It transforms nontrivially under the relevant symmetry operations.

The order parameter can be:

- A real scalar: the magnetization of an Ising ferromagnet or the liquid–gas density difference.
- A real vector: the magnetization of a Heisenberg ferromagnet, with rotational symmetry.
- A complex scalar ${\mechanical{\Psi}}=|{\mechanical{\Psi}}|e^{i\theta}$: the condensate amplitude in a superfluid, with U(1) phase symmetry. A complex pairing amplitude also describes a superconductor.
- A tensor: the orientational order of a nematic liquid crystal.

The normalization and units depend on the system. The generic Landau model in this chapter uses a dimensionless scalar.

The zero-versus-nonzero rule assumes a suitable reference value and an appropriate symmetry-selected phase. In a finite sample with exactly symmetric boundary conditions, averaging equally over opposite ordered states can give zero. One must state whether the average describes one phase, all phases, or the magnitude of the order parameter.

The liquid–gas example requires special care: liquid and gas are not related by an exact microscopic spin-reversal operation. Their density difference is nevertheless a useful measure of coexistence and tends to zero when the phases merge. Thus the definition's symmetry language is the conventional symmetry-breaking case, not a test that every useful phase-distinguishing quantity must pass. Check a proposed normalization before comparing systems: a dimensionless Landau variable does not make the original density or magnetization dimensionless.
