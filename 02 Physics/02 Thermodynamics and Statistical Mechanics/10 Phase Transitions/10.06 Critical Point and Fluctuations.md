> [!TLDR]
> Large correlated fluctuations control the behavior near a critical point.

This section connects critical opalescence, response functions, correlation length, and the heat-capacity cusp.

A large response to a small perturbation suggests that the equilibrium state is easily rearranged. Near a critical point, correlated fluctuations connect this macroscopic sensitivity to microscopic structure. The argument assumes thermal equilibrium; a large fluctuation during a driven instability is not automatically a critical equilibrium fluctuation.

Read critical opalescence as a proposed optical window onto density correlations. Then use the response-function note to connect fluctuations to thermodynamic derivatives, and the correlation-function note to identify a length scale rather than only a fluctuation amplitude. The lambda-transition example tests a useful boundary: strong critical correlations do not require a divergent heat capacity. In finite samples the correlation length is bounded by the available size, and slow equilibration can further limit how closely an experiment approaches the bulk singularity.
