> [!TLDR]
> A renormalization step groups short-distance variables and rescales the system. Repeated steps define a flow.

A direct microscopic calculation tracks many fluctuations that are unimportant to a long-distance observer. Coarse graining instead averages over or integrates out short-distance degrees of freedom, while retaining their effects in the interactions of the remaining variables. The goal is to preserve long-wavelength predictions, not each microscopic configuration.

At a critical point, the correlation length diverges:

$$
{\mechanical{\xi}}\to\infty.
$$

There is no finite characteristic correlation length. The long-distance description becomes invariant under changes of scale.

The renormalization-group procedure has three steps:

1. **Group variables.** Combine degrees of freedom on distances smaller than a scale factor $b>1$. An example is the decimation of spins on an Ising lattice.
2. **Rescale.** Change lengths according to $\mathbf {\mechanical{r}}\mapsto\mathbf {\mechanical{r}}/b$. Rescale the order-parameter field according to ${\mechanical{\phi}}\mapsto b^\Delta{\mechanical{\phi}}$, with a suitable scaling dimension $\Delta$.
3. **Repeat.** The transformed system has a new effective Hamiltonian. Repeated transformations define a flow in the space of Hamiltonians. A critical system approaches a critical fixed point.

Here $b$ and $\Delta$ are mathematical scaling parameters. They are not the quartic Landau coefficient or the superconducting gap.

The scale factor is dimensionless: on a lattice, one groups over a region measured in units of the current lattice spacing, then rescales the spacing back to the reference value. Integrating out variables generally generates additional symmetry-allowed interactions, so an exact flow acts in a space of Hamiltonians broader than the original nearest-neighbor form. Truncating that space is an approximation.

A finite bulk correlation length shrinks in rescaled coordinates after a step, whereas an infinite critical length remains infinite. This gives a useful check on why a critical description can approach a scale-invariant fixed point. The transformed parameters label descriptions at different resolution, not successive times of a relaxing sample. A blocking calculation can test internal consistency, but it is not independent experimental evidence for a material's universality class.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{\xi}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Correlation length | Length over which fluctuations remain correlated. |
| ${\mechanical{r}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Separation | Distance between the two points of a correlation function. |
| ${\mechanical{\phi}}$ | $\unit{1}$ | Order parameter | Dimensionless normalized order parameter used in the generic Landau model. |
