> [!TLDR]
> The Maxwell construction replaces the unstable loop by liquid–gas coexistence at constant pressure.

A fluid with fixed temperature and amount can lower its free energy by separating into phases when the uniform branch is unstable or lies above the appropriate mixture free energy. The construction selects bulk liquid–gas equilibrium and neglects interface energy. It determines a coexistence pressure, not a nucleation barrier or a transition rate.

For ${\thermal{T}}<{\thermal{T}}_c$, the uncorrected van der Waals isotherm is not monotonic. Its segment with positive pressure–volume slope has negative isothermal compressibility and is mechanically unstable.

The equilibrium isotherm contains a horizontal coexistence segment. Its pressure satisfies the equal-area condition

$$
\int_{v_L}^{{\mechanical{v}}_G}{\mechanical{P}}({\mechanical{v}},{\thermal{T}})\,d{\mechanical{v}}={\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{coex}}({\mechanical{v}}_G-{\mechanical{v}}_L).
$$

The endpoints are the coexisting liquid and gas molar volumes. This construction is equivalent to equality of the chemical potentials:

$$
{\energy{\mu}}_L({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{coex}})={\energy{\mu}}_G({\thermal{T}},{\mechanical{P}}_{\mathrm{coex}}).
$$

See [[09.02.06 Maxwell Construction]] for the thermodynamic proof, and [[10.04.04 P–V Diagram with Isotherms]] for the equilibrium isotherms.

The pressure integral is the negative change of molar Helmholtz free energy along an isotherm. Adding coexistence pressure times the molar-volume change converts this into the molar Gibbs free-energy difference between the endpoints. The equal-area condition sets that difference to zero, which explains the equality of chemical potentials rather than merely prescribing a graphical rule. Both sides have energy-per-mole units.

The plateau replaces more than the strictly positive-slope interval: it also excludes uniform metastable states between coexistence endpoints and spinodals from the global equilibrium branch. The positive-slope region itself has negative compressibility and is not locally stable. A volume between the plateau endpoints represents a mixture with changing phase fractions; it is not an additional homogeneous phase.
