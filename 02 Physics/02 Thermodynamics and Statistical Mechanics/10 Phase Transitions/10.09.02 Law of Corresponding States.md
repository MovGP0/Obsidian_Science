> [!TLDR]
> All van der Waals fluids obey the same equation in reduced variables.

Why can rescaling collapse different model fluids onto one curve? Each van der Waals fluid has only two material parameters, and its own critical pressure, volume, and temperature absorb them. After substitution, the remaining relation is the same dimensionless equation. This is an exact parameter reduction within the stated equation of state, rather than an exact empirical law for all substances.

Equation (10.4) contains no parameters specific to a gas. Fluids at the same reduced pressure, volume, and temperature are in corresponding states. This is the **law of corresponding states** within the van der Waals model.

The model predicts a universal reduced equation of state. For real substances, corresponding-states behavior is approximate. Microscopic details and additional molecular parameters can cause deviations.

A useful reconstruction check is to choose the same reduced temperature and volume for two model fluids. Their reduced pressures must agree, although their pressures in pascals and their molar volumes need not. A comparison with measured reduced isotherms would test how closely actual fluids follow that model; no such data collapse is shown here.

Corresponding states and critical universality are different claims. The first compares an entire reduced equation of state within a chosen model. The second concerns leading singular behavior sufficiently close to a critical point, and can apply to systems that do not share a van der Waals equation away from it. Molecular structure or quantum effects can spoil a broad corresponding-states collapse without disproving a local critical universality assignment.
