> [!TLDR]
> Landau theory describes a transition with a free-energy expansion in the order parameter.

The theory gives a simple connection between symmetry, equilibrium, and critical behavior. It neglects spatial fluctuations. Its critical exponents are mean-field values.


Landau theory asks how much transition behavior follows from symmetry and stability before solving a microscopic model. Choose a small order parameter and an analytic free-energy expansion that respects the unbroken symmetries. Then identify equilibrium by the global minimum at the imposed controls. A stationary point alone can be a maximum or a metastable state.

Read the free-energy note first to fix coefficient units and the symmetry restriction on odd powers. Compare the positive-quartic case with the stabilized negative-quartic case to see why continuity depends on the shape of the energy landscape. Use the exponent note last, after understanding which minimum is followed. The calculation predicts mean-field behavior; it supplies a model to compare with measured order parameters and response functions, while critical spatial fluctuations can invalidate its asymptotic powers.
