> [!TLDR]
> Liouville's theorem states that phase-space density is constant along Hamiltonian trajectories.

Liouville's theorem is a central result of analytical mechanics for statistical mechanics. In simple terms, the density of an ensemble of phase-space points is conserved as those points move along Hamiltonian trajectories.

The theorem answers how an ensemble is transported, including away from equilibrium. It does not choose the initial density. Read the probability-density definition first so that the conserved object is clear, then follow the continuity-equation proof. The geometric interpretation describes a moving region of possible states; the consequences explain which information is preserved.

The essential condition is Hamiltonian flow in canonical coordinates. An effective equation with friction can contract the reduced phase space because degrees of freedom that absorb energy have been omitted. This does not contradict a theorem applied to the larger Hamiltonian system. Do not infer relaxation toward a uniform density from incompressibility alone.
