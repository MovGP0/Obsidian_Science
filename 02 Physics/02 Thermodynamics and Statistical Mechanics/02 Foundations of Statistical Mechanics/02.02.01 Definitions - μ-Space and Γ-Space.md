> [!TLDR]
> μ-space describes one particle; Γ-space describes the complete system.

For ${\mechanical{N}}$ point particles in three dimensions, the microscopic state is specified by $3{\mechanical{N}}$ generalized coordinates ${\mechanical{q}}_1,\ldots,{\mechanical{q}}_{3{\mechanical{N}}}$ and $3{\mechanical{N}}$ canonically conjugate momenta ${\mechanical{p}}_1,\ldots,{\mechanical{p}}_{3{\mechanical{N}}}$.

## Definition 2.1 — μ-space

**μ-space**, or single-particle phase space, is the space $\mathbb R^6$ of the three coordinate–momentum pairs $({\mechanical{q}}_i,{\mechanical{p}}_i)$ of one particle, with $i=1,2,3$.

## Definition 2.2 — Γ-space

**Γ-space** is the phase space of the complete system:

$$
\Gamma=\{({\mechanical{q}}_1,\ldots,{\mechanical{q}}_{3{\mechanical{N}}};{\mechanical{p}}_1,\ldots,{\mechanical{p}}_{3{\mechanical{N}}})\}.
$$

For unconstrained Cartesian point particles, this is $\mathbb R^{6{\mechanical{N}}}$. One point in this space specifies a complete microscopic state.

For ${\mechanical{N}}=10^{23}$ monatomic particles:

$$
\dim\Gamma=6\times10^{23}.
$$

For a gas in a container, not every point of the ambient space is allowed. Positions must lie inside the container, and a fixed total energy restricts the momenta and positions jointly. The full coordinate count describes the space before these restrictions; the accessible set can have lower dimension.

Single-particle information also does not in general specify the complete statistical state. Two particles can each have the same position and momentum distribution in two preparations but have different correlations between them. Γ-space retains these joint relations. A description using only a distribution in μ-space needs additional assumptions before it can replace the complete ensemble description.

We cannot draw this space directly. Its mathematical description still permits precise and useful statements.

> [!note]
> The coordinate and momentum units in the table below are for Cartesian coordinates. Generalized coordinates can have other units; each conjugate product has the unit of action.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\mechanical{q}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Position coordinate | A Cartesian coordinate; generalized coordinates can have different units. |
| ${\mechanical{p}}$ | $\unit{kg\,m\,s^{-1}}$ | Momentum | Momentum conjugate to a Cartesian position coordinate. |
