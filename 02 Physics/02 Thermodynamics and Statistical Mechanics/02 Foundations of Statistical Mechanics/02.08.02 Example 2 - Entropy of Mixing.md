> [!TLDR]
> Mixing two different ideal gases increases entropy because each species gains accessible position states.

## Example 2.2 — Entropy of mixing of ideal gases

Consider two different ideal gases with particle counts ${\mechanical{N}}_A$ and ${\mechanical{N}}_B$. A partition initially separates them in a container of total volume ${\mechanical{V}}$. Their initial volumes are:

$$
{\mechanical{V}}_A=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}_A {\mechanical{V}}}{{\mechanical{N}}_A+{\mechanical{N}}_B},
\qquad
{\mechanical{V}}_B=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}_B {\mechanical{V}}}{{\mechanical{N}}_A+{\mechanical{N}}_B}.
$$

Take both gases at the same temperature ${\thermal{T}}$. These volume ratios then also give equal initial pressures. Remove the partition. The gases mix irreversibly when the species are distinguishable.

> [!note] Symbol convention
> Here the subscripts A and B identify the gas species. The symbol ${\mechanical{N}}_A$ is a particle count, not the Avogadro constant used in [[02.01.01 The Scale Problem]].

The initial equal-temperature, equal-pressure preparation isolates composition mixing from other macroscopic changes. For ideal gases, internal energy depends on temperature rather than volume. In an isolated rigid container, removal of the partition therefore leaves the equilibrium temperature unchanged, and the momentum-state factors cancel in the entropy difference. The extra count comes from the newly available positions.

This calculation compares equilibrium endpoints. It does not supply the rate of interdiffusion. To relate the positive entropy change to thermodynamics, compare with a reversible separation or mixing path using ideal species-selective membranes. Such membranes distinguish the two physical species. Artificial labels on identical particles do not provide that distinction.

## Count the extra position states

Ideal-gas particles do not interact. The number of position microstates for gas A is proportional to ${\mechanical{V}}_A^{{\mechanical{N}}_A}$ initially and ${\mechanical{V}}^{{\mechanical{N}}_A}$ after mixing. Thus:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta {\thermal{S}}_A
&={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\frac{{\mechanical{V}}^{{\mechanical{N}}_A}}{{\mechanical{V}}_A^{{\mechanical{N}}_A}}\\
&={\mechanical{N}}_A{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\mechanical{V}}_A}\\
&={\mechanical{N}}_A{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln\frac{{\mechanical{N}}_A+{\mechanical{N}}_B}{{\mechanical{N}}_A}\\
&=-{\mechanical{N}}_A{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln x_A,
\end{aligned}
$$

where $x_A={\mechanical{N}}_A/({\mechanical{N}}_A+{\mechanical{N}}_B)$ is the mole fraction. Similarly, $\Delta {\thermal{S}}_B=-{\mechanical{N}}_B{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln x_B$.

The total mixing entropy is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta {\thermal{S}}_{\mathrm{mix}}
&=-{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}({\mechanical{N}}_A\ln x_A+{\mechanical{N}}_B\ln x_B)\\
&=-{\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{R}}(x_A\ln x_A+x_B\ln x_B)>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
$$

Here ${\mechanical{n}}={\mechanical{n}}_A+{\mechanical{n}}_B$ is the total amount of substance, and ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\mechanical{N}}_A={\mechanical{n}}_A{\thermal{R}}$, with the corresponding relation for species B.

Because $0<x_\alpha<1$, each logarithm is negative. Mixing therefore increases entropy, as expected from the second law.

Two limits check the composition formula. For equal fractions, each species gains a factor of two in its available volume, so the result is ${\mechanical{n}}{\thermal{R}}\ln2$. If one fraction tends to zero at fixed total amount, the mixing entropy tends to zero because $x\ln x\to0$. A mere change of the species names must leave the result unchanged, which the symmetric expression in Eq. (2.3) confirms.

## Gibbs paradox

If A and B are identical gases, there is no physical distinction between the species, and the entropy change for removal of this partition is zero. Applying Eq. (2.3) with artificial labels would still give a positive value.

The resolution is to count indistinguishable configurations correctly. Permutations of identical particles do not produce new states. The factorial correction in the state count removes the artificial mixing entropy in the thermodynamic description.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume occupied by the system. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | Boltzmann or Gibbs entropy, as specified by the context or subscript. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion factor between microscopic counting and thermodynamic entropy. |
