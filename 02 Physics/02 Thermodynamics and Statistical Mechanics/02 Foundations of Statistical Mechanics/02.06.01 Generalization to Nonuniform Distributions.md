> [!TLDR]
> Gibbs entropy describes distributions with unequal microstate probabilities.

The simple Boltzmann formula assumes equally likely microstates. A canonical system in contact with a heat reservoir has unequal probabilities, so a more general expression is needed.

The inputs are normalized, nonnegative probabilities for mutually exclusive microstates. These are probabilities of complete states, not just occupation probabilities of arbitrary macrostates. Grouping several microstates into one label changes what information the distribution retains.

For two allowed microstates, certainty in either state gives zero Gibbs entropy, whereas equal probabilities give ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln2$. This checks the minus sign and the convention for zero probability. A nonuniform pair gives an intermediate value. The Boltzmann entropy of the two-state macrostate still counts two compatible states; it does not become a different count merely because an ensemble favors one state. The two definitions coincide when that ensemble is uniform over the same allowed states.

## Definition 2.8 — Discrete Gibbs entropy

For an ensemble with probability $p_i$ of microstate $i$:

$$
{\thermal{S}}_G=-{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\sum_i p_i\ln p_i.
$$

Use the continuous limiting convention $0\ln0=0$.

> [!note] Remark 2.5 — Information entropy
> In information theory, Shannon entropy is $H=-\sum_i p_i\log_2p_i$, measured in bits. Thus ${\thermal{S}}_G={\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\ln2\,H$. Here $H$ is information entropy, not a Hamiltonian or enthalpy. This relation shows the information content of the Gibbs expression.
