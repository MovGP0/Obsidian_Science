> [!TLDR]
> Ergodicity is an additional dynamical property, not a consequence of having many particles.

The ergodic hypothesis has a difficult history. Four points help to define its scope.

1. **Not all systems are ergodic.** Integrable systems have many constants of motion. For a Liouville-integrable system, regular joint level sets of the independent commuting constants of motion are invariant. When such a joint level set is compact and connected, it is an invariant torus; see [Dunajski, Integrable Systems, Theorem 1.2.2, p. 9](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/md327/ISlecture_notes_2026.pdf). For the many-particle systems at issue, these tori have dimension smaller than the full energy surface. Noncompact or singular joint levels need not be tori. The Fermi–Pasta–Ulam–Tsingou numerical experiment, started in 1953, showed that anharmonic oscillator chains can fail to approach thermal equilibrium over long time intervals.

2. **KAM theorem.** The Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser theorem shows that many invariant tori survive sufficiently small perturbations of suitable integrable systems. This limits a simple claim that a small interaction must make a system ergodic.

3. **Practical validity.** Predictions of statistical mechanics agree with experiments in a very broad range of systems, despite the difficulty of proving the required dynamics in full generality. The source identifies the general explanation as an active research question.

4. **Other foundations.** Statistical mechanics can also be developed from a Bayesian maximum-entropy approach, associated with Jaynes, or from coarse-graining arguments associated with Gibbs and Boltzmann.

For a concrete obstruction, consider two uncoupled oscillators. Each oscillator keeps its own energy. A microcanonical average constrained only by their total energy includes energy partitions that a single trajectory never reaches. Even if a time average exists, it can retain information about the initial energy partition.

This example also shows what an interaction must accomplish before a thermal description becomes appropriate: it must permit the relevant energy exchange on the observation time scale. Merely adding a small coupling is insufficient evidence that this occurs rapidly. A useful reconstruction task is to identify which constants of motion remain after a proposed coupling is added and which observable would reveal slow exchange. No general relaxation rate follows from the equilibrium postulate alone.

> [!note] Scope clarification
> The source describes ergodicity as occurring above a perturbation threshold when chaos occupies almost all phase space. KAM theory does not supply one universal threshold of this kind. Chaotic motion alone does not prove ergodicity on the entire energy surface.

> [!note] Remark 2.3 — Mixing
> A stronger property is **mixing**. For suitable observables, the connected correlation tends to zero:
>
> $\langle A(0)B({\signal{t}})\rangle-\langle A\rangle\langle B\rangle\longrightarrow0$ as ${\signal{t}}\to\infty$.
>
> Mixing implies ergodicity; the converse does not hold. It supports relaxation of the observables covered by the mixing assumption. A general statement about thermalization also needs the system and its invariant measure to be specified.
