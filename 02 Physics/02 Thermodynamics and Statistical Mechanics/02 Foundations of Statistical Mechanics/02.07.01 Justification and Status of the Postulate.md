> [!TLDR]
> Equal a priori probabilities require assumptions beyond the mechanical equations alone.

The fundamental postulate cannot be proved from classical mechanics alone without additional assumptions. The source gives four complementary reasons for its use.

1. **Ergodic hypothesis.** If the system is ergodic, long-time averages equal averages over the energy surface with its invariant microcanonical measure. Equal weighting follows within this dynamical description.

2. **Maximum entropy.** As shown in [[02.06.03 Constrained Maximization - Derivation of the Boltzmann Distribution]], a uniform distribution maximizes Gibbs entropy over the allowed microstates when only normalization remains to be imposed. It represents the absence of further information beyond ${\energy{E}},{\mechanical{V}},{\mechanical{N}}$.

3. **Invariant measure.** Hamiltonian flow preserves the Liouville measure $\mathrm d\Gamma$. Equal weighting with respect to this measure is a natural choice when no additional information favors some accessible microstates.

4. **Experimental validation.** The predictions obtained with this postulate agree with experiments over a broad range of physical systems.

The four reasons have different roles. Ergodicity is a dynamical property for a specified invariant measure. Maximum entropy is an inference rule conditional on the chosen constraints and reference measure. Liouville invariance establishes consistency with Hamiltonian transport. Comparison with experiment tests predictions of the complete physical model, including its Hamiltonian and approximations; it does not test the probability postulate in isolation.

A useful test of an application is to prepare systems with the same claimed equilibrium constraints and compare a measurable response, such as pressure or heat capacity. Persistent preparation dependence can indicate an omitted slow variable or a failure to reach equilibrium. This is a proposed test; no measurements are supplied here. It also explains why the logical distinctions matter in practice.

> [!note] Uniqueness clarification
> The source describes Liouville measure as the only natural invariant measure. Invariance alone does not make it unique: for an autonomous Hamiltonian, a suitable weighted measure $f({\energy{\mathcal H}})\,\mathrm d\Gamma$ is also invariant. Choosing the microcanonical distribution therefore also specifies the energy constraint and the statistical assumptions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system or level energy; ΔE is an energy interval. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The physical volume occupied by the system. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | $1$ | Particle or oscillator count | The number of particles or distinguishable oscillators. |
| ${\energy{\mathcal H}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy function of the system or individual oscillator. |
| $\mathrm d\Gamma$ | $\unit{(J\,s)^{3N}}$ | Phase-space volume element | The product of all coordinate and conjugate-momentum intervals in three dimensions. |
