> [!TLDR]
> Oscillators, gas mixing, and a bounded spin system show how microstate counting produces thermodynamic results.

Use each example to test a different step in the foundations. The oscillator calculation tests the phase-space Jacobian and the classical energy scale. Mixing tests whether particle labels represent a physical distinction. The two-level system tests how the slope of entropy can change sign when the energy is bounded.

Before calculating, predict which change increases the compatible state count. More oscillator energy enlarges the allowed region; more accessible volume increases position states for each distinct gas; adding energy above equal spin populations reduces the spin multiplicity. The last case shows why a statement such as “entropy always increases with energy” requires a model and a regime. None of these equilibrium calculations by itself determines a mixing or thermalization time.
