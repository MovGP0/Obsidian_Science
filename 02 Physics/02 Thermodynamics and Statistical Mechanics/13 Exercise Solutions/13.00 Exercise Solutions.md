> [!TLDR]
> These worked solutions derive the results of the chapter exercises and check their units and physical limits.

This final chapter collects the full solutions of the exercises given at the ends of the preceding chapters. Each solution includes a short statement of the task, a step-by-step derivation, dimensional checks, and the relevant physical limits. A final comment explains the meaning of the result.

Use the first three collections to practise the choice of ensemble. An isolated system requires state counting at fixed energy. Thermal contact fixes temperature and permits energy fluctuations. A particle reservoir also permits particle-number fluctuations. Before each calculation, identify these exchanges and the variables held fixed in each derivative.

Then use the ideal quantum-gas and Bose-gas solutions to test where classical statistics fails. The interaction, Ising, and Landau solutions add a second choice: which correlations can the model retain? Compare an exact finite-system result with a thermodynamic-limit or mean-field result only after you identify the relevant limit. A smooth finite-system partition function alone does not establish a phase transition.

For independent study, stop after the task statement and predict the sign, units, and one limiting value of the answer. After the solution, change one assumption: allow particle exchange, reduce the dimension, or restore an interaction. State which step of the original argument then needs revision. This reconstruction tests model selection as well as algebra.
