> [!TLDR]
> Kadanoff's block-spin idea treats critical fluctuations by repeated changes of spatial scale.

The problem is that a single microscopic approximation must account for fluctuations of many sizes near criticality. Read the many-scale note for why this occurs, then the block-spin note for how a change in spatial resolution can isolate the large-distance behavior.

Blocking is a statistical operation on a model. It does not physically heat the sample or move its spins. Temperature-like couplings change because the effective description changes. The decisive check is whether correlations at retained distances are reproduced by the effective model.
