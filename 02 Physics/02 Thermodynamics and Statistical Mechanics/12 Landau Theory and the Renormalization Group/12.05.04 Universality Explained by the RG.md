> [!TLDR]
> Systems share critical exponents when their RG flows approach the same critical fixed point.

The RG explains **universality**. Systems with different microscopic structures, such as magnets, fluids near a critical point, and binary mixtures, can have the same critical exponents.

Critical behaviour is set by the fixed point approached along the critical manifold. The source identifies three main properties:

1. Spatial dimension $d$.
2. Order-parameter symmetry, such as $\mathbb Z_2$ for Ising or $O(n)$ for vector spins.
3. Interaction range: short range or long range.

Microscopic details include coupling strengths, lattice structure, and additional further-neighbour interactions.

In the RG description, these microscopic differences correspond to irrelevant operators that decay along the flow. Systems with the same dimension, symmetry, and interaction range can therefore belong to one universality class.

**Theorem 12.1 (Universality in the RG).** Two systems whose critical RG flows converge to the same fixed point have the same critical exponents and universal scaling function. They belong to the same universality class.

> [!note] Scope of the statement
> The comparison assumes the same fixed-point basin and the appropriate scaling fields. Universal scaling functions agree after nonuniversal metric factors and field normalizations are fixed. A microscopic change that leads to a different fixed point need not be irrelevant.

Universality does not require equal transition temperatures, equal magnetization amplitudes or identical microscopic correlations. Those can change when couplings or lattice scales change. The claim concerns asymptotic exponents and properly normalized scaling functions at the same type of critical point. It also does not identify relaxation rates: dynamics requires additional assumptions about conservation and evolution.

An operational test is to compare equilibrium data after each system's critical temperature and amplitude scales are determined. Agreement of exponent estimates and a common scaled curve supports the proposed class; a persistent difference may indicate crossover, long-range interactions or a different fixed point. Such a comparison is a test proposal here, not supplied experimental evidence.
