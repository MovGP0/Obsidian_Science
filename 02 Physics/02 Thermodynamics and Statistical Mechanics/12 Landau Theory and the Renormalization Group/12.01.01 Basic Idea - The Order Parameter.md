> [!TLDR]
> An order parameter is zero in the disordered phase and nonzero in the ordered phase.

Landau theory starts from a basic observation. At a continuous phase transition, a physical quantity called the **order parameter** distinguishes the disordered phase from the ordered phase.

The order parameter is zero above the critical temperature and nonzero below it.

**Definition 12.1 (Order parameter).** The thermodynamic quantity $\phi$ has these properties:

- It is zero in the high-symmetry phase, $\thermal{T}>\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$.
- It has a nontrivial value in the low-symmetry phase, $\thermal{T}<\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$.
- It is continuous at $\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$ for a second-order transition.

The zero value assumes the field conjugate to the order parameter is zero. An applied magnetic field can produce a nonzero magnetization above the transition, so magnetization alone would then not identify spontaneous order. For the Ising example, a finite zero-field sample that samples both orientations has zero mean spin even below the transition. Spontaneous magnetization refers to a phase selected by an arbitrarily weak field after the thermodynamic limit.

The listed order parameters have different units and symmetry properties. Their common feature is the distinction between phases, not a common microscopic meaning. This definition fits the conventional symmetry-breaking transitions used in Landau theory; it does not describe every possible phase transition.

Examples are:

- **Magnets:** magnetization $\magnetic{m}=\langle\magnetic{s}_i\rangle$.
- **Gas–liquid systems:** density difference $\mechanical{\rho}_{\mathrm L}-\mechanical{\rho}_{\mathrm G}$.
- **Superconductors:** energy gap $\energy{\Delta}$, or a Cooper-pair order parameter.
- **Superfluids:** amplitude $|\psi_0|$ of the condensed wavefunction.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\Delta}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Superconducting gap | The energy gap used as a superconducting order parameter. |
| $\magnetic{m}$ | $1$ | Magnetization | The mean normalized spin. |
| $\magnetic{s}_i$ | $1$ | Spin variable | The normalized spin at site i. |
| $\mechanical{\rho}_{\mathrm L}-\mechanical{\rho}_{\mathrm G}$ | — | Density difference | The liquid density minus the gas density; the unit depends on the density convention. |
| $\phi$ | — | Order parameter | The quantity that distinguishes the phases; its unit depends on the system. |
| $\psi_0$ | — | Condensate wavefunction | The wavefunction of the condensed component; its unit depends on normalization. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; subscript c marks the transition temperature. |
