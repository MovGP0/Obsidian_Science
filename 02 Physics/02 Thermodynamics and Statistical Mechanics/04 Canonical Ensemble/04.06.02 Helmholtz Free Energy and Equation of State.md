> [!TLDR]
> The canonical ideal gas gives the ideal-gas law, equipartition energy, and Sackur–Tetrode entropy.

Use the Stirling approximation $\ln {\mechanical{N}}!\approx {\mechanical{N}}\ln {\mechanical{N}}-{\mechanical{N}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\energy{F}}&=-{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\ln\mathcal Z_N\\
&=-{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\left[{\mechanical{N}}\ln\left(\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3}\right)-\ln {\mechanical{N}}!\right]\\
&\approx-{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}\left[\ln\left(\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3}\right)+1\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

Stirling's formula is a large-particle-number approximation. The exact classical expression on the preceding line can be retained for finite particle count. Its factorial has no volume or temperature dependence, so the pressure and internal-energy derivatives below do not need Stirling's approximation.

When taking the entropy derivative, the thermal wavelength changes with temperature. Its inverse cube grows as temperature to the power three halves; that dependence produces the additional three-halves contribution to entropy. At fixed temperature, doubling the available volume lowers free energy and increases entropy, as expected when the same particles have more positions available. These formulas must remain within the dilute Maxwell–Boltzmann regime stated in the preceding note.

The equation of state follows from Eq. (4.7):

$$
{\mechanical{P}}=-\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{F}}}{\partial {\mechanical{V}}}\right)_{T,N}
=\frac{{\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}{{\mechanical{V}}},
\qquad {\mechanical{P}}{\mechanical{V}}={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

The internal energy is:

$$
{\energy{U}}=-\frac{\partial\ln\mathcal Z_N}{\partial{\beta}}
=\frac{3{\mechanical{N}}}{2}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}.
$$

Each quadratic degree of freedom contributes ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}/2$. The entropy is:

$$
{\thermal{S}}=-\left(\frac{\partial {\energy{F}}}{\partial {\thermal{T}}}\right)_{V,N}
={\mechanical{N}}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}\left[\ln\left(\frac {\mechanical{V}}{{\mechanical{N}}{\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}^3}\right)+\frac52\right]. \tag{4.12}
$$

This is the Sackur–Tetrode formula for a classical monatomic ideal gas, with no internal-state degeneracy included.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{U}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Internal energy | The canonical mean energy. |
| ${\energy{F}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Helmholtz free energy | The equilibrium potential at fixed temperature, volume, and particle count. |
| ${\thermal{S}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Entropy | The thermodynamic or Gibbs entropy. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The volume available to the particles. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | 1 | Particle or subsystem count | The number of particles, localized spins, or independent oscillators. |
| ${\mechanical{P}}$ | $\unit{Pa}$ | Pressure | The force per area conjugate to volume. |
| ${\signal{\lambda}}_{\mathrm{th}}$ | $\unit{m}$ | Thermal wavelength | The thermal de Broglie wavelength. |
| ${\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The conversion between temperature and thermal energy. |
| ${\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse thermal energy | The parameter 1/(kᴮT). |
| $\mathcal Z$ | 1 | Partition function | The dimensionless sum or integral of Boltzmann weights. |
