> [!TLDR]
> The Brillouin function extends the independent-spin result to arbitrary angular-momentum quantum number.

For angular-momentum quantum number $J$, the magnetic quantum number has $2J+1$ values: $m_J=-J,\ldots,J$. The one-spin partition function is:

$$
\mathcal Z_1=\sum_{m_J=-J}^J e^{{\beta} {g}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}m_J {\magnetic{B}}}
=\frac{\sinh[(2J+1)\eta/2]}{\sinh(\eta/2)},
\qquad \eta=\frac{{g}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}{\magnetic{B}}}{{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{\thermal{T}}}.
$$

The states here belong to one fixed angular-momentum multiplet; other multiplets and interactions between moments are omitted. The finite sum is a geometric series in the magnetic projection. Its zero-field limit is the number of available projections, $2J+1$, even though the ratio of hyperbolic sines then has a removable zero-over-zero form.

The Brillouin function is a dimensionless response function, not the magnetic flux density despite the reused letter. Its argument compares the maximum moment's field energy with thermal energy. For spin one half, there are just two projections and the earlier hyperbolic-tangent result must follow. The classical limit stated below keeps that argument fixed; taking large spin at fixed field and temperature changes the argument as well and is a different limit.

The total magnetic moment is:

$$
{\magnetic{M}}={\mechanical{N}}{g}{\magnetic{\mu}_{\mathrm B}}J B_J(\eta J).
$$

The **Brillouin function** is:

$$
B_J(y)=\frac{2J+1}{2J}\coth\left(\frac{2J+1}{2J}y\right)
-\frac1{2J}\coth\left(\frac y{2J}\right).
$$

For $J=1/2$, $B_{1/2}(y)=\tanh y$, consistent with Eq. (4.16). For $J\to\infty$ at fixed $y$:

$$
B_J(y)\longrightarrow\mathcal L(y)=\coth y-\frac1y.
$$

This is the Langevin function of classical paramagnetism. At zero argument, the apparent singularities are removed by taking the continuous limit.
