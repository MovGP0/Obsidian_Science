> [!TLDR]
> A heat bath fixes temperature while the system exchanges energy with it.

The microcanonical formalism in the previous chapter describes isolated systems. Energy, volume, and particle count are fixed. This description is conceptually useful, but an experimental system usually has a much larger environment.

A **heat bath**, or thermostat, exchanges energy with the system and keeps its temperature constant. The **canonical ensemble**, also called the Gibbs or NVT ensemble, describes this situation. This chapter develops the theory and its main physical applications.

The fixed variables are ${\mechanical{N}},{\mechanical{V}},{\thermal{T}}$. The energy ${\energy{E}}$ can fluctuate.

The choice of ensemble follows the boundary. A rigid, closed container can exchange heat with a reservoir but cannot exchange particles or volume. Its temperature is fixed by equilibrium with the reservoir; its energy is a random variable. An isolated container instead requires the microcanonical description.

Follow the reservoir argument first, then use [[04.02 Canonical Partition Function]] to normalize its probabilities. [[04.03 Connection to Thermodynamics - Helmholtz Free Energy]] turns those probabilities into measurable responses. Read [[04.04 Energy Fluctuations]] before [[04.05 Ensemble Equivalence in the Thermodynamic Limit]]: small relative fluctuations explain why the two descriptions can agree macroscopically. The ideal gas, oscillator, and spin examples then test the same method with different spectra.

Start with [[04.01 System in Contact with a Thermostat]]. The chapter then develops the partition function, free energy, fluctuations, ideal gases, oscillators, and magnetic spins.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\energy{E}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Energy | The system, level, bath, or reference energy, as indicated by the subscript. |
| ${\thermal{T}}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The positive absolute temperature unless a limit is stated. |
| ${\mechanical{V}}$ | $\unit{m^3}$ | Volume | The volume available to the particles. |
| ${\mechanical{N}}$ | 1 | Particle or subsystem count | The number of particles, localized spins, or independent oscillators. |
