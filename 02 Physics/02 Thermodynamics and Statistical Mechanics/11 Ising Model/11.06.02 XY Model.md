> [!TLDR]
> Planar spins have a continuous symmetry and can undergo a topological BKT transition in two dimensions.

The **XY model** uses two-component spins in a plane:

$$
\magnetic{\mathbf S}_i=(\cos\mechanical{\theta}_i,\sin\mechanical{\theta}_i),
\qquad
\energy{\mathcal H}=-\energy{J}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}
\magnetic{\mathbf S}_i\cdot\magnetic{\mathbf S}_j
=-\energy{J}\sum_{\langle i,j\rangle}
\cos(\mechanical{\theta}_i-\mechanical{\theta}_j).
$$

In two dimensions, at positive temperature, the model does not spontaneously break its $U(1)$ symmetry, as required by the Mermin–Wagner theorem. It does have a **Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless (BKT) transition**.

This topological transition involves vortices and antivortices. It has no local order parameter of the usual symmetry-breaking type. Instead, the correlation function changes from exponential decay to power-law decay.

The setting is a classical, zero-field, short-range ferromagnet. A common rotation of every angle leaves each angle difference unchanged, which checks the continuous symmetry. In an aligned configuration every dot product is one and each bond has energy $-\energy{J}$. Small relative angles raise that energy quadratically: $\cos(\mechanical{\theta}_i-\mechanical{\theta}_j)\approx1-(\mechanical{\theta}_i-\mechanical{\theta}_j)^2/2$. This spin-wave approximation assumes small local angle differences; it does not by itself account for vortex unbinding.

On cooling through the BKT transition, the two-dimensional model changes from short-range correlations to algebraic correlations. In the low-temperature phase, opposite vortex charges remain paired; above the transition, free vortices destroy that algebraic order. Power-law correlations throughout a temperature interval differ from the isolated critical-point power law of the Ising model. They also tend to zero at large separation, so they are compatible with zero spontaneous magnetization. See [Tong, Statistical Field Theory, Section 4.4](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/sft/sfthtml/S4.html).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{\mathbf S}_i$ | $1$ | Classical spin | The normalized two-component or three-component spin. |
| $\mechanical{\theta}_i$ | $\unit{rad}$ | Spin angle | The orientation of a planar spin. |
| $\energy{\mathcal H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy of a spin configuration. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
