> [!TLDR]
> A macroscopic system can select one magnetized equilibrium state although its zero-field Hamiltonian has spin-inversion symmetry.

Spontaneous symmetry breaking requires the thermodynamic limit. The order of the field and size limits is essential.

The apparent puzzle is that the zero-field Hamiltonian treats opposite orientations equally, yet an ordered phase has a definite magnetization sign. The resolution distinguishes the symmetric ensemble from a selected equilibrium phase. A small field or a boundary condition selects a sign; an infinite ordered system can retain that selection when the field tends to zero.

Start with [[11.05.01 Mechanism of Spontaneous Breaking]] and reconstruct the two orders of limits before using a magnetization formula. Then read [[11.05.02 Physical Interpretation - Equilibrium States and Domains]] to distinguish equilibrium states, interfaces, and reversal barriers. [[11.05.03 Order Parameter and Phase Transition]] compares two paths through the same phase diagram: varying temperature at zero field and crossing the zero-field coexistence line below the transition. A finite sample can show a large and persistent magnetization, but this alone is not a proof of a nonanalytic thermodynamic limit.
