> [!TLDR]
> The square-lattice Ising model has an exact zero-field solution and a phase transition at positive temperature.

The two-dimensional model gives exact results for the critical temperature, critical exponents, and spontaneous magnetization.

The results in this section concern the classical nearest-neighbour ferromagnet on an infinite square lattice, with equal coupling along both axes and zero applied field. The question is how ordering differs from the chain and from the mean-field approximation. Unlike the chain, this model has an ordered phase at positive temperature. Unlike mean field, it retains the effects of correlated fluctuations.

Read [[11.04.01 Onsager Solution]] for the bulk free energy and its critical condition. [[11.04.02 Kramers–Wannier Duality]] gives a shorter route to that condition, with an explicit assumption about the number of transitions. Use [[11.04.03 Exact Critical Exponents in Two Dimensions]] to compare singular responses, then [[11.04.04 Spontaneous Magnetization - Yang's Result]] for the ordered branch. The zero-field free energy by itself must not be differentiated as if it supplied the full field dependence; the spontaneous magnetization requires a selected-phase limit.
