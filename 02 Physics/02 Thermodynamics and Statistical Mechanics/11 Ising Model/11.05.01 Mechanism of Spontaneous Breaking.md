> [!TLDR]
> The thermodynamic limit and zero-field limit do not commute below the critical temperature.

For a finite system of $N$ spins, the zero-field partition function

$$
\mathcal Z(\energy{h}=0)=
\sum_{\{\sigma\}}e^{-\thermal{\beta}\energy{\mathcal H}}
$$

is invariant under $\magnetic{\sigma}_i\to-\magnetic{\sigma}_i$. Therefore $\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_i\rangle_{N,h=0}=0$.

This argument assumes spin-inversion-symmetric boundary conditions, such as periodic or free boundaries, and positive temperature. A boundary that fixes positive spins already selects an orientation and is not covered by the same finite-system cancellation. With symmetric boundaries, every configuration has a reversed partner of identical Boltzmann weight and opposite local spin.

Spontaneous symmetry breaking occurs only in the thermodynamic limit. Below the critical temperature, the limits do not commute:

$$
\magnetic{m}_{\mathrm s}
=\lim_{h\to0^+}\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_i\rangle
\ne
\lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{h\to0^+}\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_i\rangle
=0.
$$

The first order of limits takes $N\to\infty$ before the field tends to zero. It defines the **spontaneous magnetization**.

Read the nested limits from the inside out. At each fixed positive field, first form an infinite system. Then let the field tend to zero within the positive phase. Reversing the procedure restores the symmetric finite-system ensemble before taking the size limit; its mean remains zero at every size. A negative selecting field gives the symmetry-related negative phase. The inequality presumes a temperature at which an ordered phase exists; the positive-temperature one-dimensional chain is a counterexample where both limits give zero.

**Definition 11.3 (Spontaneous symmetry breaking).** The system spontaneously breaks $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry if

$$
\magnetic{m}_{\mathrm s}\equiv
\lim_{h\to0^+}\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle\magnetic{\sigma}_i\rangle>0.
$$

The order parameter remains nonzero when the applied field tends to zero.

This is an equilibrium definition. It does not specify a spin-flip rate or a relaxation time. A simulation or experiment on a finite sample must separately check how boundary selection, finite size, and observation time affect the measured mean. An ensemble that mixes the positive and negative phases equally can still have zero mean although each pure phase is ordered.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $N$ | $1$ | Spin count | The number of lattice sites or spins. |
| $\magnetic{\sigma}_i$ | $1$ | Ising spin | The normalized spin at site i. |
| $\mathcal Z$ | $1$ | Partition function | The sum of Boltzmann weights of the configurations. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\energy{\mathcal H}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Hamiltonian | The energy of a spin configuration. |
| $\magnetic{m}$ | $1$ | Magnetization per spin | The mean normalized spin; subscripts identify particular or spontaneous values. |
