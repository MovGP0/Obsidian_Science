> [!TLDR]
> Potts, XY, and Heisenberg models extend the number of spin states or spin components.

The Ising model has two discrete spin states. Related models change the number of states or allow continuous spin directions.

Use the state space and symmetry to choose the extension. [[11.06.01 Potts Model]] retains discrete labels but gives each site more choices; the interaction rewards equal labels. [[11.06.02 XY Model]] allows a continuous direction in a plane, and [[11.06.03 Heisenberg Model]] allows a direction on a sphere. These continuous rotations admit long-wavelength distortions that are absent from a binary spin.

The number of spin components is separate from the dimension of the lattice. A planar spin can live on a three-dimensional lattice, and a three-component spin can live on a chain. First specify both choices, then ask whether the symmetry can break at positive temperature. The two-dimensional XY case is a useful check: absence of ordinary spontaneous magnetization does not exclude a transition in spatial correlations. For the two-state Potts limit, also check the coupling normalization before comparing an Ising critical temperature.
