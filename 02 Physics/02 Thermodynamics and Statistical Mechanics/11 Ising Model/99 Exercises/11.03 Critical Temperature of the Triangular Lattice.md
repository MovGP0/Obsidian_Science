> [!TLDR]
> Apply duality with the honeycomb lattice to obtain the triangular-lattice critical temperature.

**Exercise 11.3.** Use Kramers–Wannier duality to determine the critical temperature of the Ising model on the triangular lattice. Each site has $z=6$ nearest neighbours.

The triangular lattice is not self-dual. Its dual is the honeycomb lattice, with $z=3$. The self-duality condition $\sinh(2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c})=1$ therefore applies only to the square lattice in the form used in this chapter.

For the triangular lattice, the source supplies the exact result

$$
e^{4\thermal{\beta}_{\mathrm c}\energy{J}}=3,
\qquad
\tanh(2\thermal{\beta}_{\mathrm c}\energy{J})=\frac12,
$$

or

$$
\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}
=\frac{4\energy{J}}{\ln3}
\approx3.641\,\energy{J}.
$$

See [[11.04.02 Kramers–Wannier Duality]].

> [!note] Additional relation needed
> Duality alone relates the triangular and honeycomb models. It does not locate their critical temperatures independently. A further relation, such as the star–triangle transformation, is needed to derive the supplied result.

## Complete the lattice transformation

Assume a zero-field, isotropic, nearest-neighbour ferromagnet and one transition on each lattice. Let $\thermal{K}$ be the triangular reduced coupling and $\thermal{L}$ the honeycomb reduced coupling. They are positive dimensionless interaction energies divided by thermal energy. Summing the central spin of a honeycomb star produces a triangular interaction among its three neighbours. A regular positive factor, denoted by the helper $A$, accounts for the normalization.

There are only two inequivalent outer-spin arrangements. If all three are equal, the summed star weight is $2\cosh(3\thermal{L})$ and the triangle weight is $Ae^{3\thermal{K}}$. If one is opposite to the other two, these weights are $2\cosh\thermal{L}$ and $Ae^{-\thermal{K}}$. Dividing removes $A$:

$$
e^{4\thermal{K}}
=\frac{\cosh(3\thermal{L})}{\cosh\thermal{L}}
=2\cosh(2\thermal{L})-1.
$$

This star–triangle map preserves criticality because the removed factor is regular. At the corresponding critical couplings, combine it with the triangular–honeycomb duality relation

$$
e^{-2\thermal{L}_{\mathrm c}}=\tanh\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}.
$$

For the algebra, set the helper $x=e^{2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}}>1$. Duality gives $e^{2\thermal{L}_{\mathrm c}}=(x+1)/(x-1)$. Substitution into the star–triangle relation gives

$$
x^2=\frac{x+1}{x-1}+\frac{x-1}{x+1}-1
=\frac{x^2+3}{x^2-1},
\qquad
(x^2-3)(x^2+1)=0.
$$

The positive ferromagnetic solution is $x^2=3$, which gives the supplied $e^{4\thermal{\beta}_{\mathrm c}\energy{J}}=3$. The two transformations and their lattice roles are developed in [Baxter, Exactly Solved Models in Statistical Mechanics, Sections 6.3–6.5](https://physics.anu.edu.au/research/ftp/_files/Exactly.pdf).

For a check, $e^{4\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c}}=3$ implies $\tanh(2\thermal{K}_{\mathrm c})=(3-1)/(3+1)=1/2$, so the two supplied critical conditions agree. The dimensionless temperature ratio is exactly $4/\ln3$ before rounding to $3.641$. The mean-field estimate would use $z=6$ and give $6$, showing that coordination alone misses the exact effect of lattice fluctuations. This derivation does not apply unchanged to negative coupling on the frustrated triangular lattice.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $z$ | $1$ | Coordination number | The number of nearest neighbours at a lattice site. |
| $\thermal{K}$ | $1$ | Triangular reduced coupling | The triangular interaction energy divided by thermal energy. |
| $\thermal{L}$ | $1$ | Honeycomb reduced coupling | The honeycomb interaction energy divided by thermal energy. |
