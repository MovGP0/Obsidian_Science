> [!TLDR]
> Mean-field theory predicts power laws for thermodynamic quantities near the critical temperature.

Near the critical temperature, thermodynamic quantities vanish or diverge as power laws. **Critical exponents** specify these laws. The mean-field predictions are given below.

The proportionality signs suppress amplitudes and reference energy or temperature scales; they are not dimensionally complete equations for measured values. The thermal exponents refer to zero field near a continuous transition in the thermodynamic limit. Below the transition, magnetization means the magnitude in a selected ordered phase. The field exponent instead approaches zero field at the critical temperature.

| Quantity | Scaling law | Mean-field exponent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magnetization | $\magnetic{m}\sim(\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}-\thermal{T})^\beta$ | $\beta=1/2$ |
| Susceptibility | $\magnetic{\chi}\sim\lvert\thermal{T}-\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}\rvert^{-\gamma}$ | $\gamma=1$ |
| Heat capacity | $\thermal{C}\sim\lvert\thermal{T}-\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}\rvert^{-\alpha}$ | $\alpha=0$ (finite jump) |
| Magnetization at the critical temperature | $\magnetic{m}\sim\energy{h}^{1/\delta}$ | $\delta=3$ |
| Correlation length | $\xi\sim\lvert\thermal{T}-\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}\rvert^{-\nu}$ | $\nu=1/2$ |

## Susceptibility

For $\thermal{T}>\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c}$ and $\energy{h}\to0$, differentiate the self-consistency equation:

$$
\magnetic{\chi}=\frac{\partial\magnetic{m}}{\partial\energy{h}}
=\thermal{\beta}(z\energy{J}\magnetic{\chi}+1)(1-\magnetic{m}^2).
$$

Above the critical temperature, $\magnetic{m}=0$, so

$$
\magnetic{\chi}
=\frac{\thermal{\beta}}{1-\thermal{\beta}z\energy{J}}
=\frac{1}{\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}(\thermal{T}-\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})}.
\tag{11.7}
$$

This is the **Curie–Weiss law**, with $\magnetic{\chi}\sim(\thermal{T}-\thermal{T}_{\mathrm c})^{-1}$.

The denominator has units of energy, which gives the required inverse-energy susceptibility for the field convention used here. Far above the mean-field transition, it tends to the independent-spin value $1/(\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}\thermal{T})$. Near the transition, the divergence shows why linear response requires a progressively smaller field.

## Heat capacity

At zero field the heat capacity has a finite jump:

$$
\Delta\thermal{C}
=\thermal{C}_{T_{\mathrm c}^-}-\thermal{C}_{T_{\mathrm c}^+}
=\frac{3\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}}{2}.
$$

There is no divergence. This corresponds to $\alpha=0$ for a continuous transition.

> [!note] Normalization
> The jump above is per spin. The source writes $C$; the heat capacity for all $N$ spins has a jump $3N\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}/2$.

The exponent $\alpha=0$ alone does not distinguish a finite jump from a logarithmic divergence. Here the stated jump is the extra information. Compare the exact two-dimensional model, where the same exponent value accompanies a logarithm. Also, the correlation-length exponent requires a theory that allows spatial variation; it does not follow from the uniform one-spin equation alone. All values in this table are mean-field predictions, whose asymptotic validity is discussed in [[11.03.05 Limits of the Mean-Field Approximation]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\magnetic{m}$ | $1$ | Magnetization per spin | The mean normalized spin; subscripts identify particular or spontaneous values. |
| $\thermal{T}$ | $\unit{K}$ | Temperature | The absolute temperature; a c subscript marks a critical value. |
| $\magnetic{\chi}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Susceptibility per spin | The derivative of magnetization with respect to the field energy. |
| $\thermal{C},\ \Delta\thermal{C}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Heat capacity per spin | The heat capacity and its jump at the transition. |
| $\energy{h}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Field parameter | The applied field multiplied by the spin magnetic moment. |
| $\xi$ | $1$ | Correlation length | The correlation range, measured in lattice spacings. |
| $\thermal{\beta}$ | $\unit{J^{-1}}$ | Inverse temperature | The inverse of the thermal energy. |
| $z$ | $1$ | Coordination number | The number of nearest neighbours at a lattice site. |
| $\energy{J}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Exchange coupling | The energy scale of a nearest-neighbour interaction. |
| $\thermal{k}_{\mathrm B}$ | $\unit{J\,K^{-1}}$ | Boltzmann constant | The constant that converts temperature to energy. |
| $N$ | $1$ | Spin count | The number of lattice sites or spins. |
