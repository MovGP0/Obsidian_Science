> [!TLDR]
> Each lattice site carries a binary spin that interacts with nearby spins and an applied field.

The model uses a regular lattice with discrete spin variables. The lattice geometry sets the number of nearest neighbours. Without an applied field, global spin inversion leaves the energy unchanged.

The modeling choice is to retain the orientation along one preferred axis and discard continuous spin rotations. A configuration specifies one binary value at every site. The Hamiltonian then assigns an energy to that complete configuration; equilibrium statistics determine how often configurations occur. The energy rule and the thermal probability rule are separate inputs.

Read [[11.01.01 Hamiltonian and Spin Variables]] first to fix the exchange and field sign conventions. Next, [[11.01.02 Lattice and Coordination Number]] explains how to count each interaction once. Finish with [[11.01.03 ℤ₂ Symmetry]]: reversing every spin preserves the interaction energy, whereas reversing one spin generally does not. This distinction prevents confusion between a global symmetry and independent changes at each site.
