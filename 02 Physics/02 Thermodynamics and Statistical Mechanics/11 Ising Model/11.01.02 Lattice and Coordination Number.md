> [!TLDR]
> The coordination number gives the number of nearest neighbours at each lattice site.

The **coordination number** $z$ depends on the lattice geometry.

| Lattice | Dimension $d$ | Coordination number $z$ | Bond count $N_{\mathrm{bonds}}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Linear chain | 1 | 2 | $N$ |
| Square | 2 | 4 | $2N$ |
| Triangular | 2 | 6 | $3N$ |
| Simple cubic | 3 | 6 | $3N$ |
| Body-centred cubic | 3 | 8 | $4N$ |
| Face-centred cubic | 3 | 12 | $6N$ |

Each bond connects two sites. Thus the total number of bonds is

$$
N_{\mathrm{bonds}}=\frac{Nz}{2}.
$$

The counts assume periodic boundaries, or a bulk lattice where boundary corrections can be neglected.

The factor of one half removes double counting: summing the neighbours of every site counts each bond from both ends. For an open chain, the two end sites have only one neighbour. The total is then $N-1$ bonds, rather than $N$. Thus a bulk coordination number cannot be assigned to every boundary site without a correction.

Coordination counts local interactions; it does not specify all lattice geometry. The triangular and simple-cubic lattices both have six neighbours per bulk site, but their spatial dimensions and closed paths differ. A theory that uses only the coordination number, such as the simplest mean-field approximation, cannot resolve those differences. Check the bond count before applying the Hamiltonian or comparing energies per site.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $N$ | $1$ | Spin count | The number of lattice sites or spins. |
| $z$ | $1$ | Coordination number | The number of nearest neighbours at a lattice site. |
| $d$ | $1$ | Spatial dimension | The dimension of the lattice. |
| $N_{\mathrm{bonds}}$ | $1$ | Bond count | The number of distinct nearest-neighbour bonds. |
