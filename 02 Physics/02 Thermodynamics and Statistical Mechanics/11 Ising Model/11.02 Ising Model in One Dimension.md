> [!TLDR]
> The exact one-dimensional solution has no phase transition at positive temperature.

Ising solved the one-dimensional model in 1925. The solution has no phase transition for $\thermal{T}>0$. This result initially disappointed Ising, who had hoped to find critical behaviour in one dimension.

The system here is a classical chain with finite nearest-neighbour coupling in canonical equilibrium. The exact solution tests whether local alignment produces order across an arbitrarily long chain. It does not at positive temperature: correlations can extend over many sites, but their range stays finite. A very long correlation length must therefore be distinguished from true long-range order.

Follow [[11.02.01 Solution by Recursion]] to see why only the end spin must be retained when a segment is extended. [[11.02.02 Transfer-Matrix Method]] turns that recursion into matrix multiplication. The largest eigenvalue gives the bulk free energy, while the ratio of eigenvalues controls spatial correlations. Use [[11.02.04 Magnetization and Susceptibility]] and [[11.02.05 Correlation Function and Correlation Length]] to compare two responses to cooling: the mean spin stays zero at zero field, but the response to a small field becomes large. These conclusions concern equilibrium and the infinite-chain limit, rather than the time needed for a finite chain to reverse.
