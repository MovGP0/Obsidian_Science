> [!TLDR]
> Creation and annihilation operators change one occupation and encode the bosonic or fermionic exchange rule.

These operators create or remove a particle in one single-particle state.

## Bosons

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat a_\alpha^\dagger|\ldots,{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha,\ldots\rangle
&=\sqrt{{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha+1}\,|\ldots,{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha+1,\ldots\rangle,\\
\hat a_\alpha|\ldots,{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha,\ldots\rangle
&=\sqrt{{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha}\,|\ldots,{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha-1,\ldots\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

The commutation relations are $[\hat a_\alpha,\hat a_\beta^\dagger]=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$, with $[\hat a_\alpha,\hat a_\beta]=0$ and the corresponding relation for two creation operators. Annihilation of an empty state gives zero.

The square-root factors preserve the normalization convention for number states. Apply creation and then annihilation to a bosonic number state: the two factors multiply to the original occupation plus one. Reverse the order and they multiply to the original occupation. Their difference is one, which checks the commutator, including its action on the vacuum.

## Fermions

Fix an ordering of the single-particle states. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat c_\alpha^\dagger|\ldots,{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha,\ldots\rangle
&=(1-{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha)(-1)^{\sum_{\gamma<\alpha}{\mechanical{n}}_\gamma}|\ldots,{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha+1,\ldots\rangle,\\
\hat c_\alpha|\ldots,{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha,\ldots\rangle
&={\mechanical{n}}_\alpha(-1)^{\sum_{\gamma<\alpha}{\mechanical{n}}_\gamma}|\ldots,{\mechanical{n}}_\alpha-1,\ldots\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

The factors give zero when creation would double-occupy a state or annihilation would remove a nonexistent particle. The sign counts occupied states before $\alpha$.

The anticommutation relations are $\{\hat c_\alpha,\hat c_\beta^\dagger\}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ and $\{\hat c_\alpha,\hat c_\beta\}=0$, with the corresponding creation relation.

For two fermionic modes, creating first in the earlier mode and then in the later mode gives the opposite sign to the reverse order. Both results describe the same two occupied modes with a convention-dependent overall sign. Relative signs between configurations must remain consistent. The occupation restrictions and this ordering sign are separate parts of the fermionic rule.

In either case, the number operator is $\hat n_\alpha=\hat a_\alpha^\dagger\hat a_\alpha$ or $\hat c_\alpha^\dagger\hat c_\alpha$. For a noninteracting system,

$$
\hat H=\sum_\alpha{\energy{\varepsilon}}_\alpha\hat n_\alpha.
$$

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\mechanical{n}}_\alpha$ | $\unit{1}$ | Occupation number | Number or mean number of particles in one state. |
| ${\energy{\varepsilon}}$ | $\unit{J}$ | Single-particle energy | Energy of one single-particle state. |
