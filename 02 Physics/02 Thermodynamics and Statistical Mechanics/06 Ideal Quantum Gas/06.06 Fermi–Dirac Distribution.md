> [!TLDR]
> Fermi–Dirac occupations are bounded by one and approach a step at zero temperature.

Why do low-temperature fermions occupy excited energy states even without interactions? Pauli exclusion permits only one particle in each complete state. A many-particle ground state must therefore fill successive available states rather than put all particles in the lowest one.

The distribution predicts a mean occupation per complete state, not a probability density per energy interval. To count particles in an interval, include the density of states. The ideal-gas assumption is used in the derivation; a general interacting momentum distribution need not have this form.

The Fermi–Dirac law follows from the empty and occupied contributions to one fermionic state. See [[06.06.01 Derivation]] and [[06.06.02 Properties of the Fermi–Dirac Distribution]].
