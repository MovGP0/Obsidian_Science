> [!TLDR]
> The grand partition function of an ideal quantum gas is a product over single-particle states.

Use the grand canonical ensemble to sum over all allowed occupations. The absence of interactions permits exact factorization.

This choice of ensemble removes a fixed-total-number constraint from the occupation sums. It is useful even when the physical sample has a nearly fixed particle number: choose the chemical potential so that the calculated mean has the required value. Exact finite-system canonical probabilities can still differ.

The calculation has two distinct inputs: the additive ideal-gas energy makes the exponential separable, and the allowed number-state basis determines each sum. Exchange symmetry is already included in that basis. Adding a Gibbs factorial here would count it a second time.

- [[06.04.01 Grand Canonical Ensemble]] gives the trace.
- [[06.04.02 Factorization over Individual Levels]] separates the trace into state contributions.
- [[06.04.03 Grand Partition Function for Fermions]] sums occupations zero and one.
- [[06.04.04 Grand Partition Function for Bosons]] sums all nonnegative occupations.
