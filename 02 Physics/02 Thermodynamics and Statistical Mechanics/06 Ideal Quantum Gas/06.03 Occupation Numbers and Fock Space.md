> [!TLDR]
> Occupation numbers describe how identical particles fill single-particle states.

Particle labels are not needed. We specify the number of particles in each single-particle state.

The basis must be fixed before an occupation list has a meaning. For the ideal gas, choose single-particle energy eigenstates so that the total energy is obtained by adding the energies of occupied states. A degenerate energy can correspond to several separate states; do not merge them into one occupation subject to a fermionic upper bound of one.

As a small counting test, place two identical particles in two complete states. Bosons have three number-basis configurations: both in the first, one in each, or both in the second. Fermions have only the configuration with one in each. Follow the links to see how this restriction becomes a basis and an operator algebra.
