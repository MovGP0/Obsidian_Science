> [!TLDR]
> Bose–Einstein occupations have no upper bound and require the chemical potential to lie below the lowest state energy.

Why can the lowest state gain many particles as the gas cools? Bosonic symmetry allows repeated occupation of the same complete state. The ensemble then sums zero, one, two, and all higher occupations with their thermal weights.

The result is a mean particle count in one state. It can exceed one and is therefore not a probability of finding that state occupied. Whether a large ground-state population becomes a phase transition depends also on the density of excited states and the thermodynamic limit.

The Bose–Einstein law follows from the geometric series for the occupation of one bosonic state. See [[06.07.01 Derivation]] and [[06.07.02 Properties of the Bose–Einstein Distribution]].
