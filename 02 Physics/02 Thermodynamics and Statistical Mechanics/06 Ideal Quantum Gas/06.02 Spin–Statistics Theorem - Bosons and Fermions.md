> [!TLDR]
> Integer-spin particles are bosons; half-integer-spin particles are fermions under the usual spin–statistics assumptions.

The choice between a symmetric and an antisymmetric wavefunction is fixed by the particle spin. The result is the **spin–statistics theorem**.

**Theorem 6.1 — Spin–statistics (Pauli, 1940).** In a local relativistic quantum field theory in three spatial dimensions, with the usual positivity and causality assumptions:

- Integer spin ${\mechanical{s}}=0,1,2,\ldots$ gives **bosons**, with symmetric many-particle wavefunctions.
- Half-integer spin ${\mechanical{s}}=\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\ldots$ gives **fermions**, with antisymmetric many-particle wavefunctions.

The full proof requires quantum field theory and is outside this text. The connection is consistent with positive energy and microcausality: observables at spacelike separation commute.

This theorem supplies information that the nonrelativistic permutation calculation does not contain. That calculation permits either sign; the relativistic assumptions connect the sign to spin. Pauli’s original free-field argument identifies positive energy and spacelike commutativity as essential conditions. See [Pauli, Physical Review 58, 716 (1940)](https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.58.716).

As a counting check, two electrons with different spin projections may share a spatial orbital because their complete states differ. Two electrons in the same complete spatial-and-spin state are forbidden. The theorem classifies particle statistics; it does not say that all components of a spatial wavefunction must be antisymmetric when the spin part is omitted.

**Example 6.1 — Particle examples.**

| Particle | Spin | Statistics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photon; W and Z bosons; gluon | $1$ | Bosonic |
| Higgs boson | $0$ | Bosonic |
| Electron, proton, neutron, quark | $1/2$ | Fermionic |
| ⁴He atom: two protons, two neutrons, two electrons | Integer total spin | Bosonic |
| ³He atom: two protons, one neutron, two electrons | Half-integer total spin | Fermionic |

Composite atoms behave as bosons or fermions when their internal structure remains intact and the effective particle description applies.
