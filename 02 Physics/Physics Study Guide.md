> [!TLDR]
> Choose a study route through classical mechanics, thermal physics, quantum theory, particle physics, or relativity.

## Study collections

- [[00.00 Classical Mechanics|Classical mechanics]]. Start with motion and forces, then study conservation laws and the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations.
- [[00.00 Statistical Mechanics|Thermodynamics and statistical mechanics]]. Review equilibrium thermodynamics, then choose ensembles and study gases and phase transitions.
- [[00 Introduction|Mathematical foundations of quantum mechanics]]. Learn the mathematical language of states, operators, and probability.
- [[00.00 Quantum Mechanics|Quantum mechanics course]]. Work through measurement, dynamics, spin, atomic systems, and approximation methods.
- [[00.00 Quantum Field Theory|Quantum field theory]]. Use quantum mechanics, special relativity, and Lagrangian mechanics to study fields, particles, and interactions.
- [[Elementary Particles - Contents|Elementary particles]]. Follow the book from relativistic kinematics and symmetries to the fundamental interactions.
- [[Gauge Theories - Contents|Gauge theories]]. Study gauge invariance and its applications to the strong, weak, and electromagnetic interactions.
- [[Relativists Toolkit - Contents|A Relativist’s Toolkit]]. Study the geometry and methods of general relativity and black-hole mechanics.
- [[Kaluza-Klein - Study Guide|Kaluza–Klein]]. Work through the five-dimensional model and its relation to four-dimensional fields.
- [[00 Cosmic Relativity|Cosmic Relativity]]. Read the source’s proposed model and examine its claimed experimental tests.
- [[Kyle Kabasares Course Notes|Kyle Kabasares course notes]] includes courses in electromagnetism, waves, relativity, and astrophysics.

## Electromagnetism, waves, and relativistic applications

- [[Electric potential on the axis of a charged disk]] uses concentric rings to connect Coulomb’s law with an axial field.
- [[RC and RL response in time and frequency]] connects switching transients with sinusoidal filtering.
- [[Vacuum electromagnetic waves and optical boundaries]] links Maxwell’s equations to refraction, interference, and imaging.
- [[String modes, reflection, and dispersion]] uses boundary conditions to find normal modes and reflection at a junction.
- [[Lorentz transformations and clock comparisons]] uses pairs of events to explain time dilation, length contraction, and synchronization.
- [[Four-momentum and reaction thresholds]] uses an invariant to find the lab energy needed for particle production.
- [[Nuclear binding and radioactive decay]] relates mass differences, half-life, and activity.
