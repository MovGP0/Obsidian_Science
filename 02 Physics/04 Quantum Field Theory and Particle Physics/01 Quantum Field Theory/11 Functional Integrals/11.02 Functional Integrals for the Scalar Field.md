title: "Functional Integrals for the Scalar Field"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A scalar-field functional integral sums over field configurations, extending the path-integral construction from quantum mechanics to field theory.

The scalar-field construction extends the particle path integral to a field with a degree of freedom at each spatial point.

The physical question now concerns vacuum fluctuations and propagation between spacetime points. A field history assigns a value to every spacetime point; it is not the trajectory of one particle. Start with a finite lattice or a finite set of modes so that the measure is a multiple integral. The continuum notation then summarizes a regulated limit, rather than an ordinary product of infinitely many Lebesgue measures.

The source functional in the next article inserts fields by differentiation. [[11.02.02 The Free Scalar Field and Gaussian Functional Integrals]] solves the quadratic case and fixes the factor of i between the inverse differential kernel and the time-ordered correlator. [[11.02.03 Functional Determinants and Zeta Regularization]] explains why the remaining normalization needs a spectral prescription.

Keep the boundary-state choice separate from the action: the same local field equation admits Feynman, retarded, and advanced inverses. The vacuum functional in this chapter uses the Feynman prescription. A useful first check is to set the source to zero after two derivatives and recover the free vacuum propagator, with the same prescription and normalization throughout.
