---
title: "Proper Vertex Functions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Functional derivatives of the effective action generate proper vertices, which cannot be disconnected by cutting one internal propagator.

The effective action generates **proper vertex functions**, also called **one-particle-irreducible (1PI) functions**.
A connected 1PI diagram cannot be split into disconnected parts by cutting one internal propagator.

In the source's Minkowski convention,
$$
\signal{\Gamma}^{(n)}(\mechanical{x_1},\ldots,\mechanical{x_n})
=\left.
i\frac{\delta^n\energy{\Gamma}[\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}]}
{\delta\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{x_1})\cdots
\delta\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{x_n})}
\right|_{\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}=0}.
\tag{11.14}
$$

With this convention, the proper vertices match the corresponding 1PI Feynman-rule factors.

A connected function includes external propagators and chains of proper vertices. A proper vertex is the amputated building block after these propagator connections have been removed. The two-point case is especially useful: it turns repeated self-energy insertions into an inverse-kernel problem rather than a separate sum of every chain.
In momentum space,
$$
\signal{\Gamma}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p},-\mechanical{p})
=i\big(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2
-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^2)\big).
$$
Up to the convention-dependent sign, this is the inverse exact propagator.
The quantity $\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}^2)$ is the scalar self-energy.

The connected and 1PI functions obey
$$
\int\mathrm{d}^4\mechanical{z}\,
\frac{\delta^2\signal{W}}
{\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{x})\delta\signal{J}(\mechanical{z})}
\frac{\delta^2\energy{\Gamma}}
{\delta\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{z})
\delta\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}(\mechanical{y})}
=-\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}).
\tag{11.15}
$$
In momentum space this gives
$\signal{G_c}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})
\signal{\Gamma}^{(2)}(\mechanical{p})=-1$.
Thus the exact connected propagator and proper two-point function are inverse to one another with a minus sign in these conventions.

The sign in the inverse relation is fixed here, rather than left arbitrary: the connected correlator is the second source derivative divided by i, whereas the proper vertex is i times the effective-action Hessian. These factors cancel in their product, leaving the minus identity from (11.15). At zero self-energy the product is $[i/(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)]\,[i(\mechanical{p}^2-\mechanical{m}^2)]=-1$, with the causal prescription understood.

Vanishing of the inverse kernel identifies candidate propagator poles. Pole positions and residues, together with the higher vertices and external-state normalization, connect this organization to scattering amplitudes. A proper vertex alone is generally an off-shell, convention-dependent object; it is not itself a measured cross section.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\Gamma}$ | $1$ | Effective action | The functional whose field derivatives generate proper vertices. |
| $\signal{\Gamma}^{(n)}$ | — | Proper n-point vertex | The source includes a factor i in its vertex definition; dimensions depend on n and representation. |
| $\signal{\phi_{\mathrm{cl}}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mean scalar field | The field used as the effective-action argument. |
| $\mechanical{x_i}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime insertion points | The coordinates of the proper-vertex field derivatives. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momentum | The momentum of the two-point function. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar mass | The mass in the inverse propagator. |
| $\energy{\Sigma}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Scalar self-energy | The mass-squared correction in the proper two-point function. |
| $\signal{G_c}^{(2)}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Connected momentum-space propagator | The exact two-point Green function inverse to the proper two-point vertex up to the stated sign. |
| $\signal{W}$ | $1$ | Connected generating functional | The functional whose source Hessian enters the inversion identity. |
| $\signal{J}$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | External scalar source | The source in the connected-functional derivatives. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y},\mechanical{z}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinates | The arguments of the Hessian kernels and their convolution. |
