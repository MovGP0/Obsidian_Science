---
title: "Derivation from Quantum Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Time slicing and repeated position-state completeness turn the evolution operator into a path integral.

The aim is to derive the measure as well as the action phase from the same evolution operator. Consider one nonrelativistic coordinate on the real line, constant positive mass, and a time-independent position potential. The endpoint coordinates are fixed; only the intermediate coordinates are integrated. The position and momentum states use delta-function normalization.

Let $\mechanical{T}=\mechanical{t_f}-\mechanical{t_i}$.
Divide the interval into $N$ slices of width
$\mechanical{\epsilon}=\mechanical{T}/N$, with
$\mechanical{t_k}=\mechanical{t_i}+k\mechanical{\epsilon}$.
Insert $N-1$ completeness relations
$1=\int\mathrm{d}\mechanical{q_k}\,
|\mechanical{q_k}\rangle\langle\mechanical{q_k}|$:
$$
\signal{K}
=\int\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\mathrm{d}\mechanical{q_k}
\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}
\langle\mechanical{q_{k+1}}|
e^{-i\hat{\energy{H}}\mechanical{\epsilon}}
|\mechanical{q_k}\rangle.
\tag{11.3}
$$
For each elementary factor, use
$\hat{\energy{H}}=\hat{\mechanical{p}}^2/(2\mechanical{m})
+\energy{V}(\hat{\mechanical{q}})$
and
$\langle\mechanical{q}|\mechanical{p}\rangle
=e^{i\mechanical{p}\mechanical{q}}/\sqrt{2\pi}$.
The small-time expansion starts with
$$
\langle\mechanical{q_{k+1}}|
e^{-i\hat{\energy{H}}\mechanical{\epsilon}}
|\mechanical{q_k}\rangle
\approx
\langle\mechanical{q_{k+1}}|
(1-i\mechanical{\epsilon}\hat{\energy{H}})
|\mechanical{q_k}\rangle.
$$

Insert a momentum completeness relation into the short-time factor:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\langle\mechanical{q_{k+1}}|
e^{-i\hat{\energy{H}}\mechanical{\epsilon}}
|\mechanical{q_k}\rangle\\
&=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\mechanical{p_k}}{2\pi}
e^{i\mechanical{p_k}(\mechanical{q_{k+1}}-\mechanical{q_k})}
\left[
1-i\mechanical{\epsilon}\left(
\frac{\mechanical{p_k}^2}{2\mechanical{m}}
+\energy{V}(\mechanical{q_k})\right)
\right]\\
&\approx\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\mechanical{p_k}}{2\pi}
\exp\left[
i\mechanical{\epsilon}\left(
\mechanical{p_k}\dot{\mechanical{q_k}}
-\frac{\mechanical{p_k}^2}{2\mechanical{m}}
-\energy{V}(\mechanical{q_k})\right)
\right],
\end{aligned}
$$
where
$\dot{\mechanical{q_k}}
=(\mechanical{q_{k+1}}-\mechanical{q_k})/\mechanical{\epsilon}$.
Complete the square:
$$
\mechanical{p_k}\dot{\mechanical{q_k}}
-\frac{\mechanical{p_k}^2}{2\mechanical{m}}
=-\frac1{2\mechanical{m}}
(\mechanical{p_k}-\mechanical{m}\dot{\mechanical{q_k}})^2
+\frac{\mechanical{m}\dot{\mechanical{q_k}}^2}{2}.
$$
The Gaussian momentum integral gives
$$
\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\mechanical{p_k}}{2\pi}
e^{-i\mechanical{\epsilon}
(\mechanical{p_k}-\mechanical{m}\dot{\mechanical{q_k}})^2/(2\mechanical{m})}
=\sqrt{\frac{\mechanical{m}}{2\pi i\mechanical{\epsilon}}}.
$$
There are $N$ momentum integrals but only $N-1$ intermediate position integrals. This explains the power $N/2$ of the normalization below. Each slice contributes inverse length, and each integrated intermediate coordinate contributes length, so the complete kernel has inverse-length units. The normalization does not disappear when it is put into $\mathcal{D}\mechanical{q}$.

Insert this result:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{K}
={}&\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}}{2\pi i\mechanical{\epsilon}}\right)^{N/2}
\int\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}\mathrm{d}\mechanical{q_k}\\
&\times\exp\left[
i\mechanical{\epsilon}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
\left(\frac{\mechanical{m}\dot{\mechanical{q_k}}^2}{2}
-\energy{V}(\mechanical{q_k})\right)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
As $N\to\infty$ and $\mechanical{\epsilon}\to0$, the exponent's sum becomes
$\int_{\mechanical{t_i}}^{\mechanical{t_f}}
\mathrm{d}\mechanical{t}\,\energy{L}
=\energy{S}[\mechanical{q}]$.
The product of integrals defines the functional measure
$\mathcal{D}\mechanical{q}$.
This gives Feynman's formula (11.2), with the normalization factor absorbed into the measure definition.

> [!note]
> The finite-step expressions use the small-time approximation from the previous page. Their equality to the full kernel is obtained in the time-slicing limit. The oscillatory Gaussian is defined with its usual convergence prescription.

> [!note]
> **Remark 11.1.** The derivation identifies the phase-space path integral as the more general starting point:
> $$
> \signal{K}
> =\int\mathcal{D}\mechanical{q}\mathcal{D}\mechanical{p}\,
> \exp\left[
> i\int_{\mechanical{t_i}}^{\mechanical{t_f}}
> \mathrm{d}\mechanical{t}
> \big(\mechanical{p}\dot{\mechanical{q}}
> -\energy{H}(\mechanical{p},\mechanical{q})\big)\right].
> $$
> For $\energy{H}=\mechanical{p}^2/(2\mechanical{m})+\energy{V}(\mechanical{q})$, the momentum integrals are Gaussian and give the configuration-space Lagrangian path integral. For more general Hamiltonians, working in phase space can be useful.

The left-endpoint potential in this discretization is appropriate to the stated kinetic-plus-potential Hamiltonian. A momentum-dependent interaction or a position-dependent kinetic coefficient requires an operator-ordering and discretization prescription. One cannot simply replace the classical Hamiltonian in the last phase-space formula and assume that all such choices give the same quantum operator.

Set the potential to zero as a reconstruction check: successive Gaussian slice integrals must reduce to (11.4), with the total interval replacing the slice width. For the relation between the finite convolution and the continuum path integral, see [Skinner, §3.1.1, pp. 44–46](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/dbs26/AQFT/chap3.pdf#page=3).

| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The time integral of the Lagrangian in natural units. |
