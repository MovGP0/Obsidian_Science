> [!TLDR]
> The path integral describes quantum evolution through sums over histories weighted by the action. It connects the Lagrangian formulation with the classical limit.

## Chapter introduction

> “The career of a young theoretical physicist consists of treating the harmonic oscillator in ever-increasing levels of abstraction.”
>
> — Sidney Coleman

The functional-integral formulation, developed mainly by Richard Feynman in the 1940s, provides a systematic reformulation of quantum mechanics and quantum field theory.
The canonical operator and commutator formalism makes symmetry and statistical-mechanical structure explicit.
The path integral instead emphasizes the theory's Lagrangian structure and its connection with the stationary-action principle in the classical limit.

It is a central tool for gauge theories, fermions, and nonperturbative investigations.

The central question is how an action can determine a quantum transition amplitude. A classical action selects stationary histories. A quantum amplitude also includes fluctuations about them and interference between different histories. The path integral retains that information; it is not an average over classical trajectories with positive probabilities.

This chapter assumes ordinary quantum evolution and rewrites it in a new form. The particle examples first fix the endpoint conditions and normalization. The free particle and oscillator then provide exact tests before the method is applied to infinitely many field degrees of freedom. For this comparison between time slicing and operator evolution, see [Skinner, QFT in one dimension, §3.1.1](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/dbs26/AQFT/chap3.pdf#page=2).
