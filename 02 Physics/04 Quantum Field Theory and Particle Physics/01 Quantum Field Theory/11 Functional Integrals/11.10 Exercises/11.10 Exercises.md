> [!TLDR]
> Work through the chapter's exercises with the question, source solution, and correction notes together in each linked article.

Begin with Exercises 11.1–11.4 to test Gaussian normalization and exchange signs. Exercise 11.1 compares periodic modes with the oscillator spectrum; Exercise 11.2 separates full from connected correlations; Exercises 11.3 and 11.4 test determinant multiplicity and measure orientation. These provide exact benchmarks before a loop approximation is used.

Then compare Exercises 11.5 and 11.8: the first approximates the effective action at one loop, whereas the second proves an exact inverse-kernel relation. Finally, Exercises 11.6, 11.7, 11.9, and 11.10 test gauge fixing, conserved-current exchange, and translation identities. A symmetry identity can hold exactly even when solving the underlying dynamics requires an approximation.

Before reading each solution, state the boundary conditions and predict one simple limit or sign. Keep a short convention record: the scalar inverse kernel in this chapter has no numerator i; the field correlator does. A Hessian and a Feynman-rule proper vertex can also differ by i. The correction notes identify where the supplied source changes these conventions or omits a required factor.
