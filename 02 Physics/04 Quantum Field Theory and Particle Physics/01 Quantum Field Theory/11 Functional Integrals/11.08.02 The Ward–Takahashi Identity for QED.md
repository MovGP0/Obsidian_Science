---
title: "The Ward–Takahashi Identity for QED"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The QED Ward–Takahashi identity relates the electron–photon vertex to the inverse electron propagator and gives Z₁ = Z₂.

For QED, local gauge symmetry acts as
$$
\signal{\psi}\to e^{i\alpha(\mechanical{x})}\signal{\psi},
\qquad
\bar{\signal{\psi}}\to e^{-i\alpha(\mechanical{x})}\bar{\signal{\psi}},
\qquad
\electric{A}_\mu\to\electric{A}_\mu-\frac{\partial_\mu\alpha}{\electric{e}}.
$$
This agrees with the covariant derivative
$D_\mu=\partial_\mu+i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu$ and the interaction
$\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{int}}
=-\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\not{\electric{A}}\signal{\psi}$.
The electromagnetic current is conserved:
$$
\electric{j}^\mu=\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
Apply a local gauge transformation with infinitesimal parameter $\alpha(\mechanical{x})$ in the functional integral.
The gauge-fixed action is not invariant: the gauge-fixing term
$(\partial^\mu\electric{A}_\mu)^2/(2\xi)$ varies.
The action and measure together have a controlled transformation.
The source gives the following momentum-space identity:
$$
\mechanical{k}^\mu
\langle0|T\{\electric{A}_\mu(\mechanical{k})\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{p})
\bar{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})\}|0\rangle
=-\electric{e}\left[
\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})-\signal{S}_F(\mechanical{p})
\right].
\tag{11.34}
$$
It relates the three-point vertex $\Gamma^\mu$ to the electron propagator:
$$
\mechanical{k}_\mu\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})
=\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})-\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p}).
\tag{11.35}
$$
A tree-level check fixes the stripped-vertex convention. The inverse free Dirac kernel is the slashed momentum minus the mass; its difference at the two electron momenta is the slashed photon momentum. Contracting the stripped tree vertex, the gamma matrix, gives exactly that difference. Keeping a factor of electric charge in only one side would fail this check.

In the limit $\mechanical{k}\to0$, this gives the differential Ward identity:
$$
\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p})
=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mechanical{p}_\mu}\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p}).
\tag{11.36}
$$
Thus, the vertex is related to the momentum derivative of the inverse propagator.

This differential form assumes that the regulated or renormalized functions admit the stated small-momentum limit. Singular infrared limits must be handled before using an ordinary derivative. The finite-momentum identity is the safer starting point when such a limit is not smooth.

> [!note]
> Equation (11.34) preserves the source's displayed expression. A raw three-point correlator with an unamputated photon leg also contains the longitudinal photon-propagator factor, proportional to $\xi/\mechanical{k}^2$, with factors of $i$ fixed by convention. The displayed relation requires a suitable photon-amputation convention. In (11.35) and (11.36), $\Gamma^\mu$ denotes the proper vertex with the electric coupling removed, and the inverse-propagator convention must be chosen consistently.
>
> The source's preceding sentence suggests that the identity prevents charge renormalization beyond tree level. Its continuation correctly identifies the photon factor as the remaining correction. More precisely, the identity gives $Z_1=Z_2$, so the electron vertex and field factors cancel in charge renormalization. Photon-field renormalization $Z_3$ still changes the charge and produces QED running.

## Physical consequences

The QED Ward identities imply:

1. **$Z_1=Z_2$.** Vertex renormalization equals fermion-field renormalization.

The bare and renormalized charges obey
$$
\electric{e}_0=\frac{Z_1}{Z_2}Z_3^{-1/2}\electric{e}.
$$
Thus, after $Z_1=Z_2$, only $Z_3$ (photon renormalization) changes the charge.

2. **The photon mass remains zero.** Corrections to the photon propagator are transverse:
   $$
   \electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}(\mechanical{k})
   =\left(g^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{k}^\mu\mechanical{k}^\nu\right)
   \electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{k}^2).
   $$
3. **Unitarity of the S-matrix.** Unphysical longitudinal and scalar polarization modes cancel from physical amplitudes.

These consequences also need their physical qualifications. In perturbative QED, transversality together with the absence of a mass-generating singularity in the scalar polarization keeps the photon pole massless. Transversality by itself is not a general theorem against every possible infrared pole. The amplitude cancellation applies to conserved physical external currents and a consistent treatment of the physical state space; the displayed vertex identity alone is not a complete proof of S-matrix unitarity.

The identity constrains the component of the vertex contracted with photon momentum. It does not determine every transverse vertex structure, so it does not set all radiative vertex effects to zero. In particular, the equality of renormalization factors is compatible with a nonzero finite vertex correction.

## Example 11.7: Explicit one-loop check

Consider the one-loop QED vertex.
The Ward–Takahashi identity (11.35) gives a precise relation between the vertex correction $\delta\Gamma^\mu$ and the self-energy correction to $\signal{S}_F^{-1}$.
Write
$$
\Gamma^\mu=\gamma^\mu+\delta\Gamma^\mu,
\qquad
\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p})
=\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}).
$$
Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{k}_\mu(\gamma^\mu+\delta\Gamma^\mu)
={}&\left(\not{\mechanical{p}}+\not{\mechanical{k}}-\mechanical{m}
-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})\right)\\
&-\left(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p})\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $\mechanical{k}_\mu\gamma^\mu=\not{\mechanical{k}}$,
$$
\mechanical{k}_\mu\delta\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p},\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k})
=\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p})-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}+\mechanical{k}).
$$
This is a nontrivial consistency check at each perturbative order.

> [!note]
> This example derives the relation that the one-loop integrals must satisfy; it does not evaluate the loop integrals explicitly. The transversality statement assumes that gauge symmetry is preserved by regularization and renormalization.

For the one-loop relation, take the photon momentum to zero only after checking smoothness. Expanding the self-energy difference gives minus its momentum derivative, which is exactly the correction to the inverse free propagator in (11.36). Both sides of (11.35) have mass dimension one; the stripped vertex is dimensionless. These are useful checks on a calculation, but the example still does not evaluate a loop integral.
