---
title: "Schwinger–Dyson Equations from the Functional Integral"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Schwinger–Dyson equations are quantum equations of motion for Green functions. They follow from changes of variables in the functional integral.

The Schwinger–Dyson (SD) equations follow directly from the functional integral and provide quantum equations of motion for Green functions.
A scalar-field derivation is given in [[11.07.01 Derivation for the Scalar Field]].

The question is how to retain quantum fluctuations in an equation of motion. Replacing the field by its expectation value loses correlations of nonlinear terms. Instead, integration by parts in field space relates complete correlators, including terms that occur when the variation acts on an inserted field.

The derivation assumes that the regulated integral has no contribution from the boundary of its integration domain. The vacuum contour supplies the causal boundary condition. These assumptions are needed before an integral of a total derivative can be set to zero.

The identities are exact for the specified theory, but the hierarchy is not a closed calculation: the equation for a two-point function contains higher correlations. A finite truncation needs an additional approximation and must be tested against the free limit and relevant Ward identities. In the free limit the hierarchy closes on the inverse quadratic operator, giving an immediate normalization and sign check.
