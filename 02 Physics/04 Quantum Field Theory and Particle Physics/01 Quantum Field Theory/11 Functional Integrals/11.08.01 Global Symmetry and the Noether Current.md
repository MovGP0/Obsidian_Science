---
title: "Global Symmetry and the Noether Current"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A symmetry variation in the functional integral relates the divergence of the Noether-current insertion to contact terms at the other field insertions.

Consider a theory with a global symmetry,
$$
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})\to\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})
+\epsilon\,\delta\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}).
$$
The symmetry is assumed continuous, so an infinitesimal parameter and a Noether current exist. To obtain a local statement, first let that parameter vary with spacetime with compact support. The terms containing its derivative identify the current; integration by parts then isolates the current divergence. Only after this step can one compare the coefficient at each spacetime point.

The functional measure is invariant. The action varies as
$$
\delta\energy{S}=\epsilon\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,
\partial_\mu\signal{j}_N^\mu.
$$
Here $\signal{j}_N^\mu$ is the Noether current.
Invariance of the functional integral under this transformation implies
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\left\langle
\partial_\mu\signal{j}_N^\mu(\mechanical{x})\,
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\cdots\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_n)
\right\rangle\\
&\quad=-i\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta^{(4)}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{x}_k)
\left\langle
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\cdots
\delta\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_k)\cdots
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_n)
\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11.33}
$$
The terms on the right come from the variation of the insertion operator
$\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_1)\cdots\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}_n)$.
Each point at which the transformation acts on an insertion gives a delta-function contact term.

> [!note]
> The local identity follows by making the symmetry parameter depend on spacetime and comparing the coefficient at each point. The action-variation formula above is the source's integrated shorthand. Its sign needs a correction when it is used locally: for the current convention of (11.33), integration by parts gives minus the integral of the local parameter times the current divergence. Indeed, $0=\langle\delta O\rangle+i\langle\delta\energy{S}\,O\rangle$, where $O$ is the product of insertions. A positive local action-variation sign would instead give positive-i contact terms. The displayed source shorthand and the negative-i contacts therefore cannot be used together without this sign correction.
>
> The correlators are time ordered. The identity assumes an invariant measure and a symmetry-preserving regulator; an anomalous measure adds an extra term.

At a point separated from every insertion, the right side of (11.33) vanishes: the current is conserved inside the correlator there. Integrating the identity over a small region containing one insertion instead keeps that insertion's contact term. This expresses the charge acting on that field, rather than a failure of conservation. Dropping the contact terms would give the wrong transformation of charged correlators.

With no field insertions, the identity reduces to the vanishing divergence of the current expectation value, provided no boundary flux or anomaly has been added. The invariant-measure assumption is part of the quantum statement. For the change-of-variables and local-parameter derivation, see [Skinner, §§6.3–6.3.1, pp. 123–125](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/dbs26/AQFT/chap6.pdf#page=8).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi},\delta\signal{\phi}$ | — | Field and symmetry variation | Their units depend on the field representation. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The action varied under the symmetry in natural units. |
| $\signal{j}_N^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Noether current | The current associated with the global symmetry in four dimensions. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{x}_1,\ldots,\mechanical{x}_n$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinates | The current position and the field insertion positions. |
