---
title: "Functional Integrals for Fermions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Fermionic functional integrals use anticommuting Grassmann variables to represent fermion fields.

The algebraic construction starts with [[11.05.01 Grassmann Variables]].

The question is how an integral can reproduce antisymmetric fermion contractions. Ordinary commuting integration variables would give symmetric exchanges and the wrong loop determinant. Grassmann variables supply the exchange signs before any integral is evaluated; their values are algebraic objects, not possible measured values of an electron field.

After the algebra, read [[11.05.02 Berezin Integral]] to fix the orientation of the measure. [[11.05.03 Gaussian Integrals over Grassmann Variables]] then shows why paired fermions give a determinant rather than its inverse. [[11.05.04 Fermionic Functional Integral]] applies those rules to the Dirac operator and source derivatives.

Check each convention first with one barred and one unbarred generator. Reversing their order must reverse the sign of their product; integrating the Gaussian with the chapter's measure must return its one matrix coefficient. These two small tests detect sign mistakes that can otherwise appear as an incorrect propagator or fermion-loop sign.
