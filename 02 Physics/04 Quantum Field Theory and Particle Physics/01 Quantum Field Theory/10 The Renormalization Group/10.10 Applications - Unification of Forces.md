> [!TLDR]
> The different Standard Model couplings evolve with scale. Their high-energy comparison motivates studies of force unification.

Unification is a test of an assumed high-energy theory, starting from low-energy coupling inputs. First use [[10.10.01 Coupling Constants of the Standard Model]] to fix normalization and the sign convention. Then read [[10.10.02 Quasi-Unification and Supersymmetry]] to distinguish the Standard Model extrapolation from a changed particle spectrum. [[10.10.03 The Philosophy of Effective Field Theories]] explains how threshold matching connects such descriptions.

Three curves being near one another is weaker than one common crossing. The useful test compares all pairwise meeting scales with the same input conventions and uncertainty treatment. [[10.13.07 Gauge-coupling unification]] makes that distinction explicit with the source's rounded inputs. The comparison is a theoretical extrapolation; it does not itself observe a unified interaction.

The comparison starts with [[10.10.01 Coupling Constants of the Standard Model]].
