---
title: "Wilson's Idea — Integrating Out High-Energy Modes"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Wilson explains renormalization-group behavior by removing high-energy modes and describing their effects in an effective theory.

The perturbative Callan–Symanzik approach is useful, but it leaves a basic question open: why does renormalization-group structure apply across systems with different microscopic details?

Kenneth Wilson's approach, developed in the 1970s, gives a systematic answer.

Consider a field theory with an ultraviolet cutoff $\energy{\Lambda}$, an upper limit on accessible momenta.
This cutoff can represent a physical scale, such as a Planck or quark-compositeness scale, or serve as a formal regulator.
The partition function is
$$
\signal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}\,
\exp(-\energy{S}[\signal{\phi}]),
$$
with all modes satisfying $\lvert\mechanical{k}\rvert<\energy{\Lambda}$ included.

Separate low- and high-momentum modes:
$$
\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{k})
=\underbrace{\signal{\phi}^{<}(\mechanical{k})}_{
\lvert\mechanical{k}\rvert<b\energy{\Lambda}\text{, slow modes}}
+\underbrace{\signal{\phi}^{>}(\mechanical{k})}_{
b\energy{\Lambda}<\lvert\mechanical{k}\rvert<\energy{\Lambda}
\text{, fast modes}}.
$$
Here $0<b<1$, typically $b=e^{-t}$ for $t\ll1$.
Integrate over the fast modes:
$$
\signal{Z}
=\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}^{<}\,
\exp(-\energy{S_{\mathrm{eff}}}[\signal{\phi}^{<}]),
$$
where the effective action is defined by
$$
e^{-\energy{S_{\mathrm{eff}}}[\signal{\phi}^{<}]}
\equiv\int\mathcal{D}\signal{\phi}^{>}\,
e^{-\energy{S}[\signal{\phi}^{<}+\signal{\phi}^{>}]}.
$$

The effective action is defined so that the remaining slow-mode integral gives the same partition function. To compare low-momentum correlation functions, keep their sources or insertions in this construction as well. Fast modes are not set equal to zero: their fluctuations change the coefficients of the slow-field action and can generate new symmetry-allowed operators.

The exponential uses a Euclidean action, with ħ absorbed in natural units. For a local expansion, the slow external momenta must be small compared with the removed scale. Keeping every generated term gives a formal exact relation; a finite operator or loop expansion adds an approximation. If the rescaling is omitted, the cutoff remains reduced rather than returning to its original value.

The effective action has a reduced cutoff $b\energy{\Lambda}$.
To restore the original form with cutoff $\energy{\Lambda}$ and renormalized mass and coupling, the source gives the rescaling
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{x}&\to\mechanical{x}'=b^{-1}\mechanical{x},\\
\signal{\phi}^{<}(\mechanical{x})
&\to\signal{\phi}'(\mechanical{x}')
=b^{(d-2)/2}\signal{\phi}^{<}(\mechanical{x}).
\end{aligned}
$$
It states that the rescaled action has the original form with modified parameters
$\signal{g}\to\signal{g}'(\signal{g},b)$.
The limit $b\to1^-$, or $t\to0^+$, generates the beta function.

> [!note]
> The source's coordinate rescaling does not restore the cutoff as stated. For the active relation $\mechanical{x}'=b^{-1}\mechanical{x}$, the conjugate momentum is $\mechanical{k}'=b\mechanical{k}$, so the reduced cutoff becomes $b^2\energy{\Lambda}$. To restore $\energy{\Lambda}$ after removing modes above $b\energy{\Lambda}$, use $\mechanical{k}'=\mechanical{k}/b$, $\mechanical{x}'=b\mechanical{x}$, and the corresponding canonical scalar-field rescaling $\signal{\phi}'(\mechanical{x}')=b^{-(d-2)/2}\signal{\phi}^{<}(\mechanical{x})$. The printed relations are retained above.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Ultraviolet cutoff | The upper momentum scale before the shell integration. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode momentum | The momentum used to divide slow and fast field modes. |
| $\mechanical{x},\mechanical{x}'$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Original and rescaled coordinates | The position variables conjugate to the momentum variables. |
| $\signal{\phi},\signal{\phi}^{<},\signal{\phi}^{>},\signal{\phi}'$ | — | Original, slow, fast, and rescaled fields | Their dimensions depend on spacetime dimension and position or momentum representation. |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Partition function | The Euclidean functional integral over the field modes. |
| $\energy{S},\energy{S_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ | $1$ | Original and effective Euclidean actions | The dimensionless actions in natural units after division by ħ. |
| $\signal{g},\signal{g}'$ | — | Original and transformed couplings | The parameters of the effective action; their units depend on the operator and dimension. |
