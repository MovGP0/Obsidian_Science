> [!TLDR]
> Renormalization introduces an arbitrary scale. Physical predictions must remain independent of that scale even when intermediate formulas contain it.

## Motivation: the scale problem

The renormalization procedure developed in the preceding chapters makes the theory's predictions finite.
Its parameters are fixed experimentally, such as the fine-structure constant $\electric{\alpha}$, the electron mass, and the Lamb shift.

However, the resulting expressions contain a formal ambiguity.
Consider the first-order QED vacuum-polarization correction to the photon two-point Green function.
After $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ renormalization, for
$-\mechanical{q}^2\gg\mechanical{m}^2$, the source writes
$$
\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=+\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi}
\ln\left(\frac{-\mechanical{q}^2}{\energy{\mu}^2}\right)
+\mathcal{O}(\electric{\alpha}^2).
$$
Here $\energy{\mu}$ is the **renormalization scale** introduced during subtraction.
The physical theory cannot depend on this arbitrary choice of subtraction convention. Yet the displayed expression contains $\energy{\mu}$ explicitly.

> [!note]
> The expression shows the leading one-loop logarithm. Equation (9.57) also gives a finite one-loop constant in the high-momentum $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ limit. Thus the source's remainder $\mathcal{O}(\electric{\alpha}^2)$ does not account for every omitted term unless a corresponding finite subtraction convention is understood.

The apparent paradox is resolved because the renormalized parameters also depend on $\energy{\mu}$.
The coupling $\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu})$ and mass
$\mechanical{m}(\energy{\mu})$ are functions of scale.
When $\mechanical{q}^2$ changes, choose
$\energy{\mu}^2\sim-\mechanical{q}^2$ to avoid large logarithms.
Then use the value of $\electric{\alpha}(\energy{\mu})$ at that new scale.
The scale dependence of the coefficients is compensated by that of the parameters.

The cancellation refers to a fixed physical process: keep the external momentum and the measured input fixed while changing the subtraction scale. Changing the physical momentum is a different operation. It can change the measured response, even though changing the arbitrary subtraction scale cannot.

At a finite loop order, the cancellation is only accurate through that order. A remaining scale dependence estimates omitted terms; it is not a new measurable parameter. The logarithm is dimensionless because its numerator and denominator both have mass dimension two. Choosing the subtraction scale near the momentum scale reduces this logarithm, but does not control the expansion if the coupling itself is large.

This is the structure of the **renormalization group (RG)**.
The physics is independent of $\energy{\mu}$, but different parameter values at different scales can describe the same physics.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The electromagnetic coupling in the one-loop correction. |
| $\electric{\Pi}$ | $1$ | Scalar vacuum-polarization function | The renormalized scalar coefficient of the photon two-point correction. |
| $\mechanical{q}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | External photon momentum | The spacelike momentum in the high-energy logarithm. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron mass | The mass compared with the large spacelike momentum scale. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The arbitrary scale introduced by the subtraction convention. |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Generic running coupling | The coupling plotted against the logarithm of the scale ratio. |
| $\signal{\beta}$ | $1$ | Generic beta function | Its sign determines the direction of the displayed coupling evolution. |
| $\energy{\mu_0}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Reference renormalization scale | The fixed scale in the horizontal logarithmic ratio. |
