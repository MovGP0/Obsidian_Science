---

> [!TLDR]
> The Wilsonian renormalization group explains scale evolution by integrating out high-energy field modes.

This route asks which microscopic details remain visible to a long-wavelength probe. Begin with [[10.06.01 Wilson's Idea - Integrating Out High-Energy Modes]] to define the effective action. Then use [[10.06.02 Relevant, Marginal, and Irrelevant Operators]] to rank its interactions, and [[10.06.03 Theory Space and Basins of Attraction]] to explain universality.

Integrating out modes and rescaling are separate steps. The first preserves low-energy predictions when all generated interactions are retained; the second puts actions at different cutoffs into comparable coordinates. A practical truncation keeps only selected interactions, so its accuracy must be assessed. Also track flow direction: increasing coarse-graining length means decreasing energy, the reverse of the scale convention used in the earlier beta functions.

The Wilsonian view starts from [[10.06.01 Wilson's Idea - Integrating Out High-Energy Modes]].
