---
title: "Scale Anomalies and Conformal Invariance"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A nonzero beta function expresses quantum breaking of classical scale invariance. Fixed points remove this running contribution.

An **anomaly** is the failure of a classical symmetry to survive quantization.
The **scale anomaly** is the breaking of scale invariance by quantum corrections, expressed through a nonzero beta function.

At a fixed point with $\signal{\beta}(\signal{g^*})=0$, the source describes the theory as scale invariant at the quantum level.

In two-dimensional conformal theories, such as theories used for strings or two-dimensional critical systems, conformal symmetry gives strong constraints.
The source describes it as fully determining the theory's structure.

In four dimensions, interacting conformal theories are less common.
A prominent example is $\mathcal{N}=4$ super-Yang–Mills theory, whose gauge beta function satisfies $\signal{\beta}(\signal{g})=0$ at every perturbative order.

> [!note]
> Conformal symmetry strongly constrains a two-dimensional theory, but it does not alone select all its data. Its spectrum and operator-product coefficients are also needed. A fixed-point claim assumes that all running couplings and relevant deformations have been treated consistently.

The fixed-point test concerns the complete massless theory in flat space. A mass term supplies an explicit scale even when a gauge beta function vanishes. Moreover, conformal symmetry includes transformations beyond uniform scale changes; its status needs the appropriate assumptions about the stress tensor and the theory.

There is also a separate curved-background qualification: a conformal fixed point can retain a Weyl anomaly in curved space even when its flat-space couplings do not run. Thus zero beta function does not force every trace-anomaly coefficient to vanish. This distinction is visible in the curved-space discussion of [Cardy's c-theorem notes, p. 2](https://www-thphys.physics.ox.ac.uk/people/JohnCardy/cft14/ctheoremnotes.pdf).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\beta}$ | $1$ | Beta function | The scale derivative whose nonzero value contributes to the scale anomaly. |
| $\signal{g^*}$ | $1$ | Fixed-point coupling | The coupling value at which the beta function vanishes. |
| $\signal{g}$ | $1$ | Gauge coupling | The coupling in the super-Yang–Mills beta-function statement. |
