## Exercise 10.8: Relevant, marginal, and irrelevant operators

Consider the general Lagrangian for a real scalar field in $d=4$ with symmetry $\signal{\phi}\to-\signal{\phi}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal{L}}
={}&\frac12(\partial_\mu\signal{\phi})^2
-\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\signal{\phi}^2
-\frac{\signal{\lambda}}{4!}\signal{\phi}^4\\
&-\frac{\signal{\lambda_6}}{\energy{\Lambda}^2\,6!}\signal{\phi}^6
-\frac{\signal{\lambda_8}}{\energy{\Lambda}^4\,8!}\signal{\phi}^8
-\cdots.
\end{aligned}
$$

(a) Determine the mass dimension of each operator $\signal{\phi}^{2n}$ in $d=4$.

(b) Classify the operators as relevant, marginal, or irrelevant.

(c) Estimate the contribution of irrelevant operators to a low-energy observable with $\energy{E}\ll\energy{\Lambda}$.
Show that it is suppressed by powers
$(\energy{E}/\energy{\Lambda})^{2n-4}$.

## Solution 10.8 — Relevant, marginal, and irrelevant operators

Start with the kinetic term, since the action must be dimensionless in natural units. This fixes the field dimension and then the dimensions of all powers of the field. Classify the field operator separately from its coefficient: the explicit cutoff powers in the Lagrangian make the named higher-operator coefficients dimensionless without making the operators marginal.

For the suppression estimate, assume that the process energy sets the typical momentum and field scale and that the dimensionless coefficients are not anomalously large. The estimate is a power-counting guide; a particular observable also depends on its external states, symmetry selection rules, and possible interference. Large background fields can invalidate an estimate based on energy alone. At the cutoff, the energy-ratio expansion no longer orders the omitted terms.

**(a)** In four dimensions, the scalar field has canonical mass dimension one. The kinetic operator $(\partial\signal{\phi})^2$ has dimension four, the measure $d^4\mechanical{x}$ has dimension minus four, and each Lagrangian-density term must have dimension four.

For a term $\signal{\lambda}_{2n}\signal{\phi}^{2n}/\energy{\Lambda}^{2n-4}$,
$$
[\signal{\phi}^{2n}]=2n,\qquad
\left[\frac{\signal{\lambda}_{2n}}{\energy{\Lambda}^{2n-4}}\right]=4-2n.
$$
The coefficient $\signal{\lambda}_{2n}$ is dimensionless because the explicit cutoff power carries the required mass dimension.

**(b)** For the operator $\mathcal O_n=\signal{\phi}^{2n}$, the canonical operator dimension is $2n$.

Near the Gaussian fixed point in four dimensions, the classification is:

| Operator | Canonical dimension | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}^2$ | $2<4$ | Relevant mass operator. |
| $\signal{\phi}^4$ | $4$ | Marginal by engineering dimension. |
| $\signal{\phi}^6$ | $6>4$ | Irrelevant. |
| $\signal{\phi}^{2n}$ with $n\ge3$ | $2n>4$ | Irrelevant. |

Quantum corrections determine the running of canonically marginal couplings.

**(c)** For an energy scale $\energy{E}\ll\energy{\Lambda}$, dimensional analysis gives the contribution of a dimension-$2n$ operator relative to an energy-density scale $\energy{E}^4$:
$$
\frac{\signal{\lambda}_{2n}\signal{\phi}^{2n}}{\energy{\Lambda}^{2n-4}}
\bigg|_{\signal{\phi}\sim\energy{E}}
\sim\frac{\signal{\lambda}_{2n}\energy{E}^{2n}}{\energy{\Lambda}^{2n-4}}
=\signal{\lambda}_{2n}\energy{E}^4
\left(\frac{\energy{E}}{\energy{\Lambda}}\right)^{2n-4}.
$$
The suppression is $(\energy{E}/\energy{\Lambda})^2$ for $n=3$ and
$(\energy{E}/\energy{\Lambda})^4$ for $n=4$. Thus higher-dimension operators become small in the infrared, which permits an effective theory organized by increasing powers of the energy-to-cutoff ratio. This is an expansion at a stated accuracy, with possible logarithmic running of its coefficients.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal{L}}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Lagrangian density | The scalar effective theory organized by operator dimension. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The four-dimensional scalar field has canonical mass dimension one. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar mass | The mass parameter of the theory. |
| $\signal{\lambda}$ | $1$ | Quartic coupling | The dimensionless coefficient of the four-dimensional quartic operator. |
| $\signal{\lambda_6},\signal{\lambda_8},\signal{\lambda}_{2n}$ | $1$ | Scalar operator coefficients | The dimensionless coefficients after explicit cutoff powers are extracted. |
| $\energy{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Effective-theory cutoff | The high-energy suppression scale in the scalar operator expansion. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Low process energy | The energy used to estimate the higher-operator contributions. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinate | The integration coordinate in the four-dimensional action. |
