## Exercise 10.7: Force unification

Use the one-loop evolution equations for the gauge couplings.

For the Standard Model, use
$$
\signal{\alpha_i}^{-1}(\energy{\mu})
=\signal{\alpha_i}^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
-\frac{b_i}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{M}_Z},
$$
with $b_1=41/10$, $b_2=-19/6$, $b_3=-7$, and reference values
$\signal{\alpha_1}^{-1}=59$,
$\signal{\alpha_2}^{-1}=29$, and
$\signal{\alpha_3}^{-1}=8.5$ at $\mechanical{M}_Z$.

(a) Find the pairwise meeting scales
$\energy{\mu_{12}}$, $\energy{\mu_{13}}$, and $\energy{\mu_{23}}$.
Do these scales agree?

(b) In the MSSM, use
$b_1^{\mathrm{SUSY}}=33/5$,
$b_2^{\mathrm{SUSY}}=1$, and
$b_3^{\mathrm{SUSY}}=-3$.
Repeat the calculation. The source asks for a common meeting near
$\energy{\mu_{\mathrm{GUT}}}\approx2\times10^{16}\,\unit{GeV}$.

> [!note]
> The supplied rounded reference couplings and one-loop coefficients give an approximate unification comparison. They do not enforce three exactly coincident pairwise crossings. Precision matching also requires the particle thresholds and scheme choices.

## Solution 10.7 — Gauge-coupling unification

Subtract two inverse-coupling equations before exponentiating. Their difference eliminates any proposed common coupling and isolates a crossing scale. Repeat for all three pairs: one equality is not enough to establish unification.

Because the scale is exponential in the logarithmic crossing variable, a modest change in that variable can produce a large ratio of meeting energies. This explains why nearby inverse-coupling lines need not give pairwise energies that agree to the same percentage. The rounded inputs and fixed particle coefficients below define the approximation. A physical supersymmetric extrapolation also needs a superpartner threshold prescription, rather than applying the MSSM coefficients at every scale by default.

**(a)** A pairwise meeting condition is
$$
\signal{\alpha}_i^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
-\frac{b_i}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{M}_Z}
=
\signal{\alpha}_j^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
-\frac{b_j}{2\pi}\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{M}_Z}.
$$
It gives
$$
\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}_{ij}}{\mechanical{M}_Z}
=\frac{2\pi[
\signal{\alpha}_i^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)
-\signal{\alpha}_j^{-1}(\mechanical{M}_Z)]}{b_i-b_j}.
$$
The source uses the rounded inputs
$$
\signal{\alpha}_1^{-1}=59,\qquad
\signal{\alpha}_2^{-1}=29,\qquad
\signal{\alpha}_3^{-1}=8.5,
$$
at $\mechanical{M}_Z$, and $b_1=4.1$, $b_2\approx-3.17$, $b_3=-7$.
The first inverse coupling decreases with scale while the second increases:
$$
\signal{\alpha}_1^{-1}(\energy{\mu})=59-\frac{4.1}{2\pi}L,
\qquad
\signal{\alpha}_2^{-1}(\energy{\mu})\approx29+\frac{3.17}{2\pi}L,
\qquad L=\ln\frac{\energy{\mu}}{\mechanical{M}_Z}.
$$
Their meeting gives
$$
L_{12}\approx\frac{30\cdot2\pi}{7.27}\approx25.93,
$$
$$
\energy{\mu}_{12}\approx\mechanical{M}_Ze^{25.93}
\approx1.66\times10^{13}\unit{GeV}.
$$
Similarly,
$$
L_{13}
=\frac{(59-8.5)2\pi}{4.1+7}
=\frac{50.5\cdot2\pi}{11.1}
\approx28.58.
$$
The source's numerical steps use the rounded $b_2$ above; the exercise gives its exact one-loop value $-19/6$.

The source's rounded SM calculation gives
$$
\energy{\mu}_{13}\approx2.38\times10^{14}\unit{GeV},
$$
$$
L_{23}\approx
\frac{(29-8.5)2\pi}{-3.17+7}
=\frac{20.5\cdot2\pi}{3.83}
\approx33.63,
\qquad
\energy{\mu}_{23}\approx3.7\times10^{16}\unit{GeV}.
$$
The three SM scales differ by more than three orders of magnitude. The supplied one-loop inputs do not give exact unification.

**(b)** For the MSSM, use
$b_1=33/5=6.6$, $b_2=1$, and $b_3=-3$:
$$
L_{12}=\frac{30\cdot2\pi}{6.6-1}\approx33.66,
$$
$$
L_{13}=\frac{50.5\cdot2\pi}{6.6+3}\approx33.05,
\qquad
L_{23}=\frac{20.5\cdot2\pi}{1+3}\approx32.20.
$$
The scales are in a common high-energy region. The source summarizes them by $L\sim33$, giving
$$
\energy{\mu}_{\mathrm{GUT}}\sim\mechanical{M}_Ze^{33}
\approx2\times10^{16}\unit{GeV}.
$$

> [!note] Numerical precision of the meeting claim
> The source calls this agreement approximately 1%. Direct evaluation of the supplied MSSM inputs gives pairwise scales $3.79\times10^{16}$, $2.06\times10^{16}$, and $8.81\times10^{15}\unit{GeV}$. At $L=33$, the three inverse couplings are approximately $24.34$, $23.75$, and $24.26$, a spread of about 2.4%. The comparison is approximate and does not give one exactly common crossing. Threshold and scheme corrections are needed for a precision calculation.
>
> Using the exact SM coefficient $b_2=-19/6$ rather than the source's rounded $-3.17$ gives SM scales near $1.68\times10^{13}$, $2.37\times10^{14}$, and $3.57\times10^{16}\unit{GeV}$; the noncoincidence remains.
