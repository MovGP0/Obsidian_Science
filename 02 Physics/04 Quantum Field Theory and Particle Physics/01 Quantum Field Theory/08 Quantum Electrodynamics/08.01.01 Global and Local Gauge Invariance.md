---
title: "Global and Local Gauge Invariance"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A constant phase rotation leaves the free Dirac Lagrangian invariant and gives a conserved current; local phase rotations require an additional field.

The starting point is the free Dirac Lagrangian studied in Chapter 4:
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}
=\bar{\signal{\psi}}(i\not\partial-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}.
$$

This density is invariant under the global phase transformation
$$
\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\to e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x}),
\qquad
\bar{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{x})\to e^{+i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}}\bar{\signal{\psi}}(\mechanical{x}),
$$
where $\signal{\alpha}$ is constant and $\electric{e}$ is the charge parameter of the field. With the covariant derivative $D_\mu=\partial_\mu+i\electric{e}\electric{A}_\mu$ of [[08.01.02 Covariant Derivative and Minimal Coupling]] and $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\electric{J}^\mu\electric{A}_\mu$, the current is $\electric{J}^\mu=\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}$. Thus the particles annihilated by $\signal{\psi}$ carry charge $\electric{e}$, and their antiparticles carry $-\electric{e}$. For the electron field, set $\electric{e}=-|\electric{e}|$, so that $\electric{Q}_e=\electric{e}=-|\electric{e}|$. Quantization alone does not select this sign. Positron interactions with an external field have the opposite sign.

> [!note]
> The source uses $\electric{e}>0$ and states that the electron charge $\electric{Q}_e=-|\electric{e}|$ follows from quantization. With the conventions above, $\electric{e}>0$ instead gives charge $+\electric{e}$ to the particles annihilated by $\signal{\psi}$.

Noether's theorem gives conservation of
$$
\signal{j}^\mu=\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}.
$$
Multiplication by the charge gives the electromagnetic current.

A constant phase change acts equally at all spacetime points, so differentiation cannot detect it. A local change acts differently at nearby points. The kinetic term compares those points and therefore acquires a derivative of the phase. This is the specific obstruction that the gauge field must remove; the mass term contains no derivative and has no such obstruction.

Can this global symmetry become a local symmetry, with a spacetime-dependent parameter $\signal{\alpha}=\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x})$? The required transformation is
$$
\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x})\to
e^{-i\electric{e}\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x})}\signal{\psi}(\mechanical{x}).
$$

The mass term $\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}$ is invariant, but the kinetic term transforms as
$$
\bar{\signal{\psi}}\,i\not\partial\,\signal{\psi}
\to
\bar{\signal{\psi}}\,i\not\partial\,\signal{\psi}
+\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}\,\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}(\mechanical{x}).
$$
An extra term proportional to $\partial_\mu\signal{\alpha}$ appears. The ordinary derivative must be modified to restore invariance.

Set the gauge parameter to a constant as a check: its derivative vanishes and the global result is recovered. The conserved number current and the electromagnetic current must still be distinguished. Current conservation fixes neither the magnitude nor the naming convention for the electron charge; use the explicit sign caution in [[08.01.02 Covariant Derivative and Minimal Coupling]] when translating between conventions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Dirac}}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Lagrangian density | The free Dirac density in natural units. |
| $\signal{\psi},\bar{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac fields | The fermion field and its adjoint. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fermion mass | The mass in the free Dirac equation. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime coordinate | The argument of the fields and local phase. |
| $\electric{e},\electric{Q}_e$ | $1$ | Electric charge parameters | The charge parameter of the field, equal to $-\lvert\electric{e}\rvert$ for the electron, and the physical electron charge. |
| $\electric{A}_\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electromagnetic potential | The gauge field in the covariant derivative. |
| $\electric{J}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Electromagnetic current | The charge parameter times the number current. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Interaction density | The minimal coupling of the current to the potential. |
| $\signal{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Gauge parameter | The constant or local phase parameter. |
| $\signal{j}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV^3}$ | Conserved number current | The Dirac current before multiplication by charge. |
