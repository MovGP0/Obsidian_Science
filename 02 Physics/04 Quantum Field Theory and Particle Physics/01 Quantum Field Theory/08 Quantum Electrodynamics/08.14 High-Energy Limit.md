---
title: "High-Energy Limit"
---

> [!TLDR]
> At energies much larger than the fermion masses, QED amplitudes simplify into helicity sectors and high-energy scattering limits.

High collision energy does not specify a unique approximation. In [[08.14.01 Chiral Structure of the Amplitudes]], masses are small relative to hard momenta and helicity selection simplifies the spin dependence. In [[08.14.02 Regge Limit]], energy increases while momentum transfer stays fixed, so the scattering becomes increasingly forward.

Keep these limits distinct. A fixed-angle massless result need not be uniform near a forward or collinear pole. Before discarding a mass, identify whether it only gives a small correction or also regulates a singular angular region. The distinction explains why high-energy total cross sections can retain mass logarithms after fixed-angle amplitudes have simplified.
