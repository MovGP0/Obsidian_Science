---
title: "Helicity Asymmetries"
---

> [!TLDR]
> At high energy, the vector photon coupling preserves chirality and gives two nonzero final helicity configurations for each allowed initial pair.

A helicity-resolved angular distribution can be asymmetric even when the final-helicity sum is symmetric. The two channels below show how this happens: one favors the forward direction and the other the backward direction. The displayed formulas are tree-level photon exchange with negligible lepton masses.

For longitudinally polarized fermions with definite helicity in the ultrarelativistic limit $\sqrt{\energy{s}}\gg\mechanical{m}$, the photon vector coupling $\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}$ preserves chirality. The virtual photon couples to the pairs $\mathrm e_L^-\mathrm e_R^+$ or $\mathrm e_R^-\mathrm e_L^+$.

> [!note]
> The external-state labels $L,R$ here denote helicity in the massless limit. An antifermion's helicity is opposite to the chirality of its $v$ spinor. This distinction makes the source's statement about antifermion chirality precise.

For each allowed initial helicity configuration, only two final helicity amplitudes are nonzero. Explicitly, at vanishing mass,
$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
(\mathrm e_L^-\mathrm e_R^+\to\mu_L^-\mu_R^+)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}^2}{4\energy{s}}
(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2,
$$
$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
(\mathrm e_L^-\mathrm e_R^+\to\mu_R^-\mu_L^+)
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}^2}{4\energy{s}}
(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2.
$$

The same formulas hold with $L\leftrightarrow R$. The source states that “the other four helicity combinations,” with equal chirality on both sides of a photon vertex, vanish.

> [!note]
> For a full assignment of the four external helicities there are $16$ combinations. A massless vector coupling leaves four nonzero amplitudes. The source's “other four” therefore does not count all remaining external-helicity assignments.

The angular factors $(1\pm\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2$ reflect angular-momentum conservation along the beam axis. The initial state has $\mechanical{J}_z=\pm1$, and rotation between the initial and final quantization axes is described by the Wigner matrices $d^1_{m'm}(\mechanical{\theta})$.

Sum over final helicities for a fixed allowed initial helicity:
$$
\frac{d\mechanical{\sigma}}{d\mechanical{\Omega}}
\propto(1+\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2+(1-\cos\mechanical{\theta})^2
=2(1+\cos^2\mechanical{\theta}).
$$
Average over initial helicities to recover the unpolarized result (8.9).

The sum for one allowed initial pair is twice the unpolarized distribution. There are two such allowed initial pairs and two forbidden pairs. Averaging all four incident assignments therefore restores (8.9), without an extra final-state average.

## Example 8.5 — Forward–backward asymmetry

For an electron–positron collision with a partially polarized beam, define
$$
\mechanical{A}_{FB}
=\frac{\signal{N}_F-\signal{N}_B}{\signal{N}_F+\signal{N}_B}.
$$
Here $\signal{N}_F$ counts negative muons in the hemisphere $\cos\mechanical{\theta}>0$ and $\signal{N}_B$ counts those with $\cos\mechanical{\theta}<0$.

For pure QED, with final helicities summed, $\mechanical{A}_{FB}=0$. The angular distribution is symmetric under $\mechanical{\theta}\to\pi-\mechanical{\theta}$ for a purely vector interaction. The source identifies observation of $\mechanical{A}_{FB}\ne0$ as evidence for photon–$\mathrm Z^0$ interference and thus for the chiral structure of the Standard Model.

> [!note]
> The zero asymmetry above is the tree-level, one-photon result with final helicities summed and symmetric angular acceptance. It is not an all-orders QED theorem. QED radiative interference can also generate a charge and forward–backward asymmetry, so a nonzero measured value does not by itself identify weak exchange. A weak-interaction extraction must include the QED correction and the event cuts; see [Kuraev and collaborators, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0601206).

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{s}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Squared collision energy | The invariant energy scale in the high-energy limit. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fermion mass | The mass neglected in the helicity approximation. |
| $\signal{\psi},\bar{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac fields | The fermion field and adjoint in the vector current. |
| $\mechanical{\sigma}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Helicity cross section | The scattering area for fixed initial and final helicities. |
| $\mechanical{\Omega},\mechanical{\theta}$ | $1$ | Scattering angles | The solid angle and polar deflection angle. |
| $\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The electromagnetic expansion parameter. |
| $\mechanical{J}_z$ | $1$ | Angular-momentum projection | The beam-axis projection in units with reduced Planck constant equal to one. |
| $\mechanical{A}_{FB}$ | $1$ | Forward–backward asymmetry | The normalized difference between forward and backward muon event counts. |
| $\signal{N}_F,\signal{N}_B$ | $1$ | Event counts | The numbers of negative muons in the two hemispheres. |
