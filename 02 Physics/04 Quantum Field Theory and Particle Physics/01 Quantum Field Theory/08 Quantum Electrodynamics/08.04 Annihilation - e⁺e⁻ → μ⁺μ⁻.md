> [!TLDR]
> Electron–positron annihilation into a muon pair proceeds through one virtual photon at tree level.

How does a spin-one electromagnetic interaction distribute a newly produced muon pair in angle? The process $\mathrm e^+\mathrm e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$ gives a clean first calculation because the incoming and outgoing charged particles have different flavors. At lowest electromagnetic order there is one annihilation diagram, so there is no exchange-diagram interference.

Read [[08.04.01 Kinematics and Feynman Diagram]] before [[08.04.02 Feynman Amplitude]]. Then follow [[08.04.03 Calculation of the Squared Amplitude]] through the initial spin average and final spin sum. [[08.04.04 Mandelstam Variables]] separates Lorentz-invariant algebra from frame choice; [[08.04.05 Differential and Total Cross Sections]] supplies flux and phase space.

The final high-energy expression assumes both lepton masses can be neglected and retains photon exchange only. Its inverse-energy-squared scale and forward–backward symmetry are checks of this approximation. Near the muon threshold, the neglected final-state speed cannot be replaced by one.
