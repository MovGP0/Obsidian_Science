---
title: "Index Contractions in d = 4"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Four-dimensional contraction identities shorten products of gamma matrices.

A photon propagator often contracts the first and last gamma matrices of a fermion chain. These identities remove that contracted pair before the remaining trace is evaluated. The reversal of the three interior factors in the last line is essential; the identity is not permission to commute gamma matrices.

In $d=4$, the gamma-matrix contraction identities are
$$
\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu=4\mathbf1,
$$
$$
\gamma^\mu\not a\gamma_\mu=-2\not a,
$$
$$
\gamma^\mu\not a\not b\gamma_\mu
=4(a\cdot b)\mathbf1,
$$
$$
\gamma^\mu\not a\not b\not c\gamma_\mu
=-2\not c\not b\not a.
$$
The four-vectors $a,b,c$ are algebraic arguments. The identity matrix is written explicitly where the source leaves it implicit.

One check on the two-slash identity is to set its arguments equal: the interior pair becomes the vector norm times the identity, and the outer contracted pair supplies the factor four. The coefficients here use exactly four spacetime dimensions. Do not carry them unchanged into dimensional regularization, where the contracted metric trace is the continued dimension.
