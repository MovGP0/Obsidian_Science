---
title: "The Sudakov Double Logarithm"
---

> [!TLDR]
> At high energy, powers of the coupling can be accompanied by large squared logarithms, called Sudakov logarithms.

At very high energy, $\sqrt{\energy{s}}\gg\mechanical{m}$, the expansion contains **Sudakov logarithms** of the form
$$
\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}^n
\ln^{2n}\left(\frac{\energy{s}}{\mechanical{m}^{\,2}}\right).
$$

The large logarithms can make fixed-order perturbation theory inadequate at high energy. One logarithm comes from a broad range of soft energies and another from nearly collinear directions. The expansion must be judged by the coupling multiplied by these logarithms, not by the coupling alone. Resummation collects the repeated enhanced contributions.

The source gives the exclusive electromagnetic form factor in the Sudakov form (1956):
$$
\electric{F}(\energy{s})\sim\electric{F}_0
\exp\left[
-\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}}{4\pi}
\ln^2\frac{|\energy{s}|}{\mechanical{m}^{\,2}}
\right].
$$

> [!note] Soft-factor scope of the form-factor expression
> A nonzero electron mass regulates collinear singularities but does not remove the soft infrared dependence of the full on-shell virtual form factor. The mass-only exponential above gives its leading hard-scale double-logarithmic factor after a separate soft factor has been removed. It is not a complete infrared-finite virtual form factor. The source does not specify that separation here. [Penin, page 2, Eq. (2)](https://inspirehep.net/files/1f51530525e2ab72094a61a8827abba3#page=2) explicitly separates the photon-mass-dependent soft factor from this leading Sudakov factor. [Mastrolia and Remiddi, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0302162) also retain an infrared photon-mass regulator for finite-mass on-shell form factors.

For an exclusive cross section with resolution $\energy{\Delta E}\ll\sqrt{\energy{s}}$,
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{excl}}(\energy{\Delta E})
\sim\mechanical{\sigma}_0
\exp\left[
-\frac{\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}}{\pi}
\ln\frac{\energy{s}}{\mechanical{m}^{\,2}}
\ln\frac{\energy{s}}{(\energy{\Delta E})^2}
\right].
$$
This describes exponential suppression of exclusive states at high energy. As $\sqrt{\energy{s}}\to\infty$, the probability of emitting no soft or collinear photon tends to zero.

> [!note]
> At finite energy resolution, “exclusive” means no resolved real photon. Photons below $\energy{\Delta E}$ remain part of the inclusive unresolved contribution. The source describes this as “without real emission.”

Expanding either exponential to first order gives a negative leading correction, consistent with vetoing radiation rather than adding accepted radiative events. The exponential remains positive when a truncated large negative correction would make a fixed-order rate unphysical. This is a check on the interpretation of suppression, not a proof that the displayed leading-logarithm formulas include all finite terms.

These expressions summarize leading-logarithmic form-factor or photon-veto limits. The virtual form-factor expression needs the soft-factor qualification above; the cross section instead uses a finite photon resolution. Their coefficients and infrared prescriptions should not be transferred to an arbitrary process without derivation. The small-resolution and high-energy limits also differ from a fully inclusive sum over resolved radiation.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{s}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Squared hard energy | The high-energy invariant in the logarithm. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fermion mass | The low mass scale compared with the hard energy. |
| $\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The QED expansion parameter. |
| $\electric{F},\electric{F}_0$ | $1$ | Electromagnetic form factors | The exclusive form factor and its reference value. |
| $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{excl}},\mechanical{\sigma}_0$ | $\unit{MeV^{-2}}$ | Exclusive cross sections | The resolved-photon-veto rate and reference scattering area. |
| $\energy{\Delta E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Energy resolution | The upper energy of unresolved photon emission. |
