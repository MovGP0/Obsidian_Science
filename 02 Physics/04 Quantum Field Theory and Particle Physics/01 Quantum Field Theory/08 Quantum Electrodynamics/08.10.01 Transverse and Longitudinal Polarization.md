---
title: "Transverse and Longitudinal Polarization"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Real photons have two transverse polarization states, which can be expressed as linear or circular combinations.

The physical states are equivalence classes of polarization vectors: adding a multiple of the null photon momentum does not change a gauge-invariant amplitude. The vectors below choose a convenient representative with zero time component for propagation along the positive z axis.

In QED, real photons have two transverse polarization degrees of freedom, perpendicular to their propagation direction. For a photon with
$$
\mechanical{k}^\mu=(\energy{\omega},0,0,\energy{\omega}),
$$
which propagates along $\hat z$, the physical linear-polarization vectors are
$$
\signal{\epsilon}_{(1)}^\mu=(0,1,0,0)
\quad\text{along }\hat x,
$$
$$
\signal{\epsilon}_{(2)}^\mu=(0,0,1,0)
\quad\text{along }\hat y.
$$
The circular combinations are
$$
\signal{\epsilon}_{(\pm)}^\mu
=\mp\frac1{\sqrt2}(0,1,\pm i,0).
$$

Both linear vectors have zero contraction with the photon momentum and spacelike norm minus one in the mostly-minus metric. The circular vectors are a unitary change of basis, so summing both circular states gives the same unpolarized probability as summing both linear states. A relative phase remains important when a particular polarization is prepared.

In Feynman gauge, $\xi=1$, the photon propagator also contains unphysical scalar and longitudinal components. These cancel in physical observables, as ensured by the Ward–Takahashi identity discussed in Section 8.13.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{k}^\mu$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon four-momentum | The momentum of a photon propagating along the z direction. |
| $\energy{\omega}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon energy | The temporal momentum component in natural units. |
| $\signal{\epsilon}_{(1)}^\mu,\signal{\epsilon}_{(2)}^\mu,\signal{\epsilon}_{(\pm)}^\mu$ | $1$ | Photon polarizations | The two linear vectors and their circular combinations. |
