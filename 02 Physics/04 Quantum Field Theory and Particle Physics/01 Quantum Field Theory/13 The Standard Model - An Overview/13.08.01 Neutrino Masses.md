---
title: "Neutrino Masses"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Neutrino oscillations require nonzero masses and lepton mixing. Dirac and Majorana mechanisms extend the minimal Standard Model to account for these masses.

The minimal Standard Model predicts massless neutrinos.
Oscillation experiments, including Super-Kamiokande, SNO, and KamLAND, show that neutrinos have nonzero mass differences and mix between generations.
The lepton mixing matrix is the **Pontecorvo–Maki–Nakagawa–Sakata (PMNS) matrix**, analogous to the quark CKM matrix.
Kajita and McDonald received the 2015 Nobel Prize for the discoveries associated with neutrino oscillations.

The minimal massless-neutrino statement assumes the stated renormalizable field content and excludes higher-dimensional neutrino-mass operators. Oscillations probe relative propagation phases. If all masses were equal, the common phase would not change a flavor probability; nonzero mass-squared differences and mixing are needed. Oscillation data do not alone determine the common absolute mass scale or whether the mass is Dirac or Majorana. The [Super-Kamiokande atmospheric-neutrino paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ex/9807003) is a primary example of an energy- and direction-dependent flavor test.

The seesaw formulas below use a one-generation hierarchy between the Dirac mass and the singlet Majorana mass. Their light mass falls as the inverse heavy mass at fixed Yukawa coupling, which is the low-energy decoupling check. This is a proposed mechanism, not a measurement of the heavy mass. Inferring a very high scale requires the additional assumption that the Yukawa coupling is of order one. The sub-eV values below are the source's illustrative targets, rather than new absolute-mass limits.

Two main mass scenarios are considered.

## Dirac mechanism

Add a right-chiral neutrino $\signal{\nu}_R$ with Yukawa interaction
$\signal{y}_\nu\bar{\signal{L}}\,\tilde{\signal{H}}\,\signal{\nu}_R$.
The mass is
$$
\mechanical{m}_\nu=\frac{\signal{y}_\nu\signal{v}}{\sqrt2}.
$$

For $\mechanical{m}_\nu\lesssim0.1\,\unit{eV}$, the Dirac mechanism requires
$\signal{y}_\nu\lesssim10^{-12}$. The source describes this as an unusually small coupling.

## Seesaw mechanism

Add a right-chiral neutrino with a large Majorana mass $\mechanical{M}_R\gg\signal{v}$:
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_\nu
=-\signal{y}_\nu\bar{\signal{L}}\,\tilde{\signal{H}}\,\signal{\nu}_R
-\frac{\mechanical{M}_R}{2}\bar{\signal{\nu}}_R^{\,c}\signal{\nu}_R
+\text{h.c.}
$$
Diagonalization gives a light and a heavy mass, approximately
$$
\mechanical{m}_\nu\simeq\frac{\signal{y}_\nu^2\signal{v}^2}{2\mechanical{M}_R}\ll\signal{v},
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{heavy}}\simeq\mechanical{M}_R\gg\signal{v}.
$$
For $\mechanical{M}_R\sim10^{14}$–$10^{15}\,\unit{GeV}$ and couplings of order one, the source gives
$\mechanical{m}_\nu\sim0.03$–$0.3\,\unit{eV}$.
The mechanism relates a small neutrino mass to a large Majorana mass scale.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\nu}_R,\signal{L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Neutrino singlet and lepton doublet | The fermion fields in a Dirac neutrino Yukawa interaction. |
| $\tilde{\signal{H}}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Conjugate Higgs doublet | The scalar doublet needed to match the neutrino hypercharge. |
| $\signal{y}_\nu$ | $1$ | Neutrino Yukawa coupling | The coefficient that determines the Dirac neutrino mass. |
| $\signal{v}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Electroweak vacuum parameter | The Higgs vacuum magnitude in the mass relation. |
| $\mechanical{m}_\nu$ | $\unit{eV}$ | Neutrino mass | The small fermion mass produced by the proposed mechanism. |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}_\nu$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Neutrino Lagrangian density | The Yukawa and Majorana mass terms in the seesaw extension. |
| $\mechanical{M}_R,\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{heavy}}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Heavy Majorana mass | The large singlet-neutrino mass and its approximate heavy eigenvalue. |
