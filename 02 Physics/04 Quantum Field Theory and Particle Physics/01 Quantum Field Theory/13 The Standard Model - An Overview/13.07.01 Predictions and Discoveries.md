title: "Predictions and Discoveries"

> [!TLDR]
> The Standard Model anticipated the weak gauge bosons, the top quark, and the Higgs boson. Their later discoveries tested its gauge, fermion, and symmetry-breaking structure.

Read the chronology as a sequence of conditional predictions. The weak mass relations need electroweak input parameters, and a three-generation fermion assignment requires the missing up-type partner. The minimal Higgs model predicts a scalar and relations among its couplings, but its quartic coupling is free, so it does not predict the Higgs mass without another input.

Observing a resonance is the first step in testing its identity. For the Higgs case, the discovery analysis combined several decay channels and compared their event distributions with background expectations. The [ATLAS 2012 discovery paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.7214) describes the observed neutral boson as compatible with Standard Model Higgs production and decay. This historical discovery claim and the later coupling tests summarized by the source are distinct pieces of evidence.

## W and Z bosons

The Standard Model predicted the weak gauge-boson masses before their discovery.
In 1983, UA1, led by Carlo Rubbia, and UA2 observed the bosons in proton–antiproton collisions at CERN's SpS collider, with
$$
\sqrt{\mechanical{s}}=540\,\unit{GeV}.
$$
The source quotes:

- $\mechanical{m}_W=80.4\,\unit{GeV}$, compared with a predicted range of approximately $78$–$80\,\unit{GeV}$.
- $\mechanical{m}_Z=91.2\,\unit{GeV}$, compared with a predicted range of approximately $88$–$92\,\unit{GeV}$.

Rubbia and Simon van der Meer, who developed stochastic cooling, received the 1984 Nobel Prize in Physics.

## Top quark

The three-generation Standard Model requires a weak-doublet partner for the bottom quark.
CDF and D0 discovered the top quark at Fermilab's Tevatron in 1995.
The source quotes
$$
\mechanical{m}_t\approx173\,\unit{GeV}
$$
and identifies it as the heaviest known elementary particle.

## Higgs boson

ATLAS and CMS announced the Higgs-boson discovery at CERN's LHC on 4 July 2012.
The source quotes
$$
\mechanical{m}_h\approx125\,\unit{GeV}.
$$
Peter Higgs and François Englert received the 2013 Nobel Prize in Physics.
The source reports agreement between the measured Higgs spin, parity, and couplings to fermions and gauge bosons and the Standard Model predictions; see Fig. 13.4.

> [!note]
> The masses and prediction ranges above reproduce the source's rounded summary values. They are not a table of the original discovery measurements or a new precision-data update.
