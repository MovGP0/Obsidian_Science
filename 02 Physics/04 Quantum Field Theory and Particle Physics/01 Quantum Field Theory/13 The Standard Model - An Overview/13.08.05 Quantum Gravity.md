title: "Quantum Gravity"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Standard Model does not include gravity. Perturbative quantization of general relativity is not renormalizable by a finite set of couplings, which motivates quantum-gravity theories.

The Standard Model contains no gravitational interaction.
General relativity is the classical theory of gravity. The source describes it as consistent with observations, but its perturbative quantization is not renormalizable in the usual sense.
In natural units, Newton's constant has mass dimension
$$
[\mechanical{G}_N]=[\text{mass}]^{-2}.
$$
A consistent quantum theory of gravity remains an open problem.
The source names **string theory** and **loop quantum gravity** as major proposals that remain under development and have no experimental confirmation in its account.

> [!note]
> Perturbative nonrenormalizability does not prevent the use of general relativity as a low-energy effective field theory. It means that a finite set of parameters does not absorb all ultraviolet divergences at arbitrarily high perturbative orders.

The negative mass dimension explains the perturbative problem. At successively higher orders, gravitational loops require operators with more derivatives. A finite list of the original Einstein-theory coefficients cannot absorb all such terms. This statement concerns prediction at arbitrarily high energy; it does not imply that every low-energy quantum correction is unknown.

As an effective theory, retain the operators needed at a specified low-energy accuracy and fit their coefficients or leave them as explicit unknown inputs. Higher-derivative contributions are then organized by powers of momentum relative to the heavy scale. [Donoghue's effective-field-theory calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9405057) shows how low-energy quantum effects can be separated from unknown high-energy contributions. This controlled expansion and the source's proposed ultraviolet theories answer different questions.
