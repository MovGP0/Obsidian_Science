## Exercise 13.5

Verify the $[U(1)_Y]^3$ anomaly-cancellation condition (13.7).
Sum all fermion contributions in one generation:

- $Q_L$: weak doublet, three colors.
- $u_R$: weak singlet, three colors.
- $d_R$: weak singlet, three colors.
- $L_L$: weak doublet, one color.
- $e_R$: weak singlet, one color.

Left-chiral fermions contribute with a positive sign and right-chiral fermions with a negative sign.

## Solution 13.5 — Cubic hypercharge anomaly

This calculation tests quantum gauge consistency, rather than conservation of a fitted global particle number. Work with one generation and the hypercharge convention of (13.3). A right-chiral field contributes with a minus sign before its hypercharge cube is evaluated. In particular, the negative hypercharges of the down quark and electron do not replace that chirality sign.

An independent check is to express the result with denominator 36: the signed numerator must vanish exactly, not only within a floating-point tolerance. Doubling every hypercharge multiplies every term by eight and leaves the zero sum unchanged. Removing the lepton sector, however, leaves a nonzero quark contribution. Thus cancellation tests the complete chiral field content; it does not follow from color multiplicity alone.

The cubic anomaly coefficient is the sum of cubed hypercharges, including color and weak-component multiplicities. Left-chiral fields contribute with a plus sign and right-chiral fields with a minus sign:
$$
\mathcal A=\sum_f\operatorname{sgn}(f)\,N_c(f)\,\electric{Y}_f^3,
$$
where the sum includes every weak component.

For this solution the source uses the convention
$Q=T_3+\electric{Y}$, so $\electric{Y}$ is half the hypercharge used in the convention $Q=T_3+\electric{Y}/2$. The cancellation is independent of this common normalization.

The contributions for one generation are:

| Multiplet | Hypercharge | Multiplicity | Signed cubic contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $Q_L$ | $\electric{Y}=1/6$ | $3\times2$ | $+3(2)(1/6)^3=1/36$ |
| $u_R$ | $\electric{Y}=2/3$ | $3$ | $-3(2/3)^3=-8/9$ |
| $d_R$ | $\electric{Y}=-1/3$ | $3$ | $-3(-1/3)^3=1/9$ |
| $L_L$ | $\electric{Y}=-1/2$ | $2$ | $+2(-1/2)^3=-1/4$ |
| $e_R$ | $\electric{Y}=-1$ | $1$ | $-(-1)^3=1$ |

The sum is
$$
\mathcal A=\frac1{36}-\frac89+\frac19-\frac14+1
=\frac{1-32+4-9+36}{36}=0.
$$
Thus the anomaly cancels exactly between the quark and lepton sectors. The source relates this cancellation to an $SO(10)$ embedding in a spinor $\mathbf{16}$.

> [!note] SO(10) field count
> The $\mathbf{16}$ includes the charge-conjugate right-chiral neutrino in addition to the fifteen chiral Standard Model components. Its hypercharge is zero, so it does not change this cubic hypercharge sum.
