## Exercise 13.9 — Feynman diagrams for e⁺e⁻ → μ⁺μ⁻

Consider $e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$ in electroweak theory, with both photon and Z-boson exchange.

1. **(a)** Draw the tree-level Feynman diagrams and write the corresponding amplitudes.

2. **(b)** Show that, for center-of-mass energy $\sqrt{\mechanical{s}}\ll\mechanical{m}_Z$, photon exchange dominates and the cross section reduces to the QED result
   $$
   \mechanical{\sigma}=\frac{4\pi\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}^2}{3\mechanical{s}}.
   $$
3. **(c)** At the resonance $\sqrt{\mechanical{s}}=\mechanical{m}_Z$, calculate the peak cross section $\mechanical{\sigma}(e^+e^-\to Z\to\mu^+\mu^-)$ in terms of $\signal{G}_F$, $\mechanical{m}_Z$, and $\energy{\Gamma}_Z$. Use the Breit–Wigner form
   $$
   \mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{peak}}
   =\frac{12\pi\,\energy{\Gamma}_e\energy{\Gamma}_\mu}
   {\mechanical{m}_Z^2\energy{\Gamma}_Z^2}.
   $$
   Compare with the source's LEP value
   $$
   \left.\mechanical{\sigma}(e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-)\right|_{\mathrm{peak}}
   \approx2.0\,\unit{nb}.
   $$
   The quoted hadronic peak is $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{had}}^0\approx41.5\,\unit{nb}$.

## Solution 13.9 — Photon and Z exchange in electron–positron annihilation

The two exchange diagrams lead to the same external state, so add their amplitudes before squaring. Their relative phase produces photon–Z interference. A sum of separate positive cross sections would omit that term. Keep the complete vertex prefactors with the chosen vector and axial coefficients; the half-sized couplings used here compensate the factor-of-two change from the worked Z-decay example.

There are two distinct expansions. Far below the Z pole and well above the muon threshold, the heavy-vector propagator becomes a local interaction, whose amplitude is suppressed relative to photon exchange by powers of the collision energy divided by the Z scale. Near the pole, a zero-width tree propagator is insufficient: its self-energy must supply the width in the resonant denominator. The Breit–Wigner expression is this resonance treatment with backgrounds and radiation handled separately. Check that its peak value has inverse-energy-squared units and depends on partial-width fractions rather than on a divergent pole.

### (a) Tree amplitudes

The two diagrams are $s$-channel $\gamma^*$ and $Z^*$ exchange. In a consistent overall phase convention,
$$
i\signal{\mathcal M}_{\gamma}
=\bar v_e(-i\electric{e}\gamma^\mu)u_e
\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{s}+i0}
\bar u_\mu(-i\electric{e}\gamma^\nu)v_\mu,
$$
and
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\signal{\mathcal M}_{Z}
={}&\bar v_e\frac{-i\signal{g}}{\cos\signal{\theta}_W}
\gamma^\mu(\signal{g}_V^e-\signal{g}_A^e\gamma^5)u_e\\
&\times
\frac{-i(g_{\mu\nu}-\mechanical{q}_\mu\mechanical{q}_\nu/\mechanical{m}_Z^2)}
{\mechanical{s}-\mechanical{m}_Z^2+i\mechanical{m}_Z\energy{\Gamma}_Z}
\bar u_\mu\frac{-i\signal{g}}{\cos\signal{\theta}_W}
\gamma^\nu(\signal{g}_V^\mu-\signal{g}_A^\mu\gamma^5)v_\mu.
\end{aligned}
$$
For these vertex conventions,
$$
\signal{g}_V^f=\frac{T_3^f}{2}-Q_f\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W,
\qquad
\signal{g}_A^f=\frac{T_3^f}{2}.
$$
For charged leptons $T_3^e=T_3^\mu=-1/2$, $Q_e=Q_\mu=-1$. These are half the couplings used with a vertex prefactor $\signal{g}/(2\cos\signal{\theta}_W)$.

### (b) Below the Z resonance

For $4\mechanical{m}_\mu^2\ll\mechanical{s}\ll\mechanical{m}_Z^2$, the photon amplitude scales with $1/\mechanical{s}$ while the Z exchange is suppressed by $1/\mechanical{m}_Z^2$. The leading massless QED result is
$$
\boxed{
\mechanical{\sigma}(e^+e^-\to\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-)
=\frac{4\pi\electric{\alpha}_{\mathrm{em}}^2}{3\mechanical{s}}.
}
$$

### (c) At the Z resonance

At $\mechanical{s}=\mechanical{m}_Z^2$, the resonant denominator is
$i\mechanical{m}_Z\energy{\Gamma}_Z$. The width regulates the pole, and Z exchange dominates the resonant cross section.

The pure resonant peak formula is
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{peak}}(e^+e^-\to Z\to\mu^+\mu^-)
=\frac{12\pi}{\mechanical{m}_Z^2}
\frac{\energy{\Gamma}_e\energy{\Gamma}_\mu}{\energy{\Gamma}_Z^2}.
$$
To express the result through the Fermi constant, first keep the coupling convention explicit. Define the coupling sum in the normalization of [[13.09 Worked Example - The Decay Width Z → ff̄]]:
$$
A_\ell\equiv(2\signal{g}_V^\ell)^2+(2\signal{g}_A^\ell)^2
=\left(-\frac12+2\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W\right)^2+\frac14.
$$
The factors of two convert the half-sized coefficients of part (a) to the full worked-example coefficients. Neglecting lepton masses, electron–muon universality gives the tree-level partial widths and their substitution:
$$
\energy{\Gamma}_e=\energy{\Gamma}_\mu
=\frac{\signal{G}_F\mechanical{m}_Z^3}{6\pi\sqrt2}\,A_\ell,
\qquad
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{peak}}
=\frac{\signal{G}_F^2\mechanical{m}_Z^4}{6\pi\energy{\Gamma}_Z^2}\,A_\ell^2.
$$
The coupling sum is dimensionless, so the peak expression has inverse-energy-squared units. It retains a mixing-angle input through the neutral-current couplings.

> [!note] Inputs in part (c)
> The source asks for the peak in terms of the Fermi constant, Z mass, and total Z width. Those three values alone do not fix the charged-lepton partial widths: the weak mixing angle, or equivalent neutral-current coupling information, is also required. The expression above makes this additional input explicit. It is a tree-level coupling substitution in the ideal resonant formula; precision pole observables also require radiative corrections.

With the supplied leptonic partial widths near $0.084\,\unit{GeV}$,
$\energy{\Gamma}_Z=2.50\,\unit{GeV}$, and
$\mechanical{m}_Z=91.2\,\unit{GeV}$,
$$
\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{peak}}
=\frac{12\pi(0.084)^2}{(91.2)^2(2.50)^2}\,\unit{GeV^{-2}}
\simeq2\,\unit{nb},
$$
using $1\,\unit{GeV^{-2}}=0.389\,\unit{mb}=3.89\times10^5\,\unit{nb}$.

> [!note] Peak channels
> The source calls $41.5\,\unit{nb}$ the total peak cross section over all final states. This value is the supplied hadronic peak benchmark, $\mechanical{\sigma}_{\mathrm{had}}^0$, not the sum over all Z decay channels. The formula above is the idealized resonant contribution; a measured line shape also requires the relevant radiation and experimental corrections.
