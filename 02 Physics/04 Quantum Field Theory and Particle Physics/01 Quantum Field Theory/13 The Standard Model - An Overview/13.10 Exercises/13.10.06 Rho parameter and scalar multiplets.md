## Exercise 13.6

The rho parameter is
$$
\signal{\rho}=\frac{\mechanical{m}_W^2}{\mechanical{m}_Z^2\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W}.
$$

1. **(a)** Show that $\signal{\rho}=1$ at tree level for a general set of scalar multiplets with isospin $T$ and hypercharge $\electric{Y}$ when custodial $SU(2)_c$ symmetry is satisfied.
2. **(b)** Explain why a Higgs triplet with $\electric{Y}=1$ would give a contribution with $\signal{\rho}\ne1$.
3. **(c)** The source quotes $\signal{\rho}_0=1.00038\pm0.00020$ after subtraction of the Standard Model top and Higgs radiative corrections. What does this imply for the structure of the Higgs sector?

> [!note] Source hint for part (a)
> Calculate $\mechanical{m}_W^2$ and $\mechanical{m}_Z^2$ in terms of the vacuum expectation values and the generators. For the Standard Model doublet, show that the ratio is $\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W$.

## Solution 13.6 — Rho parameter and scalar multiplets

The vacuum contributes to vector masses through the squared action of each gauge generator in the scalar kinetic term. A neutral component must have its weak-isospin weight cancel its hypercharge. Use this condition before inserting multiplet labels. The formulas in part (a) assume canonical complex scalar fields and the stated square-root-two convention for each vacuum value.

The ratio removes the common overall electroweak scale and tests the relative charged and neutral contributions. For the doublet-plus-complex-triplet example, increasing the triplet vacuum at fixed doublet vacuum lowers the tree-level ratio. Setting the triplet vacuum to zero returns one, while a triplet-only vacuum returns one half. These limits concern this particular extension. The adjusted fit in part (c) constrains additional contributions after the stated loop subtraction; it does not identify a unique scalar representation.

### (a) Tree-level mass contributions

Use the convention $Q=T_3+\electric{Y}$ for this solution. Consider complex scalar multiplets with a neutral vacuum component of isospin weight $T_3$. Write its expectation value as $\signal{v}_{T_3}/\sqrt2$, so that the Standard Model doublet has $\signal{v}\simeq246\,\unit{GeV}$. The tree-level contributions are
$$
\mechanical{m}_W^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{2}
\sum\left[T(T+1)-T_3^2\right]\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2,
$$
and
$$
\mechanical{m}_Z^2
=(\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2)
\sum T_3^2\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W}
\sum T_3^2\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2.
$$
Each sum runs over the neutral vacuum components. Real scalar multiplets require the corresponding real-field normalization factors.

Taking the ratio gives the general result for these complex multiplets:
$$
\signal{\rho}
=\frac{\sum\left[T(T+1)-T_3^2\right]\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2}
{2\sum T_3^2\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2},
$$
provided the neutral mass contribution in the denominator is nonzero. Custodial symmetry of the scalar vacuum makes the three weak-isospin mass directions isotropic when the hypercharge coupling is set to zero. The charged and neutral weak-isospin mass coefficients are then equal. After the neutral gauge rotation, this implies
$$
\mechanical{m}_W^2=\mechanical{m}_Z^2\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W,
\qquad
\sum\left[T(T+1)-3T_3^2\right]\lvert\signal{v}_{T_3}\rvert^2=0,
$$
and hence $\signal{\rho}=1$ at tree level. The summed equality is a test of the vector mass terms. It does not by itself prove that the full scalar potential has custodial symmetry; an accidental mass relation can also satisfy it. For the distinction between custodial mass relations and stronger scalar-sector symmetry requirements, see [Kundu, Mondal, and Pal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.14195).

Throughout this generalization, the gauge mixing angle is defined by $\tan\signal{\theta}_W=\signal{g}'/\signal{g}$. The mass ratio instead obeys $\mechanical{m}_W/\mechanical{m}_Z=\sqrt{\signal{\rho}}\cos\signal{\theta}_W$. Thus it cannot define the gauge angle by itself when the scalar sector gives $\signal{\rho}\ne1$.

For the Standard Model doublet, $T=1/2$, $\electric{Y}=1/2$, and the neutral component has $T_3=-1/2$. Therefore
$$
\mechanical{m}_W^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{2}\left(\frac34-\frac14\right)\signal{v}^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2}{4},
\qquad
\mechanical{m}_Z^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2}{4\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W}.
$$
It follows that
$$
\boxed{\signal{\rho}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}_W^2}
{\mechanical{m}_Z^2\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W}=1.}
$$
The source relates this tree-level relation to the custodial $SU(2)_c$ structure of the doublet sector, described by $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\supset SU(2)_c$. Hypercharge and unequal Yukawa couplings break the full custodial symmetry, so radiative corrections must be treated separately.

### (b) Complex triplet

For $T=1$, $\electric{Y}=1$, the neutral component has $T_3=-1$. Then
$$
T(T+1)-T_3^2=1,
\qquad 2T_3^2=2.
$$
A vacuum supplied only by this complex triplet gives $\signal{\rho}=1/2$, rather than one. With an additional doublet vacuum,
$$
\signal{\rho}
=\frac{\signal{v}_d^2+2\signal{v}_t^2}
{\signal{v}_d^2+4\signal{v}_t^2}.
$$

> [!note] Neutral component and multiplet scope
> The source uses $T_3=+1$ for $\electric{Y}=1$; with $Q=T_3+\electric{Y}$ the neutral component is $T_3=-1$. The squared weight, and thus the mass ratio, is unchanged. A single additional complex triplet generally shifts the ratio, but suitably combined and aligned multiplets can preserve custodial symmetry. Triplet field content alone does not exclude that possibility.

### (c) Constraint from the supplied fit

The source gives $\signal{\rho}_0=1.00038\pm0.00020$ after subtraction of the stated Standard Model corrections. The central value differs from one by $1.9$ quoted standard deviations. The result constrains additional custodial violation at the level of a few parts in $10^4$.

The source concludes that a doublet-dominated scalar sector, or another custodially symmetric structure, is favored. Its estimate
$\lvert\signal{v}_t/\signal{v}_d\rvert\lesssim10^{-2}$
is an order-of-magnitude bound for a simple unaligned triplet extension; an exact limit needs a specified model and confidence interval. Gauge singlets do not contribute directly to these vector masses.
