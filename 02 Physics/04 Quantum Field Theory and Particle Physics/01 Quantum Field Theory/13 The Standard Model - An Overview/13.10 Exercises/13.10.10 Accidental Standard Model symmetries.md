## Exercise 13.10 — Accidental global symmetries of the Standard Model

With the given field representations, the classical Standard Model Lagrangian has accidental symmetries. They are not imposed as additional symmetries; they follow from the gauge structure, field content, and restriction to renormalizable interactions.

1. **(a)** Ignore neutrino masses. Show that the Lagrangian is invariant under baryon number $U(1)_B$ and the three separate lepton numbers $U(1)_{L_e}\times U(1)_{L_\mu}\times U(1)_{L_\tau}$. Which dimension-six operators violate baryon number $\signal{B}$?
2. **(b)** Show that $\signal{B}-\signal{L}$ is free of the relevant Standard Model gauge anomalies, while $\signal{B}+\signal{L}$ is violated by $SU(2)_L$ instantons. This is the 't Hooft effect, with
   $$
   \Delta\signal{B}=\Delta\signal{L}=3.
   $$

> [!note]
> The source calls $B-L$ an anomaly-free global symmetry at the quantum level. Here this refers to its mixed anomalies with the Standard Model gauge fields. Without right-chiral neutrinos, the minimal model still has cubic and mixed gravitational $B-L$ anomalies if that symmetry is gauged or coupled to background gravity.

## Solution 13.10 — Accidental Standard Model symmetries

Accidental means that every permitted operator in the stated renormalizable model happens to preserve the charge. It is not a promise that all gauge-invariant operators of higher dimension, or the quantum measure, preserve it. Audit these two issues separately: count charges in an interaction monomial, then evaluate the mixed anomaly of the associated current.

The four-fermion monomial has mass dimension six, so its inverse-heavy-scale-squared coefficient restores dimension four for the interaction density. Its three quark charges and one lepton charge give equal baryon and lepton changes. The same equality holds for the electroweak topological transition, but the allowed changes and dynamical suppression differ. Conservation of baryon-minus-lepton number alone therefore does not identify which process occurred. In the lifetime estimate, raising the heavy scale suppresses the amplitude and increases the lifetime as its fourth power; numerical limits additionally require the omitted coefficients and hadronic matrix elements.

### (a) Classical baryon and lepton numbers

Assign baryon charge $\signal{B}=1/3$ to quarks and zero to leptons and the Higgs. With the charge-normalized transformation
$$
\signal{q}\mapsto e^{i\alpha/3}\signal{q},
$$
the kinetic, gauge, and renormalizable Yukawa terms preserve baryon number. In particular, $\bar{\signal{Q}}_L\signal{H}\signal{q}_R$ has zero net baryon charge. The source writes $e^{i\alpha}$ for the quark phase, which uses a rescaled group parameter.

Neglecting neutrino masses, a charged-lepton mass basis has separate classical lepton-family symmetries
$U(1)_{L_e}\times U(1)_{L_\mu}\times U(1)_{L_\tau}$.
There is no physical neutrino mixing in this massless-neutrino limit.

A schematic dimension-six baryon-violating interaction is
$$
\mathcal O_{qqq\ell}
\sim\frac1{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{UV}}^2}
\varepsilon^{ijk}\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon_{\gamma\delta}
(\signal{Q}_L^{i\alpha}\signal{Q}_L^{j\gamma})
(\signal{Q}_L^{k\delta}\signal{L}_L^\beta),
$$
with Lorentz spinor contractions understood. Color and weak indices are contracted to a gauge singlet. It contains three quarks and one lepton, so its field monomial carries $\signal{B}=\signal{L}=1$.

> [!note] Operator classes
> The source calls $QQQL$ the only dimension-six gauge-invariant operator type that violates baryon number. It is one class. Operators with right-chiral quark and charged-lepton fields also exist, with schematic field content $duq\ell$, $qque$, and $duue$, using the appropriate Lorentz and gauge contractions. Their Hermitian conjugates carry the opposite baryon and lepton charges.

The operator conserves $\signal{B}-\signal{L}$ and allows proton decay such as $p\to e^+\pi^0$ through the appropriate interaction or its Hermitian conjugate. The source estimates
$$
\mechanical{\tau}_p\sim
\frac{\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{UV}}^4}{\mechanical{m}_p^5}
$$
in natural units, and quotes about $10^{30}$ years for
$\energy{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{UV}}\sim10^{15}\,\unit{GeV}$.

> [!note] Lifetime estimate
> This scaling suppresses operator coefficients, hadronic matrix elements, and phase-space factors. With only the displayed ratio, $\mechanical{m}_p\simeq0.938\,\unit{GeV}$, and the unit conversion from inverse GeV to years, the estimate is about $3\times10^{28}$ years. The quoted $10^{30}$ years requires additional numerical factors. This dimensional estimate is not an experimental lifetime limit.

### (b) Mixed anomalies and nonperturbative baryon-plus-lepton violation

For one generation, baryon-minus-lepton charges are $1/3$ for quarks and $-1$ for leptons. The mixed $[SU(2)_L]^2U(1)_{B-L}$ anomaly coefficient is
$$
\frac12\left[3\left(\frac13\right)-1\right]=0,
$$
where $T_F=1/2$ is the weak-doublet trace normalization. The mixed $[U(1)_Y]^2U(1)_{B-L}$ sum, with the half-hypercharge convention used above and right-chiral contributions subtracted, is
$$
\frac1{18}-\frac49-\frac19-\frac12+1=0.
$$
The color mixed anomaly cancels as well. Thus $\signal{B}-\signal{L}$ has no mixed anomaly with the Standard Model gauge fields.

> [!note] Meaning of anomaly free
> These mixed gauge-anomaly cancellations do not require a right-chiral neutrino. A singlet right-chiral neutrino makes the usual baryon-minus-lepton assignment vectorlike and cancels the additional cubic and mixed gravitational anomalies. Without it, the minimal model does not have an entirely anomaly-free gauged $B-L$ symmetry. A Majorana singlet mass would also explicitly break this global charge.

For baryon-plus-lepton number, the weak mixed anomaly is nonzero:
$$
\frac12\left[3\left(\frac13\right)+1\right]=1
$$
per generation. The source displays the bracket as $2$ while suppressing the common $T_F$ factor.

A convention with explicit gauge normalization is
$$
q_{\mathrm{top}}(\mechanical{x})
=\frac{\signal{g}^2}{32\pi^2}
\signal{W}_{\mu\nu}^a\tilde{\signal{W}}^{a\mu\nu},
\qquad
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,q_{\mathrm{top}}=n_{\mathrm{inst}}\in\mathbb Z.
$$
Then
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{j}_{B+L}^{\mu}=2N_gq_{\mathrm{top}},
\qquad
\Delta(\signal{B}+\signal{L})=2N_gn_{\mathrm{inst}}.
$$
For three generations and unit topological charge,
$$
\boxed{\Delta\signal{B}=\Delta\signal{L}=3,}
\qquad \Delta(\signal{B}-\signal{L})=0.
$$
The source writes the anomaly schematically as
$N_g\operatorname{Tr}(W\tilde W)/(16\pi^2)$; the trace and inclusion of the gauge coupling must be defined to compare coefficients.

At zero temperature, electroweak instanton effects are exponentially suppressed. The source quotes an $e^{-8\pi^2/\signal{g}^2}$ suppression of order $10^{-80}$; this is a semiclassical instanton weight, not by itself a complete physical transition-rate formula. At sufficiently high temperatures near and above the electroweak scale, thermal sphaleron transitions can efficiently violate $\signal{B}+\signal{L}$ while conserving $\signal{B}-\signal{L}$. The source gives $\thermal{T}\gtrsim100\,\unit{GeV}$ as an order-of-magnitude scale. These processes are important in baryogenesis and leptogenesis scenarios.
