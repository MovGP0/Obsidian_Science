---
title: "Fermion Masses and Yukawa Couplings"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Yukawa interactions couple left- and right-chiral fermions through the Higgs doublet. The Higgs vacuum value turns these interactions into fermion masses and fixes each Higgs–fermion coupling.

An ordinary Dirac mass term,
$$
\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}
=\mechanical{m}\left(
\bar{\signal{\psi}}_L\signal{\psi}_R
+\bar{\signal{\psi}}_R\signal{\psi}_L
\right),
$$
is not $SU(2)_L$ gauge invariant when the left-chiral field is a doublet and the right-chiral field is a singlet.
Instead, **Yukawa interactions** with the Higgs doublet generate the masses.

Gauge invariance selects the Higgs field in each Yukawa term. For the charged lepton, the hypercharges of the adjoint lepton doublet, Higgs doublet, and right-chiral electron add to zero. For the up quark, the conjugate Higgs reverses the scalar hypercharge and makes the corresponding sum zero. Its weak index still transforms as a doublet, so the product is a singlet.

The Yukawa couplings remain independent model parameters. Symmetry does not predict the observed fermion mass hierarchy. Once a mass and the vacuum parameter are supplied, however, the linear Higgs coupling follows from the same term. The mass has dimension one and the mass-to-vacuum ratio is dimensionless, as a four-dimensional Yukawa coupling must be. Setting one Yukawa coupling to zero removes both that fermion's tree-level mass and its direct Higgs coupling.

## Charged leptons

For the first generation,
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{Y,e}
=-\signal{y}_e\bar{\signal{L}}\,\signal{H}\,\signal{e}_R+\mathrm{h.c.}
\tag{13.5}
$$
Here $\signal{y}_e$ is the Yukawa coupling.
In unitary gauge after symmetry breaking,
$\signal{H}\to(0,(\signal{v}+\signal{h})/\sqrt2)^T$.
Then
$$
\left.\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{Y,e}\right|_{\langle\signal{H}\rangle}
=-\frac{\signal{y}_e\signal{v}}{\sqrt2}\bar{\signal{e}}\signal{e}
-\frac{\signal{y}_e}{\sqrt2}\bar{\signal{e}}\signal{e}\,\signal{h}
+\cdots.
$$
Thus,
$$
\mechanical{m}_e=\frac{\signal{y}_e\signal{v}}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\frac{\signal{y}_e}{\sqrt2}=\frac{\mechanical{m}_e}{\signal{v}}.
$$
The second relation gives the Higgs–electron coupling.

## Up-type quarks

For up-type quarks $(u,c,t)$, use the conjugate doublet
$$
\tilde{\signal{H}}=i\sigma^2\signal{H}^*,
\qquad \electric{Y}(\tilde{\signal{H}})=-\frac12.
$$
The Yukawa term is
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{Y,u}
=-\signal{y}_u\left(\bar{\signal{Q}}\,\tilde{\signal{H}}\,\signal{u}_R
+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),
$$
giving
$$
\mechanical{m}_u=\frac{\signal{y}_u\signal{v}}{\sqrt2}.
$$

## Down-type quarks

Similarly,
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{Y,d}
=-\signal{y}_d\left(\bar{\signal{Q}}\,\signal{H}\,\signal{d}_R
+\mathrm{h.c.}\right),
$$
giving
$$
\mechanical{m}_d=\frac{\signal{y}_d\signal{v}}{\sqrt2}.
$$
For three generations, the Yukawa coefficients are $3\times3$ matrices in generation space.
Diagonalizing these matrices introduces mixing between generations in the charged quark currents.
This mixing is described by the **Cabibbo–Kobayashi–Maskawa (CKM) matrix**.

> [!note]
> The displayed one-generation masses can be written with real positive Yukawa couplings after field-phase choices. For complex coefficients or matrices, Hermitian conjugation acts on the complete Yukawa term, including its coefficient.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m},\mechanical{m}_e,\mechanical{m}_u,\mechanical{m}_d$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Fermion masses | The generic Dirac mass and the masses generated for first-generation charged fermions. |
| $\signal{\psi},\signal{\psi}_{L,R}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Generic fermion fields | The Dirac field and its chiral components. |
| $\signal{L},\signal{Q},\signal{e},\signal{e}_R,\signal{u}_R,\signal{d}_R$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Lepton and quark fields | The weak doublets and charged fermion fields in the Yukawa terms; bars denote their adjoints. |

| $\signal{v}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Electroweak vacuum parameter | The Higgs vacuum magnitude that converts Yukawa couplings to masses. |
| $\signal{y}_e,\signal{y}_u,\signal{y}_d$ | $1$ | Yukawa couplings | The coefficients for charged leptons, up-type quarks, and down-type quarks. |
| $\electric{Y}$ | $1$ | Weak hypercharge | The charge that is reversed in the conjugate Higgs doublet. |
| $\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{Y,e},\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{Y,u},\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{Y,d}$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Yukawa Lagrangian densities | The Higgs interactions that generate charged-fermion masses. |
