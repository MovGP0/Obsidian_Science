---
title: "The Higgs Doublet and Electroweak SSB"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Standard Model Higgs is a complex weak doublet with hypercharge 1/2. Its neutral vacuum value leaves electromagnetism unbroken and supplies three Goldstone modes for W⁺, W⁻, and Z.

The Standard Model Higgs sector uses a complex scalar $SU(2)_L$ doublet with hypercharge $\electric{Y}=+1/2$:
$$
\signal{H}=\begin{pmatrix}\signal{H}^+\\\signal{H}^0\end{pmatrix}
\sim\left(1,2,+\frac12\right).
$$
The components $\signal{H}^+$ and $\signal{H}^0$ are complex scalar fields, giving four real degrees of freedom.
The Higgs-sector Lagrangian density is
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}
=(D_\mu\signal{H})^\dagger(D^\mu\signal{H})-\energy{V}(\signal{H}),
$$
with
$$
\energy{V}(\signal{H})
=-\energy{\mu}^2\signal{H}^\dagger\signal{H}
+\signal{\lambda}(\signal{H}^\dagger\signal{H})^2,
\qquad \energy{\mu}^2>0,\quad\signal{\lambda}>0.
$$
The covariant derivative is
$$
D_\mu\signal{H}
=\left(
\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\frac{\sigma^a}{2}\signal{W}_\mu^a
-i\signal{g}'\frac12\signal{B}_\mu
\right)\signal{H},
$$
where $\sigma^a$ are the Pauli matrices.

The representation is chosen to do two jobs. Its neutral component can have a vacuum value without breaking electric charge, and its doublet index can contract with the left-chiral fermion doublets in Yukawa terms. The potential determines a magnitude but no preferred weak direction. Choosing the displayed neutral direction is a gauge choice for the perturbative description.

To test the residual symmetry, apply the charge generator to each component. The lower component has weak-isospin eigenvalue minus one half, which cancels the hypercharge plus one half. A vacuum in this component is therefore electrically neutral. The four real scalar coordinates supply three longitudinal vector modes and one radial scalar; they do not predict four observable scalar particles after gauging.

The potential is minimized at
$$
\signal{H}^\dagger\signal{H}
=\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{2\signal{\lambda}}
=\frac{\signal{v}^2}{2},
\qquad
\signal{v}\approx246\,\unit{GeV}.
$$
The source calls $\signal{v}$ the **Fermi scale**.
Choose
$$
\langle\signal{H}\rangle
=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}0\\\signal{v}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
This vacuum gives
$$
SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\to U(1)_{\mathrm{em}}.
$$
The generator $\electric{Q}_{\mathrm{em}}=T^3+\electric{Y}$ annihilates the vacuum.
The generators $T^1$, $T^2$, and $T^3-\electric{Y}$ do not.
There are
$$
\dim[SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y]-\dim[U(1)_{\mathrm{em}}]
=4-1=3
$$
Goldstone directions, exactly the number needed to give the three vectors $W^+$, $W^-$, and $Z$ their longitudinal polarizations.
