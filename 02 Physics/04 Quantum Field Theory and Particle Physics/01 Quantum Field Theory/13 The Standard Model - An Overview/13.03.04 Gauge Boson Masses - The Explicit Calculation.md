title: "Gauge Boson Masses — The Explicit Calculation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Higgs kinetic term generates a charged W mass and mixes the neutral W³ and B fields. The Weinberg rotation separates the massive Z from the massless photon.

Parametrize the Higgs doublet about the vacuum:
$$
\signal{H}(\mechanical{x})
=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
\sqrt2\signal{G}^+(\mechanical{x})\\
\signal{v}+\signal{h}(\mechanical{x})+i\signal{G}^0(\mechanical{x})
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The fields $\signal{G}^\pm$ and $\signal{G}^0$ are the would-be Goldstone modes, and $\signal{h}$ is the physical Higgs field.
In unitary gauge, $\signal{G}^\pm=\signal{G}^0=0$ and
$$
\signal{H}=\frac{\signal{v}+\signal{h}}{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The Higgs kinetic term, expanded to quadratic order about the vacuum, gives the gauge-boson masses.
At $\langle\signal{H}\rangle=(0,\signal{v}/\sqrt2)^T$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_\mu\langle\signal{H}\rangle
&=\left(
-i\signal{g}\frac{\sigma^a}{2}\signal{W}_\mu^a
-i\signal{g}'\frac12\signal{B}_\mu
\right)\langle\signal{H}\rangle\\
&=\frac{-i\signal{v}}{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{\signal{g}}2(\signal{W}_\mu^1-i\signal{W}_\mu^2)\\
-\dfrac{\signal{g}}2\signal{W}_\mu^3+\dfrac{\signal{g}'}2\signal{B}_\mu
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The quadratic norm is
$$
\lvert D_\mu\langle\signal{H}\rangle\rvert^2
=\frac{\signal{v}^2}{2}
\left[
\frac{\signal{g}^2}{2}\signal{W}_\mu^+\signal{W}^{-\mu}
+\frac14(\signal{g}\signal{W}_\mu^3-\signal{g}'\signal{B}_\mu)^2
\right].
$$
Define the physical charged fields:
$$
\signal{W}_\mu^\pm
=\frac{\signal{W}_\mu^1\mp i\signal{W}_\mu^2}{\sqrt2}.
$$
The neutral combination that diagonalizes the mass matrix is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{Z}_\mu\\\electric{A}_\mu\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\signal{\theta}_W&-\sin\signal{\theta}_W\\
\sin\signal{\theta}_W&\cos\signal{\theta}_W
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\signal{W}_\mu^3\\\signal{B}_\mu\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The **Weinberg angle** is defined by
$$
\tan\signal{\theta}_W=\frac{\signal{g}'}{\signal{g}},
\qquad
\cos\signal{\theta}_W=\frac{\signal{g}}{\sqrt{\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2}},
\qquad
\sin\signal{\theta}_W=\frac{\signal{g}'}{\sqrt{\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2}}.
$$

In the physical basis,
$$
\lvert D_\mu\langle\signal{H}\rangle\rvert^2
=\frac{\signal{g}^2\signal{v}^2}{4}\signal{W}_\mu^+\signal{W}^{-\mu}
+\frac{(\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2)\signal{v}^2}{8}
\signal{Z}_\mu\signal{Z}^\mu
+0\cdot\electric{A}_\mu\electric{A}^\mu.
$$
The neutral square has only one nonzero mass direction. In the original neutral basis, its coefficient matrix has zero determinant: the combination orthogonal to the square is the photon. This is an algebraic form of the unbroken-charge argument, and it checks the relative sign in the Weinberg rotation.

Take care when reading coefficients as masses. A real neutral vector has a mass term with a factor of one half; the charged fields form one complex conjugate pair and their mass term has no such factor. This accounts for the different factors in front of the charged and neutral bilinears. Both masses are proportional to the vacuum parameter and vanish when that parameter is zero. The formulas and the rho relation here are tree-level statements, so a precision mass test must use the radiative corrections and input scheme described in the source note.

Read off the masses:

> [!note]
> **Electroweak gauge-boson masses**
> $$
> \mechanical{m}_W=\frac{\signal{g}\signal{v}}{2},
> \qquad
> \mechanical{m}_Z=\frac{\sqrt{\signal{g}^2+\signal{g}'^2}\,\signal{v}}{2}
> =\frac{\mechanical{m}_W}{\cos\signal{\theta}_W},
> \qquad
> \mechanical{m}_\gamma=0.
> \tag{13.4}
> $$

The relation $\mechanical{m}_W=\mechanical{m}_Z\cos\signal{\theta}_W$ is a Standard Model prediction at tree level.
Define the **rho parameter**:
$$
\signal{\rho}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}_W^2}
{\mechanical{m}_Z^2\cos^2\signal{\theta}_W}=1.
$$
Its tree-level value follows from the Higgs doublet with hypercharge $\electric{Y}=1/2$ and is related to custodial $SU(2)$ symmetry.

> [!note]
> **Remark 13.4 from the source:** With $\signal{v}\approx246\,\unit{GeV}$ and $\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W\approx0.231$, the source quotes $\mechanical{m}_W\approx80.4\,\unit{GeV}$ and $\mechanical{m}_Z\approx91.2\,\unit{GeV}$. It notes that the masses were predicted before the 1983 discoveries of the $W$ and $Z$ bosons by UA1 and UA2 at CERN.
>
> The two stated inputs alone do not fix both masses; a gauge coupling or an equivalent electroweak input is also required. The value $0.231$ is associated with common renormalized or effective weak-angle conventions. The tree-level mass ratio of the quoted masses gives an on-shell $\sin^2\signal{\theta}_W$ near $0.223$. Precision comparisons include radiative corrections and a specified parameter scheme.

The electromagnetic coupling is related to the electroweak couplings by
$$
\electric{e}=\signal{g}\sin\signal{\theta}_W
=\signal{g}'\cos\signal{\theta}_W.
$$
This is **electroweak unification**: the electromagnetic coupling is expressed in terms of the two gauge couplings and their mixing angle.
