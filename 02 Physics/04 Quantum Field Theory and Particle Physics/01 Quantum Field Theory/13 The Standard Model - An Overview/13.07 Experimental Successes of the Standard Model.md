> [!TLDR]
> Particle discoveries and precision measurements test the Standard Model across its gauge, matter, and Higgs sectors.

There are two different tests in this section. A discovery test asks whether an expected particle channel and its properties occur. A precision test asks whether several measured observables follow from one common set of fitted parameters, including loop corrections. Agreement in one channel cannot establish every interaction or exclude every extension of the model.

Use [[13.07.01 Predictions and Discoveries]] to identify which part of the model each particle probes, then [[13.07.02 Precision Tests]] to see how widths and loop-sensitive quantities constrain the same theory. The rounded values reproduce the source's benchmarks. Their dates, definitions, and theoretical uncertainties must be retained before comparing them with another data set. A diagram illustrates a proposed amplitude; experimental support comes from measured event distributions and their stated analysis.

The source describes the Standard Model as the most precisely tested physical theory in the history of science. The following sections summarize its predictions, discoveries, and precision tests.
