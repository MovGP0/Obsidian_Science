---
title: "Four-Momentum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The four-momentum combines energy and three-momentum. Its invariant square equals the squared mass for an on-shell particle.

The four-momentum is
$$
\mechanical{p}^\mu=(\mechanical{p}^0,\mechanical{\mathbf p})
=(\energy{E},\mechanical{\mathbf p}),
\qquad
\mechanical{p}^2=\energy{E}^2-\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}\rvert^2=\mechanical{m}^2.
\tag{A.6}
$$
The final equality is the on-shell mass relation.

For a physical free particle use the positive-energy branch of the mass-shell relation. In its rest frame, the spatial momentum vanishes and the energy equals the mass. A massless particle instead has energy equal to the magnitude of its three-momentum and has no rest frame. Both checks use the mostly-minus metric.

Four-momentum remains a valid integration variable for an internal propagator away from the mass shell. In that case its invariant square is not constrained to the particle mass squared; do not impose the last equality in (A.6) inside an unrestricted loop integral. For external on-shell particles, [[A.07.03 Invariant Single-Particle Measure]] builds the mass-shell condition into the integration measure.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p}^\mu,\mechanical{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Four-momentum and three-momentum | The relativistic momentum and its spatial components. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Energy | The temporal momentum component in natural units. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{GeV}$ | Particle mass | The mass fixed by the on-shell invariant. |
