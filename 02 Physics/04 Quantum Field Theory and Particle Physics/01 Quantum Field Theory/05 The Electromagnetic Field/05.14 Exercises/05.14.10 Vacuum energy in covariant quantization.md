## Exercise 5.10 — Vacuum energy in covariant quantization

Before normal ordering, the Gupta–Bleuler Hamiltonian includes all four modes with commutators (5.17). Show that their signed zero-point contributions sum to the Coulomb-gauge result from the two physical transverse modes.

The exercise compares two state-space descriptions, but an additive vacuum constant is not fixed by a normal-ordered Hamiltonian. To audit the source argument, return to one discrete mode before restoring the continuum integral. Keep the sign of its Hamiltonian coefficient and the sign of its commutator separately; a desired two-polarization count cannot determine either sign.

## Solution 5.10 — Vacuum energy in covariant quantization

The source starts with the normal-ordered Gupta–Bleuler Hamiltonian
$$
:\energy{H}:
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}
\left[
\sum_{i=1}^3a_{\mathbf k}^{(i)\dagger}a_{\mathbf k}^{(i)}
-a_{\mathbf k}^{(0)\dagger}a_{\mathbf k}^{(0)}
\right],
\qquad \energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}=|\mechanical{\mathbf k}|.
$$
The scalar-mode minus sign accompanies the negative commutator; both signs also appear in [Tong, section 6.2.2, equations (6.539) and (6.551)](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S6.html#S6.SS2.SSS2). Keep the two signs separate in undoing normal ordering:
$$
[a_{\mathbf k}^{(0)},a_{\mathbf k'}^{(0)\dagger}]
=-(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf k}-\mechanical{\mathbf k}').
$$
The source then assigns positive half-quanta to the three spatial modes and a negative half-quantum to the scalar mode, obtaining the formal count
$$
\energy{E}_{0,\mathrm{source}}
=\frac12\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
[3-1]\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\delta(0)
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\delta(0).
$$
Here $\delta(0)$ is the source's shorthand for the continuum normalization factor.

> [!note] Limitation of the source zero-point argument
> That signed count does not follow from the displayed canonical commutators. In a discrete mode basis, write the unnormal-ordered oscillator term as $\tfrac12\energy{\omega}\,\zeta_\lambda(a_\lambda^\dagger a_\lambda+a_\lambda a_\lambda^\dagger)$, with $\zeta_0=-1$ and $\zeta_{1,2,3}=+1$. Since $[a_\lambda,a_\lambda^\dagger]=\zeta_\lambda$, its vacuum constant is $+\tfrac12\energy{\omega}\zeta_\lambda^2=+\tfrac12\energy{\omega}$ for every mode. A second minus sign comes from the scalar commutator, so it cannot be omitted.
>
> The unconstrained four-mode canonical expression therefore does not yield the source's $3-1$ subtraction by this argument. Physical photon states have two transverse degrees of freedom, and a consistent physical-state prescription or vacuum normalization must remove the unphysical sector. After normal ordering, both descriptions assign zero energy to the vacuum.

The source interprets its formal result as two contributions of $\tfrac12\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}$, as in Coulomb gauge, and says that one scalar contribution cancels one spatial contribution. The intended physical count is two transverse photon modes. The canonical zero-point calculation needs the qualification in the preceding note; the stated signed count alone does not establish the claimed cancellation.

A useful negative check is to ignore the scalar commutator sign: that reproduces the source's subtraction, which is exactly why the signed count is not a canonical derivation. With both signs retained, one must specify the physical-state treatment and vacuum normalization before comparing constants. Excitation energies in the physical transverse sector agree after the stated normal-ordering convention; a gravitational vacuum-energy prediction requires more input.
