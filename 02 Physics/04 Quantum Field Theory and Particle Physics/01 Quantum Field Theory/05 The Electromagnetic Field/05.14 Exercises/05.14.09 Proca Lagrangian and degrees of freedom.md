## Exercise 5.9 — Proca Lagrangian and degrees of freedom

Start from (5.26).

(a) Derive the equations of motion and prove that $\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu=0$ follows for $\mechanical{m}\ne0$.

(b) Identify the conjugate momenta $\signal{\pi}^\mu$ and show $\signal{\pi}^0=0$. The source asks you to identify $\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu=0$ as a secondary constraint that follows from the equations of motion.

(c) Calculate the Hamiltonian density and compare it with the electromagnetic result (5.8).

> [!note]
> In a canonical Dirac–Bergmann analysis, preservation of the primary constraint $\signal{\pi}^0=0$ first gives the massive Gauss constraint, $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\signal{\mathbf E}_{\mathrm{Proca}}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^0=0$. The Lorenz condition follows from the Proca equations, but should not be confused with that canonical secondary constraint.

The key comparison with Maxwell theory is not whether the scalar momentum vanishes: it does so in both theories. Ask instead what preservation of that constraint determines. With positive Proca mass it fixes the scalar potential from spatial canonical data, while the Maxwell case retains gauge freedom. Use boundary conditions that remove the spatial divergence in the Hamiltonian integral.

## Solution 5.9 — Proca Lagrangian and degrees of freedom

**(a)** Write the Proca field strength as
$\signal{F}_{P,\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\signal{A}_\nu-\partial_\nu\signal{A}_\mu$.
The Lagrangian density is
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_P
=-\frac14\signal{F}_{P,\mu\nu}\signal{F}_P^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\signal{A}_\mu\signal{A}^\mu.
$$
Its derivatives are
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_P}{\partial\signal{A}_\nu}
=\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^\nu,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_P}{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{A}_\nu)}
=-\signal{F}_P^{\mu\nu}.
$$
The Euler–Lagrange equations are
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}_P^{\mu\nu}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$
Take their divergence:
$$
\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\signal{F}_P^{\mu\nu}
+\mechanical{m}^2\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$
The first term vanishes because the two derivatives are symmetric and the field strength is antisymmetric. For $\mechanical{m}\ne0$,
$$
\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$
The Lorenz condition is a consequence of the equations of motion, not a gauge choice.

**(b)** The canonical momenta are
$$
\signal{\pi}^\mu
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}_P}{\partial(\partial_0\signal{A}_\mu)}
=-\signal{F}_P^{0\mu}=\signal{F}_P^{\mu0}.
$$
The kinetic term contains no $\partial_0\signal{A}_0$, and the mass term contains no derivatives. Thus
$$
\signal{\pi}^0=\signal{F}_P^{00}=0
$$
also for the massive Proca field.

Preserving the primary constraint gives the massive Gauss constraint,
$$
\partial_i\signal{F}_P^{i0}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^0=0,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\signal{\mathbf E}_{\mathrm{Proca}}
=-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^0.
$$
It fixes $\signal{A}^0$ through the spatial canonical variables. It is not a gauge-fixing condition: the mass term has removed the local gauge freedom. The massive vector has three physical polarizations, two transverse and one longitudinal.

**(c)** With
$$
\signal{\pi}^i=-\signal{F}_P^{0i}=\signal{F}_P^{i0}
=\signal{E}_{\mathrm{Proca}}^i,
$$
the Hamiltonian density follows from
$\energy{\mathcal H}_P=\signal{\pi}^i\partial_0\signal{A}_i-\energy{\mathcal L}_P$.
After removing a total spatial divergence,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\mathcal H}_P
={}&\frac12\left(
\signal{\mathbf E}_{\mathrm{Proca}}^2
+\signal{\mathbf B}_{\mathrm{Proca}}^2\right)
-\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2}(\signal{A}^0)^2
+\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\signal{\mathbf A}^2\\
&-\signal{A}^0\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\signal{\mathbf E}_{\mathrm{Proca}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
A multiplier times the primary constraint can be added in the total constrained Hamiltonian. Compared with Maxwell theory, the mass contribution before eliminating $\signal{A}^0$ is
$-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}_\mu\signal{A}^\mu/2$.

> [!note] Correction to the source Gauss-law sign
> The source first derives $\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\signal{\mathbf E}_{\mathrm{Proca}}=-\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^0$, but later prints the opposite sign in the comparison paragraph. The negative sign follows from its own Proca equation.

On the constraint surface, the density can also be written as
$$
\energy{\mathcal H}_{P,\mathrm{red}}
=\frac12\left(
\signal{\mathbf E}_{\mathrm{Proca}}^2
+\signal{\mathbf B}_{\mathrm{Proca}}^2
+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{\mathbf A}^2
+\frac{(\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\signal{\mathbf E}_{\mathrm{Proca}})^2}
{\mechanical{m}^2}
\right).
$$
This makes the positive energy of the three physical modes explicit. Check the scalar terms before elimination: the negative scalar mass term and the Gauss-multiplier term combine into the positive divergence-squared term shown above. The opposite source Gauss sign would fail this check. Each term has units of energy to the fourth power, and every displayed square has a nonnegative coefficient for positive mass squared.

The reduced expression divides by mass squared. It therefore does not define the exactly massless Hamiltonian by direct substitution. At zero mass, the constraint must be imposed and the renewed gauge freedom treated before counting physical modes.
