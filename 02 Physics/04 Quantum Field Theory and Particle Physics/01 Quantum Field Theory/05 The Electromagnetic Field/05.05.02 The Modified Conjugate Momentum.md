---
title: "The Modified Conjugate Momentum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Covariant gauge fixing makes the momentum conjugate to the scalar potential nonzero and permits a manifestly covariant canonical relation.

For the gauge-fixed density (5.13), the conjugate momentum is
$$
\signal{\pi}^\mu
=\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_0\electric{A}_\mu)}
=\electric{F}^{\mu0}
-\frac{g^{\mu0}}{\xi}(\partial_\nu\electric{A}^\nu).
$$
Its time component is $\signal{\pi}^0=-(1/\xi)\partial_\nu\electric{A}^\nu$, which no longer vanishes identically. Gauge fixing has made $\electric{A}^0$ a dynamical variable in the enlarged theory.

For a component check, the explicit gauge-fixing contribution is zero for spatial $\mu$ because $g^{i0}=0$, while it survives for $\mu=0$. Both the field tensor and the divergence of the potential have units of energy squared, so the two terms can be added. The new scalar momentum makes the enlarged canonical problem possible; it does not turn the scalar component into a physical photon.

One can impose the manifestly covariant relation
$$
[\electric{A}^\mu(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\signal{t}),
\signal{\pi}^\nu(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\signal{t})]
=ig^{\mu\nu}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}). \tag{5.16}
$$

The metric in (5.16) follows from pairing the upper-index field with the momentum defined by differentiation with respect to the lower-index coordinate. It is not an ordinary positive Euclidean delta for four oscillators. Keep this index convention when deriving the polarization commutators; their timelike sign is the next physical issue to resolve.
