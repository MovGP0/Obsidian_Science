---
title: "Replacement of the Polarization Sum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> For a conserved on-shell photon amplitude, momentum-dependent terms in the polarization sum vanish and the sum can be replaced by the negative metric tensor.

Let $\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu$ be the transition amplitude with the external photon's Lorentz index left free. The Ward theorem gives
$$
\mechanical{k}_\mu\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu=0 \tag{5.24}
$$
for an on-shell photon, $\mechanical{k}^{\,2}=0$, coupled to a conserved current.

In a squared physical amplitude, equation (5.23) can therefore be replaced by
$$
\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}
\signal{\epsilon}^{\mu(\lambda)}
\signal{\epsilon}^{\nu(\lambda)*}
\overset{\mathrm{Ward}}{\longrightarrow}-g^{\mu\nu}.
$$
Each omitted term contains $\mechanical{k}^\mu$ or $\mechanical{k}^\nu$ and vanishes upon contraction with the conserved amplitudes. Use the complete on-shell amplitude, with the required diagrams and external equations of motion, before making this replacement. It can fail on a single nonconserved diagram.

For a sign check, choose a real photon along $z$. Current conservation makes the temporal and longitudinal components of the amplitude equal in the appropriate contraction. Their contributions to the negative-metric contraction cancel, leaving the sum of the two positive transverse squared magnitudes. Thus the replacement does not introduce negative physical probabilities.

> [!note]
> This is an equality after contraction with gauge-invariant amplitudes. It is not a tensor identity between the two-polarization sum and $-g^{\mu\nu}$.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu$ | — | Transition amplitude | The amplitude with the external photon index left free; its unit depends on the process. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Photon four-momentum | The null momentum of the external photon. |
| $\signal{\epsilon}^{\mu(\lambda)}$ | $1$ | Polarization vectors | The normalized physical photon polarizations. |
