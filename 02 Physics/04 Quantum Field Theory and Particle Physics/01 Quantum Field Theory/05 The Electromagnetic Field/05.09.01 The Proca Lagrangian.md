---
title: "The Proca Lagrangian"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Proca mass term breaks the Maxwell gauge symmetry and makes the Lorenz condition a consequence of the field equations.

The model is a free real vector field with positive mass in flat spacetime. The mass term is chosen so that the physical modes have the massive dispersion relation. No gauge-fixing term or Higgs scalar is included.

For a massive vector field,
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{Proca}}
=-\frac14\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{2}\signal{A}_\mu\signal{A}^\mu. \tag{5.26}
$$
The mass term explicitly breaks the Maxwell gauge symmetry. The unextended Proca theory is therefore a theory of a massive vector field rather than a Maxwell gauge field.

The Euler–Lagrange equations give
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^\nu=0. \tag{5.27}
$$
Taking the divergence gives
$$
\mechanical{m}^2\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0
\quad(\mechanical{m}\ne0). \tag{5.28}
$$
This is the Lorenz condition as a consequence of the field equation, not a gauge choice. Inserting it into (5.27) gives
$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$
Each component satisfies the massive Klein–Gordon equation, subject to constraint (5.28). The constraint prevents four independent scalar oscillators from appearing. At rest, it removes the temporal polarization and leaves three spatial ones.

Two checks are useful. Both terms in (5.26) have units of energy to the fourth power. A spatially uniform physical mode has frequency equal to the mass, as follows from the Klein–Gordon equation. The implication in (5.28) explicitly divides by a nonzero mass squared: at exactly zero mass this argument gives no Lorenz constraint, and Maxwell gauge freedom must be treated separately.
