---
title: "Quantization in the Coulomb Gauge"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Coulomb-gauge quantization retains only transverse photon modes and builds their bosonic Fock space.

The aim is to quantize the free radiation field without first adding unphysical oscillators. Coulomb gauge restricts the spatial potential to transverse components. Gauss's law fixes the scalar potential from the source and boundary data; in the source-free radiation problem with vanishing boundary potential, it can be set to zero. This last step does not apply to a general electrostatic field.

Read the polarization construction first, then insert its two basis vectors into the mode expansion. The commutator must act as the identity only on transverse data. The final Hamiltonian then counts quanta in those modes.

For a check before the calculation, choose a nonzero momentum along $z$: only the $x$ and $y$ oscillators should remain. The zero-momentum sector and boundaries need separate treatment because the usual transverse projector divides by momentum squared.
