> [!TLDR]
> Photon statistics, helicity, and zero-point energy follow from the two physical bosonic polarizations.

These articles use the physical two-polarization space in three different ways. Statistics determines how several photons occupy modes. Helicity chooses a basis adapted to rotations about the propagation axis. Zero-point energy concerns the additive constant in the free Hamiltonian, which requires a separate convention and, with gravity, additional physics.

Read statistics before helicity so that changing from linear to circular polarization is recognized as a basis change, not a change of particle type or statistics. Then compare the vacuum mode count in Coulomb and covariant quantization. A basis change must preserve the two physical oscillators. Polarization measurements can distinguish helicity superpositions; they do not measure a chosen additive constant of a flat-spacetime Hamiltonian.
