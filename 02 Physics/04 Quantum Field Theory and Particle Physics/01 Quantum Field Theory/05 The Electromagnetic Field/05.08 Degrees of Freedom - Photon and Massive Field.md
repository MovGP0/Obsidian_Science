> [!TLDR]
> A massless vector field has two physical polarizations, while a massive spin-one field has three.

This section asks why adding a mass changes a vector field's polarization count instead of only changing its dispersion relation. Compare the constraint and symmetry of the equations before comparing representations. A Lorenz condition imposed as a gauge choice and one derived from a massive equation have the same algebraic form but different physical roles.

For a massive particle, the rest frame provides three independent spatial spin directions. A nonzero null photon has no rest frame, and gauge equivalence removes the apparent extra direction. Use these cases to check the counts in the linked articles. A smooth limit of a specified conserved-current amplitude does not imply a componentwise smooth limit of the polarization basis or equality of the state spaces.
