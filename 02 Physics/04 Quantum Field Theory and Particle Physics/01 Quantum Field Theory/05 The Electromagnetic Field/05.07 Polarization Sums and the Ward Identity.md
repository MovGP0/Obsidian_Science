title: "Polarization Sums and the Ward Identity"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Ward identity makes physical polarization sums independent of the auxiliary reference vector and permits a covariant replacement.

When a detector does not resolve a final photon's polarization, the prediction sums probabilities over its two physical states. A covariant formula for that sum can still contain an arbitrary reference vector. Gauge invariance must make this arbitrary choice disappear from the predicted probability.

Read the physical sum first and test its Coulomb-gauge form. Next learn why its momentum terms vanish only after contraction with a suitable amplitude. The last article explains the Green-function identity behind this simplification. A useful diagnostic is to replace an external polarization by the photon momentum in the complete amplitude: the result must vanish. Individual diagrams need not pass that test separately.
