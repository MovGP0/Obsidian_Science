---
title: "Vacuum Energy (Cosmological Constant)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Two physical photon polarizations give a divergent zero-point contribution; normal ordering removes that free-field constant.

Before normal ordering, the source writes the electromagnetic vacuum contribution as
$$
\energy{E}_0
=\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}\sum_{\lambda=1}^{2}\frac12
=2\times\frac12\int\frac{d^3\mechanical{\mathbf k}}{(2\pi)^3}
\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}
\longrightarrow\infty.
$$
The factor two counts the physical transverse polarizations. With an illustrative upper momentum cutoff, the radial measure supplies two powers of momentum and the mode energy supplies one more. The integral therefore grows as the fourth power of that cutoff. This is a dimensional and ultraviolet check, not a measured value of the vacuum energy. A total energy in a finite homogeneous region also needs its volume, as noted below.

The source assigns the following signed contributions in covariant quantization: each physical mode contributes $+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}/2$, the longitudinal mode contributes $+\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}/2$, and the scalar mode contributes $-\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}/2$ because of (5.18). Their sum is
$$
\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
+\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
+\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
-\frac{\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}2
=\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}.
$$
The source interprets this formal signed count as cancellation of scalar and longitudinal contributions. The canonical ordering argument below does not establish that cancellation. Normal ordering sets the free-field vacuum constant to zero. In a flat-spacetime calculation without gravity, shifting all energies by this constant leaves transition-energy differences unchanged. The cosmological constant is a gravitational parameter, so this subtraction prescription alone is not an explanation or a prediction of its observed value. Boundary-dependent energy differences also require a common regulator and a specified comparison, rather than subtraction of two undefined infinities.

> [!note]
> The source calls the displayed integral $\energy{E}_0$ a vacuum energy, but the momentum integral has units of energy density. A total energy also needs a spatial-volume factor. Normal ordering removes this flat-spacetime free-field constant; it does not by itself determine the gravitational cosmological constant.

> [!note] Correction to the source zero-point argument
> The displayed signed sum preserves the source's intended two-polarization count, but it is not the zero-point constant of four unprojected canonical oscillators. The scalar Hamiltonian coefficient and its commutator both carry a minus sign. In the symmetrized oscillator expression their product is positive, so that scalar oscillator also contributes a positive half-quantum before a physical-state prescription or subtraction is specified. See [[05.14.10 Vacuum energy in covariant quantization]] for the explicit two-sign calculation. The independent canonical Hamiltonian and commutator signs are given in [Tong, section 6.2.2, equations (6.539) and (6.551)](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S6.html#S6.SS2.SSS2).
>
> The physical sector has two transverse modes. Its vacuum normalization must be specified separately; a raw auxiliary $3-1$ zero-point subtraction is not a derivation of that physical count.
