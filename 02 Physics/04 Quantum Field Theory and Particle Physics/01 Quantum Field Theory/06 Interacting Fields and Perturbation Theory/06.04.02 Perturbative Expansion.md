---
title: "Perturbative Expansion"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Expanding the Dyson exponential orders S-matrix terms by the number of interaction insertions.

Expand (6.3):
$$
\mathcal S
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-i)^n}{n!}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}_1\cdots d^4\mechanical{x}_n\,
\mathcal T\{\energy{\mathcal H}_I(\mechanical{x}_1)\cdots
\energy{\mathcal H}_I(\mechanical{x}_n)\}.
$$
For the quartic theory, the first nontrivial term is
$$
\mathcal S^{(1)}
=-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_I^4(\mechanical{x})\}.
$$
The second-order term is
$$
\mathcal S^{(2)}
=\frac1{2!}\left(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\right)^2
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,d^4\mechanical{y}\,
\mathcal T\{\signal{\phi}_I^4(\mechanical{x})\signal{\phi}_I^4(\mechanical{y})\}.
$$
At order $n$ in this model, each insertion contributes one power of $\lambda$; this counts vertices, not final particles or loops. For example, two quartic insertions can form different connected and disconnected structures depending on which fields are contracted.

For connected two-to-two scattering at first order, all four fields at the vertex attach to the external particles. There are $4!$ attachments, which cancel the $4!$ in the density. At second order the additional $1/2!$ accounts for exchange of the two insertion labels. Any remaining symmetry factor must come from the actual contraction count. This explains why coefficients cannot be inferred from the coupling power alone.

Wick's theorem gives a systematic way to evaluate these matrix elements between physical states.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{x}_i,\mechanical{x},\mechanical{y}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime points | The coordinates of the interaction insertions. |
| $\energy{\mathcal H}_I$ | $\unit{MeV^4}$ | Interaction density | The Hamiltonian density in the Dyson expansion. |
| $\signal{\phi}_I$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Scalar field | The free interaction-picture field. |
| $\lambda$ | $1$ | Quartic coupling | The interaction strength that orders the expansion. |
