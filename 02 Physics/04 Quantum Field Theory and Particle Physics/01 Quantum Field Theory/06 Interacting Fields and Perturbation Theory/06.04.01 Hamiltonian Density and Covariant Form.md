---
title: "Hamiltonian Density and Covariant Form"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A local interaction Hamiltonian is the spatial integral of its density, giving a spacetime form of the Dyson formula.

It is useful to write (6.2) with the interaction Hamiltonian density:
$$
\energy{H}_I(\signal{t})
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
\energy{\mathcal H}_I(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\signal{t})
=\int d^3\mechanical{\mathbf x}\,
\energy{\mathcal H}_I(\mechanical{x}).
$$

The time integral becomes a spacetime integral:
$$
\mathcal S=\mathcal T\exp\left(-i\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,
\energy{\mathcal H}_I(\mechanical{x})\right). \tag{6.3}
$$
For the quartic theory, $\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\lambda\signal{\phi}^4/4!$. Because the interaction contains no time derivatives, $\energy{\mathcal H}_{\mathrm{int}}=-\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}=+\lambda\signal{\phi}^4/4!$. Thus
$$
\mathcal S
=\mathcal T\exp\left(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,\signal{\phi}_I^4(\mechanical{x})\right).
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\mathcal S=\mathcal T\exp\left(i\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,
\energy{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mechanical{x})\right).
$$
This simple Lagrangian form assumes that the interaction contains no time derivatives.

The quartic sign can be checked before any contractions: the potential energy is positive for positive $\lambda$, so the interaction Hamiltonian density is positive and its contribution to the evolution exponent is negative imaginary. The integral of the density has mass dimension zero, as an exponent must.

Calling (6.3) covariant also needs locality. Two spacelike interaction insertions can exchange their time order under a Lorentz transformation. Their bosonic local densities commute at spacelike separation, so this exchange does not change the ordered product. Merely replacing a spatial integral and a time integral by $d^4\mechanical{x}$ would not prove this property. For derivative interactions, compute the canonical Hamiltonian before using the last formula.
