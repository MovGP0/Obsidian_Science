title: "Connection to Feynman Diagrams"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Feynman diagrams encode Wick contractions as propagator lines, interaction vertices, and external-state factors.

Wick's theorem assigns each perturbative term a product of propagators, one for each internal contraction, and external-state factors, one for each uncontracted field. The contraction count and topology determine the contribution's structure.

Feynman diagrams give this combinatorial structure a graphical form. Chapter 7 translates each Wick term into a diagram and derives rules for writing the invariant amplitude directly. It treats quartic scalar theory, Yukawa theory, and QED.

![[QFT-06-03-Fundamental-Propagators.svg|**Fig. 6.3** The three fundamental propagators: scalar as a solid line, fermion as a line with an arrow, and photon as a wavy line. Chapter 7 derives the complete Feynman rules.]]

A line is a propagator factor, not a measured trajectory through spacetime. In Fig. 6.3, the arrow on a fermion line records the fermion-line orientation used for spinor contractions; it is not an arrow showing laboratory motion. The scalar, fermion, and photon line styles identify different algebraic propagators and index structures.

To recover a diagram from a Wick term, identify each paired field with a line and each interaction insertion with a vertex. Preserve the contraction multiplicity and fermion permutation signs. As a check, a single quartic vertex has four scalar attachments and produces the same $-i\lambda$ coefficient found directly by Wick contraction. A drawing without its coefficient, index contractions, and external-state convention is not yet an amplitude.

The diagram labels use four-momentum $\mechanical{k}$ and particle mass $\mechanical{m}$. The photon expression is in Feynman gauge.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Four-momentum | The momentum carried by each propagator line. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Particle mass | The scalar or fermion mass in the displayed propagator. |
| $\lambda$ | $1$ | Quartic coupling | The dimensionless coefficient in the vertex-counting check. |
