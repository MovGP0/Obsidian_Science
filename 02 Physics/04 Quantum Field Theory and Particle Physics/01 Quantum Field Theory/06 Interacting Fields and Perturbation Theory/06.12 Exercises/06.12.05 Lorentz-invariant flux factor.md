## Exercise 6.5 — Incoming flux

Show that the incoming flux $\energy{F}=4\energy{E}_1\energy{E}_2
\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf v}_1-\mechanical{\mathbf v}_2\rvert$ can be written
$$
\energy{F}=4\sqrt{(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2
-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2}.
$$
*Hint:* Start from $\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf v}_1-\mechanical{\mathbf v}_2\rvert
=\lvert\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1/\energy{E}_1-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2/\energy{E}_2\rvert$ in a frame with $\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2=0$, then verify the invariant form.

> [!note]
> The simple velocity difference assumes collinear incoming velocities, including a target-rest frame. For arbitrary velocities the Møller flux also contains the velocity cross-product term; see [[06.08.02 Differential Cross Section]].

## Solution 6.5 — Lorentz-invariant flux factor

In the target-rest laboratory frame, particle 2 is at rest:
$$
\energy{F}_{\mathrm{lab}}
=4\energy{E}_1\mechanical{m}_2|\mechanical{\mathbf v}_1|
=4\mechanical{m}_2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1|.
$$
The momenta are
$$
\mechanical{p}_2^\mu=(\mechanical{m}_2,\mathbf0),\qquad
\mechanical{p}_1^\mu=(\energy{E}_1,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1),
\qquad
\energy{E}_1=\sqrt{|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1|^2+\mechanical{m}_1^2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2=\energy{E}_1\mechanical{m}_2,
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2
&=\energy{E}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2\\
&=\mechanical{m}_2^2(\energy{E}_1^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2)
=\mechanical{m}_2^2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1|^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus
$$
4\sqrt{(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2}
=4\mechanical{m}_2|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1|=\energy{F}_{\mathrm{lab}}.
$$
The squared masses and the four-momentum scalar product are Lorentz invariant. This constructs the invariant continuation of the laboratory flux:
$$
\energy{F}=4\sqrt{(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2}.
$$
In the CM frame,
$$
\mechanical{p}_1^\mu=(\energy{E}_1,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i),\qquad
\mechanical{p}_2^\mu=(\energy{E}_2,-\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i),
$$
$$
\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2=\energy{E}_1\energy{E}_2+|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|^2.
$$
Using the on-shell conditions gives
$$
(\mechanical{p}_1\cdot\mechanical{p}_2)^2-\mechanical{m}_1^2\mechanical{m}_2^2
=|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|^2\energy{s},
\qquad
\energy{F}_{\mathrm{CM}}=4|\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i|\sqrt{\energy{s}}.
$$

A test of the qualification in the question uses two noncollinear beams. For perpendicular velocities of equal nonzero magnitude, the cross-product term in the Møller expression is nonzero. The plain velocity difference then overestimates the invariant flux. This counterexample shows why proving equality in the target-rest frame does not make the simple speed difference valid in every frame.

The CM expression supplies a separate check for unequal masses: use the two on-shell energies, not twice either single-particle energy, in $\sqrt{\energy{s}}$. The result still has dimension energy squared and is nonnegative. It tends to zero as the relative incoming motion tends to zero, where dividing a rate by an incident collision flux needs a limiting procedure.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{m}_1,\mechanical{m}_2$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Incoming masses | The two beam-particle rest masses. |
| $\energy{s}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Collision invariant | The square of the total incoming CM energy. |
| $\energy{F},\energy{F}_{\mathrm{lab}},\energy{F}_{\mathrm{CM}}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Incoming flux | The invariant normalization and its frame-specific expressions. |
| $\energy{E}_1,\energy{E}_2$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Incoming energies | The on-shell energies, which need not be equal. |
| $\mechanical{\mathbf v}_1,\mechanical{\mathbf v}_2$ | $1$ | Incoming velocities | The beam velocities in the chosen frame. |
| $\mechanical{p}_1,\mechanical{p}_2,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_1,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_2,\mechanical{\mathbf p}_i$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Incoming momenta | The four-momenta and their laboratory or CM spatial parts. |
