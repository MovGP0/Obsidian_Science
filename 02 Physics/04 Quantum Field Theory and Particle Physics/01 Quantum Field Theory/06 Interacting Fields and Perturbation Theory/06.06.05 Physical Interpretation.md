---
title: "Physical Interpretation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> LSZ relates field-theory Green functions to scattering observables while including external-field normalization.

LSZ establishes that:

1. Cross sections and decay rates can be obtained from the theory's full Green functions, including their perturbative corrections.
2. The connection between Green functions and S-matrix elements is defined independently of a particular perturbative order.
3. Field renormalization, including $\signal{Z}\ne1$, is included systematically.

In practical perturbative calculations, compute the connected amputated diagrams, attach the external spinors or polarization vectors, include the required residue factors, and extract the invariant amplitude.

The field is an interpolating operator, not a detector reading. Two choices of field normalization can describe the same scattering because their Green functions and their external residue factors change together. What the detector tests is the resulting rate or cross section, after the final-state sums and phase-space integration.

A useful reconstruction check is to rescale a scalar field by a nonzero real constant. Each external field insertion supplies one constant, and each LSZ residue denominator removes its magnitude; a consistent state-phase convention removes any remaining sign convention. If the calculated cross section changes under this choice, the external normalization has been used inconsistently. LSZ does not by itself estimate the error from truncating the Green function's perturbative expansion.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{Z}$ | $1$ | Field residue | The external-field normalization that LSZ includes systematically. |
