---
title: "Physical Meaning of Propagators"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A propagator's particle pole selects on-shell propagation, while its iε prescription specifies the time-ordered boundary condition.

Wick contractions produce propagators such as $\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})$. In momentum space, the scalar factor is
$$
\frac{i}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
The pole at $\mechanical{k}^{\,2}=\mechanical{m}^2$ is the analytic signature of a stable physical particle. Away from its mass shell, an internal line is virtual.

The Feynman prescription specifies how the complex $\mechanical{k}^0$ contour passes the poles. The source describes positive-frequency propagation forward in time and the negative-frequency contribution as antiparticles propagating backward.

> [!note]
> Backward-in-time antiparticle propagation is a diagrammatic interpretation of the negative-frequency term; physical antiparticles have positive energy and propagate forward in time. The Feynman prescription implements time ordering. Microcausality additionally requires spacelike commutativity or anticommutativity of the appropriate fields and observables.

Energy and momentum are conserved at each vertex, including vertices joined by virtual lines. The internal four-momentum generally does not satisfy a free mass-shell relation. This is not a temporary violation of energy conservation; the line is an internal factor in a correlation function, not an extra detected particle with an uncertain energy.

The coordinate-space propagator has mass dimension two, while its momentum-space factor has dimension minus two; the four-momentum integration supplies the difference. A particle interpretation relies on the pole and its residue. An internal momentum away from that pole cannot be assigned a positive probability by squaring the propagator alone. Cross sections require the full amplitude, including interference between all allowed diagrams.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\Delta}_F$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Scalar propagator | The position-space Feynman two-point function. |
