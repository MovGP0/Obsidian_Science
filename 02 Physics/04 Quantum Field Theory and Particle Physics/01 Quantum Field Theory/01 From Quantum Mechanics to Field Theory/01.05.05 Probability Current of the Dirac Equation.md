---
title: "Probability Current of the Dirac Equation"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Dirac current is conserved and has a nonnegative probability density for every four-component wavefunction.

A viable single-particle probability density must be real, nonnegative, and part of a locally conserved current. The Dirac equation supplies these properties for a free spinor with real mass; they must not be confused with a lower bound on its energy spectrum.

Define the Dirac adjoint as $\overline{\signal{\psi}}=\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}$. Use the Dirac equation (1.32) and its adjoint:

$$
-i\partial_{\mu}\overline{\signal{\psi}}\,\gamma^{\mu}
-\mechanical{m}\overline{\signal{\psi}}=0. \tag{1.48}
$$

Together, the two equations give

$$
\partial_{\mu}\left(\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\signal{\psi}\right)=0. \tag{1.49}
$$

Thus the conserved current is $\signal{j}^{\mu}=\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{\mu}\signal{\psi}$, with probability density

$$
\signal{j}^{0}=\overline{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^{0}\signal{\psi}
=\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}\signal{\psi}
=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{4}|\signal{\psi}_{\alpha}|^{2}\ge0. \tag{1.50}
$$

To obtain (1.49), multiply the Dirac equation on the left by the adjoint and the adjoint equation on the right by the spinor. The equal mass terms cancel, and the product rule combines the remaining derivatives. Integrating the resulting continuity equation conserves total probability only if the net flux at the spatial boundary vanishes.

The Dirac probability density is always nonnegative. This resolves the probability-density problem of the Klein–Gordon single-particle equation and was a major success of the Dirac equation as a relativistic quantum-mechanical theory.

At a point where only one spinor component is nonzero, (1.50) reduces to that component's absolute square; there is no frequency-dependent sign as in the scalar candidate current. This is a useful local check. It does not prevent pair production when interactions are introduced, so it does not establish that a fixed-particle description is sufficient at all energies.
