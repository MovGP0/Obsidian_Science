---
title: "Limits of Nonrelativistic Quantum Mechanics"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Schrödinger equation treats time and space differently, which prevents Lorentz covariance.

The free Schrödinger equation is an approximation for momenta small compared with the rest-mass scale. Its preferred relation between kinetic energy and momentum is consistent with Galilean changes of inertial frame. The question here is why that same equation cannot be exact under Lorentz changes of frame.

Read [[01.03.01 Covariance of the Schrödinger Equation]] first to compare the derivative structure with the relativistic dispersion relation. Then use [[01.04 The Klein–Gordon Equation]] to test a covariant replacement: covariance must be checked separately from a positive probability interpretation.

A useful reconstruction test is to explain why a successful nonrelativistic limit does not establish an exact relativistic theory. The approximation discards terms that become important at relativistic momenta.
