---
title: "Lagrangian Density and Action"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Stationarity of the spacetime integral of the Lagrangian density gives the Euler–Lagrange field equation.

In field theory, the Lagrangian density $\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi},\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})$ takes the role of the mechanical Lagrangian. It depends on the field $\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x})$ and its derivatives. The action is

$$
\energy{S}=\int d^{4}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{\mathcal L}(\signal{\phi},\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}), \tag{1.74}
$$

where the integral covers all spacetime or a suitable region. Vary the field while keeping the coordinates fixed. The variation of its derivative is the derivative of its variation. Integrating that term by parts transfers one derivative to the Lagrangian coefficient; variations vanish at the boundary, so the surface term drops out. Requiring the remaining integral to vanish for every interior variation gives

$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{\phi}}
-\partial_{\mu}\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})}=0. \tag{1.75}
$$

For the real scalar field, the Lagrangian density is

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})
-\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{\phi}^{2}. \tag{1.76}
$$

Here the positive time-derivative term and negative spatial-gradient term follow from the metric. Differentiation with respect to the field gives minus mass squared times field, while differentiation with respect to its gradient gives the raised field derivative. Substitution into (1.75) gives the Klein–Gordon equation (1.11). For the Dirac field,

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=\overline{\signal{\psi}}(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}, \tag{1.77}
$$

treat the spinor and its adjoint as independent variables during the variation; impose their conjugacy on the physical solutions. Their Euler–Lagrange equations give the Dirac equation (1.32) and its adjoint. For quantum fermions, consistent Grassmann ordering is also required.

The action is stationary, not necessarily a minimum. In four spacetime dimensions it is dimensionless in natural units, so the density has energy dimension four. The scalar and Dirac kinetic terms then require field dimensions one and three-halves, respectively, consistent with the table. These dimensions are a useful check before adding interactions.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Lagrangian density | The field's local dynamical density in natural units. |
| $\energy{S}$ | $1$ | Action | The spacetime integral of the Lagrangian density, in units of the reduced Planck constant. |
| $\signal{\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Real scalar field | The canonically normalized field varied in the action. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime position | The integration coordinate. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The rest-mass parameter in each free-field equation. |
| $\signal{\psi}$, $\overline{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac field | The fermion field and its adjoint in the Dirac Lagrangian. |
