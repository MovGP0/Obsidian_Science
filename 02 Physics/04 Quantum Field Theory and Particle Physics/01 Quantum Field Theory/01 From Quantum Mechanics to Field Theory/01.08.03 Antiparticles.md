---
title: "Antiparticles"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A complex scalar quantum field creates particles and antiparticles with equal masses and opposite charges; its conserved current measures charge.

A complex scalar has two independent real components. With an exact global phase symmetry, these can be organized into independent particle and antiparticle modes of opposite conserved charge. In contrast, the real-field condition in the preceding note identifies the two mode sets. For a free complex scalar, quantization gives

$$
\signal{\hat\phi}(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}}
\left[\hat a(\mechanical{\mathbf p})e^{-i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}
+\hat b^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})e^{+i\mechanical{p}\cdot\mechanical{x}}\right], \tag{1.69}
$$

where $\hat a^{\dagger}$ creates a particle and $\hat b^{\dagger}$ creates an antiparticle. They have the same mass $\mechanical{m}$ and opposite charges.

The classical conserved current is

$$
\electric{j}^{\mu}=i\left(\signal{\phi}^{*}\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}
-\signal{\phi}\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{*}\right).
$$

After quantization and normal ordering, its total charge $\electric{\hat Q}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\electric{\hat j}^{0}$ becomes

$$
\electric{\hat Q}
=\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}}
\left[\hat a^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\hat a(\mechanical{\mathbf p})
-\hat b^{\dagger}(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\hat b(\mechanical{\mathbf p})\right]. \tag{1.70}
$$

This is the particle number minus the antiparticle number. It measures charge, rather than probability. The normalization assigns charge plus one to a particle and minus one to its antiparticle; multiply by the physical charge unit when needed. Normal ordering makes the vacuum charge zero in this free-field convention. The negative density of the classical Klein–Gordon equation is reinterpreted as the negative charge density of antiparticles.

> [!note]
> Equations (1.69) and (1.70) retain the source's k/p notation for the integration measure and mode labels.

Create one particle and one antiparticle from the vacuum as a check. Their positive energies add, while their contributions to (1.70) cancel. A neutral two-particle state is therefore different from the vacuum even though both have zero net charge. If interactions preserve the phase symmetry, they can change the total particle count while leaving this charge difference fixed.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{\phi}$, $\signal{\hat\phi}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Complex scalar field | The classical and quantized charged scalar field with canonical normalization. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Spacetime position | The field coordinate and spatial integration variable. |
| $\mechanical{p}$, $\mechanical{\mathbf p}$, $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Momentum | The mode momentum and source integration variable. |
| $\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode energy | The positive on-shell particle energy. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The common particle and antiparticle mass. |
| $\electric{j}^{\mu}$, $\electric{\hat j}^{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3}}$ | Charge current | The classical and quantum currents in unit-charge normalization. |
| $\electric{\hat Q}$ | $1$ | Charge | The particle-minus-antiparticle number, in units of the particle charge. |
