## Exercise 1.8 — Proca Equation and the Massless Limit

A massive vector field, or Proca field, has Lagrangian density

$$
\energy{\mathcal L}=-\frac{1}{4}\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\frac{1}{2}\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{A}_{\mu}\signal{A}^{\mu},\qquad
\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\signal{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\signal{A}_{\mu}.
$$

**(a)** Derive the equations of motion for $\signal{A}^{\mu}$ with the Euler–Lagrange equations. Show that $\partial_{\mu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}+\mechanical{m}^{2}\signal{A}^{\nu}=0$.

**(b)** Take the divergence of the result from (a) and derive the automatic Lorenz condition $\partial_{\mu}\signal{A}^{\mu}=0$, valid for $\mechanical{m}\ne0$. Why does this condition not follow automatically in the Maxwell case, $\mechanical{m}=0$?

**(c)** Use the Lorenz condition to show that each component of $\signal{A}^{\mu}$ satisfies $(\square+\mechanical{m}^{2})\signal{A}^{\mu}=0$.

**(d)** Count the physical degrees of freedom. For $\mechanical{m}>0$, the vector field has three physical polarizations. For $\mechanical{m}=0$, the photon has only two. Explain this reduction using gauge symmetry.

This is a free vector field in flat spacetime with no external current. The mass term changes a constraint as well as the dispersion relation. Before calculating, predict whether dividing a divergence equation by the squared mass remains valid at exactly zero mass; it does not.

## Solution 1.8 — Proca equation and the massless limit

**(a)** For
$$
\energy{\mathcal L}
=-\frac14\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
+\frac12\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}_\mu\signal{A}^\mu,
$$
the derivatives are
$$
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}{\partial\signal{A}_\nu}
=\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^\nu,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\energy{\mathcal L}}
{\partial(\partial_\mu\signal{A}_\nu)}
=-\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}.
$$
The Euler–Lagrange equation gives
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}+\mechanical{m}^2\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$

**(b)** Take the divergence and use the antisymmetry of the field tensor:
$$
\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}=0.
$$
The two derivatives are symmetric under exchange of their indices, while the field strength is antisymmetric, so their contraction vanishes. Then
$$
\mechanical{m}^2\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\partial_\nu\signal{A}^\nu=0
\qquad(\mechanical{m}\ne0).
$$
For $\mechanical{m}=0$, the divergence equation is an identity and does not impose the Lorenz condition.
The Maxwell potential retains gauge freedom.

**(c)** With $\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu=0$,
$$
\partial_\mu\signal{F}^{\mu\nu}
=\Box\signal{A}^\nu-\partial^\nu(\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu)
=\Box\signal{A}^\nu.
$$
Therefore every component satisfies
$$
(\Box+\mechanical{m}^2)\signal{A}^\nu=0.
$$

**(d)** The vector field has four components.
For $\mechanical{m}>0$, the constraint $\partial_\mu\signal{A}^\mu=0$ leaves three physical degrees of freedom: the three polarizations of a massive spin-one particle.

For $\mechanical{m}=0$, gauge invariance removes another unphysical mode.
For example, in temporal gauge take $\signal{A}^0=0$ and use the remaining gauge freedom to impose
$\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\signal{\mathbf A}=0$.
For source-free propagating modes with suitable boundary conditions, two transverse photon polarizations remain. Constraint equations and residual gauge freedom must be used together; four independent scalar equations alone would overcount vector polarizations.
The extra freedom is the massless gauge symmetry
$$
\signal{A}_\mu\longrightarrow\signal{A}_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha.
$$

A rest-frame check is available only for nonzero mass: the plane-wave Lorenz constraint sets the temporal polarization to zero and leaves three spatial directions. A massless wave has no rest frame; a null wavevector, the field constraint, and gauge equivalence leave two transverse polarizations. The change at exactly zero mass is therefore a change in physical state counting, not an algebraic substitution into a step that divided by mass squared. Statements about a smooth interacting limit would require the interactions and their conserved sources to be specified.
