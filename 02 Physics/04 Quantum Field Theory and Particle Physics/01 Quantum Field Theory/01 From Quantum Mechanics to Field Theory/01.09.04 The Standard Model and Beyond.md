title: "The Standard Model and Beyond"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Standard Model describes the electromagnetic, weak, and strong interactions with a product gauge group.

The Standard Model is the field theory of the fundamental interactions other than gravity. Its gauge group organizes three interaction sectors, but a group alone is not a complete theory: the matter representations, scalar sector, couplings, and symmetry-breaking state must also be specified. Its gauge group is

$$
G_{\mathrm{SM}}=SU(3)_C\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y. \tag{1.73}
$$

The Higgs mechanism spontaneously breaks this group to $SU(3)_C\times U(1)_{\mathrm{em}}$. The Higgs particle was discovered at CERN in 2012, completing the experimental particle content of the Standard Model.

Color remains an unbroken gauge factor in this construction. The electroweak scalar background leaves one electromagnetic combination unbroken; the existence of that remaining factor accounts for a massless photon in the model. This is a structural statement, whereas boson masses and scattering rates require parameter values.

Despite its experimental success, the Standard Model leaves major questions open: unification with gravity, the nature of dark matter and dark energy, and the matter–antimatter asymmetry of the universe. The source names supersymmetry, string theory, and loop quantum gravity as approaches in the search for a more fundamental theory. Quantum field theory supplies the theoretical framework for subnuclear physics. The listed extensions are research approaches, not experimentally established replacements. An open question is not itself evidence for one of them.

As a reconstruction prompt, explain why the observed Higgs state tests the scalar sector but does not by itself test gravity, identify dark matter, or establish any one proposed extension. Those require distinct observables and comparisons.
