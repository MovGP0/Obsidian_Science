title: "The Lagrangian Formalism in Field Theory"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Lagrangian density defines field dynamics through the action and connects symmetries with conserved quantities.

How can one state local field dynamics and conservation laws in one framework? The action specifies the model; its variation gives the equations, and its continuous symmetries constrain the allowed currents. A Lagrangian is a chosen dynamical input, not a consequence of notation alone.

Begin with the scalar example and track which boundary term is discarded in the variation. Read Noether's theorem next to distinguish local continuity from a conserved spatial integral. Finish with the Hamiltonian to identify canonical variables and the additional quantum commutation postulate. Check the free scalar energy density is nonnegative before interpreting its modes as stable excitations.
