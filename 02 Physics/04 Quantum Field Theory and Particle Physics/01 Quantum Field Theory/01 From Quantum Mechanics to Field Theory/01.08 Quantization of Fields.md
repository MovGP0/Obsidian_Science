---
title: "Quantization of Fields"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Quantum field theory quantizes classical fields and uses a state space that permits arbitrary particle numbers.

A fixed-number wavefunction cannot connect states with different numbers of particles. Quantization addresses this by making field modes into creation and annihilation operators acting on a larger state space. A field value is therefore not a probability amplitude at one point.

Start with the real scalar field to see positive-energy quanta emerge from both frequency terms. Fock space then specifies the state-space construction. Compare the complex scalar field with the real one to identify independent antiparticles, and finish with the Dirac field to see why fermionic anticommutation replaces bosonic commutation.

The free theory is the controlled example here. Merely admitting many number sectors does not make their populations change: that also depends on the Hamiltonian.
