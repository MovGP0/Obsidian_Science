---
title: "Electron Self-Energy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Electron self-energy is the propagator correction produced by emission and reabsorption of a virtual photon.

The other fundamental one-loop correction is electron self-energy, shown in Fig. 7.16. Define
$$
\begin{aligned}
-i\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p})
={}&(-i\electric{e})^2\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}\,
\gamma^\mu
\frac{i(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})}
{(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}\\
&\times\gamma^\nu\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is an amputated two-point insertion. It corrects the inverse electron propagator rather than giving a separate measurable emission-and-reabsorption history. No closed fermion chain occurs, so the loop does not supply the additional minus sign used for vacuum polarization.

The unit check distinguishes this result from scalar self-energy. Here one fermion propagator, one photon propagator, and one loop measure give total mass dimension one. A scalar inverse propagator has dimension two instead. Renormalization must fix both the electron pole position and its field normalization; the unregulated integral alone does not define a measured mass shift.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\Sigma}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron self-energy | The spinor-valued correction to the electron inverse propagator. |
| $\mechanical{p}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | External electron momentum | The momentum entering the self-energy insertion. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Loop momentum | The virtual photon integration momentum. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron mass | The mass in the internal electron propagator. |
| $\electric{e}$ | $1$ | Electric coupling | The charge magnitude at the two vertices. |
