## Exercise 7.2 — Position-space scalar propagator

The source asks you to show that, in four Euclidean dimensions at large separation,
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})
\sim\frac{e^{-\mechanical{m}\lvert\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}\rvert}}
{4\pi\lvert\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}\rvert}.
$$
Interpret the result physically.

> [!note]
> The printed $1/r$ prefactor is the three-dimensional static Yukawa form and has the wrong dimension for a four-dimensional scalar propagator. The massive four-dimensional Euclidean large-distance behavior has an $r^{-3/2}$ prefactor, with the corresponding mass-dependent coefficient. The exponential decay remains the common feature.

## Solution 7.2 — Scalar propagator in position space

Start with the Minkowski integral
$$
\signal{\Delta}_F(\mechanical{x})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{i\,e^{-i\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{x}}}
{\mechanical{k}^2-\mechanical{m}^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
A Wick rotation gives the four-dimensional Euclidean propagator
$$
\signal{\Delta}_{\mathrm E}(\mechanical{x}_{\mathrm E})
=\int\frac{d^4\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{e^{i\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}\cdot\mechanical{x}_{\mathrm E}}}
{\mechanical{k}_{\mathrm E}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}.
$$
In radial coordinates, set $\mechanical{k}=|\mechanical{\mathbf k}_{\mathrm E}|$ for the magnitude of the four-dimensional Euclidean momentum and $\mechanical{r}=|\mechanical{x}_{\mathrm E}|$. Angular integration gives
$$
\signal{\Delta}_{\mathrm E}(\mechanical{r})
=\frac1{(2\pi)^2\mechanical{r}}
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\mechanical{k}^2J_1(\mechanical{k}\mechanical{r})}
{\mechanical{k}^2+\mechanical{m}^2}\,d\mechanical{k}
=\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi^2\mechanical{r}}K_1(\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}).
$$
Here $J_1$ is the ordinary Bessel function and $K_1$ is the modified Bessel function of the second kind. For large positive argument,
$$
K_1(z)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2z}}e^{-z}.
$$
Therefore, for $\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}\to\infty$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{\Delta}_{\mathrm E}(\mechanical{r})
&\sim\frac{\mechanical{m}}{4\pi^2\mechanical{r}}
\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}}
e^{-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{\pi\mechanical{m}}}{4\pi^2\sqrt2}
\frac{e^{-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}}{\mechanical{r}^{3/2}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The exact Bessel expression has mass dimension two: the mass divided by the separation supplies two powers of energy, while the Bessel function has a dimensionless argument. The large-distance coefficient also has that dimension, because the square root of mass multiplies inverse separation to the power three halves. The source question's static inverse-distance form has dimension one instead.

Keep the order of limits explicit. The asymptotic Bessel expansion requires mass times separation to be large; one cannot set the mass to zero in that expansion at a fixed separation. The massless static limit discussed below is a limit of the time-integrated kernel, not a limit of the four-dimensional large-distance approximation.

The massive four-dimensional Euclidean propagator decays exponentially with length scale $1/\mechanical{m}$. This supplied solution has the correct $\mechanical{r}^{-3/2}$ prefactor, unlike the three-dimensional static Yukawa form printed in the exercise question.

Separations much larger than $1/\mechanical{m}$ are suppressed by $e^{-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}$. Integrating over the time separation gives the three-dimensional static Yukawa kernel. With the source's unit attractive coupling convention,
$$
\energy{V}(\mechanical{r})=-\frac{e^{-\mechanical{m}\mechanical{r}}}{4\pi\mechanical{r}}.
$$
Exchange of a massive field therefore gives a short-range interaction. As $\mechanical{m}\to0$, the radial dependence becomes $1/(4\pi\mechanical{r})$, the Coulomb form for massless exchange. The interaction couplings and charges determine the potential's overall coefficient and sign.
