---
title: "The Ward Identity and Its Consequences"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Ward–Takahashi identity relates the electromagnetic vertex to the inverse fermion propagator.

Gauge invariance in QED implies a fundamental relation between Green functions, the Ward–Takahashi identity.

For incoming fermion momentum $\mechanical{p}$, outgoing momentum $\mechanical{p}'$, and incoming photon momentum $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}$,
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p}')-\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p}).
$$
The source uses the inverse Dirac kernel
$$
\signal{S}_F^{-1}(\mechanical{p})
=\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p}).
$$
At tree level, $\Gamma_{\mathrm{tree}}^\mu=\gamma^\mu$ and the identity is
$$
\mechanical{q}_\mu\gamma^\mu
=\not{\mechanical{q}}
=(\not{\mechanical{p}}'-\mechanical{m})
-(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}).
$$

> [!note]
> The notation $\signal{S}_F^{-1}$ here denotes the inverse Dirac kernel with the propagator's overall factor of $i$ removed. It is not the literal matrix inverse of the earlier $i/(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}-\energy{\Sigma})$ expression. Keep this convention when using the Ward–Takahashi identity.

The tree check shows why the momentum must enter the photon leg as stated: reversing that convention reverses the difference of inverse kernels. Both sides have mass dimension one. This is an identity of Green functions, so the external fermion momenta need not be on shell.

For a physical photon amplitude, on-shell external spinors annihilate the appropriate inverse kernels. The remaining longitudinal contraction vanishes after all required contributions have been combined. This is why a polarization sum may be simplified in a complete gauge-invariant amplitude, while the same substitution can fail for a single Compton diagram.

> [!note] Remark 7.7 — Physical consequences
> 1. Vertex and electron-field renormalization are related: $\signal{Z}_1=\signal{Z}_2$. In the source's convention, $\electric{e}=\sqrt{\signal{Z}_3}\electric{e}_0$, where $\signal{Z}_3$ is the photon-field factor.
> 2. Real photons have two transverse physical polarizations. Longitudinal terms vanish in physical amplitudes by $\mechanical{q}_\mu\signal{\mathcal M}^\mu=0$, so the polarization sum can be replaced by $-g_{\mu\nu}$ after contraction.
> 3. The polarization tensor $\electric{\Pi}^{\mu\nu}$ is transverse and does not generate a photon mass term.
