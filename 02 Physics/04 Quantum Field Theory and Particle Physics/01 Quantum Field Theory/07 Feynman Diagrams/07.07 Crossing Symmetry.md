---
title: "Crossing Symmetry"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Crossing symmetry relates amplitudes with a particle moved between the incoming and outgoing states as its antiparticle.

Crossing symmetry follows from the analytic dependence of scattering amplitudes on external four-momenta.

Formally, an incoming particle of species $A$ with momentum $\mechanical{p}$ can be crossed to an outgoing antiparticle $\bar A$ with momentum $-\mechanical{p}$. Equivalently, one reverses the energy sign as part of the analytic continuation. Physical reaction regions must be reached with the appropriate continuation and external-state conventions.

The negative momentum in this statement is an analytic argument, not a negative-energy particle prepared in the laboratory. After continuation, the crossed experiment is described with positive-energy incoming and outgoing states in its own physical region. Momentum conservation and the external mass-shell conditions must be checked again with those assignments.

Crossing relates amplitudes. A cross section also depends on flux, phase space, and which initial spins or polarizations are averaged. These factors generally differ between crossed reactions, so one cannot cross a measured differential cross section by changing a single invariant label.
