title: "Vacuum Diagrams and Connected Structure"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Vacuum diagrams factor from normalized correlators, while connected and one-particle-irreducible diagrams organize physical amplitudes.

There are two separate questions: which graphs cancel when the vacuum is normalized, and which surviving graphs contain new irreducible interaction information? Read the vacuum article first for the common factor with no external attachments. Then compare connected and one-particle-irreducible graphs: the second condition is stronger.

A connected exchange graph can split when one internal line is cut, so connectedness alone does not make it a proper vertex. This distinction prevents a propagator correction or a repeated exchange from being counted again as a new irreducible interaction.
