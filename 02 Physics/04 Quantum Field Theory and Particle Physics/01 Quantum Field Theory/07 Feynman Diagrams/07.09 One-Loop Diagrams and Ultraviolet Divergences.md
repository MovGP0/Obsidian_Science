---
title: "One-Loop Diagrams and Ultraviolet Divergences"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Loop corrections introduce momentum integrals whose ultraviolet behavior must be regulated and renormalized.

Tree diagrams have no internal loops and supply the leading powers of $\electric{\alpha}$. They do not contain ultraviolet loop integrals. Higher perturbative orders introduce closed internal loops and the associated momentum integrations.

A loop integral sums over internal momenta that are not fixed by the external scattering kinematics. Its ultraviolet region tests arbitrarily short distances in the local field model. A divergence is therefore an obstruction in the perturbative expression, not a measured infinite scattering probability.

The photon, electron, and vertex examples identify the quantities that require common renormalization conditions. Count the large-momentum powers first, then test the gauge identities: the degree alone can overestimate the surviving divergence. A finite physical prediction requires both a regulator and a prescription that fixes the masses and charge from specified physical input.
