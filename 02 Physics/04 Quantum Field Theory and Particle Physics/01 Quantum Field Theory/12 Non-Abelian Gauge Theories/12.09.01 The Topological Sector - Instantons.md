---
title: "The Topological Sector — Instantons"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Instantons are finite-action Euclidean gauge-field solutions with nonzero topological charge. They describe tunneling between winding sectors and are related to QCD vacuum-angle physics.

QCD admits classical Euclidean solutions with nonzero **topological charge**: instantons.
They are self-dual or anti-self-dual:
$$
\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}^a=\pm\hat{\signal{F}}_{\mu\nu}^a,
\qquad
\hat{\signal{F}}_{\mu\nu}^a
=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
\signal{F}^{a\rho\sigma}.
$$
The source writes the topological charge as
$$
\signal{Q}=\frac{1}{32\pi^2}
\int d^4\mechanical{x}\,
\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}^a\hat{\signal{F}}^{a\mu\nu}
\in\mathbb{Z}.
$$
Instantons give transitions between vacuum winding sectors.
The source discusses vacua denoted by $|\signal{\theta}\rangle$, a contribution to strange-quark mass generation, and the strong CP problem: why the QCD vacuum angle is consistent with zero experimentally.

> [!note]
> With the canonical field-strength convention used in (12.4), the integer topological charge contains an additional factor $\signal{g}_s^2$:
> $$
> \signal{Q}=\frac{\signal{g}_s^2}{32\pi^2}
> \int d^4\mechanical{x}\,
> \signal{F}_{\mu\nu}^a\hat{\signal{F}}^{a\mu\nu}.
> $$
> The source's coefficient applies if the coupling is absorbed into the field strength. Integer-winding vacua and their $\signal{\theta}$-dependent superpositions must be distinguished. Instantons induce effective chiral interactions; this is not by itself a derivation of the strange-quark mass. The physical strong-CP angle also includes the phase of the quark-mass determinant.

Euclidean signature is essential to the displayed real self-duality condition. The Bianchi identity says that the covariant divergence of the dual curvature vanishes. For a self-dual or anti-self-dual field it therefore also gives the source-free field equation. This checks why the special configurations solve the second-order dynamics. Finite action requires appropriate behavior at infinity for the integer topological classification. See [Tong, section 2.3.1](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gaugetheory/gt.pdf#page=63).

In the corrected charge integral, four powers of inverse energy from the volume cancel four powers of energy from the two field strengths. This dimensional test permits a dimensionless coupling factor but cannot determine it; matching the connection normalization is necessary. The topology statement alone does not provide a controlled instanton approximation to all strong-coupling QCD observables.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}^a,\hat{\signal{F}}_{\mu\nu}^a$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Field strength and dual | The Euclidean Yang–Mills tensor and its Hodge dual. |
| $\signal{Q}$ | $1$ | Topological charge | The integer instanton winding number with the stated normalization. |
| $\mechanical{x}$ | $\unit{MeV^{-1}}$ | Euclidean coordinate | The position integrated over the instanton configuration. |
| $\signal{g}_s$ | $1$ | Strong gauge coupling | The coupling required with the canonical field-strength normalization. |
| $\signal{\theta}$ | $1$ | QCD vacuum angle | The parameter labeling the vacuum superposition and CP-violating topological term. |
