---
title: "QCD as an SU(3) Gauge Theory"
---

> [!TLDR]
> QCD is an SU(3) color gauge theory. Quarks transform in the fundamental representation, and gluons transform in the adjoint representation.

The color degrees of freedom are described in [[12.05.01 Color Charge and Representations]].

The group describes color rotations, while flavor labels distinguish quark species. Gauge interactions use the same color generators for each flavor; different quark masses do not turn the color group into a flavor symmetry. [[12.05.01 Color Charge and Representations]] explains which spaces the fields occupy, and [[12.05.02 Color Factors]] shows how those spaces enter amplitudes.

A useful first check is the count: three fundamental components per quark flavor and eight adjoint gauge fields. Physical hadrons use singlet combinations, while perturbative calculations retain colored fields as internal and short-distance degrees of freedom. The appearance of those fields in a calculation does not assert that a detector can observe an isolated quark or gluon.
