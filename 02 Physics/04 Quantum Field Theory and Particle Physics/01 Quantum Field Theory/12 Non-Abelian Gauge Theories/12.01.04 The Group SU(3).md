---
title: "The Group SU(3)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> SU(3) has eight generators, conventionally one half of the Gell-Mann matrices, with trace normalization T_F = 1/2.

$SU(3)$ is the group of unitary $3\times3$ matrices with determinant one.
It has $\dim SU(3)=8$ generators, usually chosen as $T^a=\lambda^a/2$.
The Gell-Mann matrices are
$$
\begin{gathered}
\lambda^1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\lambda^2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i&0\\i&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\lambda^3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&-1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\\
\lambda^4=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\lambda^5=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-i\\0&0&0\\i&0&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\lambda^6=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\\
\lambda^7=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&-i\\0&i&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad
\lambda^8=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&-2\end{pmatrix}.
\end{gathered}
$$
They obey
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda^a\lambda^b)=2\delta^{ab}.
$$
Thus,
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(T^aT^b)=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{ab},
$$
which is the standard normalization $T_F=1/2$.

The independent nonzero structure constants of $SU(3)$ are
$$
\begin{gathered}
f^{123}=1,
\qquad
f^{147}=f^{246}=f^{257}=f^{345}=\frac{1}{2},\\
f^{156}=f^{367}=-\frac{1}{2},
\qquad
f^{458}=f^{678}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.
\end{gathered}
$$
In addition to $f^{abc}$, there are symmetric symbols $d^{abc}$, defined by
$$
\{T^a,T^b\}=\frac{1}{N}\delta^{ab}\mathbf{1}+d^{abc}T^c.
$$
They occur in some color-factor calculations.
The completeness relation is
$$
\sum_{a=1}^{8}(T^a)_{ij}(T^a)_{kl}
=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}
-\frac{1}{N}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\right),
\qquad N=3.
$$

Use these matrices as a fixed basis, rather than treating the eight labels as eight quark colors. The fundamental vector has three components; the eight matrices span the traceless Hermitian transformations of that vector. The first three matrices form the displayed SU(2) algebra acting on its first two components, which checks the sign of the first structure constant.

The subtraction in the completeness relation removes the identity matrix. To check its coefficient, contract the first matrix's two indices: each generator has zero trace, and the two terms on the right cancel. Without this subtraction the relation would include an extra singlet generator and would describe the full unitary matrix basis. The symmetric symbols describe anticommutators; only the antisymmetric structure constants enter the classical Yang–Mills commutator.

## Example 12.1: Check of the SU(2) Casimir

In the two-dimensional fundamental representation,
$$
T^aT^a=\sum_{a=1}^{3}\left(\frac{\sigma^a}{2}\right)^2.
$$
Since $(\sigma^a)^2=\mathbf{1}$ for each $a$,
$$
T^aT^a=\sum_{a=1}^{3}\frac{(\sigma^a)^2}{4}
=3\cdot\frac{1}{4}\mathbf{1}
=\frac{3}{4}\mathbf{1}.
$$
This agrees with $C_F=(N^2-1)/(2N)$ at $N=2$, which gives $C_F=3/4$.
