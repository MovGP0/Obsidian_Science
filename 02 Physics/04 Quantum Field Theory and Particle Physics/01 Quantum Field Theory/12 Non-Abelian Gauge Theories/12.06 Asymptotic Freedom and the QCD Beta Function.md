---
title: "Asymptotic Freedom and the QCD Beta Function"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The one-loop QCD beta function is negative for fewer than 33/2 quark flavors. The strong coupling then decreases at high energy, giving asymptotic freedom.

The physical question is why the same interaction can be weak in a short-distance process and strong at hadronic distances. Renormalization relates a coupling defined at one scale to a coupling defined at another; it does not change a measured observable merely by changing a bookkeeping scale. The following result is a weak-coupling expansion for a fixed set of active quark flavors.

The minus sign before the coefficient matters. With three colors and six active flavors the coefficient is seven, so raising the scale reduces a positive small coupling. Removing quarks increases the antiscreening coefficient. Adding enough flavors reverses its sign. Neither this sign test nor extrapolation of a one-loop equation into strong coupling proves confinement.

Gross, Politzer, and Wilczek discovered asymptotic freedom in 1973 and received the Nobel Prize in 2004.
This result changed the understanding of the strong interaction.

The one-loop QCD beta function is
$$
\signal{\beta}(\signal{g}_s)
=\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\signal{g}_s}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
=-\frac{\beta_0}{16\pi^2}\signal{g}_s^3
+\mathcal{O}(\signal{g}_s^5).
$$

> [!note]
> **One-loop QCD coefficient**
> $$
> \beta_0=\frac{11C_A-4T_FN_f}{3}
> =\frac{11N_c-2N_f}{3}.
> \tag{12.13}
> $$

For $N_c=3$ and $N_f<33/2$, $\beta_0>0$, so $\signal{\beta}(\signal{g}_s)<0$ at weak positive coupling.
The coupling decreases as the scale increases.
This is **asymptotic freedom**.
