---
title: "Overall Result"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Combining gauge and quark contributions gives β₀ = (11C_A − 4T_FN_f)/3. The gauge term drives antiscreening, while quarks screen the color charge.

The source combines the three loop classes as
$$
\signal{\Pi}(\mechanical{k}^2)
=\frac{\signal{g}_s^2}{16\pi^2}
\left(\frac{11}{6}C_A-\frac{2}{3}T_FN_f\right)
\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\mu}^2}{-\mechanical{k}^2}\right)
+\text{finite terms}.
$$
It identifies the ultraviolet logarithm, written schematically as $\ln\energy{\Lambda}^2$, with the coefficient
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta_0
&=2\left(\frac{11}{6}C_A-\frac{2}{3}T_FN_f\right)\\
&=\frac{11}{3}C_A-\frac{4}{3}T_FN_f.
\end{aligned}
$$
With $C_A=N_c$ and $T_F=1/2$,
$$
\beta_0=\frac{11N_c-2N_f}{3}.
$$

> [!note]
> The final $\beta_0$ is the standard one-loop result. The preceding polarization formula is retained in the source's convention and must not be treated as a universal ordinary-gluon two-point function in every covariant gauge; see [[12.06.01 Structure of the One-Loop Calculation]]. A dimensionful ultraviolet scale in a logarithm stands for a ratio to a reference scale. Renormalizability permits local counterterms and does not by itself require every ultraviolet divergence to be logarithmic.

Read the final result as a statement about the renormalized coupling, after the required identities have combined the counterterms. The two terms have opposite roles: the gauge-sector Casimir gives antiscreening, and the fundamental trace factor multiplies one screening contribution per active Dirac flavor. The coefficients are pure numbers because the four-dimensional coupling is dimensionless.

Check the result independently of the source polarization normalization: for three colors and no quarks the coefficient is eleven; for six active flavors it is seven. Increasing the active flavor count by one lowers the coefficient by two thirds. These tests check the algebra and the sign of the running, but do not validate a gauge-independent interpretation of the earlier ordinary two-point function.
