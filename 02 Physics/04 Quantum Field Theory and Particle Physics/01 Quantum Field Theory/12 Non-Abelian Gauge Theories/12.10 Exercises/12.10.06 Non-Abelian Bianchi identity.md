## Exercise 12.6: Non-Abelian Bianchi identity

With
$$
\tilde{\signal{F}}^{a\mu\nu}
=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}^a,
$$
prove the Bianchi identity
$$
(D_\mu\tilde{\signal{F}}^{\mu\nu})^a=0.
$$
Use the field-strength definition (12.4) and the properties of the structure constants.

## Solution 12.6 — Non-Abelian Bianchi identity

Use
$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]=-i\signal{g}\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}
$$
in the Jacobi identity
$$
[D_\mu,[D_\nu,D_\rho]]
+[D_\nu,[D_\rho,D_\mu]]
+[D_\rho,[D_\mu,D_\nu]]=0.
$$
It follows that
$$
[D_\mu,\signal{F}_{\nu\rho}]
+[D_\nu,\signal{F}_{\rho\mu}]
+[D_\rho,\signal{F}_{\mu\nu}]=0.
$$
Contracting the cyclic identity with the Levi-Civita tensor gives the equivalent dual form
$$
\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}[D_\nu,\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}]=0.
$$

Define the dual tensor by
$$
\tilde{\signal{F}}^{\mu\nu}
=\frac12\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\signal{F}_{\rho\sigma}.
$$
The identity is $\boxed{D_\mu\tilde{\signal{F}}^{\mu\nu}=0}$.
It follows from the definition of the field strength as a curvature, without use of the equations of motion.

The brackets with a curvature mean that the differential operator acts on that matrix-valued curvature. They must not be replaced by ordinary matrix commutators of the connection alone. Apply the operator identity to a test field if the distinction is unclear; derivative-on-test-field terms cancel in the nested commutator.

No action was varied in this proof. The identity holds for every smooth connection in the region under study, even one that does not solve the Yang–Mills field equation. In the Abelian limit it becomes the homogeneous Maxwell identity. This contrast teaches the useful separation: curvature definitions give Bianchi identities, while a selected action supplies dynamics. Singular fields or nontrivial patches require care with the local assumptions before extending the identity globally.
