## Exercise 12.2: Covariance of the derivative

With $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\signal{A}_\mu$ and the gauge-field transformation (12.3), verify that
$$
D_\mu\signal{\psi}\to U(\mechanical{x})(D_\mu\signal{\psi}).
$$

> [!note]
> **Hint from the source:** Calculate
> $(\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\signal{A}_\mu^U)(U\signal{\psi})$
> and show that it equals $U(D_\mu\signal{\psi})$, using
> $$
> \signal{A}_\mu^U
> =U\signal{A}_\mu U^\dagger+\frac{i}{\signal{g}}U\partial_\mu U^\dagger.
> $$

## Solution 12.2 — Covariance of the covariant derivative

With
$$
D_\mu=\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\signal{A}_\mu,
\qquad
\signal{A}'_\mu
=U\signal{A}_\mu U^\dagger+\frac{i}{\signal{g}}U\partial_\mu U^\dagger,
\qquad
\signal{\psi}'=U\signal{\psi},
$$
expand
$$
\begin{aligned}
D'_\mu(U\signal{\psi})
&=(\partial_\mu U)\signal{\psi}+U\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}
-i\signal{g}
\left[U\signal{A}_\mu U^\dagger
+\frac{i}{\signal{g}}U\partial_\mu U^\dagger\right]U\signal{\psi}\\
&=(\partial_\mu U)\signal{\psi}+U\partial_\mu\signal{\psi}
-i\signal{g}U\signal{A}_\mu\signal{\psi}
+U(\partial_\mu U^\dagger)U\signal{\psi}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Differentiating $UU^\dagger=1$ gives
$$
(\partial_\mu U)U^\dagger=-U(\partial_\mu U^\dagger),
\qquad
U(\partial_\mu U^\dagger)U=-\partial_\mu U.
$$
The two derivative-of-$U$ terms cancel:
$$
D'_\mu(U\signal{\psi})
=U(\partial_\mu-i\signal{g}\signal{A}_\mu)\signal{\psi}
=U(D_\mu\signal{\psi}).
$$

The cancellation uses the product rule and the derivative of the inverse matrix, not commutativity. Keep the transformed connection to the left of the matter field and preserve every matrix order. For a constant transformation the derivative terms vanish separately; for a position-dependent transformation their cancellation tests the sign of the inhomogeneous term.

To reconstruct the argument, remove the inhomogeneous term temporarily. The leftover derivative of the transformation identifies exactly what must be restored. This is why a connection is needed to compare local internal bases. Covariance here means that the entire differentiated field transforms like the original field; it does not mean that either the connection or the spinor is gauge invariant.
