## Exercise 12.12: Symmetries of the four-gluon vertex

Verify that the four-gluon vertex (12.8) is invariant under simultaneous exchange of the complete labels $(a,\mu)\leftrightarrow(b,\nu)$ and $(c,\rho)\leftrightarrow(d,\sigma)$.
This is the Bose symmetry required for identical gluons.

> [!note]
> **Historical note from the source:** The source traces the gauge-symmetry idea to Hermann Weyl's work in the 1920s in the context of general relativity and local phase redefinition. Yang and Mills developed the non-Abelian generalization in 1954. Faddeev and Popov gave a consistent quantization with ghost fields in 1967. Gross and Wilczek at Princeton and Politzer at Harvard discovered asymptotic freedom in 1973, which supported the identification of QCD as the strong-interaction theory. The three received the 2004 Nobel Prize. Becchi, Rouet, and Stora (1974), and independently Tyutin (1975), formulated BRST symmetry.
>
> The source's Weyl attribution concerns the development of gauge ideas more broadly. Local phase redefinition by itself describes an Abelian symmetry; it does not supply the non-Abelian Yang–Mills structure.

## Solution 12.12 — Symmetry of the four-gluon vertex

With all external legs in the same momentum convention, the vertex is
$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{4,abcd}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
=-i\signal{g}^2\big[&
f^{abe}f^{cde}(g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})\\
&+f^{ace}f^{bde}(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho})\\
&+f^{ade}f^{bce}(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}-g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma})
\big].
\end{aligned}
$$
Check the exchange of complete external labels $(a,\mu)\leftrightarrow(b,\nu)$.

In the first term, $f^{abe}\mapsto-f^{abe}$ and
$$
g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}-g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}
\mapsto
g^{\nu\rho}g^{\mu\sigma}-g^{\nu\sigma}g^{\mu\rho},
$$
which is the negative of the original tensor. These signs cancel. The second and third terms exchange places. Thus the full vertex is invariant.

The exchange $(c,\rho)\leftrightarrow(d,\sigma)$ works in the same way. Complete external-leg permutations leave the vertex unchanged, as required by Bose symmetry. For this momentum-independent vertex, the paired labels are the color and Lorentz indices; a general momentum-dependent vertex also requires exchange of the associated momenta.

Exchange complete leg labels, rather than color labels alone. In the first term the structure constant changes sign and the antisymmetric metric combination changes sign, so their product survives unchanged. The remaining two terms must be exchanged as whole color–Lorentz products; checking only one of them would incorrectly suggest a failure of Bose symmetry.

A useful explicit test chooses SU(2) color labels and diagonal metric components, evaluates the sum, and repeats after the same paired exchanges. Zero components are valid checks but should be supplemented by a nonzero component so that sign errors are visible. The quartic rule is dimensionless and has no momentum dependence, which explains why the exercise needs no momentum exchange in its displayed labels.
