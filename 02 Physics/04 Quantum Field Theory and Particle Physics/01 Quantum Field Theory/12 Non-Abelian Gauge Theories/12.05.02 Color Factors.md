> [!TLDR]
> QCD color factors separate group structure from kinematics. The main invariants are C_F = 4/3, C_A = 3, and T_F = 1/2 for SU(3).

In perturbative QCD, color structures separate from kinematic structures.
The main color factors are as follows.

## Fundamental Casimir C_F

This factor occurs in quark corrections:
$$
C_F=\frac{N_c^2-1}{2N_c}=\frac{4}{3},
\qquad N_c=3.
$$
It weights the color strength of a virtual-gluon correction to a quark.

## Adjoint Casimir C_A

This factor occurs in gluon corrections:
$$
C_A=N_c=3.
$$
The value $C_A=N_c$, rather than $C_F$, reflects the larger color factor for the gluons' adjoint representation.

## Normalization factor T_F

This factor occurs in quark loops:
$$
T_F=\frac{1}{2}.
$$

## Useful relations

$$
(T^aT^a)_{ij}=C_F\delta_{ij},
\qquad
f^{acd}f^{bcd}=C_A\delta^{ab}.
$$

Further useful relations are
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(T^aT^b)=T_F\delta^{ab},
$$
and
$$
\sum_a(T^a)_{ij}(T^a)_{kl}
=T_F\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}
-\frac{1}{N_c}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\right).
$$

## Example 12.3: Color factor for quark–quark scattering

Consider $qq\to qq$ through gluon exchange between quarks of different flavors.
The single-gluon $t$-channel diagram has color structure
$$
C=(T^a)_{i'i}(T^a)_{j'j}.
$$
Here $i,i'$ are the incoming and outgoing colors of the first quark, and $j,j'$ those of the second.
In the squared amplitude,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i,i',j,j'}
\left\lvert(T^a)_{i'i}(T^a)_{j'j}\right\rvert^2
&=\operatorname{Tr}(T^aT^b)\operatorname{Tr}(T^aT^b)\\
&=T_F^2\delta^{ab}\delta^{ab}\\
&=T_F^2(N_c^2-1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Average over the $N_c^2$ incoming color states:
$$
\langle C^2\rangle
=\frac{T_F^2(N_c^2-1)}{N_c^2}
=\frac{(1/2)^2\cdot8}{9}
=\frac{2}{9}.
$$
For comparison, the corresponding QED color factor in
$e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-$ is one.

The average in Example 12.3 describes an equally weighted, incoherent ensemble of incoming parton colors. It is not the singlet projection used to construct a hadron. The exchanged adjoint label is already summed in the amplitude; the second adjoint label appears when that amplitude is complex conjugated. Hermiticity converts the external sums into two traces.

One can check the normalization before inserting three colors: omitting the initial average multiplies the result by the square of the number of colors. For three colors the unaveraged sum is two, and the averaged sum is two ninths. This color factor alone does not include spin sums, channel interference, couplings, or kinematic factors. Equal-flavor quark scattering requires the additional exchange channel.

## Example 12.4: Calculation of Tr(TᵃTᵇTᵃ)

Use the identity
$$
T^aT^bT^a=\left(C_F-\frac{C_A}{2}\right)T^b.
$$
It can be derived from the Lie algebra and
$$
T^aT^b=\frac{1}{2}[T^a,T^b]
+\frac{1}{2}\{T^a,T^b\}.
$$
Explicitly,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T^aT^bT^a
&=T^a(T^aT^b+if^{bac}T^c)\\
&=C_FT^b+if^{bac}T^aT^c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Since
$$
\begin{aligned}
if^{bac}T^aT^c
&=\frac{i}{2}f^{bac}[T^a,T^c]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}f^{bac}f^{acd}T^d
=-\frac{1}{2}C_AT^b,
\end{aligned}
$$
one obtains
$$
T^aT^bT^a
=\left(C_F-\frac{C_A}{2}\right)T^b
=-\frac{1}{2N_c}T^b.
$$
Taking the trace and using $\operatorname{Tr}(T^b)=0$ gives
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(T^aT^bT^a)=0.
$$
