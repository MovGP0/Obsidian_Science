---
title: "Color Charge and Representations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> QCD quarks carry three fundamental color components for each flavor. Gluons carry the eight adjoint color components of SU(3).

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is the non-Abelian gauge theory with group $G=SU(3)_c$.
The subscript $c$ means **color**.
Its fundamental degrees of freedom include quarks.

## Quarks

There are $N_f=6$ flavors: up, down, strange, charm, bottom, and top.
Each transforms in the fundamental representation $\mathbf{3}$ of $SU(3)$.

Each quark therefore has three color components,
$\signal{q}_\alpha^i$, where $\alpha$ is a Dirac index and $i=1,2,3$ labels red, green, and blue.
The quark–gluon interaction density is
$$
\energy{\mathcal{L}}_{q\text{-}g}
=\signal{g}_s\bar{\signal{q}}_i\gamma^\mu(T^a)_{ij}
\signal{A}_\mu^a\signal{q}_j.
$$
Here $\signal{g}_s$ is the strong coupling, and
$$
\signal{\alpha}_s=\frac{\signal{g}_s^2}{4\pi}.
$$

## Gluons

Gluons are the gauge bosons in the adjoint representation $\mathbf{8}$ of $SU(3)_c$.
They are denoted by $\signal{A}_\mu^a$, with $a=1,\ldots,8$.
Gluons are electrically neutral but carry color charge.
In contrast, the QED photon carries no electric charge.

The names red, green, and blue label a basis in an internal vector space. A local gauge change rotates that basis; an individual color component is not a separate gauge-invariant observable. An antiquark uses the conjugate fundamental representation, which is why a meson combines a fundamental with its conjugate.

Color and flavor must be counted separately. Six quark flavors in the theory does not require six active massless flavors in every running-coupling approximation. The appropriate low-energy effective description treats heavy thresholds separately. The interaction density has dimension four: two quark fields contribute three powers of energy and the gauge field contributes one, while the coupling and generators are dimensionless.

## Mesons and baryons

Observed hadrons are color-singlet states, which are gauge invariant.
The color-singlet meson combination is contained in
$$
\mathbf{3}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{3}}
=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{8}.
$$
The color-singlet baryon combination is contained in
$$
\mathbf{3}\otimes\mathbf{3}\otimes\mathbf{3}
=\mathbf{1}\oplus\mathbf{8}\oplus\mathbf{8}\oplus\mathbf{10}.
$$
The fully antisymmetric tensor $\varepsilon^{ijk}$ gives the baryon singlet contraction.

> [!note]
> The full quark–antiquark or three-quark tensor product is not itself a singlet. The decompositions show the singlet components selected for the physical meson and baryon color states.
