---
title: "Pauli–Villars Regularization"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Pauli–Villars regularization subtracts heavy auxiliary propagators to improve ultraviolet momentum decay.

Pauli and Villars proposed replacing a propagator by a difference:
$$
\frac1{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}}
\longrightarrow
\frac1{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}}
-\frac1{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-\mechanical{M}^{\,2}}.
\tag{9.6}
$$
Here $\mechanical{M}\gg\mechanical{m}$ is the regulator mass. The source calls the auxiliary field a ghost regulator and assigns it the opposite statistics to obtain the subtraction.

For $\mechanical{k}^{\,2}\gg\mechanical{M}^{\,2}$, the difference decays as $\mechanical{k}^{-4}$ rather than $\mechanical{k}^{-2}$. The physical limit is $\mechanical{M}\to\infty$.

> [!note]
> One subtraction improves the ultraviolet decay by two momentum powers. This does not make every integral finite: a quadratic divergence can remain logarithmic. More regulator terms are required when the remaining degree is still divergent. The auxiliary fields are regulator devices rather than physical external states.

Subtract the two fractions before expanding: their numerator is the difference of the two squared masses, while their denominator contains two quadratic propagator factors. The leading inverse-square momentum terms cancel. At any fixed momentum away from the physical pole, the auxiliary propagator vanishes when the regulator mass grows. Neither statement permits taking that limit inside a divergent unrenormalized integral: the convergence is not uniform over arbitrarily large momentum.

The subtraction therefore controls the intermediate integral, while renormalization still fixes the surviving dependence on the heavy mass. The auxiliary pole is not an additional particle-production channel of the final theory; it must disappear from its predictions when the regulator is removed.

The method respects Lorentz covariance. The source notes difficulties in extending the construction to fermion fields and beyond one loop.
