title: "The Lamb Shift"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Lamb shift is a radiative shift of atomic energy levels that removes a degeneracy predicted by the Dirac equation.

The physical question is whether the hydrogen levels that coincide in the ideal Dirac–Coulomb model remain degenerate after the electron interacts with the quantized electromagnetic field. The measured quantity is a transition frequency, or equivalently the difference of the two corrected energies divided by Planck's constant. A common shift of both levels would not explain the splitting.

Start with [[09.06.01 Historical and Experimental Context]] for the source measurement, then [[09.06.02 Physical Origin]] for the state-dependent corrections. [[09.06.03 Self-Energy Estimate - The Bethe Method]] explains why free-electron mass renormalization must precede the bound-state estimate. The leading estimates use an ideal Coulomb atom and small electromagnetic coupling. Precision comparison also requires the recoil and nuclear effects identified in that note; the leading logarithm is not a complete error budget.

The Lamb shift provides an atomic test of QED radiative effects. Its history and experimental setting are described in [[09.06.01 Historical and Experimental Context]].
