---
title: "Renormalization Constants in QED — Summary"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The one-loop QED renormalization constants remove ultraviolet poles. The Ward identity makes the vertex and electron-field constants equal.

The source summarizes the one-loop QED renormalization constants in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z_1}&=1-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
+\text{finite terms},\\
\signal{Z_2}&=1-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
+\text{finite terms},\\
\signal{Z_3}&=1-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi}\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
+\text{finite terms}.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> As in (9.82) and (9.83), the source's added “finite terms” are not part of strict $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ pole counterterms. Additional finite subtraction terms belong to a specified different scheme.

The mass renormalization factor is
$$
\signal{Z_m}
=1-\frac{3\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
+\text{finite terms}.
$$
The identity $\signal{Z_1}=\signal{Z_2}$ holds explicitly at this order.
The electromagnetic beta function follows from $\signal{Z_3}$:
$$
\electric{\beta}(\electric{e})
=\left.\energy{\mu}\frac{\partial\electric{e}}{\partial\energy{\mu}}
\right|_{\electric{e_0}\,\mathrm{fixed}}
=\frac{\electric{e}^3}{12\pi^2}
+\mathcal{O}(\electric{e}^5)
=\frac{\beta_0\electric{e}^3}{(4\pi)^2},
\qquad\beta_0=\frac43.
\tag{9.91}
$$
To use the constants as a consistency set, first check the equality of the electron-field and vertex pole coefficients. Their cancellation in the charge relation leaves the photon field factor. Then check the sign of the scale derivative at fixed bare charge: a positive derivative in the convention of (9.91) means an increase of renormalized charge with increasing scale. The constants themselves are intermediate, scheme-dependent quantities, not separate observables.

The one-species coefficient must be used in the theory with that charged species active. It cannot supply the full Standard Model evolution by itself. Also, removing ultraviolet poles does not remove infrared sensitivity from an on-shell residue. The present list is a one-loop ultraviolet summary in the stated gauge and convention; a precision amplitude needs finite matching, all contributions at that order, and its applicable infrared treatment.

The positive beta function means that the electromagnetic coupling grows with energy.
The source contrasts this with QCD, where it uses a negative $\beta_0$ and the coupling decreases: **asymptotic freedom**.
The discussion continues in [[10.01 Motivation - The Scale Problem]].

> [!note]
> The coefficient sign depends on how the beta function is defined. Here $\beta_0$ is the signed coefficient in $\beta=\beta_0e^3/(4\pi)^2$. Another common convention extracts an explicit minus sign and gives the asymptotically free QCD coefficient a positive name. Equation (9.91) is the one-loop result for one charged Dirac species.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{Z_1},\signal{Z_2},\signal{Z_3}$ | $1$ | QED renormalization constants | The vertex, electron-field, and photon-field factors. |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The electromagnetic coupling in the one-loop pole coefficients. |
| $\signal{Z_m}$ | $1$ | Mass renormalization constant | The multiplicative electron mass factor. |
| $\electric{e},\electric{e_0}$ | — | Renormalized and bare charges | The four-dimensional charge and the bare parameter held fixed in the scale derivative. |
| $\electric{\beta}$ | $1$ | Electromagnetic beta function | The change of the dimensionless coupling with logarithmic renormalization scale. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The scale used in the beta-function derivative. |
