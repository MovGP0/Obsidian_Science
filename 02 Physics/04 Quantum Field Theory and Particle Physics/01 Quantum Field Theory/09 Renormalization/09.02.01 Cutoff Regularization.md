---
title: "Cutoff Regularization"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Cutoff regularization imposes an upper bound on Euclidean loop momentum.

The most direct method imposes an upper bound $\energy{\Lambda}$ on the magnitude of Euclidean loop momentum:
$$
\int_0^\infty
\frac{\mechanical{k}^{\,3}\,d\mechanical{k}}{(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+\energy{\Delta})^2}
\longrightarrow
\int_0^{\energy{\Lambda}}
\frac{\mechanical{k}^{\,3}\,d\mechanical{k}}{(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+\energy{\Delta})^2}.
\tag{9.5}
$$
The parameter $\energy{\Lambda}$ represents a scale above which the theory is no longer used. The method is physically intuitive but has important drawbacks.

For a positive denominator scale, the integrand is regular at zero momentum and approaches the inverse momentum at large momentum. Thus (9.5) has a logarithmic ultraviolet divergence, consistent with a dimensionless radial integral: the numerator and denominator each have four powers of energy. The quadratic mass sensitivity listed below concerns other integrals, not the degree of this example.

If the cutoff is only an auxiliary regulator, take it to infinity after combining loops and counterterms while holding the chosen physical inputs fixed. If it represents an effective-theory boundary, predictions are restricted to external scales below that boundary. These two uses of the same symbol must not be interchanged.

The source lists three drawbacks:

1. Explicit violation of Lorentz covariance by treating space and time separately.
2. Violation of gauge-theory Ward identities, which complicates consistency checks.
3. Quadratic scalar mass terms of order $\energy{\Lambda}^{\,2}$, connected to the naturalness problem.

> [!note]
> The space–time distinction in the first statement applies directly to a spatial momentum cutoff. Equation (9.5) instead uses radial four-dimensional Euclidean momentum and does not itself single out a time component. A sharp cutoff can still obstruct loop-momentum shifts and gauge identities.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\Lambda}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Ultraviolet cutoff | The upper magnitude of the Euclidean loop momentum. |
| $\mechanical{k}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Radial loop momentum | The Euclidean momentum magnitude in the integral. |
| $\energy{\Delta}$ | $\unit{MeV^2}$ | Denominator scale | The mass-squared parameter in the radial denominator. |
