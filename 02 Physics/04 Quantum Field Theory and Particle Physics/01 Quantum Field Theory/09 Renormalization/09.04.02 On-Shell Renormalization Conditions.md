---
title: "On-Shell Renormalization Conditions"
---

> [!TLDR]
> On-shell renormalization fixes the electron pole mass, the propagator residue, and the electric charge at zero momentum transfer.

In the on-shell (OS) scheme, renormalization conditions make the renormalized parameters equal to physically measurable quantities. For QED:

1. **Electron-propagator pole.** The full propagator has its pole at $\not{\mechanical{p}}=\mechanical{m_e}$, where $\mechanical{m_e}$ is the physical electron mass. This fixes $\mechanical{\delta m}$.
2. **Propagator residue.** The pole residue is $i$, with the standard normalization. The source writes “$\signal{Z_2}=1$” in the sense of normalizing the physical residue. This fixes $\signal{\delta_2}$.
3. **Physical charge.** The renormalized charge is the Thomson charge at $\mechanical{q}^2=0$. Thus $\electric{\Pi_R}(0)=0$, which fixes $\signal{\delta_3}$.

> [!note]
> Unit residue applies to the renormalized propagator. It does not set the bare-to-renormalized field factor $\signal{Z_2}$ equal to one; that factor generally contains the counterterm needed to obtain the unit residue.

The pole and residue conditions fix two different terms in an expansion near the electron mass. A constant remainder moves the zero of the inverse denominator; its first-order momentum term changes the residue. Setting both remainders to zero therefore fixes the mass definition and field normalization separately. For the photon, zero-momentum subtraction normalizes the long-distance electric interaction, rather than forcing the polarization function to vanish at every momentum.

For example, subtract the value of the polarization at zero momentum from its value at a nonzero momentum. The momentum-independent ultraviolet pole cancels, while the difference of logarithms remains finite. Its argument is a ratio of squared-mass scales. This finite momentum dependence is the physical content that charge normalization must retain. A separate infrared prescription is still needed when the electron residue is evaluated on shell.

The source defines the inverse Dirac denominator by
$S^{-1}=\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}-\energy{\Sigma}$
and lists
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mechanical{\delta m}
&=\left.\energy{\Sigma}(\not{\mechanical{p}})\right|_{\not{\mechanical{p}}=\mechanical{m}},\\
\signal{\delta_2}=\signal{Z_2}-1
&=\left.
\frac{\partial\energy{\Sigma}}{\partial\not{\mechanical{p}}}
\right|_{\not{\mechanical{p}}=\mechanical{m}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> The counterterm signs require a specified convention for the loop self-energy and its counterterm contribution. For the full renormalized inverse denominator written above, the on-shell conditions are $\energy{\Sigma_R}(\mechanical{m})=0$ and a zero first derivative at the pole. The two displayed source formulas must be read with its counterterm convention rather than as independent definitions of the full renormalized self-energy.

> [!note] Additive mass-counterterm mapping
> The definition $\mechanical{m_0}=\mechanical{m}+\mechanical{\delta m}$ in [[09.04.01 Bare and Renormalized Quantities]] fixes the additive bare-mass counterterm. With the insertion derived in [[09.08.07 Counterterm Feynman rules]], the total self-energy on shell is $\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{tot}}(\mechanical{m})=\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{loop}}(\mechanical{m})+\mechanical{\delta m}$ at one loop: the field-counterterm terms cancel there. The pole condition therefore requires $\mechanical{\delta m}_{\mathrm{OS}}=-\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{loop}}(\mechanical{m})$. The printed positive source formula instead describes the loop-induced pole-mass shift before that counterterm is added. These two quantities have opposite signs in this convention.

The source states $\signal{\delta_2}<0$ because
$\left.\partial\energy{\Sigma}/\partial\not{\mechanical{p}}\right|_{\mechanical{m}}<0$,
in agreement with the pole term in (9.82).
For vacuum polarization, impose
$\electric{\Pi_R}(\mechanical{q}^2)
=\electric{\Pi}(\mechanical{q}^2)-\signal{\delta_3}=0$
at $\mechanical{q}^2=0$:
$$
\signal{\delta_3}=\electric{\Pi}(0)
=-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi}\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
+\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{3\pi}
\ln\frac{\mechanical{m}^2}{\energy{\mu}^2}.
\tag{9.84}
$$
This removes the UV divergence and fixes the charge through the low-energy fine-structure constant.
The source expresses this as
$\electric{\alpha}(\mechanical{m_e})=1/137.036$
at $\energy{\mu}=\mechanical{m_e}$.
Its on-shell $\signal{\delta_3}^{\mathrm{OS}}$ has the same divergent term
$-\electric{\alpha}/(3\pi\bar\epsilon)$ as
$\signal{\delta_3}^{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}$,
with an additional finite subtraction containing the mass-to-scale logarithm.
The source also mentions $\gamma_E$ and $\ln(4\pi)$ among these constants.

> [!note]
> Once both expressions use $1/\bar\epsilon$, the universal combination $-\gamma_E+\ln(4\pi)$ is already included in both pole subtractions. Their difference in (9.84) is the finite logarithm. The OS charge is defined in the Thomson limit; identifying it with a running $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ coupling at $\energy{\mu}=\mechanical{m_e}$ requires the corresponding scheme matching. The on-shell electron residue also has infrared sensitivity, so its full counterterm is not determined by the UV sign alone.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mechanical{p},\mechanical{q}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron and photon momenta | The momenta at the propagator pole and charge subtraction point. |
| $\mechanical{m_e},\mechanical{m},\mechanical{\delta m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron mass and mass counterterm | The physical on-shell mass and the counterterm fixed by the pole condition. |
| $\signal{Z_2},\signal{\delta_2},\signal{\delta_3}$ | $1$ | Field and counterterm coefficients | The electron field factor and electron and photon counterterm coefficients. |
| $\electric{\Pi_R}$ | $1$ | Renormalized polarization function | The function set to zero at zero momentum transfer. |
| $\energy{\Sigma},\energy{\Sigma_R}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Electron self-energies | The source self-energy and the fully renormalized self-energy in the pole conditions. |
| $\electric{\Pi}$ | $1$ | Unrenormalized polarization function | The scalar function whose zero-momentum value fixes the on-shell photon counterterm. |
| $\electric{\alpha}$ | $1$ | Fine-structure constant | The coupling fixed by the low-energy charge condition. |
| $\energy{\mu}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Renormalization scale | The scale in the finite subtraction logarithm. |
