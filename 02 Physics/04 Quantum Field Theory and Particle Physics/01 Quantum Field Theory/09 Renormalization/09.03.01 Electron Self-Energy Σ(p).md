---
title: "Electron Self-Energy Σ(p)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The one-loop electron self-energy comes from emission and reabsorption of a virtual photon and is evaluated with dimensional regularization.

## Diagram and starting integral

In Fig. 9.1, an electron emits a virtual photon of momentum $\mechanical{k}$, propagates with momentum $\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k}$, and then reabsorbs it.

![[QFT-09-01-Electron-Self-Energy-Momenta.svg|**Fig. 9.1** One-loop electron self-energy insertion $-i\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{p})$. Thick arrows show electron flow; thin arrows show the external momentum $\mechanical{p}$ and loop momentum $\mechanical{k}$.]]

The Feynman rules give
$$
\begin{aligned}
-i\energy{\Sigma}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
&=(-i\electric{e})^2\energy{\mu}^{\,2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}\\
&\quad\times
\frac{i\gamma^\mu
(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma^\nu}
{(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}+i\epsilon}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.21}
$$
The factor $\energy{\mu}^{\,2\epsilon}$ follows from (9.18). Contract the Lorentz indices and use the $d$-dimensional gamma identities, starting with
$$
\gamma^\mu\gamma_\mu=d\mathbf1.
$$
The identity matrix is explicit here and implicit in the source.

The additional gamma identities are
$$
\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{k}}\gamma_\mu=(2-d)\not{\mechanical{k}},
$$
$$
\gamma^\mu\not{\mechanical{k}}\not{\mechanical{q}}\gamma_\mu
=4\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{q}
-(4-d)\not{\mechanical{k}}\not{\mechanical{q}}.
$$
The scalar term in the second identity multiplies the identity matrix.

The numerator of (9.21) becomes
$$
\gamma^\mu(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}}+\mechanical{m})\gamma_\mu
=(2-d)(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}})+d\mechanical{m}.
\tag{9.22}
$$
Hence
$$
-i\energy{\Sigma}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
=-\electric{e}^{\,2}\energy{\mu}^{\,2\epsilon}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac{(2-d)(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}})+d\mechanical{m}}
{[\mechanical{k}^{\,2}+i\epsilon]
[(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}+i\epsilon]}.
\tag{9.23}
$$

## Feynman parametrization

Combine the denominators with
$$
\frac1{AB}
=\int_0^1dx\,\frac1{[xA+(1-x)B]^2}.
\tag{9.24}
$$
Set
$$
A=\mechanical{k}^{\,2},
\qquad
B=(\mechanical{p}-\mechanical{k})^2-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}
=\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-2\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{p}
+\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}.
$$
Then the combined denominator is
$$
\begin{aligned}
&x\mechanical{k}^{\,2}
+(1-x)(\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-2\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{p}
+\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2})\\
&=\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-2(1-x)\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{p}
+(1-x)(\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.25}
$$

Complete the square with
$$
\mechanical{\ell}^\mu=\mechanical{k}^\mu-(1-x)\mechanical{p}^\mu.
$$
This gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mechanical{k}^{\,2}-2(1-x)\mechanical{k}\cdot\mechanical{p}
+(1-x)(\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2})\\
&=\mechanical{\ell}^{\,2}
+\underbrace{-(1-x)^2\mechanical{p}^{\,2}
+(1-x)(\mechanical{p}^{\,2}-\mechanical{m}^{\,2})}_{-\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma}\\
&=\mechanical{\ell}^{\,2}-\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma,
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.26}
$$
where
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma(x,\mechanical{p}^{\,2})
&=-x(1-x)\mechanical{p}^{\,2}+(1-x)\mechanical{m}^{\,2}\\
&=(1-x)(\mechanical{m}^{\,2}-x\mechanical{p}^{\,2}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.27}
$$

Substitute $\mechanical{k}=\mechanical{\ell}+(1-x)\mechanical{p}$ into the numerator:
$$
(2-d)(\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{k}})+d\mechanical{m}
=(2-d)[x\not{\mechanical{p}}-\not{\mechanical{\ell}}]+d\mechanical{m}.
\tag{9.28}
$$

The term linear in $\mechanical{\ell}$ integrates to zero because it is odd. The numerator that remains is
$$
(2-d)x\not{\mechanical{p}}+d\mechanical{m}.
\tag{9.29}
$$
Thus
$$
\begin{aligned}
-i\energy{\Sigma}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
=-\electric{e}^{\,2}\energy{\mu}^{\,2\epsilon}
\int_0^1dx\,[(2-d)x\not{\mechanical{p}}+d\mechanical{m}]
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{(\mechanical{\ell}^{\,2}-\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma)^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.30}
$$

> [!note]
> The source keeps $\mechanical{k}$ in the integration measure after shifting the denominator to $\mechanical{\ell}$. The shift has unit Jacobian, so a consistent relabeling uses $d^d\mechanical{\ell}=d^d\mechanical{k}$.

## Evaluation with the master formula

Use (9.13) with $b=2$ and $a=0$. Writing $\energy{\Delta}=\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma$ in this master-formula step,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{d^d\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^d}
\frac1{(\mechanical{\ell}^{\,2}-\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma)^2}
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\frac{\Gamma(2-d/2)}{\Gamma(2)}
\,\energy{\Delta}^{\,d/2-2}\\
&=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}
\Gamma(2-d/2)\,\energy{\Delta}^{\,d/2-2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.31}
$$

For $d=4-2\epsilon$, one has $2-d/2=\epsilon$, and
$$
\Gamma(\epsilon)=\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E+\mathcal O(\epsilon).
\tag{9.32}
$$
Also,
$$
\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma^{\,d/2-2}
=\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma^{-\epsilon}
=e^{-\epsilon\ln\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma}
=1-\epsilon\ln\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma+\mathcal O(\epsilon^2).
\tag{9.33}
$$

It is useful to write
$$
\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma^{-\epsilon}
=\left(\frac{\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma}{\energy{\mu}^{\,2}}\right)^{-\epsilon}
\energy{\mu}^{-2\epsilon}.
$$
The last factor cancels the $\energy{\mu}^{\,2\epsilon}$ from the two couplings:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\electric{e}^{\,2}\energy{\mu}^{\,2\epsilon}
\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon}}
\Gamma(\epsilon)\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma^{-\epsilon}\\
&\quad\xrightarrow{\epsilon\to0}
\frac{i\electric{e}^{\,2}}{(4\pi)^2}
\left[
\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E
+\ln\left(\frac{4\pi\energy{\mu}^{\,2}}{\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma}\right)
+\mathcal O(\epsilon)
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
The separate logarithm of $\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma$ in the intermediate expansion is shorthand with a common reference unit. The final scale ratio in the logarithm is dimensionless.

Insert $d=4-2\epsilon$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Sigma}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
=\frac{\electric{e}^{\,2}}{(4\pi)^2}\int_0^1dx\,
&[(2\epsilon-2)x\not{\mechanical{p}}+(4-2\epsilon)\mechanical{m}]\\
&\times\left[
\frac1\epsilon-\gamma_E
+\ln\left(\frac{4\pi\energy{\mu}^{\,2}}{\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma}\right)
\right].
\end{aligned}
$$

The divergent part is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{div}}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
&=\frac{\electric{e}^{\,2}}{(4\pi)^2}\frac1\epsilon
\int_0^1dx\,(-2x\not{\mechanical{p}}+4\mechanical{m})\\
&=\frac{\electric{e}^{\,2}}{(4\pi)^2}\frac1\epsilon
(-\not{\mechanical{p}}+4\mechanical{m}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The source gives the finite part in the $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ scheme as
$$
\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
=\frac{\electric{e}^{\,2}}{(4\pi)^2}
\int_0^1dx\,(-2x\not{\mechanical{p}}+4\mechanical{m})
\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\mu}^{\,2}}
{\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma(x,\mechanical{p}^{\,2})}\right).
\tag{9.34}
$$
With $\electric{\alpha}=\electric{e}^{\,2}/(4\pi)$, its complete expression is written as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\energy{\Sigma}(\not{\mechanical{p}})
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}\Biggl[
&-(\not{\mechanical{p}}-4\mechanical{m})\frac1{\bar\epsilon}\\
&+\int_0^1dx\,(2x\not{\mechanical{p}}-4\mechanical{m})
\ln\left(\frac{\energy{\Delta}_\Sigma}{\energy{\mu}^{\,2}}\right)
\Biggr].
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!note]
> The displayed finite part omits a term from the $\epsilon$-dependent numerator on the preceding page. Multiplication of $2\epsilon x\not{\mechanical{p}}-2\epsilon\mechanical{m}$ by the $1/\epsilon$ pole gives the finite contribution $(\electric{\alpha}/4\pi)(\not{\mechanical{p}}-2\mechanical{m})$ after the parameter integral. Removing this term would require an additional finite subtraction beyond the stated prescription.

The ultraviolet pole is local: its coefficient contains only a term linear in external momentum and a mass term. These have the forms already present in the electron kinetic and mass terms, which is why two counterterms suffice for this two-point function at this order. The logarithmic finite part can depend nonpolynomially on momentum and cannot in general be removed by those local counterterms.

For the pole-mass equation below, expand about the unperturbed mass. Evaluating the one-loop self-energy at the shifted mass would change it only at the next perturbative order. The residue instead depends on the slope of the inverse propagator. These are distinct conditions, and ultraviolet subtraction alone does not remove the soft-photon issue in the on-shell slope. Throughout, the self-energy has mass dimension one, so it can be subtracted from the Dirac denominator in (9.35).

## Mass and field renormalization

The corrected propagator follows the Dyson equation:
$$
\signal{S}'_F(\mechanical{p})
=\frac{i}{\not{\mechanical{p}}-\mechanical{m}-\energy{\Sigma}(\not{\mechanical{p}})}.
\tag{9.35}
$$
Its physical pole lies at $\not{\mechanical{p}}=\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}}$ and satisfies
$$
\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}}-\mechanical{m}
-\energy{\Sigma}(\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}})=0.
$$
At first order,
$$
\mechanical{\delta m}
\equiv\mechanical{m}_{\mathrm{phys}}-\mechanical{m}
=\energy{\Sigma}(\not{\mechanical{p}}=\mechanical{m}).
\tag{9.36}
$$

The pole residue defines the field-renormalization factor:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\signal{Z}_2
&=\left(
1-\left.\frac{\partial\energy{\Sigma}}{\partial\not{\mechanical{p}}}
\right|_{\not{\mechanical{p}}=\mechanical{m}}
\right)^{-1}\\
&\simeq1+
\left.\frac{\partial\energy{\Sigma}}{\partial\not{\mechanical{p}}}
\right|_{\not{\mechanical{p}}=\mechanical{m}}\\
&=1-\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{4\pi}\frac1{\bar\epsilon}
+\text{finite terms}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9.37}
$$
The ultraviolet derivative is $\partial\energy{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{div}}/\partial\not{\mechanical{p}}=-\electric{\alpha}/(4\pi\bar\epsilon)$.

> [!note]
> The source refers to (9.34) for this pole coefficient, but (9.34) labels the finite part. The coefficient follows from the divergent part shown above. Also, (9.37) displays only the ultraviolet pole: the on-shell residue with a massless photon can have an infrared singularity, so the remainder requires the infrared prescription discussed in Chapter 8.
