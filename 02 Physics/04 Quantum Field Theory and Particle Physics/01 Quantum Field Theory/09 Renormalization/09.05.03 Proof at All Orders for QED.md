---
title: "Proof at All Orders for QED"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The all-orders QED proof classifies divergent amplitudes, shows that their counterterms are local, and constrains those counterterms by gauge symmetry.

The full proof of QED renormalizability at all orders of perturbation theory has three steps.

1. **Classify divergent diagrams.** By (9.3), only functions with superficial degree $D\ge0$ can be superficially divergent. In QED, the relevant external-line counts are
   $$
   (E_F,E_B)\in\{(0,2),(2,0),(2,1),(0,4)^*\}.
   $$
   The source marks $(0,4)$ with an asterisk because Ward identities make the complete four-photon contribution finite.

2. **Counterterm structure.** The divergences have the same form as terms already present in the Lagrangian (9.79). Gauge invariance requires the divergent terms to satisfy the Ward identity. They can therefore renormalize only the coefficients of
   $\bar{\signal{\psi}}\,i\not\partial\signal{\psi}$,
   $\mechanical{m}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\signal{\psi}$,
   $\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$, and
   $\electric{e}\bar{\signal{\psi}}\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}\electric{A}_\mu$.
3. **Complete BPHZ subtraction.** The forest formula makes the higher-order diagrams finite by subtraction, without new independent types of parameters. The source describes this in terms of fixing three renormalized quantities
   $(\mechanical{m_R},\electric{e_R},\signal{Z_{2,R}})$
   at a given order.

The induction behind these steps assumes that all proper divergent subgraphs have already been renormalized at lower order. The remaining overall divergence is then local, so power counting restricts it to a polynomial of bounded degree. Gauge identities restrict which of those polynomials can occur. This combines the subtraction theorem with the QED symmetry argument; power counting alone does not prove the allowed counterterm list.

The physical gain is reuse of the same measured mass and charge definitions at every order, with improved predictions as more coefficients are calculated. A useful failure test is a regulator result containing a photon mass term or unequal electron-field and vertex ultraviolet factors: such a result needs an identity check before it can support a QED prediction. Order-by-order renormalizability does not show that perturbation theory is accurate at arbitrarily large coupling.

The key point is that gauge symmetry and approximate chiral symmetry reduce the independent divergent structures to those of the original Lagrangian.
QED approaches a classically chiral theory as $\mechanical{m}\to0$.
The source connects this constraint with the Slavnov–Taylor identities, which generalize Ward identities to non-Abelian gauge theories.

> [!note]
> The field normalization $\signal{Z_{2,R}}$ is a normalization choice, not a third independent measured QED coupling. At each perturbative order, the same counterterm structures are sufficient, but their coefficients must be determined to that order. Classical axial chiral symmetry also requires the quantum-anomaly qualification; it must not be assumed to be an exact quantum symmetry.
