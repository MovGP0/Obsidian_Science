---
title: "The Lorentz Group SO(1, 3) and Its Universal Cover SL(2, ℂ)"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Lorentz algebra contains rotations and boosts. The connected proper orthochronous Lorentz group has SL(2, ℂ) as its universal double cover.

This section gives the Lorentz algebra, its complexified decomposition, and its spinor representations.

> [!note]
> The universal-cover statement refers to the identity component $SO^+(1,3)$, the proper orthochronous Lorentz group. The full $SO(1,3)$ also has a disconnected component.

## Local algebra, global group, and fields

Read [[B.08.01 Lorentz Algebra]] for the sign that distinguishes boosts from rotations. [[B.08.02 Decomposition into su(2) ⊕ su(2)]] classifies finite-dimensional complex field representations by two labels. Its complexification step does not turn real Lorentz boosts into compact rotations.

The global step is [[B.08.03 The Universal Cover SL(2, ℂ)]]. A full spatial $2\pi$ rotation is the identity on a four-vector but can act as minus the identity on a spinor. Thus infinitesimal commutators do not determine whether a field representation descends to the vector Lorentz group. [[B.08.04 Generators in the Spinor Representation]] and [[B.08.05 Lorentz Algebra in the Dirac Representation]] supply explicit matrices for this test.

These finite-dimensional transformations act on field components. The unitary representation on the quantum state space is a different construction. In particular, a four-component field is not automatically four independent physical polarizations. Dynamics, constraints, and the particle's mass must enter that count. Parity and time reversal also require more than the identity-component cover described here.
