---
title: "Zero-Point Energy"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Summing the half-quantum contribution of every mode gives an ultraviolet-divergent vacuum contribution.

The second term of (3.37) is the zero-point contribution. Each oscillator supplies half its mode energy. The source writes

$$
\energy{E}_0=\frac12\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}=\frac12\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sqrt{\mechanical{\mathbf k}^{2}+\mechanical{m}^{2}}\longrightarrow+\infty. \tag{3.38}
$$

For large momentum the mode energy grows linearly with momentum. The radial three-dimensional measure supplies two further powers, so the integral grows as the fourth power of a large momentum cutoff. This checks the energy-density dimension in the correction note below. At small momentum the massive integrand is integrable; for a massless field it remains integrable in three spatial dimensions. The divergence here is not an infrared effect.

This is an ultraviolet divergence: arbitrarily large momenta contribute. Regularization and renormalization provide systematic ways to treat such divergences, a central part of QFT.

> [!note]
> As written, (3.38) has units of energy density and omits the volume factor present formally in (3.37). The source's E₀ label and expression are retained.

A regulator makes the sum a well-defined intermediate expression; it does not by itself identify a measurable absolute vacuum energy. First separate the volume factor, then state which reference vacuum or boundary configuration is subtracted. Normal ordering in this free flat-spacetime theory is one such reference choice. This calculation alone does not determine vacuum energy in a theory with gravity.
