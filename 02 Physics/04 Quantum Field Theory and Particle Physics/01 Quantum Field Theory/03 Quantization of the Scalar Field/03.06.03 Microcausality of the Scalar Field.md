## Theorem 3.9 — Scalar-Field Microcausality

For spacelike-separated events, $(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})^{2}<0$,

$$
[\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{x}),\signal{\phi}(\mechanical{y})]=i\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=0. \tag{3.60}
$$

The assumptions are the free scalar mode expansion, the canonical oscillator algebra, and proper orthochronous Lorentz invariance of the mass-shell measure. The argument concerns separated events and the associated distribution, not a finite value assigned at coincidence.

**Proof.** The commutator function is odd and depends only on the event difference. For a spacelike difference, a proper orthochronous transformation can map that difference to its negative. Lorentz invariance then gives $\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})=\signal{\Delta}(-(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y}))=-\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})$, so it vanishes.

Explicitly, transform to a frame with $\mechanical{x}^{0}-\mechanical{y}^{0}=0$. The time phase is one, and

$$
\signal{\Delta}(\mechanical{x}-\mechanical{y})\propto\int\frac{d^{3}\mechanical{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\energy{\omega}_{\mathbf k}}[e^{i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}-e^{-i\mechanical{\mathbf k}\cdot(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y})}]=0.
$$

Why can a spacelike vector be mapped to its negative without time reversal? First boost to its equal-time frame. A spatial rotation through half a turn then reverses its spatial direction, and the inverse boost returns to the original frame. A timelike vector cannot be reversed by such a transformation because its time orientation is preserved. The same reasoning therefore does not force the timelike commutator to vanish.

The two exponentials cancel under reversal of the integration momentum.

Only the difference of the two frequency integrals cancels. Either Wightman integral can remain nonzero outside the light cone. This supplies a useful counterexample to the claim that every propagation function must have causal support. The zero commutator, together with local observables, is the relevant condition for independent operations.
