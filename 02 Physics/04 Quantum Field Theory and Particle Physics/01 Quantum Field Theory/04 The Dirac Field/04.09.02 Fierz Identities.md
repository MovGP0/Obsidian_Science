---
title: "Fierz Identities"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Fierz identities change the pairing of spinors in products of bilinear forms.

Fierz identities reorder bilinear forms. For commuting spinors, the scalar identity is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\signal{\psi}_2)(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\signal{\psi}_4)
=\frac14\Big[&(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\signal{\psi}_4)(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\signal{\psi}_2)
+(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\gamma^\mu\signal{\psi}_4)(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\gamma_\mu\signal{\psi}_2)
+\tfrac12(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\sigma^{\mu\nu}\signal{\psi}_4)(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\sigma_{\mu\nu}\signal{\psi}_2)\\
&-(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\signal{\psi}_4)(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\gamma_\mu\gamma^5\signal{\psi}_2)
+(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_1\gamma^5\signal{\psi}_4)(\bar{\signal{\psi}}_3\gamma^5\signal{\psi}_2)\Big].
\end{aligned} \tag{4.61}
$$
For anticommuting field operators, the right side has an additional overall minus sign. The sum uses the 16 basis matrices $1,\gamma^\mu,\sigma^{\mu\nu},\gamma^\mu\gamma^5,\gamma^5$. These identities help calculate amplitudes for processes with four fermions.

The identity changes which external spinor is paired with which adjoint. It does not change the physical four-field operator when the complete matrix basis and its dual are used. This can reveal that two apparent interaction terms are alternate forms of the same coupling.

First do a matrix-component completeness calculation, then restore the field ordering. Numerical external spinors commute, whereas field components anticommute; exchanging the latter can supply an additional minus sign. This order of work separates the matrix identity from its statistics sign.

A simple diagnostic is to test all four external spinors on chosen basis columns. Failure even for one component choice detects missing tensor weights or a wrong dual basis. Equation (4.61) passes this check for all component choices.
