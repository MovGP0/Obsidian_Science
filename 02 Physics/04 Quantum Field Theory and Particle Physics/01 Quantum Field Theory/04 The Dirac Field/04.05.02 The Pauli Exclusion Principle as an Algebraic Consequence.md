---
title: "The Pauli Exclusion Principle as an Algebraic Consequence"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A fermionic creation operator squares to zero, preventing double occupation of one mode.

The creation–creation anticommutator implies

$$
2b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(b^{s\dagger}_{\mathbf p})^{2}=0. \tag{4.34}
$$

Applying the squared operator to the vacuum gives zero. Two fermions cannot occupy the same momentum and spin state. The Pauli exclusion principle is thus an algebraic consequence of the anticommutation relations.

The rule excludes two identical fermions from the same complete one-particle state. It does not exclude two particles from the same position region or two opposite spin states at the same momentum. Antiparticles have their own mode family and obey the same rule within that family.

To test the distinction, try to create two particles with equal momentum and opposite spin. Their operators anticommute, but their product need not vanish. Setting both spin and momentum equal makes the product its own negative and therefore zero. For a continuum basis this argument is made precisely with normalized wave-packet modes or finite-volume modes.
