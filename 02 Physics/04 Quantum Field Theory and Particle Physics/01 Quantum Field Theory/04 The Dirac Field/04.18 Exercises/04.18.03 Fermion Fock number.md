## Exercise 4.3 — Fermion Fock number

For one fermionic mode, let $b,b^\dagger$ satisfy $\{b,b^\dagger\}=1$ and $b^2=(b^\dagger)^2=0$.

(a) Construct explicit $2\times2$ matrices for $b$ and $b^\dagger$.

(b) Verify that $\hat{\signal{n}}=b^\dagger b$ has eigenvalues $\{0,1\}$.

(c) Calculate $[\energy{H},b]$ and $[\energy{H},b^\dagger]$ for $\energy{H}=\energy{E}\hat{\signal{n}}$. Interpret the results physically.

## Solution 4.3 — Fermion Fock number

**(a)** In the basis $|0\rangle=(1,0)^T$, $|1\rangle=(0,1)^T$, choose
$$
b=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
b^\dagger=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $b|0\rangle=0$, $b^\dagger|0\rangle=|1\rangle$,
$b|1\rangle=|0\rangle$, and $b^\dagger|1\rangle=0$.
Direct multiplication gives
$$
bb^\dagger=\operatorname{diag}(1,0),\qquad
b^\dagger b=\operatorname{diag}(0,1),
$$
so $\{b,b^\dagger\}=\mathbf1$ and $b^2=(b^\dagger)^2=0$.

**(b)** The number operator is
$$
\hat{\signal{n}}=b^\dagger b=\operatorname{diag}(0,1).
$$
Its eigenvalues are zero and one. More generally,
$$
\hat{\signal{n}}^2
=b^\dagger bb^\dagger b
=b^\dagger(1-b^\dagger b)b
=b^\dagger b-(b^\dagger)^2b^2
=\hat{\signal{n}}.
$$
Thus $\hat{\signal{n}}(\hat{\signal{n}}-1)=0$.

**(c)** With $\energy{H}=\energy{E}\hat{\signal{n}}$,
$$
[\energy{H},b]
=\energy{E}[\hat{\signal{n}},b]
=\energy{E}(b^\dagger bb-bb^\dagger b).
$$
Use $b^2=0$ and $bb^\dagger=1-b^\dagger b$:
$$
[\energy{H},b]
=\energy{E}\bigl[0-(1-b^\dagger b)b\bigr]
=-\energy{E}b.
$$
Similarly,
$$
[\energy{H},b^\dagger]=+\energy{E}b^\dagger.
$$
The creation operator raises the energy by $\energy{E}$, and the annihilation operator lowers it by $\energy{E}$. This agrees with
$$
\energy{H}|1\rangle=\energy{E}|1\rangle,\qquad \energy{H}|0\rangle=0.
$$

The commutators in part (c) use the even Hamiltonian and an odd creation or annihilation operator. Fermionic statistics does not replace every commutator by an anticommutator; it fixes the graded rule according to the two operator parities.

Check the result on both basis vectors. Creation raises the vacuum to the occupied state, but its action on the occupied state is zero, rather than a state with twice the energy. The two-dimensional construction describes one normalized mode only. Two independent fermion modes require a larger state space and relative signs that enforce anticommutation between the modes.

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\signal{n}$ | $1$ | Mode occupation | The measured occupation, zero or one. |
| $\energy{H}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mode Hamiltonian | The energy of the one-mode system. |
| $\energy{E}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Excitation energy | The energy needed to occupy the mode. |
