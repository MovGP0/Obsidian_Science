## Exercise 4.8 — First-order anomalous magnetic moment

In QED, the corrected interaction vertex has the form
$$
\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\gamma^\mu\electric{F}_1(\mechanical{q}^{\,2})
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\magnetic{F}_2(\mechanical{q}^{\,2}), \tag{4.119}
$$
with $\mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}$. Use the Gordon identity (4.106) to show that as $\mechanical{q}\to0$, the form factor $\magnetic{F}_2(0)$ contributes to the electron magnetic moment as
$$
\magnetic{\mu}=(1+\magnetic{F}_2(0))\magnetic{\mu}_B,
\qquad
\magnetic{\mu}_B=\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}}.
$$
Here $\magnetic{\mu}_B$ is the Bohr magneton. The explicit first-order result $\magnetic{F}_2(0)=\electric{\alpha}/(2\pi)$ is Schwinger's result from 1948.

## Solution 4.8 — Anomalous magnetic moment

The corrected vertex is
$$
\Gamma^\mu(\mechanical{p}',\mechanical{p})
=\gamma^\mu\electric{F}_1(\mechanical{q}^2)
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
\magnetic{F}_2(\mechanical{q}^2),
\qquad \mechanical{q}=\mechanical{p}'-\mechanical{p}.
$$
Insert the Gordon identity:
$$
\bar u\Gamma^\mu u
=\bar u\left[
\frac{(\mechanical{p}'+\mechanical{p})^\mu}{2\mechanical{m}}\electric{F}_1
+\frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu}{2\mechanical{m}}
(\electric{F}_1+\magnetic{F}_2)
\right]u.
$$
The first, convective term gives the minimal charge coupling. The second gives the magnetic dipole coupling, so
$$
\frac{\magnetic{g}_s}{2}=\electric{F}_1(0)+\magnetic{F}_2(0).
$$
Charge normalization fixes $\electric{F}_1(0)=1$. In the long-wavelength limit $\mechanical{q}\to0$, the spin-one-half magnetic moment is
$$
\magnetic{\mu}
=\magnetic{g}_s\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}}\frac12
=[1+\magnetic{F}_2(0)]\frac{\electric{e}}{2\mechanical{m}}
=[1+\magnetic{F}_2(0)]\magnetic{\mu}_B.
$$
This uses the source's positive moment magnitude; the electron's signed charge fixes the direction relative to its spin. The anomaly is
$$
\magnetic{a}_e=\frac{\magnetic{g}_s-2}{2}=\magnetic{F}_2(0).
$$
At first order in QED, Schwinger's result is
$$
\magnetic{a}_e^{(1)}
=\frac{\electric{\alpha}}{2\pi}
\approx1.16\times10^{-3}.
$$
The calculation evaluates the one-loop vertex diagram with a virtual photon and extracts the coefficient of $i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mechanical{q}_\nu/(2\mechanical{m})$ from the renormalized vertex. Chapter 10 gives the details.

Do not set the transfer to zero before identifying the magnetic structure. The term linear in transfer determines response to a slowly varying magnetic field; its coefficient can remain finite even though the term vanishes at exactly zero transfer. The charge normalization and the magnetic coefficient are distinct inputs to this limit.

As a check, turn off the loop correction. The Pauli form factor vanishes and the result reduces to the Dirac spin factor two. The quoted first-order anomaly is a dimensionless perturbative prediction, not an exact value or a measurement. Its derivation needs the loop calculation; the Gordon identity only identifies the coefficient that the calculation must extract.
