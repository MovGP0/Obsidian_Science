---
title: "The Spin–Statistics Theorem"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Relativistic locality and positive energy tie integer spin to bosonic commutators and half-integer spin to fermionic anticommutators.

The spin–statistics theorem requires integer-spin bosons to use commutators and half-integer-spin fermions to use anticommutators. The wrong choice conflicts with locality or energy positivity.

For fermions with the wrong statistics, the spectrum can be unbounded below or energy density can fail positivity. The rigorous proof, associated with Pauli (1940) and Lüders–Zumino (1958), uses positive state norms, commutation of spacelike observables, Lorentz covariance, and analytic correlation functions.

The theorem supplies a consistency condition for relativistic quantum fields. It does not derive half-integer spin from the exclusion principle alone. The assumptions do work: the state inner product must be positive, the energy spectrum must have a lower bound, and local observables must commute at spacelike separation.

For a fermion field, locality is expressed by spacelike anticommutation of the field operators; the measurable local even products then have ordinary spacelike commutation. Keep these two statements separate. Also, positivity of the total Hamiltonian is not the claim that every local energy density is pointwise positive. The failed free-field calculation in [[04.03.01 The Attempt with Commutators]] illustrates the total-spectrum obstruction. [Tong, section 5.1](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/qfthtml/S5.html) gives this explicit model check.
