---
title: "Dirac Brackets and Quantization"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Dirac brackets include second-class constraints before the brackets are promoted to quantum commutators or anticommutators.

For a second-class system with constraints $\phi_a$, define
$$
\{F,G\}_D
=\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{PB}}
-\{F,\phi_a\}_{\mathrm{PB}}(C^{-1})^{ab}
\{\phi_b,G\}_{\mathrm{PB}}, \tag{4.104}
$$
where $C^{ab}=\{\phi_a,\phi_b\}_{\mathrm{PB}}$ in the source notation. The constraint matrix must be invertible on the constrained sector. For fermionic variables, use the consistent graded Poisson bracket and its index conventions.

Canonical quantization promotes the brackets, with $\hbar=1$, as
$$
\{F,G\}_D\longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
\dfrac1i[F,G],&\text{bosons},\\
\dfrac1i\{F,G\},&\text{fermions}.
\end{cases} \tag{4.105}
$$
For the Dirac field, the result agrees with canonical anticommutation relations (4.25)–(4.26). It thus supports the direct prescription in §4.3.

The correction in (4.104) removes precisely the bracket components normal to the constraint surface. Multiplying it by a constraint bracket and using the inverse-matrix identity shows that every constraint has zero Dirac bracket with any allowed function. The constraints can then be imposed strongly within the reduced bracket algebra.

This gives a useful reconstruction test: if a proposed reduced bracket does not annihilate the full second-class set, the inverse matrix or graded signs are wrong. The boson/fermion shorthand in (4.105) must be applied according to the parity of both entries: two odd variables give an anticommutator, while brackets involving an even observable use the corresponding graded commutator. In particular an even Hamiltonian evolves a fermionic field by an ordinary commutator.
