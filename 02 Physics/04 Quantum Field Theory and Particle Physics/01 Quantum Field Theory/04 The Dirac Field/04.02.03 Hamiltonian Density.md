title: "Hamiltonian Density"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Dirac Hamiltonian density contains a spatial first-order operator and a mass term.

The Legendre transformation gives

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=\mechanical{\pi}\partial_0\signal{\psi}-\energy{\mathcal L}=i\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}\partial_0\signal{\psi}-\overline{\signal{\psi}}(i\not{\partial}-\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}. \tag{4.20}
$$

The field equation writes the time derivative in terms of spatial derivatives, with $\boldsymbol\alpha=\gamma^{0}\boldsymbol\gamma$. Hence

$$
\energy{\mathcal H}=\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}(-i\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\nabla+\mechanical{m}\gamma^{0})\signal{\psi}=\overline{\signal{\psi}}(-i\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\nabla+\mechanical{m})\signal{\psi}. \tag{4.21}
$$

The time-derivative terms cancel directly in the Legendre transformation. No use of the field equation is needed for this cancellation. The field equation then identifies the remaining spatial operator as the generator of time evolution.

With fields that vanish at spatial infinity, integration by parts makes the spatial derivative term Hermitian. At rest its one-particle matrix has eigenvalues of both signs; this is a check on the classical operator, not yet a claim about the energy of physical antiparticles. Positive particle and antiparticle excitation energies emerge after fermionic quantization and reordering in [[04.06.01 Hamiltonian Operator in Mode Variables]].

## Physical quantities

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\energy{\mathcal H},\energy{\mathcal L}$ | $\unit{MeV^{4}}$ | Dynamical densities | The Dirac Hamiltonian and Lagrangian densities. |
| $\signal{\psi},\overline{\signal{\psi}}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Dirac field and adjoint | The spinor variables in the energy density. |
| $\mechanical{\pi}$ | $\unit{MeV^{3/2}}$ | Conjugate momentum density | The variable used in the Legendre transformation. |
| $\mechanical{m}$ | $\unit{MeV}$ | Mass | The fermion rest mass. |
