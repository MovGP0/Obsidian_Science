---
title: "Normalization Products and Orthogonality Relations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Dirac adjoint gives opposite invariant normalization signs for the two mode branches and zero mixed products.

Define the Dirac adjoint by

$$
\overline{\signal{\psi}}=\signal{\psi}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}. \tag{4.9}
$$

It forms Lorentz-covariant bilinears. The mode-spinor products are

$$
\overline{\signal{u}}(\mechanical{p},s)\signal{u}(\mechanical{p},s')=2\mechanical{m}\delta_{ss'}, \tag{4.10}
$$

$$
\overline{\signal{v}}(\mechanical{p},s)\signal{v}(\mechanical{p},s')=-2\mechanical{m}\delta_{ss'}, \tag{4.11}
$$

$$
\overline{\signal{u}}(\mechanical{p},s)\signal{v}(\mechanical{p},s')=\overline{\signal{v}}(\mechanical{p},s)\signal{u}(\mechanical{p},s')=0.
$$

The negative sign in the second branch matters in the Hamiltonian calculation. At the same four-momentum,

$$
\signal{u}^{\dagger}(\mechanical{p},s)\signal{u}(\mechanical{p},s)=\signal{v}^{\dagger}(\mechanical{p},s)\signal{v}(\mechanical{p},s)=2\energy{E}_{\mathbf p}. \tag{4.12}
$$

Two different products serve different purposes. The adjoint with gamma zero gives a Lorentz scalar, so its value is fixed by the rest mass. The Hermitian product gives the positive time component of a current and therefore changes with energy under boosts. A negative invariant product for the second branch is not a negative Hilbert-space probability.

At rest, gamma zero acts as plus the identity on the nonzero particle block and minus the identity on the nonzero antiparticle block. This immediately checks (4.10)–(4.12). For zero mass the invariant diagonal products vanish, while the Hermitian norm remains nonzero at nonzero momentum. Thus a vanishing invariant product does not mean that the mode is absent.

| Symbol | Unit | Quantity | Description |

| $\signal{\psi},\overline{\signal{\psi}}$ | — | Dirac amplitude and adjoint | The wave amplitude and its Lorentz-covariant conjugate. |
