title: "The Belinfante–Rosenfeld Tensor"

> [!TLDR]
> The Belinfante–Rosenfeld improvement produces a symmetric energy–momentum tensor without changing the conserved charges.

## Definition 2.8 — Belinfante–Rosenfeld Tensor

The symmetric energy–momentum tensor is

$$
\energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}+\frac12\partial_{\rho}(\mechanical{S}^{\rho\mu\nu}-\mechanical{S}^{\mu\rho\nu}-\mechanical{S}^{\nu\rho\mu})
\equiv\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\rho}B^{\rho\mu\nu}. \tag{2.38}
$$

> [!note] Spin-current convention correction
> Equations (2.37) and (2.38) retain different source sign conventions. With the orbital order in (2.37), angular-current conservation requires
> $$
> \partial_{\rho}\mechanical{S}^{\rho\mu\nu}=\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}-\energy{T}^{\nu\mu}.
> $$
> The improvement in (2.38) instead has the antisymmetric part of its superpotential equal to this spin tensor. Using the same spin tensor in both formulas would therefore double the antisymmetric part of the energy–momentum tensor. To use the spin current of (2.37), reverse the overall sign of the improvement:
> $$
> \energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}-\frac12\partial_{\rho}(\mechanical{S}^{\rho\mu\nu}-\mechanical{S}^{\mu\rho\nu}-\mechanical{S}^{\nu\rho\mu}).
> $$
> Equivalently, the spin tensor used in the printed (2.38) is minus the spin tensor in (2.37). Compare [Blaschke et al., equations (2.7) and (2.10)](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.01121#S2.SS2.SSS1), whose orbital order is opposite to (2.37).

The spin tensor is defined by the field variation under Lorentz transformations. By construction, $\energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=\energy{\Theta}^{\nu\mu}$ and $\partial_{\mu}\energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=0$. Only divergences of antisymmetric tensors have been added. The conserved charges remain unchanged:

$$
\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{\Theta}^{0\nu}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\,\energy{T}^{0\nu}=\mechanical{P}^{\nu}. \tag{2.39}
$$

The extra term is a spatial divergence whose surface contribution vanishes at infinity.

With gravitation, this tensor is the source in Einstein's equation, $G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi\mechanical{G}\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}$. Alternatively, it follows by varying the action with respect to the metric:

$$
\energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}=-\left.\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\partial(\sqrt{-g}\,\energy{\mathcal L})}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}\right|_{g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}}. \tag{2.40}
$$

This gravitational-coupling method gives a symmetric tensor for the appropriate matter action; the coupling and boundary terms must be specified.

> [!note] Sign correction in the metric variation
> Equation (2.40) retains the source expression. For this chapter's scalar Lagrangian and signature, a minus sign in variation with respect to the inverse metric gives the negative of (2.41). The convention consistent with positive scalar energy is
> $$
> \energy{\Theta}_{\mu\nu}=\left.\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\energy{S}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\right|_{g=\eta}.
> $$
> A functional derivative is required in general; the partial derivative suffices when the matter density has no metric derivatives. See [Blaschke et al., section 3.2.1, following (3.9)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.01121#page=18).

For the minimally coupled scalar, varying the inverse metric in the kinetic contraction and in the volume factor reproduces (2.41). A spatially uniform, time-dependent field then has positive kinetic energy. This is a direct sign check, independent of the gravitational curvature convention. Nonminimal curvature couplings can add further improvement terms, so a flat-space action alone does not uniquely fix its curved-space extension.

## Example 2.9 — Scalar-Field Belinfante Tensor

For $\energy{\mathcal L}=\tfrac12(\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi})^{2}-\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})$, the canonical tensor is already symmetric. The scalar spin contribution vanishes, so

$$
\energy{T}^{\mu\nu}=\energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=(\partial^{\mu}\signal{\phi})(\partial^{\nu}\signal{\phi})-g^{\mu\nu}\energy{\mathcal L}. \tag{2.41}
$$

The scalar components are

$$
\energy{T}^{00}=\tfrac12\signal{\dot\phi}^{2}+\tfrac12|\nabla\signal{\phi}|^{2}+\energy{V}(\signal{\phi})=\energy{\mathcal H},
$$

$$
\energy{T}^{0i}=\signal{\dot\phi}\partial^{i}\signal{\phi},\qquad
\energy{T}^{ij}=(\partial^{i}\signal{\phi})(\partial^{j}\signal{\phi})-g^{ij}\energy{\mathcal L}.
$$

The equality $\energy{T}^{0i}=\energy{T}^{i0}$ expresses the equality of energy flux and momentum density in natural units.

## Example 2.10 — Electromagnetic Energy–Momentum Tensor

For $\energy{\mathcal L}=-\tfrac14\electric{F}_{\mu\nu}\electric{F}^{\mu\nu}$, the canonical tensor is

$$
\energy{T}_{\mathrm{can}}^{\mu\nu}=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\partial^{\nu}\electric{A}_{\rho}+\tfrac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}. \tag{2.42}
$$

It is neither symmetric nor gauge invariant: it depends on the potential rather than only on field strength. The improved tensor is

$$
\energy{\Theta}^{\mu\nu}=-\electric{F}^{\mu\rho}\electric{F}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}+\tfrac14g^{\mu\nu}\electric{F}_{\rho\sigma}\electric{F}^{\rho\sigma}. \tag{2.43}
$$

It is symmetric and gauge invariant. The component $\energy{\Theta}^{00}=\tfrac12(\electric{\mathbf E}^{2}+\magnetic{\mathbf B}^{2})$ is electromagnetic energy density, and $\energy{\Theta}^{0i}=(\electric{\mathbf E}\times\magnetic{\mathbf B})^{i}$ is the Poynting vector.
