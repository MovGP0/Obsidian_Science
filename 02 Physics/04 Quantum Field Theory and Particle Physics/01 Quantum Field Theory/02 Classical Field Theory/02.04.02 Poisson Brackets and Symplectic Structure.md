---
title: "Poisson Brackets and Symplectic Structure"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Field Poisson brackets replace coordinate derivatives with spatial integrals of functional derivatives.

The Poisson bracket of two field functionals is defined by

$$
\{F,G\}=\int d^{3}\mechanical{x}\left[\frac{\delta F}{\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})}\frac{\delta G}{\delta\mechanical{\pi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})}-\frac{\delta F}{\delta\mechanical{\pi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})}\frac{\delta G}{\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x})}\right]. \tag{2.18}
$$

Here $\delta/\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{x})$ denotes a functional derivative. The fundamental brackets are

$$
\{\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),\mechanical{\pi}^{b}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}=\delta^{ab}\delta^{(3)}(\mechanical{\mathbf x}-\mechanical{\mathbf y}), \tag{2.19}
$$

$$
\{\signal{\phi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),\signal{\phi}^{b}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}=0=\{\mechanical{\pi}^{a}(\mechanical{\mathbf x},\mechanical{t}),\mechanical{\pi}^{b}(\mechanical{\mathbf y},\mechanical{t})\}. \tag{2.20}
$$

The delta distribution expresses locality on the chosen time slice: integrating it against a smooth spatial test function returns that function at the field point. Its dimension is inverse spatial volume, which matches the product of a four-dimensional scalar field and its conjugate momentum density.

For a regular bosonic theory, the Hamiltonian generates evolution through this bracket. Spatial gradients in the Hamiltonian require integration by parts when taking functional derivatives; boundary terms must again vanish or be treated explicitly. Constraints require a reduced phase space or an appropriate constrained bracket before quantization.

> [!note] Scope of the quantization rule
> The displayed bracket is the ordinary bosonic canonical bracket. Fermionic canonical variables require graded brackets; their anticommutators do not follow by replacing the ordinary bracket without this extra structure.

Canonical quantization promotes the bosonic relations to commutators using the formal replacement $\{\cdot,\cdot\}\to-i[\cdot,\cdot]$.
