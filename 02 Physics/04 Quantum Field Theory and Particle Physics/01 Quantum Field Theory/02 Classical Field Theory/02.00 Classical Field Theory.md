title: "Classical Field Theory"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Classical field dynamics, symmetries, and spacetime representations provide the structure needed before quantization.

The central question is how a local field can carry energy, momentum, and charge while its evolution respects spacetime symmetry. A classical field assigns a value to each point; it does not yet assign quantum probabilities to measurements. The action is the model input. Its stationary points give evolution equations, and its continuous symmetries identify candidate conserved currents.

Read the chapter in three passes. First, follow the oscillator chain to the field action and its boundary terms. Next, use the scalar and Maxwell examples to connect equations to energy and charge flow. Finally, study Lorentz representations to see why fields with different component structures need different transformation laws. The exercises test these connections: the Proca example exposes a constraint, while Derrick scaling tests whether a proposed static field configuration can exist.

A local continuity equation gives a constant total charge only when the boundary carries no net flux. Keep this distinction throughout the chapter: local equations and global conservation require different information.

Quantum field theory has its roots in Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics. Before quantization, one must understand the classical structure: the Lagrangian formalism for infinitely many degrees of freedom, Noether's theorem and the relation between symmetries and conserved quantities, Minkowski spacetime, and representations of the Lorentz group.
