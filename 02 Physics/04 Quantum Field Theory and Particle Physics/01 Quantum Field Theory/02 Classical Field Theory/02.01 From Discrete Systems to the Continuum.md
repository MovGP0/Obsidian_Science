---
title: "From Discrete Systems to the Continuum"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A field extends a discrete mechanical system to a continuum of degrees of freedom.

The continuum is useful when the displacement changes little between adjacent sites. It replaces many coupled ordinary differential equations with one partial differential equation, but it discards information at the lattice scale. A smooth field is therefore a model of collective motion, rather than a statement that real matter has no microscopic structure.

Start with [[02.01.01 Review of Lagrangian Mechanics]] to see which endpoint conditions make a variation admissible. Then use [[02.01.02 Oscillator Chain and Continuum Limit]] to track what must remain fixed as the spacing decreases. Holding the individual spring constant fixed would give a different limit from holding the elastic stiffness fixed.
