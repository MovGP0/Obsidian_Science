---
title: "Decomposition and Representations"
---

> [!TLDR]
> Rotation and boost generators organize the six-dimensional Lorentz algebra.

Separate the generators into rotations $J^{i}=\tfrac12\varepsilon^{ijk}M_{jk}$ and boosts $K^{i}=M^{0i}$. Their commutation relations are

$$
[J^{i},J^{j}]=i\varepsilon^{ijk}J^{k}, \tag{2.63}
$$

$$
[J^{i},K^{j}]=i\varepsilon^{ijk}K^{k}, \tag{2.64}
$$

$$
[K^{i},K^{j}]=-i\varepsilon^{ijk}J^{k}. \tag{2.65}
$$

The minus sign in the boost commutator reflects the group's noncompact nature, which constrains its unitary representations. Define $A^{i}=\tfrac12(J^{i}+iK^{i})$ and $B^{i}=\tfrac12(J^{i}-iK^{i})$. Then

$$
[A^{i},A^{j}]=i\varepsilon^{ijk}A^{k},\qquad[B^{i},B^{j}]=i\varepsilon^{ijk}B^{k},\qquad[A^{i},B^{j}]=0. \tag{2.66}
$$

The imaginary coefficient in the definitions of the two new generator sets is essential. In their mixed commutator, the rotation–rotation term cancels the boost–boost term; the two cross terms also cancel. Within either set, the same signs instead reproduce a rotation algebra. This is a calculational tool for finite-dimensional complex representations.

Do not replace the real Lorentz group by two independent compact rotation groups. The decomposition holds after complexification. A finite-dimensional field representation need not be unitary; unitary transformation of quantum states is a separate requirement. Also, the algebra labels first specify representations of the covering group. Half-integer spinor labels do not define single-valued representations of the proper Lorentz group itself. See [Tong, section 4.4, following (4.61)](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/qft/four.pdf#page=11).

Thus $\mathfrak{so}(1,3)_{\mathbb C}\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\mathbb C}\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)_{\mathbb C}$. Irreducible complex representations are classified by $(j_A,j_B)$, with each label in $\{0,\tfrac12,1,\ldots\}$.
