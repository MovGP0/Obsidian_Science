---
title: "Transformation of Fields"
---

> [!TLDR]
> A field transforms through its Lorentz representation together with a change in its spacetime argument.

For a field in representation $D(\Lambda)$,

$$
\signal{\phi}'^{a}(\mechanical{x}')=D(\Lambda)^{a}{}_{b}\signal{\phi}^{b}(\mechanical{x}),\qquad
\mechanical{x}'^{\mu}=\Lambda^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\mechanical{x}^{\nu}. \tag{2.67}
$$

For $\Lambda=1+\omega$,

$$
\delta\signal{\phi}^{a}=-\frac{i}{2}\omega_{\mu\nu}(S^{\mu\nu})^{a}{}_{b}\signal{\phi}^{b}-\omega^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\mechanical{x}^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\signal{\phi}^{a}. \tag{2.68}
$$

To obtain (2.68), first express the transformed field at the original coordinate. The inverse coordinate map shifts its argument by the negative of the coordinate displacement; expansion of this argument gives the second term. The first term changes components at the transported point. For a scalar only the transport term remains.

As a sign check, insert the displayed vector generators in the first term. Their contraction gives the same infinitesimal component matrix as the coordinate Lorentz transformation. This fixes the convention and explains why changing between a Lorentz matrix and its inverse changes a sign. The full transformation preserves tensor contractions even though each component can change.

The matrices $S^{\mu\nu}$ are the representation generators. For a scalar they vanish. For a vector, $(S^{\mu\nu})^{\rho}{}_{\sigma}=i(g^{\mu\rho}\delta^{\nu}{}_{\sigma}-g^{\nu\rho}\delta^{\mu}{}_{\sigma})$. For a Dirac spinor, $S^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{i}{4}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]$, where $\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2g^{\mu\nu}$.
