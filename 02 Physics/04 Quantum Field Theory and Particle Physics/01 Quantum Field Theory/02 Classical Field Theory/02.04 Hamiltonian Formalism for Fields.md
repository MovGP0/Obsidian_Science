title: "Hamiltonian Formalism for Fields"
---

> [!TLDR]
> The Hamiltonian field formalism uses conjugate momentum densities and canonical brackets.

The Hamiltonian question is which field data on one time slice determine the next slice. For a regular scalar theory, the field and its conjugate momentum supply those data. The spatial-gradient terms stay in the Hamiltonian because the Legendre transformation acts on time derivatives only.

Read [[02.04.01 Conjugate Momentum Density and the Hamiltonian]] before [[02.04.02 Poisson Brackets and Symplectic Structure]]. First check whether velocities can be recovered from momenta. Only then use canonical brackets for the independent variables. Gauge fields require care: a momentum that vanishes identically is a constraint, rather than a new freely specifiable initial value.
