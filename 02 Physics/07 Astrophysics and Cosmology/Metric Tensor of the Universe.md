The **metric tensor of the Universe** is given by

$$g_{00} = -c^2$$
$$g_{ij} = {a(t)}^2\ \delta_{ij}$$
where

- $a(t)$ is the scale factor of the universe. Near the present time, its first-order Taylor expansion is:

$$a(t) \approx 1+H\,t = 1+\frac{t}{\tau}$$

Here $t$ is measured from now, so $a(0)=1$. This linear form is not the general expansion law.

- $\tau = \frac{1}{H}$ is (close to) the age of the universe and $H = H_0$ is the present value of the Hubble parameter. The Hubble parameter changes with time.
    $\frac{1}{H} \approx 13.97\,10^{9}\,\mathrm{yrs.}$
    $H ​\approx 70\, \frac{ \mathrm{km} }{ \mathrm{s} } / {\mathrm{Mpc}}$

- $\delta_{ij}$ is the Kronecker Delta, which in 3 dimensions is given by:

$$\delta = \begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}$$

Therefore

$$g = \begin{pmatrix}
-c^2 & 0 & 0 & 0\\
0 & a(t)^2 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & a(t)^2 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & a(t)^2 \\
\end{pmatrix}$$
